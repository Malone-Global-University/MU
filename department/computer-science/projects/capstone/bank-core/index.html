Banking Software Engineering (Bank Stack)

Project Overview

Bank Stack is a multidisciplinary computer science capstone devoted to the study and development of banking software from first principles. The project examines the historical evolution of accounting, the automation of banking operations, the principles of modern banking, and the computational mechanisms that make financial software possible.

The educational objective is to understand how these disciplines interact and to apply that understanding through practical engineering. The project is informed by real software development work; public MGU materials present selected research, general principles, educational examples, and lessons without requiring disclosure of proprietary systems.

Research and Project Documentation

Research Foundations

An initial index of 25 research subjects and their principal concepts, covering accounting, banking automation, computing theory, system architecture, transaction processing, payment systems, security, reliability, and verification.

Additional documentation will be organized as research and practical development progress.

Research and Engineering Objectives

  • Understand the origins and operating principles of journals, ledgers, and double-entry accounting.
  • Investigate banking automation and contemporary banking operations, including payment processing and reconciliation.
  • Connect computational theory, computer architecture, databases, networking, and security to financial software engineering.
  • Construct and test educational prototypes that demonstrate accounting integrity, transaction correctness, and system reliability.
  • Document findings, design alternatives, unsuccessful approaches, and subsequent revisions.

Development Methodology

The project follows an iterative research, design, implementation, testing, and revision process. The initial research index is a starting point rather than a finalized curriculum or approved technical specification. No particular implementation architecture is presumed to be correct before investigation and testing.

Educational prototypes are not represented as production-ready or regulatory-compliant banking systems. Practical deployment would require separate validation of operational, security, legal, and regulatory requirements.

Current stage: Foundational research and project formulation.

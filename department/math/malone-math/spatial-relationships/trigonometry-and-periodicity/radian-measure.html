Radian Measure

Degrees divide a circle into \(360\) equal parts. Radians measure angles more directly through the geometry of the circle itself. One radian is the angle that subtends an arc equal in length to the radius. This makes radian measure the natural language for the unit circle, periodic functions, angular motion, and calculus.

The Central Idea

Radian measure compares arc length with radius.

If an angle \(\theta\) subtends an arc of length \(s\) in a circle of radius \(r\), then

\[ \boxed{\theta=\frac{s}{r}} \]

when \(\theta\) is measured in radians.

Equivalently,

\[ \boxed{s=r\theta}. \]

This relationship is the defining geometric idea of radian measure.

One Radian

One radian is the central angle that intercepts an arc whose length equals the radius.

If

\[ s=r, \]

then

\[ \theta=\frac{s}{r}=1. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{\theta=1\text{ radian}}. \]

Radians Come from a Ratio

The expression

\[ \frac{s}{r} \]

compares two lengths.

If both are measured in meters,

\[ \frac{\text{meters}}{\text{meters}}=1. \]

Therefore radian measure is dimensionless in the formal dimensional sense, even though we often write the label

\[ \text{rad} \]

to make clear that the number represents an angle.

Why a Full Circle Is \(2\pi\) Radians

The circumference of a circle is

\[ 2\pi r. \]

A full revolution subtends this entire arc, so

\[ \theta = \frac{2\pi r}{r} = 2\pi. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{360^\circ=2\pi\text{ radians}}. \]

Half a Circle

A half-circle has arc length

\[ \pi r. \]

Therefore its angle measure is

\[ \frac{\pi r}{r}=\pi. \]

Thus,

\[ \boxed{180^\circ=\pi\text{ radians}}. \]

A Quarter Circle

One quarter of a full revolution is

\[ \frac{2\pi}{4} = \frac{\pi}{2}. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{90^\circ=\frac{\pi}{2}\text{ radians}}. \]

The Fundamental Degree–Radian Equivalence

The most useful conversion fact is

\[ \boxed{180^\circ=\pi\text{ radians}}. \]

Every exact degree–radian conversion can be built from this relationship.

Degrees per Radian

Divide

\[ 180^\circ=\pi\text{ rad} \]

by \(\pi\):

\[ 1\text{ rad} = \frac{180^\circ}{\pi}. \]

Numerically,

\[ \boxed{1\text{ rad}\approx57.3^\circ}. \]

Radians per Degree

Divide

\[ 180^\circ=\pi\text{ rad} \]

by \(180\):

\[ 1^\circ = \frac{\pi}{180}\text{ rad}. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{1^\circ=\frac{\pi}{180}\text{ radians}}. \]

Converting Degrees to Radians

Multiply by

\[ \boxed{\frac{\pi}{180^\circ}}. \]

The degree unit cancels, leaving radians.

Symbolically,

\[ \boxed{ \theta_{\text{rad}} = \theta_{\text{deg}} \cdot \frac{\pi}{180} }. \]

Example: Convert \(60^\circ\) to Radians

\[ 60^\circ \cdot \frac{\pi}{180^\circ} = \frac{\pi}{3}. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{60^\circ=\frac{\pi}{3}}. \]

Example: Convert \(45^\circ\) to Radians

\[ 45^\circ \cdot \frac{\pi}{180^\circ} = \frac{\pi}{4}. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{45^\circ=\frac{\pi}{4}}. \]

Example: Convert \(30^\circ\) to Radians

\[ 30^\circ \cdot \frac{\pi}{180^\circ} = \frac{\pi}{6}. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{30^\circ=\frac{\pi}{6}}. \]

Converting Radians to Degrees

Multiply by

\[ \boxed{\frac{180^\circ}{\pi}}. \]

Symbolically,

\[ \boxed{ \theta_{\text{deg}} = \theta_{\text{rad}} \cdot \frac{180}{\pi} }. \]

Example: Convert \(\pi/4\) to Degrees

\[ \frac{\pi}{4} \cdot \frac{180^\circ}{\pi} = 45^\circ. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{\frac{\pi}{4}=45^\circ}. \]

Example: Convert \(2\pi/3\) to Degrees

\[ \frac{2\pi}{3} \cdot \frac{180^\circ}{\pi} = 120^\circ. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{\frac{2\pi}{3}=120^\circ}. \]

Example: Convert \(5\pi/6\) to Degrees

\[ \frac{5\pi}{6} \cdot \frac{180^\circ}{\pi} = 150^\circ. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{\frac{5\pi}{6}=150^\circ}. \]

Common Exact Angles

\[ \boxed{ \begin{array}{c|c} \text{Degrees} & \text{Radians}\\ \hline 0^\circ & 0\\ 30^\circ & \frac{\pi}{6}\\[4pt] 45^\circ & \frac{\pi}{4}\\[4pt] 60^\circ & \frac{\pi}{3}\\[4pt] 90^\circ & \frac{\pi}{2}\\[4pt] 120^\circ & \frac{2\pi}{3}\\[4pt] 135^\circ & \frac{3\pi}{4}\\[4pt] 150^\circ & \frac{5\pi}{6}\\[4pt] 180^\circ & \pi \end{array} }. \]

Common Angles through One Full Revolution

\[ \boxed{ \begin{array}{c|c} \text{Degrees} & \text{Radians}\\ \hline 180^\circ & \pi\\ 210^\circ & \frac{7\pi}{6}\\[4pt] 225^\circ & \frac{5\pi}{4}\\[4pt] 240^\circ & \frac{4\pi}{3}\\[4pt] 270^\circ & \frac{3\pi}{2}\\[4pt] 300^\circ & \frac{5\pi}{3}\\[4pt] 315^\circ & \frac{7\pi}{4}\\[4pt] 330^\circ & \frac{11\pi}{6}\\[4pt] 360^\circ & 2\pi \end{array} }. \]

Unit-Circle Angles in Radians

On the unit circle, radian measure has an especially direct meaning.

Since

\[ r=1, \]

the arc-length formula

\[ s=r\theta \]

becomes

\[ \boxed{s=\theta}. \]

Numerically, the radian measure of an angle equals the length of its intercepted arc on the unit circle.

Why the Unit Circle Makes Radians Natural

A point moving around the unit circle travels an arc length equal to the angle measure in radians.

For example, rotating through

\[ \frac{\pi}{2} \]

radians corresponds to traveling

\[ \frac{\pi}{2} \]

units along the unit circle.

Positive and Negative Radians

The same rotation convention used for degrees applies to radians.

  • counterclockwise rotation is positive;
  • clockwise rotation is negative.

For example,

\[ -\frac{\pi}{2} \]

is a quarter-turn clockwise.

Coterminal Angles in Radians

One full revolution is

\[ 2\pi. \]

Therefore angles of the form

\[ \boxed{\theta+2\pi k,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z} \]

are coterminal.

Example: Coterminal Angles

The angle

\[ \frac{\pi}{3} \]

is coterminal with

\[ \frac{\pi}{3}+2\pi = \frac{7\pi}{3} \]

and

\[ \frac{\pi}{3}-2\pi = -\frac{5\pi}{3}. \]

Reference Angles in Radians

Reference-angle reasoning works the same way in radians.

The relevant axis angles are

\[ 0,\qquad \frac{\pi}{2},\qquad \pi,\qquad \frac{3\pi}{2},\qquad 2\pi. \]

Quadrant-II Reference Angle

If

\[ \frac{\pi}{2}<\theta<\pi, \]

then the reference angle is

\[ \boxed{\alpha=\pi-\theta}. \]

Quadrant-III Reference Angle

If

\[ \pi<\theta<\frac{3\pi}{2}, \]

then

\[ \boxed{\alpha=\theta-\pi}. \]

Quadrant-IV Reference Angle

If

\[ \frac{3\pi}{2}<\theta<2\pi, \]

then

\[ \boxed{\alpha=2\pi-\theta}. \]

Example: Reference Angle of \(5\pi/6\)

Since

\[ \frac{5\pi}{6} \]

lies in Quadrant II,

\[ \alpha = \pi-\frac{5\pi}{6} = \boxed{\frac{\pi}{6}}. \]

Example: Reference Angle of \(7\pi/6\)

This angle lies in Quadrant III:

\[ \alpha = \frac{7\pi}{6}-\pi = \boxed{\frac{\pi}{6}}. \]

Example: Reference Angle of \(11\pi/6\)

This angle lies in Quadrant IV:

\[ \alpha = 2\pi-\frac{11\pi}{6} = \boxed{\frac{\pi}{6}}. \]

Arc Length

The defining radian relationship

\[ \theta=\frac{s}{r} \]

can be rearranged as

\[ \boxed{s=r\theta}. \]

This formula is valid directly when \(\theta\) is in radians.

Example: Arc Length

A circle has radius

\[ r=8 \]

and central angle

\[ \theta=\frac{3\pi}{4}. \]

Then

\[ s = 8\left(\frac{3\pi}{4}\right) = \boxed{6\pi}. \]

Why Degrees Must Be Converted before Using \(s=r\theta\)

The compact formula

\[ s=r\theta \]

assumes \(\theta\) is a pure radian ratio.

If an angle is given as \(60^\circ\), first convert:

\[ 60^\circ=\frac{\pi}{3}. \]

Then use

\[ s=r\frac{\pi}{3}. \]

Sector Area

The area of a full circle is

\[ \pi r^2. \]

Since a full angle is \(2\pi\) radians, the fraction of the circle covered by angle \(\theta\) is

\[ \frac{\theta}{2\pi}. \]

Therefore the sector area is

\[ \boxed{ K=\frac12r^2\theta } \]

when \(\theta\) is in radians.

Example: Sector Area

Suppose

\[ r=6 \]

and

\[ \theta=\frac{2\pi}{3}. \]

Then

\[ K = \frac12(6^2)\left(\frac{2\pi}{3}\right) = 18\cdot\frac{2\pi}{3} = \boxed{12\pi}. \]

Radians and Similar Circles

Suppose the same central angle appears in circles of different radii.

The arc lengths scale with the radii:

\[ s_1=r_1\theta, \qquad s_2=r_2\theta. \]

Therefore,

\[ \frac{s_1}{r_1} = \frac{s_2}{r_2} = \theta. \]

Radian measure is independent of the size of the circle.

Angular Displacement

Angular displacement measures the change in angular position.

If an object moves from angle \(\theta_1\) to angle \(\theta_2\), then

\[ \boxed{ \Delta\theta=\theta_2-\theta_1 }. \]

In advanced work, angular displacement is naturally measured in radians.

Angular Speed

Average angular speed is angular displacement per unit time:

\[ \boxed{ \omega_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\Delta\theta}{\Delta t} }. \]

Common units include radians per second.

Linear Speed along a Circle

Since

\[ s=r\theta, \]

divide by time:

\[ \frac{s}{t} = r\frac{\theta}{t}. \]

Therefore, for uniform circular motion,

\[ \boxed{v=r\omega}, \]

where \(v\) is tangential speed and \(\omega\) is angular speed in radians per unit time.

Example: Tangential Speed

A point moves on a circle of radius

\[ 0.5\text{ m} \]

with angular speed

\[ 8\text{ rad/s}. \]

Then

\[ v=r\omega = 0.5(8) = \boxed{4\text{ m/s}}. \]

Revolutions and Radians

One revolution equals

\[ 2\pi\text{ radians}. \]

Therefore,

\[ n\text{ revolutions} = \boxed{2\pi n\text{ radians}}. \]

Example: Convert \(3.5\) Revolutions to Radians

\[ 3.5(2\pi) = 7\pi. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{3.5\text{ revolutions}=7\pi\text{ radians}}. \]

Radians and Periodic Functions

The basic sine and cosine periods are most naturally expressed as

\[ \boxed{2\pi}. \]

Tangent and cotangent have basic period

\[ \boxed{\pi}. \]

These values directly reflect the unit-circle rotations that reproduce the same coordinates or coordinate ratios.

Why Radians Are Natural in Calculus

When angles are measured in radians, the fundamental small-angle behavior of sine is

\[ \frac{\sin x}{x}\to1 \]

as

\[ x\to0. \]

This leads to the especially simple derivative formulas

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\sin x=\cos x \]

and

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\cos x=-\sin x. \]

These exact forms depend on using radians.

Degrees Introduce a Scale Factor in Calculus

If angle input were measured in degrees instead, the change of units would introduce conversion constants into differentiation formulas.

Radians eliminate that artificial scale factor because they arise directly from the ratio

\[ \frac{s}{r}. \]

Small Angles

For a small angle measured in radians,

\[ \sin\theta\approx\theta. \]

This approximation is meaningful because radian measure directly compares the angle with arc length on the unit circle.

The approximation is not numerically valid in the same form if \(\theta\) is entered in degrees.

Example: Small-Angle Comparison

For

\[ \theta=0.01\text{ rad}, \]

we have

\[ \sin(0.01)\approx0.0099998. \]

This is extremely close to

\[ 0.01. \]

Exact Radian Values versus Decimal Radians

An angle may be written exactly, such as

\[ \frac{5\pi}{6}, \]

or approximately, such as

\[ 2.618. \]

Exact forms involving \(\pi\) preserve the geometry more clearly and should usually be kept when possible.

Calculator Mode

A calculator may interpret trigonometric inputs in degree mode or radian mode.

For example,

\[ \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right) \]

should be evaluated in radian mode.

The mathematically equivalent expression

\[ \sin30^\circ \]

should be evaluated in degree mode.

Radians Are Often Written without the Unit Label

In mathematics, an unlabeled angular input such as

\[ \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) \]

is normally interpreted in radians.

Degrees, by contrast, are explicitly marked with the degree symbol:

\[ 45^\circ. \]

Common Error: Using \(180/\pi\) in the Wrong Direction

To convert degrees to radians, multiply by

\[ \boxed{\frac{\pi}{180}}. \]

To convert radians to degrees, multiply by

\[ \boxed{\frac{180}{\pi}}. \]

Dimensional cancellation helps identify the correct direction.

Common Error: Treating \(\pi\) as a Unit

The symbol \(\pi\) is a number, approximately

\[ 3.14159. \]

In

\[ \frac{\pi}{3}, \]

\(\pi\) is part of the numerical angle value, not a separate unit.

Common Error: Using \(s=r\theta\) with Degrees

The formula

\[ s=r\theta \]

requires radian measure.

If \(\theta=90^\circ\), first convert:

\[ 90^\circ=\frac{\pi}{2}. \]

Then

\[ s=r\frac{\pi}{2}. \]

Common Error: Thinking One Radian Equals \(60^\circ\)

One radian is approximately

\[ 57.3^\circ, \]

not \(60^\circ\).

The familiar angle

\[ 60^\circ \]

equals

\[ \frac{\pi}{3}\approx1.0472\text{ rad}. \]

Common Error: Forgetting Negative Radians

Radian measure can be negative just like degree measure.

For example,

\[ -\frac{\pi}{3} \]

represents a \(60^\circ\) clockwise rotation.

Common Error: Forgetting Angles beyond \(2\pi\)

Radian measure is not restricted to one revolution.

Angles such as

\[ \frac{13\pi}{4} \]

are meaningful and represent multiple rotations.

Subtracting multiples of \(2\pi\) finds a simpler coterminal angle.

A Reliable Procedure for Degrees to Radians

  1. Write the degree measure.
  2. Multiply by \(\pi/180^\circ\).
  3. Cancel the degree unit.
  4. Simplify the numerical fraction.
  5. Keep \(\pi\) exact when possible.

A Reliable Procedure for Radians to Degrees

  1. Write the radian measure.
  2. Multiply by \(180^\circ/\pi\).
  3. Cancel \(\pi\) when possible.
  4. Simplify.
  5. Attach the degree symbol.

A Reliable Procedure for Arc Length

  1. Identify the radius \(r\).
  2. Confirm that the angle is in radians.
  3. If necessary, convert degrees to radians.
  4. Use \(s=r\theta\).
  5. Attach the appropriate length unit.

A Reliable Procedure for Sector Area

  1. Identify the radius.
  2. Express the central angle in radians.
  3. Use \(K=\frac12r^2\theta\).
  4. Simplify exact expressions before approximating.
  5. Attach square units.

What to Remember

Radian measure is defined by

\[ \boxed{\theta=\frac{s}{r}}. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{s=r\theta} \]

when \(\theta\) is in radians.

The fundamental conversion is

\[ \boxed{180^\circ=\pi\text{ radians}}. \]
  • Degrees to radians: multiply by \(\pi/180\).
  • Radians to degrees: multiply by \(180/\pi\).
  • A full revolution is \(2\pi\) radians.
  • On the unit circle, radian measure equals intercepted arc length numerically.
  • Coterminal radian angles differ by \(2\pi k\).
  • Reference-angle formulas translate naturally from degrees into radians.
  • Sector area is \(K=\frac12r^2\theta\) for radian \(\theta\).
  • Angular and linear speed are related by \(v=r\omega\).
  • Radians are dimensionless ratios of lengths.
  • Radians are the natural angle measure for calculus and periodic analysis.
  • Calculator mode must match the angle unit being used.

Where We Go Next

Radian measure gives angle a direct connection to arc length and makes the unit circle numerically natural.

With degrees and radians both available, trigonometric graphs and periodic models can be expressed in the language most useful for geometry, calculus, and applied analysis.

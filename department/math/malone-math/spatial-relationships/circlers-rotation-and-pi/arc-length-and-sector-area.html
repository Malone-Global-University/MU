Arc Length and Sector Area

An angle at the center of a circle determines both a length along the circle and an area inside it. The larger the angle, the larger the intercepted arc and the larger the sector it sweeps out. Arc length and sector area are therefore two expressions of the same central idea: taking a fraction of a complete circle.

The Central Idea

A central angle determines a fraction of a complete revolution. That same fraction determines a fraction of the circumference and a fraction of the circle's area.

If a central angle represents one quarter of a full turn, then its arc is one quarter of the circumference and its sector is one quarter of the circle's area.

This proportional structure is the foundation of every arc-length and sector-area formula in this lesson.

Arc Length

An arc is a portion of a circle's circumference. Its arc length is the actual linear distance measured along that curved portion of the circle.

Arc measure and arc length are related but different quantities.

  • Arc measure describes an angular amount, such as \(60^\circ\) or \(\pi/3\) radians.
  • Arc length describes a linear distance, such as \(5\text{ cm}\) or \(3\pi\text{ m}\).

The symbol \(s\) is commonly used for arc length.

Sector Area

A sector is the region bounded by two radii and the arc connecting their endpoints.

Its area is a fraction of the total area of the circle. A sector can be small, such as a narrow slice, or large, such as most of the circle.

The symbol \(A\) will be used for sector area.

One Angle Controls Both Quantities

Arc Length as a Fraction of Circumference

A complete circle has circumference

\[ C=2\pi r. \]

If a central angle is \(\theta\) degrees, then it represents the fraction

\[ \frac{\theta}{360^\circ} \]

of a complete revolution.

Therefore, the intercepted arc has the same fraction of the full circumference:

\[ \boxed{s=\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}(2\pi r)}. \]

This is the degree-based arc-length formula.

Example: Arc Length Using Degrees

Find the length of the arc intercepted by a \(72^\circ\) central angle in a circle of radius \(10\text{ cm}\).

Use

\[ s=\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}(2\pi r). \]

Substitute \(\theta=72^\circ\) and \(r=10\):

\[ s=\frac{72}{360}(2\pi)(10). \]

Since \(72/360=1/5\),

\[ s=\frac{1}{5}(20\pi)=\boxed{4\pi\text{ cm}}. \]

As a decimal,

\[ s\approx12.57\text{ cm}. \]

Radians Make Arc Length Simpler

Radian measure was defined by the ratio

\[ \theta=\frac{s}{r}, \]

where \(s\) is arc length and \(r\) is radius.

Solving for \(s\) gives the fundamental radian formula

\[ \boxed{s=r\theta}. \]

This compact formula works when and only when \(\theta\) is measured in radians.

Why \(s=r\theta\) Is Natural

One radian is the central angle that intercepts an arc whose length equals the radius. Therefore,

\[ \theta=1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad s=r. \]

Two radians intercept an arc twice as long:

\[ \theta=2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad s=2r. \]

In general, \(\theta\) radians intercept an arc \(\theta\) times the radius:

\[ \boxed{s=r\theta}. \]

Example: Arc Length Using Radians

Find the arc length in a circle of radius \(9\text{ m}\) when the central angle is \(2\pi/3\) radians.

\[ s=r\theta. \]

Substitute:

\[ s=9\left(\frac{2\pi}{3}\right). \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{s=6\pi\text{ m}}. \]

The Degree and Radian Formulas Agree

The degree formula is

\[ s=\frac{\theta_{\deg}}{360^\circ}(2\pi r). \]

Since

\[ \theta_{\text{rad}}=\theta_{\deg}\frac{\pi}{180^\circ}, \]

we have

\[ r\theta_{\text{rad}} =r\left(\theta_{\deg}\frac{\pi}{180^\circ}\right) =\frac{\theta_{\deg}}{360^\circ}(2\pi r). \]

The formulas are not competing rules. They are the same geometric relationship expressed in different angular units.

Sector Area as a Fraction of Circle Area

A complete circle has area

\[ A_{\text{circle}}=\pi r^2. \]

A \(\theta^\circ\) sector represents the fraction

\[ \frac{\theta}{360^\circ} \]

of the full circle. Therefore,

\[ \boxed{A=\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2}. \]

This is the degree-based sector-area formula.

Example: Sector Area Using Degrees

Find the area of a \(135^\circ\) sector in a circle of radius \(8\text{ cm}\).

\[ A=\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2. \]

Substitute:

\[ A=\frac{135}{360}\pi(8)^2. \]

Reduce the fraction:

\[ \frac{135}{360}=\frac{3}{8}. \]

Therefore,

\[ A=\frac{3}{8}(64\pi)=\boxed{24\pi\text{ cm}^2}. \]

Sector Area in Radians

If \(\theta\) is measured in radians, then the fraction of a full revolution is

\[ \frac{\theta}{2\pi}. \]

Multiply that fraction by the circle's area:

\[ A=\frac{\theta}{2\pi}(\pi r^2). \]

Cancel \(\pi\):

\[ \boxed{A=\frac12 r^2\theta}. \]

This formula requires \(\theta\) to be in radians.

Example: Sector Area Using Radians

Find the area of a sector with radius \(12\text{ m}\) and central angle \(5\pi/6\) radians.

\[ A=\frac12r^2\theta. \]

Substitute:

\[ A=\frac12(12)^2\left(\frac{5\pi}{6}\right). \] \[ A=72\left(\frac{5\pi}{6}\right)=\boxed{60\pi\text{ m}^2}. \]

Arc Length and Sector Area Share the Same Fraction

For any central angle, the following ratios are equal:

\[ \boxed{ \frac{\text{central angle}}{\text{full turn}} =\frac{\text{arc length}}{\text{circumference}} =\frac{\text{sector area}}{\text{circle area}} }. \]

In degrees,

\[ \boxed{ \frac{\theta}{360^\circ} =\frac{s}{2\pi r} =\frac{A}{\pi r^2} }. \]

This proportion is often more useful than memorizing isolated formulas because it reveals why those formulas work.

A Visual Fraction-of-a-Circle Model

Finding an Angle from Arc Length

The radian formula

\[ s=r\theta \]

can be solved for the angle:

\[ \boxed{\theta=\frac{s}{r}}. \]

If \(s\) and \(r\) use the same linear unit, the units cancel and the result is a radian measure.

Example: Find the Central Angle

An arc is \(14\text{ cm}\) long in a circle of radius \(8\text{ cm}\). Find the central angle in radians.

\[ \theta=\frac{s}{r}=\frac{14}{8}=\boxed{\frac74\text{ radians}}. \]

If a degree measure is desired, convert afterward:

\[ \frac74\left(\frac{180^\circ}{\pi}\right) \approx\boxed{100.3^\circ}. \]

Finding the Radius from Arc Length

Starting from

\[ s=r\theta, \]

solve for \(r\):

\[ \boxed{r=\frac{s}{\theta}}. \]

Again, this form assumes that \(\theta\) is measured in radians.

Example: Find the Radius

An arc of length \(15\pi\text{ cm}\) is intercepted by an angle of \(3\pi/5\) radians. Find the radius.

\[ r=\frac{s}{\theta} =\frac{15\pi}{3\pi/5}. \]

Dividing by \(3\pi/5\) is the same as multiplying by \(5/(3\pi)\):

\[ r=15\pi\left(\frac{5}{3\pi}\right)=\boxed{25\text{ cm}}. \]

Finding an Angle from Sector Area

From

\[ A=\frac12r^2\theta, \]

solve for \(\theta\):

\[ \boxed{\theta=\frac{2A}{r^2}}. \]

The resulting angle is in radians.

Example: Find an Angle from Sector Area

A sector has area \(30\text{ cm}^2\) in a circle of radius \(5\text{ cm}\). Find its central angle in radians.

\[ \theta=\frac{2A}{r^2} =\frac{2(30)}{25} =\boxed{\frac{12}{5}\text{ radians}}. \]

Finding the Radius from Sector Area

Starting from

\[ A=\frac12r^2\theta, \]

multiply by \(2\), divide by \(\theta\), and take the positive square root:

\[ r^2=\frac{2A}{\theta}, \] \[ \boxed{r=\sqrt{\frac{2A}{\theta}}}. \]

Radius is a length, so the nonnegative square root is used.

Connecting Arc Length Directly to Sector Area

In radians,

\[ s=r\theta \]

and

\[ A=\frac12r^2\theta. \]

Since \(r\theta=s\), substitute into the area formula:

\[ A=\frac12r(r\theta). \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{A=\frac12rs}. \]

This useful relationship gives sector area directly from radius and arc length, without first finding the angle.

Example: Sector Area from Radius and Arc Length

A sector has radius \(7\text{ cm}\) and arc length \(11\text{ cm}\). Find its area.

\[ A=\frac12rs. \] \[ A=\frac12(7)(11)=\boxed{\frac{77}{2}\text{ cm}^2}. \]

In decimal form,

\[ A=\boxed{38.5\text{ cm}^2}. \]

Minor and Major Arc Lengths

A pair of radii usually determines two arcs between the same endpoints: a minor arc and a major arc.

If the minor central angle is \(\theta\), then the corresponding major angle is

\[ 2\pi-\theta \]

in radians, or

\[ 360^\circ-\theta \]

in degrees.

The same arc-length formulas then apply to either arc.

Example: Minor and Major Arcs

A circle has radius \(6\text{ cm}\), and two radii form a \(120^\circ\) central angle.

The minor arc length is

\[ s_{\text{minor}} =\frac{120}{360}(2\pi)(6) =\boxed{4\pi\text{ cm}}. \]

The major angle is

\[ 360^\circ-120^\circ=240^\circ. \]

Therefore, the major arc length is

\[ s_{\text{major}} =\frac{240}{360}(2\pi)(6) =\boxed{8\pi\text{ cm}}. \]

Together,

\[ 4\pi+8\pi=12\pi, \]

which is the full circumference \(2\pi(6)\).

Minor and Major Sectors

The same two radii determine a minor sector and a major sector. Their areas add to the area of the full circle.

If \(A_{\text{minor}}\) is known, then

\[ \boxed{A_{\text{major}}=\pi r^2-A_{\text{minor}}}. \]

This subtraction method is often faster than recalculating the major angle.

Semicircles and Half-Disks

A semicircle corresponds to a central angle of

\[ 180^\circ=\pi\text{ radians}. \]

Its arc length is half the circumference:

\[ s=\pi r. \]

Its sector area is half the circle's area:

\[ A=\frac12\pi r^2. \]

Be careful: the perimeter of a semicircular region also includes the diameter, so its perimeter is \(\pi r+2r\), not merely \(\pi r\).

Quarter Circles

A quarter circle corresponds to

\[ 90^\circ=\frac{\pi}{2}\text{ radians}. \]

Its arc length is

\[ s=\frac14(2\pi r)=\boxed{\frac{\pi r}{2}}, \]

and its sector area is

\[ A=\frac14\pi r^2. \]

Exact and Approximate Answers

If a calculation produces a multiple of \(\pi\), that expression is usually the preferred exact answer.

For example,

\[ s=5\pi\text{ cm} \]

is exact, while

\[ s\approx15.71\text{ cm} \]

is an approximation.

Unless a decimal is requested, preserve \(\pi\) as long as possible and round only at the end.

Units Matter

Arc length is a linear measurement, so it uses units such as

  • centimeters,
  • meters,
  • inches,
  • miles.

Sector area is two-dimensional, so it uses square units such as \(\text{cm}^2\), \(\text{m}^2\), or \(\text{in}^2\).

A result of \(20\text{ cm}\) for a sector area is dimensionally incorrect; the unit must be \(\text{cm}^2\).

Why Radians Are Dimensionless

Radian measure is defined by

\[ \theta=\frac{s}{r}. \]

If \(s\) and \(r\) are both measured in centimeters, then

\[ \frac{\text{cm}}{\text{cm}}=1. \]

The linear units cancel. This is why radians are mathematically dimensionless, even though the word radian is retained to communicate that the number represents an angle.

Dimensional Check for Arc Length

In

\[ s=r\theta, \]

the radius carries the linear unit and \(\theta\) in radians is dimensionless. Therefore, the result has a linear unit, exactly as arc length should.

Dimensional Check for Sector Area

In

\[ A=\frac12r^2\theta, \]

\(r^2\) supplies square units and \(\theta\) is dimensionless. Therefore, the result has units of area.

Scaling a Circle

Suppose every linear dimension of a circle is multiplied by a scale factor \(k\), while the central angle stays the same.

Since

\[ s=r\theta, \]

replacing \(r\) with \(kr\) gives

\[ s'=kr\theta=ks. \]

Arc length therefore scales linearly with the radius.

Sector area satisfies

\[ A=\frac12r^2\theta. \]

Replacing \(r\) with \(kr\) gives

\[ A'=\frac12(kr)^2\theta=k^2A. \]

Sector area therefore scales with the square of the scale factor.

Example: Doubling the Radius

If the central angle remains fixed and the radius doubles, then

  • the arc length doubles;
  • the sector area becomes four times as large.

This mirrors the familiar scaling laws for circumference and circle area.

Using a Proportion Instead of a Memorized Formula

Suppose a \(40^\circ\) sector lies in a circle of radius \(18\text{ cm}\). The fraction of the circle is

\[ \frac{40}{360}=\frac19. \]

Therefore, the arc length is one ninth of the circumference:

\[ s=\frac19(36\pi)=4\pi\text{ cm}. \]

The sector area is one ninth of the circle area:

\[ A=\frac19(324\pi)=36\pi\text{ cm}^2. \]

Thinking in fractions of a circle often reduces the amount of formula memorization required.

Arc Length on Concentric Circles

Two concentric circles can share the same central angle but have different radii. For a fixed angle \(\theta\),

\[ s=r\theta. \]

The circle with the larger radius has the longer intercepted arc.

If one radius is three times another, the corresponding arc length is also three times as large for the same angular displacement.

The Same Angle Does Not Mean the Same Arc Length

A \(60^\circ\) angle in a small circle and a \(60^\circ\) angle in a large circle have the same angular measure, but they do not generally intercept arcs of the same length.

Arc length depends on both

  • the angle, and
  • the radius.

This distinction is one reason angle and distance must be treated as separate quantities.

Choosing the Right Formula

First identify the unit of the angle.

Quantity Angle in degrees Angle in radians
Arc length \(s=\dfrac{\theta}{360^\circ}(2\pi r)\) \(s=r\theta\)
Sector area \(A=\dfrac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2\) \(A=\dfrac12r^2\theta\)

If the angle is in degrees, either use the degree formula directly or convert to radians first. Do not place a degree measure directly into a radian formula.

Common Error: Using Degrees in \(s=r\theta\)

Suppose \(r=5\text{ cm}\) and \(\theta=60^\circ\).

Writing

\[ s=5(60)=300\text{ cm} \]

is incorrect because \(s=r\theta\) requires radians.

Convert first:

\[ 60^\circ=\frac{\pi}{3}. \]

Then

\[ s=5\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right) =\boxed{\frac{5\pi}{3}\text{ cm}}. \]

Common Error: Confusing Arc Measure with Arc Length

Saying that an arc is \(90^\circ\) describes its angular measure. It does not specify a unique length.

A \(90^\circ\) arc in a circle of radius \(2\) has length

\[ \pi, \]

while a \(90^\circ\) arc in a circle of radius \(10\) has length

\[ 5\pi. \]

The angle is the same, but the circles have different scales.

Common Error: Using Circumference When Area Is Needed

Arc length is a fraction of circumference, so it begins with

\[ 2\pi r. \]

Sector area is a fraction of circle area, so it begins with

\[ \pi r^2. \]

The presence or absence of \(r^2\) is a useful warning sign when checking a calculation.

Common Error: Forgetting to Square the Radius

The radian sector-area formula is

\[ \boxed{A=\frac12r^2\theta}, \]

not

\[ \frac12r\theta. \]

The latter expression has units of length, not area.

Common Error: Rounding Too Early

Suppose a calculation contains \(\pi\). Replacing \(\pi\) with a short decimal at the beginning can accumulate avoidable error.

Prefer to simplify symbolically first, preserve the exact form, and compute a decimal only at the final step when one is required.

Common Error: Reporting the Wrong Units

If the radius is measured in meters, then

  • arc length is measured in meters;
  • sector area is measured in square meters.

Units provide a built-in consistency check.

A Reliable Procedure for Arc Length

  1. Identify the radius and central angle.
  2. Determine whether the angle is in degrees or radians.
  3. If using radians, apply \(s=r\theta\).
  4. If using degrees, apply \(s=(\theta/360^\circ)(2\pi r)\).
  5. Simplify exactly before converting to a decimal.
  6. Attach linear units.
  7. Check that the answer is a reasonable fraction of the circumference.

A Reliable Procedure for Sector Area

  1. Identify the radius and central angle.
  2. Determine whether the angle is in degrees or radians.
  3. If using radians, apply \(A=\frac12r^2\theta\).
  4. If using degrees, apply \(A=(\theta/360^\circ)\pi r^2\).
  5. Simplify exactly before approximating.
  6. Attach square units.
  7. Check that the answer is a reasonable fraction of the full circle area.

Example: One Problem, Both Quantities

A circle has radius \(15\text{ cm}\). A central angle measures \(4\pi/5\) radians. Find the intercepted arc length and sector area.

Arc length:

\[ s=r\theta =15\left(\frac{4\pi}{5}\right) =\boxed{12\pi\text{ cm}}. \]

Sector area:

\[ A=\frac12r^2\theta =\frac12(15)^2\left(\frac{4\pi}{5}\right). \] \[ A=\frac{225}{2}\cdot\frac{4\pi}{5} =\boxed{90\pi\text{ cm}^2}. \]

Example: Recovering the Circle from Arc Data

An arc of length \(8\pi\text{ m}\) is one third of a complete circle. Find the radius.

If the arc is one third of the circumference, then

\[ \frac13(2\pi r)=8\pi. \]

Multiply by \(3\):

\[ 2\pi r=24\pi. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{r=12\text{ m}}. \]

Example: Recovering a Sector from Area

A sector occupies \(3/10\) of a circle whose radius is \(20\text{ cm}\). Find the sector area and central angle.

Circle area:

\[ \pi r^2=400\pi. \]

Sector area:

\[ A=\frac{3}{10}(400\pi)=\boxed{120\pi\text{ cm}^2}. \]

The angle is the same fraction of a full turn:

\[ \theta=\frac{3}{10}(360^\circ)=\boxed{108^\circ}. \]

In radians,

\[ \theta=\frac{3}{10}(2\pi)=\boxed{\frac{3\pi}{5}}. \]

Arc Length and Motion Along a Circle

If a point moves around a circle, angular displacement describes how much it turns about the center while arc length describes how far it travels along the circular path.

In radians, the connection is immediate:

\[ \boxed{s=r\theta}. \]

This relationship will become especially important when rotation is treated as motion rather than as a static geometric configuration.

What to Remember

  • Arc length is a linear distance measured along a circle.
  • Sector area is the area enclosed by two radii and their intercepted arc.
  • Angle fraction, circumference fraction, and area fraction are equal.
  • With degrees, \(s=(\theta/360^\circ)(2\pi r)\).
  • With radians, \(s=r\theta\).
  • With degrees, \(A=(\theta/360^\circ)\pi r^2\).
  • With radians, \(A=\frac12r^2\theta\).
  • Sector area can also be written \(A=\frac12rs\).
  • Use radians before applying the compact radian formulas.
  • Keep exact answers in terms of \(\pi\) unless a decimal is requested.
  • Arc length uses linear units; sector area uses square units.

The two most important radian formulas are

\[ \boxed{s=r\theta} \]

and

\[ \boxed{A=\frac12r^2\theta}. \]

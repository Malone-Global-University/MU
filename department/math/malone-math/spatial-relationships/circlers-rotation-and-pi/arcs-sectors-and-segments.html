Arcs, Sectors, and Segments

A circle can be divided in more than one way. Points on the circumference determine arcs; radii and arcs bound sectors; and chords with arcs bound circular segments. Learning to tell these objects apart gives us a precise language for describing portions of a circle before we begin measuring their angles, lengths, and areas.

The Central Idea

In the previous lesson, points, radii, diameters, chords, secants, and tangents described important objects associated with an entire circle. We now use those objects to describe parts of the circle.

Three distinctions organize this lesson:

  • an arc is part of the circumference;
  • a sector is a region bounded by two radii and an arc;
  • a segment is a region bounded by a chord and an arc.

The boundary determines the name of the object.

What Is an Arc?

An arc is a connected portion of a circle's circumference.

If points \(A\) and \(B\) lie on a circle, then the circumference can be followed from \(A\) to \(B\) in two directions. Those two routes determine two arcs with the same endpoints.

The points \(A\) and \(B\) are called the endpoints of the arc.

Two Points Usually Determine Two Arcs

Choose two distinct points \(A\) and \(B\) on a circle. Unless they are the endpoints of a diameter, one route from \(A\) to \(B\) is shorter than the other.

The shorter route is the minor arc. The longer route is the major arc.

Together, the two arcs make the entire circumference.

Minor Arcs

A minor arc is an arc measuring less than \(180^\circ\).

It is normally named with its two endpoints. For example, the minor arc from \(A\) to \(B\) is written

\[ \widehat{AB}. \]

When there is no ambiguity, two letters are enough because the minor arc is the shorter path between its endpoints.

Major Arcs

A major arc is an arc measuring more than \(180^\circ\) but less than \(360^\circ\).

Major arcs are commonly named with three letters. The first and last letters give the endpoints, while the middle letter identifies a point lying on the intended arc.

If \(C\) lies on the long route from \(A\) to \(B\), then that major arc can be written

\[ \widehat{ACB}. \]

The third letter removes the ambiguity between the two possible arcs having endpoints \(A\) and \(B\).

Semicircles

If the endpoints of an arc are the endpoints of a diameter, the arc measures exactly

\[ \boxed{180^\circ}. \]

Such an arc is a semicircle.

A diameter divides a circle into two semicircles. Because both have the same endpoints, a third point is normally included when naming a particular semicircle.

For example, if \(A\) and \(C\) are endpoints of a diameter and \(B\) lies on one of the semicircles, that semicircle may be named

\[ \widehat{ABC}. \]

Minor Arc, Major Arc, and Semicircle

Arc Measure

An arc has an angular measure. In degrees, a complete circle has measure

\[ \boxed{360^\circ}. \]

The measure of a minor arc equals the measure of the central angle that intercepts it. If radii \(OA\) and \(OB\) form a central angle of \(70^\circ\), then

\[ m\widehat{AB}=70^\circ. \]

This relationship will become a major tool in the next lesson on central, inscribed, and tangent angles.

Arc Measure Is Not Arc Length

Arc measure describes how much of a full turn the arc occupies. Arc length describes the physical distance along the curved circumference.

Two arcs in circles of different sizes can both measure \(60^\circ\) while having different lengths.

Angular measure depends on the fraction of the circle. Arc length also depends on the circle's radius. The later lesson Arc Length and Sector Area will quantify that distinction.

The Whole-Circle Relationship

If a minor arc from \(A\) to \(B\) has measure \(m\), then its corresponding major arc has measure

\[ 360^\circ-m. \]

For example, if

\[ m\widehat{AB}=125^\circ, \]

then the major arc from \(A\) to \(B\) measures

\[ 360^\circ-125^\circ=\boxed{235^\circ}. \]

Arc Addition

Adjacent arcs can be combined by adding their measures.

If point \(B\) lies on the arc from \(A\) to \(C\), then

\[ \boxed{m\widehat{AB}+m\widehat{BC}=m\widehat{AC}}. \]

This is the circular counterpart of adding adjacent lengths or adjacent angle measures.

Example: Finding a Missing Arc

Suppose \(B\) lies between \(A\) and \(C\) along an arc, with

\[ m\widehat{AB}=48^\circ \]

and

\[ m\widehat{AC}=117^\circ. \]

Then

\[ 48^\circ+m\widehat{BC}=117^\circ. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{m\widehat{BC}=69^\circ}. \]

Congruent Arcs

Arcs in the same circle, or in congruent circles, are congruent when they have the same measure.

If

\[ m\widehat{AB}=m\widehat{CD}, \]

then

\[ \widehat{AB}\cong\widehat{CD}. \]

Congruent arcs connect naturally to congruent chords: in the same circle or in congruent circles, congruent chords intercept congruent minor arcs, and congruent minor arcs subtend congruent chords.

Arcs and Chords

Every chord cuts the circumference at two endpoints. Those endpoints determine two arcs.

A chord that is not a diameter determines one minor arc and one major arc. A diameter determines two semicircles.

This gives a direct correspondence:

\[ \boxed{\text{chord endpoints} \longleftrightarrow \text{arcs}}. \]

Later circle theorems frequently translate information about chords into information about arcs, and vice versa.

What Is a Sector?

A sector is a region of a disk bounded by two radii and the arc between their endpoints.

If \(OA\) and \(OB\) are radii, then the two radii together with an arc from \(A\) to \(B\) enclose a sector.

A familiar real-world analogy is a slice of pizza cut from the center of a circular pizza.

Minor and Major Sectors

Two radii usually determine two sectors, just as their endpoints determine two arcs.

  • The minor sector is bounded by the two radii and the minor arc.
  • The major sector is bounded by the same radii and the major arc.

If the radii point in opposite directions and form a diameter, the two sectors are semicircular regions of equal size.

A Sector Is a Region, Not an Arc

An arc is one-dimensional in the elementary geometric sense: it is a curved portion of the circumference. A sector is two-dimensional: it includes an interior region of the disk.

The arc is part of the boundary of the sector, not the entire sector itself.

Sector Anatomy

The Central Angle Controls the Sector

The two radii bounding a sector form a central angle. That angle determines what fraction of the full circle belongs to the sector.

For example, a \(90^\circ\) central angle represents

\[ \frac{90^\circ}{360^\circ}=\frac14 \]

of a complete turn, so its minor sector occupies one fourth of the disk.

The later lesson on arc length and sector area will turn this fraction into measurement formulas.

Example: Fraction of a Circle

A sector has central angle \(72^\circ\). What fraction of the full circle does the sector represent?

\[ \frac{72^\circ}{360^\circ}=\frac15. \]

Therefore, the sector represents

\[ \boxed{\frac15} \]

of the disk.

What Is a Circular Segment?

A circular segment is a region of a disk bounded by a chord and an arc having the same endpoints.

This meaning of segment is different from an ordinary line segment. In the phrase circular segment, we mean a two-dimensional region cut off by a chord.

Minor and Major Circular Segments

A chord that is not a diameter divides the disk into two circular segments.

  • The minor segment lies between the chord and its minor arc.
  • The major segment lies between the same chord and its major arc.

The minor segment is the smaller region; the major segment is the rest of the disk.

What Happens When the Chord Is a Diameter?

If the chord is a diameter, the two arcs are semicircles. The diameter divides the disk into two semicircular regions of equal area.

Each region is bounded by a diameter and a semicircular arc. They may be viewed as special circular segments, although elementary problems often simply call them semicircles.

Circular Segment Anatomy

Sector Versus Segment

Sectors and circular segments are both regions inside a disk, but their boundaries are different.

  • A sector uses two radii and an arc.
  • A circular segment uses one chord and an arc.

A radius runs from the center to the circle. A chord joins two points on the circle. That distinction makes the two region types visually and structurally different.

A Sector Can Contain a Circular Segment

Suppose radii \(OA\) and \(OB\) form a minor sector. Draw chord \(\overline{AB}\).

The chord divides that sector into two parts:

  • triangle \(AOB\);
  • the minor circular segment between chord \(AB\) and arc \(AB\).

Conceptually,

\[ \boxed{\text{minor sector}=\text{central triangle}+\text{minor segment}}. \]

This decomposition later provides a method for finding the area of a circular segment.

The Center Matters for Sectors but Not for Arc Endpoints

Two points on a circle are enough to determine a pair of arcs. A sector, however, explicitly uses the center because its straight boundaries are radii.

A circular segment does not require the center as part of its boundary. Its straight boundary is a chord.

This gives another useful classification test: if the center is joined to both arc endpoints, think sector; if the endpoints are joined directly to each other, think segment.

Arc Endpoints Organize the Picture

Start with two points \(A\) and \(B\) on a circle.

  • The curved routes between them are arcs.
  • The straight segment \(\overline{AB}\) is a chord.
  • The chord and an arc bound a circular segment.
  • Radii \(\overline{OA}\) and \(\overline{OB}\), together with an arc, bound a sector.

Much of circle geometry can be understood as different structures built from the same two endpoints.

Example: Classifying a Boundary

A region is enclosed by \(\overline{OA}\), \(\overline{OB}\), and \(\widehat{AB}\), where \(O\) is the center.

Because the two straight boundaries are radii, the region is a

\[ \boxed{\text{sector}}. \]

If the straight boundary were instead \(\overline{AB}\), the region would be a circular segment.

Example: Minor or Major Arc?

Suppose the central angle intercepting arc \(AB\) measures \(138^\circ\).

Since

\[ 138^\circ<180^\circ, \]

the intercepted arc is a minor arc.

Its corresponding major arc measures

\[ 360^\circ-138^\circ=\boxed{222^\circ}. \]

Example: Is It a Semicircle?

Suppose \(A\) and \(B\) are endpoints of a chord through the center \(O\).

A chord through the center is a diameter. Therefore, each arc from \(A\) to \(B\) measures

\[ \boxed{180^\circ}. \]

Each arc is a semicircle.

Example: Using Arc Addition Around a Circle

Suppose three consecutive arcs have measures

\[ 95^\circ,\qquad 110^\circ,\qquad x^\circ. \]

If they complete the circle, their measures total \(360^\circ\):

\[ 95+110+x=360. \]

Therefore,

\[ x=155, \]

so the missing arc measures

\[ \boxed{155^\circ}. \]

Common Error: Calling the Curved Boundary a Sector

The curved piece of the circumference is an arc. A sector is the entire interior region enclosed by two radii and an arc.

Keep boundary and region separate:

\[ \boxed{\text{arc}=\text{curve},\qquad \text{sector}=\text{region}}. \]

Common Error: Confusing a Sector with a Circular Segment

Both are regions inside a disk, but a sector reaches the center while a typical circular segment does not.

Ask what forms the straight boundary:

  • two radii \(\rightarrow\) sector;
  • one chord \(\rightarrow\) circular segment.

Common Error: Naming a Major Arc with Only Two Letters

Two endpoints determine two arcs. A two-letter arc name conventionally denotes the minor arc.

To identify a major arc clearly, include a third point on that arc. Thus, \(\widehat{ACB}\) can distinguish the major arc from the minor arc \(\widehat{AB}\).

Common Error: Treating Degrees as a Length Unit

Degrees measure angle and arc measure; they do not measure linear distance.

An arc measuring \(90^\circ\) occupies one fourth of a circle, but its length depends on the radius of that circle.

The distinction is analogous to saying how large a turn is versus how far a point travels during that turn.

Common Error: Assuming Every Pair of Arc Endpoints Gives a Minor and Major Arc

If the endpoints are opposite ends of a diameter, neither arc is less than \(180^\circ\) and neither is greater than \(180^\circ\).

Both arcs measure exactly \(180^\circ\), so both are semicircles.

A Reliable Procedure for Reading an Arc Diagram

  1. Identify the center of the circle.
  2. Identify the relevant points on the circumference.
  3. Determine the endpoints of the requested arc.
  4. Decide whether the arc is minor, major, or a semicircle.
  5. Use a third letter when needed to specify a major arc or semicircle.
  6. Use central-angle information or arc addition to determine arc measure.
  7. Keep angular measure separate from arc length.

A Reliable Procedure for Classifying Circular Regions

  1. Identify the curved boundary.
  2. Inspect the straight boundary or boundaries.
  3. If two radii meet at the center, classify the region as a sector.
  4. If one chord joins the arc endpoints directly, classify the region as a circular segment.
  5. Determine whether the associated arc is minor, major, or semicircular.
  6. Use the arc or central angle to describe what fraction of the circle is involved.

An arc measures \(84^\circ\). Is it a minor arc, major arc, or semicircle?

Practice 2: Find the Major Arc

The minor arc \(\widehat{AB}\) measures \(146^\circ\). Find the measure of the major arc from \(A\) to \(B\).

Point \(B\) lies on arc \(AC\). If

\[ m\widehat{AB}=37^\circ \]

and

\[ m\widehat{BC}=58^\circ, \]

find \(m\widehat{AC}\).

Practice 4: Missing Arc Around a Circle

Four consecutive arcs measure \(70^\circ\), \(95^\circ\), \(80^\circ\), and \(x^\circ\). Find \(x\).

Practice 5: Semicircle

Points \(A\) and \(C\) are endpoints of a diameter. What is the measure of every arc from \(A\) to \(C\) that forms a semicircle?

Practice 6: Sector or Segment?

A region is bounded by two radii and the minor arc connecting their endpoints. Classify the region.

Practice 7: Sector or Segment?

A region is bounded by chord \(\overline{PQ}\) and minor arc \(\widehat{PQ}\). Classify the region.

Practice 8: Fraction of the Circle

A minor sector has central angle \(45^\circ\). What fraction of the full disk does it occupy?

Practice 9: Congruent Arcs

In the same circle,

\[ m\widehat{AB}=62^\circ \]

and

\[ m\widehat{CD}=62^\circ. \]

What relationship holds between the two arcs?

Practice 10: Identify the Error

A student says, “A \(120^\circ\) arc in a small circle has the same arc length as a \(120^\circ\) arc in a large circle because both arcs have the same measure.” Explain the error.

Practice Answers

Practice 1

Since

\[ 84^\circ<180^\circ, \]

the arc is a

\[ \boxed{\text{minor arc}}. \]

Practice 2

The minor and major arcs together make the full circle:

\[ 360^\circ-146^\circ=\boxed{214^\circ}. \]

Practice 3

Add the adjacent arc measures:

\[ 37^\circ+58^\circ=\boxed{95^\circ}. \]

Practice 4

Arc measures around a complete circle total \(360^\circ\):

\[ 70+95+80+x=360. \] \[ 245+x=360, \] \[ \boxed{x=115}. \]

Practice 5

The endpoints of a diameter divide the circumference into two semicircles, each measuring

\[ \boxed{180^\circ}. \]

Practice 6

Two radii and an arc bound a

\[ \boxed{\text{sector}}. \]

Practice 7

A chord and an arc with the same endpoints bound a

\[ \boxed{\text{circular segment}}. \]

Practice 8

Compare the central angle with the full \(360^\circ\) turn:

\[ \frac{45^\circ}{360^\circ}=\boxed{\frac18}. \]

Practice 9

Arcs in the same circle having equal measure are congruent. Therefore,

\[ \boxed{\widehat{AB}\cong\widehat{CD}}. \]

Practice 10

Equal arc measure means the two arcs occupy the same fraction of their respective circles. It does not guarantee equal length. The larger circle has a larger circumference, so its \(120^\circ\) arc is longer.

What to Remember

  • An arc is a connected portion of a circle's circumference.
  • A minor arc measures less than \(180^\circ\).
  • A major arc measures more than \(180^\circ\) and less than \(360^\circ\).
  • A semicircle measures exactly \(180^\circ\).
  • Minor arcs are commonly named with two letters; major arcs and semicircles often use three.
  • Adjacent arc measures add.
  • Arc measure and arc length are different quantities.
  • A sector is bounded by two radii and an arc.
  • A circular segment is bounded by a chord and an arc.
  • The central angle determines the fraction of the circle represented by its intercepted arc and sector.

The key habit is to identify the boundary. Curved circumference alone gives an arc; two radii plus an arc give a sector; a chord plus an arc gives a circular segment.

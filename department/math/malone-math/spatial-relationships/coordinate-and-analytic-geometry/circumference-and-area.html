Circumference and Area

A circle has two basic measurements that answer two different questions. Circumference measures the distance around the boundary, while area measures the amount of two-dimensional space enclosed by that boundary. Both depend on the radius and on the constant \(\pi\), but they scale in fundamentally different ways.

The Central Idea

For a circle of radius \(r\),

\[ \boxed{C=2\pi r} \]

gives the circumference, and

\[ \boxed{A=\pi r^2} \]

gives the area.

These formulas look similar because both involve \(\pi\), but they measure different geometric quantities and have different units.

Circle and Disk

In precise geometry, the word circle refers to the set of points at a fixed distance from a center. It is the boundary.

The filled-in region consisting of the circle and everything inside it is called a disk.

Circumference measures the length of the circle. Area measures the size of the disk.

Radius and Diameter

The radius \(r\) is the distance from the center to any point on the circle.

The diameter \(d\) passes through the center and joins two points on the circle.

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{d=2r} \]

and

\[ \boxed{r=\frac d2}. \]

Circumference

Circumference is the total distance around a circle.

If the radius is \(r\), then

\[ \boxed{C=2\pi r}. \]

Because \(d=2r\), the same formula can be written

\[ \boxed{C=\pi d}. \]

The two forms are equivalent.

Why Circumference Contains \(\pi\)

Every circle has the same ratio of circumference to diameter:

\[ \frac{C}{d}=\pi. \]

Multiplying both sides by \(d\) gives

\[ C=\pi d. \]

Since \(d=2r\),

\[ C=\pi(2r)=2\pi r. \]

Circumference is therefore built directly from the defining geometric ratio associated with \(\pi\).

Example: Circumference from Radius

Find the circumference of a circle with radius \(5\).

\[ C=2\pi r. \]

Substitute \(r=5\):

\[ C=2\pi(5)=10\pi. \]

Therefore, the exact circumference is

\[ \boxed{10\pi}. \]

Using \(\pi\approx3.14159\),

\[ C\approx31.416. \]

Example: Circumference from Diameter

A circular table has diameter \(1.8\) meters. Its circumference is

\[ C=\pi d. \]

Therefore,

\[ C=1.8\pi\text{ m}. \]

Approximately,

\[ \boxed{C\approx5.65\text{ m}}. \]

Area of a Circle

The area enclosed by a circle of radius \(r\) is

\[ \boxed{A=\pi r^2}. \]

The radius is squared because area is two-dimensional.

If the radius doubles, the area does not merely double. It becomes four times as large.

A Geometric Way to See the Area Formula

Imagine dividing a disk into many narrow sectors and rearranging them in an alternating pattern.

As the sectors become thinner, the rearranged figure approaches a rectangle-like shape.

Its approximate height is

\[ r, \]

and its approximate base is half the circumference:

\[ \frac12(2\pi r)=\pi r. \]

Multiplying base by height gives

\[ A=(\pi r)(r)=\boxed{\pi r^2}. \]

Another View: Area Accumulates with Radius

A disk can be thought of as many very thin concentric rings.

A ring at radius \(x\) has circumference approximately

\[ 2\pi x. \]

Adding the contributions of rings from the center out to radius \(r\) produces the area

\[ \pi r^2. \]

Later calculus gives this accumulation argument a precise integral form.

Example: Area from Radius

Find the area of a circle with radius \(7\).

\[ A=\pi r^2. \]

Substitute \(r=7\):

\[ A=\pi(7^2)=49\pi. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{A=49\pi}. \]

Approximately,

\[ A\approx153.94. \]

Area from Diameter

If the diameter is known, first use

\[ r=\frac d2. \]

Then

\[ A=\pi\left(\frac d2\right)^2. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{A=\frac{\pi d^2}{4}}. \]

This form is useful when a problem gives diameter directly.

Example: Area from Diameter

A circle has diameter \(12\).

The radius is

\[ r=\frac{12}{2}=6. \]

Therefore,

\[ A=\pi(6^2)=\boxed{36\pi}. \]

Circumference Uses Linear Units

Circumference is a length, so its units are linear.

Examples include

  • centimeters;
  • meters;
  • inches;
  • feet;
  • kilometers.

If the radius is measured in meters, the circumference is measured in meters.

Area Uses Square Units

Area is two-dimensional, so its units are squared.

If a radius is measured in centimeters, then

\[ A=\pi r^2 \]

is measured in

\[ \text{cm}^2. \]

Writing square units is not optional; it records the dimension of the quantity being measured.

Exact and Approximate Answers

An expression such as

\[ 18\pi \]

is an exact value.

A decimal such as

\[ 56.55 \]

is an approximation.

Unless a problem specifically requests a decimal, preserving \(\pi\) often gives the more precise result.

Do Not Replace \(\pi\) Too Early

Early rounding can introduce avoidable error.

For example, if

\[ A=27\pi, \]

it is usually better to keep \(27\pi\) through the calculation and convert to a decimal only at the end.

Solving Backward: Radius from Circumference

From

\[ C=2\pi r, \]

divide by \(2\pi\):

\[ \boxed{r=\frac{C}{2\pi}}. \]

This lets us recover the radius when the circumference is known.

Example: Radius from Circumference

Suppose a circle has circumference

\[ C=24\pi. \]

Then

\[ r=\frac{24\pi}{2\pi}=12. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{r=12}. \]

Solving Backward: Radius from Area

From

\[ A=\pi r^2, \]

divide by \(\pi\):

\[ r^2=\frac{A}{\pi}. \]

Since radius is nonnegative,

\[ \boxed{r=\sqrt{\frac{A}{\pi}}}. \]

Example: Radius from Area

Suppose

\[ A=81\pi. \]

Then

\[ r=\sqrt{\frac{81\pi}{\pi}} =\sqrt{81} =\boxed{9}. \]

How Circumference Scales

Suppose the radius is multiplied by a scale factor \(k\).

The new circumference is

\[ C_{\text{new}} = 2\pi(kr) = k(2\pi r). \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{C_{\text{new}}=kC}. \]

Circumference scales linearly with radius.

How Area Scales

If the radius is multiplied by \(k\), then

\[ A_{\text{new}} = \pi(kr)^2. \]

Therefore,

\[ A_{\text{new}} = k^2\pi r^2. \]

So

\[ \boxed{A_{\text{new}}=k^2A}. \]

Area scales with the square of the linear scale factor.

Doubling the Radius

If the radius changes from \(r\) to \(2r\), then the circumference becomes

\[ 2\pi(2r)=2(2\pi r). \]

So the circumference doubles.

The area becomes

\[ \pi(2r)^2=4\pi r^2. \]

So the area quadruples.

This contrast is a central scaling principle.

Tripling the Diameter

Because diameter and radius scale together, tripling the diameter also triples the radius.

Therefore:

  • circumference is multiplied by \(3\);
  • area is multiplied by \(3^2=9\).

Comparing Two Circles

Suppose two circles have radii \(r_1\) and \(r_2\).

Their circumference ratio is

\[ \frac{C_2}{C_1} = \frac{2\pi r_2}{2\pi r_1} = \boxed{\frac{r_2}{r_1}}. \]

Their area ratio is

\[ \frac{A_2}{A_1} = \frac{\pi r_2^2}{\pi r_1^2} = \boxed{\left(\frac{r_2}{r_1}\right)^2}. \]

Semicircle

A semicircle is half of a disk.

Its area is

\[ \boxed{A_{\text{semi}}=\frac12\pi r^2}. \]

Its curved arc has length

\[ \frac12(2\pi r)=\pi r. \]

But the perimeter of the semicircular region also includes the diameter:

\[ \boxed{P_{\text{semi}}=\pi r+2r}. \]

Quarter Circle

A quarter circle contains one fourth of the full disk.

Its area is

\[ \boxed{A_{\text{quarter}}=\frac14\pi r^2}. \]

Its curved arc is one fourth of the circumference:

\[ \frac14(2\pi r)=\frac{\pi r}{2}. \]

The perimeter of the quarter-circle region also includes two radii:

\[ \boxed{P_{\text{quarter}}=\frac{\pi r}{2}+2r}. \]

Annulus

An annulus is the region between two concentric circles.

If the outer radius is \(R\) and the inner radius is \(r\), then its area is the difference between the two disk areas:

\[ \boxed{A_{\text{annulus}}=\pi R^2-\pi r^2}. \]

Factor:

\[ \boxed{A_{\text{annulus}}=\pi(R^2-r^2)}. \]

Example: Annulus Area

Suppose an annulus has outer radius \(10\) and inner radius \(6\).

\[ A=\pi(10^2-6^2). \] \[ A=\pi(100-36). \] \[ \boxed{A=64\pi}. \]

Composite Circular Regions

Many practical figures combine circles with rectangles, triangles, or other circles.

A reliable strategy is to decompose the figure into familiar pieces:

  • add areas of included regions;
  • subtract areas of holes or cutouts;
  • include only boundary pieces that actually belong to the perimeter.

Example: Circular Hole

A square plate has area \(400\text{ cm}^2\). A circular hole of radius \(4\text{ cm}\) is removed.

The hole has area

\[ \pi(4^2)=16\pi. \]

The remaining area is

\[ \boxed{400-16\pi\text{ cm}^2}. \]

Circumference and Perimeter

Circumference is the perimeter of a complete circle.

For partial circular regions, however, perimeter may contain both curved arcs and straight segments.

For example, a semicircle has a curved boundary of length \(\pi r\), but its full perimeter is

\[ \pi r+2r. \]

Area Is Not Circumference Squared

Although area has square units, it is not found by squaring the circumference.

Circumference and area are related through the radius, but they use different formulas:

\[ C=2\pi r, \qquad A=\pi r^2. \]

A Direct Relationship Between Area and Circumference

Since

\[ C=2\pi r, \]

we have

\[ r=\frac{C}{2\pi}. \]

Substitute this into \(A=\pi r^2\):

\[ A = \pi\left(\frac{C}{2\pi}\right)^2. \]

Simplifying gives

\[ \boxed{A=\frac{C^2}{4\pi}}. \]

This formula expresses the area directly in terms of circumference.

Another Direct Relationship

Since \(C=2\pi r\),

\[ \frac12Cr = \frac12(2\pi r)r. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{A=\frac12Cr}. \]

This identity will later resemble the sector-area relationship developed from arc length.

Measurement and Approximation

Real circles are often measured rather than given exactly.

If the measured radius is approximate, then the calculated circumference and area are approximate as well.

Because area depends on \(r^2\), measurement error in the radius can have a stronger effect on area than on circumference.

Example: Small Measurement Change

Compare radii \(10\) and \(10.1\).

The circumference ratio is

\[ \frac{10.1}{10}=1.01, \]

so circumference increases by about \(1\%\).

The area ratio is

\[ \left(\frac{10.1}{10}\right)^2 = 1.0201, \]

so area increases by about \(2.01\%\).

Common Error: Using Diameter as Radius

If a circle has diameter \(14\), then its radius is

\[ 7, \]

not \(14\).

The correct area is

\[ \boxed{49\pi}, \]

not \(196\pi\).

Common Error: Forgetting to Square the Radius

The area formula is

\[ A=\pi r^2, \]

not

\[ A=\pi r. \]

The square reflects the two-dimensional nature of area.

Common Error: Squaring in the Circumference Formula

Circumference is a length, so the radius is not squared:

\[ \boxed{C=2\pi r}. \]

Writing \(2\pi r^2\) mixes the structures of the circumference and area formulas.

Common Error: Wrong Units

A circumference of \(20\pi\) centimeters should be written with linear units:

\[ 20\pi\text{ cm}. \]

An area of \(100\pi\) square centimeters should be written

\[ 100\pi\text{ cm}^2. \]

Common Error: Omitting Straight Sides from Partial-Circle Perimeter

For a semicircle, \(\pi r\) is only the curved arc.

The full perimeter also contains the diameter:

\[ \boxed{\pi r+2r}. \]

A Reliable Procedure

  1. Identify whether the problem asks for circumference, area, or perimeter.
  2. Determine whether the given measurement is a radius or diameter.
  3. Convert diameter to radius when necessary.
  4. Choose \(C=2\pi r\), \(C=\pi d\), or \(A=\pi r^2\).
  5. For partial or composite figures, identify the fraction or decomposition.
  6. Keep \(\pi\) exact until a decimal approximation is required.
  7. Use linear units for circumference and square units for area.
  8. Check whether the result scales reasonably with the radius.

What to Remember

Circumference and area are the two fundamental size measurements of a circle and its interior.

  • \(C=2\pi r=\pi d\).
  • \(A=\pi r^2\).
  • Circumference uses linear units.
  • Area uses square units.
  • Scaling radius by \(k\) scales circumference by \(k\).
  • Scaling radius by \(k\) scales area by \(k^2\).
  • For partial circular regions, distinguish curved arc length from total perimeter.

The key contrast is:

\[ \boxed{\text{circumference measures boundary; area measures enclosed region}}. \]

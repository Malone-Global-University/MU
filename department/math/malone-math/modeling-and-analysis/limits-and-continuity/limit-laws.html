Limit Laws

Limit laws let us build complicated limits from simpler ones. Instead of reanalyzing every sum, product, quotient, power, or composition from the ground up, we can use a small set of reliable rules. These laws explain why direct substitution works for many familiar functions and provide the algebraic framework for evaluating limits efficiently and correctly.

The Central Idea

Suppose

\[ \lim_{x\to a}f(x)=L \]

and

\[ \lim_{x\to a}g(x)=M. \]

Limit laws tell us how the limits of combinations of \(f\) and \(g\) are related to \(L\) and \(M\).

In many cases, the rule is simple: take the limit first, then perform the algebraic operation.

The Constant Law

The limit of a constant is the constant itself.

\[ \boxed{\lim_{x\to a}c=c}. \]

For example,

\[ \lim_{x\to5}7=7. \]

The Identity Law

The limit of the identity function is the target input.

\[ \boxed{\lim_{x\to a}x=a}. \]

For example,

\[ \lim_{x\to3}x=3. \]

The Sum Law

If the individual limits exist, then

\[ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to a}[f(x)+g(x)] = \lim_{x\to a}f(x) + \lim_{x\to a}g(x) }. \]

Using \(L\) and \(M\),

\[ \boxed{\lim_{x\to a}[f(x)+g(x)]=L+M}. \]

Example: Sum Law

Suppose

\[ \lim_{x\to2}f(x)=5 \]

and

\[ \lim_{x\to2}g(x)=-1. \]

Then

\[ \lim_{x\to2}[f(x)+g(x)] = 5+(-1) = \boxed{4}. \]

The Difference Law

Limits distribute over subtraction:

\[ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to a}[f(x)-g(x)] = \lim_{x\to a}f(x) - \lim_{x\to a}g(x) }. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{\lim_{x\to a}[f(x)-g(x)]=L-M}. \]

Example: Difference Law

If

\[ \lim_{x\to4}f(x)=9 \]

and

\[ \lim_{x\to4}g(x)=3, \]

then

\[ \lim_{x\to4}[f(x)-g(x)] = 9-3 = \boxed{6}. \]

The Constant-Multiple Law

A constant factor can be pulled through a limit:

\[ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to a}[c\,f(x)] = c\lim_{x\to a}f(x) }. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{\lim_{x\to a}[c\,f(x)]=cL}. \]

Example: Constant Multiple

If

\[ \lim_{x\to1}f(x)=7, \]

then

\[ \lim_{x\to1}[-3f(x)] = -3(7) = \boxed{-21}. \]

The Product Law

Limits distribute over multiplication:

\[ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to a}[f(x)g(x)] = \left(\lim_{x\to a}f(x)\right) \left(\lim_{x\to a}g(x)\right) }. \]

Thus,

\[ \boxed{\lim_{x\to a}[f(x)g(x)]=LM}. \]

Example: Product Law

Suppose

\[ \lim_{x\to3}f(x)=4 \]

and

\[ \lim_{x\to3}g(x)=-2. \]

Then

\[ \lim_{x\to3}[f(x)g(x)] = 4(-2) = \boxed{-8}. \]

The Quotient Law

Limits distribute over division provided the denominator limit is not zero:

\[ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to a}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{\lim_{x\to a}f(x)} {\lim_{x\to a}g(x)} }. \]

The condition is

\[ \boxed{M\neq0}. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to a}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{L}{M} }. \]

Why the Denominator Condition Matters

Division by zero is not defined.

If

\[ \lim_{x\to a}g(x)=0, \]

the quotient law cannot be applied directly.

The limit might still exist, but additional analysis is required.

Example: Quotient Law

Suppose

\[ \lim_{x\to2}f(x)=10 \]

and

\[ \lim_{x\to2}g(x)=5. \]

Then

\[ \lim_{x\to2}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{10}{5} = \boxed{2}. \]

The Power Law

For a positive integer \(n\),

\[ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to a}[f(x)]^n = \left(\lim_{x\to a}f(x)\right)^n }. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{\lim_{x\to a}[f(x)]^n=L^n}. \]

Example: Power Law

If

\[ \lim_{x\to3}f(x)=2, \]

then

\[ \lim_{x\to3}[f(x)]^4 = 2^4 = \boxed{16}. \]

The Root Law

Under the appropriate real-domain conditions,

\[ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to a}\sqrt[n]{f(x)} = \sqrt[n]{\lim_{x\to a}f(x)} }. \]

Thus,

\[ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to a}\sqrt[n]{f(x)} = \sqrt[n]{L} }. \]

Even Roots Require Nonnegative Inputs

For an even root such as

\[ \sqrt{f(x)}, \]

the real-valued function requires

\[ f(x)\ge0. \]

The domain must therefore be respected when applying the root law.

Example: Root Law

Evaluate

\[ \lim_{x\to4}\sqrt{x+5}. \]

Since

\[ \lim_{x\to4}(x+5)=9, \]

we have

\[ \boxed{\sqrt{9}=3}. \]

Absolute-Value Law

Since the absolute-value function is continuous,

\[ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to a}|f(x)| = \left| \lim_{x\to a}f(x) \right| }. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{\lim_{x\to a}|f(x)|=|L|}. \]

Example: Absolute Value

If

\[ \lim_{x\to2}f(x)=-7, \]

then

\[ \lim_{x\to2}|f(x)| = |-7| = \boxed{7}. \]

Polynomial Limits

Because polynomials are built from constants, powers, sums, and products, the limit laws imply:

\[ \boxed{\lim_{x\to a}P(x)=P(a)}. \]

This is the algebraic reason direct substitution works for every polynomial.

Example: Polynomial by Limit Laws

Evaluate

\[ \lim_{x\to2}(3x^2-4x+5). \]

Apply the laws term by term:

\[ 3(2^2)-4(2)+5. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{9}. \]

Rational Function Limits

If

\[ R(x)=\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} \]

and

\[ Q(a)\neq0, \]

then

\[ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to a}R(x) = \frac{P(a)}{Q(a)} }. \]

Example: Rational Function

Evaluate

\[ \lim_{x\to2} \frac{x^2+3}{x+1}. \]

The denominator approaches

\[ 3\neq0. \]

Therefore,

\[ \frac{2^2+3}{2+1} = \frac{7}{3}. \]

So

\[ \boxed{\frac73}. \]

When Direct Substitution Fails

Limit laws do not eliminate the need for algebraic analysis.

Consider

\[ \lim_{x\to2} \frac{x^2-4}{x-2}. \]

Direct substitution gives

\[ \frac00. \]

The quotient law cannot be applied directly because the denominator limit is zero.

Use Algebra First, Then Apply the Laws

Factor:

\[ \frac{x^2-4}{x-2} = \frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x-2}. \]

For \(x\neq2\),

\[ \frac{x^2-4}{x-2}=x+2. \]

Now the limit laws apply:

\[ \lim_{x\to2}(x+2)=4. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{4}. \]

Limit Laws and One-Sided Limits

The same algebraic laws work for one-sided limits whenever the required one-sided limits exist.

For example, if

\[ \lim_{x\to a^+}f(x)=L \]

and

\[ \lim_{x\to a^+}g(x)=M, \]

then

\[ \lim_{x\to a^+}[f(x)+g(x)] = L+M. \]

Limit Laws at Infinity

The same structural rules also apply to limits as

\[ x\to+\infty \]

or

\[ x\to-\infty, \]

provided the individual limits and operations are well-defined.

Composition Law

Suppose

\[ \lim_{x\to a}g(x)=L \]

and \(f\) is continuous at \(L\).

Then

\[ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to a}f(g(x)) = f\left(\lim_{x\to a}g(x)\right) = f(L) }. \]

Example: Composition

Evaluate

\[ \lim_{x\to2}\sqrt{x^2+5}. \]

First,

\[ \lim_{x\to2}(x^2+5)=9. \]

Since the square-root function is continuous at \(9\),

\[ \boxed{\sqrt{9}=3}. \]

Nested Functions Can Be Handled Layer by Layer

Consider

\[ \lim_{x\to1} \left(2x^2+3\right)^4. \]

First evaluate the inner polynomial:

\[ 2(1)^2+3=5. \]

Then apply the power:

\[ 5^4=625. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{625}. \]

Repeated Use of the Laws

Complicated expressions are usually evaluated by applying several limit laws in sequence.

For example,

\[ \lim_{x\to2} \frac{3x^2+4x-1}{x+5} \]

uses:

  • the power law;
  • the constant-multiple law;
  • the sum and difference laws;
  • the quotient law.

Example: Combined Laws

Evaluate

\[ \lim_{x\to2} \frac{3x^2+4x-1}{x+5}. \]

The numerator limit is

\[ 3(4)+8-1=19. \]

The denominator limit is

\[ 7. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{\frac{19}{7}}. \]

Limit Laws Explain Direct Substitution

Direct substitution is not a separate mysterious trick.

It works because many standard functions are built from operations that preserve limits.

When the expression remains defined at the target, the limit laws often reduce evaluation to ordinary arithmetic.

When the Laws Do Not Apply Automatically

The rules have conditions.

For example:

  • the quotient law requires a nonzero denominator limit;
  • even-root limits require appropriate real-domain conditions;
  • individual component limits must exist when a law assumes them;
  • indeterminate forms require additional analysis.

Limit Laws Do Not Create Missing Limits

Suppose

\[ \lim_{x\to a}f(x) \]

does not exist.

We cannot automatically use a sum, product, or quotient law involving that missing limit.

Sometimes the combined expression may still have a limit, but it must be analyzed directly.

Example: Components May Fail While the Combination Works

Consider

\[ f(x)=\frac{1}{x} \]

and

\[ g(x)=-\frac{1}{x} \]

near \(x=0\).

Neither function has a finite two-sided limit at \(0\).

But

\[ f(x)+g(x)=0 \]

for every \(x\neq0\).

Therefore,

\[ \lim_{x\to0}[f(x)+g(x)]=0. \]

Why This Does Not Violate the Sum Law

The sum law says that if both individual limits exist, then the limit of the sum equals the sum of the limits.

It does not say that the sum cannot have a limit when the individual limits fail to exist.

The law gives a sufficient calculation rule, not a universal reverse test.

Known Limits Can Be Treated as Building Blocks

If

\[ \lim_{x\to a}f(x)=L \]

and

\[ \lim_{x\to a}g(x)=M, \]

then we can immediately evaluate expressions such as

\[ 3f(x)-2g(x), \] \[ [f(x)]^2g(x), \]

or

\[ \frac{f(x)+1}{g(x)} \]

when the required conditions hold.

Example from Known Limits

Suppose

\[ \lim_{x\to5}f(x)=3 \]

and

\[ \lim_{x\to5}g(x)=4. \]

Evaluate

\[ \lim_{x\to5}[2f(x)-3g(x)]. \]

Using the constant-multiple and difference laws:

\[ 2(3)-3(4)=6-12=-6. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{-6}. \]

Example with Product and Power

Using the same limits,

\[ \lim_{x\to5}[f(x)]^2g(x) = (3)^2(4) = \boxed{36}. \]

Example with a Quotient

Again suppose

\[ \lim_{x\to5}f(x)=3 \]

and

\[ \lim_{x\to5}g(x)=4. \]

Then

\[ \lim_{x\to5} \frac{f(x)+1}{g(x)} = \frac{3+1}{4} = \boxed{1}. \]

A Reliable Procedure

  1. Identify the outermost algebraic operation.
  2. Break the expression into simpler component limits.
  3. Apply the appropriate limit laws.
  4. Check any required conditions, especially denominator restrictions.
  5. Use direct substitution when the laws reduce the expression to ordinary arithmetic.
  6. If an indeterminate form appears, simplify before continuing.
  7. Reassemble the component limits carefully.

Example: Full Limit-Law Workflow

Evaluate

\[ \lim_{x\to3} \frac{2x^2-5x+4}{x+2}. \]

The numerator limit is

\[ 2(3^2)-5(3)+4 = 18-15+4 = 7. \]

The denominator limit is

\[ 3+2=5. \]

Since the denominator limit is nonzero,

\[ \boxed{\frac75}. \]

Common Error: Forgetting the Quotient Condition

The quotient law cannot be applied directly when the denominator limit is zero.

In

\[ \lim_{x\to2}\frac{x^2-4}{x-2}, \]

direct quotient-law substitution gives \(0/0\), which is indeterminate.

Simplify first.

Common Error: Applying a Law to a Missing Component Limit

If one of the required individual limits does not exist, the corresponding limit law cannot simply be invoked.

Analyze the combined expression directly instead.

Common Error: Losing Parentheses

When substituting limit values, preserve the original operation structure.

For example,

\[ [f(x)+g(x)]^2 \]

becomes

\[ (L+M)^2, \]

not

\[ L+M^2. \]

Common Error: Ignoring Domain Restrictions

The root law and quotient law depend on the expression being meaningful in the relevant real domain.

Always check whether the original function is defined near the target from the required direction.

Common Error: Treating the Laws as Mere Memorization

The laws are not isolated formulas.

They express a deeper principle: standard algebraic operations preserve limiting behavior under appropriate conditions.

Understanding that structure makes the rules easier to use and remember.

What to Remember

If

\[ \lim_{x\to a}f(x)=L \qquad\text{and}\qquad \lim_{x\to a}g(x)=M, \]

then the main laws include:

\[ \boxed{ \lim(f+g)=L+M } \] \[ \boxed{ \lim(f-g)=L-M } \] \[ \boxed{ \lim(cf)=cL } \] \[ \boxed{ \lim(fg)=LM } \] \[ \boxed{ \lim\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)=\frac{L}{M}, \quad M\neq0 } \] \[ \boxed{ \lim[f(x)]^n=L^n }. \]
  • Limit laws let complicated limits be assembled from simpler ones.
  • They explain why direct substitution works for continuous algebraic expressions.
  • The quotient law requires a nonzero denominator limit.
  • Domain restrictions still matter for roots and other functions.
  • Indeterminate forms require simplification before the laws can finish the problem.

The central strategy is: decompose the expression, apply the appropriate laws, check the conditions, and then recombine the results.

From Measurement to Model Behavior

The section begins by grounding mathematical symbols in measurable quantities and units, then develops dimensional consistency and derived units as tools for checking equations. Parameters and coefficients describe how particular models are shaped, while characteristic scales provide natural reference magnitudes for comparing quantities and organizing calculations.

Nondimensionalization, scaling laws, orders of magnitude, and similarity reveal structure that can remain hidden in raw numerical values. Precision, tolerance, and error establish how closely numerical information is known, while local and global parameter effects show how changes in assumptions or fitted values propagate through a model. Together, these ideas provide the scale-aware foundation needed for later approximation, simulation, optimization, and applied analysis.

Optimization

Optimization studies how to choose the best feasible decision according to one or more objectives. This sequence develops the subject from objective functions and feasible regions through calculus-based methods, linear and convex optimization, numerical search, discrete decisions, competing objectives, and robust decision-making under uncertainty.

Optimization Lessons

Work through the lessons in sequence or open the topic that matches the problem you are studying.

  1. Objective Functions — Define what an optimization problem is trying to maximize or minimize.
  2. Constraints and Feasible Regions — Identify allowable decisions and the region in which an optimum may occur.
  3. Local and Global Extrema — Distinguish nearby optima from the best values over an entire feasible set.
  4. Unconstrained Optimization — Use derivatives, gradients, and curvature when no separate constraints are imposed.
  5. Constrained Optimization — Optimize over feasible regions defined by equality and inequality restrictions.
  6. Lagrange Multipliers — Solve smooth equality-constrained problems using gradient alignment.
  7. Linear Programming — Optimize linear objectives over feasible regions defined by linear constraints.
  8. Convexity — Recognize geometric structure that turns local optimality into global guarantees.
  9. Gradient-Based Methods — Use iterative derivative-based methods to search numerically for optima.
  10. Discrete and Continuous Optimization — Compare continuous decisions with integer, binary, and mixed-integer models.
  11. Multiobjective Tradeoffs — Analyze competing objectives, Pareto efficiency, and compromise solutions.
  12. Sensitivity and Robustness — Test how optimal decisions respond to parameter changes, uncertainty, and model error.

The Optimization Framework

Across the sequence, optimization can be organized around four questions:

  1. What are the decision variables?
  2. What objective should be made best?
  3. What constraints define the feasible choices?
  4. How can the resulting solution be verified, compared, and tested for robustness?

In compact form,

\[ \boxed{ \text{model} \;\rightarrow\; \text{optimize} \;\rightarrow\; \text{verify} \;\rightarrow\; \text{test sensitivity} }. \]

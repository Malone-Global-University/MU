Dynamical Systems, Stability, and Chaos

Dynamical systems study how states evolve through time. This section develops the geometric and analytical tools used to describe trajectories, equilibria, stability, phase portraits, bifurcations, periodic motion, chaotic behavior, attractors, and long-term qualitative structure in both discrete and continuous systems.

Explore the Lessons

Begin with the basic distinction between discrete and continuous evolution, then move through state-space geometry, stability, nonlinear transitions, recurring motion, chaos, and asymptotic analysis.

  • Discrete and Continuous Dynamical Systems — Introduces evolution rules, iteration, differential equations, initial conditions, trajectories, and the basic language of dynamical systems.
  • State Spaces and Trajectories — Organizes possible system states geometrically and shows how discrete or continuous evolution traces paths through state space.
  • Fixed Points and Equilibria — Develops stationary states, equilibrium conditions, nullclines, and the algebraic structure underlying long-term behavior.
  • Stability and Instability — Examines how systems respond to small disturbances and distinguishes stable, attracting, unstable, and saddle-type behavior.
  • Phase Portraits — Uses vector fields, trajectories, nullclines, equilibria, and separatrices to visualize the geometry of dynamical systems.
  • Linearization near Equilibria — Uses derivatives, Jacobian matrices, eigenvalues, and eigenvectors to approximate nonlinear systems near equilibria.
  • Bifurcations — Studies qualitative changes caused by parameter variation, including saddle-node, transcritical, pitchfork, Hopf, and period-doubling behavior.
  • Periodic Orbits — Develops recurring trajectories, periods, cycles, limit cycles, return maps, and the stability of repeating motion.
  • Sensitive Dependence and Chaotic Behavior — Explains deterministic chaos, nearby-trajectory divergence, Lyapunov exponents, recurrence, and limits on long-term prediction.
  • Attractors — Develops equilibrium, periodic, and chaotic attractors together with invariant sets, basins of attraction, and multistability.
  • Long-Term Qualitative Analysis — Brings the section together through boundedness, invariant regions, asymptotic behavior, attractors, bifurcations, and practical qualitative reasoning.

Core Questions

Throughout this section, the same central questions appear repeatedly:

  • What information is needed to describe the current state?
  • How does the state evolve through time?
  • Where are the equilibria?
  • Which states or trajectories are stable?
  • What does the phase portrait reveal?
  • How does nonlinear behavior change as parameters vary?
  • Can the system oscillate or repeat?
  • Can nearby trajectories separate rapidly?
  • What attractors organize the long-term motion?
  • What can be concluded without solving the system exactly?

The Main Progression

The lessons follow a natural progression:

\[ \boxed{ \text{evolution} \rightarrow \text{state space} \rightarrow \text{equilibria} \rightarrow \text{stability} \rightarrow \text{phase geometry} \rightarrow \text{nonlinear change} \rightarrow \text{long-term behavior} } \]

Together, these ideas form a framework for studying systems whose most important features are patterns of change rather than isolated numerical answers.

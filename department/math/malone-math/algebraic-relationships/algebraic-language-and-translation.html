Algebraic Language and Translation

Algebra gives us a precise language for describing quantities, operations, conditions, patterns, and relationships.

From Situations to Mathematical Relationships

Imagine that a taxi charges a fixed fee of \(\$4\) and an additional \(\$3\) for every mile traveled. We could describe this arrangement in words, place the values in a table, draw a graph, write an algorithm, or represent the relationship with an equation:

\[ C=4+3m. \]

Here, \(m\) represents the number of miles traveled and \(C\) represents the total cost. The equation is compact, but it preserves the important structure of the situation:

  • \(4\) is the fixed starting cost,
  • \(3\) is the cost per mile,
  • \(m\) is the number of miles,
  • \(3m\) is the variable mileage charge, and
  • \(C\) is the resulting total cost.

This movement between words, symbols, tables, diagrams, graphs, and algorithms is called mathematical translation.

\[ \boxed{\text{translation changes the representation, not the underlying relationship}} \]

What Is Algebraic Language?

Algebraic language is the system of words, symbols, conventions, and representations used to describe mathematical relationships.

Arithmetic usually asks us to operate on known numbers. Algebra allows one or more quantities to be unknown, variable, dependent on other quantities, or described generally.

Compare the following statements:

Statement Interpretation
\(7+5=12\)A particular numerical calculation
\(x+5=12\)An equation containing an unknown quantity
\(y=x+5\)A relationship between two variable quantities
\(a+b=b+a\)A general identity expressing the commutative law of addition

The symbols may look similar, but they perform different mathematical jobs. Reading algebra well means recognizing those jobs before beginning a calculation.

The Objects of Algebraic Language

Algebraic statements are built from several recurring objects.

ObjectDescriptionExample
NumberA specific value\(8\), \(-3\), \(\frac12\), \(\pi\)
VariableA symbol representing a quantity that may vary or be unknown\(x\), \(t\), \(C\)
ConstantA value held fixed within a relationship\(5\) in \(y=3x+5\)
ParameterA quantity that controls a family of relationships\(m\) and \(b\) in \(y=mx+b\)
CoefficientA factor multiplying another quantity\(7\) in \(7x\)
TermA component separated by addition or subtraction\(4x^2\), \(-3x\), and \(8\) in \(4x^2-3x+8\)
ExpressionA mathematical combination without a comparison relation\(3x+7\)
EquationA statement that two expressions are equal\(3x+7=22\)
InequalityA statement comparing quantities by order\(3x+7<22\)
FormulaA rule connecting quantities in a defined context\(A=\pi r^2\)
IdentityAn equality true for every permitted value\((a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2\)

Roles Played by Quantities

A symbol does not have one permanent role. Its role depends on the relationship and the question being asked.

Consider the distance relationship

\[ d=rt. \]

If a vehicle travels at \(60\) miles per hour for \(t\) hours, then

\[ d=60t. \]

In this version:

  • \(d\) is the dependent quantity,
  • \(t\) is the independent quantity, and
  • \(60\) is a constant rate.

If the distance and time are known but the rate is unknown, we may instead rearrange the relationship:

\[ r=\frac{d}{t}. \]

Now \(r\) is the unknown. The underlying relationship has not changed; only our focus and representation have changed.

Reading Algebraic Structure

Before evaluating or solving an expression, identify its structure.

Consider

\[ 3(x+4)^2-7. \]

The operations occur in layers:

  1. add \(4\) to \(x\),
  2. square the result,
  3. multiply by \(3\), and
  4. subtract \(7\).

Parentheses show that \(x+4\) is being treated as a single object. The square applies to the entire grouped expression, not only to \(4\).

We can display the structure as a composition of steps:

\[ x \longmapsto x+4 \longmapsto (x+4)^2 \longmapsto 3(x+4)^2 \longmapsto 3(x+4)^2-7. \]

Reading from the outside inward can also help. The expression is a difference whose first term is three times a square.

Translating Words into Symbols

A reliable translation begins by identifying the quantities, their roles, and the relationship between them.

Common Operation Phrases

WordsPossible Symbolic Form
The sum of \(x\) and \(5\)\(x+5\)
Five more than \(x\)\(x+5\)
Five less than \(x\)\(x-5\)
Five subtracted from \(x\)\(x-5\)
The product of \(4\) and \(x\)\(4x\)
The quotient of \(x\) and \(4\)\(\frac{x}{4}\)
The square of the sum of \(x\) and \(4\)\((x+4)^2\)
The sum of the square of \(x\) and \(4\)\(x^2+4\)

Order Matters

The phrase “seven less than a number” means

\[ x-7, \]

not \(7-x\). The phrase “seven subtracted from a number” also means \(x-7\).

Subtraction and division are not commutative, so reversing their order changes the relationship:

\[ x-7\ne 7-x \]

and, in general,

\[ \frac{x}{7}\ne\frac{7}{x}. \]

Use Grouping to Preserve Meaning

“Three times the sum of a number and five” means

\[ 3(x+5). \]

Without the parentheses, \(3x+5\) means “five more than three times a number.” These are different expressions.

Translating Symbols into Words

Translation should also work in the opposite direction. If we cannot explain an expression in words, we may not yet understand its structure.

Symbolic FormPossible Verbal Translation
\(2x+9\)Nine more than twice \(x\)
\(2(x+9)\)Twice the sum of \(x\) and nine
\(\frac{x-3}{5}\)One-fifth of the difference between \(x\) and three
\(a^2+b^2\)The sum of the squares of \(a\) and \(b\)
\((a+b)^2\)The square of the sum of \(a\) and \(b\)
\(y\le 4x-1\)\(y\) is less than or equal to four times \(x\), minus one

There may be several correct verbal translations. A good translation preserves the mathematical structure without introducing ambiguity.

Moving Between Representations

A relationship can often be represented in several equivalent ways.

Suppose a tank initially contains \(20\) liters of water and fills at \(5\) liters per minute.

Words

The volume begins at \(20\) liters and increases by \(5\) liters each minute.

Formula

\[ V=20+5t. \]

Table

Time \(t\) in minutesVolume \(V\) in liters
\(0\)\(20\)
\(1\)\(25\)
\(2\)\(30\)
\(3\)\(35\)

Algorithm

  1. Begin with \(20\).
  2. Multiply the number of elapsed minutes by \(5\).
  3. Add the result to \(20\).

Input-Output Relationship

\[ t\longmapsto 20+5t. \]

Each representation emphasizes something different:

  • words provide context,
  • the formula provides a compact general rule,
  • the table displays selected values, and
  • the algorithm explains how to calculate an output.

Conditions, Restrictions, and Units

A symbolic expression may be incomplete if we omit the conditions under which it is intended to operate.

Units

In \(d=60t\), the number \(60\) is not merely a number if the context is motion. It may represent \(60\) miles per hour. If \(t\) is measured in hours, then \(d\) is measured in miles:

\[ \left(\frac{\text{miles}}{\text{hour}}\right)(\text{hours})=\text{miles}. \]

Domain Restrictions

The expression \(\frac{5}{x-2}\) is undefined when \(x=2\). Its restriction is

\[ x\ne 2. \]

Contextual Conditions

If \(n\) represents the number of employees assigned to a task, then the context may require

\[ n\in\{0,1,2,3,\ldots\}. \]

A value such as \(n=4.7\) may be algebraically possible in an intermediate calculation but impossible as a final answer in that context.

Assumptions

A model may assume that a rate remains constant, measurements are exact, or outside influences are negligible. These assumptions are part of the model and should be stated when they affect interpretation.

Worked Example: Choosing a Relationship

A gym charges a one-time enrollment fee of \(\$35\) and a monthly fee of \(\$24\). Represent the total cost after \(m\) months and determine the cost after six months.

1. Identify the Quantities

  • \(m\): number of months
  • \(C\): total cost in dollars
  • \(35\): fixed enrollment fee
  • \(24\): monthly rate

2. Translate the Relationship

The total cost is the fixed fee plus the monthly fee multiplied by the number of months:

\[ C=35+24m. \]

3. Evaluate the Relationship

For six months, substitute \(m=6\):

\[ C=35+24(6), \] \[ C=35+144, \] \[ \boxed{C=179}. \]

4. Interpret the Result

The total cost after six months is \(\$179\).

5. Verify

Six monthly payments cost \(6(\$24)=\$144\). Adding the enrollment fee gives

\[ \$144+\$35=\$179. \]

The independent verbal calculation agrees with the formula.

Common Translation Errors

Reversing Subtraction

“Eight less than \(x\)” means \(x-8\), not \(8-x\).

Losing Grouping

“Five times the difference between \(x\) and two” means \(5(x-2)\), not \(5x-2\).

Confusing a Term with a Factor

In \(3x+7\), the expression contains two terms. The \(3\) is a factor of the term \(3x\).

Treating the Equal Sign as an Instruction

The equal sign states that two expressions represent the same value. It does not simply mean “calculate what comes next.”

Ignoring Units

A rate of \(40\) miles per hour cannot be added directly to a time of \(3\) hours. Quantities must be connected through a dimensionally meaningful relationship.

Using a Familiar Formula Without Checking the Situation

A formula is useful only when its quantities and assumptions match the problem being modeled.

Practice

  1. Translate “six more than twice a number” into an algebraic expression.
  2. Translate “six times the sum of a number and two” into an algebraic expression.
  3. Explain the difference between \(4x+3\) and \(4(x+3)\).
  4. Write \(\frac{x+7}{3}\) in clear natural language.
  5. A service charges a fixed fee of \(\$12\) plus \(\$8\) per hour. Write a formula for the total cost after \(h\) hours.
  6. Identify the coefficient, variables, and constant term in \(P=6x+4y+10\).
  7. State the restriction on \(x\) in \(\frac{9}{x+4}\).
  8. A container begins with \(50\) liters and loses \(3\) liters per minute. Write a formula and state a reasonable contextual domain.
  9. Represent \(y=2x-1\) with a table containing at least four input-output pairs.
  10. Explain why translation should preserve relationships even when the representation changes.
Selected Answers
  1. \(2x+6\)
  2. \(6(x+2)\)
  3. \(4x+3\) adds \(3\) after multiplying \(x\) by \(4\); \(4(x+3)\) multiplies the entire sum by \(4\).
  4. One-third of the sum of \(x\) and seven.
  5. \(C=12+8h\)
  6. The coefficients are \(6\) and \(4\), the variables are \(x\) and \(y\), and the constant term is \(10\).
  7. \(x\ne -4\)
  8. \(V=50-3t\), with \(0\le t\le \frac{50}{3}\) if the process stops when the container is empty.

Summary

  • Algebraic language describes quantities, operations, conditions, and relationships.
  • Variables, constants, parameters, coefficients, terms, expressions, equations, inequalities, formulas, and identities perform different roles.
  • Translation can move in both directions between natural language and symbolic language.
  • Grouping and order must be preserved during translation.
  • Words, formulas, tables, diagrams, graphs, and algorithms can represent the same underlying relationship.
  • Units, restrictions, conditions, and assumptions are part of a complete mathematical model.
  • A successful translation changes the form of a relationship without changing its meaning.
\[ \boxed{\text{identify the quantities}\rightarrow\text{recognize the relationship}\rightarrow\text{choose a representation}} \]

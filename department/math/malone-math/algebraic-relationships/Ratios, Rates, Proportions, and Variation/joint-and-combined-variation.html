Joint and Combined Variation

Variation does not have to involve only two quantities. A quantity may depend directly on several variables at once, or directly on some variables and inversely on others.

The Central Idea

Suppose the amount of paint needed for a rectangular wall depends on both its width \(w\) and height \(h\).

If coverage conditions remain constant, the amount of paint \(P\) is proportional to the wall's area:

\[ P\propto wh. \]

Introducing a constant \(k\),

\[ P=kwh. \]

This is an example of joint variation: one quantity varies directly with the product of two or more other quantities.

Now suppose another quantity varies directly with \(x\) and inversely with \(z\):

\[ y\propto\frac{x}{z}. \]

Then

\[ y=\frac{kx}{z}. \]

This is a combined variation.

Review: Direct and Inverse Variation

Direct variation has the form

\[ y=kx. \]

Inverse variation has the form

\[ y=\frac{k}{x}. \]

Joint and combined variation extend these ideas to several variables.

What Is Joint Variation?

A quantity \(y\) varies jointly with \(x\) and \(z\) when

\[ y=kxz. \]

The quantity \(y\) is directly proportional to the product

\[ xz. \]

If either \(x\) or \(z\) increases while the other remains fixed, \(y\) changes by the same direct scale factor.

Joint Variation Notation

We may write

\[ y\propto xz \]

to mean that \(y\) varies jointly with \(x\) and \(z\).

Introducing the constant of variation gives

\[ \boxed{y=kxz}. \]

Joint Variation with Three Variables

A quantity can vary jointly with more than two variables.

For example,

\[ Q\propto abc \]

means

\[ Q=kabc. \]

Each variable contributes multiplicatively to the output.

Scaling in Joint Variation

Suppose

\[ y=kxz. \]

If \(x\) doubles while \(z\) stays fixed, then \(y\) doubles.

If \(z\) triples while \(x\) stays fixed, then \(y\) triples.

If \(x\) doubles and \(z\) triples at the same time, then \(y\) is multiplied by

\[ 2\cdot3=6. \]

Joint Scaling Example

Suppose

\[ y=5xz. \]

If

\[ x=2,\qquad z=3, \]

then

\[ y=5(2)(3)=30. \]

If \(x\) doubles to \(4\) and \(z\) triples to \(9\), then

\[ y=5(4)(9)=180. \]

The output increased by a factor of

\[ \frac{180}{30}=6. \]

Finding the Constant in Joint Variation

Suppose \(y\) varies jointly with \(x\) and \(z\), and

\[ y=60 \]

when

\[ x=4,\qquad z=3. \]

Write

\[ y=kxz. \]

Substitute:

\[ 60=k(4)(3). \]

So

\[ 60=12k, \] \[ \boxed{k=5}. \]

The model is

\[ \boxed{y=5xz}. \]

Using a Joint-Variation Model

Using

\[ y=5xz, \]

find \(y\) when

\[ x=7,\qquad z=2. \]

Then

\[ y=5(7)(2)=70. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{y=70}. \]

Joint Variation and Geometry

The area of a rectangle is

\[ A=lw. \]

Area varies jointly with length and width, with

\[ k=1. \]

Thus,

\[ A\propto lw. \]

Volume as Joint Variation

The volume of a rectangular prism is

\[ V=lwh. \]

Volume varies jointly with length, width, and height.

Again, the constant is

\[ k=1. \]

Physical Quantities Can Vary Jointly

Suppose mass is determined by density and volume:

\[ m=\rho V. \]

Mass varies jointly with density and volume when both are treated as variables.

In this model,

\[ k=1. \]

What Is Combined Variation?

Combined variation mixes direct and inverse relationships.

For example, if \(y\) varies directly with \(x\) and inversely with \(z\), then

\[ y\propto\frac{x}{z}. \]

Introducing a constant gives

\[ \boxed{y=\frac{kx}{z}}. \]

Direct in One Variable, Inverse in Another

Suppose

\[ y=\frac{kx}{z}. \]

Holding \(z\) fixed:

  • doubling \(x\) doubles \(y\);
  • tripling \(x\) triples \(y\).

Holding \(x\) fixed:

  • doubling \(z\) halves \(y\);
  • tripling \(z\) divides \(y\) by \(3\).

Combined Scaling

Suppose

\[ y=\frac{kx}{z}. \]

If \(x\) is multiplied by \(a\) and \(z\) is multiplied by \(b\), then \(y\) is multiplied by

\[ \frac{a}{b}. \]

The effects combine multiplicatively.

Combined Scaling Example

Suppose

\[ y=\frac{6x}{z}. \]

Initially, let

\[ x=4,\qquad z=3. \]

Then

\[ y=\frac{6(4)}{3}=8. \]

If \(x\) doubles and \(z\) triples, then \(y\) should be multiplied by

\[ \frac{2}{3}. \]

So the new value is

\[ 8\cdot\frac23=\frac{16}{3}. \]

Finding the Constant in Combined Variation

Suppose \(y\) varies directly with \(x\) and inversely with \(z\), and

\[ y=10 \]

when

\[ x=6,\qquad z=3. \]

Write

\[ y=\frac{kx}{z}. \]

Substitute:

\[ 10=\frac{6k}{3}. \]

So

\[ 10=2k, \] \[ \boxed{k=5}. \]

The model is

\[ \boxed{y=\frac{5x}{z}}. \]

Use the Combined Model

Using

\[ y=\frac{5x}{z}, \]

find \(y\) when

\[ x=14,\qquad z=7. \]

Then

\[ y=\frac{5(14)}{7}=10. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{y=10}. \]

Combined Variation with Several Direct Factors

If \(y\) varies jointly with \(x\) and \(z\), and inversely with \(w\), then

\[ y\propto\frac{xz}{w}. \]

So

\[ \boxed{y=\frac{kxz}{w}}. \]

Several Inverse Factors

If \(y\) varies directly with \(x\) and inversely with both \(z\) and \(w\), then

\[ y\propto\frac{x}{zw}. \]

Thus,

\[ \boxed{y=\frac{kx}{zw}}. \]

Variation with Powers

Variation can involve powers as well as first powers.

If \(y\) varies directly with the square of \(x\), then

\[ y\propto x^2, \]

so

\[ y=kx^2. \]

If \(y\) varies inversely with the square of \(x\), then

\[ y\propto\frac{1}{x^2}, \]

so

\[ y=\frac{k}{x^2}. \]

Combined Variation with Powers

Suppose \(y\) varies jointly with \(x^2\) and \(z\), and inversely with \(w^3\).

Then

\[ y\propto\frac{x^2z}{w^3}. \]

So

\[ \boxed{y=\frac{kx^2z}{w^3}}. \]

Scaling with Powers

Suppose

\[ y=\frac{kx^2z}{w^3}. \]

If \(x\) doubles, \(y\) is multiplied by

\[ 2^2=4. \]

If \(z\) triples, \(y\) is multiplied by \(3\).

If \(w\) doubles, \(y\) is divided by

\[ 2^3=8. \]

If all three changes happen together, the net factor is

\[ 4\cdot3\cdot\frac18 = \frac32. \]

Reading Variation Language Carefully

The phrase

“\(y\) varies jointly with \(x\) and \(z\)”

means

\[ y=kxz. \]

The phrase

“\(y\) varies directly with \(x\) and inversely with \(z\)”

means

\[ y=\frac{kx}{z}. \]

The language tells us where each variable belongs in the model.

“Jointly” Means Product

If a quantity varies jointly with two variables, multiply those variables together.

For example:

\[ A\text{ varies jointly with }b\text{ and }h \]

becomes

\[ A=kbh. \]

“Inversely” Means Denominator

If a quantity varies inversely with \(x\), place \(x\) in the denominator:

\[ y=\frac{k}{x}. \]

If it varies inversely with \(x^2\), write

\[ y=\frac{k}{x^2}. \]

“Directly as the Square” Means a Power

If \(y\) varies directly as the square of \(x\), then

\[ y=kx^2. \]

Doubling \(x\) multiplies \(y\) by \(4\), not \(2\).

Units of the Constant

In a multivariable variation model, \(k\) may carry units needed to make the equation dimensionally consistent.

For

\[ y=\frac{kxz}{w}, \]

the units of \(k\) must satisfy

\[ [k] = \frac{[y][w]}{[x][z]}. \]

This is a powerful way to check a model.

Dimensional Check Example

Suppose

\[ d=vt. \]

Here \(d\) is distance, \(v\) is distance per time, and \(t\) is time.

The units give

\[ \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}} \cdot \text{time} = \text{distance}. \]

The dimensions are consistent.

Joint Variation in Geometry

The area of a triangle is

\[ A=\frac12 bh. \]

Area varies jointly with base \(b\) and height \(h\), with constant

\[ k=\frac12. \]

Thus,

\[ A\propto bh. \]

Joint Variation in Kinetic Energy

Kinetic energy is

\[ E=\frac12 mv^2. \]

So \(E\) varies jointly with mass \(m\) and the square of speed \(v\).

The constant of variation is

\[ k=\frac12. \]

Combined Variation in Gravitation

Newton's gravitational force law has the form

\[ F=G\frac{m_1m_2}{r^2}. \]

The force varies jointly with the two masses and inversely with the square of the distance between them.

The constant is the gravitational constant \(G\).

Combined Variation in Electrostatics

Coulomb's law has a similar structure:

\[ F=k_e\frac{|q_1q_2|}{r^2}. \]

The force magnitude varies jointly with the charge magnitudes and inversely with the square of their separation.

Different physical settings can share the same mathematical variation structure.

Work and Rate as Combined Structure

For a fixed amount of work \(W\),

\[ W=rt. \]

If \(W\) is fixed, then

\[ t=\frac{W}{r}. \]

Time varies inversely with rate.

If the amount of work is also allowed to vary, then

\[ t=\frac{W}{r} \]

shows that time varies directly with total work and inversely with rate.

Travel Time as Combined Variation

For travel,

\[ d=vt. \]

Solving for time:

\[ t=\frac{d}{v}. \]

Travel time varies directly with distance and inversely with speed.

Travel Example

Suppose

\[ t=\frac{d}{v}. \]

If distance doubles and speed triples, then time is multiplied by

\[ \frac{2}{3}. \]

A trip twice as long at three times the speed takes only two-thirds as much time.

Density as Combined Variation

Density is

\[ \rho=\frac{m}{V}. \]

If both mass and volume vary, density varies directly with mass and inversely with volume.

Pressure-Type Ratios

Any model of the form

\[ P=\frac{F}{A} \]

shows a quantity \(P\) varying directly with force \(F\) and inversely with area \(A\), provided the model assumptions hold.

Increasing force raises \(P\); increasing area lowers it.

Finding a Constant from Data

Suppose

\[ y=\frac{kxz}{w} \]

and the data are

\[ y=12,\quad x=2,\quad z=9,\quad w=3. \]

Substitute:

\[ 12=\frac{k(2)(9)}{3}. \]

Simplify:

\[ 12=6k. \]

So

\[ \boxed{k=2}. \]

Use the Model After Finding \(k\)

The model becomes

\[ y=\frac{2xz}{w}. \]

If

\[ x=5,\qquad z=8,\qquad w=4, \]

then

\[ y=\frac{2(5)(8)}{4}=20. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{y=20}. \]

Finding an Unknown Input

Suppose

\[ y=\frac{6xz}{w} \]

and

\[ y=24,\qquad x=2,\qquad w=3. \]

Find \(z\).

Substitute:

\[ 24=\frac{6(2)z}{3}. \]

Simplify:

\[ 24=4z. \]

So

\[ \boxed{z=6}. \]

Comparing Two Situations Without Finding \(k\)

Suppose

\[ y=kxz. \]

For two situations,

\[ y_1=kx_1z_1 \]

and

\[ y_2=kx_2z_2. \]

Divide:

\[ \frac{y_2}{y_1} = \frac{x_2z_2}{x_1z_1}. \]

The constant \(k\) cancels.

This lets us compare outputs directly from scale changes.

Ratio Comparison in Combined Variation

Suppose

\[ y=\frac{kxz}{w}. \]

Then for two states,

\[ \frac{y_2}{y_1} = \frac{x_2z_2w_1}{x_1z_1w_2}. \]

This is useful when the constant \(k\) is unknown but the relative change is all we need.

Relative-Change Example

Suppose

\[ y=\frac{kxz}{w}. \]

If \(x\) doubles, \(z\) is halved, and \(w\) is divided by \(4\), then the net multiplier on \(y\) is

\[ 2\cdot\frac12\cdot4=4. \]

So \(y\) becomes four times as large.

Power-Law Comparison

Suppose

\[ y=\frac{kx^2}{z^3}. \]

If \(x\) doubles and \(z\) doubles, then

\[ y \]

is multiplied by

\[ \frac{2^2}{2^3} = \frac12. \]

The output is cut in half.

Do Not Add the Variable Effects

In variation models, scaling effects multiply.

If

\[ y=kxz, \]

and \(x\) doubles while \(z\) triples, the new \(y\) is multiplied by

\[ 2\cdot3=6, \]

not by

\[ 2+3=5. \]

Do Not Confuse Joint Variation with Addition

Joint variation

\[ y=kxz \]

is fundamentally different from

\[ y=k(x+z). \]

The first depends on a product.

The second depends on a sum.

Do Not Confuse Combined Variation with a Sum of Fractions

The statement

“\(y\) varies directly with \(x\) and inversely with \(z\)”

means

\[ y=\frac{kx}{z}, \]

not

\[ y=kx+\frac{k}{z}. \]

The direct and inverse factors combine multiplicatively.

Domain Restrictions

Any variable appearing in a denominator must be nonzero.

For

\[ y=\frac{kxz}{w}, \]

we require

\[ w\ne0. \]

If the context uses positive physical quantities, we may also require

\[ x>0,\qquad z>0,\qquad w>0. \]

Context Can Restrict the Algebraic Model

The formula

\[ t=\frac{W}{r} \]

is algebraically meaningful for many nonzero \(r\).

But if \(r\) is a positive work rate and \(t\) is elapsed time, the practical domain may require

\[ W\ge0,\qquad r>0,\qquad t\ge0. \]

Mathematical and contextual domains should both be respected.

Variation Models Depend on Assumptions

A formula may describe a system only under specific conditions.

For example:

  • travel time \(t=d/v\) assumes an appropriate average speed model;
  • work-time models may assume constant productivity;
  • physical laws may assume fixed temperature, negligible resistance, or idealized conditions;
  • geometric formulas assume the stated shape.

Variation describes structure within the model's assumptions.

Worked Example: Joint Variation

Suppose \(y\) varies jointly with \(x\) and \(z\), and

\[ y=48 \]

when

\[ x=4,\qquad z=6. \]

Write

\[ y=kxz. \]

Then

\[ 48=k(4)(6)=24k. \]

So

\[ k=2. \]

The model is

\[ \boxed{y=2xz}. \]

Worked Example: Use Joint Variation

Using

\[ y=2xz, \]

find \(y\) when

\[ x=7,\qquad z=5. \]

Then

\[ y=2(7)(5)=70. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{y=70}. \]

Worked Example: Combined Variation

Suppose \(y\) varies directly with \(x\) and inversely with \(z\), and

\[ y=12 \]

when

\[ x=8,\qquad z=2. \]

Write

\[ y=\frac{kx}{z}. \]

Then

\[ 12=\frac{8k}{2}=4k. \]

So

\[ k=3. \]

The model is

\[ \boxed{y=\frac{3x}{z}}. \]

Worked Example: Powers

Suppose \(y\) varies jointly with \(x^2\) and \(z\), and inversely with \(w\):

\[ y=\frac{kx^2z}{w}. \]

If

\[ y=30,\quad x=2,\quad z=5,\quad w=4, \]

then

\[ 30=\frac{k(2^2)(5)}{4}. \]

So

\[ 30=5k, \] \[ \boxed{k=6}. \]

Worked Example: Relative Scaling

Suppose

\[ Q=\frac{kab^2}{c}. \]

If \(a\) triples, \(b\) doubles, and \(c\) is multiplied by \(6\), then \(Q\) is multiplied by

\[ 3\cdot2^2\cdot\frac16. \]

Simplify:

\[ 3\cdot4\cdot\frac16 = 2. \]

So \(Q\) doubles.

A Reliable Translation Workflow

  1. Identify the dependent quantity.
  2. Place directly varying factors in the numerator.
  3. Multiply jointly varying factors together.
  4. Place inversely varying factors in the denominator.
  5. Apply any stated powers.
  6. Introduce the constant \(k\).
  7. Record denominator restrictions.

A Reliable Solving Workflow

  1. Translate the variation statement into an equation.
  2. Substitute a known set of values to determine \(k\).
  3. Write the completed model.
  4. Substitute new values or solve for the requested variable.
  5. Carry units when relevant.
  6. Check domain and contextual restrictions.
  7. Verify that the scaling behavior matches the original variation statement.

Common Errors

Adding Instead of Multiplying Joint Factors

Joint variation uses products.

Putting Inverse Factors in the Numerator

Inverse factors belong in the denominator.

Forgetting Powers

“Varies as the square” and “varies inversely as the cube” must be represented with exponents.

Assuming the Constant Is Always \(1\)

Many familiar formulas have \(k=1\), but variation problems generally require \(k\) to be determined from data.

Ignoring Denominator Restrictions

Any inverse factor must remain nonzero.

Applying Scale Factors Additively

Scaling effects multiply, especially when several variables change at once.

Ignoring Model Assumptions

A mathematically correct variation equation can still be inappropriate if the real system does not obey the assumed relationship.

Key Ideas

  • Joint variation means a quantity varies directly with the product of two or more variables.
  • A typical joint model is \(y=kxz\).
  • Combined variation mixes direct and inverse factors, such as \(y=kx/z\).
  • Direct factors belong in the numerator; inverse factors belong in the denominator.
  • Variation can involve powers, such as \(y=kx^2/z^3\).
  • Scaling effects multiply across variables.
  • The constant \(k\) is found by substituting known values into the variation equation.
  • Units of \(k\) are determined by dimensional consistency.
  • Geometry, motion, work, density, gravitation, electrostatics, and many other models use joint or combined variation structures.
  • Relative-change problems can often be solved without finding \(k\) because the constant cancels.
  • Inverse factors create domain restrictions.
  • Variation equations are models whose usefulness depends on the assumptions under which the relationship remains valid.

Where This Leads

Joint and combined variation extend proportional reasoning into multivariable systems.

The same multiplicative structure appears later in dimensional analysis, geometry, physics, economics, scaling laws, power laws, multivariable modeling, and formulas in which several quantities work together to determine an output.

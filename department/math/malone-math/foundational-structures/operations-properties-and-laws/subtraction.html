Subtraction

Removing, comparing, measuring change, and finding the amount that separates two values.

The Big Idea

Subtraction finds a difference. Depending on the situation, that difference may describe what remains, how much one quantity exceeds another, the change from one value to another, or the missing part of an addition statement.

\[ a-b=c \]

Here, \(a\) is the minuend, \(b\) is the subtrahend, and \(c\) is the difference. In everyday work, it is usually clearer to say “the number being subtracted from,” “the number being subtracted,” and “the result.”

The most useful connection is

\[ a-b=a+(-b). \]

Subtracting a quantity is the same as adding its additive inverse. This one idea explains subtraction of signed numbers and connects subtraction to the laws of addition.

Subtraction Has Several Connected Meanings

Common interpretations of subtraction
Interpretation Question Example
Taking away How much remains after some is removed? \(12-5=7\)
Comparing How much more or less is one value than another? \(18-11=7\)
Missing addend What must be added to reach the total? \(9+?=15\), so \(15-9=6\)
Signed change What is the final value minus the initial value? \(4-10=-6\)
Distance How far apart are two values? \(|3-10|=7\)

These interpretations answer different questions. A signed change may be negative, while a distance is conventionally nonnegative. Reading the context matters as much as carrying out the arithmetic.

Taking Away

Suppose a box holds \(14\) markers and \(6\) are removed. The number remaining is

\[ 14-6=8. \]

This concrete model is a natural starting point when the subtrahend is nonnegative and no more than the minuend. It does not, by itself, explain expressions such as \(5-9\) or \(5-(-9)\). For those, the number-line and additive-inverse views are more flexible.

Comparing Quantities

If Mia has \(17\) books and Arun has \(12\), then Mia has

\[ 17-12=5 \]

more books than Arun. Nothing was physically removed. Subtraction measures the gap between the two counts.

Comparison language is directional. “How much more is \(17\) than \(12\)?” corresponds to \(17-12\). Reversing the order produces \(12-17=-5\), which describes the comparison in the opposite direction.

Finding a Missing Addend

Subtraction undoes addition. The statement

\[ 23-8=15 \]

is equivalent to the missing-addend statement

\[ 8+15=23. \]

This view is especially helpful for mental calculation. To find \(63-58\), ask, “What must be added to \(58\) to reach \(63\)?” The answer is \(5\).

Subtraction on the Number Line

On a number line, \(a-b\) means start at \(a\) and add the opposite of \(b\).

Subtracting a positive number moves left:

\[ 4-7=4+(-7)=-3. \]

Subtracting a negative number moves right:

\[ 4-(-7)=4+7=11. \]

The second example is not a mysterious “double-negative trick.” The opposite of a negative movement is a positive movement.

Subtracting Signed Numbers

A reliable method has two steps:

  1. Rewrite subtraction as addition of the opposite.
  2. Apply the signed-addition rules.
Examples of rewriting subtraction
Original expression Add the opposite Result
\(9-4\) \(9+(-4)\) \(5\)
\(9-(-4)\) \(9+4\) \(13\)
\(-9-4\) \((-9)+(-4)\) \(-13\)
\(-9-(-4)\) \((-9)+4\) \(-5\)

Parentheses make the role of each sign easier to see. In \(9-(-4)\), the first minus sign is the operation and the second is part of the number \(-4\).

Order Matters

Subtraction is generally not commutative:

\[ a-b\ne b-a. \]

For example,

\[ 8-3=5, \qquad 3-8=-5. \]

The reversed differences are additive inverses:

\[ b-a=-(a-b). \]

A useful habit is to say the expression aloud: \(a-b\) means “\(a\) minus \(b\),” not merely “the difference between \(a\) and \(b\).”

Grouping Matters Too

Subtraction is generally not associative:

\[ (a-b)-c\ne a-(b-c). \]

For example,

\[ (12-5)-2=5, \qquad 12-(5-2)=9. \]

Without parentheses, a chain of additions and subtractions is evaluated from left to right:

\[ 12-5-2=(12-5)-2=5. \]

Rewriting as addition makes the structure explicit: \(12-5-2=12+(-5)+(-2)\).

Zero and Equal Values

Important subtraction patterns
Pattern Result Reason
Subtract zero \(a-0=a\) Zero changes nothing
Subtract a value from itself \(a-a=0\) A value and its opposite cancel
Subtract from zero \(0-a=-a\) The result is the additive inverse
Subtract a negative \(a-(-b)=a+b\) The opposite of \(-b\) is \(b\)

Notice that zero acts as an identity only when it is subtracted: \(a-0=a\). In general, \(0-a\ne a\), so subtraction has no two-sided identity of its own.

Closure Depends on the Number System

Whole numbers are not closed under subtraction because \(3-8=-5\), which is not a whole number. Integers are closed under subtraction, as are rational, real, and complex numbers.

This is one reason number systems expand. Negative integers allow every difference of whole numbers to have a home.

The Place-Value Subtraction Algorithm

The standard written algorithm subtracts like place values. When a place does not contain enough units, regroup one unit from the place to its left.

Consider

\[ 742-368. \]

  1. Ones: regroup one ten as ten ones, so \(12-8=4\).
  2. Tens: after regrouping, \(3\) tens remain. Regroup one hundred, giving \(13-6=7\) tens.
  3. Hundreds: \(6-3=3\) hundreds.

\[ 742-368=374. \]

“Borrowing” is informal shorthand. The value is not borrowed and later returned; it is regrouped. One hundred becomes ten tens, or one ten becomes ten ones, without changing the total value.

Regrouping Across Zeros

A zero may require regrouping across more than one place. For

\[ 1{,}002-587, \]

regroup one thousand as ten hundreds, one of those hundreds as ten tens, and one of those tens as ten ones. The available places then represent \(9\) hundreds, \(9\) tens, and \(12\) ones:

\[ 1{,}002-587=415. \]

A quick check confirms the result: \(415+587=1{,}002\).

Subtracting Decimals

Align decimal points so equal place values appear in the same column:

\[ 12.4-3.75 =12.40-3.75 =8.65. \]

Adding a trailing zero does not change the value: \(12.4=12.40\). It simply displays the hundredths place needed for the calculation.

Aligning the final digits rather than the decimal points mixes different place values and changes the problem.

Subtracting Fractions

Fractions can be subtracted directly only when they name equal-sized parts:

\[ \frac ac-\frac bc=\frac{a-b}{c}, \qquad c\ne0. \]

With unlike denominators, first create a common denominator:

\[ \frac ab-\frac cd =\frac{ad-bc}{bd}, \qquad b,d\ne0. \]

Example:

\[ \frac56-\frac14 =\frac{10}{12}-\frac3{12} =\frac7{12}. \]

The denominator describes the size of each part. Subtract the number of equal-sized parts, not the part sizes themselves.

Subtracting Mixed Numbers

One reliable approach is to convert mixed numbers to improper fractions:

\[ 4\frac13-2\frac56 =\frac{13}{3}-\frac{17}{6} =\frac{26}{6}-\frac{17}{6} =\frac96 =1\frac12. \]

Another approach is to regroup one whole into fractional parts. Both methods preserve value; choose the one that makes the structure easiest to see.

Subtracting Quantities with Units

Subtraction requires compatible dimensions and a common unit:

\[ 3.2\text{ m}-85\text{ cm} =3.20\text{ m}-0.85\text{ m} =2.35\text{ m}. \]

Expressions such as \(5\text{ kg}-2\text{ s}\) do not produce a meaningful mass or time. Convert compatible units before calculating, and carry the unit into the result.

Subtracting Measured Values

Measured values have limited precision. Suppose

\[ x=(9.4\pm0.2)\text{ cm} \qquad\text{and}\qquad y=(3.1\pm0.1)\text{ cm}. \]

The central difference is \(6.3\text{ cm}\). A conservative interval-style uncertainty report is

\[ x-y=(6.3\pm0.3)\text{ cm}. \]

Subtracting central values does not mean subtracting the uncertainty magnitudes. Uncertainty represents possible spread, so worst-case absolute uncertainties add. Other statistical models may use different rules when the measurements’ relationship is known.

Mental Subtraction Strategies

Useful mental strategies
Strategy Example Idea
Count up \(63-58=5\) Find the missing addend from \(58\) to \(63\)
Subtract in parts \(84-27=84-20-7=57\) Decompose the subtrahend by place value
Use compensation \(92-48=94-50=44\) Add the same amount to both values
Use a nearby benchmark \(300-98=300-100+2=202\) Subtract an easy amount, then correct
Bridge through a ten \(52-8=52-2-6=44\) Pass through \(50\)

A sound strategy changes the form of the calculation while preserving the difference.

Equal Changes Preserve a Difference

Adding the same quantity to both numbers keeps their difference unchanged:

\[ a-b=(a+k)-(b+k). \]

For example,

\[ 403-198 =405-200 =205. \]

Both positions moved two units in the same direction, so the gap between them stayed fixed. This is exact compensation, not approximation.

Estimate Before Trusting the Difference

Rounding gives a quick scale check:

\[ 7{,}914-3{,}087 \approx7{,}900-3{,}100 =4{,}800. \]

The exact result \(4{,}827\) is reasonable. A result of \(48{,}270\) would indicate a place-value problem.

For nonnegative numbers with \(a\ge b\), the difference \(a-b\) must lie between \(0\) and \(a\). This simple bound catches many errors.

Check Subtraction with Addition

If

\[ a-b=c, \]

then

\[ c+b=a. \]

For example, \(936-478=458\) can be checked by

\[ 458+478=936. \]

Estimation checks whether the scale is sensible; addition checks the exact inverse relationship. Both are worth using.

Subtraction in Equations

The position of the variable matters. To solve

\[ x-a=b, \]

add \(a\) to both sides, giving \(x=b+a\). Thus \(x-17=42\) has solution \(x=59\).

To solve

\[ a-x=b, \]

the missing subtrahend is \(x=a-b\). Thus \(59-x=42\) has solution \(x=17\).

These forms look similar, but subtraction is not commutative. Avoid moving symbols by memory alone; use inverse operations while preserving equality.

Signed Change and Distance Are Different

If a temperature moves from \(10^\circ\text{C}\) to \(4^\circ\text{C}\), the signed change is

\[ \text{final}-\text{initial}=4-10=-6^\circ\text{C}. \]

The negative sign indicates a decrease. The distance between the two temperatures is

\[ |4-10|=6^\circ\text{C}. \]

Distance removes direction and is nonnegative. A signed difference retains direction.

Vectors, Matrices, and Functions

Vector subtraction is componentwise:

\[ (a,b)-(c,d)=(a-c,b-d). \]

For example,

\[ (5,2)-(1,7)=(4,-5). \]

Matrices of the same dimensions subtract entry by entry:

\[ \begin{pmatrix}7&5\\3&9\end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix}2&1\\6&4\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}5&4\\-3&5\end{pmatrix}. \]

Functions subtract by their output values:

\[ (f-g)(x)=f(x)-g(x), \]

wherever both functions are defined. In each setting, subtraction means adding the corresponding additive inverse.

Translate the Relationship, Not Just a Keyword

Words such as left, less, difference, decrease, and fewer often suggest subtraction, but no keyword decides the operation by itself.

“Nora has \(5\) fewer tickets than Luis” means

\[ N=L-5. \]

If Nora has \(12\) tickets and we need Luis’s count, then \(12=L-5\), so the required computation is addition: \(L=17\). Model the relationship first; then choose the operation needed to answer the question.

A Practical Subtraction Routine

  1. Identify the question: remainder, comparison, missing part, signed change, or distance.
  2. Put the quantities in the intended order.
  3. Confirm that units and dimensions are compatible.
  4. Estimate the sign and approximate size.
  5. Choose a helpful form: count up, add the opposite, decompose, compensate, or use the written algorithm.
  6. Align place values or create common denominators when needed.
  7. Compute the difference carefully.
  8. Check by addition and estimation.
  9. Interpret the sign, unit, and meaning of the result.

Common Confusions

Does subtraction always make a number smaller?

No. Subtracting a negative number makes a real number larger, and subtracting zero leaves it unchanged.

Why does subtracting a negative become addition?

Subtraction means adding the opposite. The opposite of \(-b\) is \(b\), so \(a-(-b)=a+b\).

Can I always subtract the smaller number from the larger?

Only when the problem asks for a nonnegative absolute difference. Signed change and directed comparison require the stated order.

Is subtraction commutative?

No. Reversing the operands reverses the sign: \(b-a=-(a-b)\).

Is subtraction associative?

No. In general, \((a-b)-c\ne a-(b-c)\). Parentheses matter.

Do I subtract denominators when subtracting fractions?

No. Rewrite both fractions using equal-sized parts, then subtract their numerators.

Does regrouping change the value?

No. It changes only the representation: one ten becomes ten ones, one hundred becomes ten tens, and so on.

Is a negative answer automatically wrong?

No. A negative result can correctly represent a deficit, decrease, value below zero, or comparison in the opposite direction.

Working Summary

Subtraction finds a difference. It can represent taking away, comparing, finding a missing addend, measuring signed change, or finding distance.

The structural rule \(a-b=a+(-b)\) unifies subtraction of signed numbers. Unlike addition, subtraction is generally neither commutative nor associative, so order and grouping matter.

Place-value subtraction regroups equal values, fraction subtraction requires equal-sized parts, and measured subtraction requires compatible units. Estimate the result, compute carefully, and check the difference by addition.

Check Your Understanding

Name the parts of \(41-16=25\).

\(41\) is the minuend, \(16\) is the subtrahend, and \(25\) is the difference.

Evaluate \(7-(-12)\).

Rewrite the subtraction as addition of the opposite: \(7-(-12)=7+12=19\).

Evaluate \(-7-12\).

\(-7-12=(-7)+(-12)=-19\).

Use compensation to evaluate \(503-297\).

Add \(3\) to both values: \(503-297=506-300=206\).

Subtract \(\frac78-\frac13\).

A common denominator is \(24\): \(\frac{21}{24}-\frac8{24}=\frac{13}{24}\).

Subtract \(75\text{ cm}\) from \(2.3\text{ m}\).

\(75\text{ cm}=0.75\text{ m}\), so \(2.3\text{ m}-0.75\text{ m}=1.55\text{ m}\).

A value changes from \(14\) to \(9\). What are the signed change and the distance?

The signed change is \(9-14=-5\). The distance is \(|9-14|=5\).

Solve \(x-28=45\).

Add \(28\) to both sides: \(x=73\).

Solve \(73-x=45\).

The missing subtrahend is \(x=73-45=28\).

Subtract \((-2,5)\) from \((6,-1)\).

Subtract corresponding components: \((6,-1)-(-2,5)=(8,-6)\).

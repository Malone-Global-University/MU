Exponentiation

Building powers, describing repeated scaling, and modeling growth across many levels of mathematics.

The Big Idea

Exponentiation combines a base and an exponent to form a power. It begins as a compact way to write repeated multiplication, then expands into a language for geometric measurement, powers of ten, growth, decay, roots, functions, and transformations.

\[ a^n=b \]

says that \(a\) is the base, \(n\) is the exponent, and \(b\) is the resulting power or value.

When \(n\) is a positive whole number,

\[ a^n=\underbrace{a\cdot a\cdot a\cdots a}_{n\text{ factors}}. \]

Later definitions extend the exponent to zero, negative integers, fractions, real numbers, and complex numbers while preserving the exponent laws wherever the relevant expressions are defined.

The word power may refer to the entire expression \(a^n\) or to its evaluated result. When precision matters, name the base, exponent, and value separately.

Repeated Multiplication

The expression

\[ 7^4 \]

contains four factors of \(7\):

\[ 7^4=7\cdot7\cdot7\cdot7=2{,}401. \]

The exponent counts factors, not multiplications. Four factors are connected by three multiplication operations. Also, \(7^4\) does not mean \(7\times4\).

Squares, Cubes, and Dimensions

Exponents naturally record repeated dimensions. A square with side length \(s\) has area

\[ A=s^2, \]

and a cube with side length \(s\) has volume

\[ V=s^3. \]

This explains the language “squared” and “cubed.” Higher powers extend the algebraic pattern even when a direct physical picture is no longer available.

The Base Must Be Identified Carefully

Parentheses determine whether a sign or coefficient belongs to the base:

\[ (-3)^2=9, \qquad -3^2=-(3^2)=-9. \]

In the first expression, the base is \(-3\). In the second, exponentiation applies to \(3\) before the leading negative sign.

The same distinction appears with fractions:

\[ \left(\frac23\right)^2=\frac49, \qquad \frac{2}{3^2}=\frac29. \]

Negative Bases and Integer Exponents

For a negative base raised to a positive integer exponent, parity determines the sign:

Sign of a power with a negative base
Exponent Result sign Example
Even Positive \((-2)^4=16\)
Odd Negative \((-2)^5=-32\)

An even number of negative factors produces a positive product; an odd number produces a negative product.

Order and Grouping Matter

Exponentiation is generally neither commutative nor associative:

\[ 2^3\ne3^2, \qquad (2^3)^2\ne2^{(3^2)}. \]

Exponent towers are conventionally grouped from the top down, or right-associatively:

\[ 2^{3^2}=2^9=512, \]

while

\[ (2^3)^2=8^2=64. \]

Parentheses are the clearest way to communicate the intended structure.

Exponentiation also has no two-sided identity element. Although \(a^1=a\), the reversed expression \(1^a\) equals \(1\), not \(a\) in general.

The Core Laws of Exponents

Exponent laws
Law General form Key condition
Product of like bases \(a^m a^n=a^{m+n}\) Same base
Quotient of like bases \(\frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}\) \(a\ne0\)
Power of a power \((a^m)^n=a^{mn}\) Expressions must be defined
Power of a product \((ab)^n=a^n b^n\) Expressions must be defined
Power of a quotient \(\left(\frac ab\right)^n=\frac{a^n}{b^n}\) \(b\ne0\)
Zero exponent \(a^0=1\) \(a\ne0\)
Negative exponent \(a^{-n}=\frac1{a^n}\) \(a\ne0\)

Integer exponents provide the broadest elementary setting for these rules. With fractional or real exponents, additional domain conditions may be required, especially for negative bases.

Multiplying Powers with the Same Base

Multiplying like bases joins their factor lists:

\[ a^m a^n=a^{m+n}. \]

For example,

\[ 2^3\cdot2^4 =(2\cdot2\cdot2)(2\cdot2\cdot2\cdot2) =2^7 =128. \]

The exponents add because the number of equal factors adds. The bases do not add or multiply into a new base.

Dividing Powers with the Same Base

Dividing like nonzero bases cancels matching factors:

\[ \frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}, \qquad a\ne0. \]

Example:

\[ \frac{5^7}{5^3}=5^{7-3}=5^4=625. \]

When the denominator contains more factors than the numerator, the remaining factors appear reciprocally. This leads naturally to negative exponents.

Powers of Powers, Products, and Quotients

A power raised to a power repeats the inner factor group:

\[ (a^m)^n=a^{mn}. \]

Thus \((x^3)^4=x^{12}\), not \(x^7\).

An exponent applies to every factor in a product or quotient:

\[ (3x)^4=3^4x^4=81x^4, \qquad \left(\frac{2x}{5}\right)^3=\frac{8x^3}{125}. \]

Why a Nonzero Base to the Zero Power Equals One

The quotient law gives a consistent explanation:

\[ \frac{a^n}{a^n}=a^{n-n}=a^0. \]

For \(a\ne0\), the left side equals \(1\), so

\[ a^0=1. \]

The restriction \(a\ne0\) is essential because the quotient \(0^n/0^n\) would involve division by zero.

Negative Exponents Represent Reciprocals

A negative exponent does not make the power negative. It reverses the base into a reciprocal:

\[ a^{-n}=\frac1{a^n}, \qquad a\ne0. \]

Examples:

\[ 2^{-3}=\frac1{2^3}=\frac18, \qquad \left(\frac35\right)^{-2}=\left(\frac53\right)^2=\frac{25}{9}. \]

Because zero has no reciprocal, \(0^{-n}\) is undefined for positive \(n\).

Rational Exponents Connect to Radicals

Fractional exponents combine radicalization and exponentiation:

\[ a^{1/n}=\sqrt[n]{a}, \qquad a^{m/n}=\sqrt[n]{a^m}=\left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^m, \]

wherever the real-valued expressions are defined.

For example,

\[ 27^{2/3}=\left(\sqrt[3]{27}\right)^2=3^2=9. \]

The denominator of the reduced exponent gives the radical index; the numerator gives the power.

Real-Number Domain Restrictions

Positive bases can be raised to any real exponent. Zero and negative bases require special care.

Selected real-valued cases
Expression type Real-valued condition Example
\(a^x\) with \(a>0\) Defined for every real \(x\) \(2^{\sqrt2}\)
\(a^{m/n}\) with even reduced \(n\) Require \(a\ge0\) for a real principal value \((-4)^{1/2}\) is not real
\(a^{m/n}\) with odd reduced \(n\) Negative \(a\) may be allowed \((-8)^{1/3}=-2\)
\(0^x\) Defined as \(0\) for \(x>0\) \(0^5=0\)

Complex numbers extend exponentiation further, but complex powers may involve multiple values or a chosen branch. The number system and definition must be stated.

What About \(0^0\)?

In elementary real-number arithmetic, \(0^0\) is usually left undefined. Competing patterns point in different directions: \(a^0=1\) for nonzero \(a\), while \(0^n=0\) for positive \(n\).

In calculus, the form \(0^0\) is called indeterminate because different limiting processes can approach different values. In combinatorics and computer science, defining \(0^0=1\) can be a useful convention that keeps formulas uniform.

The appropriate treatment depends on the context; a convention should be stated rather than assumed.

Exponents Do Not Distribute Over Addition

Powers distribute over multiplication and division, but not over addition or subtraction:

\[ (ab)^n=a^n b^n, \qquad (a+b)^n\ne a^n+b^n \]

in general. For instance,

\[ (2+3)^2=25, \qquad 2^2+3^2=13. \]

Expanding \((a+b)^2\) requires the distributive law:

\[ (a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2. \]

Powers of Ten and Place Value

Decimal place value is organized by powers of ten:

\[ 10^3=1{,}000, \quad 10^0=1, \quad 10^{-3}=0.001. \]

A decimal numeral can be expanded by place value:

\[ 4{,}207.35 =4\cdot10^3+2\cdot10^2+7\cdot10^0+3\cdot10^{-1}+5\cdot10^{-2}. \]

Powers of ten make magnitude and digit position part of the same structure.

Scientific Notation

Scientific notation writes a nonzero number as

\[ a\times10^n, \qquad 1\le|a|<10, \]

where \(n\) is an integer. For example,

\[ 6{,}430{,}000=6.43\times10^6, \qquad 0.000052=5.2\times10^{-5}. \]

Multiplication adds powers of ten, and division subtracts them:

\[ (a\times10^m)(b\times10^n)=ab\times10^{m+n}. \]

Exponents Apply to Units

When a measured quantity is raised to a power, both its numerical value and its unit are raised:

\[ (4\text{ m})^2=16\text{ m}^2, \qquad (2\text{ m})^3=8\text{ m}^3. \]

A squared unit measures area; a cubed unit measures volume. More generally,

\[ \left(\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}\right)^2 =\frac{\text{m}^2}{\text{s}^2}. \]

Forgetting to exponentiate the unit changes the dimension of the result.

Repeated Scaling Creates Growth and Decay

If a quantity begins at \(P_0\) and is multiplied by the same factor \(b\) during each period, then after \(t\) periods,

\[ P(t)=P_0b^t. \]

Behavior of a positive exponential base
Base \(b\) Behavior Example
\(b>1\) Exponential growth \(P(t)=100(1.08)^t\)
\(b=1\) Constant value \(P(t)=100\)
\(0<b<1\) Exponential decay \(P(t)=100(0.8)^t\)

The exponent counts how many times the scale factor acts.

Compound Interest

If a principal \(P\) earns periodic rate \(r\) for \(t\) periods, with the earnings reinvested, then

\[ A=P(1+r)^t. \]

An investment of \(\$1{,}000\) growing by \(5\%\) annually for \(4\) years becomes

\[ A=1{,}000(1.05)^4\approx\$1{,}215.51. \]

Each year scales the entire current balance, not only the original principal. That repeated scaling is why the exponent appears.

Powers and Exponential Functions Are Different

In a power function, the variable is the base:

\[ f(x)=x^3. \]

In an exponential function, the variable is the exponent:

\[ g(x)=3^x. \]

These families behave differently. Power functions scale according to a fixed exponent; exponential functions repeatedly apply a fixed base.

Efficient Exponentiation

Large integer powers can be computed with repeated squaring instead of multiplying the base one factor at a time. For example,

\[ 3^{13}=3^{8+4+1}=3^8\cdot3^4\cdot3. \]

Build the needed powers by squaring:

\[ 3^2=9, \quad 3^4=81, \quad 3^8=6{,}561. \]

This method is fundamental in calculators, computer algebra, modular arithmetic, and cryptography.

Estimate Before Trusting a Power

Powers grow quickly, so order-of-magnitude checks are valuable. Since \(3^5=243\) and \(3^6=729\), a claimed value of \(3^{5.5}\) should lie between \(243\) and \(729\).

For bases near \(1\), compare repeated scale factors. For example,

\[ 1.1^{10}\approx2.59, \]

so ten \(10\%\) increases more than double the original quantity but do not multiply it by ten.

Exponentiation Has Two Inverse Questions

In

\[ a^n=b, \]

the inverse operation depends on which input is unknown.

Undoing exponentiation
Unknown Inverse question Operation
Base \(a\) What number raised to \(n\) gives \(b\)? Radicalization: \(a=\sqrt[n]{b}\), with domain and sign care
Exponent \(n\) To what exponent must \(a\) be raised to give \(b\)? Logarithm: \(n=\log_a b\), under appropriate conditions

This is why both radicals and logarithms are connected to exponentiation. They undo different input positions.

Exponentiation in Equations

To solve

\[ x^2=49, \]

both \(7\) and \(-7\) work, so

\[ x=\pm7. \]

For an odd power such as \(x^3=125\), there is one real solution: \(x=5\).

When the variable is in the exponent, logarithms are used. From

\[ 2^x=20, \]

we obtain \(x=\log_2 20\approx4.322\). Always check the original equation and its real-number domain.

Powers of Functions, Matrices, and Other Objects

A function can be raised pointwise to an integer power:

\[ (f^n)(x)=[f(x)]^n. \]

This is different from function iteration, where \(f\) is composed with itself repeatedly.

A square matrix may be raised to a nonnegative integer power by repeated matrix multiplication:

\[ A^3=AAA, \qquad A^0=I, \]

where \(I\) is the identity matrix. Negative matrix powers require \(A\) to be invertible. Matrix exponentiation inherits the noncommutative nature of matrix multiplication, so familiar scalar manipulations must be used with care.

A Practical Exponentiation Routine

  1. Identify the complete base and exponent.
  2. Note the number system and domain restrictions.
  3. Use parentheses to make signs and grouped bases explicit.
  4. Predict the sign and approximate magnitude.
  5. Choose the applicable exponent law or computation strategy.
  6. Apply the exponent to every factor and unit in the base.
  7. Simplify without distributing over addition.
  8. Check using multiplication, radicalization, logarithms, or estimation.
  9. Interpret the power in context.

Common Confusions

Does \(3^4\) mean \(3\times4\)?

No. It means four factors of \(3\): \(3^4=3\cdot3\cdot3\cdot3=81\).

Does the exponent count factors or multiplication signs?

It counts factors. The expression \(a^4\) contains four factors of \(a\) joined by three multiplication signs.

Are \((-3)^2\) and \(-3^2\) equal?

No. \((-3)^2=9\), while \(-3^2=-(3^2)=-9\).

Does a negative exponent make the answer negative?

No. A negative exponent forms a reciprocal: \(a^{-n}=1/a^n\) for \(a\ne0\).

Can exponents distribute over addition?

Not in general. \((a+b)^n\) must be expanded using multiplication and the distributive law.

Why is \(a^0=1\)?

For nonzero \(a\), the quotient law gives \(a^n/a^n=a^{n-n}=a^0=1\).

Is \(0^0\) always equal to \(1\)?

No universal context-free answer is used. Elementary arithmetic usually leaves it undefined, calculus treats \(0^0\) as an indeterminate form in limits, and some discrete fields define it as \(1\) by convention.

Is \((a^m)^n=a^{m+n}\)?

No. A power raised to a power multiplies exponents: \((a^m)^n=a^{mn}\).

Are \(x^3\) and \(3^x\) the same type of function?

No. \(x^3\) is a power function; \(3^x\) is an exponential function.

Working Summary

Exponentiation combines a base and an exponent into a power. Positive integer exponents count equal factors; extended exponents describe reciprocals, radicals, continuous scales, growth, and decay.

Like bases multiply by adding exponents, divide by subtracting exponents, and form powers of powers by multiplying exponents. Exponents apply across products and quotients but not across sums and differences.

Domain restrictions matter for zero, negative bases, and fractional exponents. Identify the base carefully, estimate the magnitude, apply the laws only where defined, and check through multiplication, radicalization, logarithms, or substitution.

Check Your Understanding

Name the parts of \(4^3=64\).

\(4\) is the base, \(3\) is the exponent, and \(64\) is the value of the power.

Evaluate \((-5)^4\) and \(-5^4\).

\((-5)^4=625\), while \(-5^4=-(5^4)=-625\).

Simplify \(x^7x^{-3}\).

Add the exponents: \(x^{7+(-3)}=x^4\), under the restrictions required by the original expression.

Simplify \(\frac{a^9}{a^4}\).

\(a^{9-4}=a^5\), with \(a\ne0\) because the original denominator cannot be zero.

Simplify \((2x^3)^4\).

Apply the exponent to both factors: \(2^4x^{12}=16x^{12}\).

Evaluate \(16^{-3/4}\).

\(16^{1/4}=2\), so \(16^{-3/4}=2^{-3}=\frac18\).

Write \(0.0000072\) in scientific notation.

\(0.0000072=7.2\times10^{-6}\).

What unit results from \((6\text{ cm})^3\)?

\((6\text{ cm})^3=216\text{ cm}^3\).

Solve \(x^3=216\) over the real numbers.

Take the cube root: \(x=6\).

Solve \(5^x=125\).

Since \(125=5^3\), \(x=3\).

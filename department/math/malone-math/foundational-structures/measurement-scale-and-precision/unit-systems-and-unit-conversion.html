Unit Systems and Unit Conversion

The same quantity can be expressed in different units. Unit conversion changes the numerical representation while preserving the underlying quantity, and dimensional cancellation gives us a reliable way to make those changes.

Changing the Unit without Changing the Quantity

The length

\[ 1\text{ m} \]

is the same physical quantity as

\[ 100\text{ cm}. \]

The numerical values differ because the units differ.

\[ \boxed{ 1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm} } \]

Unit conversion is therefore a change of representation:

\[ \boxed{ \text{same quantity} \longrightarrow \text{different number-unit form} } \]

The goal is not to change the amount being measured. The goal is to express that amount in a different unit.

Unit Systems

A unit system is a coordinated collection of units used to measure quantities.

Different systems may choose different standard units for the same dimension.

Length

meter, centimeter, kilometer, inch, foot, yard, mile

Mass

kilogram, gram, pound, ounce

Volume

liter, milliliter, gallon, quart, pint, cup

Time

second, minute, hour, day

The existence of several unit systems makes conversion an important practical skill.

The SI System

The International System of Units, abbreviated SI, is a widely used measurement system based on standardized base units and decimal prefixes.

Common SI units include:

Quantity SI Unit Symbol
Lengthmeter\(\mathrm{m}\)
Masskilogram\(\mathrm{kg}\)
Timesecond\(\mathrm{s}\)
Temperaturekelvin\(\mathrm{K}\)
Electric currentampere\(\mathrm{A}\)

Many SI conversions are especially convenient because metric prefixes are powers of ten.

U.S. Customary Units

U.S. customary units include familiar units such as:

  • inch, foot, yard, mile,
  • ounce and pound,
  • fluid ounce, cup, pint, quart, gallon.

Unlike metric prefixes, many customary conversion relationships are not powers of ten.

For example,

\[ 1\text{ ft}=12\text{ in}, \] \[ 1\text{ yd}=3\text{ ft}, \] \[ 1\text{ mi}=5280\text{ ft}. \]

This makes a systematic conversion method especially useful.

Conversion Factors

A conversion factor is a ratio formed from two equivalent measurements.

Because

\[ 1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}, \]

both of these ratios equal \(1\):

\[ \frac{100\text{ cm}}{1\text{ m}}=1, \] \[ \frac{1\text{ m}}{100\text{ cm}}=1. \]

Multiplying by either ratio changes the representation of a quantity without changing the quantity itself.

\[ \boxed{ \text{conversion factor} = \frac{\text{one unit expression}}{\text{equivalent unit expression}} } \]

Dimensional Cancellation

Units can often be treated like algebraic factors.

Suppose we convert

\[ 3\text{ m} \]

to centimeters.

Choose the conversion factor so that meters cancel:

\[ 3\text{ m} \left( \frac{100\text{ cm}}{1\text{ m}} \right). \]

The unit \(\text{m}\) cancels:

\[ 3 \left( 100\text{ cm} \right) = 300\text{ cm}. \] \[ \boxed{ 3\text{ m}=300\text{ cm} } \]

This method is often called dimensional analysis, factor-label conversion, or the unit-factor method.

The Conversion Chain

One-Step Conversions

A one-step conversion uses a single conversion factor.

Example

Convert

\[ 72\text{ in} \]

to feet.

Since

\[ 1\text{ ft}=12\text{ in}, \]

use

\[ 72\text{ in} \left( \frac{1\text{ ft}}{12\text{ in}} \right). \]

Inches cancel:

\[ 72\left(\frac1{12}\right)\text{ ft} = \boxed{6\text{ ft}}. \]

Multi-Step Conversions

Sometimes no convenient direct conversion is available.

Then chain several factors together.

Example

Convert

\[ 2\text{ mi} \]

to inches.

Use

\[ 1\text{ mi}=5280\text{ ft} \]

and

\[ 1\text{ ft}=12\text{ in}. \] \[ 2\text{ mi} \left( \frac{5280\text{ ft}}{1\text{ mi}} \right) \left( \frac{12\text{ in}}{1\text{ ft}} \right). \]

Miles and feet cancel:

\[ 2(5280)(12)\text{ in} = \boxed{126720\text{ in}}. \]

Compound Units

Rates often contain units in both numerator and denominator.

For example,

\[ 60\frac{\text{mi}}{\text{h}}. \]

To convert miles per hour to feet per second, convert both parts:

\[ 60\frac{\text{mi}}{\text{h}} \left( \frac{5280\text{ ft}}{1\text{ mi}} \right) \left( \frac{1\text{ h}}{3600\text{ s}} \right). \]

Miles and hours cancel:

\[ 60\cdot\frac{5280}{3600} \frac{\text{ft}}{\text{s}} = \boxed{88\frac{\text{ft}}{\text{s}}}. \]

The placement of units tells us which conversion factor orientation to use.

Squared and Cubed Units

Area and volume conversions require special care because the unit itself is squared or cubed.

Area

Since

\[ 1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}, \]

then

\[ 1\text{ m}^2 = (100\text{ cm})^2 = 10000\text{ cm}^2. \] \[ \boxed{ 1\text{ m}^2=10000\text{ cm}^2 } \]

Volume

\[ 1\text{ m}^3 = (100\text{ cm})^3 = 1000000\text{ cm}^3. \] \[ \boxed{ 1\text{ m}^3=10^6\text{ cm}^3 } \]

The conversion factor must be raised to the same power as the unit.

Metric Prefixes

Metric prefixes scale a base unit by powers of ten.

Prefix Symbol Factor
kilo\(\mathrm{k}\)\(10^3\)
centi\(\mathrm{c}\)\(10^{-2}\)
milli\(\mathrm{m}\)\(10^{-3}\)
micro\(\mathrm{\mu}\)\(10^{-6}\)

For example,

\[ 1\text{ km}=1000\text{ m}, \] \[ 1\text{ cm}=0.01\text{ m}, \] \[ 1\text{ mm}=0.001\text{ m}. \]

Because the factors are powers of ten, many metric conversions can be checked quickly by estimating the change in magnitude.

Temperature Conversion

Not every unit conversion is a simple multiplication by a factor.

Celsius and Fahrenheit use different zero points as well as different degree sizes.

The conversions are:

\[ \boxed{ F=\frac95C+32 } \]

and

\[ \boxed{ C=\frac59(F-32) }. \]

Because an offset is involved, ordinary cancellation with a single ratio is not sufficient.

This is an important reminder: conversion-factor multiplication works directly when the units differ only by scale. Affine scales such as Celsius and Fahrenheit require both scaling and translation.

Checking a Conversion

A good conversion should pass several checks.

1. Do the Units Cancel Correctly?

The unwanted unit should disappear and the desired unit should remain.

2. Is the Direction Reasonable?

Converting meters to centimeters should produce a larger numerical value because centimeters are smaller units.

3. Is the Magnitude Reasonable?

A few miles should not convert to only a few feet.

4. Are Unit Powers Handled Correctly?

Area and volume conversions require squared and cubed factors.

5. Does the Result Preserve the Same Quantity?

Conversion changes representation, not the amount being measured.

Practical Applications

Science and Engineering

Measurements from different instruments or sources often need to be converted to a common unit system before formulas are used.

Construction

Plans, materials, and equipment may mix inches, feet, meters, and millimeters.

Travel

Distance, fuel economy, and speed may be reported in different unit systems.

Medicine and Laboratory Work

Mass, volume, concentration, and rate conversions require careful unit tracking.

Computing and Data

Storage, bandwidth, time intervals, pixel dimensions, and scientific data may all involve unit or scale conversion.

Business

Rates such as dollars per kilogram, cost per hour, and units per minute often must be converted before comparison.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Centimeters to Meters

Convert

\[ 350\text{ cm} \]

to meters.

\[ 350\text{ cm} \left( \frac{1\text{ m}}{100\text{ cm}} \right) = 3.5\text{ m}. \] \[ \boxed{350\text{ cm}=3.5\text{ m}} \]

Example 2: Hours to Seconds

Convert

\[ 2.5\text{ h} \]

to seconds.

\[ 2.5\text{ h} \left( \frac{60\text{ min}}{1\text{ h}} \right) \left( \frac{60\text{ s}}{1\text{ min}} \right). \] \[ = 2.5(60)(60)\text{ s} = \boxed{9000\text{ s}}. \]

Example 3: Kilometers per Hour to Meters per Second

Convert

\[ 72\frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}} \]

to meters per second.

\[ 72\frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}} \left( \frac{1000\text{ m}}{1\text{ km}} \right) \left( \frac{1\text{ h}}{3600\text{ s}} \right). \] \[ = 72\cdot\frac{1000}{3600} \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} = \boxed{20\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}}. \]

Example 4: Square Feet to Square Inches

Convert

\[ 3\text{ ft}^2 \]

to square inches.

Since

\[ 1\text{ ft}=12\text{ in}, \]

then

\[ 3\text{ ft}^2 \left( \frac{12\text{ in}}{1\text{ ft}} \right)^2. \] \[ = 3(144)\text{ in}^2 = \boxed{432\text{ in}^2}. \]

Example 5: Cubic Meters to Cubic Centimeters

\[ 0.002\text{ m}^3 \left( \frac{100\text{ cm}}{1\text{ m}} \right)^3. \] \[ = 0.002(1000000)\text{ cm}^3 = \boxed{2000\text{ cm}^3}. \]

Example 6: Celsius to Fahrenheit

Convert

\[ 20^\circ\text{C} \]

to Fahrenheit.

\[ F= \frac95(20)+32 = 36+32 = \boxed{68^\circ\text{F}}. \]

Common Errors

1. Flipping the Conversion Factor the Wrong Way

Choose the orientation that cancels the unwanted unit.

2. Converting the Number but Ignoring the Unit

The unit is part of the quantity and must be carried through the calculation.

3. Forgetting to Square or Cube the Conversion Factor

Area and volume units require powered conversion factors.

4. Mixing Units inside a Formula

Convert quantities to compatible units before combining them unless the formula explicitly accounts for the different units.

5. Assuming Every Conversion Is Pure Scaling

Temperature scales such as Celsius and Fahrenheit also require an offset.

6. Accepting an Unreasonable Magnitude

Estimate whether the numerical value should increase or decrease before finalizing the result.

Convert

\[ 5\text{ m} \]

to centimeters.

Check Answer \[ 5\text{ m} \left( \frac{100\text{ cm}}{1\text{ m}} \right) = \boxed{500\text{ cm}}. \]

Time

Convert

\[ 3\text{ h} \]

to minutes.

Check Answer \[ 3\text{ h} \left( \frac{60\text{ min}}{1\text{ h}} \right) = \boxed{180\text{ min}}. \]

Speed

Convert

\[ 36\frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}} \]

to meters per second.

Check Answer \[ 36 \left( \frac{1000}{3600} \right) \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} = \boxed{10\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}}. \]

Convert

\[ 2\text{ m}^2 \]

to square centimeters.

Check Answer \[ 2 \left( 100^2 \right) \text{ cm}^2 = \boxed{20000\text{ cm}^2}. \]

Temperature

Convert

\[ 0^\circ\text{C} \]

to Fahrenheit.

Check Answer \[ F= \frac95(0)+32 = \boxed{32^\circ\text{F}}. \]

The Deeper Idea

Unit conversion is a concrete example of equivalence between representations.

The quantity

\[ 1\text{ m} \]

and the quantity

\[ 100\text{ cm} \]

are not merely close. They are equal measurements written in different units.

The conversion factor works because it is a carefully written form of multiplying by \(1\):

\[ \frac{100\text{ cm}}{1\text{ m}}=1. \]

This connects unit conversion to algebraic identity:

\[ Q\cdot1=Q. \]

Dimensional analysis therefore combines representation, equivalence, multiplication, cancellation, and measurement into one practical method.

\[ \boxed{ \text{same quantity} + \text{factor equal to }1 = \text{new unit representation} } \]

Summary

  • A unit system is a coordinated collection of measurement units.
  • SI uses standardized units and decimal prefixes.
  • U.S. customary units use conversion relationships such as \[ 1\text{ ft}=12\text{ in}. \]
  • A conversion factor is a ratio of equivalent measurements and therefore equals \(1\).
  • Arrange conversion factors so unwanted units cancel.
  • Multi-step conversions chain several conversion factors.
  • Compound-unit conversions may require conversions in both numerator and denominator.
  • Area and volume conversions require squared or cubed conversion factors.
  • Metric prefixes represent powers of ten.
  • Temperature conversions such as Celsius and Fahrenheit involve both scaling and an offset.
  • Reasonableness checks help catch unit-direction and magnitude errors.
  • Unit conversion changes the numerical-unit representation without changing the underlying quantity.

The safest conversion method is to keep the units visible, multiply by equivalent ratios, and let the unwanted units cancel. This turns unit conversion from memorized guesswork into a controlled algebraic process.

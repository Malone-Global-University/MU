Rates and Derived Units

A rate tells how one quantity changes, accumulates, or compares relative to another quantity. Its unit is not decoration: the derived unit records the relationship being measured.

A Quantity per Another Quantity

Suppose a car travels

\[ 180\text{ miles} \]

in

\[ 3\text{ hours}. \]

The average distance traveled per hour is

\[ \frac{180\text{ mi}}{3\text{ h}} = 60\frac{\text{mi}}{\text{h}}. \]

The number

\[ 60 \]

is only part of the meaning.

The unit

\[ \frac{\text{mi}}{\text{h}} \]

tells us that distance is being compared with time.

\[ \boxed{ \text{rate} = \frac{\text{one quantity}}{\text{another quantity}} } \]

What Is a Rate?

A rate compares two quantities by division.

If quantity \(A\) is compared with quantity \(B\), then a rate may have the form

\[ \frac{A}{B}. \]

The units divide in the same way:

\[ \frac{\text{unit of }A}{\text{unit of }B}. \]

Examples include:

  • miles per hour,
  • dollars per kilogram,
  • liters per minute,
  • people per square kilometer,
  • bits per second.

Ratios and Rates

A ratio compares quantities by division.

A rate is a ratio in which the quantities are understood relative to their units or kinds.

For example,

\[ \frac{3\text{ red marbles}}{5\text{ blue marbles}} \]

is a ratio between counts.

But

\[ \frac{60\text{ miles}}{1\text{ hour}} \]

compares distance with time and is naturally interpreted as a rate.

In applied work, the unit structure is often what gives a rate its practical meaning.

Unit Rates

A unit rate expresses a rate per one unit of the denominator quantity.

For example,

\[ \frac{240\text{ miles}}{4\text{ hours}} = 60\frac{\text{miles}}{\text{hour}}. \]

This means:

60 miles for each 1 hour.

Unit rates make comparisons easier because they normalize quantities to a common denominator of \(1\).

Example

A \(12\)-ounce package costs \(\$4.20\).

\[ \frac{\$4.20}{12\text{ oz}} = \$0.35/\text{oz}. \]

The unit price is

\[ \boxed{\$0.35\text{ per ounce}}. \]

Derived Units

A derived unit is built by multiplying, dividing, or raising other units to powers.

Area

\[ \mathrm{m^2} \]

length × length

Speed

\[ \mathrm{m/s} \]

length ÷ time

Density

\[ \mathrm{kg/m^3} \]

mass ÷ volume

Acceleration

\[ \mathrm{m/s^2} \]

speed change ÷ time

The unit records how the quantity was constructed.

\[ \boxed{ \text{derived unit} = \text{algebraic structure of the underlying quantities} } \]

How Derived Units Encode Meaning

Speed and Velocity Units

Average speed compares distance traveled with elapsed time:

\[ \text{average speed} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}. \]

Its dimension is

\[ LT^{-1}. \]

Common units include:

\[ \mathrm{m/s}, \qquad \mathrm{km/h}, \qquad \mathrm{mi/h}. \]

Velocity has the same derived unit structure as speed, but also includes direction in its interpretation.

Density

Density compares mass with volume:

\[ \boxed{ \rho=\frac{m}{V} } \]

Common units include

\[ \mathrm{kg/m^3} \]

and

\[ \mathrm{g/cm^3}. \]

A density of

\[ 2.7\frac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3} \]

means that each cubic centimeter contains \(2.7\) grams of mass.

Reading the unit verbally often clarifies the quantity immediately.

Flow and Production Rates

A flow rate compares an amount passing or being produced with time.

Fluid Flow

\[ \frac{\text{liters}}{\text{minute}}. \]

Production

\[ \frac{\text{parts}}{\text{hour}}. \]

Data Transfer

\[ \frac{\text{bits}}{\text{second}}. \]

If a machine produces

\[ 600\text{ parts} \]

in

\[ 5\text{ h}, \]

then its average production rate is

\[ \frac{600}{5} = \boxed{120\text{ parts/h}}. \]

Price and Cost Rates

Prices are often rates.

Examples include:

\[ \$3.20/\text{kg}, \] \[ \$18/\text{hour}, \] \[ \$0.12/\text{kWh}. \]

The denominator tells what one unit of cost is being compared against.

If labor costs

\[ \$24/\text{hour} \]

for \(7\) hours, then

\[ 7\text{ h} \left( \frac{\$24}{1\text{ h}} \right) = \boxed{\$168}. \]

The time unit cancels and leaves dollars.

Average Rate

An average rate compares total change with total change in another quantity.

For a quantity \(y\) changing with \(x\),

\[ \boxed{ \text{average rate of change} = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} } \]

where

\[ \Delta y=y_2-y_1 \]

and

\[ \Delta x=x_2-x_1. \]

Example

If temperature rises from \(18^\circ\text{C}\) to \(30^\circ\text{C}\) over \(4\) hours, then

\[ \frac{30-18}{4} = 3 \]

so the average rate is

\[ \boxed{ 3^\circ\text{C}/\text{h} }. \]

Instantaneous Rate

An average rate describes change over an interval.

An instantaneous rate describes the rate at a particular moment or input value.

A car's speedometer, for example, displays an approximation to instantaneous speed rather than the average speed of the entire trip.

Later in calculus, instantaneous rate of change will be formalized with the derivative:

\[ \frac{dy}{dx}. \]

For now, the important idea is that a rate can describe either an interval or a local moment.

Rates of Rates

A rate can itself change with respect to another quantity.

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity with respect to time:

\[ a= \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}. \]

If velocity has units

\[ \frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}}, \]

then acceleration has units

\[ \frac{\mathrm{m/s}}{\mathrm{s}} = \boxed{ \mathrm{m/s^2} }. \]

The squared time in the denominator means “meters per second, per second.”

Compound Rates

Some quantities combine several rates or derived units.

Fuel Economy

\[ \frac{\text{miles}}{\text{gallon}}. \]

Power Density

\[ \frac{\text{watts}}{\text{square meter}}. \]

Population Density

\[ \frac{\text{people}}{\text{square kilometer}}. \]

Volumetric Flow per Area

\[ \frac{\mathrm{m^3/s}}{\mathrm{m^2}} = \mathrm{m/s}. \]

Unit algebra can sometimes reveal surprising simplifications.

Reading Rates Dimensionally

Derived units can be translated into dimensions.

Speed

\[ \frac{L}{T}=LT^{-1}. \]

Density

\[ \frac{M}{L^3}=ML^{-3}. \]

Acceleration

\[ \frac{L}{T^2}=LT^{-2}. \]

This gives a useful connection to dimensional consistency:

\[ \boxed{ \text{rate unit} \longleftrightarrow \text{dimension quotient} } \]

If a proposed formula produces the wrong rate dimension, the formula cannot be correct.

Converting Rates

To convert a rate, convert each unit in the numerator or denominator as needed.

Example

Convert

\[ 90\frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}} \]

to meters per second.

\[ 90\frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}} \left( \frac{1000\text{ m}}{1\text{ km}} \right) \left( \frac{1\text{ h}}{3600\text{ s}} \right). \]

The units cancel:

\[ 90\cdot\frac{1000}{3600} \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} = \boxed{ 25\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} }. \]

Practical Applications

Transportation

Speed, fuel economy, and cost per mile are all rates.

Manufacturing

Production rates describe parts per hour, defects per batch, or energy per unit produced.

Business

Revenue per customer, cost per employee, and sales per square foot are rate measures.

Science

Density, reaction rate, flow rate, and decay rate connect measured quantities through derived units.

Computing

Bandwidth, frame rate, instructions per second, and storage throughput are all rate-based quantities.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Unit Rate

A cyclist travels

\[ 84\text{ km} \]

in

\[ 4\text{ h}. \]

The average speed is

\[ \frac{84}{4} = \boxed{21\text{ km/h}}. \]

Example 2: Unit Price

A \(5\)-kg bag costs \(\$18.50\).

\[ \frac{\$18.50}{5\text{ kg}} = \boxed{\$3.70/\text{kg}}. \]

Example 3: Density

A sample has mass

\[ 540\text{ g} \]

and volume

\[ 200\text{ cm}^3. \] \[ \rho= \frac{540\text{ g}}{200\text{ cm}^3} = \boxed{ 2.7\text{ g/cm}^3 }. \]

Example 4: Production Rate

A machine makes \(960\) parts in \(6\) hours.

\[ \frac{960}{6} = \boxed{160\text{ parts/h}}. \]

Example 5: Average Rate of Change

A tank volume increases from \(120\) L to \(300\) L in \(9\) minutes.

\[ \frac{300-120}{9} = \frac{180}{9} = \boxed{20\text{ L/min}}. \]

Example 6: Acceleration Units

Velocity changes from

\[ 10\text{ m/s} \]

to

\[ 22\text{ m/s} \]

in

\[ 4\text{ s}. \]

Average acceleration is

\[ \frac{22-10}{4} = 3. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{ 3\text{ m/s}^2 }. \]

Common Errors

1. Reporting the Number without the Rate Unit

A value such as \(60\) is incomplete if the intended quantity is \(60\text{ mi/h}\).

2. Reversing the Rate

\[ \frac{\text{miles}}{\text{hour}} \]

and

\[ \frac{\text{hours}}{\text{mile}} \]

are different quantities.

3. Mixing Incompatible Units before Dividing

Convert units when necessary so the result has the intended meaning.

4. Confusing Total Amount with Rate

A total of \(500\) liters is not the same as a flow rate of \(500\text{ L/min}\).

5. Confusing Average and Instantaneous Rate

An average over an interval does not necessarily equal the rate at every moment in the interval.

6. Misreading Squared Denominator Units

\[ \mathrm{m/s^2} \]

means meters per second per second, not meters divided by a geometric square of seconds.

Practice

Unit Rate

A runner travels \(15\) km in \(3\) hours. Find the average speed.

Check Answer \[ \frac{15}{3} = \boxed{5\text{ km/h}}. \]

Unit Price

Eight notebooks cost \(\$20\). Find the cost per notebook.

Check Answer \[ \frac{\$20}{8} = \boxed{\$2.50/\text{notebook}}. \]

Density

A material has mass \(250\) g and volume \(100\text{ cm}^3\). Find its density.

Check Answer \[ \frac{250\text{ g}}{100\text{ cm}^3} = \boxed{2.5\text{ g/cm}^3}. \]

Average Rate of Change

A quantity rises from \(40\) to \(70\) over \(5\) minutes. Find the average rate of change.

Check Answer \[ \frac{70-40}{5} = \boxed{6\text{ units/min}}. \]

Acceleration

Velocity changes by \(18\text{ m/s}\) over \(6\text{ s}\). Find the average acceleration.

Check Answer \[ \frac{18\text{ m/s}}{6\text{ s}} = \boxed{3\text{ m/s}^2}. \]

The Deeper Idea

A rate is not just a quotient of numbers. It is a quotient of quantities.

That is why its unit matters so much.

\[ \boxed{ \frac{\text{quantity }A}{\text{quantity }B} \longrightarrow \frac{\text{unit }A}{\text{unit }B} } \]

The derived unit records the relationship between the quantities.

This connects rates to several earlier ideas:

  • division creates ratios and quotients,
  • units behave algebraically,
  • dimensions constrain valid formulas,
  • functions can describe one quantity changing with another,
  • graphs can display rates visually as slope.

Later, rate will become one of the central ideas of calculus, modeling, and applied mathematics. The derivative is a rate, density is a rate-like quotient, probability densities are rates over intervals, and many physical laws are built from derived units.

Summary

  • A rate compares two quantities by division.
  • A unit rate expresses the comparison per one unit of the denominator quantity.
  • Derived units are built by multiplying, dividing, or powering other units.
  • Speed uses distance per time.
  • Density uses mass per volume.
  • Flow and production rates use amount per time.
  • Prices are often cost per unit of another quantity.
  • Average rate of change has the form \[ \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}. \]
  • Instantaneous rate describes change at a particular moment or input.
  • Acceleration is a rate of change of velocity and has units such as \[ \mathrm{m/s^2}. \]
  • Compound rates may involve several derived units at once.
  • Derived units can be translated directly into dimensional structure.
  • Rate conversion requires converting numerator and denominator units as needed.
  • The unit is part of the meaning of the rate, not an optional label.

Rates give mathematics a language for “per,” “for each,” and “with respect to.” Their derived units preserve that relationship and make rates useful across science, business, engineering, computing, and everyday quantitative reasoning.

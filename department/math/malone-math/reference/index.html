Purpose: Provide centralized, non-instructional support for definitions, notation, laws, formulas, methods, examples, history, connections, and navigation throughout Malone Math.

Reference is not a sixth instructional branch and does not prescribe a learning progression. It reduces friction during study: a learner can confirm a symbol, recover a formula, compare related definitions, review a theorem, identify a mathematician, diagnose an error, or follow a concept into another bucket without interrupting the larger exploration.

The governing movement is:

[ \text{locate}\longrightarrow\text{clarify}\longrightarrow\text{compare}\longrightarrow\text{reconnect}\longrightarrow\text{continue} ]

Standard reference-entry structure

Reference entries should use the fields that materially apply:

  1. Name
  2. Classification
  3. Concise definition
  4. Plain-language explanation
  5. Notation and pronunciation
  6. Required conditions or domain
  7. Example
  8. Nonexample or common error
  9. Properties and related laws
  10. Representations
  11. Applications
  12. Primary instructional home
  13. Related concepts and cross-references
  14. Historical note or attribution
  15. Sources and further reading

Terminology pages may additionally use the four-axis treatment: Etymology and Roots; Operational Nuance; Written versus Spoken Syntax; Case Usage and Field Variations.

Reference roles

Resource Primary role
Dictionary definition Supply a short, direct meaning
Terminology page Explain a word or expression deeply across use cases
Encyclopedia article Develop a concept, structure, person, or historical subject
Formula or method entry Provide rapid procedural recovery with conditions
Worked example Demonstrate a representative application
Textbook exploration Teach an idea systematically through narrative, reasoning, practice, and application

Boundary with instructional buckets

Reference treatment Instructional development
Define and cross-reference an object Introduce and use it within the appropriate bucket
List a law, theorem, or formula with conditions Derive, justify, and apply it in an exploration
Summarize a method or algorithm Develop procedural fluency through worked problems
Catalog functions, curves, diagrams, and examples Connect their behavior through algebraic, spatial, discrete, or analytical study
Record history, biography, and attribution Integrate historical context where it enriches instruction
Diagnose common errors Teach checking strategies and conceptual repair throughout the series

Governing editorial principle

Reference should help the learner recover exact information quickly, understand enough context to use it correctly, and return to the larger mathematical exploration with stronger connections and less friction.

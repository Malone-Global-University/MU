Prehistory of China

The development of human societies in China from the Paleolithic through agriculture, permanent settlements, regional cultures, chiefdoms, and the emergence of the earliest states.

The Formation of Early Chinese Civilization

The prehistory of China encompasses the immense span of human development before the establishment of historically recorded dynastic civilization. During this period, communities across the lands that later became China moved from mobile hunting and gathering to agriculture, permanent villages, increasingly complex regional societies, political hierarchies, and eventually early states.

This development did not occur through a single continuous culture. Different societies emerged across the Yellow River basin, the Yangtze River basin, the northeast, the eastern coastal regions, and other parts of China. These communities developed distinctive agricultural systems, technologies, pottery traditions, ritual practices, settlement patterns, and forms of political organization while also interacting through migration, exchange, competition, and cultural transmission.

Over thousands of years, increasingly complex societies produced permanent settlements, specialized crafts, extensive agricultural systems, elite burials, ceremonial centers, defensive works, organized labor, and regional political authority. These developments created the social and institutional foundations from which the earliest Chinese states and dynastic traditions emerged.

Prehistoric Development

The Malone Global University history of prehistoric China is organized as a developmental sequence from early human occupation through the emergence of state-level political organization.

  • Paleolithic

    Examine early human occupation of China, including hunter-gatherer societies, stone-tool traditions, changing environments, and the long development of human communities before agriculture.

  • First Agrarian Societies

    Explore the transition toward food production, including the cultivation and domestication of plants, the management of animals, and the development of early agricultural economies centered on crops such as millet and rice.

  • First Villages

    Study the growth of permanent settlements, houses, storage systems, pottery production, cemeteries, local exchange, and increasingly organized village communities.

  • Great Regional Cultures

    Survey the major prehistoric cultural traditions that emerged across different regions of China, including their distinctive technologies, artistic traditions, subsistence systems, settlement patterns, and ritual practices.

  • Early Chiefdoms

    Examine increasingly hierarchical societies characterized by powerful regional elites, ceremonial authority, specialized labor, prestige goods, fortified settlements, warfare, and organized control over communities and resources.

  • Early States

    Explore the final stage of Chinese prehistory, when larger political centers, organized labor systems, specialized production, fortified settlements, elite institutions, and increasingly centralized authority prepared the way for the first dynastic states.

Transition to Dynastic China

The emergence of early states marks the closing stage of prehistoric China in this collection. Political consolidation, social stratification, specialized production, large population centers, organized warfare, and increasingly formal systems of authority created conditions from which dynastic civilization could emerge.

The next stage of the chronology is the Xia dynasty, traditionally regarded as the first Chinese dynasty. Because the relationship between archaeological cultures and the traditional Xia chronology remains an important historical question, the early-states section examines state formation as an archaeological process, while the Xia section addresses the dynasty as a distinct historical tradition.

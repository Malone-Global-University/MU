Shriners International

Overview

Shriners International is a male Masonic appendant fraternity founded in New York City in 1872. It was originally called the Ancient Arabic Order of the Nobles of the Mystic Shrine, commonly abbreviated A.A.O.N.M.S.

The fraternity was created by Master Masons who wanted a less formal organization centered on fellowship, entertainment, pageantry, social activity, and charity. It later established the pediatric health-care system now known as Shriners Children's.

Shriners International developed within the historically White American Masonic system during an era of racial segregation. It remained institutionally separate from the Black Shrine order now commonly called the Prince Hall Shriners and officially organized as A.E.A.O.N.M.S., Inc.

Current public membership requirements do not state a racial restriction. They require an applicant to be a man and a Master Mason in good standing. The description historically White therefore identifies the institution's origin and development, not necessarily the race of every current member or an express present-day racial rule.

Historical Conditions

Shriners International arose during the expansion of American fraternal societies after the Civil War. Freemasonry, Odd Fellows, Knights of Pythias, ethnic mutual-aid societies, veterans' organizations, and professional clubs offered fellowship, status, insurance-like assistance, ritual, leadership, and social connection.

The founders believed conventional Masonic meetings were serious and formal. They wanted an additional fraternity for men who had already become Master Masons but desired organized entertainment, humor, travel, spectacle, and social fellowship.

The institution was also shaped by the racial, religious, gender, and class exclusions of nineteenth-century American associational life. Access depended first upon admission to a recognized Masonic lodge, and mainstream American Freemasonry was generally segregated.

Founding Circle

Around 1870, a group of Masons regularly met for lunch at the Knickerbocker Cottage in New York City. The group discussed creating a new body that would preserve Masonic fellowship while adding a lighter social character.

The fraternity's public history emphasizes two principal founders:

  • Walter M. Fleming — A physician and Mason who developed major portions of the ritual, structure, costumes, emblem, salutation, and organizational system.
  • William Jermyn “Billy” Florence — An actor and Mason whose travel experiences and account of an Arabian-themed event helped inspire the fraternity's Middle Eastern theme.

Additional members of the dining group contributed to the early organization, but Fleming and Florence became the names most closely attached to its institutional creation.

Arabian-Themed Inspiration

According to Shriners International's official history, Florence attended an elaborate party hosted by an Arabian diplomat while touring Europe. He was impressed by its costumes, music, atmosphere, and dramatic ceremonial style.

Florence suggested an Arabian theme for the proposed fraternity. Fleming and other organizers then created the ritual, fez, emblem, titles, costumes, salutation, and institutional name.

This account describes cultural inspiration, not legal or religious authority from an Arab government, Islamic institution, or ancient Middle Eastern order. Shriners International is a modern American Masonic fraternity.

First Meetings and Mecca Shriners

The fraternity held an early official meeting in 1871. On September 26, 1872, a meeting at Masonic Hall in New York formally established its first local chapter, Mecca Shriners.

Mecca remains the Shriners International chapter associated with New York City. The organization expanded through the chartering of additional chapters in other American cities and later in other countries.

Historical sources may refer to local bodies as temples. Current organizational language increasingly uses chapter, Shrine center, or the chapter's proper name.

Original Name and Anagram

The original name was the Ancient Arabic Order of the Nobles of the Mystic Shrine. Its initials form A.A.O.N.M.S.

Shriners International states that A.A.O.N.M.S. can be rearranged to spell “A MASON.” This wordplay reinforces the requirement that every Shriner first be a Master Mason.

The name should not be confused with A.E.A.O.N.M.S., Inc., the separate Prince Hall Shrine order. The difference of one letter reflects different institutional identities, histories, and legal organizations.

Name Changes

The fraternity's name changed as its geographic reach and public identity developed.

  • Ancient Arabic Order of the Nobles of the Mystic Shrine — Original formal name.
  • Shriners of North America — Name used during the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries when the organization was concentrated primarily in the United States, Canada, and Mexico.
  • Shriners International — Adopted by resolution in 2010 to reflect expansion beyond North America.

Older records, legal cases, property deeds, and publications may therefore identify the same fraternity under different names.

Stated Mission and Values

Shriners International describes itself as a fraternity based upon fun, fellowship, family, philanthropy, and the Masonic principles of brotherly love, relief, and truth.

Its stated institutional goals include:

  • Building men of good character.
  • Creating lifelong friendship and fraternal support.
  • Providing social, recreational, and family-oriented activity.
  • Developing leadership and self-improvement.
  • Supporting local communities.
  • Sustaining Shriners Children's as its official philanthropy.
  • Expanding international Masonic fellowship.

Relationship to Freemasonry

Shriners International is an appendant Masonic body. It is related to Freemasonry but is not a Craft lodge or grand lodge.

The organization summarizes the relationship through the statement: not every Mason is a Shriner, but every Shriner is a Mason.

A man must first become a Master Mason in a recognized lodge before petitioning a Shrine chapter. The Shrine does not confer the Entered Apprentice, Fellow Craft, or Master Mason degrees.

A Masonic grand lodge and a Shrine chapter may cooperate while retaining separate constitutions, officers, finances, discipline, buildings, and institutional authority.

Historical Eligibility Requirements

Historically, Shrine membership generally required a Mason to complete additional York Rite or Scottish Rite qualifications, such as becoming a Knights Templar or receiving the thirty-second degree.

Shriners International later removed those additional prerequisites. Current public eligibility requires Master Mason status rather than prior completion of the York Rite or Scottish Rite.

Older documents and court opinions may therefore describe requirements that no longer govern present admission.

Current Membership

Current public materials invite men of integrity, character, and compassion who are Master Masons to petition for Shrine membership.

Membership is not automatic. A Master Mason must apply through a chapter, complete the prescribed process, and remain in good standing under applicable Masonic and Shrine rules.

The fraternity publicly reports more than 195,000 members worldwide, nearly 200 chapters, and thousands of clubs and units. Membership numbers change as chapters form, consolidate, close, and recruit.

Race and Present-Day Membership

Shriners International is properly described as historically White because it developed through the historically White mainstream Masonic system and litigated against the separate Black Shrine order during segregation.

Its current public membership materials do not state that applicants must be White. They speak of men from different backgrounds, career paths, ages, and interests, while retaining the requirements of male sex and Master Mason status.

Present racial inclusion may also depend upon the admission practices and recognition relationships of the Masonic grand lodges from which candidates come. A racial history should therefore not be erased merely because current eligibility language is formally neutral.

Male Membership and Women's Participation

Shriners International is a fraternity for men. Women do not become Shriners through the same membership system.

Women participate through family programs, philanthropy, hospital support, volunteer activity, and separate partner organizations, including:

  • Daughters of the Nile.
  • Ladies' Oriental Shrine of North America.
  • Shrine Guilds of America.
  • Order of the Eastern Star.
  • Order of the Amaranth.

These organizations are not divisions of one single women's body. They have separate histories, membership rules, officers, finances, and legal identities.

Local Chapters and Shrine Centers

The basic local Shriners International organization is a chapter, historically called a Temple and commonly associated with a Shrine center.

A chapter may operate:

  • Meetings and ceremonial activity.
  • Social events and family programs.
  • Clubs and specialized units.
  • Fundraising and patient transportation.
  • Community service and local charitable activity.
  • Membership recruitment and retention.
  • Property, event venues, circuses, parades, and public programs.

Conduct by one chapter should not automatically be attributed to every chapter or to the Imperial governing body.

Clubs and Units

Shriners International reports thousands of clubs and units serving different interests, geographic areas, professions, hobbies, and ceremonial functions.

Examples may include:

  • Motorcycle and automobile units.
  • Clown units.
  • Miniature-car and parade units.
  • Flying clubs.
  • Mounted patrols.
  • Pipe, drum, band, and musical units.
  • Veterans and first-responder groups.
  • Regional Shrine clubs located far from the chapter headquarters.
  • Professional, recreational, and cultural interest groups.

A club or unit may operate under a chapter while retaining its own officers and activities. It should not be mistaken for an independent chapter or the full international fraternity.

Chapter Officers

Local leadership commonly includes:

  • Potentate — Presiding chapter officer.
  • Chief Rabban
  • Assistant Rabban
  • High Priest and Prophet
  • Oriental Guide
  • Recorder
  • Treasurer
  • Additional appointed ceremonial and administrative officers

Specific titles, powers, election practices, and terms are controlled by the fraternity's governing law and chapter bylaws.

The Imperial Divan

The Imperial Divan is the elected governing body of Shriners International. The official organization identifies twelve Imperial officers.

Imperial officers participate in governance of the fraternity and, through related board roles, Shriners Children's. The precise legal capacities in which an officer acts should be distinguished.

The senior elected officer is the Imperial Potentate. As of July 27, 2026, Shriners International's official leadership page identifies Lawrence J. “Larry” Leib as Imperial Potentate.

Current leadership must be reverified whenever this dossier is updated.

Imperial Session

The Imperial Session is the fraternity's principal annual international gathering for governance, elections, legislation, reports, education, ceremony, competitions, social events, and fellowship.

Delegates and officers consider organizational business affecting chapters, membership, finances, bylaws, programs, and leadership.

The Imperial Session is also a major public event for host cities. Its parades, conventions, hotels, vendors, and family activities can produce substantial local economic activity.

Geographic Reach

Shriners International began in New York and expanded across the United States. Chapters later formed in Canada, Mexico, Panama, Puerto Rico, the Philippines, Germany, Brazil, Bolivia, Lebanon, and other jurisdictions represented in its current directory.

The official organization describes a network of approximately 200 chapters, members on six continents, and thousands of local clubs and units.

A chapter's legal status, Masonic recognition, tax treatment, and programs may differ according to the laws and Masonic systems of its country.

The Fez

The red fez with a black tassel is the fraternity's most recognizable item of regalia. It was selected to complement the organization's invented Middle Eastern theme, pageantry, costumes, and ceremonies.

A member's fez may display his chapter, office, club, unit, or other authorized information.

Similar red fezzes are worn by members of other Shrine systems. A fez alone does not prove whether a person belongs to Shriners International, A.E.A.O.N.M.S., an independent Shrine organization, or a theatrical group.

Emblem and Symbols

The fraternity's principal emblem includes a crescent, scimitar, claws, sphinx, star, and the phrase Robur et Furor.

Shriners International's public explanation assigns the following symbolic meanings:

  • The scimitar represents the members as the fraternity's backbone.
  • The two claws represent the fraternity and its philanthropy.
  • The sphinx represents the governing body.
  • The star represents children assisted through the philanthropy.

These are modern fraternal symbols. They do not establish political authority from Egypt, Morocco, Arabia, Islam, or an ancient empire.

Orientalism and Cultural Borrowing

Shriners International's Arabian and Middle Eastern imagery was created by nineteenth-century White American entertainers and Masons. It reflects the period's fascination with an imagined “Orient” rather than an authentic representation of one Arab, Muslim, North African, Turkish, Persian, or Egyptian culture.

The fraternity's names, costumes, theatrical language, and ceremonies may be studied as examples of American Orientalism and cultural borrowing.

A Shriner does not become Arab, Muslim, Moorish, Egyptian, Moroccan, or diplomatically privileged through membership or regalia.

Early Charity

Before establishing a permanent health-care system, Shrine chapters provided assistance during epidemics, floods, hurricanes, and other emergencies. They also supported families in distress, orphanages, Masonic homes, and medical institutions.

Charity grew from a secondary expression of Masonic relief into a defining public identity of the fraternity.

Creation of the Hospital System

During the early twentieth century, members proposed a permanent national philanthropy focused on children with orthopedic conditions.

In 1922, the first Shriners hospital opened in Shreveport, Louisiana. The hospital system expanded to provide specialized pediatric orthopedic care and later added treatment for burns, spinal cord injuries, cleft lip and palate, craniofacial conditions, sports injuries, and other needs.

The health-care system is now branded Shriners Children's.

Shriners Children's

Shriners Children's is a pediatric specialty health-care system founded by Shriners International and supported as its official philanthropy.

Its mission includes:

  • Specialty pediatric care.
  • Research and development of improved treatment.
  • Education of physicians and other health-care professionals.
  • Family-centered care without regard to a family's ability to pay.

The organization currently reports 22 locations and care activity in the United States, Canada, and Mexico, supplemented by outreach and international patient programs.

Separate Legal Entities

Shriners International and Shriners Children's are closely connected but are separate entities.

The combined annual report itself explains that the two organizations are separate while deeply linked by history, mission, leadership, and values.

A fraternity fundraiser, chapter bank account, hospital donation, medical decision, employment matter, board action, or charitable solicitation should be attributed to the actual legal entity involved.

The phrase the Shriners is often too imprecise for financial, legal, employment, medical, or governance analysis.

Health-Care Governance

Shriners Children's maintains boards, executives, physicians, administrators, compliance systems, financial records, research programs, and clinical operations appropriate to a health-care organization.

Imperial officers may serve on boards connected to both the fraternity and health-care system, creating institutional linkage. Their actions must still be evaluated according to the specific board, legal entity, fiduciary duty, and authority involved.

Fraternal status does not replace medical licensing, hospital regulation, patient rights, professional standards, or applicable law.

Financial Transparency

Shriners Children's publishes annual reports, federal tax filings, combined financial statements, impact reports, and other information concerning its health-care and philanthropic operations.

The health-care system explains that multiple corporations and international entities are involved. Individual tax returns may therefore cover only part of the full system.

Financial analysis should distinguish:

  • Shriners International fraternity funds.
  • Shriners Children's health-care funds.
  • Local chapter accounts.
  • Transportation funds.
  • Hospital or location-specific foundations.
  • Independent women's organizations.
  • Event promoters, circuses, clubs, and contracted vendors.

Fundraising

Shriners raise money through donations, events, direct appeals, investments, bequests, sponsorships, circuses, raffles where lawful, golf tournaments, parades, club activity, and other local or international campaigns.

Not every event displaying the Shrine name sends all proceeds directly to Shriners Children's. Event expenses, chapter operations, transportation programs, and other purposes may be involved.

Fundraising materials should be reviewed for:

  • The exact beneficiary.
  • The percentage or amount benefiting charity.
  • The legal entity receiving funds.
  • Disclosure of expenses and vendor compensation.
  • Whether a solicitation is authorized.

Patient Transportation

Local Shriners often assist families with transportation to Shriners Children's appointments. Chapters may operate vans, coordinate drivers, support travel documentation, or assist with non-medical logistical costs.

Transportation activity is distinct from medical care. Drivers and chapters do not replace physicians, hospitals, insurers, immigration authorities, or government travel requirements.

Community and Family Programs

Shriners International emphasizes family participation and local community activity. Chapters may host festivals, parades, meals, holiday programs, veterans' events, youth activities, public education, and disaster response.

The quality and political significance of these programs vary by chapter. Local actions should be evaluated separately from international policy.

Masonic Youth Relationships

Shriners International supports Masonic youth organizations, including DeMolay International, Job's Daughters International, and the International Order of the Rainbow for Girls.

This support may include meeting space, adult volunteers, fundraising, education, transportation, event assistance, and leadership development.

Youth organizations retain separate governance and safeguarding obligations. Affiliation with the Masonic family does not erase the need for background screening, youth-protection rules, supervision, and incident reporting.

Racial Litigation Against the Black Shrine Order

White Shrine bodies pursued litigation seeking to prevent the Black Shrine order from using similar names, titles, constitutions, emblems, and regalia.

The dispute reached the United States Supreme Court in Ancient Egyptian Arabic Order v. Michaux. The Court described the parties as separate White and Black Shrine orders that had developed independently within segregated Masonic systems.

In 1929, the Court reversed a Texas injunction against the Black order, concluding that the White order's long delay and acquiescence barred the requested equitable relief.

The case is part of Shriners International's institutional racial history and should not be omitted from a complete profile.

Meaning of the 1929 Decision

The Supreme Court decision protected the Black order from the injunction at issue. It did not merge the two organizations, declare either body racially superior, or establish one as the Masonic government of the other.

The Court also rejected romantic claims that the modern Shrine orders were continuations of an ancient secret fraternity in Muslim countries. It treated them as relatively modern American organizations derived from Freemasonry.

Present Relations With Prince Hall Masonry

Relations between historically White and Prince Hall Masonic institutions have changed as grand lodges entered mutual-recognition agreements and members participated in shared events.

Shriners International has publicly highlighted shared Masonic ideals and cooperation with Prince Hall Masonic leaders. Such cooperation does not erase separate corporate identities, histories, governing bodies, or philanthropic systems.

A joint event is evidence of cooperation, not proof of merger or mutual command.

Private Society Versus Secret Society

Shriners International is a private membership fraternity with ceremonial practices, modes of recognition, internal deliberation, membership records, disciplinary processes, and other confidential matters.

Its existence is highly public. It maintains headquarters, websites, chapter directories, public leaders, annual conventions, parades, magazines, philanthropic campaigns, hospitals, financial reports, and community events.

It is therefore more accurately described as a public institution with private membership and confidential ceremonial practices than as an invisible secret organization.

Illuminati and Hidden-Control Claims

Shriners International is frequently included in conspiracy narratives concerning Freemasonry, the Illuminati, occult government, hidden finance, celebrity initiation, religious betrayal, and political control.

Documented facts include:

  • Private Masonic and Shrine ceremonies.
  • Selective membership and fraternal loyalty.
  • Members who hold influential professional and public positions.
  • Significant charitable and investment assets connected to the broader system.
  • International chapters and institutional coordination.
  • Symbols that appear mysterious to outsiders.

These facts justify analysis of power, governance, conflicts of interest, financial stewardship, and political relationships. They do not independently prove the existence of one global Illuminati command directing every Shriner.

Occult and Religious Criticism

Some Christian, Muslim, and other religious critics object to Shriners and Freemasonry because of private oaths, ritual, religious pluralism, titles, symbols, dramatized Middle Eastern imagery, and references to a Supreme Being.

Shriners generally describe the fraternity as Masonic and philanthropic rather than as a religion. Members may belong to different faith traditions.

A church or religious authority may conclude that Masonic participation is incompatible with its doctrine. That theological judgment is different from empirical proof that every Shriner worships a secret deity, practices criminal occultism, or follows one political religion.

Networking

Shriners International openly offers members a global network of relationships, mentors, social support, and fraternal assistance.

Networking may provide:

  • Friendship and mutual assistance.
  • Professional introductions.
  • Business and employment referrals.
  • Leadership opportunities.
  • Travel contacts.
  • Access to civic and Masonic leaders.
  • Organized charitable participation.
  • Support during illness, disaster, or family difficulty.

These are real institutional benefits and sources of influence. They should be mapped without assuming that every relationship is improper.

Gatekeeping Risks

A selective network can become a gatekeeping mechanism when members control employment, contracts, licensing, funding, media access, political appointments, or organizational recognition.

Potential abuses include:

  • Conditioning opportunity upon Masonic or Shrine loyalty.
  • Favoring members without disclosure.
  • Protecting members from discipline or investigation.
  • Retaliating against outsiders or whistleblowers.
  • Using public office to benefit private associates.
  • Excluding independent organizations from resources.

A gatekeeping allegation requires evidence identifying the person, authority, decision, benefit, excluded party, relationship, and organizational knowledge.

Political Influence

Shriners International is not a political party, but its members may include elected officials, judges, business leaders, military officers, clergy members, law-enforcement personnel, educators, and civic leaders.

Chapters and leaders may communicate with governments concerning conventions, charitable regulation, health care, transportation, taxation, community programs, and institutional interests.

The political activity of an individual Shriner should not automatically be attributed to the fraternity. Institutional attribution requires evidence of authorization, funding, instruction, endorsement, or coordinated action.

Potential Conflicts of Interest

Conflicts may arise when Shrine relationships intersect with:

  • Hospital governance and contracting.
  • Medical employment and referrals.
  • Government grants and regulation.
  • Law enforcement, courts, and prosecution.
  • Public procurement and licensing.
  • Real estate and chapter property.
  • Charitable solicitation and vendor arrangements.
  • Insurance, investments, and fiduciary management.

The proper question is not merely whether two people are Shriners, but whether the undisclosed relationship materially affected a decision or duty.

Misconduct and Attribution

Allegations involving hazing, alcohol, harassment, discrimination, financial misuse, unsafe events, abuse, fraud, or retaliation must be assigned to the correct actor.

Possible actors include:

  • An individual member.
  • A club or unit.
  • A local chapter.
  • An Imperial officer or committee.
  • Shriners International as a legal entity.
  • Shriners Children's or a health-care location.
  • An independent partner organization.
  • A contractor, vendor, or event promoter.

Organizational responsibility becomes stronger when leaders authorized, funded, knew of, concealed, tolerated, or failed to correct conduct within their duties.

Charitable Reputation and Scrutiny

Shriners Children's has given the fraternity an unusually strong public reputation. Pediatric treatment and family assistance are concrete, documented benefits.

A valuable philanthropy does not make every fraternal decision immune from scrutiny. Nor should unrelated misconduct by an individual automatically be used to discredit medical personnel, patients, or the entire health-care system.

The fraternity and philanthropy should each be judged according to their actual conduct, governance, finances, outcomes, and legal duties.

Institutional Responsibility Test

  1. Identify the exact actor. Determine whether the actor is a member, unit, club, chapter, Imperial body, hospital, foundation, partner organization, contractor, or vendor.
  2. Verify affiliation. Determine whether the person belongs to Shriners International, the Prince Hall Shriners, or another similarly named organization.
  3. Identify the authority used. Separate Shrine office from Masonic, governmental, medical, business, religious, or professional office.
  4. Identify the legal entity. Determine whether the matter concerns the fraternity, health-care system, local chapter, or another corporation.
  5. Trace resources. Determine whose funds, property, staff, records, vehicles, donor lists, or platform supported the action.
  6. Locate authorization. Seek minutes, resolutions, policies, contracts, directives, public statements, and credible testimony.
  7. Test knowledge and response. Determine whether leaders knew, approved, concealed, ignored, investigated, or corrected the conduct.
  8. Separate association from command. Shared membership proves a relationship, not automatic institutional direction.
  9. Assign responsibility proportionately. Attribute conduct only to the persons and institutions supported by evidence.

Evidence Classification

  • Documented fact: Supported by official records, court decisions, governing documents, financial statements, archives, or corroborated reporting.
  • Official claim: A position stated by Shriners International, Shriners Children's, a chapter, or another identified institution.
  • Documented association: Evidence of membership, office, attendance, donation, correspondence, or organizational connection.
  • Testimony: A witness account evaluated for specificity, credibility, access, bias, and corroboration.
  • Circumstantial evidence: Facts supporting an inference without directly proving intent or command.
  • Reasonable inference: An analytical conclusion explicitly separated from verified fact.
  • Speculation: A possibility lacking adequate evidence.
  • Brand conflation: Treating the fraternity, philanthropy, Prince Hall Shriners, chapters, clubs, hospitals, and partner organizations as one actor without proof.

Preliminary Alignment Assessment

Shriners International is an externally governed, historically White Masonic institution with significant charitable capacity, international reach, private membership networks, and a highly visible pediatric health-care system.

Its medical philanthropy and community programs can produce direct public benefit, including benefit to Black children and families. Its racial history, relationship to mainstream Freemasonry, organizational power, financial resources, and historical conflict with the Prince Hall Shriners warrant continuing scrutiny.

The appropriate initial classification is: historically exclusionary; presently public-benefit producing; externally controlled relative to the Malone Network; potentially useful; influence-bearing; requires entity-specific and conduct-specific verification.

Responsible Analysis

Shriners International should not be reduced either to harmless men in fezzes or to an all-controlling occult conspiracy.

It is a real institution with history, hierarchy, private ritual, public charity, substantial networks, recognizable symbols, and measurable influence.

Its hospital system deserves recognition for documented pediatric care. Its racial history, institutional relationships, finances, and member conduct remain proper subjects of investigation.

A rigorous dossier must distinguish charity from fraternity, historical fact from official mythology, membership from command, and association from institutional responsibility.

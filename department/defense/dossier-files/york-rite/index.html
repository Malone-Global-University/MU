York Rite

Overview

York Rite Freemasonry, also called the American Rite in the United States, is a family of Masonic degrees and separately governed organizations generally entered after a man becomes a Master Mason.

York Rite is not one corporation, one national command, or one universal hierarchy. In the American system, its three principal appendant bodies are:

  1. Chapter of Royal Arch Masons
  2. Council of Royal and Select Masters, or Cryptic Masons
  3. Commandery of Knights Templar

Each body has its own degrees, officers, finances, membership records, charters, governing authorities, charities, and disciplinary processes. They cooperate as the York Rite family but do not automatically form one sovereign institution.

Separate mainstream and Prince Hall York Rite systems exist. Additional organizations use terms such as Ancient York Rite, Free and Accepted Ancient York Rite Masons, or National Grand York Rite. Similar language does not establish common authority or recognition.

York Rite Is an Umbrella Term

The term York Rite creates a misleading appearance of one unified organization. It is more accurately understood as an umbrella describing related but independently governed Masonic systems.

A man may belong to:

  • A Craft lodge but no York Rite body.
  • A Royal Arch Chapter but no Cryptic Council or Commandery.
  • A Chapter and Council but not a Commandery.
  • All three principal York Rite bodies.
  • Mainstream bodies, Prince Hall bodies, or another Masonic system depending on his lodge affiliation and recognition.

Membership in one York Rite body does not prove membership in all others.

Why It Is Called York Rite

The name refers to the English city of York, which holds an important place in Masonic legends and historical traditions concerning early English Masonry.

The American York Rite did not emerge as one intact medieval system directly transferred from York. Its degrees and governing bodies developed through British, European, colonial, and American Masonic practice and were assembled into their present relationships over time.

The name is therefore traditional and symbolic. It should not be treated as proof that every degree originated in York or that the modern system is governed from the city.

American Rite

Some Masonic sources describe American Rite as the more technically accurate name because the familiar Chapter–Council–Commandery sequence developed principally in North America.

Related degrees exist outside the United States, but they may be organized differently. A body called Royal Arch, Royal and Select Masters, or Knights Templar in another country may not belong to one structure labeled York Rite.

International comparisons must therefore examine the constitution and recognition of each actual governing body.

Relationship to the Craft Lodge

York Rite bodies are appendant to Craft Freemasonry. A man ordinarily must first receive the three Craft degrees:

  1. Entered Apprentice
  2. Fellow Craft
  3. Master Mason

The Craft lodge and its grand lodge remain separate from the York Rite bodies. York Rite membership does not make a Mason the superior of other Master Masons in lodge government.

A grand lodge may recognize, regulate contact with, or withdraw recognition from appendant bodies within its jurisdiction. Exact authority differs by Masonic constitution.

Chapter of Royal Arch Masons

The Chapter is the first principal York Rite appendant body in the American sequence. Its members are commonly called Companions.

Royal Arch Masonry develops themes associated with the loss, preservation, recovery, and meaning of knowledge connected to the Master Mason tradition. Its ceremonies draw upon biblical narratives concerning King Solomon's Temple, exile, return, rebuilding, and the recovery of sacred objects or words.

A local body is called a Chapter. The presiding officer is generally the High Priest.

Royal Arch Degrees

In much of the United States, a Royal Arch Chapter confers:

  1. Mark Master
  2. Past Master, or Virtual Past Master
  3. Most Excellent Master
  4. Royal Arch Mason

Degree order, titles, and ritual details vary by jurisdiction. In some systems, degrees that Americans associate with a Chapter are governed separately or arranged differently.

The Royal Arch degree is often presented as completing or expanding the symbolic narrative left unresolved in the Master Mason degree.

Royal Arch Governance

Local Chapters are usually governed by state or national Grand Chapters.

The General Grand Chapter of Royal Arch Masons International was organized on October 24, 1797. It coordinates and governs affiliated Capitular jurisdictions under its constitution.

Not every Royal Arch jurisdiction is necessarily subordinate to the General Grand Chapter. Some Grand Chapters possess independent histories or different recognition relationships.

The General Grand Chapter should therefore not be described automatically as the universal government of all Royal Arch Masonry.

Royal Arch Symbols

Common Royal Arch symbols include:

  • The Triple Tau.
  • The keystone.
  • The arch.
  • The equilateral or interlaced triangle.
  • Temple and rebuilding imagery.
  • Red regalia in many jurisdictions.

Symbols should be interpreted according to the body using them. Similar emblems may appear in mainstream, Prince Hall, foreign, independent, or unrecognized Masonic systems.

Council of Royal and Select Masters

The second principal body is the Council of Royal and Select Masters, commonly called Cryptic Masonry.

The name Cryptic refers to degree narratives involving a hidden vault or crypt beneath King Solomon's Temple. The degrees expand themes of preservation, secrecy, fidelity, loss, and the transmission of sacred knowledge.

Members are commonly called Companions. A local body is called a Council, and its presiding officer is generally the Illustrious Master or Thrice Illustrious Master, depending on jurisdictional usage.

Cryptic Degrees

The principal American Cryptic degrees are:

  1. Royal Master
  2. Select Master
  3. Super Excellent Master

The Super Excellent Master may be treated differently among jurisdictions and is not always required in the same way as the Royal and Select Master degrees.

Some jurisdictions confer Cryptic degrees within the Royal Arch system rather than through a separately organized Council.

Cryptic Governance

Local Councils are usually governed by jurisdictional Grand Councils.

The General Grand Council of Cryptic Masons International developed from efforts to coordinate state Grand Councils. Its constitution was sufficiently ratified for the organization to become operative by 1881.

As with Royal Arch Masonry, not every Cryptic jurisdiction is necessarily subordinate to the General Grand Council.

Commandery of Knights Templar

The third principal American York Rite body is the Commandery of Knights Templar.

Its ceremonies move from Temple-centered Masonic narratives into expressly Christian chivalric themes. A local body is called a Commandery, and its members are called Sir Knights.

The presiding local officer is the Eminent Commander.

Commandery Orders

The principal American Commandery sequence generally includes:

  1. Illustrious Order of the Red Cross
  2. Order of Malta
  3. Order of the Temple

Some systems divide or supplement these ceremonies differently.

The Order of the Temple is commonly described by York Rite bodies as the culmination of the American York Rite journey.

Christian Requirement

Mainstream United States Knights Templar membership requires a candidate to profess belief in the Christian religion.

This requirement applies to the Commandery. It should not be transferred automatically to Royal Arch Chapters, Cryptic Councils, Craft lodges, or all international bodies bearing similar names.

The Christian qualification makes the Commandery more religiously specific than much of mainstream Craft Freemasonry, which ordinarily requires belief in a Supreme Being without prescribing one religion.

Grand Encampment

The Grand Encampment of Knights Templar of the United States of America was formed in 1816 by delegates associated with early Grand Commandery structures.

It governs affiliated Grand Commanderies and subordinate Commanderies under its constitution, statutes, disciplinary rules, and triennial conclaves.

The Grand Encampment is not the government of medieval European Templars, Prince Hall Knights Templar, every international Templar organization, or all Christian chivalric orders.

Modern Masonic Templars Versus Medieval Templars

Medieval Knights Templar were a Catholic military-religious order created during the Crusading era and suppressed in the early fourteenth century.

Modern Masonic Knights Templar use the historical Templars as a source of symbolism, moral instruction, ritual drama, uniforms, and chivalric ideals.

The Grand Encampment's own educational material states that the modern fraternity does not claim direct descent from the medieval order.

Similar names and symbols do not establish legal, genealogical, ecclesiastical, military, or property succession.

Uniforms and Public Display

Knights Templar Commanderies are more visibly uniformed than many other Masonic bodies. Members may appear in formal chapeaux, swords, crosses, military-style coats, gloves, sashes, and other regulated regalia.

Commanderies may conduct:

  • Public processions.
  • Easter observances.
  • Funeral escorts.
  • Honor guards.
  • Church services.
  • Drill competitions.
  • Memorial ceremonies.

Uniformed ceremony does not make the Commandery a military unit, police organization, or government-recognized order of knighthood.

State York Rite Systems

In many states, the Grand Chapter, Grand Council, and Grand Commandery hold coordinated annual meetings or operate through a shared administrative office.

This coordination can make them appear to be one organization. Shared websites, buildings, calendars, officers, and conventions do not erase their separate charters and authority.

A document titled “Grand York Rite” may be an administrative consortium rather than a sovereign body over all three systems.

Jurisdictional Variations

York Rite structure varies. Examples include:

  • Cryptic degrees administered through Royal Arch Chapters in some jurisdictions.
  • Independent Grand Chapters or Grand Councils not affiliated with the corresponding general body.
  • Different degree orders or optional ceremonies.
  • Different membership prerequisites.
  • Different recognition relationships with Craft grand lodges.
  • Different uses of Chapter, Preceptory, Priory, Encampment, or Commandery terminology.

No claim about York Rite governance should be generalized globally without examining the relevant constitution.

Membership Path

A common American membership path is:

  1. Become a Master Mason in a recognized Craft lodge.
  2. Petition and complete the Royal Arch Chapter degrees.
  3. Petition a Cryptic Council, where separately organized.
  4. Petition a Commandery and satisfy its Christian profession requirement.

Some candidates join the Chapter without proceeding further. Others receive multiple bodies' degrees during a festival or coordinated class.

Degree Numbers and Rank Confusion

York Rite degrees are not normally presented as one continuous numerical ladder comparable to the Scottish Rite's numbered degree system.

A Knight Templar is not automatically the commanding officer of Royal Arch Masons, Cryptic Masons, Craft Masons, or Scottish Rite members.

Office, jurisdiction, charter authority, and organizational law determine actual command. Ceremonial advancement does not create universal Masonic rank.

York Rite and Scottish Rite

York Rite and Scottish Rite are separate systems through which Master Masons may pursue additional degrees and organizations.

They are not automatically rivals, and many men belong to both.

The two systems differ in degree structure, governing bodies, themes, terminology, and jurisdiction. Neither automatically commands the other.

The popular image of York Rite and Scottish Rite as two opposing supreme factions controlling all Freemasonry is not established by their formal structures.

Allied and Invitational Bodies

York Rite members may also join invitational or allied Masonic organizations, including bodies such as:

  • York Rite Sovereign College of North America.
  • Knights of the York Cross of Honour.
  • Allied Masonic Degrees.
  • Red Cross of Constantine.
  • Holy Royal Arch Knight Templar Priests.
  • Societas Rosicruciana bodies where eligibility requirements are met.

These organizations are not automatically part of the three principal York Rite governing chains. Invitation, honor, or shared eligibility does not make them departments of one central York Rite government.

Women's and Family-Related Bodies

Mainstream York Rite includes relationships with women through family, charitable, and separate Masonic-affiliated organizations.

The Social Order of the Beauceant is historically associated with wives and widows of Knights Templar. Other women may participate through Eastern Star, Amaranth, youth organizations, or local support programs.

These bodies have separate constitutions and should not be treated as one women's branch of York Rite.

Royal Arch Research Assistance

Royal Arch Research Assistance, commonly abbreviated R.A.R.A., was established in 1974.

It supports research concerning Central Auditory Processing Disorder and related auditory challenges.

The charity should be distinguished from the General Grand Chapter, state Grand Chapters, and local Chapters when evaluating finances, grants, claims, or legal responsibility.

Cryptic Masons Medical Research Foundation

The Cryptic Masons Medical Research Foundation, abbreviated CMMRF, supports medical research associated especially with cardiovascular and vascular science.

It is a charitable institution connected to Cryptic Masonry but is not identical to every Council, Grand Council, or the General Grand Council.

Its annual reports and research partnerships provide sources for evaluating actual charitable output.

Knights Templar Charities

Major charitable programs associated with the Grand Encampment include:

  • Knights Templar Eye Foundation
  • Knights Templar Educational Foundation
  • Holy Land Pilgrimage program for Christian ministers

The Eye Foundation historically assisted people facing loss of sight and now emphasizes research, grants, and pediatric ophthalmology.

These charitable entities and programs must be distinguished from the Grand Encampment, state Grand Commanderies, and local Commanderies.

Charitable Reputation and Verification

York Rite bodies conduct legitimate charitable and educational activity. Public benefit should be recognized where supported by grants, reports, recipients, institutional partners, or measurable outcomes.

Charitable reputation does not make every member or governing decision immune from scrutiny.

Conversely, misconduct by one member should not automatically be attributed to a medical foundation, research beneficiary, or every York Rite body.

Prince Hall York Rite

Prince Hall York Rite is the parallel Black Masonic York Rite tradition operating within Prince Hall Freemasonry.

Its principal bodies likewise include:

  • Holy Royal Arch Chapters.
  • Royal and Select Master Councils.
  • Knights Templar Commanderies.

Prince Hall bodies possess their own charters, jurisdictional authorities, conferences, histories, officers, membership rolls, and recognition relationships.

They are not local racial subdivisions of the mainstream General Grand Chapter, General Grand Council, or Grand Encampment.

Prince Hall Royal Arch Masonry

The General Conference of Grand Chapters Holy Royal Arch Masons, Prince Hall Affiliation is a cooperative conference within the Prince Hall York Rite family.

Prince Hall Grand Chapters remain jurisdictional institutions. A conference relationship should not automatically be described as universal national command unless its constitution grants that authority.

Historical accounts identify the Prince Hall Grand Chapter of Pennsylvania, organized in 1820, as an early foundational body in Black Royal Arch Masonry.

Prince Hall Cryptic Masonry

Prince Hall Royal and Select Master Councils operate through jurisdictional Grand Councils and cooperative structures such as the General Conference of Grand Councils Royal and Select Masters, Prince Hall Affiliation.

Their degree names may resemble mainstream Cryptic Masonry, but governing authority and recognition must be traced through Prince Hall charters and jurisdictional records.

Prince Hall Knights Templar

Prince Hall Knights Templar Commanderies are organized through separate Grand Commanderies and the Grand Encampment of Knights Templar Masons, Prince Hall Affiliation.

The Prince Hall Grand Encampment identifies itself as the national governing body of its Christian-oriented chivalric branch and traces its formal organization to 1926.

It is not subordinate to the mainstream Grand Encampment of Knights Templar of the United States merely because both use Commandery and Templar terminology.

Heroines of Jericho

Prince Hall Royal Arch Masonry includes a related women's tradition known as the Heroines of Jericho.

Local Courts and Grand Courts may operate in association with Prince Hall Royal Arch Masons while retaining their own officers, membership, proceedings, finances, and responsibilities.

The Heroines of Jericho should not be confused with mainstream Royal Arch Chapters, Order of the Eastern Star, Imperial Court Daughters, or every Masonic women's organization.

Prince Hall Affiliation Versus Prince Hall Origin

York Rite identity must also distinguish Prince Hall Affiliation from Prince Hall Origin or National Compact systems.

Organizations using names such as Free and Accepted Ancient York Rite Masons may claim authority through National Compact or another lineage rejected by PHA and mainstream bodies.

Their legal existence and their internal claim of legitimacy are separate from recognition by other Masonic jurisdictions.

Ancient York Rite Brand Confusion

The words Ancient York Rite may refer to:

  • A traditional description of Craft or York Masonry.
  • A phrase in the legal name of a particular grand lodge.
  • A Prince Hall Origin or National Compact organization.
  • An independent or unrecognized Masonic body.
  • A historical ritual claim rather than one corporation.

The phrase alone does not establish affiliation with mainstream York Rite, Prince Hall Affiliation, or a recognized grand lodge.

Recognition and Regularity

Masonic recognition determines whether governing bodies permit official visitation, communication, or institutional relations.

Recognition is relational. There is no government agency that licenses all York Rite bodies worldwide.

An organization may be legally incorporated while remaining unrecognized by a particular Masonic authority. Terms such as regular, irregular, and clandestine are primarily internal Masonic classifications rather than automatic criminal judgments.

Racial History

Mainstream York Rite developed within White-controlled American Freemasonry during slavery, segregation, and Jim Crow.

Black Masons built separate Prince Hall York Rite bodies because mainstream institutions denied equal admission or recognition.

Mutual recognition between mainstream and Prince Hall grand lodges has expanded, but historical exclusion and present jurisdictional separation remain relevant.

Current racial neutrality in some published membership language should not be used to erase the system's institutional history.

Religion and Theological Criticism

Religious critics object to York Rite practices for reasons including:

  • Private oaths and ceremonies.
  • Use of scripture in ritual drama.
  • Claims to preserve or recover sacred knowledge.
  • Christian knighthood ceremonies in Commandery.
  • Religious pluralism in other Masonic bodies.
  • Titles and symbols that resemble religious offices.

York Rite Masons generally describe the system as fraternal moral instruction, not a church or substitute religion.

A religious body's doctrinal judgment should be distinguished from empirical claims of criminal occult activity or political conspiracy.

Private Ritual and Secrecy

York Rite bodies conduct confidential rituals, membership deliberations, modes of recognition, disciplinary proceedings, and internal communications.

Their existence, governing names, buildings, public officers, charities, conventions, degree titles, and many historical records are public.

York Rite is therefore better described as a family of public institutions with private membership and confidential ceremonial practices than as one invisible secret government.

Illuminati and Hidden-Hierarchy Claims

York Rite bodies frequently appear in conspiracy literature as:

  • A hidden Christian military wing of Freemasonry.
  • A rival or partner of the Scottish Rite controlling government.
  • A modern continuation of the medieval Templars.
  • A repository of secret political, financial, or religious authority.
  • A pathway into the Illuminati or an inner Masonic government.

Evidence supports the existence of selective membership, private ritual, interlocking Masonic affiliations, influential members, coordinated governance, international relations, and charitable assets.

Those facts justify investigation of influence and conflicts of interest. They do not independently prove a single hidden command controlling all York Rite bodies, governments, churches, banks, or political movements.

Networking and Leadership Pipelines

York Rite membership can produce relationships among professionals, clergy, military veterans, public officials, business owners, judges, law-enforcement personnel, educators, and other Masonic leaders.

The system provides:

  • Officer training and parliamentary experience.
  • Mentorship and fraternal support.
  • Professional and social introductions.
  • Access to charitable and civic projects.
  • Travel connections across jurisdictions.
  • Recognition and honors within Masonic networks.

Networking is not automatically improper. It becomes an accountability issue when private loyalty affects public, fiduciary, employment, regulatory, or legal duties.

Gatekeeping Risks

York Rite networks may become gatekeeping systems where members control:

  • Employment or professional advancement.
  • Government contracts or appointments.
  • Masonic recognition and access.
  • Church, charitable, or civic platforms.
  • Property, meeting space, or donor relationships.
  • Legal, law-enforcement, or disciplinary decisions.

A gatekeeping allegation should identify the decision-maker, office, relationship, benefit, excluded party, and evidence of coordination.

Potential Conflicts of Interest

Potential conflicts arise when York Rite membership overlaps with:

  • Courts and prosecution.
  • Police and military institutions.
  • Public procurement and licensing.
  • Health and research charities.
  • Religious leadership.
  • Nonprofit boards and foundations.
  • Real estate and Masonic property.
  • Employment, promotion, or discipline.

The relevant question is whether an undisclosed fraternal relationship materially affected a duty or decision.

Institutional Responsibility Test

  1. Identify the exact body. Name the lodge, Chapter, Council, Commandery, Grand Chapter, Grand Council, Grand Commandery, general body, charity, or allied organization.
  2. Identify the Masonic system. Determine whether it is mainstream, Prince Hall Affiliated, Prince Hall Origin, foreign, independent, or disputed.
  3. Verify membership and office. Establish the person's actual body, jurisdiction, dates, and authority.
  4. Separate degree from command. Holding a degree does not establish governing power.
  5. Trace resources. Determine whose funds, property, staff, mailing lists, vehicles, or records supported the act.
  6. Locate authorization. Seek minutes, constitutions, directives, votes, contracts, correspondence, and credible testimony.
  7. Test knowledge and response. Determine whether leadership approved, knew, concealed, ignored, investigated, or corrected the conduct.
  8. Assign responsibility proportionately. Attribute conduct only to the actors and institutions supported by evidence.

Proximity to the Malone Network

York Rite has no documented institutional alignment with or subordination to the Malone Network.

Its proximity is potentially significant because Masonic members may operate in the same civic, educational, religious, legal, military, political, philanthropic, and professional environments as Malone personnel.

Proximity should be rated by actual contact:

  • Remote — No identified overlap or access.
  • Adjacent — Shared community, profession, issue, or institutional territory.
  • Connected — Identified relationships with Malone personnel, partners, or audiences.
  • Embedded — Members hold access inside Malone programs, communications, facilities, or leadership circles.
  • Interlocked — Significant shared personnel, funding, property, or operational dependence.

No body should be classified as embedded or interlocked without specific evidence.

Hostility Assessment

York Rite's separate existence, private ritual, Masonic loyalty, Christian Commandery identity, or lack of Malone alignment does not independently prove hostility.

Hostility would require documented conduct such as:

  • Targeted infiltration of Malone institutions.
  • Coordinated obstruction of funding, partnerships, employment, or access.
  • Deception concerning affiliation or authority.
  • Retaliation against Malone personnel or supporters.
  • Capture or redirection of Malone programs.
  • Organized reputational, legal, political, or physical attack.

The current default classification is external and untrusted, not proven hostile.

Controlled Opposition Assessment

York Rite does not ordinarily present itself as a Black liberation movement, Malone-aligned resistance organization, or political opposition group.

The category controlled opposition is therefore not generally applicable to the entire York Rite system.

A particular Masonic-led civic organization or public figure could still be evaluated for controlled-opposition behavior if evidence shows that it claims to resist a power structure while being financed, directed, constrained, or repeatedly used by that structure.

Confusion, compromise, social conservatism, institutional loyalty, or poor strategy do not by themselves prove external control.

Confusion and Fragmentation Assessment

Public misunderstanding of York Rite is high because:

  • It is described as one Rite despite separate governing bodies.
  • Mainstream and Prince Hall systems use similar names.
  • Grand bodies and general bodies are easily confused.
  • Modern Knights Templar are conflated with medieval Templars.
  • Degree advancement is mistaken for universal rank.
  • Ancient York Rite labels are used by competing organizations.

York Rite itself is decentralized rather than necessarily confused. Some confusion arises from outsiders, while some results from complex and inconsistent institutional branding.

Risk Escalation Indicators

York Rite risk to Malone would increase if evidence establishes:

  • Undisclosed Masonic bloc activity inside Malone leadership.
  • Use of Malone resources for outside Masonic purposes.
  • Pressure to place fraternal loyalty above Malone Doctrine.
  • Coordinated recruitment of Malone officers or supporters.
  • Suppression of investigations involving Masonic associates.
  • Shared donors or partners conditioned upon Masonic access.
  • False representation that York Rite speaks for or controls Malone.
  • Documented hostile communication, retaliation, or sabotage.

Evidence Classification

  • Documented fact: Supported by charters, constitutions, proceedings, court records, official publications, financial reports, or corroborated archives.
  • Official claim: A statement attributed to a named York Rite governing body or related organization.
  • Documented association: Verified membership, office, attendance, correspondence, donation, or institutional connection.
  • Testimony: A witness account evaluated for access, specificity, credibility, bias, and corroboration.
  • Circumstantial evidence: Facts supporting an inference without directly proving command or intent.
  • Reasonable inference: An analytical conclusion clearly separated from established fact.
  • Speculation: A possibility lacking adequate supporting evidence.
  • Brand conflation: Treating separate Chapters, Councils, Commanderies, grand bodies, Prince Hall bodies, or charities as one actor.

Preliminary Alignment Assessment

York Rite institutions are not aligned with, governed by, or accountable to the Malone Network.

Some York Rite bodies and members may perform useful charitable, civic, educational, religious, or professional work. Tactical cooperation may be possible where conduct and objectives are compatible.

The system's private loyalties, external governance, Masonic recognition rules, institutional longevity, professional networks, racial history, and complex branding justify continued vetting.

The appropriate preliminary classification is: external; untrusted by default; structurally fragmented; potentially useful in limited contexts; influence-bearing; not proven hostile or controlled opposition; requires body-specific, jurisdiction-specific, and conduct-specific verification.

Responsible Analysis

York Rite should not be treated as one secret organization merely because its bodies share symbols, degrees, members, buildings, or conventions.

It should also not be treated as harmless merely because it describes itself as moral, charitable, and fraternal.

The correct method is to identify the exact body, system, jurisdiction, leader, office, resource, authorization, and conduct before assigning responsibility.

Transparency requires exposing structure accurately. Accountability requires evidence linking that structure to particular actions.

Malone Primer

The Seven Strategic Problems

A diagnostic framework for identifying the forces that obstruct African American security, development, representation, and durable institutional power.

Introduction

The Seven Strategic Problems organize the principal conditions that Malone Doctrine was developed to confront. The framework gives the Malone Primer a stable method for classifying historical evidence, current conditions, institutional failures, political behavior, public narratives, and proposed remedies.

These categories are diagnostic containers rather than claims that every event has one cause or that every institution, official, organization, or individual bears equal responsibility. Each conclusion must identify the responsible actor, conduct, authority, evidence, affected population, and practical consequence.

Relationship Among the Problems

Government oppression is the central historical thesis of the framework. Lack of organization is the first operational priority because organization creates the capacity to investigate the other problems, preserve institutional memory, coordinate action, accumulate resources, develop leadership, and build durable counterpower.

Oppression creates disadvantage; institutions reproduce it; social racism rationalizes it; political misalignment fails to correct it; misdirection diverts the response; misrepresentation obscures legitimate interests; and weak organization prevents the accumulation of durable counterpower.

The Seven Problem Areas

  1. Government Oppression

    Examines the use of law, public policy, administration, policing, surveillance, dispossession, exclusion, violence, and other governmental powers to constrain African American security, liberty, property, opportunity, and institutional development.

  2. Institutional Discrimination

    Examines how public and private institutions can reproduce disadvantage through rules, discretion, access barriers, resource allocation, procedural delay, administrative attrition, and unequal standards of treatment.

  3. Social Racism

    Examines the stereotypes, customs, narratives, incentives, and informal social practices that normalize unequal treatment, rationalize structural disadvantage, and weaken public resistance to discriminatory conduct.

  4. Political Misalignment

    Examines political loyalty, coalition dependency, weak bargaining power, unmeasured representation, and support for parties, candidates, or programs that do not reliably advance defined African American interests.

  5. Strategic Misdirection and Arrested Development

    Examines how attention, leadership, money, emotion, and collective effort can be diverted toward symbolic disputes, performative activity, external agendas, and recurring reaction instead of sustained human and institutional development.

  6. Misrepresentation and Representative Capture

    Examines how unauthorized, dependent, unaccountable, or externally selected representatives can claim to speak for African Americans, distort legitimate interests, redirect resources, and control public interpretation without a valid mandate.

  7. Lack of Organization

    Examines how fragmentation and weak institutional continuity prevent the accumulation of capital, knowledge, records, leadership, expertise, coordinated action, and durable counterpower. It is the framework's central operational problem and the first condition that Malone institutions are intended to correct.

Analytical Standard

Work developed under this framework should distinguish documented fact, attributed claim, testimony, inference, hypothesis, and speculation. Evidence should support internal judgment, protection, planning, recordkeeping, and action; analysis should not depend on obtaining agreement from hostile or indifferent audiences before reasonable defensive measures may be considered.

Each study should identify the problem container being examined, explain its relationship to the other six problems, define the evidence required to support its conclusions, and connect diagnosis to an appropriate doctrinal, institutional, political, educational, charitable, economic, or defensive response.

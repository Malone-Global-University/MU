Beginner Practice Labs / Lab 6

## Lesson introduction

A [[digital-calendar|digital calendar]] helps you reserve time for appointments, classes, projects, and other commitments. A handwritten note that says "study this week" can be easy to overlook. A correctly scheduled [[calendar-event|calendar event]] records *when* something is supposed to happen, *how long* it is expected to last, and any information needed to prepare for it. Scheduling is a practical computer skill because it turns a plan into an entry you can locate, review, and update.

This is the **sixth MGU Beginner Practice Lab**. You have already practiced organizing files, producing documents and spreadsheets, creating a presentation, and sending a carefully checked email. In this lab you will create and revise a **fictional, private planning appointment**, rather than contact a real organization or invite another person. You will select a date, choose the correct [[calendar-account|calendar account]], enter an unambiguous title, set a start and end time, add a [[reminder|reminder]], save the event, find it again, and change its time without accidentally creating a duplicate.

The main walkthrough uses **Google Calendar in a desktop browser**. Equivalent approaches for **Outlook**, **Apple Calendar**, and mobile devices appear later. Product names and menu positions may vary by version, account, and screen size. You do not need a paid plan, a workplace account, an actual meeting, a video-conferencing subscription, or permission to invite anyone. If no authorized calendar account is available, use the included paper-based or text-based planning alternative and distinguish the simulation from a completed digital task.

> **Lab objective:** Create a **private, one-time event** titled `MGU Practice Lab 6 - Planning Session` on a personal calendar you control, for a date **7 to 21 days from today**. First schedule it for **2:00–2:45 p.m. in your calendar's local time zone**, add a **30-minute advance reminder** if supported, then edit the *same event* to **2:30–3:15 p.m.** Finally, reopen it and verify its saved details. **Do not add guests, meeting links, or real addresses.**

**Level:** Beginner · **Suggested study and lab time:** 90–120 minutes · **Recommended prior work:** Beginner Practice Labs 1–5 · **Equipment:** One authorized digital calendar account on a computer or mobile device; paper alternative available · **Safety:** Use a personal or specifically approved practice calendar; never invite people or alter shared calendars without consent.

### Learning objectives

By the end of the lesson, you should be able to:

1. Identify an event's [[event-title|title]], [[event-date|date]], [[start-time|start time]], [[end-time|end time]], [[duration|duration]], and [[time-zone|time zone]].
2. Distinguish a personal [[appointment|appointment]] from an event with [[attendee|attendees]] and describe when invitations can notify other people.
3. Check which calendar and account will own a new event, including whether it is private or part of a shared workspace.
4. Create the assigned 45-minute practice event and verify the exact details after saving.
5. Set a sensible notification or reminder, while recognizing that a reminder is not a guarantee of delivery.
6. Change the event time by editing the existing event and ensure that no duplicate was created.
7. Explain when all-day entries, recurring series, and time-zone choices are appropriate.
8. Recognize common problems involving wrong dates, unavailable calendars, accidental guests, time-zone differences, and missing notifications.
9. Complete the six guided activities and demonstrate the final event against a clear assessment rubric.

### Materials and safety preparation

- Choose **one account and calendar you own** or that an instructor explicitly authorized for practice. A school or employer calendar may be monitored or shared; do not treat it as private merely because it appears under your login.
- Have access to the calendar app and know the account already signed in. You do **not** have to register an account for this exercise.
- Keep a note of the date you choose. It must be a date 7–21 days in the future **relative to the day you are performing the lab**, not relative to the lesson publication date.
- Use the fictional title and description below. Do not include any real person's email address, phone number, street address, medical appointment, or private work information.
- If a class session does not permit actual calendar editing, use the offline worksheet in [Activity 1](#7-guided-practice-build-check-and-revise-an-event) and mark the software task as not attempted, not completed.

## 1. What is a calendar event?

A [[calendar-event|calendar event]] is a dated entry saved in a calendar system. At minimum, most events identify what is happening and the time or date it occupies. Other fields can store a place, explanation, notification, expected participants, or repeated schedule. The purpose is not to decorate the calendar; it is to keep a dependable record of when you intend to do something.

Imagine that you plan to spend 45 minutes organizing study materials. You could write the plan on a loose note, but that note may not say which Tuesday, whether the task begins at 2 p.m. or 3 p.m., or what reminder you want. A scheduled event can retain those details together, and you can check them later rather than relying entirely on memory.

### Calendar versus event versus reminder

A [[calendar|calendar]] is the collection or display that holds scheduled entries. The event is one item placed in it. A [[reminder|reminder]] or alert is a prompt connected to a time, potentially before the event begins. The reminder is not the event itself. In some systems a separate [[task|task]] may track work to be done and whether it is complete; tasks and events may appear on the same calendar but behave differently.

| Item | Main purpose | Example in this lab |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calendar | Organizes dated entries | Your approved personal calendar |
| Event | Reserves time on a date | Practice planning session |
| Reminder | Prompts you before an event | Alert 30 minutes before start |
| Task | Tracks an action to complete | Review the lesson checklist |
| Invitation | Asks other people to attend | Not used in the mandatory lab |

A calendar entry is not proof that an activity took place. It is evidence that a plan was recorded. Likewise, seeing an event does not confirm that another person has accepted a meeting. For this exercise, **success means accurate scheduling and verification**, not real-world attendance.

### The essential fields

A suitable event usually answers five questions: **What? Which day? When does it start? When does it end? Where or how is it happening?** Some calendar systems display duration instead of asking you to enter an end time. The relationship is the same: an appointment beginning at 2:00 p.m. and ending at 2:45 p.m. lasts 45 minutes.

| Field | Required practice value | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title | `MGU Practice Lab 6 - Planning Session` | Identifies the event without exposing private facts |
| Date | One date 7–21 days from your lab day | Keeps the exercise in the future |
| Initial start | 2:00 p.m. local time | Establishes the planned start |
| Initial end | 2:45 p.m. local time | Creates a 45-minute block |
| Location | `Practice only - no physical meeting` or blank | Avoids implying a real venue |
| Description | `Fictional lab. Review the scheduling checklist. No attendance required.` | Clarifies that this is not a real appointment |
| Reminder | 30 minutes before, where supported | Tests the notification control |
| Guests | **None** | Prevents accidental invitations |
| Repeat | **Does not repeat** | Ensures one practice event |

### An all-day entry is different

An [[all-day-event|all-day event]] marks a date without a particular clock-time interval, such as a holiday, travel day, or birthday. The lab requires a timed appointment, **not** an all-day event. If the all-day option is selected by mistake, switch it off before entering the start and end times. Some calendars show all-day events in a separate row; their position on the screen does not mean they begin at midnight as ordinary appointments do.

### Personal events and meetings

A personal appointment is generally a saved event for the owner's own use. A [[meeting|meeting]] is an event that involves other people and may send invitations. Some products use the same event editor for both; adding guests can turn a simple entry into an invitation workflow. The difference matters because a click on **Send**, **Save and send**, or an invitation dialog may deliver messages to real people. **The mandatory lab never needs that step.**

### Busy time is not the same as privacy

Many calendar apps let you choose an availability state such as [[busy|Busy]] or [[free|Free]]. Busy may affect how a scheduling assistant displays the time to others who can see availability. It does not automatically hide event details. Separately, an event or calendar can have [[visibility|visibility]] settings. Visibility options vary with the app and organization, and even events marked private may be subject to organizational access or account policies. Always choose an approved personal calendar first rather than trusting a single toggle to provide confidentiality.

## 2. How do you plan an accurate appointment?

### Write the plan before opening the application

The simplest way to prevent errors is to plan the event in plain language first. Read the requirements above, select a practice date within the stated window, and write that date in a temporary personal note. Confirm the month, day, and year. Then copy the details exactly into the calendar editor. This eliminates the common problem of choosing a day in the current month when the intended date is in the next month.

Write a small practice card such as this:

```text
Title: MGU Practice Lab 6 - Planning Session
Date: [your chosen date, 7–21 days from today]
Start: 2:00 p.m. local time
End: 2:45 p.m. local time
Duration: 45 minutes
Reminder: 30 minutes before start, if supported
Location: Practice only - no physical meeting
Description: Fictional lab. Review the scheduling checklist. No attendance required.
Calendar: [approved personal calendar name]
Guests: none
Repeat: no
```

The bracketed entries are placeholders, not content to paste verbatim. The learner supplies an actual date and real calendar name. The lab does not require revealing the name of the account or a screenshot of other personal appointments.

### Confirm that your planned interval is possible

The start must precede the end. The interval from 2:00 p.m. to 2:45 p.m. is 45 minutes; after the revision the interval from 2:30 p.m. to 3:15 p.m. is also 45 minutes. Moving the event later does not mean making it longer. A common mistake is to update only the starting time; some applications adjust the ending time automatically while others behave differently. **Read both fields after every change.**

A beginner should also recognize the [[twelve-hour-clock|12-hour clock]] and [[twenty-four-hour-clock|24-hour clock]]. The times 2:00 p.m. and 14:00 represent the same instant on the chosen local date. An entry at 02:00 is 2:00 a.m., not 2:00 p.m. The precise display depends on your device's language and regional settings.

| Written form | Equivalent form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2:00 p.m. | 14:00 | Initial start |
| 2:45 p.m. | 14:45 | Initial end |
| 2:30 p.m. | 14:30 | Revised start |
| 3:15 p.m. | 15:15 | Revised end |
| 1:30 p.m. | 13:30 | Initial 30-minute reminder time |

The initial reminder time would be 1:30 p.m.; after editing the event it should be tied to the new start and therefore correspond to 2:00 p.m., if the reminder is still set to "30 minutes before." An app may show only the relative rule rather than a separate reminder-clock field. **Check the rule**, not just the calculated time.

### Understand time zones before sharing anything

A [[time-zone|time zone]] determines how local clock times correspond to an absolute instant. For a private lab performed on one device, the normal choice is to use the time zone configured in your calendar or account, then check it in the editor if the app exposes it. If your device is traveling, your calendar is configured for another region, or the event will involve people elsewhere, time-zone settings matter more. Do not assume that the same displayed hour means the same moment in different locations.

For this exercise, there is **no need to calculate a time-zone conversion** or schedule a meeting across countries. A safe beginner habit is to record the displayed time-zone label whenever it is available and to avoid changing account-wide settings merely to finish one practice event.

### Select a reminder for a reason

A reminder 30 minutes before start may help you prepare. However, a notification depends on application preferences, device permissions, account synchronization, internet access in some cases, and the device's sound or focus settings. You may not receive a visible or audible pop-up even when the saved event contains the correct reminder. The assessment therefore checks **the configured reminder value**, not whether an alert happened to appear at exactly the expected time.

| Reminder choice | Typical use | Lab status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| At event time | Prompt when activity starts | Not the target |
| 10 minutes before | Brief transition | Acceptable outside this lab |
| 30 minutes before | Allows preparation | **Target value** |
| One day before | Advance planning | Optional additional reminder |
| No notification | Quiet calendar entry | Use only if the app or policy prevents alerts; document exception |

### Keep the exercise deliberately small

Do not create a separate event for each activity in the lesson. You need one event that can be created, found, revised, and verified. Each additional entry makes it harder to prove that you edited the original instead of adding another. If you accidentally make two, follow the cleanup guidance in Section 8 rather than deleting anything without checking the title, calendar, and date.

## 3. How do you select the right calendar?

### Know which account is currently active

Calendar applications may display information from multiple signed-in accounts at once. A user might see a personal calendar, a work calendar, a class calendar, a family calendar, public holidays, and subscribed calendars in a single interface. **Seeing a calendar does not necessarily mean you own it or have editing permission.** Before you create the lab event, identify which account and calendar the event editor will save into.

In Google Calendar, available calendars are generally listed in the side panel. An event editor may offer a calendar selection near the event details. In Outlook, an account may include several calendars, and a meeting can be associated with one of them. In Apple Calendar, events are assigned to a calendar in the app. The exact controls depend on account configuration. If you cannot determine where the event will be stored, stop and use an approved alternative instead of guessing.

### Local, online, and shared calendars

An [[online-calendar|online calendar]] uses an account-based service that can synchronize entries across supported devices. A [[local-calendar|local calendar]] may store entries on a single device or in an account with limited synchronization. A [[shared-calendar|shared calendar]] is visible to other authorized people, possibly including classmates, family members, or coworkers. An organization can also enforce calendar permissions and retention rules.

| Calendar type | Usual purpose | Lab guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Personal calendar | Your own appointments | Preferred when authorized |
| Instructor-approved test calendar | Classroom practice | Use only as directed |
| Shared family calendar | Coordinating household activities | Avoid unless explicit permission is given |
| Work or school group calendar | Team or institutional schedules | Not appropriate without authorization |
| Read-only subscribed calendar | View holidays or published events | Do not attempt to add the lab event |

### Ask four questions before creating

1. **Who owns the calendar?** Confirm that it is your personal account or an approved practice destination.
2. **Who can see the event?** Check whether account, sharing, or organizational rules make the entry visible to others.
3. **Can this account edit the calendar?** You need appropriate [[write-permission|write permission]] to create and revise the event.
4. **Will a notification or invitation be sent?** The initial lab must have no guests or automatically enabled meeting request.

### Why the visible calendar matters

Suppose an app shows the same Thursday on both a personal and a shared team calendar. You click the time slot and enter the correct title. If the event is created in the team calendar by default, your fictional appointment may become visible to coworkers or students. That is not a successful private practice event. Use the calendar selector before saving; then inspect the saved entry afterward to verify its destination.

### If you do not have an authorized destination

You can complete the planning questions, the quiz, and the paper simulation. You cannot honestly claim to have created, saved, or edited a digital event if no authorized calendar was used. This distinction is part of digital [[verification|verification]]: describe what you actually did rather than checking off tasks based only on intention.

## 4. How do you create the practice event?

### Google Calendar on a computer: primary method

The following sequence follows the general workflow documented in Google's current Calendar help. The app may open a small quick-entry window first; a **More options** control may open the detailed event editor. Button placement can vary.

1. Open [Google Calendar](https://calendar.google.com/) in a desktop browser and sign in to an account you are permitted to use. Do not type account credentials into the MGU lesson page.
2. Confirm the account and select or identify your personal calendar in the list of available calendars. If multiple calendars appear, do not assume the top one is correct.
3. Navigate to the date you selected for the lab, 7–21 days in the future. Check the full date and year in the calendar header or event editor.
4. Select **Create → Event**, or choose an empty time area if that method is available. If the quick editor hides needed fields, use **More options** or the equivalent detailed editor.
5. In the title field, enter `MGU Practice Lab 6 - Planning Session` exactly.
6. Set the date. Switch **All day** off if it is enabled, and enter **2:00 p.m.** as the start and **2:45 p.m.** as the end.
7. Inspect the [[time-zone|time zone]] if shown. Use the calendar's intended local time zone; do not change the account-wide setting for this lab.
8. Confirm the event belongs to the authorized personal calendar, not a public, shared, or workplace calendar.
9. Put `Practice only - no physical meeting` in the location field if available, or leave that field blank. The lab does not require reserving a room or sharing a street address.
10. Put `Fictional lab. Review the scheduling checklist. No attendance required.` in the description. This is explanatory text, not a message to be sent.
11. Find the event's [[notification-setting|notification setting]] and choose **30 minutes before** if supported. The app may offer presets, a custom value, or multiple notification choices. Avoid changing global default reminders unless instructed by your teacher.
12. Confirm there are **no guests**, no video meeting link automatically enabled, and no recurrence pattern. Do not enter any email addresses.
13. Choose **Save**. If a dialog unexpectedly asks whether to send invitations, stop and review the guest field; the basic lab should not require sending any invitation.
14. Return to the date view. Find the event by its title, open it, and verify the exact saved values. The act of saving is not the last step: verification is required.

### What should appear after saving?

You should see one event on the selected date, occupying 45 minutes. It should display the exact practice title and should not show an attendee list, a recurring-series symbol, or an invitation status requiring a reply. Some calendar views compress event titles or show only the start time; **open the event's detail view** to read the end time, reminder, calendar, and description.

### Avoid accidental date shifts

When clicking a time slot, some apps populate the start and end automatically. The end time might default to 30 or 60 minutes after the start, not 45. Replace the values rather than assuming the default is correct. If the month view automatically jumps after you save, search the intended date again. If you cannot locate your event, check for a wrong calendar, wrong month, wrong year, or unexpected time-zone conversion before creating another copy.

### Outlook: a closely related path

In **Outlook on the web**, **Outlook.com**, or the **new Outlook** desktop interface, open **Calendar** and choose **New Event** (or the equivalent). Enter the title, chosen date, 2:00 p.m. start and 2:45 p.m. end, approved calendar, location/description, and 30-minute reminder where available. A personal event without attendees may offer **Save**; an event with invitees may offer **Send**. Do not add attendees or turn on a Teams meeting unless an instructor explicitly requires it. After saving, open the event again and check the details. Older Outlook interfaces can differ, so use the application's labels rather than blindly following a screenshot from a different version.

### Why the calendar app is not the MGU page

The lesson dashboard provides text, practice instructions, vocabulary, and quiz feedback. It does not access your Google or Microsoft account or create calendar events for you. You perform the steps in the authorized calendar app. The lesson's checkboxes, answers, and rubric do not imply that an invitation was sent or an event was actually saved.

## 5. How do you check and edit an event?

### Verification comes before revision

Open the event you just saved. Do not merely glance at its colored rectangle in the weekly grid. A proper check inspects the full date, title, calendar destination, start time, end time, description, and reminder. A successful save should be confirmed by finding the entry again rather than assuming that the application's feedback toast guarantees the correct information.

Use this small evidence table while you inspect the event:

| Field to inspect | Initial required value | Check method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title | `MGU Practice Lab 6 - Planning Session` | Open event details |
| Calendar | Your approved personal or practice calendar | Read calendar name in details |
| Date | Your recorded date 7–21 days ahead | Compare full date |
| Start and end | 2:00–2:45 p.m. | Inspect both time fields |
| Duration | 45 minutes | Check difference between times |
| Reminder | 30 minutes before, if supported | Inspect notification setting |
| Guests | None | Inspect attendees or guest section |
| Repeat | Does not repeat | Inspect recurrence setting |
| Description | Fictional lab text | Read full description |

### Edit the original instead of making a second event

Now change the appointment to begin **30 minutes later** while preserving its duration. This is intentionally a separate skill from creating an event.

1. Find and open the **existing** practice event in your calendar.
2. Choose **Edit** or the application's equivalent, not **Duplicate**, **Copy**, or **New Event**.
3. Leave the title, selected date, calendar, and description unchanged.
4. Change the start to **2:30 p.m.** and the end to **3:15 p.m.** on the same date.
5. Confirm the reminder still means **30 minutes before** if that option remains supported.
6. Check that there are no guests and no repeat schedule.
7. Save the changes. If the app asks you to update attendees, review the entry for accidental guests and avoid sending a practice invitation.
8. Return to the calendar and reopen the event to confirm **2:30–3:15 p.m.** is stored.
9. Inspect the same date and calendar to confirm there is **only one** practice event with this title.

The resulting duration remains 45 minutes. The 30-minute reminder, when correctly tied to the new start, would correspond to **2:00 p.m.** on the chosen event date. This is a useful self-check: the time of the reminder changes because the event start moved, even though the reminder's lead time did not change.

### Edit, move, duplicate, and delete are not interchangeable

**Edit** means changing an existing entry. **Move** may mean shifting its time by dragging it or reassigning it to another calendar. **Duplicate** creates a second separate event, and **Delete** removes an event or, with a repeating entry, may offer to remove more than one occurrence. The lab asks you to **edit** the same event. Do not solve a mistake by creating many replacements or deleting unrelated calendar entries.

### If you notice a conflict

A [[calendar-conflict|calendar conflict]] exists when your event overlaps another commitment or a time you are unavailable. A personal calendar might allow the overlap but display it visibly. For the lab, choose another permitted date within the 7–21 day window if the original would conflict with a real appointment. If you change the lab date, update your paper practice card and verify the new full date afterward. Do not reschedule a real person's appointment to accommodate the exercise.

### When is the exercise complete?

You have completed the digital portion only after **one accurate event has been saved, found again, revised, and rechecked**. Seeing a filled-in editor before clicking Save is not enough; a vanished pop-up is not enough; a reminder appearing on your phone is not a substitute for checking the event fields. The lab's evidence is the final correct event in the correct calendar.

## 6. What changes when events include other people or time zones?

The practice exercise stays private, but many real calendar activities involve invitations, multiple locations, or repetition. Understanding those differences prevents a beginner from inadvertently contacting others or scheduling the wrong time.

### Adding guests changes the workflow

An [[attendee|attendee]] is someone invited to an event. A meeting [[organizer|organizer]] creates or manages the invitation; attendees may receive an email, an app notification, or a calendar request depending on their service. A guest may respond **Yes**, **No**, or **Maybe/Tentative**, and different apps show these answers differently. An organizer should not assume that entering a guest address means that the person has accepted.

If you invite someone, verify that the address is exact and authorized, not merely the first suggestion from [[autocomplete|autocomplete]]. Review guest-permission options such as whether attendees can modify events or invite others. A practice scenario must never send a meeting invitation to a random address, fabricated email address, or unconsenting contact.

| Action | Potential effect | Allowed in required lab? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Save a private personal event | Adds an item to your own calendar | **Yes** |
| Add someone to the guests field | May prepare a real invitation | **No** |
| Send a meeting invitation | May notify another person | **No** |
| Add a video-conference link | May create meeting access information | Not needed |
| Share a calendar | May expose other appointments | **No** |
| Change attendee permissions | Alters others' access to an event | **No** |

### A shared calendar is not the same as adding a guest

A calendar can be shared with a person or group independently of any particular event. Saving an entry on that calendar may expose information to people who can view it even if the guest list is empty. The guest list controls invitations to the event; the calendar's sharing settings govern who can see the calendar. These are related but separate permissions. For this lesson, choose a suitable personal destination **before** adding the entry.

### A meeting request and a calendar event are not identical

In some versions of Outlook, creating a meeting request with attendees ends with **Send**, whereas a personal appointment may end with **Save**. Google Calendar may offer to email guests when an event with invitations is changed. Apple Calendar can likewise send invitations for supported accounts. This is why the lab requires **no guests**. In real workplace scheduling, follow the organization's protocol for notifying participants of changes; do not quietly alter an event and presume everyone will notice.

### Recurrence is powerful but easy to misuse

A [[recurring-event|recurring event]] repeats on a schedule, such as every Monday at 9 a.m. or the first business day of the month. Applications may let you edit **one occurrence**, **this and following**, or **the whole series**. Those options have different consequences. A practice lab requiring one appointment must be set to **Does not repeat**, **Never**, or the equivalent. Do not choose Weekly simply because you see a weekday on the calendar.

### Time zones and daylight saving changes

A calendar can display an event at different clock times in different time zones while representing the same moment. Time-zone rules and [[daylight-saving-time|daylight saving time]] can change seasonally in some regions. Use the location's intended time zone and inspect the date; do not treat abbreviations such as "PT" as a fixed UTC offset all year. If a real event involves another region, confirm the time zone explicitly with participants and verify the resulting invitation. The lab avoids cross-zone scheduling because it tests basic date/time entry first.

### A reminder is not attendance tracking

Notifications can appear as pop-ups, emails, mobile alerts, or other prompts, depending on the calendar and device. A reminder arriving does not prove the event happened; a missing reminder does not necessarily mean the event was unsaved. To test notification setup, reopen the entry and inspect the stored notification rule. To test actual reminder delivery, you would also need appropriate device permissions and a later live test, which is **not required** in this lab.

### Choose descriptive but respectful event titles

Some calendars display event titles on lock screens or shared scheduling displays. A title such as `Planning Session` is more appropriate for a privacy-sensitive calendar than one containing diagnoses, financial account numbers, employee disciplinary details, or other confidential information. For this lab, use the precise fictional title. For real activity, use the least sensitive wording that still tells you what the time block is for.

## 7. Guided practice: build, check, and revise an event

These six activities are cumulative. Complete them in order so that your instructor can distinguish planning, creation, verification, revision, and safe judgment. Each activity includes a simple paper alternative where appropriate, but **paper planning does not establish that a digital event was created**. Reveal answers after attempting the task, not before.

### Activity 1: Build the appointment card

**Purpose:** Plan precise event data before opening an editor.

1. Write today's date and choose a calendar date 7–21 days ahead. Include month, day, and year.
2. Write the exact practice title: `MGU Practice Lab 6 - Planning Session`.
3. Write the start and end times 2:00 p.m. and 2:45 p.m.; calculate the duration.
4. Record your intended personal calendar and the local time zone shown in the app, if available.
5. Write `None` next to Guests, `Does not repeat` next to Recurrence, and `30 minutes before` next to Reminder.
6. Explain in one sentence why the event is fictional and should not be sent to another person.

**Evidence:** A complete appointment card that contains all relevant details and no real private identifiers.

:::answer Show Activity 1 sample answer
A correct card uses a genuine date 7–21 days after the learner's actual lab date. It gives the specified title, 2:00–2:45 p.m. local time, **45 minutes** of duration, the authorized calendar, **no guests**, **no repetition**, and a 30-minute reminder where supported. The specific date cannot be predetermined because learners may complete the lesson on different days. A valid explanation says the session is only a practice entry, not a real meeting, so no invitations should be issued.
:::

### Activity 2: Find the correct calendar and explain its access

**Purpose:** Prevent wrong-account and shared-calendar mistakes.

1. Open the authorized calendar app without changing an event.
2. Inspect the signed-in account and available calendars; do not copy email addresses into the assignment.
3. Identify one personal or instructor-approved practice calendar with editing permission.
4. Find the control that determines which calendar a new event will use.
5. State whether your chosen calendar is personal, shared, or instructor-controlled and why you have permission to edit it.
6. If the destination is unclear or unauthorized, stop; explain the issue and use the simulated workflow.

**Evidence:** A verbal or written explanation naming the type of calendar and how its destination was checked, without exposing account details.

:::answer Show Activity 2 model response
**Suitable response:** "I selected an authorized personal calendar. I checked the calendar selector in the new-event editor and confirmed that the practice item would be saved there. I did not use the team calendar because other members may see it." Learners using a classroom test calendar should describe the instructor's authorization. A read-only or unverified shared calendar is not suitable.
:::

### Activity 3: Create the first 45-minute event

**Purpose:** Enter and save all required fields.

1. Start a new calendar event in the approved calendar.

**Purpose:** Read saved information rather than trusting a preview.

:::answer Show Activity 4 expected findings

### Activity 5: Edit the event without duplication

**Purpose:** Change an existing item while keeping its other fields accurate.

1. Open the existing event and choose Edit, not New or Duplicate.
2. Change the start time from 2:00 to **2:30 p.m.**.
3. Set or confirm the end time as **3:15 p.m.**.
4. Verify that the date, title, selected calendar, guest list, description, and one-time status are unchanged.
5. Confirm the reminder still says 30 minutes before if available.
6. Save, leave the event, return, and reopen it.
7. Look through that date and calendar to make sure no second event with the same title was created.
8. Calculate the duration and the expected reminder clock time.

**Evidence:** Exactly one edited event beginning at 2:30 p.m. and ending at 3:15 p.m. on the chosen date.

:::answer Show Activity 5 expected result
The final time is **2:30–3:15 p.m.**, so the event still lasts **45 minutes**. A reminder configured for 30 minutes before its new start corresponds to **2:00 p.m.** The original event was revised, not duplicated; there should be **one** matching practice entry on the chosen calendar and date.

### Activity 6: Detect and correct a scheduling mistake

**Purpose:** Apply safe judgment to common calendar errors.

Read each situation and write the best next step before opening the answer:

1. You see two identical practice events on the same date, one at 2:00 p.m. and one at 2:30 p.m.
2. The event editor defaults to a shared work calendar even though you meant to use a personal calendar.
3. An autocomplete suggestion inserts another person's address into Guests.
4. The event displays 2:30 a.m. instead of 2:30 p.m.
5. The reminder did not pop up on the device, but the saved event still shows a 30-minute notification.
6. The app says you do not have permission to edit the selected calendar.

**Evidence:** Six safe next actions, explaining what to inspect before sending, rescheduling, or deleting anything.

:::answer Show Activity 6 model decisions
1. **Duplicate:** Verify the full title, date, calendar, and both entries. Keep the one that meets the final required time, and remove only the accidental extra if you own it and deletion is authorized. Do not delete an unrelated event.
2. **Wrong destination:** Switch to the authorized personal calendar before saving. If an entry was already created in the wrong place, handle its removal or reassignment only with the appropriate permission.
3. **Unexpected guest:** Remove the accidental guest before saving. Do not send an invitation.
4. **AM/PM error:** Correct the displayed start and end to **2:30–3:15 p.m.**, save, and reopen to verify.
5. **Missing pop-up:** Check device notifications, focus modes, connectivity, and app settings if necessary, but grade the required lab using the saved 30-minute rule.
6. **No permission:** Stop using that calendar and request an approved destination or use the simulation. Do not try to bypass access controls.
:::

## 8. How do you adapt the lab and troubleshoot?

### Microsoft Outlook on the web and Windows

1. Open the Calendar view in the account you may use and identify the intended personal calendar.
2. Select **New Event** or an equivalent calendar creation control.
3. Use the title, practice date, and 2:00–2:45 p.m. initial interval. Do not make it all day.
4. Set a description and an available 30-minute reminder; leave attendees empty.
5. Check for any default Teams meeting setting and avoid creating a meeting link unnecessarily.
6. Save the personal appointment. If the screen switches to an invitation-sending workflow, recheck whether someone was added as an attendee.
7. Open the saved event, edit it to 2:30–3:15 p.m., save, and verify one matching entry remains.

The exact commands vary between new Outlook, classic Outlook, Outlook.com, and Microsoft 365 organizational configurations. Some meeting workflows use **Send** or **Send Update** rather than Save. Because this exercise should not contact other people, stop and investigate if the app prompts you to notify attendees.

### Apple Calendar on Mac

1. Open **Calendar** on the Mac and identify the approved calendar in the calendar list.
2. Create a new event using the event control, a selected time slot, or the app's supported quick-entry method.
3. Enter the exact title, the chosen future date, and the 2:00–2:45 p.m. time span.
4. Select the correct calendar, add the fictional note if supported, and configure an alert 30 minutes before.
5. Make sure the event has no invitees and no repetition.
6. Save or confirm the entry according to the version; reopen it, edit the time to 2:30–3:15 p.m., and verify one final event.

Apple Calendar can combine calendars from different accounts. Read the calendar name in the event details rather than assuming that its color identifies ownership. If you are using iCloud or another connected calendar, synchronization may take time or depend on account status.

### Android, iPhone, iPad, or Chromebook

The steps are conceptually similar. In a supported calendar app, choose Create or Add Event, select your authorized calendar, enter the date and times, choose a reminder, save, and inspect the stored entry. Mobile devices may hide additional fields behind **More options**, **Event details**, or scrolling panels. A Chromebook can use a supported browser-based calendar. On small screens, double-check AM/PM and the date because the time picker can be changed unintentionally with a swipe.

For accessibility, use screen-reader labels, keyboard controls, larger text, screen magnification, or assistive touch as appropriate. A student who enters the correct information using accessible controls meets the same learning objective as someone using a mouse. The lab evaluates decisions, accuracy, and verification rather than how quickly the learner can tap or click.

### Troubleshooting guide

| Symptom | Likely cause | Safe first action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Event appears on wrong date | Wrong month, year, or day selected | Open details; compare against written practice date |
| Event appears at 2:30 a.m. | 12-hour clock selection error | Correct PM setting and check the end time |
| End time is 3:00 rather than 3:15 | App default duration or auto-update | Enter both revised fields explicitly |
| Event disappears from current view | Wrong calendar, hidden calendar, date, or sync | Check calendar list, date, and account before creating another |
| New event saves on team calendar | Incorrect default destination | Stop and choose approved personal calendar |
| Save prompts an invitation email | Guest field or meeting mode is active | Check guests and remove unintended invitees before proceeding |
| Cannot edit | Read-only calendar, account restriction, or access issue | Use an approved editable destination; never bypass permissions |
| Repeated events appeared | Recurrence option unintentionally enabled | Inspect series settings and modify only owned practice entries |
| Reminder never appeared | App/device notifications disabled or delayed | Verify stored reminder rule, then check device permissions |
| Two matching events appear | A second event was created rather than edited | Identify original and duplicate before removing an owned mistake |
| Calendar shows another time zone | Device/account time-zone configuration differs | Verify exact zone and local date; do not randomly shift hours |
| Event visible to others | Shared calendar or policy | Review ownership/visibility, avoid real private content |
| Cannot open calendar service | Connection, authentication, or service issue | Use the offline plan temporarily; retry through approved support |
| Guest address seems unfamiliar | Autocomplete chose wrong contact | Remove the address; do not send an invitation |

### If the event fails to save

Stay in the calendar app long enough to read any error. Network connectivity, an expired sign-in session, lack of calendar permission, or required fields can prevent saving. A visible event editor is not proof of a successful save. If the app reports failure, check the issue and use the application or institution's normal recovery procedure. Once the save is reported, go to the date and confirm the entry exists before attempting another creation. Otherwise you may generate duplicates.

### If a duplicate appears

Do not press Delete automatically. First inspect both entries, their owning calendars, date and time, and whether either has attendees. If the extra entry is your own accidental practice item and you are authorized to remove it, delete only that duplicate according to the app's normal controls. If an item belongs to a recurring series or another person, do not remove it without checking how the deletion affects others. A teacher may accept a written explanation if the student cannot safely perform cleanup.

### Keep other people out of the test

Calendar invitations and shared calendars can expose more information than beginners expect. Do not paste real contact details into the lesson, do not submit calendar screenshots showing unrelated personal appointments, and do not invite an address simply to see whether the Send button works. Some meeting links are automatically generated when attendees are added in workplace accounts; the lab neither needs nor evaluates such links. A simulated invitation plan is sufficient.

### On shared or institution-managed devices

A school or employer may monitor, retain, or control events in its calendar service. Private event labels do not necessarily override administrative access. If an institution directs learners to use a temporary practice account, follow its cleanup policy. Do not sign out of or remove an account owned by another person. If you must demonstrate the event on screen, prefer opening the event details directly instead of exposing the rest of a personal schedule.

### Accessibility and equitable assessment

Calendar grids can be difficult to navigate with a keyboard or screen reader. A legitimate alternative is to use a list or agenda view, an accessible new-event form, or guided keyboard navigation in an approved calendar app. Instructors should grant time for careful reading of time fields and support accessible presentation of the event. A learner who requires assistance with navigating controls should still explain the calendar, date, reminder, and guest decisions independently whenever possible.

### Demonstrating skill without a live account

When software access is unavailable, have students fill out the appointment card and describe the save-edit-reopen sequence. Label the outcome **planning demonstrated; digital execution not observed**. Do not award full software-execution credit for a simulated calendar view. This preserves the integrity of the lab while allowing learners to practice the logic safely.

## 9. Essential vocabulary

These terms appear throughout the lesson. Registered terms may display explanations when selected in the dashboard. The definitions below are also visible without an interactive popover. Technical terms are grouped by function so that beginners can review only the concepts that need reinforcement.

### Scheduling essentials

- **Calendar:** A dated system for organizing appointments and other scheduled information.
- **Digital calendar:** A calendar managed with software on a device or through an online service.
- **Calendar event:** A dated item that records a scheduled activity or time block.
- **Task:** A tracked action that may have a due date or completion status.
- **Appointment:** A scheduled event that may be for one person or several, depending on context.
- **Meeting:** A scheduled gathering that commonly involves invited participants.
- **Event title:** The short name displayed for a calendar entry.
- **Event date:** The calendar day on which the event occurs or begins.
- **Start time:** The moment the event is scheduled to begin.
- **End time:** The moment the event is scheduled to finish.
- **Duration:** The length of time between the event's start and end.
- **All-day event:** A dated entry without a specific clock-time interval.
- **Reminder:** A configured advance or at-start prompt associated with a scheduled item.
- **Notification:** An app, device, or email signal intended to call attention to an event.
- **Description:** Extra explanatory text stored with an event.
- **Location:** A place or mode of attendance associated with an event.
- **Time zone:** A rule set connecting local clock time to a common time reference.
- **12-hour clock:** A time representation using 1–12 with a.m. and p.m.
- **24-hour clock:** A time representation using hours 00–23.
- **Daylight saving time:** Seasonal adjustments to clock time used in some locations.
- **Calendar view:** A display such as day, week, month, or agenda.

### Accounts, access, and sharing

- **Calendar account:** The signed-in identity providing access to calendar information.
- **Personal calendar:** A calendar maintained for an individual's activities.
- **Online calendar:** Account-based calendar that can be accessible across connected devices.
- **Local calendar:** Calendar data stored on or tied mainly to one device or local account.
- **Shared calendar:** A calendar that other authorized people can view or edit.
- **Read-only calendar:** Calendar visible without permission to change its entries.
- **Write permission:** Authorization to create or modify entries in a calendar.
- **Organizer:** Person or account controlling a meeting invitation.
- **Attendee:** Person invited to attend an event.
- **Invitation:** A meeting request delivered to an intended participant.
- **Guest permissions:** Rules determining actions invitees may perform on an event.
- **Busy status:** Setting that marks time as unavailable to compatible scheduling views.
- **Free status:** Setting that marks an event as not blocking availability in compatible views.
- **Visibility:** Setting or policy determining who can see an event's details.
- **Privacy:** Appropriate control of personal information and its audience.
- **Shared workspace:** An account or service area used by multiple authorized people.
- **Autocomplete:** A feature that proposes entries as a user types.
- **Read access:** Permission to inspect information without necessarily changing it.
- **Calendar ownership:** The account or person responsible for a calendar and its controls.

### Working with events

- **Create event:** Make a new calendar entry.
- **Event editor:** Form or panel used to enter or change an event's details.
- **Save:** Record entered information to a calendar or service.
- **Edit:** Change an existing saved event.
- **Duplicate:** Create an additional copy of an event.
- **Delete:** Remove a calendar entry within the app's rules.
- **Recurring event:** Event programmed to repeat according to a schedule.
- **Occurrence:** One dated instance of a recurring event.
- **Series:** A collection of events governed by a recurrence pattern.
- **Calendar conflict:** Overlap between a scheduled event and another commitment.
- **Scheduling assistant:** Tool that may help find times when participants are available.
- **Video meeting link:** Address that joins an online meeting when authorized.
- **Reschedule:** Change an event's planned time or date.
- **Draft:** Unsaved or not-yet-finalized content under preparation.
- **Event details:** Stored fields describing an entry.
- **Notification setting:** Control defining whether and when an alert is requested.
- **Default setting:** A value an application preselects unless the user changes it.
- **Verification:** Checking an action's outcome against a defined expectation.
- **Evidence:** Observable result supporting a claim that a task was performed.
- **Rubric:** A structured set of criteria for evaluating work.

### Practical safety and accessibility

- **Synchronization:** Coordinating data across connected applications or devices.
- **Offline:** Working without active access to an online service.
- **Account permission:** Authorization associated with an account or resource.
- **Time picker:** Control for selecting a clock time in an app.
- **Date picker:** Control for choosing a calendar date in an app.
- **Screen reader:** Assistive technology that announces on-screen information.
- **Keyboard navigation:** Operating an interface with keyboard input.
- **Accessibility:** Design and practice that enable people with different needs to use technology.
- **Practice scenario:** Fictional exercise designed to reduce real-world consequences.
- **Consent:** Permission freely given for a particular interaction or disclosure.
- **Notification permission:** Device or app authorization to display alerts.
- **Focus mode:** Device setting that may silence or restrict notifications.
- **Account synchronization:** Coordinating account data across devices or applications.
- **Organizational policy:** Rules governing use of systems controlled by an organization.
- **Troubleshooting:** Systematic process of identifying and correcting a problem.
- **Self-check:** Reviewing one's own work against clear expectations.
- **Activity record:** Written record of what actions were attempted and observed.

### Instructors: distinguish the levels of demonstration

A learner who accurately fills out the worksheet has shown **conceptual understanding**. A learner who enters fields in a new event but does not save has shown **data-entry practice**. A learner who saves but never returns to check the result has shown **basic operation, not complete verification**. A learner who creates, reopens, edits, and confirms a single correct event has demonstrated the full beginner lab. Give specific feedback on the missing step rather than treating all four outcomes as identical.

If students must submit evidence, use an instructor-observed demonstration or a carefully cropped depiction of the fictional event. Avoid showing email addresses, unrelated appointments, private calendar titles, attendee lists, or other sensitive information. Do not require students to share account credentials or grant MGU access to their calendars.

### Common transfer scenarios

Once you can schedule and verify a fictional appointment, you can apply the same process to studying, interviews, scheduled errands, office hours, family plans, and team coordination. For a real commitment, add the information actually needed, confirm all participants and time zones when applicable, and follow the relevant organization's policies. More complex features such as recurring series, room reservations, and participant scheduling can be learned after the basic one-event workflow is reliable.

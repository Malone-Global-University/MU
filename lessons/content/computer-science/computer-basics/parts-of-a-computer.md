## Lesson 2: Parts of a Computer

A computer may look like one machine, but it contains several parts that perform different jobs. Some parts are easy to see, such as a screen, keyboard, or charging connector. Others are inside the device, such as the processor, working memory, and storage. This lesson teaches you to recognize the major parts, explain what each does, and use that knowledge when working with an everyday computer.

**Level:** Beginner · **Estimated study time:** 55–75 minutes · **Prerequisites:** Lesson 1, *Understanding Computers*, recommended · **Practice equipment:** A desktop, laptop, tablet, or smartphone (optional; no tools or disassembly required)

> **Essential idea:** The computer's parts work as a system. **Input devices** receive information, **processing components** carry out instructions, **output devices** present results, **memory** supports work in progress, and **storage** preserves saved information. Power, cooling, and connections help all these parts operate together.

### Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you should be able to:

1. Identify common external computer parts and describe their purposes.
1. Distinguish input devices, output devices, and devices that perform both functions.
1. Recognize common ports and explain why connectors and cables must be compatible.
1. Describe, in beginner-friendly language, the jobs of the motherboard, CPU, RAM, storage, and power system.
1. Explain how different parts cooperate during an everyday task.
1. Locate parts on a familiar device without opening it or making unsafe changes.

## 1. What are the main parts of a computer?

The physical parts of a computer are called [[hardware|hardware]]. A desktop may have a separate screen, keyboard, mouse, and computer case. A laptop combines many of these parts into one portable device. A smartphone has a touchscreen and a number of small components housed together. All are examples of computer hardware.

A practical way to organize computer parts is by their **job**, not just their appearance:

1. **Input:** Parts that provide information or commands to the computer.
1. **Processing:** Components that carry out instructions and work with information.
1. **Output:** Parts that present results to a person or another device.
1. **Memory and storage:** Components that hold information during use or preserve it for later.
1. **Support and connections:** Parts that supply power, control temperature, and connect the system together.

A component may serve more than one job. For example, a touchscreen both displays information and detects your touch. A network connection can send and receive information. These categories are learning tools, not strict labels that every device fits into only once.

### External parts and internal parts

**External parts** are on the outside of a device or attached to it. You can usually see a monitor, keyboard, mouse, speakers, or cable without taking anything apart. An attached accessory is often called a [[peripheral|peripheral device]].

**Internal parts** are located inside the case or body. Examples include the CPU, RAM, motherboard, and storage hardware. Some are removable in certain desktop computers; many are built in or soldered into laptops, tablets, and phones.

> **Remember:** You do not need to open a computer to learn its parts. This lesson is about identification and understanding, not repair or disassembly.

### Hardware is not the same as software

A keyboard is hardware. A typing application is software. A photograph stored in the computer is data. A screen is hardware; the webpage shown on it is information presented by software. The parts and the programs work together, but they are not the same thing.

## 2. What parts can you see and use?

The most familiar computer parts help people enter information, view results, communicate, and control a device.

### Monitor or display

A [[monitor|monitor]] is a screen that displays text, images, videos, windows, and messages. It is normally an [[output-device|output device]]. A desktop monitor may stand on a desk and connect to a separate computer. A laptop has a built-in display. The display is **not necessarily the whole computer**; many of the parts doing the processing are located elsewhere.

### Keyboard

A [[keyboard|keyboard]] is an [[input-device|input device]] used to enter letters, numbers, symbols, and commands. Some computers have a physical keyboard, while phones and tablets commonly display an on-screen keyboard. Different layouts may place keys in different positions.

### Mouse and touchpad

A [[mouse|mouse]] is a pointing device used to move an on-screen pointer, select items, and perform actions such as clicking or dragging. A [[touchpad|touchpad]] serves a similar purpose with a touch-sensitive surface, commonly below a laptop keyboard. The pointer is the image on the screen; the mouse or touchpad is the hardware used to control it.

### Touchscreen

A [[touchscreen|touchscreen]] combines two roles: it **shows** pictures and controls (output) and **detects** taps or gestures (input). This is why a phone may not need a separate mouse or physical keyboard for normal use.

### Speakers, headphones, microphone, and camera

- [[speakers|Speakers]] and headphones produce sound, such as voices, music, or notification tones. Their primary role is output.
- A [[microphone|microphone]] captures sound. Its primary role is input.
- A [[webcam|webcam]] or other camera captures images and video. Its primary role is input.
- A headset may combine headphones and a microphone in one accessory, so it handles both input and output.

Some computers have built-in cameras, microphones, and speakers. Others use attached accessories. A camera indicator or microphone permission notice is a useful privacy signal, but its exact appearance differs by device.

### Computer case, power button, and accessories

On many desktop computers, the [[computer-case|computer case]] (sometimes called the **tower** or **system unit**) houses the major internal components. The case is not itself the CPU, even though people sometimes casually call the whole box “the CPU.” All-in-one desktops instead place the computer hardware within the display housing or stand.

A **power button** tells the system to start or initiate a power-related action. The button's location and behavior vary; normal shutdown procedures are covered in a later lesson.

Printers, scanners, external webcams, and external drives are examples of accessories that add functions to a computer. Not every computer requires every accessory.

*Common visible components and their primary functions*

| Part | Typical role | Everyday example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monitor or display | Output | Shows a document or video |
| Keyboard | Input | Enters a name or sentence |
| Mouse or touchpad | Input | Selects an item on the screen |
| Touchscreen | Both | Displays a button and receives a tap |
| Microphone | Input | Captures your voice during a call |
| Camera or webcam | Input | Captures a photo or video |
| Speakers or headphones | Output | Play a voice message |
| Printer | Output | Produces a paper copy |
| Scanner | Input | Captures information from a paper page |

For an expanded explanation of these categories, see the MGU Encyclopedia entries on [Input Devices](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/input-devices), [Output Devices](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/output-devices), and [Peripheral Devices](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/peripheral-devices).

## 3. How do computer parts connect?

A computer may connect to other devices through cables, built-in connectors, or wireless technologies. A [[port|port]] is a connection point on a device. A **connector** is the end of a compatible cable or accessory that fits a port. The exact shape matters, but so does the connection's supported function.

### Common ports and what they do

Common connection types include [[usb|USB]], [[hdmi|HDMI]], [[audio-jack|audio jacks]], [[ethernet|Ethernet]], and [[power-connector|power connectors]]. The table summarizes their everyday uses.

| Port or connection | Common purpose | What to recognize |
| --- | --- | --- |
| USB-A | Connects many keyboards, mice, flash drives, and accessories | Familiar flat, rectangular connector |
| USB-C | Connects compatible accessories; may support data, charging, and/or video | Small oval-shaped, reversible connector; capabilities differ by device |
| HDMI | Carries digital video and usually audio to a monitor or television | Often used for external displays |
| 3.5 mm audio jack | Connects compatible headphones, microphones, or headsets | Small round socket; input/output support varies |
| Ethernet port | Connects a computer to a wired network | Usually accepts a rectangular network-cable plug with a retaining clip |
| Power connector | Connects an approved charger or external power supply | Shape varies; some devices receive power through compatible USB-C ports |
| SD or microSD slot | Accepts a supported removable memory card | A card slot rather than a normal USB socket |

A USB-C port **does not automatically support every USB-C feature**. One device may use USB-C for charging and data, while another also supports an external monitor. Read the device's instructions before assuming a cable or adapter will work.

### Wired and wireless connections

A **wired connection** uses a physical cable. A **wireless connection** uses radio signals instead. [[wifi|Wi-Fi]] commonly connects a computer to a local network; [[bluetooth|Bluetooth]] commonly connects nearby accessories such as headphones, mice, and keyboards. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth are connection technologies, not physical ports. An internet connection is not required for every peripheral or computer task.

### Safe connection habits

1. Identify a port and check that the connector is intended for it.
1. Align the connector correctly and **never force it** into a socket.
1. Use chargers and power adapters approved or specified for your device.
1. Avoid plugging in unknown USB drives or accessories from untrusted sources.
1. Before unplugging removable storage, save your work and use the device's **Eject** or **Safely Remove** procedure when appropriate.
1. If a cable, plug, or device is damaged, unusually hot, or wet, stop using it and seek qualified help.

> **Practical caution:** Looking at ports is enough for this lesson. You do not need to connect or disconnect a cable, open a case, handle a power supply, or move heavy equipment.

## 4. What is inside a computer?

Inside a computer are small electronic components with different responsibilities. Most beginners do not need to see them directly to understand their functions.

### Motherboard: the main connection platform

The [[motherboard|motherboard]] is the primary circuit board that connects major hardware components and provides pathways for signals and power. In a desktop, the CPU, RAM, and other devices communicate through connections associated with this board. A phone or tablet also has an internal main circuit board, although its arrangement may be quite different.

### CPU: the general-purpose processor

The [[cpu|central processing unit (CPU)]] carries out many of the computer's instructions, including calculations and logical operations. People sometimes call it the “brain” of the computer as a simple analogy, but it does not think like a person and is not the only component that performs processing. It is normally a chip or part of a chip **inside** the device, not the entire desktop case.

### RAM: working memory

[[ram|Random access memory (RAM)]] holds information and programs that the computer is actively using. Think of it as a **workspace**. It provides fast access while you use a browser, edit a document, or switch between open applications. Ordinary RAM is temporary: it normally loses its contents when power is removed.

### Storage: saved files and applications

[[storage|Storage hardware]] holds information for later use, even when the computer is switched off. A [[ssd|solid-state drive (SSD)]] is common in modern computers; a [[hdd|hard disk drive (HDD)]] uses rotating magnetic disks. Phones and tablets commonly use built-in flash storage. These technologies store operating systems, apps, photographs, and documents.

**RAM and storage are not interchangeable.** RAM helps the device work on active tasks. Storage keeps saved files and installed programs between uses. Storage may fail or be lost, so important files still need a separate backup.

### Graphics, power, battery, and cooling

A [[gpu|graphics processing unit (GPU)]] helps produce visual images and can perform other specialized calculations. It may be integrated into a processor or provided by separate hardware. You do not need a separate graphics card for every computer to display a picture.

A [[power-supply|power supply]] converts or regulates electricity for computer components. Desktop computers usually have an internal power supply unit; laptops typically use an external adapter and internal power-management circuits. A [[battery|battery]] stores electrical energy for portable use. A computer may also use a [[cooling|cooling system]], such as a fan or heat spreader, to prevent components from overheating. Some mobile devices have no fan.

*Major internal parts at a glance*

| Component | Main job | Beginner reminder |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motherboard | Connects and supports communication among components | The main connection platform |
| CPU | Executes general-purpose instructions | Processes, but is not the entire computer |
| RAM | Holds active working information | Fast and normally temporary |
| SSD, HDD, or built-in flash storage | Preserves saved data | Retains files when power is off |
| GPU | Helps create images and perform specialized work | May be integrated or separate |
| Power supply / power-management hardware | Delivers usable electrical power | Different designs for desktops and mobile devices |
| Battery | Supplies portable power | Not present in every desktop |
| Cooling parts | Move or release heat | Not every computer has a fan |

> **Safety note:** Computer cases, laptop batteries, and internal power supplies can present electrical, heat, mechanical, or battery hazards. Do not open a device or attempt an internal upgrade for this lesson. Repairs and hardware replacement require device-specific instructions and appropriate expertise.

Read more in the MGU Encyclopedia: [Motherboard](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/basics/motherboard), [Central Processing Unit](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/central-processing-unit), [Memory Hardware](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/memory-hardware), [Storage Hardware](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/storage-hardware), and [Power and Thermal Systems](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/power-and-thermal-systems).

## 5. How do the parts work together?

Different parts cooperate whenever you complete a computing task. The following examples connect the parts you can see with the components working inside the machine. These are simplified descriptions: actual systems may use many additional chips, controllers, and software processes.

### Example A: Type and save a short note

1. **Input:** You type a sentence using a keyboard or touchscreen.
1. **Processing:** Software and the CPU interpret the keystrokes and update the note.
1. **Working memory:** RAM helps hold the application and information being used.
1. **Output:** The display shows the characters and changes you make.
1. **Storage:** When the note is saved, the application preserves it on local storage or another location selected by the app, such as an online service.

If you switch off the computer without saving, you may lose recent changes unless the application has automatic saving or recovery. The monitor alone does not store the words merely because they are visible on the screen.

### Example B: Join a video call

1. **Input:** The camera captures your image and the microphone captures your voice, if enabled.
1. **Processing:** The computer processes the audio and video and the calling app manages the call.
1. **Connection:** A network connection sends and receives call data.
1. **Output:** The display presents video and the speakers or headphones reproduce sound.
1. **Storage:** A live call does not necessarily create a saved recording. Recording depends on the service, permissions, and settings.

For privacy, check which app has access to the microphone and camera. A camera being installed does not mean it is always recording.

### Example C: Print a document

The computer sends a print job to a printer through an appropriate connection. The printer turns information into marks on paper. **The printer is an output device; the document's original file may remain stored on the computer or another service.**

### Follow the task, not just the device

| Everyday action | Input | Output | Other parts involved |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Type a note | Keyboard or touchscreen | Display | CPU, RAM, storage when saved |
| Listen to music | Playback controls or app commands | Speakers or headphones | Processor, audio hardware, file or network source |
| Join a video call | Camera, microphone, controls | Display and speakers | CPU, memory, network connection |
| Print a page | Print command | Printer | CPU, memory, storage, printer connection |

> **Think about it:** When you press a key, does the keyboard, CPU, or monitor decide what appears? Explain why the answer involves more than one part and the software running on the computer.

## 6. How do the parts differ across devices?

Computers share many functions, but they do not all arrange their parts in the same way. The next lesson examines device categories in more detail; here the goal is to understand where the parts are located.

*Same functions, different arrangements*

| Device | What you can usually identify | What may be built in or hidden |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Desktop computer | Separate display, keyboard, mouse, system unit | CPU, motherboard, RAM, storage and power supply inside the case |
| All-in-one desktop | Display, keyboard, mouse or touch capability | Processor, memory and storage in the display housing or stand |
| Laptop computer | Built-in screen, keyboard, touchpad, power connector | CPU, RAM, storage, battery, speakers and often camera/microphone |
| Tablet | Touchscreen, cameras, charging connector | Processor, memory, storage, battery and wireless hardware |
| Smartphone | Touchscreen, cameras, microphone, speakers | Processor, memory, flash storage, battery and wireless hardware |

Some laptops have touchscreens; some desktops have wireless keyboards; some tablets work with an external mouse. Do not assume that a particular model has every listed feature or that its parts can be replaced. Small devices often integrate several functions onto the same chip.

### Accessibility and alternative equipment

A person may use speech input, a screen reader with audio output, a larger keyboard, a trackball, a switch control, or another assistive device instead of the most familiar mouse-and-keyboard arrangement. These tools still perform recognizable input or output functions. Understanding the **purpose** of a part is more important than memorizing one physical design.

### Which part should you look for?

Suppose you need to speak during an online meeting: look for a microphone or an approved headset, not a printer. If you want to keep a file after shutdown, you need a storage location, not merely a brighter display or more RAM. Identifying the job helps you identify the correct hardware.

## 7. Guided practice: Identify computer parts

These activities work with a desktop, laptop, tablet, or smartphone. You may complete them by observation only. If no device is available, use the examples and write your answers on paper. **Do not open the device, change internal settings, or disconnect equipment that belongs to someone else.**

### Activity 1: External parts inventory (about 5–7 minutes)

1. Identify the type of computer available to you.
1. Locate its screen or another output device.
1. Locate at least one input device, such as a keyboard, mouse, touchpad, touchscreen, camera, or microphone.
1. Find a power button or an external power connection without pressing or unplugging anything.
1. Record three parts and explain what each does.

*Example observation worksheet*

| Part observed | Input, output, both, or support? | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Laptop screen | Output | Shows a document |
| Your part 1 | ________ | ________ |
| Your part 2 | ________ | ________ |
| Your part 3 | ________ | ________ |

### Activity 2: Port and connection survey (about 5 minutes)

1. Look at the sides or back of your device, **without moving it into an unsafe position**.
1. Identify one connector or port if it is visible. Possible examples include USB, a headphone jack, HDMI, or a charging connection.
1. Describe the accessory or cable normally used with that connection.
1. If you see no physical ports, name one wireless connection or built-in feature instead.
1. Explain why you should never force a connector into the wrong port.

> **Example response:** “My laptop has a USB-A port that can connect a compatible mouse. I will check the connector's shape before connecting anything.”

### Activity 3: Match the part to the job (about 5 minutes)

Match each situation to the part that best fits. Try before revealing the answers.

1. You need to enter a long paragraph using physical keys.
1. You want a video caller to hear your voice.
1. You need to see the text you are editing.
1. You want your saved photos to remain after shutdown.
1. You need the computer to carry out general program instructions.
1. You need to listen to sound without disturbing others.
1. You need a temporary workspace for programs that are running.

:::answer Show Activity 3 answers
1. Keyboard — enters text as input.
1. Microphone — captures voice as input.
1. Monitor or display — presents text as output.
1. Storage, such as an SSD or built-in phone storage — keeps saved files.
1. CPU — executes general-purpose instructions.

### Activity 4: Trace an everyday task (about 5–10 minutes)

If a notes or text-editing application is available, create a harmless practice sentence. Otherwise, imagine the steps and complete the worksheet.

1. Identify the part used to enter the sentence.
1. Identify the part that displays it.
1. Explain what the CPU and RAM contribute while the application is running.
1. If you use a real app, save the note only in an appropriate location, or discard it when finished. Do not overwrite an existing file.
1. Explain the difference between seeing the sentence on the screen and saving it for later.

If you already know how to view your device's system information, you may look for an entry describing its processor, installed RAM, or storage. ## 8. Common misconceptions

### “The computer tower is called the CPU.”

Not precisely. The tower or system unit is the case and the components inside it. The CPU is a processor housed within the computer.

### “The monitor contains all the information because I can see it.”

Not necessarily. A monitor usually displays results. The programs, active data, and saved files are typically managed by components and storage elsewhere. Some all-in-one computers do place those components inside the display housing.

### “RAM and storage do the same job.”

Both hold information, but ordinary RAM is a temporary workspace for active tasks; storage is designed to keep saved files after power is removed.

### “All USB-C ports do the same things.”

Not true. A USB-C connector has a common physical shape, but charging power, data speeds, and video capabilities depend on the device, cable, and supported standards.

### “Every computer has a separate mouse, keyboard, and monitor.”

No. Phones and tablets use built-in touchscreens, many laptops use touchpads, and assistive equipment can provide other methods of interaction.

### “A computer with no fan has no cooling system.”

Not necessarily. Some computers are designed to release heat through their housing or other passive cooling components rather than a fan.

### “A video call is automatically recorded because a camera is being used.”

Not necessarily. A camera can capture live video for transmission without saving a recording. Whether a recording is made depends on the application and its settings or policies.

### “Learning the internal parts means I should open my computer.”

No. Identification does not require opening a case. Beginners can learn each component's function through descriptions, diagrams, or device information screens.

## 9. Essential vocabulary

Words with a dotted underline earlier in the lesson can be selected to open a brief definition. Where a verified MGU Encyclopedia article exists, the tooltip also provides a link for deeper reading. The important terms are summarized here.

- **Hardware:** The physical parts of a computer, including built-in components and connected devices.
- **Peripheral device:** Additional hardware used for input, output, storage, or another function. [Peripheral Devices](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/peripheral-devices).
- **Input device:** Hardware that sends information or commands into a computer. [Input Devices](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/input-devices).
- **Output device:** Hardware that delivers information or results from a computer. [Output Devices](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/output-devices).
- **Monitor:** A screen that presents visual output from a computer.
- **Keyboard:** A set of physical or on-screen keys for entering characters and commands.
- **Mouse / touchpad:** Devices used to move a pointer and select or manipulate on-screen items.
- **Touchscreen:** A display that presents information and senses touch as input.
- **Webcam / microphone:** Hardware used to capture video or sound as input.
- **Speakers / headphones:** Hardware used to produce sound as output.
- **Port:** A physical connection point for a compatible cable or accessory.
- **USB:** A widely used family of interfaces for compatible computer accessories and, in some cases, power and video.
- **Motherboard:** The principal circuit board that connects major components. [Motherboard](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/basics/motherboard).
- **CPU:** The central processing unit, which executes general-purpose program instructions. [Central Processing Unit](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/central-processing-unit).
- **RAM:** Random access memory; fast, normally temporary working memory. [RAM](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/basics/ram).
- **Storage:** Hardware that retains saved information after power is removed. [Storage Hardware](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/storage-hardware).
- **SSD:** A solid-state drive that stores data using nonvolatile memory chips.
- **HDD:** A hard disk drive that stores data on rotating magnetic disks.
- **GPU:** A graphics processing unit; specialized hardware that handles graphics and some other workloads. [Graphics Processing Unit](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/graphics-processing-unit).
- **Power supply:** Hardware that provides suitable electrical power to components. [Power and Thermal Systems](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/power-and-thermal-systems).
- **Cooling system:** Hardware and design features that help keep operating temperatures within safe limits.

- [Central Processing Unit](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/central-processing-unit) — the processor responsible for many general-purpose instructions.
- [Memory Hardware](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/memory-hardware) and [RAM](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/basics/ram) — temporary working memory.
- [Storage Hardware](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/storage-hardware) — persistent information storage.
- [Graphics Processing Unit](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/graphics-processing-unit) — visual processing and specialized computations.
- [Power and Thermal Systems](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/power-and-thermal-systems) — power delivery and safe operating temperatures.

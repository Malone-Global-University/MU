Basic Computer Skills / Computer Basics

## Lesson 1: Understanding Computers

A computer is a machine that follows instructions to work with information. You may use computers to write a letter, look up information, talk to someone, manage appointments, or pay a bill. You do not need programming experience to understand the basics.

**Level:** Beginner · **Estimated study time:** 35–50 minutes · **Prerequisites:** None · **Practice equipment:** Any computer, tablet, or smartphone (optional)

> **Essential idea:** Computers *receive information*, *follow instructions*, *produce results*, and often *store information* for later use. Hardware is the physical equipment; software is the set of instructions that makes it useful.

### Learning objectives

After completing this lesson, you should be able to:

1. Explain what a computer is in everyday language.
1. Recognize several types of computers, not just laptops and desktops.
1. Identify input, processing, output, and storage in a familiar activity.
1. Describe the difference between hardware and software.
1. Distinguish temporary working memory from longer-term storage at a basic level.
1. Complete simple activities on a device and explain what the computer did.

## 1. What does “computer” mean?

A [[computer|computer]] is a programmable electronic device or system that receives and processes data according to instructions. It can provide a result, keep information for later, or communicate with other devices. Most computers used in daily life are digital, which means they represent information with numbers inside the machine.

There is no single shape or size that makes something a computer. A large desktop tower is a computer, but so is a smartphone that fits in your pocket. Some computers have a screen and keyboard; others run quietly inside a car, appliance, or medical device.

What makes a computer useful is its ability to perform many tasks by following different instructions. For example, the same laptop can display a video, calculate a budget, store photographs, and open a web page without changing its physical parts.

> **Remember:** A computer is not simply “a screen.” The screen is one part that may show what the computer is doing. The actual work is performed by electronic components following software instructions.

### Information and instructions

[[data|Data]] is information represented in a form a computer can use, such as letters, numbers, pictures, or sound recordings. A [[program|program]] is a set of instructions telling the computer how to do a task. A calculator program, for instance, takes the numbers you enter and follows arithmetic rules to show an answer.

Computers do not automatically know whether something is correct, fair, or sensible. They can follow incorrect instructions or use incorrect data, resulting in incorrect answers. The person using a computer should still check important information and decisions.

## 2. Where do we find computers?

Computers appear in many settings. Their appearance changes with the job they are built to perform.

### Desktop computer

Usually stays at a desk. May have a separate monitor, keyboard, mouse, and computer case, or combine the computer with the display.

### Laptop computer

A portable personal computer with a built-in screen, keyboard, and rechargeable battery. It can handle many of the same tasks as a desktop.

### Tablet and smartphone

Portable computers operated mainly through touchscreens. They run apps, connect to networks, take photos, and store information.

### Other computing systems

Game consoles, smartwatches, servers, and computers embedded inside vehicles and appliances perform specialized or shared tasks.

A printer with digital controls, for example, may contain a small embedded computer that receives a print job, manages the mechanism, and reports problems. A smartphone is a general-purpose computer even though we commonly call it a phone.

### Is every electronic device a computer?

No. An ordinary lamp uses electricity but does not necessarily process information under a program. A basic mechanical can opener is a tool, not a computer. An electronic thermostat with a programmable controller may contain a small computer. The important question is not merely whether a device uses electricity, but whether it carries out information-processing instructions.

> **Think about it:** Name three computing devices you used or encountered today. What task did each perform?

## 3. How does a computer work?

A practical way to understand most computing activities is to look for four basic functions: [[input|input]], [[processing|processing]], [[output|output]], and [[storage|storage]]. These functions may overlap or repeat many times during a single task.

1. **1. Input** — Information enters: a tap, typed text, a microphone signal, or data from another device.
1. **2. Processing** — The computer follows instructions to work with the information.
1. **3. Output** — A result appears, plays through speakers, prints, or goes to another device.
1. **4. Storage** — Information may be kept in memory or saved for later use.

Simplified model: input → processing → output, with storage used before, during, or after the other steps. Storage is shown fourth for teaching convenience; it does not always happen last.

### Example A: Adding two numbers

1. **Input:** You enter `7 + 5` in a calculator app.
1. **Processing:** The computer follows the app's arithmetic instructions.
1. **Output:** The screen shows **12**.
1. **Storage:** The app may remember the calculation in a history, depending on its features. Saving a history is not required for the calculation to work.

### Example B: Taking a photograph

1. **Input:** The camera sensor captures light, and you tap the shutter button.
1. **Processing:** The phone processes the image data.
1. **Output:** The photo appears on the screen.
1. **Storage:** The photo is typically saved to the device and may also be copied to a cloud account if that feature is enabled.

### Examples of input and output devices

*Recognizing how devices interact with information*

| Device | Usual function | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keyboard | Input | Enters letters, numbers, and commands |
| Mouse or touchpad | Input | Moves a pointer and selects items |
| Microphone | Input | Captures your voice |
| Monitor | Output | Displays text, pictures, and windows |
| Speakers | Output | Play music, voices, and alerts |
| Printer | Output | Produces a paper copy |
| Touchscreen | Both input and output | Shows buttons and responds to touch |

For deeper explanations of how data enters or leaves a system, see the MGU Encyclopedia entries on [Input Devices](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/input-devices) and [Output Devices](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/output-devices).

## 4. What is the difference between hardware and software?

A computer works because its physical components and its instructions work together. The physical components are called [[hardware|hardware]]. The programs and other instructions are called [[software|software]].

### Hardware: physical parts

Examples include a screen, keyboard, mouse, camera, battery, processor, memory chips, and internal storage drive. These are parts you could physically handle if the device were safely disassembled by a qualified person.

### Software: instructions

Examples include a web browser, word processor, calculator app, video player, and the computer's operating system. Software uses hardware to perform tasks.

Picture a music player. The speaker and electronic circuits are hardware. The music app is software. The recording you choose is data. When you press Play, the software directs the hardware to reproduce the recording as sound.

### What is an operating system?

An [[operating-system|operating system]] is core software that manages the computer and helps other programs run. Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, and iOS are examples. An operating system typically helps manage files, screen interactions, memory, connected devices, and access to the computer.

You do not need to know how an operating system is programmed to use it. At this stage, recognize that it provides the environment in which you open apps, change basic settings, and work with files.

### What does the processor do?

The [[cpu|central processing unit (CPU)]] is a major component that executes instructions. A simple analogy is that it handles much of the computer's work and coordination. Other specialized chips also perform processing, so the CPU is not the only part involved in every task.

> **Quick identification:** A laptop keyboard is hardware. A typing program is software. The words you type are data. The document you save is stored data.

## 5. What are memory and storage?

A computer often needs to keep information while it works and to save some information for the future. These are related jobs, but they are not the same.

*Temporary working memory compared with persistent storage*

| Feature | Working memory (RAM) | Persistent storage (SSD, hard drive, phone storage) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main purpose | Holds programs and data currently being used | Holds saved files, installed apps, and other information |
| Power turned off | Normally loses its contents | Normally keeps saved information |
| Everyday comparison | A workspace where you arrange materials | A filing cabinet where you keep materials |

Suppose you are writing a letter. While the document is open, your computer is using working memory. When you save the letter to a folder, you are preserving a copy in storage. If the program closes unexpectedly before you save, recent changes could be lost, although some programs have automatic saving or recovery features.

Storage capacity does not guarantee safety. Important files should also be [[backup|backed up]] to a separate, trusted place when possible. You will study files, folders, and backup methods in later lessons.

> **Practical caution:** Do not turn off a computer abruptly or remove a storage device while files are being written. Save your work and use the normal shutdown or eject steps when appropriate.

## 6. What can a computer help you do?

Computers support activities at home, at work, at school, and in the community. The same general principles apply across different tasks.

*Everyday activities and the role of the computer*

| Task | What you provide | What the computer does | What you receive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Write a letter | Typed words and formatting choices | Arranges text and follows formatting commands | A document you can view, save, or print |
| Search the web | A search phrase | Uses a browser and a remote search service to request results | A list of pages or answers to review |
| Calculate a budget | Income and expenses | Performs calculations in a spreadsheet or calculator | Totals and comparisons |
| Join a video call | Camera, microphone, and user commands | Processes and sends audio/video over a network | Live sound and picture from other participants |
| Manage photos | Pictures and organizing choices | Displays, sorts, and saves image files | An organized collection you can revisit |

### Does a computer need the internet?

No. A computer can do many things without internet access, including using an offline calculator, writing in an installed word processor, and opening locally saved files. Tasks such as visiting websites, sending email, or participating in an online call usually require a network connection. The **computer** is the device; the **internet** is a worldwide network that many devices can use to exchange information.

### Why human judgment still matters

Using a computer does not remove the need for care. Before sharing a file, entering payment information, or following instructions from an unfamiliar message, examine what you are being asked to do. A computer can display false information as easily as accurate information. Digital safety and critical evaluation are skills to practice alongside basic operation.

## 7. Guided practice: Observe a computer at work

These activities can be completed on a Windows computer, Mac, Chromebook, tablet, or smartphone. Button names and screen layouts vary. If you have no device available, read the steps and write down what you would expect to happen.

### Activity 1: Identify your device (about 5 minutes)

1. Look at the device you are using. Is it a desktop, laptop, tablet, smartphone, or another type of computer?
1. Find at least one input method, such as a keyboard, mouse, touchscreen, camera, or microphone.
1. Find at least one output method, such as a screen, speaker, or printer.
1. Identify one app or program on the device. What task is it designed to perform?
1. Record your answers using the worksheet below or on paper.

> **Example response:** “I use a smartphone. I tap its touchscreen to enter commands. Its screen shows results, its speakers play sound, and its calculator app helps me add numbers.”

### Activity 2: Follow the four functions (about 5–10 minutes)

1. Open the built-in calculator on your device, if one is available. If not, use a basic calculator you can observe.
1. Enter **18 + 24**.
1. Look at the result: **42**.
1. Describe the input (the numbers and operation), processing (the calculation), and output (the displayed result).
1. Consider storage: will the app keep the calculation after it closes? Some apps do; others do not. You do not need to change any settings to answer.

### Activity 3: Hardware, software, or data? (about 5 minutes)

Classify each item. Work first, then open the answers.

1. A keyboard
1. A web browser
1. A saved photo
1. A speaker
1. A calculator application
1. The numbers in a spreadsheet

## 8. Common misconceptions

### “Only desktops and laptops are computers.”

Not true. Smartphones, tablets, servers, and many embedded devices perform computing tasks.

### “The screen is the entire computer.”

Not necessarily. A screen is commonly an output device, sometimes also an input device, while processing and storage happen in other components.

### “Hardware and software are the same.”

Hardware is physical. Software consists of programs and instructions. They work together, but they are different things.

### “Everything is saved automatically.”

Not always. Some apps autosave, while others need a Save command. Always check that important work was saved.

### “Without internet, the computer is useless.”

Not true. Many apps and locally stored files can be used offline.

### “A computer's answer must be right.”

Not necessarily. Errors may come from incorrect information, flawed instructions, misunderstandings, or hardware faults.

## 9. Essential vocabulary

Words with a dotted underline earlier in the lesson can be selected to open a brief definition. When a verified MGU Encyclopedia article exists, the definition panel also includes a link for deeper reading. These definitions are also listed in the glossary below.

- **Computer:** A programmable machine or system that works with data according to instructions. [Encyclopedia entry](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/computational-foundations/definition-of-a-computer).
- **Data:** Recorded information, such as numbers, words, images, or sound, represented so a computer can use it.
- **Program:** A set of instructions that tells a computer how to perform tasks.
- **Input:** Information or commands entering a computing system. [Input devices](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/input-devices).
- **Processing:** Working with information by following instructions. [Information processing](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/computational-foundations/information-processing).
- **Output:** A result sent from a computer to a person or another system. [Output devices](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/output-devices).
- **Hardware:** The physical parts of a computer, including its electronic components and attached devices.
- **Software:** Programs and instructions used by computing systems. [Software overview](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/software-systems/software-overview).
- **Operating system:** Core software that manages the device and supports applications. [Operating systems](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/software-systems/operating-systems).
- **CPU (processor):** A major chip or chip component that executes computer instructions. [Central processing unit](/library/encyclopedia/computer-science/hardware-systems/central-processing-unit).
- **Working memory (RAM):** Temporary space for programs and information in active use.
- **Storage:** Where saved information can remain after the device is turned off.
- **Backup:** An extra copy of important information kept so it can be recovered if the original is lost or damaged.

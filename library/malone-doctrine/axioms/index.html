Malone Doctrine Axioms

The constitutional and ethical foundations governing the Malone Doctrine.

Constitutional Authority

Axiom I — Supremacy of Malone Doctrine

Malone Doctrine is the supreme ethical authority governing all Malone organizations, institutions, publications, policies, programs, and subordinate documents. No subordinate authority may contradict, nullify, or supersede the ethical principles established by the Doctrine. Where conflict exists, the Doctrine shall govern, and subordinate authorities shall be interpreted or revised to conform to it.

Axiom II — Authority

The authority of Malone Doctrine derives from the ethical principles it expresses, from the voluntary constitutional submission of Malone institutions to those principles, and from its established status as the controlling ethical standard of the Malone system. Its authority is ethical rather than merely administrative, and no office, institution, or person may claim authority contrary to it.

Axiom III — Necessity and Purpose

Malone Doctrine exists to establish a permanent ethical standard governing the conduct, decisions, and development of the Malone system. It provides continuity beyond changing circumstances by ensuring that institutional convenience, temporary interests, and individual authority remain subject to enduring ethical principles.

Axiom IV — Scope

Malone Doctrine governs every exercise of Malone authority, including institutions, officers, members, publications, policies, programs, decisions, and subordinate documents. Its authority extends to every activity undertaken in the name of, on behalf of, or within the jurisdiction of a Malone institution.

Axiom V — Persons and Institutions Bound

Malone Doctrine binds every Malone institution and every person exercising authority under it. Persons who voluntarily accept offices, responsibilities, privileges, benefits, or governed relationships within the Malone system accept the ethical obligations established by the Doctrine to the extent of those relationships.

Substantive Ethical Foundations

Axiom VI — Human Dignity and Moral Agency

Every person possesses inherent moral dignity and moral agency. Because persons are capable of ethical judgment and responsible action, they shall be treated as ends in themselves rather than merely as instruments of institutional, political, or personal advantage.

Axiom VII — Rights and Duties

Rights and duties are inseparable within ethical society. Every right carries corresponding obligations, and every exercise of authority shall respect both the liberties and responsibilities necessary to preserve justice, order, and human dignity.

Axiom VIII — Truth and Integrity

Truthfulness, intellectual honesty, and institutional integrity are indispensable to ethical authority. Deliberate deception, falsification, and material misrepresentation are incompatible with Malone Doctrine and shall not be employed to secure institutional advantage or personal benefit.

Axiom IX — Responsibility

Every exercise of authority carries responsibility and accountability. Individuals and institutions are answerable for the foreseeable consequences of their conduct, and no office or position exempts its holder from ethical accountability.

Axiom X — Stewardship

Authority is exercised in trust rather than ownership. Every person entrusted with power, resources, information, or institutional responsibility shall manage those trusts faithfully for their proper purposes and for the long-term welfare of those whom they serve.

Axiom XI — Justice

Justice requires impartiality, proportionality, consistency, and respect for the equal moral standing of persons. Ethical judgments shall be governed by objective standards rather than favoritism, prejudice, arbitrary power, or personal interest.

Interpretation, Amendment, and Continuity

Axiom XII — Interpretation and Conflict

The provisions of Malone Doctrine shall be interpreted as a coherent and unified ethical system. Where multiple provisions appear to conflict, every reasonable effort shall be made to harmonize them. Where harmonization is impossible, the higher-ranking authority within the constitutional hierarchy shall control.

Axiom XIII — Revision and Continuity

Malone Doctrine may be revised only through an express, documented, and duly authorized process. Every revision shall preserve the historical record, identify the reasons for change, distinguish amendment from interpretation, and maintain the continuity and integrity of the Doctrine as a living constitutional instrument.

Axiom XIV — Foundational Permanence

The foundational ethical commitments of Malone Doctrine are permanent and may not be abolished. These commitments include the supremacy of ethical authority over institutional convenience, the inherent moral dignity and agency of every person, the accountability of all authority, the obligation of truth and integrity, the equal submission of institutions to the standards they impose, and the prohibition against converting the Doctrine into an instrument of arbitrary power. Revision may clarify, refine, and better realize these commitments, but shall not nullify or contradict them.

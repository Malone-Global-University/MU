Malone Doctrine

Internal Purpose of Malone Doctrine

Classification Type: Internal Statement
Authority: Normative

Internal Purpose

Malone Doctrine exists to convert the founder’s implicit ethical code into a permanent system of institutional self-government.

Its purpose is to establish the ethical identity, governing standards, and conditions of alignment applicable to all Malone institutions, officers, programs, publications, and authorized representatives. It makes explicit the standards that previously existed through personal practice, judgment, and example but had not yet been reduced to a teachable and durable body of written doctrine.

The Doctrine provides a common basis for instruction, decision-making, institutional discipline, interpretation, accountability, and continuity. It enables Malone institutions to operate under a coherent ethical authority rather than relying upon personal access to the founder, informal custom, political fashion, or the judgment of competing factions.

The Doctrine also establishes the conditions under which individuals and organizations may claim Malone alignment. Malone identity is not created merely by association, naming, affiliation, or public support. It is created and preserved through adherence to the ethical standards established by the Doctrine.

The Doctrine therefore serves as both a standard of formation and a standard of judgment. It is used to teach expected conduct, evaluate decisions, identify deviations, resolve ethical conflicts, and determine when conduct is incompatible with Malone institutional participation.

Its strategic purpose is to preserve an independent ethical power base capable of resisting political fragmentation, factional capture, dishonest participation, arbitrary leadership, and the erosion of institutional identity. It redirects disputes away from personality and toward published standards that can be examined, taught, cited, and consistently applied.

The Doctrine does not merely authorize leadership. It also constrains leadership. Its claim to moral legitimacy depends upon its application to the founder, senior leadership, institutions, members, and affiliates according to the same controlling standards.

Through codification, instruction, application, and institutional enforcement, Malone Doctrine transforms accumulated trust and established practice into a durable ethical inheritance.

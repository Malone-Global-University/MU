Malone Doctrine

Internal Statements

Explanatory papers documenting the internal rationale, design, and institutional role of Malone Doctrine.

Overview

The Internal Statements collection records the conceptual foundations of Malone Doctrine. These papers distinguish the Doctrine's reason for existence, its intended purpose, its institutional function, its practical use, and the benefits expected from its adoption. Together they explain why the Doctrine was created, how it operates within the Malone system, and the role it serves in establishing a permanent ethical framework for Malone institutions. They are explanatory documents intended to clarify the Doctrine's design, architecture, and institutional objectives rather than establish independent normative authority.

Internal Statements

  • Purpose of Malone Doctrine

    Defines the fundamental purpose of Malone Doctrine as the permanent ethical authority governing Malone institutions through instruction, alignment, accountability, and institutional continuity.

  • Reason for Malone Doctrine

    Explains why Malone Doctrine became necessary by documenting the need to convert the founder's implicit ethical system into a durable written standard capable of preserving institutional identity and self-representation.

  • Function of Malone Doctrine

    Describes the Doctrine's operational role as the ethical operating system of Malone institutions, providing direction, restraint, governance, judgment, protection, and long-term continuity.

  • Use of Malone Doctrine

    Explains how the Doctrine is applied to guide decision-making, evaluate conduct, establish institutional boundaries, derive subordinate policies, resolve disputes, and determine Malone alignment.

  • Benefit of Malone Doctrine

    Identifies the practical and institutional benefits of codifying Malone ethics, including clarity, consistency, accountability, continuity, institutional independence, and protection against misrepresentation.

Malone Doctrine

Internal Reason for Malone Doctrine

Internal Statement

Internal Reason

Malone Doctrine was created because the founder’s ethical standards existed through personal conviction, conduct, judgment, and institutional practice but had not yet been reduced to a permanent written code.

As Malone institutions, programs, publications, and relationships expanded, reliance upon the founder’s personal presence became increasingly insufficient. Unwritten standards could be remembered differently, applied inconsistently, weakened through succession, or displaced by the preferences of individuals who did not share the founder’s ethical position. Without codification, no stable reference existed by which authentic Malone conduct could be distinguished from conduct merely performed under the Malone name.

The absence of a written Doctrine also allowed outside persons, organizations, political factions, and competing systems of belief to define the founder’s position through inference, association, accusation, or selective interpretation. The founder’s identity and institutional intentions remained vulnerable to being interpreted by others rather than declared in his own words.

Malone Doctrine was therefore created in response to the need for ethical self-representation. It makes explicit the standards that govern the founder’s conduct, define the ethical identity of Malone institutions, and establish the boundaries within which affiliation, representation, and institutional authority may legitimately operate.

The Doctrine was also made necessary by the vulnerability of communities and institutions whose standards remain informal. Where ethical boundaries are unclear, bad actors may exploit ambiguity, promote destructive conduct, spread deliberate misinformation, manufacture division, claim false alignment, or use legitimate community concerns as a disguise for manipulation. Such conduct is especially harmful when directed toward the African American community, where organized campaigns of cultural degradation, criminal glorification, political fragmentation, and institutional distrust may weaken the ability of families and communities to identify threats and act collectively.

The reason for the Doctrine is therefore not the desire to create another institution, political platform, philosophical system, or administrative code. It is the need to preserve an ethical position that would otherwise remain dependent upon memory, personality, informal custom, and contested interpretation.

Malone Doctrine exists because implicit ethics are insufficient for institutions intended to survive, grow, and act consistently. Standards must be written before they can be taught faithfully, applied impartially, used to judge alignment, preserved through succession, and defended against misrepresentation.

The creation of Malone Doctrine converts the founder’s private ethical operating framework into a durable and reviewable body of written standards. It ensures that Malone identity is defined by declared principles and demonstrated conduct rather than association, branding, personal access, external influence, or unsupported claims of loyalty.

The Doctrine was ultimately made necessary by the requirement that the founder and Malone institutions define themselves before others define them, govern themselves before attempting to influence others, and establish clear ethical boundaries before granting individuals or organizations the right to act in the Malone name.

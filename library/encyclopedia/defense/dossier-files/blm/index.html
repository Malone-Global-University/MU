Black Lives Matter

Overview

Black Lives Matter (BLM) is a decentralized social and political activist movement founded by Alicia Garza, Patrisse Cullors, and Opal Tometi. It campaigns against anti-Black racism, systemic violence, police brutality, and other forms of institutional inequality affecting Black people.

The term may refer to the Black Lives Matter slogan, the broader international movement, local activist chapters, or specific organizations operating under the Black Lives Matter name. These entities should not automatically be treated as identical because they may have different leadership structures, policies, strategies, and institutional responsibilities.

Origins and History

  • 2013 Launch: The movement began through the hashtag #BlackLivesMatter after George Zimmerman was acquitted in the shooting death of Trayvon Martin.
  • 2014 Growth: It gained national and international prominence following the police killing of Michael Brown in Ferguson, Missouri, and the police killing of Eric Garner in New York City.
  • 2020 Surge: The movement expanded substantially through worldwide protests following the murder of George Floyd by Minneapolis police officer Derek Chauvin.

Core Goals and Focus

  • Police and Justice Reform: Demanding accountability for unjustified police killings, misconduct, and abuses of governmental authority while advocating structural changes within policing and the criminal justice system.
  • Racial Equality: Raising awareness of racial discrimination, racial profiling, unequal treatment, and economic and social disparities affecting Black communities.
  • Protection from Systemic Violence: Opposing institutional practices and governmental systems that disproportionately harm, criminalize, exclude, or endanger Black people.
  • Inclusivity: Centering groups within the Black community that have sometimes been marginalized within broader political movements, including women, LGBTQIA+ people, disabled people, immigrants, and economically disadvantaged individuals.

United States v. Wayne Pray (1988–2024)

Overview

United States v. Wayne Pray was the principal federal case against the leader of Newark's The Family organization. The criminal prosecution began in 1988, produced multiple drug convictions in 1989 and a life sentence in 1990, and returned to federal court decades later through sentence-reduction litigation.

Charges

The federal case charged Pray with administering a continuing criminal enterprise, cocaine and marijuana conspiracy, cocaine possession with intent to distribute, and cocaine importation conspiracy, among other counts.

1989 Verdict

After a lengthy trial, a Newark federal jury convicted Pray of being the principal administrator of a continuing criminal enterprise involving more than 150 kilograms of cocaine, conspiracy involving cocaine and marijuana, several substantive cocaine counts, and conspiracy to import more than 98 kilograms of cocaine. He was acquitted on several other counts.

1990 Sentence

On January 12, 1990, the district court imposed a non-parolable life sentence on the principal counts together with additional concurrent terms.

2024 Reduction

On September 30, 2024, the District of New Jersey granted Pray's amended motion for reduction of sentence and reduced his prison term to time served, with a six-year period of supervised release.

Legal Significance

The 2024 order reduced the sentence; it did not vacate the 1989 convictions. The distinction is important when describing Pray's later release.

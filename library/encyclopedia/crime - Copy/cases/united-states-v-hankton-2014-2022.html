United States v. Hankton (2014–2022)

Overview

United States v. Hankton was the principal federal racketeering prosecution of the Hankton Enterprise. The case combined drug-conspiracy, RICO, firearms, obstruction, and violent-crime charges arising from years of activity in New Orleans.

2014 Indictment

A federal grand jury indicted Telly Hankton, Andre Hankton, Walter Porter, Kevin Jackson, and nine additional defendants in June 2014.

Guilty Pleas

Nine defendants resolved their cases through guilty pleas to RICO, drug, firearms, assault, misprision, or related charges.

2016 Trial

Telly Hankton, Andre Hankton, Walter Porter, and Kevin Jackson proceeded to a three-week jury trial involving more than seventy witnesses. The jury convicted the defendants on numerous counts and acquitted them on others.

Sentences

The four trial defendants received life sentences in 2016, although their individual convictions differed.

2022 Fifth Circuit Decision

The Fifth Circuit affirmed the convictions in large part but vacated several firearms convictions and certain restitution orders because the jury may have relied on RICO conspiracy as a legally invalid predicate crime of violence under intervening Supreme Court precedent.

Legal Significance

The case demonstrates the importance of distinguishing between an affirmed RICO conviction and later appellate changes affecting individual firearms counts. A partial appellate vacatur did not erase the broader racketeering convictions.

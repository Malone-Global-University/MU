Clarence “Preacher” Heatley

Leader of the Preacher Crew, a Harlem-and-Bronx racketeering organization involved in narcotics trafficking, extortion, robbery, kidnapping, and murder.

Overview

Clarence “Preacher” Heatley led the organization identified in federal proceedings as the “Preacher Crew” or “Family.” Federal court records describe the enterprise as operating primarily in Harlem and the Bronx from approximately 1983 to 1996 and engaging in murder, assault, extortion, robbery, and narcotics trafficking.

Donnell Porter Case

The federal government’s 1997 death-penalty notice against Heatley specifically alleged that, from approximately December 5, 1989 through January 20, 1990, Heatley participated in the kidnapping, torture, and murder of William “Donnell” Porter. This is the strongest currently identified official source connecting the Preacher Crew directly to the Porter kidnapping.

Federal Prosecution

Heatley was prosecuted in the Southern District of New York in the large racketeering case United States v. Heatley. He entered a guilty plea in 1999 after the government agreed to withdraw its notice seeking the death penalty. Later federal proceedings record that he was sentenced on June 4, 1999 to life imprisonment plus consecutive terms totaling 225 years.

Network Position

  • Organization: leader of the Preacher Crew / Family.
  • John “Apple” Porter: federal records identify Porter as a founding member of the crew and an associate of Heatley.
  • Donnell Porter: federal death-penalty notice alleges Heatley’s participation in the kidnapping, torture, and murder.

Historical Significance

Heatley adds a different type of organization to the Harlem map. The Preacher Crew was not simply another cocaine-distribution group; federal records describe an enterprise combining narcotics activity with contract violence, robbery, extortion, and kidnapping. This makes it central to understanding predatory crews that targeted participants in the drug economy as well as ordinary victims.

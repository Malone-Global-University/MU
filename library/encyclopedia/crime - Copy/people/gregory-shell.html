Gregory Shell

Overview

Gregory Shell was a senior Gangster Disciples leader in Chicago and was described by the Seventh Circuit as second-in-command to Larry Hoover. He became especially important during Hoover's incarceration because he handled substantial day-to-day organizational activity in Chicago.

Leadership Position

Federal court records identify Shell as a trusted board member and Hoover's second-in-command. In 1992, Hoover designated Shell as his proxy, giving him day-to-day control over gang operations in Chicago while Hoover remained incarcerated.

Organizational Role

The Seventh Circuit described Shell as helping coordinate and systematize the Gangster Disciples' drug network. He traveled repeatedly to visit Hoover in prison and served as a key link between incarcerated leadership and Chicago-based operations.

Federal Prosecution

Shell was prosecuted in the same major federal case as Hoover and other senior leaders. He was convicted of operating a continuing criminal enterprise and received a life sentence. The Seventh Circuit later affirmed the principal convictions.

Historical Significance

Shell's role demonstrates that the Gangster Disciples did not operate solely through charismatic leadership. The organization relied on a management hierarchy capable of translating decisions made by imprisoned leaders into day-to-day street operations.

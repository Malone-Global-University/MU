Jemeker Thompson

Overview

Jemeker Thompson, also known in later media accounts as the “Queen Pin,” was a Los Angeles-based cocaine trafficker whose organization expanded beyond Southern California during the 1980s and early 1990s. Her career is important because it demonstrates that the city's cocaine economy contained multiple independent networks rather than a single organization centered on one dealer.

Los Angeles Base

Thompson developed her trafficking operation from South Central Los Angeles. Accounts of her career describe an early partnership with Anthony “Daff” Mosley and later expansion into higher-volume cocaine distribution. Her network eventually relied on distributors outside California as well as local contacts.

Interstate Distribution

Thompson's organization became an interstate operation, making her useful for studying the movement of Los Angeles cocaine networks into secondary American markets. The organization illustrates how local wholesale access could be converted into multi-city distribution through trusted associates rather than through a single national gang hierarchy.

Federal Prosecution

After investigators developed evidence against her organization, Thompson spent approximately two years as a fugitive. She was arrested in 1993 after returning to Los Angeles for her son's school graduation. She was convicted on federal drug-trafficking and money-laundering charges and received a 15-year prison sentence.

Later Life

After imprisonment, Thompson became active in Christian ministry and later publicly discussed her criminal career in interviews and a memoir. Those retrospective accounts are useful for personal perspective but should be distinguished from court records and contemporaneous reporting when establishing specific criminal events.

Historical Significance

Thompson's network broadens the Los Angeles crime narrative beyond the Ross–Blandón relationship. Her career demonstrates parallel cocaine organizations, interstate distribution, money laundering, and the significant role of women as operators rather than merely associates within some drug-trafficking networks.

Edward Byrne

NYPD officer murdered in South Jamaica, Queens in 1988 while guarding the home of a witness in a drug case.

Overview

Edward Byrne was a 22-year-old rookie New York City police officer assigned to protect the residence of a civilian witness in South Jamaica, Queens. His murder became one of the most nationally prominent symbols of crack-era drug violence.

Murder

At approximately 3:30 a.m. on February 26, 1988, Byrne was sitting in a marked patrol car near the witness’s residence when four men associated with Howard “Pappy” Mason’s Bebos organization approached. Byrne was shot five times in the head and died from the attack.

Perpetrators and Ordering Role

State prosecutions identified David McClary as the gunman and Scott Cobb, Todd Scott, and Philip Copeland as the other participants. Federal prosecutors separately convicted Mason of ordering a police officer killed in retaliation after his imprisonment.

Impact

Byrne’s killing triggered intense public attention, accelerated federal and local action against violent Queens drug organizations, and became part of the national political debate over narcotics, policing, and criminal punishment. A Justice Department historical report later described the killing as the catalyst for a sweeping investigation of the Mason and Nichols gangs.

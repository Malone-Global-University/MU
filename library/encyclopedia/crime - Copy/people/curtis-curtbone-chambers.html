Curtis “Curtbone” Chambers

Washington, D.C. drug-era figure, Rayful Edmond co-defendant, later filmmaker/entrepreneur, and participant source on the transition from the Edmond era to the Alpo Martinez era.

Overview

Curtis “Curtbone” Chambers is a Washington, D.C. figure whose importance to the Crime Encyclopedia is partly historical and partly historiographical. Retrospective reporting identifies him as one of Rayful Edmond’s co-defendants. After serving time, Chambers became an entrepreneur and collaborated on the 2005 documentary The Life of Rayful Edmond: The Rise and Fall, Vol. I.

Rayful Edmond Connection

The Washington Post reported in 2005 that filmmaker Kirk Fraser persuaded Chambers, described as one of Edmond’s co-defendants, to collaborate on the documentary. Chambers contributed on-camera recollections, behind-the-scenes knowledge, and financing. This gives him a documented connection to the Edmond case and also makes him a participant source on D.C.’s crack-era underworld.

Alpo Martinez Connection

In later interviews, Chambers describes personally meeting Alberto “Alpo” Martinez when Martinez was expanding into Washington and recounts doing business with him. He also provides participant accounts concerning Wayne Perry, Garrett Terrell, and other D.C. figures. Because these details come primarily from Chambers’s own retrospective interviews, individual claims should be attributed rather than converted automatically into court-established fact.

Historical Significance

Chambers is a useful bridge between two otherwise distinct D.C. narratives: the Rayful Edmond organization and the later Martinez/Perry network. He should not be used to imply that the organizations were one enterprise; rather, his biography documents an individual who moved through overlapping social and criminal environments and later became a narrator of them.

Darryl Whiting

Overview

Darryl Whiting was the organizer and leader of the large-scale drug-distribution ring known as the New York Boys, which operated from the Orchard Park Housing Project in Roxbury during the late 1980s and early 1990s.

New York Boys

The First Circuit described the New York Boys as a large-scale drug-distribution ring operating out of Orchard Park. A 1990 federal indictment charged Whiting and dozens of co-defendants with cocaine conspiracy and related offenses, and a 1991 superseding indictment expanded the case to fifty defendants.

Organization and Control

Federal prosecutors characterized Whiting as the organizer and supervisor of a continuing criminal enterprise. Witnesses at his trial described a large organization with substantial drug revenue and coercive discipline.

Federal Conviction

Whiting was convicted in federal court and received a life sentence in 1990. His convictions and sentence were later reviewed in the First Circuit's 1994 decision involving several New York Boys defendants.

2016 Sentence Review

Changes to federal sentencing guidelines later made Whiting eligible to seek a sentence reduction. In 2016, the district court declined to reduce his life sentence after concluding that release would pose a significant public-safety risk.

Historical Significance

Whiting's case is one of the strongest documentary anchors for Boston's crack era. It shows how a housing-project-based drug ring could expand into a large federal continuing-criminal-enterprise prosecution involving dozens of defendants.

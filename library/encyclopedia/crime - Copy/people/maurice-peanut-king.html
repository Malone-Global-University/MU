Maurice “Peanut” King

Overview

Maurice David “Peanut” King was identified by the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit as one of the leaders of a major Baltimore narcotics-distribution organization. He was prosecuted with Thomas “Joe Dancer” Ricks and others in a long-running federal case.

Organization

The published appellate record describes King, Ricks, and Clarence Meredith as heads of a major Baltimore drug-distribution organization. The network used street lieutenants to supervise heroin sales on particular corners and territories.

Federal Prosecution

King was convicted at the initial 1983 trial. After an appellate reversal based on jury-selection error, he was retried and convicted again. The Fourth Circuit affirmed the later conviction in 1989.

Continuing Criminal Enterprise

King, Ricks, and Meredith were convicted of operating continuing criminal enterprises in addition to conspiracy and substantive narcotics offenses. The charge reflected the government's position that the defendants exercised supervisory roles over broader trafficking structures rather than merely participating in isolated drug sales.

Historical Significance

King's prosecution helps establish a documented organizational layer between individual street sales and the senior leadership of Baltimore's heroin economy. It is especially useful for comparing the city's heroin networks with later multilayered cocaine and heroin conspiracies.

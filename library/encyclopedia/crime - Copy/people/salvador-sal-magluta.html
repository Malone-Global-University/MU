Salvador “Sal” Magluta

Overview

Salvador “Sal” Magluta was a principal leader of the Miami-based cocaine-trafficking organization commonly identified by federal authorities as the Falcon–Magluta organization. From the late 1970s through the early 1990s, the organization became one of the largest cocaine-distribution networks prosecuted in South Florida.

Partnership with Willie Falcon

Magluta and Augusto Guillermo “Willie” Falcon were longtime associates who built a cocaine-importation and distribution organization with operations in South Florida and other parts of the United States. A 1991 federal indictment accused them and associates of a conspiracy beginning in 1978.

Scale of Distribution

Federal records recovered from a Magluta-controlled residence documented 8,921 kilograms of cocaine distributed between January 1, 1990, and October 15, 1991, with recorded sales exceeding $142 million. Other federal allegations described a much larger organization operating across more than a decade.

1991 Arrest and 1996 Trial

Magluta was captured in October 1991 after a period as a fugitive. He and Falcon later went to trial on federal cocaine and continuing-criminal-enterprise charges. In February 1996, both were acquitted.

Post-Trial Corruption Investigation

Federal investigators later uncovered evidence that jurors had been bribed and that witnesses had been tampered with. Magluta was subsequently prosecuted for obstruction of justice, money laundering, and related conduct.

2002 Convictions and Sentence

A federal jury convicted Magluta on money-laundering and obstruction-related counts. He initially received a 205-year sentence. After appellate proceedings and resentencing, the sentence was reduced to 195 years.

Historical Significance

Magluta's history is important not only because of the scale of the cocaine enterprise but also because the later cases documented sophisticated money laundering, fugitive support, witness interference, and corruption of the judicial process.

David Barksdale

Overview

David Barksdale was a prominent South Side Chicago gang leader during the 1960s and early 1970s and is closely associated with the development of the Black Disciples. Historical scholarship and Chicago reporting identify him as one of the central figures in the period when smaller neighborhood gangs were consolidating into larger organizations.

Englewood Context

Barksdale's family moved from Mississippi to Chicago during his youth and lived in the Englewood area. The neighborhood was one of several South Side areas in which rapidly changing housing patterns, segregation, youth street organizations, and political conflict shaped the growth of large gangs during the 1960s.

Black Disciples

Barksdale became a leading figure in the Disciple network and is commonly identified with the organization that became known as the Black Disciples. Exact accounts of the group's earliest founding date and the names of predecessor groups differ among researchers, so this encyclopedia avoids presenting a single disputed origin story as settled fact.

Alliance with Larry Hoover

During the late 1960s and early 1970s, Barksdale and Larry Hoover became associated through an alliance joining Disciple and Gangster elements. Justice Department summaries later described the Gangster Disciples as arising from the alignment of Black Disciples and Supreme Gangsters leadership.

Death and Organizational Legacy

Barksdale died in 1974. His death was followed by further organizational change and factional development among Disciple groups. Later Black Disciples and Gangster Disciples histories both retained references to the earlier alliance, but the organizations should not be treated as permanently unified.

Historical Significance

Barksdale is important less as a narcotics “kingpin” than as an organizational figure in the transformation of Chicago's youth gangs into large federated street organizations. His history provides essential background for understanding both the Black Disciples and the Gangster Disciples.

Vernon “Wu” Coleman

Overview

Vernon “Wu” Coleman was an Atlanta-area Black Mafia Family associate convicted of participating in the organization's cocaine-distribution conspiracy. His appellate case provides a clear description of his role as a facilitator between BMF cocaine distributors and a buyer in Birmingham, Alabama.

Role in the Cocaine Network

The Eleventh Circuit stated that Coleman facilitated the sale of more than 150 kilograms of cocaine by BMF to a drug dealer in Birmingham. The court characterized him as an intermediary between distributors handling very large quantities of cocaine.

2007 Indictment

Coleman was charged in the Atlanta BMF case. When the indictment was announced, he was not yet in custody and was publicly listed as a fugitive.

Fugitive Period

According to the Eleventh Circuit, after learning that he had been indicted Coleman assumed a false identity and evaded arrest for at least two years.

Conviction and Sentence

Coleman was convicted of conspiracy to distribute five kilograms or more of cocaine and received an 87-month federal sentence. The Eleventh Circuit affirmed that sentence in 2011.

Historical Significance

Coleman's case is especially useful for understanding BMF as a distribution network rather than merely a collection of famous leaders. His role linked high-volume suppliers to a secondary regional market outside Atlanta and demonstrates how intermediaries extended BMF's reach across the Southeast.

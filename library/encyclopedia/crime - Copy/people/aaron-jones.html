Aaron Jones

Overview

Aaron Jones, identified in federal court records by aliases including “A” and “J,” was one of the principal leaders of Philadelphia's Junior Black Mafia (JBM). Federal prosecutors alleged that the JBM distributed large quantities of cocaine, crack cocaine, and heroin in the Philadelphia area from the mid-1980s through 1991.

Leadership Role

The Third Circuit's published opinion in the JBM prosecution identified Jones, Bryan Thornton, and Bernard Fields as principal leaders at different times. Witness testimony and recorded conversations were used to describe the organization's drug distribution, internal relationships, and leadership structure.

California Supply Connection

Federal court findings in related JBM proceedings described Jones assuming control of a California cocaine-supply connection associated with another organization member. This connection illustrates Philadelphia's role in the national cocaine distribution system: local organizations depended on wholesalers and transportation networks extending far beyond Pennsylvania.

Federal Prosecution

Jones was among the defendants charged in a 32-count federal indictment returned in October 1991. His trial was severed with those of Bryan Thornton and Bernard Fields. The government presented cooperating witnesses, wiretapped and consensually recorded conversations, documents, photographs, narcotics, firearms, and other physical evidence.

Jones was convicted on federal drug charges. The Third Circuit affirmed the convictions addressed in the 1993 appellate decision.

Historical Significance

Jones represents the leadership generation of Philadelphia trafficking organizations that emerged after the decline of the older Black Mafia. The JBM was younger, heavily oriented toward cocaine and crack as well as heroin, and prosecuted through modern federal conspiracy techniques.

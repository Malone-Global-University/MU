Frank Lucas Cooperation and Sentence Reduction — 1977–1981

The cooperation phase that transformed Frank Lucas from narcotics defendant into a major government witness.

Cooperation

By 1977, after receiving extremely long narcotics sentences, Lucas agreed to cooperate under a grant of use immunity. People v. Lucas states that he provided information used to build cases against attorneys and former narcotics associates, including Leslie Atkinson and Herman Jackson.

Consequences

The cooperation produced a chain reaction: Atkinson and Jackson later cooperated themselves, and their information helped reopen the George Ford homicide investigation. Lucas consistently denied involvement in homicides during his cooperation, according to the 1980 court opinion.

Sentence Reduction

In June 1981, Lucas’s 40-year federal sentence was reduced to the six years he had already served. Contemporary reporting credited his assistance with numerous drug-trafficking prosecutions in New York and North Carolina. His New Jersey sentence was also reconsidered.

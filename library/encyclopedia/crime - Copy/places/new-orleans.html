New Orleans

Overview

New Orleans, Louisiana, developed a distinctive urban criminal landscape in which drug organizations were frequently rooted in specific neighborhoods, blocks, and public housing developments rather than in nationally affiliated street gangs.

Neighborhood-Based Crews

A federal Louisiana Drug Threat Assessment published around 2001 stated that New Orleans had relatively few nationally affiliated gangs and that local law enforcement more often identified organizations by housing project or neighborhood. Contemporary examples included the Mosley Gang, Perry Francois Gang, Dillon Gang, and Got It Boys.

Crack Era

Crack cocaine was a major component of the city's drug economy during the late 1980s and 1990s. Federal reporting described outdoor hand-to-hand sales and the use of younger runners to insulate senior participants from law enforcement.

Heroin Market

By the late 1990s, heroin distribution was expanding in inner-city housing developments. Federal intelligence reported that New York-based Dominican wholesalers supplied some New Orleans street gangs, while black-tar heroin also reached the city from Oakland, California.

Federal Gang Strategy

From the 1990s forward, federal and local authorities increasingly used OCDETF investigations, multi-agency gang units, firearms prosecutions, wiretaps, RICO, and violent-crime statutes against neighborhood organizations.

Englewood, Chicago

Overview

Englewood on Chicago's South Side is a critical geographic setting in the history of several Black street organizations. Historical accounts connect the neighborhood to the rise of David Barksdale, Disciple organizations, and Larry Hoover's early gang activity.

Postwar Change

Englewood experienced major demographic, housing, economic, and institutional change during the postwar decades. Gang development should be understood within this broader urban context rather than explained solely through individual criminal personalities.

David Barksdale

Barksdale lived in Englewood after his family moved to Chicago and became an important gang leader during the 1960s. Later histories strongly associate him with the development of the Black Disciples.

Larry Hoover

Hoover likewise emerged from the South Side gang environment. His organization later aligned with Disciple leadership before the Gangster Disciples developed into a separate, highly structured organization.

Later Drug Markets

Englewood and surrounding South Side areas remained important to later gang and drug-market history, but specific blocks and factions changed repeatedly. Future entries should therefore document individual investigations and organizations rather than assigning the entire neighborhood to one gang.

Los Angeles

Overview

Los Angeles, California, became one of the most important American cocaine markets during the late twentieth century. Its significance derived from its large population, proximity to Pacific and Southwestern trafficking routes, extensive freeway network, international commercial connections, and its role as both a wholesale market and a launching point for interstate distribution.

The Crack Era

During the early and middle 1980s, falling wholesale cocaine prices combined with the spread of crack cocaine to transform neighborhood drug markets. South Central Los Angeles became one of the areas most strongly associated with the epidemic, but no single dealer or organization controlled the entire market.

Ricky Ross became one of the best-known distributors, while Thomas “Tootie” Reese, Wayne “Honcho” Day, Elrader “Ray Ray” Browning, the Bryant family, Jemeker Thompson, Michael “Harry-O” Harris, and other organizations operated within overlapping markets and periods.

Gang and Dealer Networks

The relationship between cocaine distribution and Los Angeles street gangs was complex. Individual Blood and Crip members participated heavily in retail and wholesale activity, but contemporary research challenged simplified claims that either gang operated as a single centrally directed narcotics organization. Dealer networks, family organizations, neighborhood crews, and gang-affiliated individuals all participated.

Law Enforcement

Los Angeles' drug economy prompted specialized task forces, aggressive narcotics enforcement, federal prosecutions, and controversial policing tactics. The Freeway Ricky Task Force became especially notable because allegations of corruption and evidence manipulation eventually compromised cases and generated separate federal investigations.

National Connections

Los Angeles organizations distributed cocaine into other states, while wholesalers in Southern California received cocaine through international and interstate supply chains. This made Los Angeles both a local retail market and a national distribution node.

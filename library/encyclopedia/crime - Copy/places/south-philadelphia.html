South Philadelphia

Overview

South Philadelphia was an important geographic setting in the origins of the criminal network later called the Philadelphia Black Mafia. During the late 1960s, neighborhood relationships, illegal gambling, robberies, narcotics activity, and street-group connections contributed to the development of the organization.

Origins of the Black Mafia

Academic histories trace the Black Mafia to a relatively small group of men associated with South Philadelphia. The organization later expanded beyond its original neighborhood base, but the area remained central to its early social and criminal formation.

South Street and the Dubrow Robbery

One of the most notorious crimes associated with members of the network occurred at Dubrow's Furniture Store on South Street on January 4, 1971. Eight men entered the store, committed an armed robbery, assaulted employees, set fires, and shot several victims, killing one employee.

Broader Philadelphia Network

As the Black Mafia expanded, its activities reached other sections of Philadelphia and the Delaware Valley. South Philadelphia should therefore be treated as an origin point rather than the exclusive territory of the organization.

Historical Significance

The neighborhood provides the geographic foundation for understanding how a local street and robbery network evolved into a broader organized-crime structure involved in narcotics and extortion.

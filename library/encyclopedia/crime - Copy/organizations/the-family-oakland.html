The Family (Oakland)

Overview

The Family was an Oakland heroin-trafficking organization associated with Milton “Mickey” Moore. It became one of the principal rivals of Felix Mitchell's 69 Mob during the late 1970s and early 1980s.

Conflict with the Mob

Contemporary reporting and later accounts of Oakland's narcotics history describe a violent struggle between the Family and Mitchell's organization. A cluster of killings in 1980 became a defining episode in the city's first major modern drug war.

The federal appellate record in the Mitchell-related prosecution describes multiple killings associated with the Mob's efforts to protect distribution territory. Reporting from the period identifies the Family as the competing organization in that conflict.

Organization and Leadership

Moore is consistently identified as the Family's principal leader. More detailed claims about internal membership, familial relationships, or command structure should be tied to court records or clearly attributed source material before being treated as established facts.

Historical Significance

The Family demonstrates that Oakland's heroin market was contested and organizationally plural. Its rivalry with the Mob is central to understanding the escalation of violence around narcotics distribution during the period.

Falcon–Magluta Organization

Overview

The Falcon–Magluta organization was a major cocaine-trafficking network led by Augusto Guillermo “Willie” Falcon and Salvador “Sal” Magluta. Federal authorities described it as based in the Southern District of Florida with operations extending to Southern California and other U.S. destinations.

Operating Period

Federal records place the organization's major cocaine activity from the late 1970s through mid-October 1991. A 1991 indictment charged its leaders and associates with a long-running conspiracy to import and distribute cocaine.

Distribution Scale

Ledgers recovered from a Magluta-controlled residence recorded 8,921 kilograms of cocaine distributed between January 1990 and October 1991 for more than $142 million. Prosecutors alleged still larger totals across the full life of the conspiracy.

California Connection

By the mid-1980s, the organization had established a Southern California base. Federal prosecutors stated that cocaine was distributed locally there and moved in large tractor-trailer shipments to other destinations, including South Florida.

Transportation and Stash System

Federal cases involving associates document trucking, stash houses, ledgers, cash storage, and concealed or distributed assets. The organization relied on multiple participants rather than only its two senior leaders.

Money Laundering and Obstruction

After the 1991 indictment, the organization's history increasingly involved efforts to protect assets and defendants. Later federal cases documented large-scale laundering of drug proceeds, witness interference, fugitive support, and bribery of jurors connected to the 1996 acquittal.

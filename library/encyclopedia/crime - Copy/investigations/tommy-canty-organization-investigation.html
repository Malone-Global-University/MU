Tommy Canty Organization Investigation

Overview

The Tommy Canty Organization investigation was a local and federal narcotics inquiry into a large Baltimore conspiracy distributing cocaine and heroin during the second half of the 1980s.

Local Investigation

Local authorities began investigating the Canty organization in 1987. Investigators were confronting a multilayered organization whose narcotics supply reached beyond Baltimore.

Federal Participation

Federal officials joined the investigation in 1988. The combined effort used undercover infiltration and wiretaps, allowing investigators to identify participants and reconstruct distribution relationships.

Supply Intelligence

The investigation established that Canty purchased cocaine and heroin from sources in New York, Miami, and Los Angeles. These findings are important because they connect Baltimore directly to several other cities in the Crime Encyclopedia's expanding national network.

Arrests and Indictment

Twenty-six members of the conspiracy were arrested. In late 1989, a federal grand jury indicted participants on charges including conspiracy to distribute cocaine and heroin between January 1986 and May 1989.

Historical Significance

The Canty investigation is a useful example of the federalization of urban narcotics enforcement: local intelligence was combined with federal resources, electronic surveillance, undercover infiltration, and a large conspiracy indictment.

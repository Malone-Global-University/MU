Overview

Film and Media History examines the development of moving-image entertainment and the theatrical, musical, technological, commercial, and cultural traditions that shaped it. The collection includes historical surveys, production histories, landmark works, legal disputes, and the backstories behind familiar figures and characters.

The initial articles trace connections between African American vaudeville, Broadway musical theater, the Jazz Age, early sound animation, and the Hollywood studio musical. As the collection grows, additional articles will extend its coverage across periods, regions, industries, and media.

Theatrical and Musical Foundations

Live variety entertainment and musical theater supplied the performers, songs, comic routines, and production conventions that early cinema adapted for the screen.

African American Vaudeville

The history of Black American variety theater, its performers, touring circuits, business networks, racial barriers, and contributions to musical theater, jazz, and motion pictures.

Shuffle Along (1921)

The landmark Broadway musical, its creators and performers, the development of syncopated musical theater, and its significance for African American theatrical opportunity.

Blackbirds of 1926

Lew Leslie's musical revue, Florence Mills's international stardom, and the transatlantic circulation of African American entertainment.

Early Animation and Entertainment Law

Early sound cartoons drew on vaudeville, popular music, celebrity performance, and new recording techniques. The resulting commercial characters also raised questions about artistic influence and the ownership of a public persona.

The Origins of Betty Boop

The character's development at Fleischer Studios, early visual designs, voice actresses, Jazz Age performance traditions, censorship, and competing historical accounts of her origins.

Helen Kane's Lawsuit Against Fleischer Studios

The litigation over Betty Boop, Kane's claims, the evidence concerning Baby Esther and earlier performance traditions, the 1934 judgment, and the subsequent appeal.

Motion Picture Histories

Individual films provide a means of examining production decisions, historical adaptations, musical repertoires, artistic collaborations, and the business of Hollywood.

Three Little Words (1950)

The MGM musical biography of songwriters Bert Kalmar and Harry Ruby, starring Fred Astaire and Red Skelton, including Debbie Reynolds's portrayal of Helen Kane and Kane's own singing contribution.

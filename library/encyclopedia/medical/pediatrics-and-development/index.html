Pediatrics and Development Overview

Pediatrics and development studies health, illness, growth, and medical care across infancy, childhood, and adolescence. This discipline focuses on how developing bodies and minds differ from adults, how age affects diagnosis and treatment, and how medical care must account for growth, nutrition, immunization, developmental milestones, family context, school life, and long-term health outcomes. It includes pediatrics, neonatology, developmental medicine, pediatric specialties, and adolescent medicine, each of which addresses a different stage or aspect of care for younger patients. By studying pediatrics and development, learners gain a framework for understanding child-centered medical decision-making, early-life risk, developmental assessment, preventive care, family involvement, and the transition from childhood healthcare to adult-oriented systems.

Categories

  • Adolescent Medicine

    Study healthcare for teenagers and young adults, including puberty, mental health, preventive care, risk behaviors, reproductive health, chronic conditions, identity, and transition to adult care.

  • Developmental Medicine

    Explore medical assessment of growth, learning, behavior, milestones, neurodevelopment, developmental delays, disabilities, early intervention, and long-term support for developing children.

  • Neonatology

    Examine the care of newborns, especially premature or medically fragile infants, including neonatal intensive care, respiratory support, feeding, infection risk, and early development.

  • Pediatric Specialties

    Learn how specialized pediatric fields manage complex child health conditions involving the heart, lungs, brain, kidneys, endocrine system, immune system, development, and chronic disease.

  • Pediatrics

    Study general medical care for infants, children, and adolescents, including well-child visits, immunizations, growth monitoring, acute illness, chronic conditions, and family-centered care.

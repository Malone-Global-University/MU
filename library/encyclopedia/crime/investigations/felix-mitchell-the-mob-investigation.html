Felix Mitchell / The Mob Investigation

Overview

The investigation of Felix Mitchell and the Mob developed over several years and combined narcotics intelligence, informant information, surveillance, homicide inquiries, searches, arrests, and federal prosecution. Court records show that federal agents were investigating the Oakland heroin organization well before the 1983 indictment.

Investigative Development

A later Ninth Circuit disposition summarized an affidavit describing a three-year investigation into an Oakland heroin organization known as the Mob. The affidavit referred to confidential-informant information concerning principals, suppliers, distribution locations, heroin and cocaine handling, and violence associated with the organization.

Because informant statements are investigative evidence rather than automatic findings of fact, this encyclopedia distinguishes allegations contained in affidavits from facts established through verdicts, judgments, or appellate findings.

1981 Arrest and Homicide Investigation

Oakland police arrested Mitchell near Los Angeles in December 1981 in connection with murder allegations arising from the city's drug war. Contemporary reporting described investigators as linking the violence to competition over Oakland's heroin trade.

Federal Indictment

On February 18, 1983, a federal grand jury returned an indictment against Mitchell and multiple co-defendants. Charges included conspiracy to distribute heroin. The first federal trial ended in a mistrial, followed by extensive litigation over prosecutorial misconduct and double jeopardy before the case returned for trial.

Investigative Significance

The Mitchell investigation is important because it demonstrates the transition from street-level narcotics enforcement toward enterprise-focused prosecution. Investigators attempted to establish hierarchy, suppliers, communications, proceeds, territorial enforcement, and the relationship between drug distribution and violence.

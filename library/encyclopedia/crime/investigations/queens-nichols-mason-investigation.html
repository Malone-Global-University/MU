Investigation of the Nichols and Mason Organizations

Multi-agency Queens investigation that targeted the Lorenzo Nichols and Howard Mason drug networks and intensified after the murders of parole officer Brian Rooney and NYPD Officer Edward Byrne.

Scope

The investigation combined narcotics, racketeering, homicide, and witness-protection concerns. The principal targets were overlapping South Jamaica networks associated with Lorenzo “Fat Cat” Nichols and Howard “Pappy” Mason.

Brian Rooney Homicide

The 1985 killing of parole officer Brian Rooney generated state and federal cases against members and associates of the Nichols organization. Later appellate decisions provide evidence about the retaliation motive, organization structure, and cooperation of defendants connected to the case.

Edward Byrne Homicide

The 1988 murder of Officer Edward Byrne substantially increased public and institutional pressure on the South Jamaica organizations. Federal prosecutors later convicted Howard Mason of ordering the killing.

1988 Coordinated Raids

Contemporary reporting described an August 1988 operation involving approximately 400 agents and arrests across multiple states. Authorities characterized the raids as the culmination of a two-year investigation into the Nichols enterprise and related defendants.

Federal Results

A Justice Department historical report stated that the broader investigation led to federal convictions of nineteen additional members of the Nichols and Mason gangs for narcotics, racketeering, or murder offenses. The Mason prosecution and later appellate cases provide the clearest surviving documentary record.

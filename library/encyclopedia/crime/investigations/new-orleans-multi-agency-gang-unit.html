New Orleans Multi-Agency Gang Unit

Overview

The New Orleans Multi-Agency Gang Unit (MAG Unit) was a coordinated federal, state, and local enforcement partnership created to investigate violent neighborhood groups and drug-trafficking organizations.

Participating Agencies

Federal public records describe participation by the New Orleans Police Department, Orleans Parish District Attorney's Office, Orleans Parish Sheriff's Office, Louisiana State Police, federal probation and parole authorities, the FBI, DEA, ATF, U.S. Marshals Service, and the U.S. Attorney's Office.

Group-Violence Strategy

The MAG Unit operated as part of the city's broader group-violence reduction strategy. Investigations attempted to identify small networks responsible for disproportionate levels of shootings, drug distribution, and firearms offenses.

FnD Investigation

The unit developed the federal case against the Frenchmen/Derbigny gang, or FnD, including defendants linked by prosecutors to the 2013 Mother's Day shooting and to drug and firearms conspiracies.

Broader Impact

By 2014, city and federal officials stated that the MAG Unit had contributed to indictments involving dozens of individuals associated with multiple groups.

Historical Significance

The unit represents the institutional counterpart to New Orleans' fragmented neighborhood-crew structure: rather than pursuing only one citywide gang, investigators targeted numerous smaller groups through shared intelligence and prosecution resources.

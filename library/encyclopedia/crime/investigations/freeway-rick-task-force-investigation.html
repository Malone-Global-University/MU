Freeway Ricky Task Force Investigation

Overview

The Freeway Ricky Task Force investigation began as a Los Angeles law-enforcement effort to dismantle the cocaine-distribution network of Ricky Donnell “Freeway Rick” Ross. It later became inseparable from a second investigative story: allegations that members of the task force themselves stole money, used excessive force, and manipulated evidence.

Initial Investigation of Ross

The Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department began investigating Ross in approximately 1985. In January 1987, the Sheriff's Department and LAPD created a joint unit, known formally as the Southwest Crew and informally as the Freeway Ricky Task Force.

April 1987 Pursuit

A major disputed incident occurred during an April 1987 pursuit of Ross. Officers claimed Ross fired a weapon and dropped cocaine while fleeing. Later federal allegations and testimony asserted instead that officers fired at Ross and planted a kilogram of cocaine to support charges against him.

Corruption Investigation

The broader scandal grew into federal scrutiny of narcotics officers for alleged theft of drug money and other misconduct. Those inquiries affected prosecutions built by the task force and undermined confidence in evidence obtained by some of its members.

Ross as Cooperating Witness

After Ross was later convicted in an interstate trafficking case, his knowledge of the task force became valuable to federal investigators. He testified against law-enforcement officers, and his cooperation contributed to a reduction in the time he served on that earlier sentence.

Assessment

The investigation is historically significant because it contains two overlapping criminal-justice narratives: a legitimate effort to investigate a major cocaine distributor and a corruption scandal involving some of the officers assigned to that effort. These should be documented together without treating allegations against particular officers as established beyond what court records support.

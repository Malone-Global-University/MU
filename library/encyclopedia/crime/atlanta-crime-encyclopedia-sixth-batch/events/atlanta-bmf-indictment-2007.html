Atlanta BMF Indictment, 2007

Overview

In July 2007, federal prosecutors in the Northern District of Georgia announced charges against sixteen Black Mafia Family members and associates in a cocaine-distribution conspiracy centered on the organization's Atlanta operations.

Defendants

The announced defendants included Fleming Daniels, Vernon Coleman, Barima McKnight, Lamar Fields, Victor Hammonds, Ramon Dobson, Franklin Nash, Darryl Taylor, Derrek Pitts, Jamal Mitchell, and others.

Relationship to Other BMF Cases

The Atlanta indictment was part of a larger multi-state enforcement campaign. Federal cases had already been brought in Detroit, Louisville, and Orlando, with additional BMF-related arrests and investigations in Los Angeles, St. Louis, Greenville, and other locations.

Disposition

Most Atlanta defendants resolved their cases through guilty pleas. Fleming Daniels proceeded to trial and was convicted of cocaine conspiracy in 2008. Vernon Coleman was arrested later after a fugitive period and ultimately received an 87-month sentence.

Historical Significance

The indictment converted Atlanta's BMF story from a highly visible local criminal culture into a fully documented federal conspiracy case. It also provides a formal defendant list for reconstructing the Atlanta network.

Atlanta

Overview

Atlanta, Georgia, became a major drug-distribution center for the southeastern and eastern United States. Its highway network, large metropolitan population, transportation infrastructure, and position between Southwestern supply routes and East Coast markets made the region attractive to national and regional trafficking organizations.

Transportation Geography

Atlanta is intersected by Interstates 20, 75, and 85. Federal drug-intelligence assessments found that traffickers used these corridors to move drugs into Atlanta and redistribute them throughout Georgia and to markets in Alabama, Florida, Kentucky, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee, and farther north.

Mexican-Source Supply

National Drug Intelligence Center reports identified Mexican trafficking organizations as major wholesale suppliers in the Atlanta HIDTA region. Cocaine, methamphetamine, marijuana, and other drugs moved to Atlanta from Texas, California, and Mexico through transportation and distribution cells.

Black Mafia Family

During the early 2000s, Atlanta became one of the principal operating hubs of the Black Mafia Family. Federal prosecutors stated that BMF moved hundreds of kilograms of cocaine into Atlanta, Detroit, and other hubs each month at its 2003–2004 peak.

Redistribution to Other Cities

Atlanta's role extended beyond local sales. Federal intelligence assessments repeatedly described the region as a redistribution point for eastern U.S. markets. BMF's Vernon Coleman case likewise documents cocaine moving from the organization toward Birmingham, Alabama.

Historical Significance

Atlanta is strategically important to the Crime Encyclopedia because it connects the Southwest and Mexican-source supply system with Southern and East Coast markets. It also links directly to the existing Detroit, Los Angeles, Baltimore, and Washington-area material.

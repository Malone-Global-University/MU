United States v. Fleming Daniels (2007–2009)

Overview

United States v. Fleming Daniels was the principal trial-level Atlanta case arising from the 2007 Black Mafia Family indictment. Daniels was the only Atlanta defendant in that indictment to proceed through a federal jury trial.

Charges

Daniels was charged with conspiracy to possess with intent to distribute five kilograms or more of cocaine and with possession with intent to distribute 500 grams or more of cocaine.

Government Evidence

The government relied on testimony from BMF members, customers, and associates. The Eleventh Circuit later summarized evidence that Daniels was a high-ranking BMF member, was often present at the Atlanta storehouse where cocaine and money were exchanged, and sold kilogram bricks of cocaine to at least one outside purchaser.

Verdict

The jury convicted Daniels of the cocaine conspiracy. It found him responsible for distributing more than fifty kilograms of cocaine. The jury did not reach a verdict on the separate possession-with-intent-to-distribute count.

Sentence

Daniels received a twenty-year federal prison sentence in December 2008. His prior Georgia cocaine conviction affected the sentencing framework.

Appeal

Daniels challenged evidentiary rulings, the use of his prior conviction, and the reasonableness of his sentence. In 2009, the Eleventh Circuit affirmed the district court.

Relationship to State Homicide Case

The federal cocaine prosecution was legally separate from the Fulton County case concerning the death of Rashannibal Drummond. Daniels later pleaded guilty to voluntary manslaughter in that state case.

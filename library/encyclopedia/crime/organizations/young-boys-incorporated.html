Young Boys Incorporated

Detroit heroin and cocaine organization whose late-1970s and 1980s structure became an important model in the city’s organized street-drug history.

Overview

Young Boys Incorporated (YBI) operated in Detroit as a structured narcotics organization whose principal activity was the purchase and sale of heroin and cocaine. Contemporary police and federal accounts drew particular attention to its use of young sellers and its highly organized distribution system.

Organization and Branding

The Sixth Circuit recorded that YBI sold heroin in small envelopes carrying a distinctive “A-team” logo and used violence to reduce competition and enforce internal discipline. The University of Michigan’s archival history project describes a layered structure in which senior leaders controlled supply and organization while younger workers handled street sales.

Leadership

Historical sources identify Milton “Butch” Jones, Dwayne “Wonderful Wayne” Davis, Raymond “Baby Ray” Peoples, and Mark “Block” Marshall among YBI’s principal leaders. Federal appellate records center especially on Jones during the organization’s later prosecution period.

Federal Pressure and Continuation

Federal convictions substantially reduced YBI activity in 1983, but the Sixth Circuit found sufficient evidence that the enterprise continued in later form through 1987, including activity allegedly directed by Jones from prison.

Historical Significance

YBI is important to Detroit crime history because its structure predates the city’s crack-era organizations. Later groups should not automatically be described as direct descendants, but YBI established an organizational precedent within the local narcotics market.

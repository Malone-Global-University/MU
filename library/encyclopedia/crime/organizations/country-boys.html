The Country Boys — Frank Lucas Organization

Harlem-centered heroin distribution network led by Frank Lucas and built substantially around relatives and associates from North Carolina.

Classification

Primary base: Harlem, Manhattan
Operational extension: New Jersey and the New York metropolitan area
Primary era: late 1960s–mid-1970s

Organization

Contemporary police material published in the Congressional Record described Frank Lucas as heading a ring known as the “Country Boys.” Later accounts emphasize his use of brothers, relatives, and trusted North Carolina associates. The name should be treated as a documented law-enforcement/public label for the network, not necessarily a formal corporate title used uniformly by every participant.

Relationship to Nicky Barnes

The Country Boys and Barnes’s Council were separate organizations operating in overlapping New York heroin markets. Later reporting described Lucas and Barnes as major competitors. Lucas was not a Council member.

Collapse

The network was weakened by investigations, the 1974 cooperation and killing of George Ford, the 1975 Teaneck raid and narcotics prosecutions, and successive cooperation by Morris, Sims, Lucas, and later Atkinson and Jackson.

Evidence Status

A/B. The Country Boys label is documented in contemporary police/public records, while the most precise internal hierarchy comes from later court opinions reconstructing the Lucas enterprise.

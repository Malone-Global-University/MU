Black P. Stone Nation and El Rukn

Overview

The organization associated with Jeff Fort changed names and structure across several decades. Federal courts summarized the sequence as the Blackstone Rangers in the 1960s, the Black P. Stone Nation in the 1970s, and the El Rukns in the 1980s.

Blackstone Rangers

The Blackstone Rangers emerged on Chicago's South Side during the 1960s as one of the city's large federated street organizations. Fort became one of its principal leaders.

Black P. Stone Nation

During the 1970s, the organization was commonly known as the Black P. Stone Nation. Federal and state records describe continuity in leadership while also showing changes in structure, membership, and criminal activity.

El Rukn Phase

By the 1980s, the organization had become known as El Rukn. Federal courts described a hierarchy headed by Fort as Imam, followed by generals, officer muftis, ambassadors, and soldiers. The organization maintained headquarters and other properties on Chicago's South Side.

Criminal Activities

Federal indictments and court opinions associated El Rukn members with narcotics distribution, weapons offenses, murders, attempted murders, witness intimidation, kidnapping, and obstruction of justice. A sweeping 1989 federal indictment charged dozens of alleged members and described more than two decades of criminal activity.

Libya Conspiracy

The most unusual federal case arose from an effort to obtain money from Libya in exchange for proposed terrorist activity in the United States. Members traveled to Libya, negotiated with officials, and obtained a rocket launcher during an undercover federal operation. Fort and several senior members were convicted in 1987.

Source and Terminology Note

The three principal names are historically connected but should not be treated as exact synonyms. A page discussing the 1960s should generally use Blackstone Rangers; 1970s material may use Black P. Stone Nation; and 1980s federal cases generally use El Rukn.

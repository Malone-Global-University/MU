New York Boys

Overview

The New York Boys were a large-scale cocaine and crack-cocaine distribution organization operating from the Orchard Park Housing Project in Roxbury. Federal appellate records identify Darryl Whiting as the principal organizer and supervisor.

Orchard Park Base

The organization used Orchard Park as its primary operating environment. The housing development became a central geographic focus of the federal investigation and later prosecution.

Scale

A December 1990 indictment charged Whiting and more than two dozen co-defendants. A superseding indictment returned in April 1991 expanded the case to fifty defendants.

Criminal Enterprise

Federal prosecutors alleged a broad cocaine conspiracy, substantive distribution offenses, firearms violations, money laundering, and a continuing criminal enterprise led by Whiting.

Undercover Investigation

The First Circuit described the case as arising from an extensive undercover law-enforcement operation. Cooperating witnesses and undercover officers were central to proving the organization's distribution activities.

Historical Significance

The New York Boys represent one of Boston's defining crack-era organizations and provide an early example of a housing-project-based ring becoming the subject of a large federal conspiracy and continuing-criminal-enterprise case.

Black Disciples

Overview

The Black Disciples are a Chicago street organization with roots in the South Side gang consolidations of the 1960s. David Barksdale became one of the organization's most important early leaders and remains central to its historical identity.

Early Development

Historical accounts agree that the organization grew from Disciple groups active on Chicago's South Side, but they differ on the exact founding date, predecessor names, and which individuals should be classified as formal founders. This encyclopedia therefore uses broad chronological language where the record is disputed.

David Barksdale

Barksdale rose to prominence during the 1960s and became closely identified with the Black Disciples. His leadership occurred during a period when Chicago's smaller neighborhood gangs were consolidating into larger federations capable of operating across multiple communities.

Relationship to Larry Hoover

Barksdale's Disciple organization and the Gangster group associated with Larry Hoover entered an alliance during the late 1960s and early 1970s. Federal Justice Department histories describe this alignment as an important precursor to the Gangster Disciples.

Later Separation

After Barksdale's death in 1974, Disciple organizations continued to evolve. Black Disciples and Gangster Disciples should be treated as distinct organizations in later periods even though their histories intersect.

Crime Encyclopedia Scope

This first Chicago batch treats the Black Disciples primarily as an organizational-history component rather than attempting to catalogue every later faction. Later expansion should document specific drug organizations, prosecutions, neighborhoods, and leaders only where reliable case-level evidence supports separate entries.

Crime Organizations

A structured directory of crime families, syndicates, cartels, drug-distribution enterprises, gangs, alliances, and neighborhood crews.

Overview

Browse criminal organizations by their principal organizational form. Select a category below to see its individual crime families, syndicates, cartels, gangs, trafficking enterprises, and documented crews.

This is a classification directory, not a claim that organizations grouped together share leaders, membership, criminal responsibility, or historical origins. Broad umbrella terms, such as “Yakuza” or “Mexican drug cartels,” should be distinguished from particular organizations.

Browse Organizational Categories

  • Mafias & Crime Syndicates

    Historical and contemporary mafia families, syndicates, organized-crime networks, and associated institutions. Not all entries describe a single unified enterprise.

  • Cartels & Transnational Trafficking

    Cartels, importation networks, international wholesale operations, and multistate trafficking enterprises. Entries are grouped here by the scale and nature of their supply networks.

  • Domestic Drug Distribution Enterprises

    Named drug-distribution organizations and supply networks centered primarily in United States cities. Geographic headings are navigational aids, not claims of common command.

  • Street Gangs & Alliances

    Street gangs, longstanding gang organizations, and umbrella alliances. A gang affiliation is not necessarily a single centrally directed criminal enterprise.

  • Prison Gangs & Prison-Origin Organizations

    Organizations formed in correctional settings or substantially shaped by prison structures, whose criminal activities may extend outside prison.

  • Neighborhood Crews & Local Criminal Networks

    Neighborhood-centered gangs, housing-development crews, and locally concentrated criminal enterprises. Some combined drug distribution with other offenses; not all possessed formal syndicate structures.

How This Directory Works

Level 1: organizational categories on this page. Level 2: named organizations and network collections on each category page. Deeper levels: specific constituent families, factions, crews, and regional branches where separate articles exist.

Each organization has one primary listing; related groups can be cross-referenced within their own articles. Geography, ethnicity, historical period, and criminal activity may be used as supplementary descriptors, but do not imply guilt by identity or group affiliation. Entries also distinguish allegations, convictions, documented history, and uncertain current status.

69 Mob / The Mob

Overview

The 69 Mob, also described in federal court records simply as the Mob, was an Oakland heroin-distribution enterprise led by Felix Mitchell. The organization became active in San Antonio Village by 1976 and developed a layered structure for street sales, money collection, surveillance, and management.

Structure

The Ninth Circuit described the organization as containing approximately twenty to twenty-five members arranged in a hierarchy. Some members sold heroin directly, others collected payments, and lookouts warned sellers about approaching police officers. Signals and walkie-talkies were used to coordinate activity.

Proceeds moved upward through the organization to Mitchell, who then distributed payments to participants. The appellate court stated that the enterprise at times generated as much as $15,000 per day.

Territory and Distribution

The organization began in San Antonio Village and adapted its street-level operations as police pressure increased. Contemporary reports described activity shifting among Oakland neighborhoods and housing complexes as enforcement intensified.

Rival Organizations

The Mob's principal competitors included The Family, associated with Mickey Moore, and later Funktown USA. Court records and news reporting describe violent clashes over drug-selling territory. These conflicts contributed to a wider pattern of narcotics-related killings in Oakland.

Federal Enforcement

A multi-year federal investigation culminated in the 1983 indictment of Mitchell and several co-defendants. The prosecution generated a complex sequence of trials and appeals. Several co-defendants' convictions were later affirmed in United States v. Patterson.

Historical Significance

The organization is important as a documented example of a highly structured urban heroin enterprise operating before the crack era. The federal record provides unusually specific information about hierarchy, communication, street distribution, proceeds, and territorial enforcement.

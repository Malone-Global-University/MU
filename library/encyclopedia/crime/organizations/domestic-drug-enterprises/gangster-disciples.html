Gangster Disciples

Overview

The Gangster Disciples are a Chicago-based street organization that developed into a highly structured criminal enterprise with activity extending beyond Illinois. Federal court records from the 1990s document an extensive hierarchy and large-scale distribution of cocaine, crack cocaine, heroin, and other drugs.

Origins

Justice Department histories trace the organization to Chicago's South Side and to the alignment of groups associated with Larry Hoover and David Barksdale. The terminology used during the 1960s and 1970s varied, and later Gangster Disciples identity should not be projected backward onto every earlier Disciple or Gangster faction.

Hierarchy

Federal cases describe a corporate-style structure headed by a chairman of the board. Below the chairman were board members, governors, assistant governors, regents, coordinators, security personnel, treasurers, enforcers, and street-level members.

Drug Distribution

The organization used territorial control to organize street-level retail drug sales. Members sold cocaine, crack cocaine, heroin, and other drugs. Supervisors coordinated sellers, collected payments, and enforced rules against internal misconduct and rival encroachment.

Financial Collection

Federal trial evidence described regular payments collected from members and, in some locations, from unaffiliated dealers permitted to sell within Gangster Disciples territory. Prosecutors also presented evidence concerning organizational fundraising and the movement of drug proceeds upward through the hierarchy.

1990s Federal Prosecutions

A series of federal cases targeted senior leadership during the 1990s. Larry Hoover, Gregory Shell, Andrew Howard, Tirenzy Wilson, Jerry Strawhorn, and others were convicted in major narcotics and continuing-criminal-enterprise prosecutions.

National Expansion

Justice Department assessments later identified Gangster Disciples factions in numerous states. National expansion should be treated separately from Chicago's original structure because local factions can vary in leadership, organization, and criminal activity.

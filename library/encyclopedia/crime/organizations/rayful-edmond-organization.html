Rayful Edmond Organization

Large Washington, D.C. cocaine-distribution organization led by Rayful Edmond III during the second half of the 1980s.

Overview

The “Rayful Edmond organization” is the label used in federal court records and contemporary reporting for the large-scale cocaine network led by Rayful Edmond III. The organization operated primarily in Washington, D.C., from the mid-1980s until a major federal law-enforcement operation culminated in Edmond’s 1989 arrest and prosecution.

Organization

The D.C. Circuit described a multi-layered enterprise composed largely of Edmond’s relatives, friends, supervisors, street sellers, couriers, stash-house operators, and wholesale associates. The organization’s principal open-air market was Morton Place and Orleans Place NE, known as “the Strip.” Senior figures insulated themselves through lieutenants who supplied sellers, collected proceeds, and supervised sales activity.

Headquarters and Distribution Geography

Federal evidence identified 407 M Street NE, a family residence, as a hub of the conspiracy. Drugs were also stored and processed at multiple residences and stash locations before distribution to the Strip and other markets. Contemporary officials estimated that at its height the organization controlled a substantial share of the District’s cocaine market, although numerical estimates varied by source and stage of the investigation.

California Supply Chain

The organization’s large-scale supply chain extended to California. The D.C. Circuit summarized evidence that Edmond and associates traveled to Los Angeles to arrange purchases and payments through Melvin D. Butler. Couriers then transported kilogram quantities of cocaine to the Washington area. Contemporary trial reporting traced the upstream supply further to Colombian sources.

Investigation and Prosecution

An intensive investigation involving federal and local agencies used surveillance, searches, informants, recorded conversations, and financial evidence. Edmond and multiple associates were arrested in April 1989. In December 1989, a federal jury convicted Edmond and ten co-defendants in the principal drug-conspiracy trial.

Relationship to the Martinez–Perry Network

The Edmond organization should not be merged with the later Washington activities of Alberto “Alpo” Martinez and Wayne “Silk” Perry. Martinez entered an already-developed D.C. drug market. The available court record establishes the Edmond organization independently and does not show Martinez as an Edmond lieutenant or member. For the encyclopedia, the safest relationship label is distinct organizations operating in overlapping D.C. narcotics markets and adjacent periods.

Key Individuals

  • Rayful Edmond III — leader.
  • Melvin D. Butler — California supply connection and convicted co-conspirator.
  • Tony Lewis — friend/partner identified in federal records; planned entry.
  • Constance “Bootsie” Perry — Edmond’s mother; family member connected to later prosecutions and cooperation history.

Evidence Status

A. The organization’s structure, personnel, supply chain, and prosecution are documented extensively in federal appellate records and contemporary reporting.

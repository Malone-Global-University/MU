Preacher Crew

Harlem-and-Bronx racketeering organization led by Clarence “Preacher” Heatley and also known in federal records as the “Family.”

Overview

Federal court records describe the Preacher Crew as a criminal organization operating primarily in Harlem and the Bronx from approximately 1983 through 1996. The enterprise combined narcotics trafficking with murder, assault, extortion, robbery, kidnapping, and other acts of violence.

Leadership and Membership

  • Clarence “Preacher” Heatley: leader of the organization.
  • John “Apple” Porter: described in a later federal order as a founding member.
  • Other charged members and associates: included John Cuff, Derrick Hailstock, Raymond Jackson, and numerous additional defendants in the federal racketeering prosecution.

Donnell Porter Connection

The United States’ 1997 death-penalty notice against Heatley alleged that he participated in the kidnapping, torture, and murder of twelve-year-old William “Donnell” Porter between December 1989 and January 1990. Because later street histories attribute additional roles to other crew members, the event page should distinguish Heatley’s explicit federal attribution from claims that currently rest on secondary or participant sources.

Federal Prosecution

The organization became the subject of a large Southern District of New York racketeering prosecution. Federal records describe charges involving RICO, violent crimes in aid of racketeering, continuing criminal enterprise offenses, firearms violations, and numerous homicides. Heatley pleaded guilty in 1999 in exchange for withdrawal of the federal death-penalty notice and received life imprisonment plus additional consecutive terms.

Relationship to Other Harlem Networks

The Preacher Crew should be kept analytically separate from the Azie Faison–Rich Porter–Alpo Martinez cocaine network and from Richard “Fritz” Simmons’s wholesale-supply story. The crews overlapped geographically and could intersect through victims, associates, and extortion targets, but overlap does not establish common organizational membership.

Meredith–Ricks–King Heroin Organization

Overview

The Meredith–Ricks–King Heroin Organization is a descriptive label for a major Baltimore narcotics network identified in federal appellate decisions. Clarence Meredith, Thomas “Joe Dancer” Ricks, and Maurice “Peanut” King were described by the Fourth Circuit as leaders of the organization.

Distribution Model

The organization distributed heroin through multiple street territories. Lieutenants supervised sales in particular areas, while higher-ranking members supplied heroin and collected proceeds. The structure combined central leadership with decentralized street-level distribution.

Stash and Processing Operations

During the reorganized 1983–1985 phase, Clarence Meredith maintained a stash house in Cockeysville, Maryland. Members of the Meredith family and other associates cut and packaged raw and street-quality heroin before distributing it to street lieutenants.

Recurring Meetings

Federal court records describe regularly scheduled meetings between senior members and street lieutenants. Heroin was supplied at these meetings, and lieutenants returned money collected from prior sales. This recurring structure provides unusually detailed evidence of the organization's internal operating rhythm.

Law-Enforcement Penetration

James “Scotty” Wiggins became a cooperating source after his July 1985 arrest while carrying hundreds of bags of heroin. Investigators used him to record conversations and monitor organizational meetings. His cooperation helped lead to the October 30, 1985 arrests of Meredith and other members.

Lorenzo “Fat Cat” Nichols Organization

Queens narcotics organization active in the mid-to-late 1980s and connected by documented supplier and personnel relationships to other South Jamaica drug networks.

Overview

The Lorenzo Nichols organization was a South Jamaica-based narcotics enterprise led by Lorenzo “Fat Cat” Nichols. Federal court records identify members operating drug-distribution locations under Nichols’s leadership by 1985 and describe violence used to retaliate against law-enforcement pressure.

Leadership and Personnel

Nichols was the organization’s leader. Federal cases identify senior figures and gang superiors including Chris “Jughead” Williams and Joseph “Bobo” Rogers. Howard “Pappy” Mason also appears as a major Nichols associate in judicial records before emerging as leader of the separate Bebos organization.

Drug Distribution

The organization controlled drug-selling locations in Queens and rewarded members with money and access to distribution territory. Contemporary reporting in 1988 described a large interstate investigation targeting the organization and related networks.

Alliance with the Supreme Team

In 1987, the Nichols organization supplied powder cocaine to the Supreme Team. Federal appellate evidence also shows Gerald “Prince” Miller assisting Nichols in an effort to locate Henry and Isaac Bolden after Nichols suspected them of robbing his organization. This establishes both a supplier relationship and operational cooperation between the two otherwise distinct organizations.

Relationship to the Bebos

The Nichols organization and the Bebos overlapped through Howard Mason and other associates but should not be treated as the same organization. By 1988, federal courts described the Bebos as a Mason-led gang with its own crack-distribution operation, while Justice Department historical reporting referred to Mason’s and Nichols’s gangs as two separate organizations targeted by the same sweeping investigation.

Violence and Retaliation

The killing of parole officer Brian Rooney is the clearest documented retaliation event tied to Nichols’s command. Court records also describe threats against cooperators and other violent conduct associated with the enterprise.

Historical Family Profiles

Northeast

Bonanno Crime Family

Historical center: New York City, New York.

One of New York City’s Five Families, this organization emerged from the Castellammarese faction associated with Salvatore Maranzano. Its history includes Joseph Bonanno’s long tenure, internal leadership conflicts, and later federal investigations and prosecutions.

Dedicated family directory: bonanno/index.html (filename only).

Return to family directory

Colombo Crime Family

Historical center: New York City, New York.

Originally associated with Joseph Profaci, this New York family acquired the Colombo name after Joseph Colombo rose to leadership. Its modern history is marked by succession struggles, factional violence, and federal racketeering cases.

Dedicated family directory: colombo/index.html (filename only).

Return to family directory

Gambino Crime Family

Historical center: New York City, New York.

A major New York family whose early leadership included Vincent Mangano before the organization became associated with Carlo Gambino. Paul Castellano’s leadership and John Gotti’s later rise are central to its twentieth-century history.

Dedicated family directory: gambino/index.html (filename only).

Return to family directory

Genovese Crime Family

Historical center: New York City, New York.

Tracing its modern structure to the Luciano organization, the Genovese family played a central role in the development of the Mafia Commission. Important leaders included Frank Costello, Vito Genovese, and Vincent Gigante.

Dedicated family directory: genovese/index.html (filename only).

Return to family directory

Lucchese Crime Family

Historical center: New York City, New York.

This New York family developed from the organization led by Gaetano Reina and later Tommy Gagliano. Under Tommy Lucchese and subsequent leaders, its history included extensive activity in labor and transportation-related rackets.

Dedicated family directory: lucchese/index.html (filename only).

Return to family directory

Buffalo Crime Family

Historical center: Buffalo, New York.

Commonly associated with Stefano Magaddino, the Buffalo family historically exercised influence in western New York and maintained connections across the U.S.–Canada border. Its relationships with Rochester and other upstate groups changed over time.

Dedicated family directory: buffalo/index.html (filename only).

Return to family directory

Philadelphia Crime Family

Historical center: Philadelphia, Pennsylvania.

Long associated with Philadelphia and southern New Jersey, this family became especially prominent under Angelo Bruno. Leadership conflicts and violent restructuring under Nicodemo Scarfo shaped its later twentieth-century history.

Dedicated family directory: philadelphia/index.html (filename only).

Return to family directory

Pittsburgh Crime Family

Historical center: Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania.

The Pittsburgh family operated primarily in western Pennsylvania, with connections extending into the Ohio Valley. John LaRocca and subsequent leadership figures were associated with gambling and other regional rackets.

Dedicated family directory: pittsburgh/index.html (filename only).

Return to family directory

Bufalino Crime Family

Historical center: Pittston / Scranton / Wilkes-Barre, Pennsylvania.

Centered in northeastern Pennsylvania rather than Philadelphia or Pittsburgh, this smaller organization became closely associated with Russell Bufalino. Its significance extended beyond its local territory through relationships with other Mafia families.

Dedicated family directory: bufalino/index.html (filename only).

Return to family directory

DeCavalcante Crime Family

Historical center: Elizabeth / Newark area, New Jersey.

The DeCavalcante organization was a distinct New Jersey Mafia family, historically associated with Elizabeth and nearby communities. Its operations and leadership have been documented through law-enforcement investigations and prosecutions.

Dedicated family directory: decavalcante/index.html (filename only).

Return to family directory

Patriarca Crime Family

Historical center: Providence, Rhode Island / Boston, Massachusetts.

The Patriarca family was a single New England Mafia organization with major centers in Providence and Boston, not two independent city families. Raymond Patriarca Sr. is one of its best-known historical leaders.

Dedicated family directory: patriarca/index.html (filename only).

Midwest

Chicago Outfit

Historical center: Chicago, Illinois.

The Chicago Outfit became one of the country’s most influential crime organizations through the Prohibition-era ascendancy of Johnny Torrio and Al Capone. Subsequent leaders and senior figures included Tony Accardo, Sam Giancana, and Joseph Aiuppa.

Dedicated family directory: chicago-outfit/index.html (filename only).

Detroit Crime Family

Historical center: Detroit, Michigan.

Often called the Detroit Partnership, this family developed a reputation for durable leadership networks associated with the Zerilli and Tocco names. Its history encompassed organized gambling, labor influence, and regional criminal enterprises.

Cleveland Crime Family

Historical center: Cleveland, Ohio.

Cleveland’s Mafia organization was closely associated with John Scalish and later with James Licavoli. Its modern history includes conflict with rival racketeers and major federal investigations.

Milwaukee Crime Family

Historical center: Milwaukee, Wisconsin.

The Milwaukee family became especially associated with Frank Balistrieri. Federal investigations connected its leadership with illegal gambling and schemes involving the diversion of revenue from Las Vegas casinos.

Madison Crime Family

Historical center: Madison, Wisconsin.

Madison had a small, separately identified Mafia family historically associated with Carlo Caputo and Joseph Aiello. By the 1970s, the group had substantially diminished and reportedly dissolved, making it a historical rather than a currently active family entry.

Rockford Crime Family

Historical center: Rockford, Illinois.

Rockford had a separately identified Mafia organization associated with figures including Antonio Musso and Joseph Zammuto. Though geographically close to Chicago, it should not automatically be treated as merely a Chicago Outfit crew.

The Kansas City family was closely associated with the Civella organization. Its history includes gambling operations and federal cases concerning the diversion of proceeds from Las Vegas casinos.

St. Louis supported its own Mafia family, historically associated with Anthony Giordano and later leadership figures. The organization engaged in regional gambling and other rackets and was investigated in federal organized-crime inquiries.

Historical center: New Orleans, Louisiana.

One of the oldest centers of Italian-American Mafia activity, New Orleans developed criminal networks well before the modern Five Families structure. Carlos Marcello became its best-known twentieth-century leader.

Based principally in Tampa, the Trafficante family was associated with Santo Trafficante Sr. and Santo Trafficante Jr. Its historical reach included Florida gambling and connections to pre-revolutionary Cuba.

Dallas had a small, historically documented Mafia family associated with the Piranio brothers and Joseph Civello. It was not as dominant as other local organized-crime syndicates and appears to have ceased operating as an independent family decades ago.

The organization associated with Frank Zito has been classified by some investigators and historians as an independent Mafia family and by others as closely subordinate to Chicago. Its precise organizational status should be qualified by period and source.

Rochester Crime Family

Historical center: Rochester, New York.

Rochester was historically associated with Buffalo’s wider Mafia sphere but developed substantial independent leadership under Frank Valenti and later Samuel Russotti. Whether it is counted as a separate family depends in part on the historical period under examination.

Classification and Sources

“Crime family” denotes a historically documented organized-crime grouping, not a biological family or an assertion of present-day criminal involvement. New England is counted as one Patriarca family spanning Boston and Providence. Las Vegas, Miami, and Atlantic City are not listed as independent families; they were important territories for various families. Rochester and Springfield require period-specific classification, and the status of some very small historical groups is disputed.

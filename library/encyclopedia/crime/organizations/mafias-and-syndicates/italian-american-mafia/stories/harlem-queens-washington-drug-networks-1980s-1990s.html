Harlem, Queens, and Washington Drug Networks, 1980s–1990s

A network history of the New York and Washington figures whose lives and cases intersected with the historical background later popularized by Paid in Full.

Overview

The people commonly grouped together in documentaries, interviews, music, and street history did not belong to one unified criminal organization. The more accurate model is a set of overlapping urban networks centered in Harlem, South Jamaica in Queens, Brooklyn, and Washington, D.C., with interstate supply and distribution connections extending to California, Northern Virginia, and Fredericksburg.

Within this structure, Alberto “Alpo” Martinez is a major bridge between Harlem and Washington. Kenneth “Supreme” McGriff and Gerald “Prince” Miller belong to the separate Supreme Team history in Queens. Rayful Edmond III led a distinct and earlier large D.C. organization whose documented California connection included Melvin D. Butler. Wayne “Silk” Perry belongs primarily to the later Martinez-associated Washington branch. Rich Porter and Azie Faison belong to the Harlem branch. Domencio Benson provides a Brooklyn-to-Washington connection.

Harlem Branch

The central Harlem cocaine story is the relationship among Azie Faison, Rich Porter, and Alpo Martinez, but the wider structure now includes two additional layers. Richard “Fritz” Simmons appears in participant and biographical sources as an upstream wholesaler/consignment supplier, while Kevin Chiles represents a separate Harlem operation that extended into Washington.

A distinct violence-and-extortion layer is represented by the Preacher Crew, led by Clarence “Preacher” Heatley. A federal death-penalty notice alleged that Heatley participated in the kidnapping, torture, and murder of Donnell Porter. John “Apple” Porter, Donnell’s uncle, was a founding Preacher Crew member; later claims that he arranged the kidnapping should remain separately attributed because the currently identified court record is more explicit about Heatley than about Apple.

Queens Branch

The Supreme Team should be treated as a distinct organization rather than folded into the Harlem trio. Federal records identify Kenneth “Supreme” McGriff as founder and Gerald “Prince” Miller as his nephew and second-in-command. The organization was based around the Baisley Park Houses in Jamaica, Queens.

The Queens branch now includes a second documented power center: the Lorenzo “Fat Cat” Nichols organization. The Second Circuit documented a 1987 alliance in which Nichols’s organization supplied the Supreme Team with powder cocaine. Howard “Pappy” Mason was closely associated with Nichols before leading the separate Bebos. The killings of parole officer Brian Rooney in 1985 and NYPD Officer Edward Byrne in 1988 form major event nodes in this Queens network.

Washington Branch I: Rayful Edmond Organization

Rayful Edmond’s organization was a large, independently documented Washington cocaine enterprise operating before and during the period when Martinez began expanding into the region. Federal records describe a layered organization centered on Morton and Orleans places NE and supplied in part through a California connection involving Melvin D. Butler. This organization should not be retroactively merged into the later Martinez–Perry network.

Washington Branch II: Martinez and Perry

Martinez’s expansion brought him into the Washington drug market and into association with Wayne Perry. Contemporary reporting and later federal proceedings connect Perry to multiple killings tied to the Martinez organization. The D.C. branch should also include investigators such as Angelo Parisi and Dan Reilly so that the story does not become a criminal-only genealogy.

Curtis “Curtbone” Chambers provides an individual bridge between the Edmond and Martinez eras. The Washington Post identified him as one of Edmond’s co-defendants and a later documentary collaborator; in participant interviews he describes meeting and doing business with Martinez. This does not make the two organizations one enterprise.

Shelton “Shorty Pop” Walker/Watkins also belongs to the late-1980s D.C. environment. Contemporary reporting documents his 1989 fugitive case and the Matthew Blake homicide charge, while later oral histories connect him socially to Perry and Martinez. Those later relationship claims should remain attributed.

Current evidence rule: geographic and chronological overlap between Edmond and Martinez does not establish that Martinez worked for Edmond or that the two organizations were one enterprise. Until direct evidence is found, the connection should be labeled market overlap/succession, not partnership.

Key Turning Points

  1. October 10, 1985 — New York State parole officer Brian Rooney is killed in Queens in retaliation ordered by Lorenzo Nichols.
  2. 1987 — the Supreme Team reaches a documented peak in Queens; federal evidence documents an alliance and powder-cocaine supply relationship with the Nichols organization.
  3. February 26, 1988 — NYPD Officer Edward Byrne is murdered in South Jamaica on an order later federally attributed to Howard Mason.
  4. August 1988 — a large coordinated federal operation targets the Nichols and Mason organizations.
  5. April 1989 — federal and local authorities arrest Rayful Edmond and numerous associates in Washington.
  6. December 5, 1989 — William “Donnell” Porter is kidnapped in Harlem; a later federal death-penalty notice alleges Clarence Heatley’s participation in the kidnapping, torture, and murder.
  7. December 6, 1989 — Matthew N. Blake is killed during a shootout in Southeast Washington; Shelton Walker/Watkins is later wanted on a murder charge in the case.
  8. December 6, 1989 — Edmond and ten co-defendants are convicted in the principal D.C. federal drug trial.
  9. January 3–4, 1990 — Rich Porter is killed and his body is found in the Bronx.
  10. January 28, 1990 — Donnell Porter’s body is found in the Bronx.
  11. 1990 — Martinez is shot in Washington, D.C.; later investigator accounts place Angelo Parisi in the hospital episode.
  12. November 1991 — Martinez is arrested in Washington; a federal affidavit described him as a liaison between a New York cocaine operation and dealers in Washington, Northern Virginia, and Fredericksburg.
  13. 1993–1994 — the federal Perry case consolidates multiple D.C. homicides and witness-retaliation allegations; Perry pleads guilty to five murders.

Popular Culture and Street History

The 2002 film Paid in Full is useful as a cultural artifact but not as a literal reconstruction. Documentary interviews, music references, and oral histories preserve names and community memory, but claims drawn from them should be attributed and cross-checked against court records and contemporary reporting.

Research Queue

Jazz (Queens) remains in the research queue pending a verified full identity and organizational role. Fritz, Curtbone, Shorty Pop, and Matthew Blake now have draft entries because their core identities can be anchored to identifiable sources; however, several relationship claims on those pages remain explicitly classified as participant, biographical, or oral-history evidence rather than court-established fact.

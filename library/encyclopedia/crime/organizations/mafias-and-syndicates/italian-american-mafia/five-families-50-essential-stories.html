Overview and Editorial Scope

Fifty stories. Five families. Ten essential episodes each. This index identifies a practical first collection for studying the Gambino, Genovese, Lucchese, Bonanno, and Colombo organizations. It emphasizes moments that changed leadership, organized-crime markets, institutional influence, internal alliances, and federal enforcement.

The entries are proposed standalone articles, not completed investigations. Each contains a working title, historical period, concise editorial scope, permanent index identifier, and a proposed HTML filename retained as non-clickable text. No nonexistent articles are linked. Family-profile links lead to already established MGU pages.

Entries are chronological within each family, not ranked by numerical importance. Some events affect several organizations; each is assigned a primary editorial home here while related entries are cross-referenced below. An allegation must not be presented as a criminal conviction, and a later recollection must not be treated as independent proof of a disputed event.

Coverage at a glance: 5 families · 50 planned articles · 10 articles per family · early twentieth century through 2024. Editorial slate adopted November 11, 2026.

Publication Framework and Editorial Standards

Recommended article structure

  1. Historical background: the organization and circumstances before the episode.
  2. Principal participants: leadership, crew members, investigators, victims, and relevant institutions.
  3. Event chronology: a sourced account of what happened and when.
  4. Immediate outcome: deaths, arrests, convictions, acquittals, leadership changes, or institutional consequences, as documented.
  5. Long-term significance: how the event changed the family or the wider Mafia system.
  6. Source register and uncertainty: primary records, scholarly accounts, disputed interpretations, and corrections.

Initial target: approximately 1,800–3,000 words per article, subject to the available primary record. This index intentionally provides brief scopes rather than substituting for the planned deep-dive articles.

Historical caution: Families are criminal organizations, not proxies for Italian-American communities. Statements of crimes, culpability, current leadership, and institutional involvement must be tied to the relevant evidence and date. Distinguish an indictment or prosecutor’s allegation from a plea, conviction, or independently established historical finding. Show conflicting interpretations where evidence does not support certainty.

Navigation rule: The story IDs and on-page fragment links are active now. The proposed filenames are planning metadata only and should become live article links after the corresponding HTML files are created and verified.

Research Starting Points

The following resources provide organizational context and documentary pathways. Individual story articles should develop more precise, event-specific citations rather than citing this index alone.

  1. Federal Bureau of Investigation, La Cosa Nostra (historical case overview) — organization and landmark investigations.
  2. United States Senate, Kefauver Committee and Organized Crime — background for the 1951 hearings.
  3. United States Department of Justice, Colombo leadership indictment, September 2021 — allegations and organization identified by prosecutors.
  4. United States Department of Justice, Colombo-related guilty pleas, July 2023 — subsequent adjudicated dispositions.
  5. MGU Crime Encyclopedia, Italian-American Mafia: The Five Families — parent category and related family profiles.
  6. Selwyn Raab, Five Families: The Rise, Decline, and Resurgence of America’s Most Powerful Mafia Empires (2005) — a significant secondary synthesis to check against primary records.

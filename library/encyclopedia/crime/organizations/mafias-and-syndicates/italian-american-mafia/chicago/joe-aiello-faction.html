Organizational overview

Joe Aiello led a Sicilian criminal faction that became one of Al Capone’s strongest opponents during the second half of the 1920s. The dispute involved illicit-liquor markets, underworld leadership, and competition for influence around the Unione Siciliana.

Aiello’s organization was not an Irish gang. It cooperated with Bugs Moran’s North Side organization against Capone, illustrating that the Beer Wars cannot be explained by an Irish-versus-Sicilian division.

[1][2]

Business foundations and Sicilian network

The Aiello family was involved in legitimate wholesale and food-import businesses as well as illicit Prohibition-era commerce. Its position in Italian immigrant commercial networks contributed to Joe Aiello’s resources and local influence.

Aiello’s family and associates interacted with Chicago’s Sicilian organizations but should not be equated automatically with every earlier Little Italy gang. In particular, overlap with surviving Genna networks does not by itself prove one uninterrupted formal command lineage.

[1][4]

Conflict over the Unione Siciliana

The Unione Siciliana was an immigrant fraternal institution that attracted competing attempts at criminal influence. The death of its influential president Mike Merlo from illness in November 1924 preceded struggles over its leadership.

Aiello’s rivalry with Capone supporter Antonio Lombardo, once his business associate, intensified as each faction sought power in the Italian criminal community. Contested influence over a legitimate fraternal organization does not mean that every Unione member was a gangster.

[1][2]

Coalition with Bugs Moran

Aiello’s confrontation with Capone produced alliances that crossed ethnic and organizational lines. By the late 1920s, the Aiello and Moran factions cooperated in opposition to the Capone syndicate.

These were two separate organizations with overlapping tactical interests, not a new unified family, and not evidence that the North Side Gang itself had turned into a Sicilian Mafia group.

[1][2]

Escalation and killing in 1930

The late-1920s conflict included attempted killings, murders, and retaliatory violence. Some specific allegations about who commissioned individual shootings appear principally in retrospective criminal histories and require attribution.

Aiello was killed in October 1930. Contemporary TIME reporting treated Aiello, Moran, and Capone as distinct major underworld leaders and documented the dramatic circumstances of Aiello’s death.

[3][2]

Classification and limits

Use “Aiello faction” or “Aiello criminal organization” as the stable primary entry title. Some historical authorities describe it as a Sicilian Mafia family, but the profile should explain the evidentiary basis for formal recognition rather than assuming the later Outfit’s hierarchy applied to it.

The proper relationship is rival to Capone and ally of Moran in a dated period; it is incorrect to call Aiello a member of the North Side Gang or an original subordinate of the Outfit.

[2][4][6]

Publication safeguards

  • Unione Siciliana was not itself equivalent to a Mafia family.
  • Do not conflate the Aiello faction with the North Side Gang or use ethnicity as a proxy for alliance.
  • Avoid implying every attempted killing attributed to Aiello was legally adjudicated.

Genna Brothers Gang

An independent Sicilian-led Little Italy bootlegging organization whose commercial disputes helped ignite Chicago’s Beer Wars.

Organizational overview

The Genna brothers built a powerful illicit-liquor organization in Chicago’s Near West Side Italian communities. They did not originate as a crew within the Colosimo–Torrio–Capone line: they exercised their own local authority and were associated with Sicilian criminal networks.

During the early 1920s, they cooperated with Torrio’s bootlegging arrangements. Their attempts to sell alcohol outside agreed territories sharpened hostility with Dean O’Banion’s North Side Gang. The 1925 murders of several brothers shattered the group’s leadership, and remaining networks were subsequently realigned.

[1][2]

Origins and family leadership

The six brothers associated with the Chicago organization were Vincenzo (often “James”), Angelo, Michele (“Mike”), Antonio (“Tony”), Pietro (“Peter”), and Salvatore (“Sam”). They came from a Sicilian family and anchored their business activities in Chicago’s Little Italy.

Many narratives refer to the organization as the “Genna family.” That phrase can describe the biological brothers and their criminal network; it should not, without qualification, imply that the entire entity operated with the later standardized American Cosa Nostra membership and hierarchy.

[5][1]

Bootlegging and neighborhood economy

The Gennas distributed alcohol illicitly during Prohibition, using a broad neighborhood network of production and supply. They profited from relationships with local businesses and from their ability to market spirits in districts served by other gangs.

Their activity in territories claimed by the North Side Gang was a major source of friction. A limited market-sharing understanding among leading organizations did not amount to centralized citywide command.

[1][2]

Relationship to Torrio, Capone, and O’Banion

The Gennas were at times aligned with Torrio’s organization against O’Banion. That alignment is analytically different from being a subordinate Outfit crew. Their rivalry with O’Banion involved territorial encroachment and hijackings.

Historical accounts connect Genna associates to O’Banion’s 1924 murder alongside a visiting associate of Torrio. The precise operational chain of command should be attributed to source accounts rather than inferred from the subsequent gang war.

[3][1]

Violent collapse in 1925

The gang experienced a concentrated series of blows in 1925. Angelo was murdered in May, Mike was killed during a confrontation with police, and Tony was murdered in July. A July 1925 Associated Press account documented the deaths as the third Genna brother killed in six weeks.

This did not mean that every person formerly associated with the Gennas instantly joined Capone. Leadership collapsed, remaining participants scattered or realigned, and some Sicilian networks later intersected with Joe Aiello’s competing faction.

[4][5]

Organizational classification

The Gennas should be treated as a separate Sicilian-led criminal syndicate with Mafia connections, not merged into the Outfit’s own founding timeline. Claims that the Genna gang was an officially chartered Cosa Nostra family should be attributed and examined in a separate membership study.

The gang’s history shows that ethnic similarity did not prevent competition among independent Sicilian and Italian criminal organizations in Chicago.

[2][5]

Publication safeguards

  • “Genna family” is not automatically synonymous with a formally recognized American Cosa Nostra family.
  • Do not describe the Gennas as the Outfit’s original subordinate faction.
  • Do not claim that every remaining Genna associate entered Capone’s organization after 1925.

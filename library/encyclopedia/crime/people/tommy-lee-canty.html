Tommy Lee Canty Jr.

Overview

Tommy Lee Canty Jr. organized and managed a multilayered Baltimore drug conspiracy during the mid-1980s. A Fourth Circuit opinion describes his organization as purchasing cocaine and heroin from suppliers in New York, Miami, and Los Angeles and distributing the drugs through an extensive Baltimore network.

Supply Network

Canty's organization demonstrates Baltimore's role as a distribution market connected to several national supply centers. Court records state that he obtained cocaine and heroin from sources in three major cities, processed and packaged the drugs, and distributed them through numerous participants.

Investigation

Local authorities began investigating the Canty organization in 1987. Federal officials joined in 1988. Investigators used undercover infiltration and wiretaps to develop evidence concerning the conspiracy.

Arrests and Indictment

The investigation led to the arrests of twenty-six members of the conspiracy. In late 1989, a federal grand jury indicted participants on charges that included conspiracy to distribute cocaine and heroin from January 1986 through May 1989.

Historical Significance

Canty's organization marks an important transition in Baltimore drug history. It combined the city's longstanding heroin market with the increasingly important cocaine trade and relied on interstate supply relationships extending to New York, South Florida, and Southern California.

Griselda Blanco

Overview

Griselda Blanco was a Colombian cocaine trafficker whose criminal career connected Colombia, Miami, New York, and other U.S. markets. She became one of the most notorious figures associated with South Florida's early cocaine era and the violent period later popularized as the “Cocaine Cowboys” era.

1975 Federal Indictment

In April 1975, Blanco and thirty-seven others were indicted in the Southern District of New York for conspiring to manufacture, import, and distribute cocaine. The federal case concerned trafficking activity dating to the early 1970s.

Miami Connection

The federal trial record described a conspiracy in which cocaine moved from Colombia through Miami or other entry points and onward to New York. Later South Florida investigations also associated Blanco-linked factions with major cocaine distribution and violence in Miami.

1985 Arrest and Conviction

DEA agents arrested Blanco in California in February 1985 after a decade-long effort to locate her. A federal jury convicted her of cocaine conspiracy that year. She received a fifteen-year federal prison sentence and a $25,000 fine.

Florida Murder Case

Florida authorities later prosecuted Blanco for three killings. The murder prosecution was complicated by misconduct involving the principal cooperating witness and employees of the state attorney's office. In 1998, Blanco resolved the case through guilty pleas to three second-degree murder counts and received concurrent twenty-year sentences.

Dadeland and Claim Status

Investigators and later reporting linked Blanco's broader trafficking organization to the 1979 Dadeland Mall shooting. Because responsibility for particular acts was often established through informants and retrospective investigative accounts, this encyclopedia distinguishes organizational association from a personal conviction for the Dadeland killings.

Historical Significance

Blanco represents an earlier organizational layer than Falcon and Magluta. Her career illustrates the Colombian-source trafficking systems that helped establish Miami as a major cocaine importation and redistribution center before the mature cartel era of the 1980s.

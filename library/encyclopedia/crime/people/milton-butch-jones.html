Milton “Butch” Jones

Detroit narcotics figure associated with two distinct criminal enterprises: Young Boys Incorporated in the heroin era and the later Dog Pound organization.

Overview

Milton David “Butch” Jones was a leading figure in Young Boys Incorporated (YBI), one of Detroit’s most consequential late-1970s and early-1980s narcotics organizations. Federal appellate records identify Jones as the group’s leader by the time major prosecutions substantially reduced YBI activity in 1983.

Young Boys Incorporated

YBI’s principal business was heroin and cocaine distribution. The Sixth Circuit later recounted that Jones allegedly continued directing YBI activity from federal prison in Texas after his conviction, using his wife Portia Jones and other members to maintain Detroit operations into the later 1980s.

Dog Pound

Jones later reappeared in a separate Detroit enterprise known as the Dog Pound. Sixth Circuit records state that Jones led the organization from approximately 1995 to 2001, overseeing cocaine, heroin, and marijuana distribution and participating in an enterprise that also robbed rival traffickers and was connected to kidnappings and murders. Jones eventually pleaded guilty to operating a continuing criminal enterprise and received a 30-year sentence.

Network Significance

Jones provides an unusual continuity point across generations of Detroit narcotics history. The encyclopedia should not treat the Dog Pound as simply “YBI continued”; the later enterprise was separately charged and operated in a different period, even though Jones connected the two histories personally.

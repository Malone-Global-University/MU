Charles Morris

Middle-management figure in the Frank Lucas heroin enterprise and later cooperating witness.

Geographic Classification

Borough/network: Manhattan — Frank Lucas Harlem/Upper-Manhattan network
Primary era: 1970s

Overview

Charles Morris was identified by the New York Supreme Court as a middle-management executive in Frank Lucas’s heroin enterprise.

Role in the Network

Morris later became a cooperating witness for federal and state authorities. The court record states that he supplied information linking Lucas to the George Ford homicide, though prosecutors initially considered his evidence insufficient to bring the murder case successfully.

Legal Record

Morris’s cooperation contributed to later narcotics cases against Lucas. His exact sentencing history is outside the scope of this initial network entry.

Historical Significance

Morris illustrates how the Lucas organization was disrupted from within through successive waves of cooperation by retailers, managers, suppliers, and ultimately Lucas himself.

Evidence Status

A. Role and cooperation are described directly in People v. Lucas.

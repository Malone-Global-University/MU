Warren Sims

Middle-management figure in the Frank Lucas organization who later became a cooperating witness.

Geographic Classification

Borough/network: Manhattan — Frank Lucas Harlem/Upper-Manhattan network
Primary era: 1970s

Overview

Warren Sims was identified in New York court records as a middle-management executive in Frank Lucas’s heroin enterprise.

Role in the Network

Sims cooperated with federal and state authorities and provided information concerning drug-related killings. In 1978, while in a witness-protection setting in Chicago, Sims encountered Martin Trowery and later told New York investigators that Trowery admitted killing George Ford for Lucas. Sims also recalled an earlier statement by Lucas concerning the Ford killing.

Legal Record

The Sims testimony became central to the 1980 litigation over whether the Ford homicide prosecution was impermissibly derived from Lucas’s own immunized cooperation. The court ruled that Sims’s evidence could be used.

Historical Significance

Sims is a key witness node linking the narcotics investigation, the Ford homicide investigation, and the later cooperation cascade.

Evidence Status

A. Role, cooperation, and evidentiary significance are documented in People v. Lucas.

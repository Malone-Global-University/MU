Augusto “Willie” Falcon

Overview

Augusto Guillermo “Willie” Falcon was a principal leader, with Salvador Magluta, of the Falcon–Magluta cocaine organization based in South Florida. Federal authorities described the organization as extraordinarily prolific during the 1980s and early 1990s.

Falcon–Magluta Partnership

Falcon and Magluta built a network that imported, transported, and distributed large quantities of cocaine. The organization operated in South Florida and also established a Southern California base that was used for cocaine distribution and cross-country tractor-trailer shipments.

1991 Indictment

Falcon was one of the lead defendants in the federal indictment returned in April 1991. Prosecutors alleged a long-running cocaine-importation and distribution conspiracy with proceeds measured in the billions of dollars.

1996 Acquittal

Falcon and Magluta were acquitted at their 1996 federal drug trial. Subsequent investigations uncovered evidence of jury bribery connected to the acquittal, leading to additional criminal cases against members of the broader organization and participants in the corruption scheme.

2003 Plea and Sentence

Falcon later pleaded guilty in a separate federal case involving laundering of drug proceeds and received a twenty-year prison sentence.

Historical Significance

Falcon's career illustrates the emergence of locally based Cuban American trafficking organizations that operated at national scale while relying on Colombian-source cocaine and interstate transportation systems.

Oscar Danilo Blandón

Overview

Oscar Danilo Blandón was a Nicaraguan expatriate who became a major cocaine trafficker in Southern California during the 1980s. He is especially important to Los Angeles crime history because he supplied cocaine to Ricky Donnell “Freeway Rick” Ross and later became a cooperating informant used in the federal sting that led to Ross' 1996 conviction.

Nicaragua and the Contra Connection

Blandón left Nicaragua after the Sandinista revolution. According to his statements and the later Department of Justice Inspector General investigation, he initially participated in cocaine trafficking in connection with fundraising for the Contra movement. The OIG concluded that he later continued trafficking for personal profit and obtained cocaine from Colombian, Mexican, and Nicaraguan sources.

Los Angeles Distribution

Blandón became a significant wholesale cocaine supplier in the Los Angeles area. Federal investigators concluded that he probably began supplying Ross in late 1983 or early 1984. The quantities increased as Blandón gained greater access to wholesale sources, including a Colombian connection around the middle of the decade.

Blandón was important to Ross' expansion, but federal investigators found that Ross had other suppliers and that cocaine and crack markets were already developing through multiple channels. The historical relationship is therefore best understood as a major wholesaler–distributor connection rather than the exclusive origin of Los Angeles crack distribution.

Investigations and Cooperation

Los Angeles County authorities investigated Blandón during the 1980s and executed searches associated with his network in 1986. He was later federally prosecuted in the early 1990s. After his arrest, Blandón cooperated with federal authorities and worked as an informant.

That cooperation became central to the 1995 undercover operation against Ross. Blandón reestablished contact with his former customer while working with the Drug Enforcement Administration, leading to negotiations for a 100-kilogram cocaine transaction and Ross' arrest.

CIA–Contra–Crack Controversy

Blandón became a central figure in the national controversy generated by allegations that Contra-connected traffickers had been protected by the U.S. government and had helped cause the crack epidemic. The Department of Justice Inspector General found evidence that Blandón had trafficked cocaine and had links to Contra fundraising, but did not find evidence that the CIA or another national-security agency protected Blandón's trafficking operation or that Blandón and Ross singularly caused the crack epidemic.

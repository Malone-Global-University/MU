Fleming “Ill” Daniels

Overview

Fleming “Ill” Daniels was a high-ranking member of the Black Mafia Family (BMF) operating in the Atlanta area. Federal appellate records state that investigators learned Daniels was frequently present at an Atlanta storehouse where drugs and money were exchanged and that he sold kilogram quantities of cocaine.

Atlanta Role

The Eleventh Circuit described BMF as a national organization distributing large quantities of cocaine through Atlanta, Detroit, Orlando, and Los Angeles. Daniels was identified by federal agents and cooperating participants as an important Atlanta member of that network.

2007 Federal Indictment

Daniels was among sixteen defendants charged in the Atlanta BMF indictment unsealed in July 2007. The indictment charged a cocaine-distribution conspiracy and related substantive drug offenses.

Trial and Conviction

Daniels was the only Atlanta defendant in that indictment to proceed to trial. In June 2008, a federal jury convicted him of conspiracy to distribute cocaine. The jury found him personally responsible for more than fifty kilograms of cocaine. The jury did not reach a verdict on a separate possession-with-intent-to-distribute count.

Federal Sentence

In December 2008, Daniels received a twenty-year federal prison sentence. The Eleventh Circuit later affirmed the conviction and sentence.

Rashannibal Drummond Killing

Daniels was also prosecuted in Fulton County for the 2004 killing of Rashannibal “Prince” Drummond outside the Velvet Room in Midtown Atlanta. In 2010, Daniels pleaded guilty to voluntary manslaughter and received a twenty-year state sentence, ordered to run concurrently with his federal sentence.

Historical Significance

Daniels is one of the best-documented Atlanta BMF figures because his federal case went to trial and produced an appellate decision. His prosecution provides a judicially tested record of the Atlanta cocaine-distribution hub rather than relying exclusively on retrospective accounts of BMF's structure.

Leroy “Nicky” Barnes

Harlem narcotics trafficker, founder and leading figure of The Council, later a federal cooperating witness.

Geographic Classification

Borough: Manhattan
Neighborhood: Harlem
City: New York City

Overview

Barnes became one of the most prominent heroin traffickers in 1970s Harlem. After his release from prison in the early 1970s, he assembled a network of major dealers that developed into The Council.

Role in the Network

Federal appellate records describe Barnes as the organizer and boss of a large narcotics organization. The later Council case identifies him as one of seven governing members whose organization pooled resources, shared sources, allocated territory, resolved disputes, and protected jailed members’ businesses.

Relationship to Frank Lucas

Lucas and Barnes led separate heroin organizations in overlapping Harlem markets. Later reporting described Lucas as a key competitor of Barnes. Lucas was not a Council member, and there is no basis for treating either organization as a subordinate branch of the other.

Legal Record

Barnes was convicted after the 1977 federal trial of narcotics conspiracy and operating a continuing criminal enterprise and received a life sentence. He later cooperated with federal prosecutors, providing evidence used against former associates.

Later Significance

After sentence reduction, Barnes was released in 1998 and entered witness protection. His cooperation became a major turning point in the dismantling of the Council network.

Evidence Status

A. Leadership, conviction, Council structure, and later cooperation are supported by federal appellate records and archival prosecution material.

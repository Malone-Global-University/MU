Wayne “Akbar” Pray

Overview

Wayne “Akbar” Pray led the northern New Jersey criminal organization known as The Family. Federal court records identify him as the leader of an organization engaged in the importation, distribution, and sale of cocaine and marijuana in New Jersey and beyond during the 1970s and 1980s.

The Family

New Jersey organized-crime investigators described The Family as one of the largest African American criminal organizations then operating in the state. Contemporary government assessments estimated the network at hundreds of members and associates and described a layered distribution structure designed to insulate senior leadership from direct street transactions.

Drug Importation and Distribution

Pray's 1989 federal convictions included operating a continuing criminal enterprise involving more than 150 kilograms of cocaine, conspiring to distribute cocaine and marijuana, possession with intent to distribute cocaine, and conspiring to import more than 98 kilograms of cocaine.

1988 Indictment

Pray and several associates were indicted in federal court in 1988. The prosecution focused not only on seized narcotics but also on travel, finances, unexplained wealth, organizational relationships, and the scale of the drug enterprise.

Conviction and Sentence

A Newark federal jury convicted Pray in October 1989 on multiple drug counts. On January 12, 1990, he received a non-parolable life sentence on the principal continuing-criminal-enterprise and conspiracy counts, together with additional concurrent terms.

2024 Sentence Reduction

After more than thirty-five years in federal custody, the District of New Jersey granted Pray's motion for a reduction of sentence on September 30, 2024, reducing his prison term to time served and imposing a term of supervised release.

Historical Significance

Pray is central to Newark crime history because his organization operated at a scale far larger than a neighborhood retail crew. The Family linked Newark street distribution to interstate and international cocaine supply and influenced the organization of smaller Newark groups that emerged after Pray's imprisonment.

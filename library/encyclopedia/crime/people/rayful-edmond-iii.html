Rayful Edmond III

Washington, D.C. cocaine trafficker who led one of the city’s largest narcotics organizations during the late 1980s and later became a long-term government cooperator.

Overview

Rayful Edmond III led a large cocaine-distribution conspiracy centered in Northeast Washington from approximately 1985 until his arrest in April 1989. Federal appellate records describe a multi-layered organization built from relatives, friends, lieutenants, sellers, couriers, stash locations, and wholesale relationships.

The Washington Organization

The organization’s principal open-air market was a two-block area of Morton Place and Orleans Place NE known as “the Strip.” Federal evidence described sellers working shifts, lieutenants supplying cocaine and collecting money, multiple storage locations, packaging operations, and a hub at 407 M Street NE.

California Supply Connection

Melvin D. Butler was a documented California connection for the Edmond organization. The D.C. Circuit summarized government evidence that Edmond and his associates traveled to Los Angeles to arrange and pay for cocaine shipments and that Butler participated in the California side of the supply chain. Contemporary reporting placed that connection within a larger pipeline extending to Colombian suppliers.

1989 Conviction and Sentence

On December 6, 1989, Edmond was convicted of engaging in a continuing criminal enterprise and several related federal offenses. In September 1990, the district court imposed a mandatory life-without-parole sentence on the continuing-criminal-enterprise count. The D.C. Circuit later vacated his separate conspiracy conviction and sentence because it duplicated punishment underlying the continuing-criminal-enterprise conviction, while affirming the remaining convictions.

Criminal Activity from Prison and Cooperation

Federal records later established that Edmond continued arranging drug transactions while incarcerated. He subsequently became a government cooperator. In 2021, a federal judge reduced his D.C. sentence after prosecutors credited nearly two decades of substantial cooperation in drug and homicide investigations.

Death

Edmond was transferred from prison to community confinement in 2024 after more than three decades in federal custody. He died on December 17, 2024, at age 60 while still in federal custody/community confinement. Later reporting based on the medical examiner’s findings attributed his death to complications of hypertensive cardiovascular disease.

Relationship to Alpo Martinez and Wayne Perry

Edmond belongs to an earlier and distinct D.C. organization from the later Martinez–Perry network. The two histories occupy overlapping Washington narcotics geography and chronology, but the current evidence set does not establish that Edmond employed Martinez, that Martinez worked for Edmond, or that the two men operated one unified organization. The encyclopedia should therefore present the relationship as market succession/overlap unless stronger direct evidence emerges.

Network Connections

  • Melvin D. Butler — California-based supply connection and convicted co-conspirator.
  • Rayful Edmond Organization — Washington organization led by Edmond.
  • Tony Lewis — friend/partner identified in federal appellate records; separate entry recommended.
  • Alberto “Alpo” Martinez — later New York–Washington trafficker; no formal organizational relationship established in the current evidence set.
  • Wayne “Silk” Perry — later D.C. figure associated with Martinez; not established here as part of Edmond’s organization.

Evidence Status

A. Core organization, conviction, sentence, supply-chain, and cooperation facts are anchored in federal appellate, Department of Justice, and contemporary reporting.

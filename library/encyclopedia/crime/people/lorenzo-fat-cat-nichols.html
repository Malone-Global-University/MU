Lorenzo “Fat Cat” Nichols

South Jamaica, Queens narcotics trafficker whose organization became a major power center in the mid-1980s and developed documented ties to both the Supreme Team and Howard “Pappy” Mason.

Overview

Lorenzo “Fat Cat” Nichols led a large narcotics organization in Queens during the 1980s. Federal appellate records identify a gang under Nichols’s leadership by 1985, while contemporary reporting described his operation as one of the major drug enterprises in South Jamaica.

Organization and South Jamaica

Nichols’s organization distributed narcotics and maintained street-level locations in Queens. Court records concerning members of the organization show a hierarchical structure in which senior associates transmitted orders, controlled distribution locations, and used violence or threats in response to law-enforcement pressure and internal conflict.

Relationship with Howard “Pappy” Mason

Howard “Pappy” Mason appears in multiple court records as an important Nichols associate during the mid-1980s. Mason later led the separate Bebos organization, which controlled a substantial portion of the South Jamaica crack trade in 1988. The evidence therefore supports a relationship that evolved from close association within the Nichols network into a distinct Mason-led organization rather than treating Nichols and the Bebos as permanently identical enterprises.

Relationship with the Supreme Team

The Second Circuit documented that, in 1987, the Supreme Team was allied with Nichols’s separate organization and that Nichols’s group supplied the Supreme Team with powder cocaine. This is one of the strongest documented organizational bridges among the major Queens drug networks of the era.

Brian Rooney Killing

New York State parole officer Brian Rooney had participated in returning Nichols to custody after a parole violation. Federal and state court records identify Nichols as the person who ordered retaliation against Rooney, who was killed on October 10, 1985. Later proceedings and accounts differ about the precise identity of the shooter and the immediate operational chain, so the encyclopedia treats Nichols’s ordering role as established while preserving the conflict over execution details.

Federal Investigation and Prosecution

A sweeping federal investigation targeted the Nichols and Mason organizations in 1988. Justice Department historical reporting stated that the investigation produced federal convictions of nineteen additional members of the two gangs and that Nichols was convicted of two drug-related murders. Nichols also faced state proceedings connected to Rooney’s killing.

Network Connections

  • Howard “Pappy” Mason — close associate and later leader of the separate Bebos organization.
  • Brian Rooney — New York State parole officer killed after enforcing Nichols’s parole conditions.
  • Kenneth “Supreme” McGriff / Supreme Team — documented 1987 alliance and supply relationship.

Evidence Status

A/B. Core organizational relationships, the Supreme Team alliance, and the Rooney retaliation are anchored in federal appellate records and contemporary reporting. Where court narratives conflict on homicide mechanics, the conflict is stated rather than harmonized.

Thomas “Joe Dancer” Ricks

Overview

Thomas Calvin “Joe Dancer” Ricks was identified by the Fourth Circuit as one of the leaders of a major Baltimore narcotics organization operating during the late 1970s and early 1980s. He was prosecuted alongside figures including Clarence Meredith and Maurice “Peanut” King.

Leadership Role

The Fourth Circuit stated that Ricks, King, and Meredith headed a major drug-distribution organization in Baltimore. The organization distributed heroin through street-level territories supervised by lieutenants and also faced prosecution for substantive narcotics offenses.

1983 Trial and Reversal

Ricks and other defendants were convicted in 1983. The Fourth Circuit later ordered a new trial because of prejudicial error involving jury selection. The reversal was procedural and did not itself constitute a finding that the alleged drug organization had not existed.

Retrial and Appeal

After retrial, Ricks, Maurice King, and Marcell “Black Barney” Moffett were again convicted. In 1989, the Fourth Circuit affirmed those convictions. Ricks and King were also convicted of heading a continuing criminal enterprise under federal law.

Historical Significance

Ricks' case documents the federal government's use of continuing-criminal-enterprise charges against Baltimore narcotics leaders before the later crack-era conspiracy cases of the late 1980s and 1990s.

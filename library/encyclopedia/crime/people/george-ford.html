George Ford

Upper-Manhattan heroin retailer in the Frank Lucas network who agreed to cooperate with federal authorities and was killed in 1974.

Geographic Classification

Borough: Manhattan
Area: Upper Manhattan
Primary era: early 1970s

Overview

George Ford was a retailer in the Frank Lucas heroin enterprise supplying upper Manhattan.

Role in the Network

After Ford and Lucas were indicted in a federal narcotics case, Ford agreed in 1974 to cooperate against Lucas. On July 24, 1974, Ford was shot and killed near his candy store. A later New York prosecution alleged that Martin Trowery was the shooter and that Lucas had arranged the killing; those allegations generated a major immunity dispute in People v. Lucas.

Legal Record

The 1980 New York Supreme Court opinion documents the homicide investigation and indictment of Lucas and Trowery but is a pretrial ruling, not a final homicide conviction. The encyclopedia therefore distinguishes the killing itself from the later prosecution theory.

Historical Significance

Ford’s killing is a pivotal event because his death caused an earlier federal narcotics case against Lucas to collapse and later became part of the chain of cooperation and retaliation that fractured the Lucas network.

Evidence Status

A for network role, cooperation, date of death, and later indictment; B for ultimate homicide attribution unless a final judgment is located.

Clarence Meredith

Overview

Clarence Meredith was identified by federal appellate courts as a ringleader of a major Baltimore heroin organization active during the early and mid-1980s. Court records document an organization with leadership, stash locations, street-distribution lieutenants, scheduled meetings, and territorial distribution arrangements.

Early Federal Case

In September 1982, a special narcotics grand jury returned a thirteen-count indictment against fifteen defendants, including Meredith. Eight defendants were convicted after trial, but those convictions were later reversed because of procedural error in jury selection.

Fugitive Period and Reorganization

Meredith became a fugitive before the 1982 indictment and remained at large until October 30, 1985. During that period, the Fourth Circuit found that he regrouped the remaining elements of his heroin ring and added new members.

Organization Structure

Meredith worked with brothers Melvin, Andrew, and Lawrence Meredith and with other senior associates. The organization stored and cut heroin at a stash house in Cockeysville, Maryland. Street lieutenants supervised distribution territories and attended recurring meetings at which heroin was supplied and proceeds from prior sales were collected.

1985 Arrest

After cooperating distributor James “Scotty” Wiggins helped investigators monitor the organization, law enforcement recorded conversations and a ring meeting. Meredith and other members were arrested at a scheduled meeting on October 30, 1985.

Historical Significance

The Meredith case provides one of the clearest judicial descriptions of how a Baltimore heroin organization operated at the neighborhood-distribution level. It documents hierarchy, recurring supply meetings, stash-house operations, street territories, and the role of cooperating insiders.

Telly Hankton

Overview

Telly Hankton was the leader of the New Orleans criminal organization identified by the Fifth Circuit as the Hankton Enterprise. Federal courts found that the group sold drugs in Central City and became involved in a prolonged violent conflict with a rival organization.

Central City Drug Organization

The Fifth Circuit described the Hankton Enterprise as a gang led by Telly Hankton that sold drugs in the Central City neighborhood of New Orleans. Federal prosecutors stated that the organization's drug ring dated back to approximately 1996.

Conflict with Rival Dealers

Beginning in 2004, the Hankton Enterprise entered a violent turf conflict with a rival group led by Brian Broussard. The feud generated repeated shootings and killings over several years.

Federal Prosecution

In 2014, Hankton and other defendants were charged in a federal RICO case. After a three-week trial in 2016, Hankton was convicted of racketeering, drug conspiracy, firearms conspiracy, multiple murders in aid of racketeering, and related offenses.

Sentence and Appeal

Hankton received life imprisonment in November 2016. In 2022, the Fifth Circuit affirmed his convictions in large part but vacated certain firearm convictions and restitution rulings because of changes in the law governing permissible predicates for federal firearms offenses.

Historical Significance

Hankton's case is one of the strongest judicial records for understanding New Orleans' neighborhood-based drug organizations. It documents narcotics distribution, territorial conflict, witness intimidation, and the later federal use of RICO against a locally rooted crew.

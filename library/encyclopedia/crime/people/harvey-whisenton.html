Harvey Whisenton

Overview

Harvey Whisenton was identified by federal authorities and contemporary news reports as the leader of Funktown USA, an Oakland drug organization active during the mid-1980s. His organization emerged in the same broader competitive environment that included Felix Mitchell's Mob and Mickey Moore's Family.

Funktown USA

Federal agents and Oakland police arrested Whisenton in 1985. Contemporary reporting described an East Oakland auto-body shop as a location used in heroin and cocaine sales and identified Whisenton as Funktown USA's reputed leader.

Authorities also described Funktown USA as a violent organization and alleged that it had used members of the Black Guerrilla Family prison gang for enforcement. Such claims should be attributed to the law-enforcement sources that made them unless independently established by court findings.

Federal Sentence

In May 1986, Whisenton received a 17-year federal prison sentence after pleading guilty in a case involving heroin and cocaine sales to undercover agents. Contemporary reporting also identified Michael Whitethurst and Kevin Johnson as lieutenants who received prison terms.

Historical Significance

Whisenton's case helps document the transition from the Mitchell–Moore rivalry into a broader and more fragmented Oakland narcotics market. Funktown USA also demonstrates why the city's criminal history should be mapped through organizations and neighborhoods rather than through a single succession of famous individuals.

Alberto “Alpo” Martinez

Harlem drug trafficker whose activities expanded into Washington, D.C. and Northern Virginia and who later became a federal cooperator.

Overview

Alberto “Alpo” Martinez was a New York drug trafficker associated with the 1980s Harlem cocaine economy and later with an interstate trafficking network extending into Washington, D.C., Northern Virginia, and the Fredericksburg area. His relationships with Azie Faison and Richard “Rich” Porter became the historical basis for part of the 2002 film Paid in Full.

Network Position

  • Harlem: associated with Azie Faison and Rich Porter.
  • Washington, D.C.: associated with Wayne “Silk” Perry and Garrett “Gary” Terrell.
  • Law enforcement: investigated by federal and D.C. authorities; Angelo Parisi and FBI agent Dan Reilly belong to the investigative branch of the network history.

1991 Arrest

Martinez was arrested in Washington on November 7, 1991. Contemporary reporting cited a federal affidavit describing him as a liaison between a New York cocaine operation and dealers in Washington, Northern Virginia, and the Fredericksburg area.

Cooperation and Later Cases

Martinez later pleaded guilty and cooperated with federal prosecutors. Contemporary reporting on the Wayne Perry prosecution states that Perry and other defendants were accused of killings carried out at Martinez’s behest and that Martinez had pleaded guilty while awaiting sentencing for contracting for multiple murders.

Historical Significance

Martinez is best understood as a bridge between the Harlem and Washington branches of this history. His biography should therefore cross-link people, events, investigations, and cases rather than stand alone as a celebrity-crime profile.

Milton “Mickey” Moore

Overview

Milton “Mickey” Moore was a major Oakland heroin trafficker during the late 1970s and early 1980s and a principal rival of Felix Mitchell. Contemporary and retrospective reporting identifies Moore with an organization known as The Family, which challenged Mitchell's 69 Mob for position in Oakland's heroin market.

Because Moore later publicly discussed his former criminal life and became a minister, this entry distinguishes his acknowledged history from allegations that are supported only by secondary accounts.

Competition with Felix Mitchell

Reporting on Oakland's drug wars identifies Moore's Family as one of the principal organizations competing with Mitchell's Mob. A particularly violent period occurred in 1980, when multiple killings were linked by investigators and later court records to conflicts over heroin distribution territory.

Moore's significance within the encyclopedia is therefore relational: he was not simply an isolated trafficker but the leader of a competing network whose rivalry with Mitchell helped shape Oakland's drug-market violence.

Later Life

By the early 2000s, Moore was publicly presenting his past as a cautionary account. The Los Angeles Times described him in 2003 as a former heroin trafficker and rival of Mitchell who had become the Rev. Moore of Keys to the Heart International Church in West Oakland.

Historical Significance

Moore's career is important for understanding Oakland as a multi-organizational market. The city's heroin economy was not controlled by a single permanent hierarchy; rival organizations competed for customers, territory, supply relationships, and enforcement power. Moore's Family is one of the clearest examples of that competitive structure.

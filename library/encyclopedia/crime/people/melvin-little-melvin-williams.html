Melvin “Little Melvin” Williams

Overview

Melvin “Little Melvin” Williams was one of the best-known narcotics traffickers in Baltimore during the second half of the twentieth century. Federal court records describe a long-running criminal history involving heroin and cocaine, while contemporary reporting associated his organization particularly with West Baltimore.

Early Drug Activity

A Fourth Circuit decision concerning property seized from the Williams family stated that a coordinated federal and local investigation found Williams had dealt in illegal drugs, including cocaine and heroin, since 1967. Court records also document earlier narcotics convictions and repeated law-enforcement attention.

West Baltimore Organization

By the early 1980s, investigators regarded Williams as the head of a substantial Baltimore drug organization. Contemporary reporting described the organization as having controlled a large share of the heroin trade in West Baltimore over an extended period. Because estimates of market control came from law-enforcement sources, this encyclopedia treats them as contemporary assessments rather than precise measurements of market share.

1984 Investigation

In 1984, the Internal Revenue Service, Drug Enforcement Administration, Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco and Firearms, and Baltimore City Police Department conducted a coordinated investigation of Williams. On December 5, 1984, agents executed a search warrant at his Baltimore residence and seized records, cash, valuables, firearms, and other property.

1985 Federal Conviction

Williams and Glenn Moore Hawkins were prosecuted after an attempted four-kilogram cocaine transaction involving an undercover agent. A federal jury convicted them in February 1985. The Fourth Circuit affirmed the convictions in 1986 while vacating special parole terms imposed on one count.

Later Life

After serving lengthy prison terms, Williams became involved in community and anti-violence work. He also appeared as the Deacon on HBO's The Wire. Series creator David Simon later emphasized that the fictional Avon Barksdale was a composite rather than a direct portrait of Williams.

Historical Significance

Williams provides a bridge between Baltimore's older heroin economy and the cocaine era of the 1980s. His career also demonstrates the increasing use of multi-agency federal investigations, financial records, asset seizures, and undercover purchases in narcotics enforcement.

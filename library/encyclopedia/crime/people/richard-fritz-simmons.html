Richard “Fritz” Simmons

Harlem cocaine wholesaler remembered in participant histories as the “Consignment King” and as an upstream supplier in the late-1980s Harlem drug economy.

Overview

Richard “Fritz” Simmons occupies an important but unusually source-limited position in Harlem crime history. Unlike many contemporaries, his reputation is preserved primarily through biography, interviews, and later street-history accounts rather than a readily identifiable federal prosecution. Those sources consistently portray him as a large-scale cocaine wholesaler who supplied other dealers on consignment.

Network Position

Later Harlem accounts place Simmons upstream from retail and mid-level traffickers, with his importance deriving from access to large quantities of cocaine and willingness to extend product on credit. Kevin Chiles has independently referred to Fritz as the “Consignment King” when identifying major New York contemporaries.

Accounts tied to the biography The Harlem Plug also connect Simmons to Richard “Rich” Porter during the Donnell Porter kidnapping, describing Simmons as providing Porter with resources or cocaine on consignment while Porter attempted to raise ransom money. That relationship should be presented as participant/biographical history unless corroborated by a court record.

Evidence Status

Source classification: participant interview and biographical/secondary reconstruction. The core identity and Harlem reputation are consistent across these sources, but claims concerning cartel connections, exact quantities, organizational size, and the amount supplied to Rich Porter require attribution rather than unqualified statement.

Historical Significance

Simmons helps explain the wholesale-credit layer of the Harlem cocaine economy. His entry should therefore connect supplier relationships, consignment practices, Rich Porter, Kevin Chiles, and the broader Harlem network rather than be treated only as a celebrity-crime biography.

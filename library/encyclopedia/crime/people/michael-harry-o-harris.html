Michael “Harry-O” Harris

Overview

Michael “Harry-O” Harris was a Los Angeles drug trafficker who became prominent during the crack era and later became associated with the early financing and business history surrounding Death Row Records. His criminal history predates that music-industry connection and belongs within the broader landscape of independent Los Angeles cocaine organizations operating during the 1980s.

Drug-Trafficking Career

Contemporary Los Angeles reporting described Harris as one of the region's notorious crack dealers. He operated during the same general period in which South Central and adjoining areas developed multiple high-volume cocaine networks. His career should therefore be treated as a separate trafficking enterprise rather than as a subordinate branch of Ricky Ross' organization unless a specific source documents a direct organizational relationship.

Convictions and Imprisonment

Harris was arrested in the late 1980s and received a lengthy prison sentence arising from drug trafficking and violent-crime convictions. Contemporary reporting in 1997 described him as serving a 28-year sentence for crack dealing and attempted murder. Federal clemency records later identified cocaine-related convictions in the Central District of California.

Business and Music-Industry Connection

While incarcerated, Harris became involved in business arrangements that later became part of competing accounts concerning the origins and financing of Death Row Records. Because those claims have been disputed by other participants, this encyclopedia should separate the documented criminal case from contested music-industry ownership narratives.

Historical Significance

Harris provides another example of the multiplicity of Los Angeles drug organizations during the 1980s. His later connection to the music business also makes him relevant to the intersection of illicit capital, legitimate enterprise, entertainment, and the public mythology surrounding Los Angeles street organizations.

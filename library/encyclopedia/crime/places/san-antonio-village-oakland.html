San Antonio Village, Oakland

Overview

San Antonio Village was an Oakland housing complex that became a central location in the history of Felix Mitchell's Mob. Federal appellate records state that the organization began dealing heroin there in early 1976.

Role in the Mob's Distribution System

The Mitchell organization used a layered street-distribution structure that included sellers, collectors, lookouts, and supervisors. Court records describe signals and walkie-talkies being used to alert sellers to police activity.

Contemporary reporting also described the housing complex as a destination for heroin purchasers from across the Bay Area. Police pressure eventually forced the organization to adapt and move portions of its street operations to other locations.

Historical Significance

San Antonio Village is important because it links organizational history to specific urban geography. The location shows how a public-housing complex could function simultaneously as a residential community, a contested drug marketplace, and a focus of police surveillance and enforcement.

Research Note

The name, boundaries, redevelopment history, and relationship between San Antonio Village and later neighborhood designations should be documented separately from the criminal-network narrative when additional municipal or housing-authority records are incorporated.

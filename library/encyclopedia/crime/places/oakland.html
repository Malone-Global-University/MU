Oakland, California

Overview

Oakland, California is a major geographic hub in the Crime Encyclopedia. During the 1970s and 1980s, the city developed a highly visible heroin economy involving structured organizations, neighborhood distribution points, violent territorial competition, and intensive local and federal enforcement.

Principal Networks

The first Oakland research cluster centers on three organizations: Felix Mitchell's 69 Mob / The Mob, Milton “Mickey” Moore's The Family, and Harvey Whisenton's Funktown USA. These groups overlapped in time but should not be treated as a single organization or lineage.

San Antonio Village and East Oakland

Federal appellate records place the beginning of Mitchell's organized heroin enterprise in San Antonio Village in 1976. The housing complex and surrounding East Oakland streets became important locations for street-level distribution and law-enforcement surveillance.

Violence and Market Competition

Competition over heroin sales contributed to a series of killings and shootings. In 1980, conflict between the Mob and the Family produced one of the most concentrated periods of violence associated with the city's drug trade. By the mid-1980s, Funktown USA had become another major organization in the local market.

Law Enforcement and Federal Cases

Oakland's narcotics organizations became subjects of long-term investigations by local police and federal agencies. The Mitchell prosecution produced extensive federal litigation, while later cases targeted Funktown USA and successor organizations.

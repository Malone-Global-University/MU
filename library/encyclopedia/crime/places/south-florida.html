South Florida

Overview

South Florida is a regional crime-history zone encompassing Miami-Dade, Broward, Palm Beach, Monroe, and adjoining trafficking corridors. Federal drug-intelligence assessments have repeatedly described the region as a principal arrival and redistribution area for cocaine and other drugs moving through the Caribbean.

Caribbean Corridor

South Florida's geography places it close to the Bahamas, Cuba, Hispaniola, and maritime routes from northern South America. Traffickers used private boats, commercial shipping, aircraft, and other transportation systems to move cocaine into the region.

Multiethnic Trafficking Networks

Federal assessments described Colombian organizations working with Bahamian, Cuban American, Dominican, Haitian, Jamaican, Puerto Rican, and other Caribbean criminal groups. No single ethnic or national group controlled the entire South Florida cocaine market.

National Redistribution

Large cocaine shipments entering South Florida were frequently broken into smaller wholesale loads and transported to other states. This upstream function made the region important to drug markets far beyond Florida.

Money Movement

South Florida also became a major location for laundering and moving drug proceeds. Financial institutions, currency exchanges, import-export businesses, real estate, and international banking channels became recurring investigative subjects.

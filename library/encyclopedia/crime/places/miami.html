Miami

Overview

Miami, Florida, became one of the principal U.S. centers of cocaine importation and distribution during the late 1970s and 1980s. Its proximity to the Caribbean, international airport and seaport infrastructure, financial sector, and connections to Latin America made the metropolitan area a critical entry and redistribution point.

Cocaine Expansion

As Colombian cocaine organizations expanded, South Florida experienced unusually visible drug-related violence. The 1979 Dadeland Mall shooting became an early public marker of this transformation.

Importation and Redistribution

Federal drug-intelligence assessments later described South Florida as a principal arrival zone for cocaine moving through the Caribbean and as a redistribution center for markets throughout the eastern United States.

Organizations

Miami's cocaine economy contained numerous unrelated organizations. Griselda Blanco's network belonged to an earlier Colombian-linked phase; Falcon and Magluta built a large South Florida organization; Medellín Cartel representatives and independent smugglers also operated through the region.

Money Laundering

The scale of drug proceeds created major laundering opportunities through banks, real estate, businesses, international transfers, and cash movement. Federal investigations increasingly targeted financial systems as well as cocaine shipments.

Law-Enforcement Response

The federal government responded to South Florida's cocaine crisis with increasingly coordinated multi-agency operations. The South Florida Drug Task Force became an important model for later national organized-crime and drug-enforcement task forces.

South Side of Chicago

Overview

The South Side of Chicago was the principal geographic setting for several of the city's largest Black street organizations. The Blackstone Rangers/Black P. Stone Nation, El Rukn, Disciple groups, and Gangster Disciples all developed important organizational bases in South Side communities.

Organizational Geography

The South Side is not a single neighborhood and should not be treated as one gang territory. Organizations operated through specific blocks, housing developments, commercial corridors, and neighborhoods, with boundaries shifting over time.

Black P. Stone and El Rukn

Federal records place important El Rukn properties on the South Side, including the headquarters at 3947 South Drexel Boulevard and a building referred to as the Hut at 6414–6416 South Kenwood Avenue.

Disciple Organizations

Englewood and surrounding South Side areas were central to the histories of David Barksdale and Larry Hoover. Later Gangster Disciples operations spread through numerous South Side territories and into other parts of Chicago and Illinois.

Narcotics Economy

Federal drug cases document cocaine, crack cocaine, heroin, and marijuana distribution through controlled street territories. The scale and structure varied substantially by organization and period.

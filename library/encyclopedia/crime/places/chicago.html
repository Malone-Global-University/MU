Chicago

Overview

Chicago, Illinois, developed one of the most complex urban street-organization systems in the United States. From the 1960s onward, large federated gangs, neighborhood factions, prison-linked leadership structures, and drug-distribution networks operated across the South and West Sides and later expanded into suburbs and other states.

1960s Gang Consolidation

The 1960s saw small neighborhood gangs consolidate into larger organizations. The Blackstone Rangers and later Black P. Stone Nation grew under Jeff Fort, while Disciple groups associated with David Barksdale expanded in South Side neighborhoods. Larry Hoover emerged from a separate Gangster organization that later aligned with Disciple leadership.

Drug Markets

Heroin, cocaine, crack cocaine, marijuana, and other drugs became major revenue sources for Chicago street organizations. Federal cases from the 1980s and 1990s document territorial systems, street sellers, stash locations, supervisory ranks, regular payments, and violence used to enforce market control.

South and West Sides

Gangster Disciples drug operations were concentrated particularly on the South and West Sides. El Rukn property and leadership were likewise centered on the South Side. Different organizations could operate in adjoining or overlapping territories, making neighborhood-level treatment essential.

Prison-Based Leadership

Chicago's gang history also demonstrates the ability of incarcerated leaders to continue exercising authority. Both Jeff Fort and Larry Hoover were documented directing aspects of their organizations through prison communications.

Federal Enforcement

By the late 1980s and 1990s, federal enforcement increasingly relied on RICO, continuing-criminal-enterprise charges, electronic surveillance, informants, prison-call monitoring, financial evidence, and large multi-defendant indictments.

Melvin Williams 1984 Investigation and Search

Overview

In 1984, federal and local authorities conducted a coordinated investigation of Baltimore narcotics trafficker Melvin “Little Melvin” Williams. The investigation culminated in a December 5 search of his Baltimore residence and the seizure of financial records, cash, valuables, firearms, and other property.

Participating Agencies

According to the Fourth Circuit, the investigation involved the Criminal Investigation and Examination Divisions of the Internal Revenue Service, the Drug Enforcement Administration, the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco and Firearms, and the Baltimore City Police Department.

Search Warrant

A judicially authorized warrant was issued in late November 1984. Agents executed it on December 5. Subsequent litigation examined both the legality of the search and ownership of property seized during the operation.

Financial Evidence

The investigation examined the relationship between Williams' reported legitimate income and his expenditures and assets. Vehicles, corporate relationships, cash, jewelry, documents, and other property became part of the financial inquiry.

Connection to the 1985 Case

Williams separately faced federal charges arising from an attempted cocaine purchase. He challenged aspects of the 1984 search during the criminal proceedings, but the warrant was upheld on appeal.

Historical Significance

The operation illustrates the evolution of narcotics enforcement from arresting street sellers toward integrated investigations of organizations, finances, assets, and leadership.

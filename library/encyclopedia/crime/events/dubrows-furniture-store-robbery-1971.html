Dubrow's Furniture Store Robbery, 1971

Overview

On January 4, 1971, eight armed men robbed Dubrow's Furniture Store on South Street in Philadelphia. Employees were restrained and assaulted, fires were set inside the business, and three employees were shot. One employee was killed.

The Attack

Pennsylvania court records state that the perpetrators entered posing as customers. During the robbery, employees were stripped of personal property, one employee was shot and killed, another was shot and wounded, and another was doused with gasoline and set on fire. Other victims were beaten or otherwise injured.

Prosecutions

Robert “Nudie” Mims was among those convicted in connection with the crime. His case later reached the Supreme Court of Pennsylvania, which in 1978 addressed claims concerning ineffective assistance of counsel and identification procedures.

Black Mafia Context

Historical research and contemporary archival descriptions associate the robbery with members of the Philadelphia Black Mafia. Because criminal organizations often have fluid membership, the event page focuses on defendants and documented participants rather than assuming that every perpetrator held the same organizational status.

Historical Significance

The crime became one of the most notorious violent incidents associated with the early Black Mafia and contributed substantially to the organization's reputation for extreme violence.

Shooting of Alberto “Alpo” Martinez (1990)

Washington shooting that produced an important early law-enforcement encounter between Alberto Martinez and narcotics officer Angelo Parisi.

Overview

In 1990, Alberto “Alpo” Martinez was shot in Washington, D.C. and hospitalized. The event later became important in investigator accounts because D.C. narcotics officer Angelo Parisi visited Martinez while he was in intensive care.

Hospital Encounter

Parisi later recalled that Martinez was badly wounded, sedated, and connected to intravenous lines when he visited him. According to Parisi’s documentary account, Martinez denied knowing who had killed Rich Porter and attempted to characterize himself as a drug dealer rather than a killer. Parisi later returned and discovered that Martinez had left the hospital.

Fredericksburg Distinction

The shooting should not be conflated with Fredericksburg, Virginia. Fredericksburg belongs to the documented trafficking corridor later described in the federal affidavit supporting Martinez’s arrest: New York, Washington, Northern Virginia, and the Fredericksburg area.

Investigative Significance

The episode is best understood as an early contact point in the law-enforcement history of Martinez. It should cross-link the Angelo Parisi biography and the broader Martinez investigation rather than be treated as proof of a comprehensive confession.

Evidence Status

C, with A/B context. The hospital sequence is based principally on Parisi’s later recollection. Martinez’s later Washington/Virginia trafficking corridor is independently documented by contemporary reporting on the federal affidavit.

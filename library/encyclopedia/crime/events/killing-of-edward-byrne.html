Killing of Edward Byrne

February 26, 1988 execution-style murder of an NYPD officer in South Jamaica, Queens, ordered by Howard “Pappy” Mason.

Overview

Officer Edward Byrne, 22, was killed in the early morning of February 26, 1988 while sitting in a marked police car outside the South Jamaica home of a witness in a drug case. The killing became one of the defining public events of New York’s crack era.

The Attack

Four men associated with the Bebos approached Byrne’s patrol car. State prosecutions identified David McClary as the gunman and Scott Cobb, Todd Scott, and Philip Copeland as the other participants. Byrne was shot five times in the head.

Howard Mason’s Order

Federal prosecutors established that Howard “Pappy” Mason, then incarcerated, had ordered members of his organization to kill a New York City police officer in retaliation for his imprisonment. Mason was convicted in federal court and later sentenced to life imprisonment.

Law-Enforcement Response

The murder accelerated a sweeping investigation of the Mason and Nichols drug organizations. Justice Department historical reporting states that the investigation resulted in federal convictions of nineteen other members of the two gangs in addition to Mason and Nichols-related prosecutions.

Public Significance

Byrne’s murder received nationwide attention and became a prominent political symbol in debates about crack cocaine, violent drug organizations, policing, and sentencing. For the Crime Encyclopedia, the event is important both as a homicide and as an institutional turning point in the law-enforcement response to South Jamaica drug networks.

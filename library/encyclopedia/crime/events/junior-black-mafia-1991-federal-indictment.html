Junior Black Mafia 1991 Federal Indictment

Overview

On October 2, 1991, a federal grand jury in the Eastern District of Pennsylvania returned a 32-count indictment charging twenty-six defendants in connection with the Philadelphia organization known as the Junior Black Mafia.

Conspiracy Allegation

The indictment alleged a drug-distribution conspiracy running from late 1985 through September 1991. Prosecutors alleged that JBM members sold and distributed large quantities of cocaine, crack cocaine, and heroin for resale in the Philadelphia area.

Leadership Allegations

Aaron Jones, Bryan “Moochie” Thornton, and Bernard Fields were alleged to have served as principal leaders of the organization at different times. Their trial was severed from those of the remaining defendants.

Subsequent Proceedings

The indictment produced multiple trials and guilty pleas. Published Third Circuit opinions from the resulting cases preserve a substantial record of the government's evidence, including cooperating witnesses, intercepted communications, documents, narcotics, firearms, and other physical evidence.

Historical Significance

The indictment represented the major federal dismantling effort against the JBM. It also provides a documentary anchor for distinguishing court-supported facts about the organization from later popular narratives.

South Florida Drug Task Force Created, 1982

Overview

In 1982, the federal government created the South Florida Drug Task Force in response to the scale of cocaine trafficking, organized crime, and violence affecting the region.

Background

South Florida had become a principal entry area for Colombian cocaine. A highly publicized 1982 seizure involving a Miami International Airport hangar illustrated the scale of the trafficking problem and intensified pressure for a coordinated federal response.

Multi-Agency Model

The task-force approach combined federal agencies with state and local law enforcement. DEA historical accounts describe the South Florida model as an important precedent for later multi-jurisdictional organized-crime and drug task forces.

Institutional Legacy

The South Florida experience helped shape the Organized Crime Drug Enforcement Task Force model and broader federal strategies emphasizing intelligence sharing, financial investigation, coordinated prosecution, and simultaneous attacks on trafficking organizations.

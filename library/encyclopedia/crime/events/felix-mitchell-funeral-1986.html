Felix Mitchell Funeral, 1986

Overview

Felix Mitchell's funeral in Oakland on August 29, 1986 became a nationally reported public event. Mitchell had been killed eight days earlier while imprisoned at the federal penitentiary in Leavenworth, Kansas.

The Procession

Contemporary reporting described more than 1,000 people lining the route outside Mitchell's East Oakland home as a horse-drawn carriage carried his bronze casket. A procession of luxury automobiles followed. The event attracted extensive press attention because of the contrast between the elaborate funeral and the damage associated with Mitchell's heroin enterprise.

Public Reaction

The funeral prompted debate over criminal celebrity, community memory, poverty, drugs, and the public display of wealth. It should not be interpreted as evidence that the Oakland community uniformly admired Mitchell; contemporary accounts described attendees with varied motivations, including curiosity and personal acquaintance.

In November 1986, roughly 10,000 people participated in an anti-drug march in Oakland. Organizers explicitly contrasted the march's message with the attention generated by Mitchell's funeral.

Historical Significance

The funeral became part of Mitchell's historical legacy because it transformed a local criminal figure into a national media symbol. For the encyclopedia, the event is useful less as spectacle than as evidence of how crime, public identity, neighborhood conditions, media attention, and community response intersected in Oakland during the 1980s.

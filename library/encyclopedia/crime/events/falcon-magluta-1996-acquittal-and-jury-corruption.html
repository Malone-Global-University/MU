Falcon–Magluta 1996 Acquittal and Jury Corruption

Overview

In February 1996, Augusto “Willie” Falcon and Salvador “Sal” Magluta were acquitted of federal cocaine charges after a lengthy trial in Miami. The verdict became one of the most controversial outcomes of the South Florida cocaine era.

Acquittal

The defendants had been accused of leading a massive cocaine-importation and distribution enterprise. The jury returned not-guilty verdicts on the drug counts presented to it.

Post-Trial Investigation

After the trial, federal investigators developed evidence that jurors had been bribed. Court records later documented substantial suspicious cash activity and communications involving the jury foreperson and organization associates.

Subsequent Prosecutions

The corruption investigation produced prosecutions of jurors and organization participants. Magluta was later convicted on obstruction and money-laundering charges. Falcon eventually resolved a separate money-laundering prosecution through a guilty plea.

Legal Distinction

The 1996 acquittal remains a formal acquittal of the drug charges tried in that case. Later evidence of jury bribery did not convert the original verdict into a conviction; instead, it supported separate criminal prosecutions.

El Rukn–Libya Conspiracy, 1986–1987

Overview

In 1986, senior members of Chicago's El Rukn organization pursued financial support from the Libyan government in exchange for proposed terrorist activity in the United States. The resulting federal investigation led to a major conspiracy and weapons prosecution.

Libya Meetings

Federal appellate records state that El Rukn members Leon McAnderson and Reico Cranshaw traveled to Libya in March 1986 with another associate and met with Libyan military officials. The organization proposed providing violent services in exchange for recurring financial support.

Prison Communications

Jeff Fort, then imprisoned in Texas, remained involved through extensive telephone communications with Chicago leadership. Recorded calls documented discussions of proposed attacks and attempts to demonstrate the organization's capability to Libyan contacts.

Rocket Launcher Purchase

As part of the federal investigation, undercover operatives facilitated the purchase of an inoperative LAW rocket launcher that appeared capable of functioning. The weapon was later recovered during an August 1986 search of an El Rukn property in Chicago along with numerous firearms and ammunition.

Indictment and Trial

Fort and senior El Rukn members were charged in a fifty-count federal indictment. Their trial ran from October 7 to November 24, 1987. Fort and four co-defendants were convicted of conspiracy and various weapons or related offenses.

Sentences

Fort received an 80-year sentence in addition to the sentence he was already serving. Other convicted defendants received substantial prison terms.

Historical Significance

The case is exceptional in American gang history because a Chicago street organization became the subject of a federal prosecution involving proposed international terrorism and a foreign government. Federal sources also state that the organization did not actually carry out the proposed terrorist attacks.

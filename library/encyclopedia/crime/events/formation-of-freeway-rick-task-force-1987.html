Formation of the Freeway Ricky Task Force, 1987

Overview

In January 1987, the Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department and Los Angeles Police Department formed a joint narcotics unit targeting Ricky Donnell “Freeway Rick” Ross and other suspected cocaine traffickers in South Central Los Angeles. The unit was formally associated with the Southwest Crew but became widely known as the Freeway Ricky Task Force because of its intense focus on Ross.

Background

The Sheriff's Department had been investigating Ross since approximately 1985 but had struggled to obtain arrests or major seizures. Ross' expanding reputation and the rapid growth of the crack market increased pressure for a coordinated response.

Composition and Mission

The joint task force combined personnel from the Sheriff's Department and LAPD. Its stated purpose was to investigate Ross and other high-level drug traffickers operating in the South Central market.

Controversy

The task force later became central to allegations of police corruption. Federal investigations and prosecutions examined claims that officers stole money and property, used excessive force, and manipulated evidence. Those allegations eventually damaged narcotics prosecutions and transformed the task force itself into a subject of criminal investigation.

Historical Significance

The formation of the task force marks a transition from ordinary narcotics enforcement to specialized high-intensity targeting of a particular dealer network. Its later collapse also demonstrates how police misconduct can undermine otherwise significant organized-crime investigations.

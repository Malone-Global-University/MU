United States v. Thornton, Jones & Fields (1991–1993)

Overview

United States v. Thornton, Jones & Fields was a major federal prosecution of alleged leaders of Philadelphia's Junior Black Mafia. The case originated in the 1991 multi-defendant indictment and resulted in convictions that were reviewed by the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit in 1993.

Indictment

A federal grand jury returned a 32-count indictment on October 2, 1991. It charged Bryan Thornton, Aaron Jones, Bernard Fields, and twenty-three other defendants with participation in a conspiracy to distribute cocaine, crack cocaine, and heroin between late 1985 and September 1991.

Severed Leadership Trial

The district court severed the trial of Thornton, Jones, and Fields from the remaining defendants because the government alleged that they had been principal leaders of the JBM. Their trial lasted fifteen days.

Evidence

The government presented testimony from ten cooperating witnesses with direct knowledge of the organization, more than sixty recorded conversations, and physical evidence including drugs, firearms, photographs, documents, and narcotics paraphernalia.

Appeal

The defendants appealed their convictions and sentences on multiple grounds. In United States v. Thornton, 1 F.3d 149 (3d Cir. 1993), the Third Circuit reviewed the claims and affirmed the judgments addressed by the opinion.

Related JBM Proceedings

Other defendants from the same indictment proceeded through separate trials or guilty pleas. A later published appellate decision, United States v. Price, addressed another group of JBM defendants and further documented the scope of the conspiracy.

Historical Significance

The case is the principal judicial anchor for the first Philadelphia JBM batch. It establishes the existence of a large federal drug conspiracy case, identifies principal leaders as alleged and proven in court, and preserves a detailed description of the evidence used to dismantle the organization.

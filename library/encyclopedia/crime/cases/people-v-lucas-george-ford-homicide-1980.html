People v. Lucas — George Ford Homicide Litigation (1980)

New York homicide prosecution involving Frank Lucas and Martin Trowery and a major dispute over evidence derived from immunized cooperation.

Case Background

Frank Lucas and Martin Trowery were indicted in connection with the 1974 killing of George Ford, a Lucas retailer who had agreed to cooperate with federal authorities.

Prosecution Theory

The prosecution alleged that Trowery was the shooter and that Lucas purchased or arranged his services. Investigators relied on information from former Lucas associates, including Warren Sims, while statements from Leslie Atkinson and Herman Jackson were recognized as tainted for use against Lucas because they had been obtained through a chain beginning with Lucas’s immunized cooperation.

1980 Ruling

The New York Supreme Court denied Lucas’s motion to dismiss the indictment, holding that preexisting untainted evidence and Sims’s later statements could be used despite the separate tainted evidence. The opinion is a pretrial evidentiary ruling; it should not be cited as proof of a final murder conviction.

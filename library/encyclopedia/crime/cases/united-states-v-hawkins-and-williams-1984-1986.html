United States v. Hawkins & Williams (1984–1986)

Overview

United States v. Hawkins & Williams was a federal narcotics case arising from an attempted multi-kilogram cocaine transaction. The prosecution resulted in the 1985 convictions of Glenn Moore Hawkins and Baltimore trafficker Melvin “Little Melvin” Williams.

Indictment

Williams and Hawkins were indicted in December 1984 on federal narcotics charges. The case involved an attempted purchase of cocaine from an undercover agent and related allegations under federal drug and unlawful-business statutes.

Pretrial Detention

The case produced an early appellate decision interpreting the recently enacted Bail Reform Act of 1984. A federal magistrate ordered the defendants detained after finding that no release conditions would reasonably assure community safety. The Fourth Circuit ultimately reinstated pretrial detention after a district judge had granted bail.

Trial

The trial began on February 5, 1985. A jury returned guilty verdicts on all counts two days later. Evidence included information supplied by a cooperating participant and the attempted cocaine transaction.

Appeal

In 1986, the Fourth Circuit affirmed the convictions. The court also upheld the legality of the search warrant used against Williams, while vacating special parole terms that had been added to one count.

Historical Significance

The case is important both for Baltimore drug history and for federal criminal procedure. It connects a prominent local trafficker to a documented cocaine transaction while also illustrating the new preventive-detention framework enacted during the expansion of federal drug enforcement in the 1980s.

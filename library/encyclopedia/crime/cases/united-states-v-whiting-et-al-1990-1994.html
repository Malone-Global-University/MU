United States v. Whiting et al. (1990–1994)

Overview

United States v. Whiting et al. was the principal federal prosecution of the New York Boys drug-distribution organization based in Orchard Park, Roxbury.

1990 Indictment

On December 11, 1990, a federal grand jury indicted Darryl Whiting and thirty co-defendants for conspiracy to distribute cocaine.

1991 Superseding Indictment

A superseding indictment returned on April 11, 1991, expanded the case to fifty defendants and included additional drug, firearms, money-laundering, and continuing-criminal-enterprise charges.

Whiting's Role

Whiting was charged as organizer and supervisor of a continuing criminal enterprise. The government's case relied on undercover investigation and testimony from co-conspirators.

Appeals

Seven defendants, including Whiting, appealed convictions, sentences, or both. In 1994, the First Circuit affirmed the district court on nearly all issues, granting relief on only one limited point.

Historical Significance

The appellate opinion is the core judicial source for the New York Boys and establishes the scale of the prosecution, the Orchard Park base, and the federal theory of the organization.

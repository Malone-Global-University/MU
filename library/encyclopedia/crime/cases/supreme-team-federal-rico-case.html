Federal RICO Case Against the Supreme Team

Federal prosecution of Gerald “Prince” Miller and other Supreme Team members for racketeering, narcotics trafficking, and related crimes.

Overview

The federal prosecution summarized in United States v. Miller followed years of state and federal investigation into the Supreme Team in Queens. The case charged members with conducting the affairs of the Supreme Team through a pattern of racketeering activity, narcotics conspiracy, cocaine-base distribution, continuing-criminal-enterprise activity, and related offenses.

Investigation

The government’s evidence included more than one hundred wiretapped conversations, telephone records, crime-scene evidence, surveillance, firearms, narcotics paraphernalia, photographs, documents, and testimony from former members and law-enforcement officers. State authorities had previously arrested several members in 1990; after state proceedings encountered legal problems, evidence was transferred to federal prosecutors.

Gerald Miller

Miller was convicted of racketeering, engaging in a continuing criminal enterprise, multiple cocaine-base distribution counts, and a money-laundering offense. The district court imposed seven concurrent life terms plus a concurrent 20-year term.

Appeal

In 1997, the Second Circuit affirmed the defendants’ convictions in nearly all respects. It reversed Miller’s separate narcotics-conspiracy conviction because the government agreed that punishment for both that conspiracy and the continuing-criminal-enterprise count violated double-jeopardy principles. Miller’s other convictions remained intact.

Importance to the Encyclopedia

The case is the strongest documentary anchor for reconstructing the original Supreme Team’s structure, leadership, distribution system, enforcement practices, supplier relationships, and 1989 reorganization. Claims about the Supreme Team should generally be checked against this record before relying on documentaries or later street-history accounts.

United States v. Hoover et al. (1995–2001)

Overview

United States v. Hoover et al. was the major federal prosecution of Larry Hoover and other senior Gangster Disciples leaders arising from the 1995 indictments. The case documented a large narcotics organization structured through a chairman, directors, governors, and lower-ranking members.

Defendants and Leadership

The Seventh Circuit's 2001 decision addressed Hoover along with Tirenzy Wilson, Gregory Shell, Jerry Strawhorn, Adrian Bradd, Darrell Branch, Andrew Howard, and William Edwards. The court described Hoover as chairman of the board, Shell as second-in-command, and Howard as the third director.

Charges

Senior defendants were charged with conspiracy to distribute narcotics and, in several cases, operating a continuing criminal enterprise. Additional convictions involved drug possession and distribution, use of telephones to facilitate drug offenses, use of minors, firearms, and money laundering.

Trial and Convictions

Hoover was convicted in May 1997. The senior leadership trial relied heavily on recorded conversations, testimony from insiders, organizational evidence, and proof concerning the gang's drug-distribution structure.

Sentences

Hoover, Shell, Howard, Wilson, and Strawhorn received life sentences for continuing-criminal-enterprise convictions. Other defendants received lengthy sentences based on their roles and offenses.

Appeal

In 2001, the Seventh Circuit affirmed the principal convictions and sentences, while ordering limited relief as to one defendant's sentence. The opinion remains one of the core judicial sources for the Gangster Disciples' hierarchy and drug operations.

Historical Significance

The case represented a federal effort to incapacitate an organization's governing structure rather than merely arrest individual street sellers. It is therefore central to understanding both the Gangster Disciples and the broader evolution of federal gang prosecutions.

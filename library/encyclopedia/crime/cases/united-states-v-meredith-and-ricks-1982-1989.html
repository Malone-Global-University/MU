United States v. Meredith and Ricks (1982–1989)

Overview

The federal prosecutions associated with Clarence Meredith, Thomas “Joe Dancer” Ricks, Maurice “Peanut” King, and their associates generated a series of trials and appeals during the 1980s. Together, the published decisions provide one of the most detailed judicial records of a Baltimore heroin organization.

1982 Indictment

In September 1982, a special narcotics grand jury returned a thirteen-count indictment against fifteen defendants, including Meredith. Eight defendants were convicted after trial.

Appellate Reversal

The Fourth Circuit ultimately ordered a new trial because of prejudicial error involving jury selection. The reversal addressed trial procedure rather than declaring that the government had failed to establish the existence of the trafficking organization.

Reorganized Ring, 1983–1985

While Meredith remained a fugitive, he regrouped the organization and added new members. A later prosecution covered activity from 1983 through October 30, 1985. Cooperating distributor James “Scotty” Wiggins helped investigators record meetings and conversations before the arrests.

Retrial of Ricks and King

Ricks, King, and Marcell “Black Barney” Moffett were retried after the earlier reversal and convicted again. In 1989, the Fourth Circuit affirmed those convictions. Ricks and King, along with Meredith, had been convicted of heading continuing criminal enterprises.

Historical Significance

The litigation documents both the organization and the legal process used to prosecute it. It also demonstrates why procedural reversals must be distinguished from factual exoneration when constructing encyclopedia entries from appellate records.

United States v. McAnderson, Fort et al. (1986–1990)

Overview

United States v. McAnderson was the principal appellate case arising from the federal prosecution of Jeff Fort and senior El Rukn members for conspiring to obtain Libyan financing in exchange for proposed terrorist acts in the United States.

Defendants

The principal defendants included Jeff Fort, Leon McAnderson, Roosevelt Hawkins, Alan Knox, and Reico Cranshaw. They were charged with conspiracy and a series of weapons, interstate-travel, and telephone-related offenses.

Fifty-Count Indictment

Federal prosecutors returned a fifty-count indictment. The central charge alleged a conspiracy to commit terrorist acts. Additional counts concerned the use of interstate facilities and firearms or explosives.

Trial

The jury trial ran from October 7 through November 24, 1987. The government's evidence included recorded prison communications, testimony regarding the Libya contacts, and the controlled purchase of a rocket launcher.

Convictions and Sentences

The defendants were convicted of various charges. Fort received an 80-year sentence in addition to his existing imprisonment. Contemporary reporting noted that the proposed terrorist acts were not actually carried out.

Appeal

The defendants raised claims involving jury composition, juror prejudice, evidentiary rulings, transcripts used to interpret coded conversations, and sufficiency of the evidence. In 1990, the Seventh Circuit affirmed the convictions addressed in the appeal.

Historical Significance

The case transformed El Rukn from a local organized-crime subject into a federal national-security matter and provides the strongest judicial record for the Libya episode.

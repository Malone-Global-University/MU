Salvador Magluta Obstruction and Money-Laundering Case (1999–2008)

Overview

The later federal prosecution of Salvador Magluta addressed conduct separate from the cocaine charges on which he had been acquitted in 1996. The case focused on obstruction of justice, money laundering, witness and juror bribery, and related conduct.

Money-Laundering Allegations

Federal prosecutors alleged that Magluta and associates moved drug proceeds through overseas banks, exchange houses, couriers, nominee accounts, attorneys, investigators, and other intermediaries.

Obstruction Allegations

The case included allegations of witness bribery, juror bribery, witness tampering, and efforts to interfere with government prosecutions.

Trial Results

A federal jury convicted Magluta of conspiracy to obstruct justice, conspiracy to launder drug proceeds, witness-bribery obstruction, juror-bribery obstruction, and multiple substantive money-laundering counts. It acquitted him on other counts.

Appeal

In 2005, the Eleventh Circuit affirmed several convictions but reversed one juror-bribery obstruction count because of an evidentiary error and remanded for resentencing.

Resentencing

Magluta's original 205-year sentence was later reduced to 195 years. The revised sentence was affirmed on appeal.

Historical Significance

The case provides one of the clearest examples in the Crime Encyclopedia of an organization attempting to preserve itself through financial concealment and interference with the justice system after senior leaders had been indicted.

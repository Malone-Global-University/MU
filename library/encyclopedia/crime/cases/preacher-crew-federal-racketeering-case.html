Federal Racketeering Case Against the Preacher Crew

Southern District of New York prosecution of Clarence “Preacher” Heatley, John “Apple” Porter, and other members and associates of the Preacher Crew.

Overview

The federal prosecution commonly captioned United States v. Heatley targeted the Preacher Crew, an organization described by the government as operating in Harlem and the Bronx and engaging in narcotics trafficking, extortion, robbery, kidnapping, assaults, and murders.

Charges

Across superseding indictments, defendants faced racketeering and racketeering-conspiracy charges, violent crimes in aid of racketeering, firearms offenses, and—in cases involving senior defendants—continuing-criminal-enterprise and homicide allegations. The government authorized the federal death penalty against Heatley and John Cuff.

Heatley Plea

Heatley pleaded guilty in 1999 under an agreement in which the government withdrew its death-penalty notice. Later court records state that he pleaded guilty to participation in thirteen murders and one attempted murder connected to the organization and received life imprisonment plus additional consecutive sentences.

John Porter

John Porter, identified in federal dockets as “Apple” and “Aps,” also pleaded guilty in 1999. A later order describes him as a founding member of the crew and records a forty-year federal sentence for murder-conspiracy, racketeering-related threats, and firearms offenses.

Importance to the Crime Encyclopedia

This case is the controlling institutional hub for Preacher Crew biographies and for distinguishing federally alleged or admitted conduct from later street-history attribution. It also provides a primary legal bridge to the Donnell Porter kidnapping through the government’s death-penalty filing against Heatley.

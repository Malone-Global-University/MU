Country Overview

The People’s Republic of China is an East Asian state with a vast continental territory, major Pacific coastlines, and one of the world’s largest economies and manufacturing systems. The Chinese Communist Party leads a party-state whose present institutions date from the PRC’s establishment in 1949. China is experiencing population aging, urbanization, transition in industry and energy, and continued tension between global integration and geopolitical competition. Mainland population data exclude Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan, and China’s claims over Taiwan should not be confused with present governing authority. [4] [3] [6]

People and Society

Population and culture
Han Chinese form the majority, alongside officially recognized minority nationalities. [17]
Officially recognized nationalities
56 ethnic groups in the state classification. [17]
Languages
Mandarin and many Sinitic varieties, plus Tibetan, Uyghur, Mongolian, Zhuang and other languages. [17]
Language policy
Putonghua is promoted for standard communication and education; local and minority-language practice varies. [17] [11]
Educational system
Compulsory basic education, secondary education, vocational tracks and a large university sector. [17] [20]
Education attainment trend
NBS reports rising average education years for the working-age population. [4]
Average schooling, 16–59, 2025
11.3 years, NBS report. [4]
Universities and science
Higher education and research institutions are integral to industrial and science policy. [12]
Religious traditions
Buddhist, Daoist, Muslim, Christian and popular ritual traditions coexist under state religious regulation. [11] [17]
Historical philosophical traditions
Confucian, Daoist and Buddhist thought shape intellectual and ritual heritage. [7]
Urban lifestyles
High-density cities combine extensive digital commerce and transit with regional differences in housing and opportunity. [20]
Rural populations
Agricultural communities and rural migrants retain major roles in the workforce and family economies. [4] [20]
Internal migration
Work-related movement between regions is a major demographic and service-delivery concern. [4] [20]
Hukou household-registration system
Household registration influences access to some locality-based public services and opportunities. [20]
Population aging
Growth in the population aged 60 and above affects pension, health and eldercare systems. [4] [20]
Health outcomes
Life expectancy at birth was about 78 years in 2024 in the World Bank series. [3] [18]
Health system
Public hospitals, primary care programs, health insurance systems and regional access disparities coexist. [18]
Communicable disease surveillance
Public health agencies coordinate monitoring and vaccination programs. [18]
Food culture
Regional cuisines include diverse northern wheat-based and southern rice-based traditions. [17]
Traditional festivals
Lunar New Year, Dragon Boat Festival and Mid-Autumn Festival are widely observed. [8]
Cultural heritage
Great Wall, imperial capitals, Buddhist monuments and living intangible traditions span varied historical periods. [7] [8]
Chinese diaspora
Overseas communities are important cultural and business connections across Asia and beyond. [20]
Sports
Table tennis, badminton, basketball, football and many traditional practices are popular. [17]
Digital ecosystem
Broad internet penetration coexists with a state-regulated domestic online environment. [3] [11]
Civil liberties assessment
Freedom House classifies China as “Not Free” (9/100) in its 2026 political-rights and civil-liberties index; this is an external assessment. [11]
Media environment
Government licensing, censorship and legal constraints shape journalism and online political expression. [11]

Chinese Communist Party (CCP). [9] [11]

Press and association

Religious regulation
Registered religious organizations and permissible worship are subject to state oversight. [11]
Xinjiang and Tibet

Sources and Methodology

Entries are a source-anchored reference compilation, not a single statistical extract. Census counts, administrative population figures and international estimates are separate series; nominal GDP is not purchasing-power-parity output. WTO, UNCTAD and national customs trade series can differ by definition, coverage and reporting period. Regional classification follows the United Nations M49 statistical convention rather than asserting exclusive cultural or political geography. UNESCO inscribed, tentative and intangible heritage are distinct categories.

Editorial cutoff: November 6, 2026 (Pacific Time). A dated statistic describes its reference period, not necessarily its publication date. Disputed or internationally contested governance claims are described with their attribution and jurisdiction. The page may require later revision when new releases or events occur.

Country Overview

Iran is a large, linguistically diverse country situated between the Caspian Sea, the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman. Its terrain includes the Zagros and Alborz mountains and extensive arid plateaus. The Islamic Republic established in 1979 combines elected offices with institutions under the authority of a supreme leader. Energy reserves, manufacturing and trade underpin the economy, which also faces inflation, sanctions and conflict-related disruptions. Its Persian-language literary legacy and diverse regional cultures form an important part of West Asian history.

Identity & State Formation

Official state name
Islamic Republic of Iran (Jomhuri-ye Eslami-ye Iran). [1]
Common short name
Iran; Persia is a historical name used in many cultural contexts. [1] [21]
Capital
Tehran, the national administrative and political center. [1]
UN statistical area
Southern Asia in the UN M49 statistical system; frequently discussed as part of West Asia or the Middle East in other regional frameworks. [1]
System of government
Islamic republic combining a supreme leader, elected president, parliament and clerical oversight institutions. [8] [12]
1979 revolution
Overthrew the Pahlavi monarchy and established the Islamic Republic. [12]
Constitutional order
Constitution of 1979, subsequently revised in 1989. [12]
Official language
Persian (Farsi); other Iranian and non-Iranian languages are widely spoken. [21]
Currency
Iranian rial (IRR); market, regulated and accounting exchange rates may differ. [7]
Calendar
Iranian solar Hijri calendar is used for official civil dating. [21]
National holiday
Islamic Republic Day, observed in early April under the Iranian calendar. [21]
Provinces
31 provinces, known as ostans. [1]
Largest city
Tehran is the main metropolitan center. [2]
Historical Achaemenid capital
Persepolis is a major surviving ceremonial complex from the Achaemenid period. [13]
Safavid historical center
Isfahan's monuments reflect Safavid-era political, architectural and cultural development. [13]
Persianate cultural sphere
Persian literature and courtly traditions influenced neighboring societies over centuries. [21]
Geostrategic setting
Borderlands connect Central Asia, South Asia, the Caucasus, Mesopotamia and the Gulf. [1]
Iranian plateau
Much of the country occupies highland basins and surrounding mountain chains. [16]
Caspian connection
Iran has a southern Caspian Sea shore. [1]
Gulf connection
Its southern shoreline spans the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman. [1]
Coastal strategic passage
The Strait of Hormuz is a major global energy-shipping chokepoint. [18]
Independence chronology
Iran's state continuity predates modern independence movements; the Islamic Republic began in 1979, not through a colonial-independence declaration. [21]

Sources and Methodology

Iranian statistical releases, World Bank indicators and international trade records are not interchangeable. The World Bank 2025 population is an estimate; the 2016 census is a counted enumeration. US-dollar GDP is a nominal exchange-rate statistic, and sanctions may complicate exchange-rate comparisons. WTO product/partner trade entries are reporting-source estimates, and recorded merchandise figures can exclude certain crude petroleum transactions. The UN M49 location is statistical, not geopolitical endorsement; descriptions of institutions distinguish formal constitutional authority from assessments of political practice.

Editorial cutoff: November 6, 2026 (Pacific Time). All stated population counts, prices, elections, and legal statuses have specific reference dates or qualifications. This page should be updated following major new statistical releases or changes of government.

Country Overview

Brunei Darussalam is a small, high-income sultanate on the north coast of Borneo. It combines a longstanding hereditary monarchy and Malay Islamic cultural institutions with an economy shaped by petroleum, liquefied natural gas and downstream petrochemicals. Public planning targets economic diversification and workforce development, while the IMF highlights continuing fiscal dependence on hydrocarbon revenue. The state has a distinctive one-for-one currency arrangement with Singapore and is a member of ASEAN. [6] [8] [12]

Government and Institutions

State form
Absolute hereditary monarchy with a Sultan and Yang Di-Pertuan exercising supreme executive authority. [8] [17]
Head of state
Sultan Hassanal Bolkiah, ruling since October 1967. [8]
Head of government
The same Sultan serves as Prime Minister. [8]
Defense portfolio
The Sultan is also Minister of Defence. [8]
Finance portfolio
The Sultan holds the finance minister role; other ministers assist in fiscal administration. [8]
Foreign affairs
The Sultan has a leading foreign affairs ministerial role, assisted by appointed ministers. [8]
Legal framework
The Constitution of Brunei Darussalam dates from 1959, with later amendments. [11]
Constitutional authority
The monarch’s powers include the executive structure and appointment of councils. [8] [11]
Legislative institution
Legislative Council (Majlis Mesyuarat Negara), an appointed national advisory and lawmaking body. [9] [10]
Nationally elected legislature
There is no nationally elected parliament as of 2026; members are appointed under the operative arrangements. [17] [10]
Constitutional membership ceiling
Up to 45 Legislative Council members under the constitutional schedule. [11] [9]
Official March 2026 Council count
The Council FAQ listed 34 serving members; composition can change by appointment. [10]
Legislative Council member term
The official Council FAQ states a five-year appointment term. [10]
Council membership categories
Ex officio ministers, titled persons, distinguished citizens and district representatives are included. [10] [11]
Legislative Council Speaker, March 2026
Abdul Rahman bin Mohamed Taib (full honorifics omitted). [10]
Sultan’s Privy Council
Advises within the monarch’s constitutional and customary structures. [8]
Council of Succession
Addresses succession-related functions under the royal institutional framework. [8]
Religious Council
An official body associated with religion and religious administration. [8]
Council of Ministers
Executive governance is conducted through the Sultan and appointed ministers. [8]
Council sessions
Legislative Council sessions are called and conducted under established rules; they are not competitive national elections. [10]
Political competition
The political system lacks the competitive national election mechanism found in parliamentary democracies. [17]
2026 freedom rating
Freedom House classifies Brunei as “Not Free” (27/100) based on political rights and civil liberties criteria. [17]
Political-rights subtotal
Freedom House 2026: 7/40. [17]
Civil-liberties subtotal
Freedom House 2026: 20/60. [17]
Press environment
The 2026 independent assessment identifies restrictions on media and public political expression. [17]
Rule-of-law context
Civil statutes and Islamic law operate in overlapping spheres; legal applications vary by subject and personal status. [11] [17]
Administrative districts
Four districts administer local government functions. [3]
Governance development goal
State planning links administrative capacity to the Wawasan Brunei 2035 objectives. [18]

Sources and Methodology

UNESCO World Heritage properties are distinct from tentative-list entries and intangible cultural heritage elements. Political-rights assessments are attributed to their issuing organizations, not presented as official government classifications.

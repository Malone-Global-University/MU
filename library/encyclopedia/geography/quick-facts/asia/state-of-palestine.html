Country Overview

The State of Palestine is recognized by many governments and holds United Nations non-member observer State status. Its claimed territory comprises the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, and the Gaza Strip. Israeli occupation and the fragmented exercise of authority have significant consequences for population statistics, government institutions, movement, the economy and everyday life. Palestinian national institutions are centered in Ramallah, while East Jerusalem is claimed as the capital; Jerusalem’s legal and political status remains disputed. This profile separates Palestinian statistical estimates from international model estimates and distinguishes diplomatic recognition from effective territorial control.

Data and status note: The Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS) reports 5,557,096 residents at the end of 2025 (3,430,062 in the West Bank and 2,127,034 in Gaza), while older PCBS pre-conflict projections and World Bank model estimates differ. PCBS current-dollar GDP for 2025 is approximately US$12.463 billion; a World Bank 2026 regional report uses US$16.9 billion for its 2025 GDP series. These are not reconciled national accounts and must be identified by publisher. The Palestinian Authority does not exercise uniform control of Gaza or the West Bank. The National Committee for the Administration of Gaza was established in January 2026 but a September 2026 UN report said it had not yet entered Gaza to assume governing duties.

Sources and Methodology

The geographical scope is the West Bank (including East Jerusalem) and Gaza Strip, not the Palestinian diaspora. Population figures use revised PCBS end-2025 estimates; older model projections and humanitarian displacement indicators are not interchangeable. PCBS 2025 national GDP (US$12.463bn) differs from World Bank estimates, and the PCBS 2025 external-trade figures are goods plus services rather than goods alone. Political descriptions distinguish recognition, UN observer status, PLO representation, Palestinian Authority institutions, Israel’s occupation and territorial access, and the January 2026 Gaza transitional committee that had not assumed physical operations according to the September UNSCO update. This profile records competing sovereignty claims and internationally recognized legal context without presuming that formal institutions exercise uninterrupted, territory-wide authority.

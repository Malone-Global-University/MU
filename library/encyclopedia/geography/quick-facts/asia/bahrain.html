Country Overview

The Kingdom of Bahrain is an island state in the Persian Gulf (also called the Arabian Gulf), situated east of Saudi Arabia and northwest of Qatar. Its capital, Manama, is a regional finance, commerce and logistics center. Since independence in 1971, Bahrain has developed from a pearling and trading hub into an economy built around petroleum refining, aluminum, finance and increasingly diversified services. It is a constitutional monarchy with a powerful hereditary executive and an elected lower legislative chamber. Three UNESCO World Heritage properties preserve the islands’ Dilmun and pearling history. [1] [13] [16] [20]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Kingdom of Bahrain (مملكة البحرين). [1]
Short name
Bahrain (البحرين). [1]
Meaning of name
Arabic al-Baḥrayn conventionally means “the two seas”; explanations of the underlying geographic reference vary. [4]
Regional classification
Western Asia under United Nations statistical classification; part of the Arabian Peninsula/Persian Gulf diplomatic region. [4]
Capital
Manama (المنامة). [1]
Largest urban center
Manama and the surrounding metropolitan agglomeration on Bahrain Island. [4]
Official language
Arabic. [1]
Other widely used language
English is widely used in business, professional services and expatriate communities. [1]
Currency
Bahraini dinar (BHD), divided into 1,000 fils. [1]
Exchange-rate regime
Fixed link to the U.S. dollar: approximately BHD 0.376 = US$1, or BHD 1 ≈ US$2.65; market transaction margins may differ. [1]
Time zone
Arabia Standard Time (UTC+3); no regular daylight saving time. [1]
International dialing code
+973. [1]
Country internet domain
.bh. [1]
ISO 3166 codes
BH (alpha-2), BHR (alpha-3), 048 (numeric). [4]
National flag
Red field with a white serrated vertical band of five points at the hoist. [1]
National Day
16 December; commemorates the assumption of power by the late Emir Isa bin Salman Al Khalifa in 1961 and is distinct from the 15 August independence date. [1]
Independence
15 August 1971, ending British protection and formalizing independent statehood. [1]
UN admission
21 September 1971. [5]
Modern monarchy
Al Khalifa family; Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa became emir in 1999 and king when Bahrain was declared a kingdom in 2002. [1]
Constitutional milestones
1973 constitution; National Action Charter approved in 2001; revised constitution promulgated in 2002, amended subsequently. [6] [7]
Ancient historical identity
Dilmun civilization was a major Bronze Age trade nexus linking Mesopotamia and the Indus region. [20]
Historic trade
Pearl diving, maritime commerce and shipbuilding shaped Bahrain’s pre-oil economy. [20]
Oil discovery
The country’s first oil discovery was at Jebel al-Dukhan on 1 June 1932, the earliest commercial discovery in the Arab Gulf region. [26]
Kingdom proclaimed
14 February 2002, following the National Action Charter process. [6]

Geography and Population

Broad location
Island archipelago in the Persian/Arabian Gulf, offshore eastern Saudi Arabia. [1]
Continent
Asia, United Nations Western Asia region. [4]
Land area
786.8 km² reported by the national portal for 2022. [1]
Land and territorial waters
8,269 km² total reported by the government: land 786.8 km² and territorial waters 7,482.2 km² (2022). [1]
Area-definition caveat
UN reports a different 778 km² surface-area figure in its summary. Land reclamation and statistical definitions affect area comparisons. [4] [1]
Land borders
No direct land borders with another state; connected to Saudi Arabia by road bridge/causeway. [1]
Maritime neighbors
Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Iran across Gulf waters; maritime boundaries are legally distinct from land borders. [23]
Principal island
Bahrain Island, which contains much of the population and Manama. [1]
Other islands
Muharraq and Sitra are major inhabited islands; Hawar Islands lie toward Qatar and are ecologically important. [1] [20]
King Fahd Causeway
Road link to Saudi Arabia, opened in 1986, crucial for cross-border movement and tourism. [1]
Principal airport
Bahrain International Airport, on Muharraq Island. [1]
Principal seaport
Khalifa Bin Salman Port serves modern container and cargo traffic. [1]
Landform
Low-lying islands, limestone desert terrain and some central uplands. [4]
Highest elevation
Jebel al-Dukhan, approximately 134 meters above sea level. [1]
Climate
Hot, arid climate with very hot summers and mild winters; low and irregular rainfall. [4]
Water resources
Very limited renewable freshwater; groundwater and seawater desalination are strategically important. [4]
Vegetation
Sparse desert vegetation, palms in cultivated areas and coastal/marine habitats. [20]
Coastline and reclamation
Urban expansion and land reclamation alter coastlines; quoted island counts and area estimates vary by survey date. [1]
Governorates
Four: Capital, Muharraq, Northern and Southern. [1]
Capital Governorate
Includes central Manama and other northern/eastern urban districts. [1]
Muharraq Governorate
Historic Muharraq, airport and culturally important pearling sites. [1] [20]
Northern Governorate
Dense residential towns and communities on the northern side of Bahrain Island. [1]
Southern Governorate
Larger southern territory with industrial, military and desert zones. [1]

People and Society

Population composition
Bahrainis were 46.54% and non-Bahrainis 53.46% of the official 2025 population. [1]
Citizenship and ethnicity
Citizenship (Bahraini vs. non-Bahraini) is not a complete ethnic classification. [1]
Major expatriate communities
Residents from South Asia, other Arab countries, Southeast Asia and elsewhere contribute to the labor force and cultural life. [23] [1]
Official religion
Islam is the state religion under the constitutional framework. [1] [6]
Muslim communities
Shi’a and Sunni Muslims both have deep histories; numerical estimates for religious affiliation among citizens vary by source. [23] [22]
Non-Muslim faiths
Christian, Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist and other communities are represented, particularly among expatriates. [1]
Religious demographics caution
No single up-to-date census of religious adherence should be inferred from older sectarian percentage estimates. [23] [22]
Languages
Arabic is official; English, Hindi, Urdu, Bengali, Malayalam and other languages are heard in a multilingual labor market. [1] [23]
Arabic dialect
Bahraini Arabic is widely spoken, including locally distinct urban, Gulf and rural varieties. [1]
School and higher education
Public education and university-level programs coexist with private/international schools serving a diverse population. [1]
Literature and poetry
Arabic poetry, prose, modern literary movements and religious scholarship remain major cultural forms. [21]
Maritime heritage
Pearling, boatbuilding, navigation and coastal trade are central to Bahrain’s historical identity. [20]
Fjiri
A vocal and musical performance associated with the historical pearling community; added to UNESCO’s intangible list in 2021. [21]
Arabic calligraphy
Shared multinational UNESCO intangible heritage inscription (2021), including Bahraini participation. [21]
Date-palm traditions
Cultivation knowledge and related craft/practice traditions inscribed on UNESCO’s list in 2022. [21]
Henna
Ritual, aesthetic and social practices inscribed in 2024 (multinational heritage). [21]
Bisht craftsmanship
Men’s bisht or abaa ceremonial garment skills and practices inscribed in 2025. [21]
Traditional crafts
Palm-frond work, metalwork, pottery, textile ornament and boatmaking remain associated with local heritage. [21] [20]
Culinary culture
Rice-and-meat dishes such as machboos, seafood, dates and spiced tea/coffee are well-known cultural traditions. [1]
Historic district
Muharraq’s pearling-related buildings and pathways form a UNESCO-listed cultural landscape. [20]
Archaeology
Dilmun-era temples, settlements, burial mounds and Qal’at al-Bahrain reflect long-distance Bronze Age trade. [20]
Major annual observances
Ramadan, Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha and national commemorations are prominent; Islamic holiday dates vary by the lunar calendar. [1]
Urban society
Manama combines Gulf Arab neighborhoods, commercial towers, markets and diverse migrant communities. [1] [4]
Public health indicator
2024 life expectancy was about 81.4 years in the World Bank series. [2]
Labor rights concerns
Migrant-worker protections, employment sponsorship and worker mobility are persistent topics of labor-policy debate. [23] [22]
Women in elected lower chamber
8 of 40 (20%) immediately following the November 2022 election, as recorded by the IPU. [9] [10]
Civic and expression restrictions
Independent observers cite significant limits on political opposition, public assembly and media expression. [22]
Cultural heritage classification
UNESCO World Heritage properties differ from items on UNESCO’s intangible heritage lists. [20] [21]

Government and Institutions

State structure
Hereditary constitutional monarchy with a strong royal executive. [1] [6]
Head of state
King Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa, reigning since 1999 (king since 2002). [1]
Head of government
Prime Minister Salman bin Hamad Al Khalifa, also Crown Prince and Deputy Supreme Commander. [1] [8]
Ruling dynasty
House of Al Khalifa. [1]
Constitution
2002 constitutional order based on the National Action Charter, with subsequent amendments including in 2012. [7] [6]
Legislature
Bicameral National Assembly (al-Majlis al-Watani). [1] [6]
Lower house
Council of Representatives: 40 seats, directly elected. [9]
Upper house
Shura Council: 40 members appointed by the King. [11]
Legislative elections
Most recent completed lower-house election was 12 and 19 November 2022 (first round and runoffs). [9]
November 2022 turnout
73.2% of registered voters, according to IPU figures derived from election reporting. [9]
Registered voters (2022)
344,713 on the 2022 election register. [9]
Votes cast (2022)
252,256 recorded voters for 2022 turnout calculation. [9]
Lower-house women elected (2022)
8 of 40; 20% at election/renewal. [9]
Upper-house women at 2022 appointment
10 of 40; 25% of appointed Shura Council. [11]
Speaker, Council of Representatives
Ahmed bin Salman Almusalam, elected 12 December 2022. [10]
Political parties
Conventional political parties are not authorized; political societies have faced substantial restrictions and dissolutions. [9] [22]
Opposition and 2022 election
Main organized opposition associations, including the dissolved Al-Wefaq group, were not active as competitive electoral parties in 2022. [9] [22]
Royal executive powers
King appoints the prime minister and Shura Council; executive influence over national policy and legislation is substantial. [6] [1]
Council of Ministers
Executive cabinet headed by the prime minister, whose ministerial appointments operate under royal constitutional powers. [6] [8]
Judiciary
Civil, criminal and religious courts operate under Bahraini law; independent analysts question judicial independence in political cases. [6] [22]
Administrative system
Four governorates administered under national law and state institutions. [1]
Constitutional rights
Rights are set out in the constitution; external evaluations distinguish formal protections from enforcement in practice. [6] [22]
Political-rights assessment (2026)
Freedom House assigns 12/100, “Not Free” for combined political rights and civil liberties; this is an external research assessment. [22]
2026 institutional snapshot
The king and prime minister and the November 2022 legislative cycle are referenced to sources available as of November 6, 2026; not described as a new 2026 parliamentary election. [1] [9] [12]
2011 protest movement
Pro-democracy demonstrations and their suppression remain central to debates over representation, sectarian relations and civil liberties. [22] [23]
Civil society
Various professional, charitable, religious and community organizations operate, but independent political advocacy faces constraints. [22]

International trade orientation

Sources and Methodology

This profile prioritizes official Bahraini institutions and original intergovernmental sources for time-specific measurements and uses independent observers for civil liberties assessments. Dates beside figures identify the observation period rather than necessarily the publication year. “2025” data cannot be assumed to refer to calendar year 2026, and neither foreign-resident shares nor religious descriptions should be inferred as ethnic census totals.

Methodology notes: Land area, total territory including territorial waters, and UN surface area are different denominators. Official national 2025 population and World Bank/UN 2025 population estimates reflect distinct methods. Trade tables use 2024 WTO detailed-products data; UNCTAD aggregate merchandise values are presented separately because their petroleum coverage differs. The 2022 National Assembly renewal is the last completed election referenced; the Freedom House 2026 score is an external institutional assessment, not an official Bahrain government classification.

Country Overview

The State of Qatar occupies a peninsula on the Arabian Gulf coast and is a hereditary monarchy governed by Emir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani. Its economy depends on large natural-gas and petroleum resources, global liquefied-natural-gas trade, public investment, aviation and expanding services. Residents include a very large expatriate workforce. The country has used gas-export revenue to invest in infrastructure, education and international diplomacy. This profile differentiates the World Bank’s 2025 population series from Qatar’s monthly population-present series and reflects the 2024 constitutional change that returned the Shura Council to appointment.

Sources and Methodology

Qatar’s World Bank annual 2025 population estimate of 2,972,215 differs from Qatar National Planning Council’s monthly count of people physically present (3,370,611 in March 2026), which changes with expatriate travel and labor mobility. National accounts use 2025 World Bank GDP of US$215.56bn and real growth of 2.9%; the IMF’s 2026 projections are neither the same year nor realized outcomes. UNCTAD 2024 goods trade (US$94.286bn exported, US$35.802bn imported) is presented separately from NPC 2025 monthly and partner-share data. Political arrangements include the 2024 constitutional amendment, 2025 appointment of 49 Shura Council members and October 2026 council session. Al Zubarah is the single inscribed UNESCO property, while other proposed sites remain tentative.

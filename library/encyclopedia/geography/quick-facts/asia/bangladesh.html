Country Overview

Bangladesh is a populous republic in South Asia occupying much of the Ganges–Brahmaputra–Meghna delta. Its modern state emerged through the 1971 Liberation War; its economy combines an internationally significant garment industry with agriculture, services and remittance income. After the 2024 political uprising and an interim administration, a parliamentary election in February 2026 returned an elected government. The country faces major adaptation, fiscal, employment and refugee-support challenges.

Precolonial history
The Bengal delta was shaped by Buddhist, Hindu and Islamic polities, trade networks and literary cultures. [16] [17]
Colonial era
The region formed part of British India until partition in 1947. [18]
Partition of 1947
Predominantly Muslim eastern Bengal became East Bengal / East Pakistan, separated geographically from West Pakistan by Indian territory. [18]
Language Movement
The 1952 Bengali language movement became a defining event in the emergence of Bengali political identity. [17] [18]
Liberation War
Armed struggle in 1971 culminated in independent Bangladesh in December. [18]

The independent republic adopted its constitution in 1972; subsequent amendments and political upheavals changed institutions. [7] [8]
United Nations admission
17 September 1974. [18]
Contemporary transition
A mass protest movement in 2024 ended Sheikh Hasina’s premiership and preceded an interim administration led by Muhammad Yunus. [7] [8]
Return to elected government

Regional identity
A Bengali-majority state with South Asian, Bay of Bengal and Indian Ocean connections. [1] [3]

Historic-political note: Bangladesh’s 2024–2026 political transition is not accurately described by simply listing the previous elected government or the 2024–2026 interim administration as today’s government. The February 2026 election established a new parliamentary baseline.

Geography and Population

Geographical setting
Low-lying country centered on the Ganges–Brahmaputra–Meghna delta, opening to the Bay of Bengal. [1] [14]
Land boundaries
Borders India on the west, north and much of the east and Myanmar to the southeast. [1]
Coastline and waters
Southern coast fronts the Bay of Bengal and the northern Indian Ocean. [1]
UN total surface area
148,460 km² in the UN country profile; land-only denominators are lower. [1]
UN land-area reference
Approximately 130,170 km²; distinct from the total-area measure including inland water. [9]
National administrative divisions
8 divisions and 64 districts. [6]
Eight divisions
Dhaka, Chattogram, Khulna, Rajshahi, Sylhet, Barishal, Rangpur and Mymensingh. [6]
Capital-region role
Dhaka and surrounding urban districts concentrate administration, services, universities and manufacturing. [6] [3]
Other principal cities
Chattogram (main seaport), Khulna, Rajshahi, Sylhet, Rangpur, Barishal, Mymensingh and Gazipur. [6] [3]
Main port
Chattogram (Chittagong) on the southeastern coast; a core national import-export gateway. [3] [6]
Delta rivers
The Padma, Jamuna and Meghna form the principal large-river network. [6]
Other major waterways
Surma, Brahmaputra, Teesta, Karnaphuli and numerous distributaries and tidal channels. [6]
River transport
Inland waterways and ferries link delta settlements, markets and islands. [6] [3]
Floodplain landform
Broad alluvial plains support densely settled agriculture but face seasonal river flooding. [14] [3]
Chattogram Hill Tracts
Hilly southeastern uplands with diverse Indigenous communities, forest landscapes and distinct land-use issues. [6] [8]
Sylhet region
Northeastern plains and wetlands include tea-producing hills and extensive seasonally flooded haor basins. [6] [14]
Sundarbans
Tidal mangrove forest shared with India; the Bangladeshi sector is a UNESCO World Heritage property. [16]
Coastal islands
Sandbars and river-mouth islands (chars) shift under sediment deposition and erosion. [14]
Climate
Tropical monsoon regime with hot, humid rainy months and generally drier, cooler winter periods. [14] [6]
Monsoon season
Most rainfall falls during the summer southwest monsoon, approximately June–September. [6] [14]
Cyclone exposure
Coastal storm surge, tropical cyclones and high winds pose major disaster risks. [14] [3]
Flood exposure
Monsoon floods, flash floods, riverbank erosion and urban inundation occur in different landscapes. [14]
Salinity risks
Sea-level rise and saltwater intrusion threaten some coastal farmland and drinking-water supplies. [14] [3]

People and Society

Ethnolinguistic majority
Bengali-speaking people constitute the majority, alongside numerous minority and Indigenous communities. [6] [8]
National language
Bangla; written predominantly in the Bengali script. [6]
English use
Used in international commerce, legal practice, universities and parts of administration. [3]
Other languages
Chakma, Marma, Santali and additional regional and Indigenous languages, particularly in upland and border communities. [6] [8]
Religious population, 2022 census
About 91.08% Muslim and 7.96% Hindu, using the 2022 enumerated census reported in the UK source. [21]
Buddhist population, 2022 census
Approximately 0.61%, distinct from the majority Islamic population. [21]
Christian population, 2022 census
Approximately 0.30%, using the same enumerated census basis. [21]
Religion and public policy
Islam is the state religion under the constitutional framework; religious freedom and minority protections are prominent public issues. [21] [8]
Ethnic and religious diversity
Minority communities include Hindus, Buddhists, Christians and several Indigenous and regional populations. [21] [8]
Literary heritage
Bengali poetry, prose and music connect modern Bangladesh with the broader Bengali cultural region. [17]
National poet
Kazi Nazrul Islam is widely honored as Bangladesh’s national poet. [17]
Rabindranath Tagore
Tagore’s poetry and music are central to Bengali literary heritage; his composition provides the national anthem. [17]
National anthem
Amar Shonar Bangla, associated with Rabindranath Tagore. [6]
International Mother Language Day
21 February, linked to the 1952 Bengali language movement and observed internationally through UNESCO. [17]
Pohela Boishakh
Bengali New Year, traditionally marked by public celebrations, crafts and cultural performance. [17]
Mangal Shobhajatra
New Year procession entered UNESCO’s Representative List in 2016. [17]
Jamdani weaving
Traditional handwoven patterned textile art inscribed by UNESCO in 2013. [17]
Tangail saree weaving
Traditional weaving art inscribed on UNESCO’s list in 2025. [17]
Baul song tradition
Mystic poetic and musical performances; UNESCO intangible heritage inscription in 2008. [17]
Shital Pati mats
Traditional woven mats of Sylhet recognized as intangible heritage in 2017. [17]
Dhaka rickshaw painting
Rickshaw artistry recognized by UNESCO in 2023. [17]
Education trend
School enrollment, especially for girls, has expanded substantially since independence, alongside persistent quality gaps. [3]
Public health
Health outcomes improved over decades through primary services, immunization and community initiatives. [3] [15]
Life expectancy, 2024
Around 75 years at birth in the World Bank series. [2]
Water and sanitation
Safe drinking water, urban sanitation, flood resilience and groundwater quality remain uneven challenges. [3] [14]
Women’s employment
Export-manufacturing jobs have expanded paid employment opportunities while occupational rights remain significant concerns. [3]
Rohingya refugee presence

Security and policing

Public policy priorities
Economic stabilization, employment, inflation, public administration, bank oversight and disaster resilience. [12] [3]

Sources and Methodology

Entries are a source-anchored reference compilation, not a single statistical extract. Census counts, administrative population figures and international estimates are separate series; nominal GDP is not purchasing-power-parity output. WTO, UNCTAD and national customs trade series can differ by definition, coverage and reporting period. Regional classification follows the United Nations M49 statistical convention rather than asserting exclusive cultural or political geography. UNESCO inscribed, tentative and intangible heritage are distinct categories.

Editorial cutoff: November 6, 2026 (Pacific Time). A dated statistic describes its reference period, not necessarily its publication date. Disputed or internationally contested governance claims are described with their attribution and jurisdiction. The page may require later revision when new releases or events occur.

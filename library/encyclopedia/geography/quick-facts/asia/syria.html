Country Overview

Syria is a Western Asian country on the eastern Mediterranean with a long history of urban civilization and regional exchange. After the fall of the Assad government in December 2024, Ahmed al-Sharaa became transitional president in January 2025 and a provisional constitutional arrangement was issued in March. A transitional People’s Assembly began meeting in 2026; it is not equivalent to a fully elected constitutional parliament. Recovery, reconstruction, contested administration, intercommunal violence, displaced populations and security negotiations continue to shape the country. Statistical information is severely affected by war and migration, and the World Bank’s last displayed current-dollar GDP figure is from 2022, not 2025.

Data and status note: The World Bank estimates 25,620,427 residents in 2025, which is a model-based estimate in a country with massive displacement, not a new national census. The last GDP figure shown on the World Bank country dashboard is US$23.74 billion for 2022; no comparable 2025 current-dollar GDP value is published there. Syria has had no separate prime minister since formation of the March 2025 transitional cabinet. Its 210-seat provisional People’s Assembly, with selection and appointment procedures, must not be described as a direct national general election. All six UNESCO World Heritage properties are cultural and are subject to extraordinary conflict-related conservation risks.

Sources and Methodology

MGU Quick Facts distinguishes wartime population estimates, surviving historical economic series, transitional appointments and indirectly selected legislatures. This country page does not claim that a 2022 nominal GDP value represents 2025 output, nor that a provisional assembly arose from universal national elections. Numbers for returnees and displacement change frequently; consult UNHCR/OCHA for current operational counts. Territorial disputes, sanctions and security developments are described as dated assessments rather than settled conditions.

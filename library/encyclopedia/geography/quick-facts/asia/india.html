Country Overview

India is a federal parliamentary republic occupying most of the South Asian subcontinent. Its population is the world’s largest in current international estimates, with major linguistic, religious, ecological and regional diversity. The country combines a large services economy with industrial production, agriculture and an expanding technological sector. Its strategic geography spans the Himalayas, Indian Ocean, and the Bay of Bengal; contemporary policy debates encompass jobs, infrastructure, climate resilience and federal governance.

Identity & State Formation

Common short names
India and Bharat are constitutional forms of the country's name. [8]
Capital
New Delhi, seat of Union government; Delhi is the surrounding National Capital Territory. [9] [8]
UN geographic region
Southern Asia under the UN M49 statistical classification. [1]
Government type
Federal parliamentary republic with a constitution and an elected legislature. [8]
National independence
15 August 1947, end of British colonial rule and partition of British India. [8]
Republic Day
26 January, marking entry into force of the Constitution in 1950. [8]
Constitution adopted
26 November 1949 by the Constituent Assembly. [8]
Constitution in force
26 January 1950; amended repeatedly since. [8]
Official Union language
Hindi in Devanagari is the constitutional official language of the Union; English remains in official use under law. [7] [8]
National-language misconception
India has no single legally designated national language; Union official languages and state languages are distinct concepts. [7] [8]
Scheduled languages
22 languages appear in the Constitution's Eighth Schedule. [8]
National currency
Indian rupee (INR), subdivided into 100 paise. [22]

ISO codes
IN (alpha-2), IND (alpha-3), and 356 (numeric). [1]

Internet country-code domain
.in; .भारत also exists as an internationalized country-code domain. [1]
Time standard
Indian Standard Time (UTC+05:30) throughout the country, with no regular daylight-saving switch. [1]
National flag
Horizontal saffron, white and green stripes with a navy-blue 24-spoke Ashoka Chakra. [8]
National emblem
Adaptation of the Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath. [8] [17]
National anthem
Jana Gana Mana, by Rabindranath Tagore. [8]
National song
Vande Mataram, associated with Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay. [8]
Colonial antecedent
Much of present-day India was ruled under the British Raj before independence. [8]
Partition of 1947
The transfer of power was accompanied by the creation of Pakistan and mass population displacement. [8]
Post-1947 integration
Former princely states were integrated into the new Union through different political and legal processes. [8]
Federal arrangement
Powers are divided between the Union and states, with constitutionally defined lists and institutions. [8]
Administrative divisions
28 states and 8 union territories as of November 2026. [9]
Citizenship
National citizenship is governed by constitutional and statutory law rather than a system of state citizenship. [8]
National election cycle
Lok Sabha general elections normally occur every five years unless dissolved earlier. [8]
International sovereignty

Ganges-Brahmaputra delta

Climate pattern
Mostly tropical monsoon in the south and plains, grading to arid, subtropical and alpine regions. [16]
Summer monsoon
Southwest monsoon typically supplies much of the annual rainfall, with large regional variation. [16]
Northeast monsoon
Especially important to rainfall in parts of southeastern India. [16]
Climate hazards
Floods, heatwaves, tropical cyclones, droughts and glacial-lake risks affect different regions. [16]

People & Society

Indo-Aryan, Dravidian, Austroasiatic and Tibeto-Burman languages are major parts of the linguistic landscape. [4]

English

Major eastern Indian language and one of the Eighth Schedule languages. [8]
Tamil
Major Dravidian language with a longstanding literary history and significant global diaspora. [8]
Telugu
Major southern Indian language with substantial literature and cultural production. [8]
Marathi
Major Indo-Aryan language associated with Maharashtra. [8]
Gujarati
Major western Indian language associated with Gujarat and overseas communities. [8]
Punjabi
Major northern language, written in Gurmukhi in Indian Punjab. [8]
Urdu
One of the Eighth Schedule languages and part of north Indian literary culture. [8]

Also include Assamese, Kannada, Malayalam, Odia, Kashmiri, Konkani and others; state language policies differ. [8]

Sources and Methodology

MGU Quick Facts synthesizes dated national and international statistical series, legislation and institutional publications. A population census is an enumeration, whereas World Bank annual values and national intercensal projections are estimates; nominal current-dollar GDP is not purchasing-power-parity GDP. WTO/UN Comtrade, UNCTAD, and national customs or fiscal-year accounts can differ. Regional classification uses UN M49 and should not be interpreted as a political claim. UNESCO World Heritage properties, tentative sites, and intangible-heritage elements are separate registries.

Editorial cutoff: November 6, 2026 (Pacific Time). Values reflect the year printed alongside each fact. Contested sovereignty, constitutional status and administrative transfer processes receive explicit jurisdictional caution. An update is required when later elections, census releases or legal changes supersede the values shown.

Country Overview

The Kingdom of Bhutan is a landlocked Himalayan country between India and China. Centered on the valleys, forested slopes and high mountains of the eastern Himalayas, it combines a distinctive Buddhist cultural heritage with a parliamentary constitutional monarchy established in 2008. Hydroelectricity, agriculture, tourism and public services anchor the economy. Bhutan’s Gross National Happiness framework seeks to assess wellbeing more broadly than gross domestic product, while the country faces challenges from youth outmigration, geographic isolation and climate exposure. Geography and Population

Location
Landlocked between India (south, east and west) and China (north). [3]
Physical setting
Eastern Himalayas, from subtropical southern foothills through temperate valleys to alpine and glaciated northern mountains. [16] [3]
Coastline
None; no direct access to the sea. [3]
UN surface area
38,394 km² in the UN statistical profile; land-only denominators may differ. [1]

796,682 (estimated). [2]

Approximately 797,000 (rounded UN profile). [1]

Approximately 0.6% in the World Bank series. [2]

Capital population, UN 2025
Approximately 203,300 for Thimphu under the UN city-population estimate, not a 2025 city census. [1]
Major urban center
Thimphu is the principal administrative, service and education center. [1] [3]
Other important towns
Phuentsholing (border commerce), Paro (aviation), Punakha (historic administrative center), and Gelephu (southern development corridor). [3] [16]
Administrative divisions
20 dzongkhags (districts), with smaller local-government subdivisions. [8] [10]
Western Himalayan valleys
Thimphu, Paro, Haa and Punakha valleys form a major corridor of settlement and institutions. [16]
Southern foothills
Warmer, wetter forests and river valleys along the Indian border. [16]
Northern mountain belt
High-altitude alpine terrain and glaciers near the Tibetan Plateau. [16] [17]
Highest mountain commonly cited
Gangkar Punsum (Gangkhar Puensum), approximately 7,570 m, recorded as the country’s high point and the world’s highest unclimbed summit. [24]
Major river systems
The Wang Chhu, Punatsang Chhu, Mangde Chhu and Drangme Chhu systems flow generally toward India. [3] [17]
Climate
Subtropical in parts of the south, temperate in central valleys, and alpine to nival at the highest elevations. [16]
Monsoon
Summer South Asian monsoon, broadly June/July–September, with spatial variation. [16]
Winter weather
Cold in upland regions and mountain passes; southern plains remain comparatively mild. [16]
Forest share, 2024
70.4% of land area in the World Bank’s forest-area indicator. [2]
Constitutional forest objective
The constitutional framework requires maintaining at least 60% forest cover for all time. [11]
Electricity access, 2024
100.0% in the World Bank’s access-to-electricity statistical series. [2]
Conservation and biodiversity
Forests, protected areas and ecological corridors safeguard temperate and subtropical ecosystems. [25] [3]
Climate exposure
Glacial-lake outburst floods, landslides, variable rainfall, drought and warming can threaten settlements, crops and hydropower. [17]
Population mobility
Overseas labor migration and urbanization affect Bhutan’s labor force and household structures. [3] [5]
Geographical planning challenge
Steep slopes, limited arable terrain and long transport routes increase costs for infrastructure and trade. [3] [17]

Area and forest coverage are not interchangeable: the World Bank forest percentage uses a land-area denominator, while the UN surface-area statistic may include other classifications. Population entries above are model-based contemporary estimates rather than the last national census count. [1] [2] [20]

People and Society

National language
Dzongkha is the official and national language. [16] [11]
Linguistic diversity
The national tourism authority describes 19 spoken languages in Bhutan. [16]
English in education
English is widely used as a language of formal instruction alongside Dzongkha. [16]
Other community languages
Sharchopkha (Tshangla), Nepali (Lhotshamkha), Bumthangkha and other regional languages are spoken. [16]
Principal religious tradition
Vajrayana Buddhism, particularly Drukpa Kagyu and Nyingma traditions, is central to national history and culture. [11] [16]
Additional religious communities
Hindu traditions are important, especially among Nepali-speaking populations in southern Bhutan. [16]
Religious demographic caution
Religious affiliations should not be equated mechanically with language or ethnicity; comparable recent census percentages are not asserted here. [20]
Gross National Happiness
Bhutan evaluates wellbeing through a multidimensional Gross National Happiness index alongside conventional economic indicators. [14] [3]
GNH domains
Nine domains, including psychological wellbeing, health, education, cultural diversity, governance, environment and living standards. [14]
GNH measurement
The 2022 GNH Survey used 33 weighted indicators across nine domains. [14]
GNH Index, 2022
0.781 on a 0-to-1 index; not a share of GDP or an international ranking. [14]
People classified as deeply/extensively happy, 2022
48.1% under the survey’s defined multidimensional thresholds; not simply a one-question mood poll. [14]
GNH interpretation
The index is defined by sufficiency cutoffs and a 66%-weighted overall happiness threshold, so “0.781” must not be read as “78.1% of people are happy.” [14]
Traditional architecture
Dzongs, whitewashed farmhouses, prayer walls, temples and ornate timber detailing are major forms. [12] [16]
Dzong function
Fortress-monasteries historically combined administrative, defensive and religious functions. [12]
Traditional dress
Gho (commonly worn by men) and kira (commonly worn by women) are characteristic dress forms. [16]
Festivals
Tshechu religious festivals feature dances, ritual performances and community gatherings. [13] [16]
Drametse mask dance
“Mask dance of the drums from Drametse” is Bhutan’s UNESCO-listed intangible heritage element (inscribed 2008). [13]
Paro Taktsang
The Tiger’s Nest monastery is an iconic Buddhist pilgrimage site above Paro valley; it is not an inscribed World Heritage property. [12] [16]
Punakha Dzong
A historic river-confluence dzong renowned for religious and administrative significance. [12]
Food traditions
Red rice, buckwheat, chilies, cheese dishes such as ema datshi, and regional vegetables and meats are common. [16]
Archery
Archery is a prominent national sporting and communal tradition. [16]
Education access
Publicly supported education has expanded, although geography can complicate rural access. [3]
Public health
Broad improvements in health outcomes accompany rising life expectancy and electricity access. [2] [3]
Life expectancy at birth
Approximately 73 years in 2024 World Bank indicators. [2]
Electricity access
100.0% in 2024 World Bank access statistics; reliability and quality still matter beyond the access indicator. [2]
Poverty
National poverty rate of 12.4% in 2022; not directly equivalent to PPP-adjusted international poverty thresholds. [3]
Youth employment challenges
Labor-market opportunities and overseas migration remain important concerns in the 2026 World Bank/IMF assessments. [6] [5]
Urban and rural communities
Urban Thimphu and valley towns coexist with remote village economies shaped by agriculture, crafts and local governance. [3] [16]
Social diversity
Regional language, terrain, religious life and mobility patterns produce varied local social experiences. [16] [3]
Cultural and natural preservation
State preservation policies connect ecological stewardship with local community traditions and development planning. [17] [12]
Religious and historic sites
Monasteries and dzongs remain culturally significant independent of formal UNESCO inscription. [12] [13]
Climate and livelihoods
Climate hazards can disrupt farming, water security and transport, particularly in isolated communities. [17]

Gross National Happiness is a specific research construct based on weighted indicator sufficiency. It is presented here as a 2022 survey-based national index, not as a claim that Bhutan has the happiest population in the world. [14]

King Jigme Khesar Namgyel Wangchuck, the fifth Druk Gyalpo. [7]

Bicameral Parliament comprising the National Assembly and National Council, together with the Druk Gyalpo in the constitutional lawmaking framework. [11] [9]

National Council party affiliations
Members may not belong to a political party under the constitutional arrangements. [11] [10]
National Assembly electoral system
The two top parties from a national primary contest field candidates in single-member general-election constituencies. [8] [9]
Prime minister appointment
Constitutional government formation follows legislative results, with the king formally appointing the prime minister. [11] [9]
Council of Ministers
Cabinet is headed by the prime minister, with ministers responsible for national portfolios. [7] [11]
Judicial system
Supreme Court and subordinate courts operate within the constitutional judicial framework. [11]
Local administration
Twenty dzongkhags and elected local bodies, including gewogs and municipalities under the domestic administrative framework. [11] [8]
State religion and religious freedom
The constitution identifies Buddhism as spiritual heritage while providing for freedom of religion under constitutional conditions. [11]
Rights and responsibilities
Constitution sets out rights, duties, environmental obligations and institutional checks. [11]
Environmental constitutional floor
At least 60% forest coverage is to be maintained for all time. [11]
National policy framework
Gross National Happiness informs governance and planning, while national accounts track output and income separately. [14] [3]
Election-date caution
No 2026 National Assembly election is claimed; the 2024 election remains the most recent completed lower-house election. [9]

Sources and Methodology

This page prioritizes original international statistical agencies and Bhutanese institutions for source-dated quantitative claims and distinguishes institutional facts from interpretations. The word “current” refers to information available at the November 6, 2026 verification cutoff, not to a claim that every displayed indicator was measured in 2026.

Cross-series conventions: The World Bank’s 2025 estimated population and GDP figures and UN’s 2025 rounded population are separate estimates. The WTO’s 2025 HS-2022 merchandise-product totals are not equivalent to Bhutan’s electricity-inclusive national customs accounts. The national figures are presented in ngultrum (Nu), not converted to US dollars. Calendar-year GDP growth and Bhutan’s fiscal-year projections are labeled separately. GNH 0.781 denotes a survey-defined 2022 index, not 78.1% of respondents. The 2024 election establishes the parliamentary baseline; zero UNESCO-inscribed World Heritage properties must not be confused with eight Tentative List submissions. [2] [1] [4] [21] [14] [9] [12]

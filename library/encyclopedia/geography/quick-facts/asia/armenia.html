Country Overview

The Republic of Armenia is a mountainous, landlocked state in the South Caucasus. The United Nations places it in Western Asia for statistical purposes, while its history, diaspora and Council of Europe membership connect it to wider European political and cultural networks. Its capital is Yerevan. Armenia’s economy combines services, technology, industry, agriculture and mineral trade; its political system is a parliamentary republic. Its three UNESCO World Heritage properties include some of the region’s best-known Armenian ecclesiastical monuments. [1] [5] [10] [14] [24]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Armenia (Հայաստանի Հանրապետություն; Hayastani Hanrapetut’yun). [1] [12]
Common name
Armenia; Armenian-language name Hayastan. [1]
Continent / UN statistical region
Asia; the United Nations classifies Armenia in Western Asia. The South Caucasus is frequently also considered part of the wider European political and historical sphere. [1] [23]
Subregion
South Caucasus (Transcaucasia), between the Black Sea and Caspian Sea regions, without coastline on either sea. [5]
Capital
Yerevan (Երևան). [1]
Largest city
Yerevan. [1]
Official language
Armenian, written in the Armenian alphabet. [7] [12]
Currency
Armenian dram (AMD). [1]
International country code
AM (ISO 3166-1 alpha-2); ARM (alpha-3). [1]
Country calling code
+374.
Time zone
Armenia Time (AMT), UTC+4; Armenia does not currently observe seasonal clock changes.
National flag
Horizontal tricolor: red, blue and orange.
First Republic
The independent First Republic of Armenia existed from 1918 to 1920, following the collapse of the Russian Empire. [29]
First Republic commemoration
28 May is observed as Republic Day, associated with independence in 1918. [29]
Soviet period
The Armenian Soviet Socialist Republic emerged through Soviet rule in the early twentieth century; it remained within the Soviet Union until 1991. [29]
Modern independence
21 September 1991 independence referendum; the modern independent republic emerged during the dissolution of the Soviet Union. [29]
Independence Day
21 September. [29]
United Nations admission
2 March 1992. [1]
National identity
Armenian-language literary, church, architectural and diaspora traditions have helped sustain national identity across centuries of imperial rule and migration. [29] [25]
Historical Christian state tradition
Armenia’s adoption of Christianity as a state religion is traditionally dated to AD 301; the precise chronology is a historical subject, not a modern census fact. [28]
Armenian alphabet
Traditionally credited to Mesrop Mashtots in the early fifth century, enabling an extensive literary and religious tradition. [25]
Diaspora
Significant Armenian diaspora communities exist in Russia, Europe, North America and the Middle East; individual estimates depend on ancestry and self-identification. [29]
Historical geography distinction
Historical Armenian cultural regions extend beyond the territory of today’s Republic of Armenia; this country profile describes the internationally recognized present-day state. [5] [29]

Geography and Population

Geographic setting
Mountainous, landlocked highland state in the Lesser Caucasus and Armenian Highland. [5] [6]
Surface area
29,743 km² (11,484 square miles), government and UN country-profile convention. [5] [1]
Coastline
0 km; landlocked. [5]
Northern neighbor
Georgia. [5]
Eastern neighbor
Azerbaijan. [5]
Southern neighbor
Iran; Armenia also borders Azerbaijan’s Nakhchivan exclave to the southwest. [5]
Western neighbor
Türkiye (Turkey). [5]
Capital region
Yerevan, which is a separate administrative unit from the ten marzes (provinces). [6]
Administrative subdivisions
Ten provinces (marzer) plus Yerevan, which has special capital-city status. [6]
Highest point
Mount Aragats, approximately 4,090 m (13,419 ft), within the Republic of Armenia. [5]
Mount Ararat distinction
The iconic Mount Ararat, strongly associated with Armenian identity and prominent from Armenia, lies across the modern border in Türkiye; it is not Armenia’s highest national point. [5]
Average elevation
Approximately 1,800 m above sea level. [5]
Major lake
Lake Sevan, a large high-altitude freshwater lake central to water supply, fisheries and tourism. [6]
Lake Sevan surface area
Approximately 1,271 km² in a 31 December 2010 government reference; the lake’s area and level vary over time. [6]
Important river systems
Aras (Araks), Akhuryan, Hrazdan, Debed and Vorotan rivers. [6]
Mountain landscapes
Volcanic massifs, folded ranges, highland grasslands, rugged valleys and intermontane basins. [6]
Climate
Marked elevation-driven variation: continental conditions in many lowland valleys and cool-to-alpine conditions at higher elevations. [6]
Earthquake exposure
Active regional tectonics create substantial seismic risk; the destructive Spitak earthquake occurred in December 1988.
Water management
Lake Sevan, river basins, irrigation reservoirs and hydropower infrastructure are strategically important. [6]
Natural resources
Copper, molybdenum, gold and other metallic ores; tufa, basalt and other construction stone. [5]
2022 census: permanent population
2,932,731 persons, as reported by Armenia’s Statistical Committee; a census definition, not a 2025 estimate. [4]
2022 census: present population
2,689,438 persons present at enumeration, a different census count from permanent population. [4]
Population, World Bank 2025
3,086,700 persons, 2025 modeled/compiled population series, not a new census enumeration. [2]
Population, UN 2025
Approximately 2,952,000 persons in the UNdata country-profile 2025 projection. Method differs from the World Bank’s current series. [1] [2]
Yerevan population
Approximately 1.083 million shown in the UNdata country profile’s 2025 capital population entry; reference-year footnotes apply. [1]
Urbanization
The 2022 census release reported 63.8% urban and 36.2% rural among the permanent population. [4]
Population trends
Recent totals reflect natural change, international migration, and the influx of displaced persons from Nagorno-Karabakh in 2023. [2] [26]
2023 displacement context
UNHCR cites an influx of roughly 115,000 persons to Armenia following the September 2023 Nagorno-Karabakh crisis. [26]
Population comparison caution
The 2022 census count, UN 2025 projection, and World Bank 2025 estimate reflect different dates, definitions and source methodologies. [4] [1] [2]

People and Society

Majority ethnic identity
Ethnic Armenians form the large majority of the population; government demographic materials report roughly 98.1% in their published breakdown, which should be read with the source’s census vintage. [7]
Minority communities
Yezidis, Russians, Assyrians, Kurds, Greeks, Ukrainians and other communities. [7] [28]
Official language
Armenian; Eastern Armenian is the predominant standard in the Republic of Armenia. [7]
Writing system
Distinctive Armenian script, historically associated with Mesrop Mashtots. [25]
Other languages
Russian and English are widely used in some educational, commercial or cross-border settings; minority community languages also persist. [7]
Religious history
Armenia is renowned for an early state Christian tradition and medieval Armenian church architecture. [28] [24]
Major church tradition
Armenian Apostolic Church, headquartered ecclesiastically at the Mother See of Holy Etchmiadzin. [7] [24]
Other faith communities
Armenian Catholic, evangelical and other Christian communities as well as Yezidi, Jewish, Muslim and other religious minorities. [28]
Religion-data caveat
Specific percentages quoted from religious or older census sources are not automatically current measures of attendance, belief or adherence. [28]
Life expectancy
About 78 years at birth, World Bank 2024 estimate. [2]
Education and literacy tradition
Armenian-language schooling, universities and cultural institutions play a central role; a precise current literacy percentage should be sourced to a dated statistical measure. [7] [25]
Leading university
Yerevan State University, founded in the early independence period after World War I (1919).
Armenian cultural heritage
Manuscript illumination, stone carving, architectural monuments, music, poetry and literature remain significant traditions. [25] [24]
Khachkars
Carved Armenian cross-stones, recognized as UNESCO intangible cultural heritage in 2010 for their artistic traditions and symbolism. [25]
Duduk
Traditional woodwind instrument and associated performance tradition recognized by UNESCO. [25]
Kochari
Traditional group dance recognized by UNESCO in 2017. [25]
Lavash
Thin traditional bread and its cultural preparation, recognized by UNESCO in 2014. [25]
Armenian letter art
Armenian letter art and associated cultural expressions inscribed by UNESCO in 2019. [25]
Cuisine
Lavash, grilled meats, grain dishes, herbs, cheeses, dried fruits and regional preparations reflect the highland and diaspora culinary traditions. [25]
Sports and recreation
Wrestling, weightlifting, chess, football and outdoor mountain recreation have active communities.
Public health and development
Continued investment in healthcare access, skills and social services is important amid population shifts and regional economic adjustment. [10]
2023 refugees and integration
The September 2023 displacement from Nagorno-Karabakh created additional housing, employment, schooling and social-service needs. [26] [10]
Refugee stocks, 2025
UNHCR’s 2025 figures report about 138,054 refugees in Armenia, a stock that is not identical to the estimated 115,000 arrivals in September 2023. [27] [26]
Historical memory
Collective memory of the 1915 Armenian genocide, the First Republic, the Soviet era and later conflicts is prominent in civic and diaspora life. [29]
Social statistics method
Religious affiliation, ethnicity and diaspora figures may use different census, survey or administrative baselines; avoid combining them into a single contemporary headcount. [7] [28] [4]

United Nations membership
2 March 1992. [1]
WTO membership
5 February 2003. [9]
Council of Europe membership
25 January 2001. [23]
Eurasian Economic Union relationship
Armenia participates in the Eurasian Economic Union and retains regional economic integration alongside deepening EU cooperation. [21] [10]
European Union status
Not an EU member state; cooperation is governed in part by the Comprehensive and Enhanced Partnership Agreement (CEPA). [21]
EU–Armenia CEPA
Fully in force from 1 March 2021. [21]
EU–Armenia strategic agenda
Adopted in December 2025 to deepen the partnership in security, connectivity, economic diversification and reform. [21]
Armenia–Azerbaijan peace process
On 8 August 2025 the foreign ministers initialed a draft peace agreement at the Washington summit; initialing is distinct from final signature, ratification or entry into force. [17] [18]
August 2026 trilateral statement
Armenia, Azerbaijan and the United States commemorated the August 2025 summit, reaffirming the distinction between the signed joint declaration and initialed peace agreement. [18]
Border delimitation process
Bilateral border-commission regulations signed on 30 August 2024 entered into force on 1 November 2024. [17]
Nagorno-Karabakh context
Following Azerbaijan’s September 2023 military operation and the subsequent exodus, large numbers of ethnic Armenians fled to Armenia; Nagorno-Karabakh is not a current independent state recognized by the UN. [26] [18]
Türkiye diplomatic context
Türkiye recognized Armenia’s independence but the two states had not established full diplomatic relations as described by Armenia’s MFA in its bilateral overview. [19]
Türkiye land-border context
The ordinary Armenia–Türkiye land border remained largely closed to regular transit as of September 2026; normalization and operational-readiness talks continued. [19] [20]
Georgia relationship
Georgia provides an important northern transport connection to Black Sea ports and external markets. [5] [10]
Iran relationship
Iran is Armenia’s southern neighbor and an important commercial and regional connectivity partner. [5] [8]
Russian Federation relationship
Russia remains a major merchandise-trade partner and source of economic linkages, although Armenia has pursued diversification. [8] [10]
International transport
Mountain geography and constrained borders make routes via Georgia and Iran strategically significant. [5] [10]
UNESCO World Heritage Convention
Armenia’s succession to the World Heritage Convention is dated 5 September 1993. [24]

Sources and Methodology

Editorial cutoff: November 6, 2026 (Pacific Time). Population data include the 2022 Armenian census, 2025 World Bank series and the UN’s 2025 projection; all use distinct definitions. Economic figures use 2025 World Bank observations, April 2026 IMF forecasts for 2026, 2024 WTO/UN Comtrade merchandise-trade records, and EU 2025 bilateral goods-trade data. Political data reflect the June 2026 parliamentary election and August 2026 government/assembly appointments.

Territorial and treaty terminology: Armenia is described by its current internationally recognized boundaries, not the extent of historical Armenian cultural regions. Armenia and Azerbaijan initialed a draft peace agreement in Washington on August 8, 2025, while their leaders signed a separate joint declaration. Initialing should not be misrepresented as completed signature or ratification of the treaty. Discussions of displaced persons and Armenia–Türkiye normalization distinguish events and their observation dates.

Trade and heritage classifications: The WTO merchandise figures record gross goods trade (including the exceptionally large 2024 precious-metal flows), not exclusively domestically produced output. The three UNESCO World Heritage inscriptions are separate from Armenia’s intangible heritage elements and tentative sites. Percentages from different data sources or years are not directly interchangeable.

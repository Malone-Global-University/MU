Country Overview

The Republic of Azerbaijan occupies the eastern South Caucasus on the western shore of the Caspian Sea. Its capital, Baku, is a major energy and transportation center. The country is classified as Western Asia in United Nations statistics and participates in European and Islamic regional institutions. Oil and natural gas dominate merchandise exports, while the state pursues economic diversification. Azerbaijan’s contemporary international profile also reflects the Nagorno-Karabakh conflicts, the 2025 Washington joint declaration with Armenia and five UNESCO World Heritage properties. Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Azerbaijan (Azərbaycan Respublikası). [1] [6]
Common name
Azerbaijan (Azərbaycan). [1]
Geographic classification
South Caucasus; the United Nations classifies the country statistically in Western Asia. Membership in European institutions is a separate political classification. [1] [29]
Capital
Baku (Bakı). [1]
Largest city
Baku, a major Caspian seaport and metropolitan center. [1] [5]
Official language
Azerbaijani (Azeri), a Turkic language. [6]
Writing system
Modern standard Azerbaijani uses the Latin alphabet in Azerbaijan. [6]
Currency
Azerbaijani manat (AZN). [1]
ISO codes
AZ (alpha-2); AZE (alpha-3). [1]
Country calling code
+994.
Time zone
Azerbaijan Time (AZT), UTC+4; no regular daylight-saving clock change.
National flag
Three horizontal bands of blue, red and green, with a white crescent and eight-pointed star centered on the red band.
National motto or nickname
Frequently described as the “Land of Fire,” reflecting historical fire-worship traditions and natural gas phenomena; this is a popular characterization, not a formal constitutional motto. [19]
Historic cultural setting
Caucasian, Iranian, Turkic, Islamic and Caspian maritime traditions have intersected in the territory over many centuries. [19] [20]
Early twentieth-century state
Azerbaijan Democratic Republic, proclaimed 28 May 1918; independent government ended with Soviet takeover in 1920. [31]
Soviet period
Azerbaijan became part of the Soviet political system and later the Soviet Union until its dissolution in 1991. [31]
Restoration of independence
18 October 1991, following the breakup of the Soviet Union. [31]
Independence Day observance
28 May, commemorating the proclamation of the 1918 republic. [31]
Restoration of Independence Day
18 October, marking the 1991 restoration of state independence. [31]
United Nations admission
2 March 1992. [1] [31]
Legal structure
Sovereign, unitary state with a presidential constitutional system; Nakhchivan has a constitutionally recognized autonomous status. [6]
Caspian identity
The main territory lies along the western Caspian Sea, an inland body of water despite its traditional “sea” name. [5]
International affiliation
UN member; also a Council of Europe and Organization of Islamic Cooperation member. [1] [29] [30]
Border and postwar context
The 2020 and 2023 conflicts fundamentally altered control of Nagorno-Karabakh; its geography and displacement history warrant separate discussion from Armenia’s territory. [23] [27]
Editorial reference date
6 November 2026; institutional and economic figures are dated to their source periods. [2] [11] [24]

Geography and Population

Total area
86,600 km² (about 33,436 sq mi). [5] [1]
Location
Eastern South Caucasus, where the Greater and Lesser Caucasus systems meet the Caspian lowlands. [5]
Sea access
Western coast of the Caspian Sea; the Caspian is an enclosed inland basin, not an ocean. [5]
Land neighbors
Russia, Georgia, Armenia, Iran and Türkiye (the Turkish border is via Nakhchivan). [5]
Eastern boundary
Caspian Sea coastline and adjacent offshore hydrocarbon fields. [5] [14]
Western boundary
Armenia and Georgia, with sections of historically disputed or recently delimited border. [5] [25]
Northern boundary
Russian Federation and the Greater Caucasus mountains. [5]
Southern boundary
Iran; the Nakhchivan exclave also borders Türkiye. [5]
Autonomous exclave
Nakhchivan Autonomous Republic is geographically separated from mainland Azerbaijan by Armenia. [6] [5]
Highest point
Mount Bazardüzü, approximately 4,466 m above sea level. [5]
Major mountain ranges
Greater Caucasus in the north and Lesser Caucasus in the west and southwest. [5]
Major lowlands
Kura–Aras Lowland and adjacent Caspian plains. [5]
Principal rivers
Kura (Kür) and Araz (Aras), vital to water supply, irrigation and downstream ecosystems. [5]
Principal reservoir
Mingachevir Reservoir on the Kura River is an important water and hydropower installation. [5]
Climate patterns
Ranges from semi-arid Caspian and lowland zones to humid southeastern districts and cool mountain climates. [5] [17]
Natural ecosystems
Caspian coastal habitats, steppe and semi-desert, forests and alpine landscapes. [5] [19]
Caspian environmental concerns
Coastal ecosystem pressures, fluctuating water levels and hydrocarbon-related industrial risks. [17] [15]

10,246,996; estimated annual-series population. [2]
Official population — 1 July 2026
10,273,450, State Statistical Committee administrative estimate released 19 August 2026. [4]
Population — UN projection, 2025
Approximately 10,398,000 in UNdata; based on a different population model from national and World Bank series. [1]

Increase of 11,099 people, approximately 0.1% from the start of 2026 to 1 July 2026. [4]
Urban and rural population
Official July 2026 reporting places roughly 54% in urban settings and 46% in rural communities; proportions are date-dependent. [4]
Major metropolitan center
Baku and its surrounding Abşeron peninsula; the capital anchors finance, industry and transport. [1] [5]
Other important cities
Ganja, Sumgayit, Lankaran, Sheki, Mingachevir, Nakhchivan, Quba and Shamakhi. [5]

People and Society

Principal people
Ethnic Azerbaijanis form the majority; population identity and minority totals must be tied to the reporting census date. [1] [21]
Ethnic minority communities
Lezgins, Talysh, Avars, Russians, Tats, Udis, Georgians, Jews and other communities live in the republic. [21] [22]
Official language
Azerbaijani, a Turkic language; significant regional and minority languages persist. [6]
Language family
Azerbaijani is part of the Oghuz branch of Turkic, related to modern Turkish.
Writing tradition
Latin script is used for contemporary official Azerbaijani; Arabic and Cyrillic scripts were important in earlier periods.
Major religion
Islam is the religion of the majority; Shi’a and Sunni communities both exist. [21]
Religious denomination caution
Estimates of Shi’a/Sunni ratios are not equivalent to a recent national census of religious observance. [21]
Secular constitutional system
Azerbaijan is constitutionally secular, despite its substantial Muslim-majority population. [6] [21]
Other religious groups
Jewish, Russian Orthodox, Georgian Orthodox, other Christian, Baha’i and non-religious communities. [21]
Religious freedom concerns
USCIRF reports state registration rules and restrictions affecting religious organizations and practice. [21]
Population trend
Modest population growth in recent official releases; urban and regional patterns vary significantly. [4]
Education and language
State and private schools teach the official language alongside foreign languages and selected minority-language programs. [6]
Cultural crossroads
The country’s music, cuisine, built environment and literature carry Turkic, Caucasian, Persian and Russian/Soviet influences. [20] [19]
Mugham
Azerbaijani classical music tradition inscribed on the UNESCO intangible heritage list. [20]
Ashiq performance
A poetic-musical art involving performers using the saz and oral storytelling. [20]
Traditional carpets
Azerbaijani carpet weaving inscribed by UNESCO in 2010. [20]
Kelaghayi
Symbolic silk headscarf-making and wearing tradition inscribed by UNESCO. [20]
Novruz
Spring festival and associated practices, included among multinational UNESCO intangible heritage inscriptions. [20]
Traditional food culture
Dolma, pilaf, regional breads, kebabs, tea and pomegranate-related traditions are well known. [20]
Traditional sports and recreation
Wrestling, chess, football, equestrian traditions and outdoor recreation. [20]
Urban cultural center
Baku contains historic architecture, concert venues, universities and major civic institutions. [19]
Regional cultural center
Sheki is noted for historic architecture and craftsmanship; its historic center is UNESCO-listed. [19]
Karabakh displacement after 2023
Following Azerbaijan’s September 2023 military operation, approximately 115,000 ethnic Armenians arrived in Armenia; postwar return, property and cultural rights remain sensitive issues. [27] [22]
Political and civil liberties
Freedom House’s independent 2026 assessment classifies Azerbaijan as “Not Free,” citing restrictions on opposition politics, independent journalism and civic participation. [22]
Cultural continuity and change
Local and diasporic traditions coexist with a high-technology urban economy and a hydrocarbon-centered industrial sector. [20] [17]

Government and Institutions

System of government
Unitary presidential republic with a strong elected presidency; IPU describes the political system as presidential-parliamentary. [6] [11]
Foundational law
Constitution of the Republic of Azerbaijan (adopted 1995, amended subsequently). [6]
Head of state
President Ilham Aliyev, president since 2003 and reelected in 2024. [7]
Presidential election
7 February 2024; official presidency biography reports Ilham Aliyev’s reelection. [7]
Presidential term
Seven years under the constitution. [6]
Executive power
The constitution vests executive power in the president. [6]
Head of government
Prime Minister Ali Asadov; cabinet leadership is distinct from the constitutionally powerful presidency. [9]
First vice president
Mehriban Aliyeva, documented in the CIA’s May 2026 cabinet snapshot. [8]
National legislature
Unicameral Milli Majlis (National Assembly). [10] [11]
Legislature membership
125 members. [10] [11]
Legislative term
Five years under parliamentary rules. [10]
Speaker of the Milli Majlis
Sahiba Gafarova. [10]
Most recent parliamentary election
1 September 2024; early parliamentary election. [11]
Parliamentary majority
New Azerbaijan Party (YAP): 68 of 125 seats in the 2024 election. [11]
Independents in 2024 election
44 independent candidates won seats, according to IPU. [11]
2024 legislative turnout
Approximately 37.2% of registered voters. [11]
Parties represented
IPU reports parliamentary seats across eleven parties, with additional independents. [11]
Women in parliament
The parliament’s official profile identifies 26 women among 125 MPs of the seventh convocation; consult current rolls for later changes. [10]
Electoral assessment
International observers and civil society sources have raised concerns about competitiveness, political pluralism and the environment for independent journalism. [22] [11]
Judicial system
Constitutional and ordinary courts operate under the republic’s legal system; independence and fair-trial safeguards are contested in outside assessments. [6] [22]
Local administration
Centralized national government with districts, municipalities and the constitutionally recognized Nakhchivan Autonomous Republic. [6]
Religious and state relationship
Constitutionally secular; religious communities face government registration and oversight requirements. [6] [21]
Political rights benchmark — 2026
Freedom House rates Azerbaijan 6/100 (“Not Free”) for political rights and civil liberties combined; this is an external institutional assessment, not an official state designation. [22]
Government data date
Leadership and parliamentary statistics reflect officials or official listings accessible as of 6 November 2026. [9] [10] [7]

International Profile and Heritage

UN membership
Admitted 2 March 1992. [31] [1]
Council of Europe membership
Since 25 January 2001; institutional membership does not alter UN geographic classification. [29] [1]
Organization of Islamic Cooperation
Member since 1992. [30]
WTO status
Not a WTO member as shown in the WTO tariff profile; customs statistics appearing in WTO databases do not imply accession. [12]
European Union membership
Not an EU member state; participates in regional cooperation and energy partnerships. [16]
EU energy partnership
The EU and Azerbaijan maintain a strategic energy cooperation framework, including the Southern Gas Corridor. [15] [16]
Southern Gas Corridor
Cross-border infrastructure carries Caspian gas toward Türkiye and European consumers. [15]
EU-linked 2025 gas deliveries
The European Commission reported 12.5 bcm supplied to EU member states by Azerbaijan and SOCAR in 2025. [32]
COP29 host
Baku hosted the 29th UN Framework Convention on Climate Change Conference of the Parties in November 2024. [28]
UNESCO World Heritage Convention
Ratified 16 December 1993. [19]
UNESCO World Heritage properties
Five inscribed properties: four cultural and one natural (transnational). [19]
Walled City of Baku
Walled City of Baku with the Shirvanshah’s Palace and Maiden Tower, inscribed 2000. [19]
Gobustan
Gobustan Rock Art Cultural Landscape, inscribed 2007. [19]
Sheki
Historic Centre of Sheki with the Khan’s Palace, inscribed 2019. [19]
Khinalig transhumance landscape
Cultural Landscape of Khinalig People and “Köç Yolu” Transhumance Route, inscribed 2023. [19]
Hyrcanian Forests
Transnational natural World Heritage property, extended to Azerbaijan in 2023. [19]
UNESCO intangible heritage
UNESCO’s Azerbaijan country list includes mugham, carpet weaving, ashiq art, kelaghayi, Yalli, Novruz and other elements; these are separate from World Heritage properties. [20]
Nakhchivan / Armenia transit
The exclave’s separation from Azerbaijan’s mainland has made regional transport links a significant diplomatic and security issue. [5] [23]
Azerbaijan–Armenia peace document
On 8 August 2025, the two foreign ministers initialed a draft peace agreement in Washington; the heads of government/state separately signed a joint declaration. Initialing is not treaty ratification. [23] [24] [25]
August 2026 diplomatic status
A trilateral anniversary statement referred to the 2025 joint declaration and initialed treaty draft; the statement itself did not establish that the peace treaty had completed ratification. [24]
Border delimitation
Bilateral regulations signed 30 August 2024 entered into force 1 November 2024, according to Armenia’s MFA. [25]
Diplomatic relations with Armenia
Armenia’s dated MFA profile states formal bilateral diplomatic relations had not yet been established; this is a source-dated status, not a prediction. [25]
OSCE Minsk Process
Formally closed at the end of November 2025 following the parties’ joint request and an OSCE decision. [26]
Nagorno-Karabakh control and displacement
Azerbaijan established full control of Nagorno-Karabakh after the September 2023 offensive; a mass exodus of ethnic Armenians to Armenia followed. [27] [22]
Human-rights and rule-of-law scrutiny
Freedom House and USCIRF present external assessments of civil liberties and religious freedom that should be distinguished from government characterizations. [22] [21]
Transregional diplomacy
Relations with Türkiye, Georgia, Russia, Iran and the EU are affected by energy infrastructure, border corridors and regional security. [5] [15] [23]
Heritage list caveat
UNESCO’s 12 tentative World Heritage candidates are not additional inscribed properties. [19]

Sources and Methodology

Editorial cutoff: November 6, 2026 (Pacific Time). Unless otherwise indicated, 2025 economic values come from the World Bank, 2025 goods-trade shares and values from WTO/UN Comtrade, 2025 energy volumes from the Ministry of Energy, and 2026 administrative population from the State Statistical Committee. IMF 2026 figures are forecasts, not realized calendar-year outcomes.

Population and trade: United Nations population projections, World Bank population estimates and nationally registered population differ in statistical modeling, date and reference populations. Goods values, energy volumes, GDP, sectoral output and EU gas deliveries use different metrics. The EU is a bloc in WTO destination/origin tables, and shares may not sum exactly because of rounding.

Political, human-rights and territorial issues: Government listings and constitutional language record formal offices and legal provisions. Independent civil-liberties assessments are attributed to their publishers, rather than labeled official government findings. Azerbaijan’s full control over Nagorno-Karabakh since September 2023 is distinct from its population history and mass displacement of ethnic Armenians. The Washington joint declaration of August 8, 2025 was signed; the separate draft peace treaty was initialed by foreign ministers. The OSCE Minsk Process was closed in late 2025. Avoid equating initialing with a fully ratified or in-force bilateral treaty.

Heritage: UNESCO’s five World Heritage properties are separate from intangible cultural elements and tentative World Heritage nominations. Heritage claims are grounded in UNESCO’s own state-party lists.

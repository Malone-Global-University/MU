Country Overview

The Republic of the Philippines is an archipelagic Southeast Asian country and constitutional democratic republic. Its more than seven thousand islands link the western Pacific, the Philippine Sea, the Sulu Sea and the South China Sea. The economy combines manufacturing, business-process services, domestic consumption, remittances, maritime trade and agriculture. This profile distinguishes the Philippine Statistics Authority’s July 2024 population census from the World Bank’s 2025 demographic model and uses official full-year 2025 merchandise-trade totals.

Data and status note: The 2024 Philippine census counted 112,729,484 people as of July 1, 2024; the World Bank estimates 116,786,962 for 2025. These are different reference dates and compilation methods. The World Bank reports 2025 nominal GDP of US$487.09 billion and real growth of 4.4%, consistent with the April 2026 PSA revisions. The Philippine Statistics Authority recorded 2025 goods exports of US$84.48 billion and imports of US$134.20 billion; these figures exclude most cross-border services. Territorial discussions involving the South China Sea, West Philippine Sea, Sabah, and other claims do not imply settled sovereignty over disputed waters.

Sources and Methodology

National statistical figures are attributed by census date or annual estimation system. The July 1, 2024 PSA census counts 112,729,484 while the World Bank 2025 estimate is 116,786,962; one is not a correction of the other. PSA revised full-year 2025 real GDP growth to 4.4%. Merchandise exports (US$84.48bn) and imports (US$134.20bn) cover goods, not digital services and other cross-border services. Government details reflect the 2022 presidential election and 2025 midterm cycle. Geographic and maritime claims, notably in the South China Sea, are described without assuming international agreement on contested sovereignty.

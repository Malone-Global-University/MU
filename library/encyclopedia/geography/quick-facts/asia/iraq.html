Country Overview

Iraq occupies the middle reaches and lower basin of the Tigris and Euphrates in Western Asia, linking ancient Mesopotamian urban civilizations with modern Arab, Kurdish and other communities. It is a federal parliamentary republic whose post-2003 institutions operate alongside the constitutionally recognized Kurdistan Region. Petroleum dominates merchandise exports and public revenue, while water scarcity, governance, diversification and post-conflict recovery remain major challenges. Its archaeological heritage includes the sites of Babylon, Ashur, Hatra and Samarra.

Sources and Methodology

The final 2024 population census is a counted total, not a 2025 projection. World Bank estimates, national results and UNCTAD trade estimates have distinct compilation rules. UNCTAD Iraq goods values for 2024 are explicitly estimated and may differ from Iraq’s non-oil customs statistics and OPEC petroleum export statistics. IPU/UN election results refer to the November 2025 voting and not to changing legislative alliances. The Kurdistan Region’s constitutional autonomy and the federal status of Halabja are kept distinct. UNESCO physical properties, tentative sites and intangible heritage are separately defined.

Editorial cutoff: November 6, 2026 (Pacific Time). All stated population counts, prices, elections, and legal statuses have specific reference dates or qualifications. This page should be updated following major new statistical releases or changes of government.

Country Overview

Saudi Arabia occupies much of the Arabian Peninsula and is the largest economy in the Arab world by nominal GDP. The kingdom is an absolute hereditary monarchy governed by the House of Saud, and it holds special religious significance as the home of Makkah and Madinah. Petroleum remains central to public revenue and exports, while Vision 2030 seeks investment, tourism, industrial development and wider private-sector employment. This profile distinguishes the 2022 census from later estimates, separates domestic currency trade from US-dollar World Bank output, and addresses economic transformation together with continuing human-rights and labor-policy debates.

Data and status note: The 2022 census enumerated 32,175,224 residents; GASTAT estimated 35,300,280 residents for mid-2024, while the World Bank series reports 36,973,555 for 2025. These figures use distinct reference dates and should not be labeled interchangeable census totals. The World Bank reports 2025 GDP of US$1,276.94 billion; GASTAT reports current-price 2025 GDP of SAR 4,789 billion. The kingdom has no elected national legislative chamber: the appointed Shura Council is consultative. UNESCO currently lists eight inscribed properties, not the 14 properties on the tentative list.

Sources and Methodology

This profile uses the Saudi 2022 census as an enumeration and the GASTAT 2023–2024 and World Bank 2025 series as population estimates. World Bank 2025 GDP in US dollars and GASTAT 2025 GDP in riyals are separately reported, as are 2024 WTO trade flows and 2025 national trade flows. Government facts distinguish appointed Shura Council consultation from elected legislative representation. The UNESCO World Heritage list is eight properties (7 cultural, 1 natural); tentative sites and intangible inscriptions are not included. Government and trade statements have an editorial cutoff of November 6, 2026.

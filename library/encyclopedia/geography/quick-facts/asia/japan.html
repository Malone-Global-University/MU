Country Overview

Japan is an island country in East Asia with a constitutional monarchy and a parliamentary cabinet system. Its mountainous archipelago extends from Hokkaido through Honshu, Shikoku and Kyushu to the Ryukyu and other island chains. Japan is a major manufacturing, technology and services economy while facing population decline, rapid aging, energy-import exposure, and earthquake and typhoon hazards. The February 2026 lower-house election confirmed Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi’s leadership; a September 2026 statistical release revised the count from the 2025 population census.

Geography & Population

Geographic setting
Island archipelago along the Pacific margin of eastern Asia. [1][17]
Territorial area
Approximately 378,000 square kilometres; exact total varies with mapping conventions. [1]
Major islands
Honshu, Hokkaido, Kyushu and Shikoku, plus many smaller islands. [17]
Ryukyu Islands
Southwestern island chain extends toward Taiwan and includes Okinawa. [17]
Capital metropolis
Tokyo metropolitan prefecture and the surrounding Kanto urban region. [5]
Northernmost main island
Hokkaido, with cold winters and substantial snow. [17]
Largest main island
Honshu, containing Tokyo, Osaka, Nagoya and much of the population. [5]
Important inland sea
Seto Inland Sea lies between Honshu, Shikoku and Kyushu. [17]
Pacific frontage
Extensive eastern and southern coastline faces the Pacific Ocean. [17]
Sea of Japan frontage
Western parts of the archipelago face the Sea of Japan. [17]
Highest mountain
Mount Fuji, an active volcano reaching roughly 3,776 metres. [13][18]
Major ranges
Japanese Alps and other ranges divide densely settled plains. [17]
Volcanic setting
Japan lies near multiple converging plate boundaries and volcanic arcs. [18]
Earthquake exposure
Destructive earthquakes and tsunamis are principal natural hazards. [18][22]
Typhoon exposure
Summer and autumn tropical cyclones can bring wind, flooding and landslides. [17][22]
Climate gradient
Conditions range from cool temperate northern zones to subtropical Okinawa. [17]
Sea of Japan snowbelt
Winter monsoon conditions bring heavy snowfall to many western uplands. [17]
Rainy season
Baiu early-summer rains influence much of the central and southern archipelago. [17]
Population estimate
123,366,734 people in the World Bank 2025 series. [2]
Final 2025 census
About 122.9 million residents on October 1, 2025, released September 29, 2026. [3][4]
Preliminary 2025 census
Earlier provisional count was 123.05 million; superseded by final basic results. [3]
October 2026 estimate
Approximately 122.69 million in the Statistics Bureau provisional series. [5]

Declining overall; measured total fell roughly 2.5 percent between the 2020 and 2025 censuses. [3][4]
Population aging
People aged 65 and above constituted about 29.4 percent of the finalized 2025 census population. [4]
Household size
Final 2025 census put mean household size near 2.1 people. [4]
Population concentration
Kanto, Kansai and Chukyo metropolitan corridors contain large urban populations. [5]
Tokyo prefecture
The largest prefectural population among 2025 census preliminary tabulations. [3]
Freshwater setting
Rivers tend to be short and steep because of mountainous terrain. [17]
Forests
Extensive forested slopes coexist with intensive development in coastal plains. [17]
Agricultural geography
Paddy agriculture and field crops concentrate in suitable plains and valleys. [19]
Environmental pressures
Heat, extreme precipitation, landslides and coastal hazards interact with urban exposure. [17][22]
Protected landscapes
National parks and UNESCO natural sites preserve diverse island and mountain ecosystems. [13]

Japanese, including regional dialects and cultural varieties. [23]

National, public and private universities support broad research and professional training. [23][16]

Health system

Constitution in force since May 3, 1947. [9]
Sovereign power
Vested in the people, with representative institutions exercising public authority. [9]
Emperor
Naruhito is the constitutional symbol of the state, not an elected executive. [9][10]

Sanae Takaichi; designated by both Diet houses on February 18, 2026. [6][7]

Cabinet is collectively responsible to the Diet. [9]

Bicameral National Diet. [9]
Lower chamber
House of Representatives with 465 elected seats. [7]
Upper chamber

Constitution permits a maximum four-year term subject to earlier dissolution. [9]

Local governments

Civil liberties

Budget process

Treaty oversight

Pacifist article

Self-Defense Forces

Election administration

Political competition

Continuity of government

Sources and Methodology

The Statistics Bureau’s finalized 2025 census and subsequent provisional monthly population estimates are different releases; the World Bank international series uses its own harmonized methodology. WTO merchandise figures exclude services and differ from Japanese fiscal-year customs aggregates. The lower-house party tally records election-night seats and an additional later affiliation separately. World Heritage classifications refer to UNESCO’s 2026 inventory.

Editorial cutoff: November 6, 2026 (Pacific Time). Official data and international series have different observation dates, definitions, and publication lags.

Country Overview

Mexico, officially the United Mexican States, is a federal republic extending between the United States and Guatemala and Belize, with coasts on the Pacific Ocean, Gulf of Mexico and Caribbean Sea. Its territories contain the histories of numerous Indigenous peoples and civilizations, including Maya, Mexica and many others, followed by three centuries of Spanish colonial rule and the establishment of an independent state in 1821. The present constitutional framework dates to 1917, though its institutions have undergone significant revisions, including electoral and judicial changes. Mexico combines major manufacturing and service industries, natural-resource production, agriculture, world-renowned cultural heritage and extensive connections with the United States and Canada. This reference distinguishes international population estimates from the 2020 census, separates the stages of national independence, and reports customs-based goods trade separately from income, remittances and services.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
United Mexican States (Estados Unidos Mexicanos); the republic uses México in common Spanish-language references [8]
Common name and demonym
Mexico; Mexican (mexicano or mexicana); Spanish-language references generally accent México
Capital city
Mexico City (Ciudad de México), a constituent federal entity with special constitutional organization, not an ordinary state [8]
Region and continental classification
North America in MGU geography; Central America in the United Nations M49 statistical subregion, which is a statistical rather than a geological division [11]
Indigenous historical foundations
Maya, Olmec, Zapotec, Mixtec, Mexica and many other peoples developed independent cultural, economic and political systems long before Spanish conquest
Teotihuacan and Classic civilizations
Teotihuacan, major Maya centers and regional exchange systems produced urban and artistic traditions across ancient Mesoamerica [10]
Mexica political power
Tenochtitlan was the center of a major political and tributary system before Spanish-led forces captured it in 1521
Spanish conquest and alliances
Hernán Cortés and Indigenous allies defeated the Mexica capital in 1521 amid warfare, epidemic disease and changing regional alliances
New Spain
The viceroyalty created in the sixteenth century linked mining, ranching, religious institutions, coerced labor and transoceanic trading systems
Independence revolt
Miguel Hidalgo’s September 16, 1810 call to revolt initiated the independence struggle; it was not the date on which independence was legally consummated [15]
Independence consummated
September 27, 1821 marks the entry of the Army of the Three Guarantees into Mexico City following the Plan of Iguala and the Córdoba agreements [15]
First Mexican Empire
The 1822–1823 imperial experiment preceded the adoption of a federal republican constitutional structure
Federal Constitution of 1824
Established the early federal republic, subject to subsequent instability and centralist constitutional experiments [8]
War with the United States
The 1846–1848 war and the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo transformed the international boundary and transferred extensive territory
Reform and foreign intervention
La Reforma and French intervention reshaped the church-state relationship, constitutional politics and republican institutions in the nineteenth century
Porfiriato
Porfirio Díaz’s long rule brought railway investment and economic integration alongside political exclusion and severe inequalities
Mexican Revolution
The revolution beginning in 1910 overthrew the Díaz regime and generated conflicts over land, suffrage, state power and social rights
Constitution of 1917
Promulgated February 5, 1917; amended repeatedly, it remains Mexico’s present constitutional foundation [8]
Political opening
Late twentieth-century electoral reforms and the 2000 presidential alternation changed the long-dominant party system
First woman president
Claudia Sheinbaum Pardo took office October 1, 2024 as Mexico’s first woman president; she remained in office at the 2026 editorial cutoff [9]
National holiday
Independence Day, September 16, commemorates the 1810 insurgent opening rather than the 1821 entry into the capital
Official national symbols
Green-white-red national flag, national coat of arms bearing the eagle and serpent, and the national anthem Himno Nacional Mexicano
United Nations accession
Mexico became a member on November 7, 1945 [2]
ISO and web codes
MX (alpha-2), MEX (alpha-3), 484 (numeric); .mx country-code top-level domain [11]

Chronology and sovereignty: September 16, 1810 commemorates the beginning of insurgency; September 27, 1821 records the consummation of independence. The 1917 Constitution defines the continuing modern constitutional order; no one milestone substitutes for the others.

Geography and Population

Geographic position
Mexico connects the northern and Central American landmasses between Pacific and Atlantic-facing marine basins
Land borders
Shares its northern border with the United States and southern borders with Guatemala and Belize, encompassing highly varied mountain, river and lowland regions
Total surface area
1,964,375 km² in UN geographic statistics, including land and inland-water components; other publications may distinguish land-only area [2]
Pacific coastline
Pacific Ocean and Gulf of California shorelines include the Baja California peninsula and major western ports
Atlantic coastline
Gulf of Mexico and Caribbean coasts include tropical wetlands, fisheries, ports and major tourism regions
Principal mountain systems
Sierra Madre Occidental and Sierra Madre Oriental frame the Mexican Plateau, while the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt crosses the central country
Southern mountains
Sierra Madre del Sur and highlands of Chiapas create steep terrain, cultural regions and locally variable climates
Highest summit
Pico de Orizaba (Citlaltépetl), about 5,636 m, is the country’s highest volcano and mountain
Major river systems
Río Bravo/Rio Grande, Grijalva-Usumacinta, Balsas, Lerma-Santiago and Pánuco systems support distinct watersheds and cross-border issues
Largest lake
Lake Chapala is the largest natural freshwater lake by surface area, while reservoir and seasonal-water measures vary
Yucatán Peninsula
Limestone landscapes, cenotes, subterranean waters and archaeological sites characterize the southeastern peninsula
Baja California
Long peninsular geography, arid ecosystems and Pacific/Gulf marine environments support fisheries, tourism and border industries
Climate variation
Desert and semi-arid regions dominate much of the north; highlands vary with elevation; southern and coastal regions include tropical climates
Seismic exposure
Plate-boundary tectonics produce powerful earthquakes especially along Pacific margins; preparedness differs by city and construction type
Volcanic activity
Popocatépetl and other active volcanic systems require monitoring, land-use planning and hazard communication
Cyclone hazards
Both Pacific and Atlantic hurricane basins affect Mexico; exposure includes floods, landslides, storm surge and agricultural losses
Principal metropolitan regions
Mexico City metropolitan area, Guadalajara and Monterrey are among the largest; municipal and metropolitan boundaries must be distinguished
Federal geography
Thirty-one states plus Mexico City make thirty-two federal entities, each with its own constitutionally defined responsibilities [8]
2020 census population
126,014,024 people enumerated by INEGI; this is a direct census count, not the same series as the 2025 annual population estimate [4]
2025 World Bank population
131,946,900 residents in the World Bank annual international population series [1]
Annual population growth
Approximately 0.8% in the World Bank 2025 estimate, which may be revised as new demographic inputs emerge [1]
Approximate density
About 67 residents per km² when 2025 population is divided by the UN 1,964,375 km² total area; regional densities differ sharply [1] [2]
Urban and rural settlement
Large metropolitan corridors coexist with Indigenous communities, agricultural valleys, small towns and sparsely inhabited northern terrain
Water and environmental challenges
Groundwater overuse, urban water supply, forest change, air pollution, drought and coastal hazards remain major regional policy concerns
UNESCO World Heritage
Thirty-six inscribed properties at the 2026 cutoff, including the 2025 Wixárika Route through Sacred Sites to Wirikuta; tentative sites are excluded [10]

Geographic and census comparability: the 2020 INEGI count of 126.0 million is a census enumeration and the 2025 World Bank figure of 131.95 million is a modeled annual estimate. Calculated density uses UN total area and should not be confused with a land-only administrative density.

Economy and International Trade

Economic classification
Upper-middle-income, diversified manufacturing and services economy integrated with international trade and North American supply chains [1]
2025 nominal GDP
Approximately US$1.83 trillion in the World Bank annual current-dollar series; this is not a constant-price GDP measure [1]
2025 GDP per capita
US$13,889.2 in current U.S. dollars, an output-per-person statistic rather than average household income [1]
Real GDP growth
Approximately 0.6% in 2025 under the World Bank annual constant-price growth series [1]
Consumer-price inflation
Approximately 3.8% in the 2025 World Bank annual consumer-price series, distinct from any single month’s inflation rate [1]
ILO-modeled unemployment
Approximately 2.7% of the labor force in 2025; informal employment and labor participation demand separate interpretation [1]
Remittance receipts
Personal remittances were approximately 3.5% of GDP in 2025; money transfers are not domestic production or merchandise exports [1]
Currency and monetary authority
Mexican peso (MXN); Banco de México is the autonomous central bank responsible for monetary policy and banknote issue [12]
Federal fiscal authority
Federal taxation, expenditure and debt administration coexist with state and municipal budgets and distinct revenue powers
Industrial manufacturing
Automobiles, vehicles and parts, machinery, electrical equipment, medical products and electronics are major components of export-oriented production
Automotive industrial clusters
Northern and central regions host complex vehicle and component supply chains linked to the United States, Canada and global producers
Aerospace and medical devices
Selected high-value manufacturing industries produce components for international markets, often under cross-border contractual arrangements
Agriculture and food
Maize, vegetables, fruit, coffee, livestock, sugar and avocados support livelihoods, processing and foreign sales across different climates
Oil and gas
Petroleum, fuels and natural gas remain important but represent a smaller fraction of export receipts than manufactured goods [3]
Tourism services
Coastal resorts, archaeological landscapes, urban cultural destinations and ecotourism generate international service receipts not included in merchandise exports
Financial and digital sectors
Banking, telecommunications, software, logistics and business services contribute to national production and urban employment
2025 goods exports
US$664.837 billion in calendar-year 2025 according to INEGI’s January 27, 2026 merchandise trade release [3]
2025 goods imports
US$664.066 billion on the paired INEGI 2025 merchandise series [3]
2025 merchandise surplus
Approximately US$0.771 billion, calculated from the published rounded exports and imports; it excludes services and investment income [3]
2025 non-oil exports
US$643.592 billion within overall 2025 merchandise exports; this is a component, not an additional amount [3]
2025 petroleum exports
US$21.246 billion, down from US$28.860 billion in 2024 in the official customs-based report [3]
Export composition
Manufactured goods 91.6%; petroleum 3.2%; agricultural 3.1%; non-petroleum extractive 2.1% of 2025 merchandise exports [3]
Leading destination
The United States is Mexico’s dominant merchandise export market, particularly for integrated vehicle, appliance and electronics supply chains [19]
North American trade agreement
The USMCA/T-MEC entered into force July 1, 2020, replacing NAFTA, with rules for trade, investment and industrial origin [17]
Trade accounting
Merchandise figures are in U.S. dollars and customs methodology; they are not identical to balance-of-payments goods or services and cannot be added to GDP as separate production

People and Society

National language environment
Spanish predominates in nationwide public communication; Indigenous languages have legal recognition as national languages under national linguistic-rights law [7]
Indigenous language families
Náhuatl, Maya languages, Mixtec, Zapotec, Otomí and many other language groups reflect distinct regions and histories [7]
2020 Indigenous-language speakers
INEGI counted 7,364,645 residents aged three or older speaking an Indigenous language at the 2020 census [7]
2023 Indigenous self-identification
INEGI’s ENADID 2023 estimated 39.2 million people who identified as Indigenous, using a different questionnaire and definition from speaking an Indigenous language [6]
Population diversity
Indigenous, European-descended, Afro-Mexican, mixed-heritage, Asian-origin and other communities are historically diverse and not captured by any single identity label
Afro-Mexican identification
Afro-Mexican and Afro-descendant identity appears in national census reporting; cultural and geographic histories differ from Indigenous-language categories [4]
2020 Catholic religious identification
77.7% of total census population declared Catholic affiliation; this measure concerns self-reported identity, not church attendance [5]
2020 Protestant and evangelical affiliation
Approximately 11.2% of census respondents identified as Protestant or evangelical Christian [5]
2020 non-affiliation
About 8.1% reported no religion and a further 2.5% identified as believers without formal religious affiliation; categories should not be merged uncritically [5]
Other religious communities
Jewish, Muslim, Orthodox, Buddhist, Indigenous spiritual and other traditions exist alongside Christianity and secular beliefs
Demographic change
Fertility decline, longer life expectancy, mobility and migration have changed the age profile; 2020 census median age was 29 years [4]
Education structure
Basic education is provided through federal and state systems; higher education includes public autonomous universities, technological institutions and private colleges
National universities
Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México (UNAM), Instituto Politécnico Nacional and many state universities support research and professional education
Healthcare system
Public social-security services, federal and state health institutions and private providers create coverage differences across employment and place
Regional inequality
Metropolitan incomes and infrastructure coexist with pronounced disadvantage in some Indigenous, rural and southern communities
Cultural heritage
Literature, cinema, muralism, architecture, music, ceramics, culinary traditions and multiple Indigenous cultural systems contribute to national life
Food cultures
Regional maize-based foods, sauces, chilies, beans, cacao and locally distinctive cuisines reflect Indigenous, colonial and modern exchanges
Popular culture
Ranchera, son, mariachi, norteño and modern popular genres coexist with numerous Indigenous and transnational traditions
Sports and recreation
Football is widely followed; baseball, boxing, basketball and Indigenous sporting traditions have varied regional prominence
Migration and diaspora
Mobility connects Mexico to the United States and elsewhere, while inward migration and transit also shape border and humanitarian policy

Language and identity: speaking an Indigenous language is not equivalent to self-identifying as Indigenous. The 2020 census and 2023 ENADID use different questions, ages and reference populations. Religion percentages measure census affiliation, not worship frequency or 2026 beliefs.

Government and Institutions

State form
Federal presidential constitutional republic with separate executive, legislative and judicial institutions under the 1917 Constitution as amended [8]
Constitutional authority
The 1917 Constitution provides rights, federalism, secular state principles and the framework for federal public institutions [8]
Head of state and government
President Claudia Sheinbaum Pardo, in office since October 1, 2024 and serving as of November 6, 2026 [9]
Presidential term
Six years without a regular consecutive presidential reelection; presidential succession rules are constitutionally defined [8]
Executive administration
The president appoints a cabinet and leads the federal administration within constitutional and statutory limits
Federal Congress
Bicameral Congress of the Union, comprising the Chamber of Deputies and Senate of the Republic [16]
Chamber of Deputies
Five hundred seats under the existing mixed electoral system, combining district and proportional-representation seats [16]
Senate
One hundred twenty-eight seats representing a federal electoral system with different allocation methods across states [16]
Federal units
Thirty-one states and autonomous Mexico City, each with executive and legislative institutions of different local designs [8]
State and municipal government
Governors and state congresses exercise constitutionally allocated powers; municipalities deliver many local services
Judicial branch
Supreme Court of Justice of the Nation and federal courts operate under a judicial framework changed by the 2024 reform and 2025 election [14]
2025 judicial elections
Judicial office elections began in 2025 under reform provisions; their implications for independence and accountability remain subjects of public and legal debate [14]
Electoral administration
Instituto Nacional Electoral administers federal elections and coordinates responsibilities with local electoral bodies [13]
Electoral dispute resolution
Electoral tribunals adjudicate specified electoral controversies while administrative electoral bodies manage the voting process [13]
Constitutional rights
Freedom of expression, equality, education and other constitutional rights are formally guaranteed, while practical enforcement varies [8]
Political representation
Multipartidism and coalition politics coexist with strong presidential leadership and legislative bargaining
Public-security organization
Federal, state and municipal forces have distinct responsibilities; human-rights oversight and organized-crime violence remain continuing issues
Fiscal federalism
Federal revenue transfers, local taxation and national public spending shape services and regional differences
Capital jurisdiction
Mexico City is a self-governing federal entity hosting national institutions; it is not treated as the thirty-second ordinary state

Institutional update: Claudia Sheinbaum began her six-year term in October 2024. Changes to judicial appointments and elections starting in 2025 must be described as constitutional and statutory developments, not as evidence that every federal institution is directly elected in the same manner.

International Profile

United Nations
Member since November 7, 1945; participates in multilateral diplomacy and specialized agencies [2]
North American economic relationship
USMCA/T-MEC links trade rules with the United States and Canada; the three countries retain separate governments and currencies [17]
United States relations
Cross-border manufacturing, trade, migration, water management and security cooperation are major bilateral policy concerns
Canada relations
Trade, investment, mobility and trilateral cooperation occur alongside independent bilateral diplomatic arrangements
Latin American relations
Mexico engages through regional diplomacy, the Pacific Alliance and ties with Central America, the Caribbean and South America
OECD
Member since 1994, participating in economic policy reviews and comparative statistical cooperation [18]
G20
Participant in the G20 forum for major economies, finance and international cooperation
World Trade Organization
WTO member participating in multilateral trade agreements and dispute-settlement rules
Other institutions
Member of the IMF, World Bank and Organization of American States, among additional multilateral bodies
UNESCO heritage
Thirty-six inscribed World Heritage properties at the 2026 cutoff, including cultural, natural and mixed properties [10]
2025 UNESCO addition
Wixárika Route through Sacred Sites to Wirikuta was inscribed in 2025, and is included in the total of 36 [10]
Environmental diplomacy
Biodiversity agreements, climate policy and marine protection connect domestic issues with international obligations
Transnational watersheds
Colorado and Rio Grande/Río Bravo management involves cross-border treaties and changing drought conditions
Migration frameworks
Mexico is a country of origin, transit, destination and return, with policies affected by regional displacement and U.S. border rules
Time zones
Multiple national time zones and differentiated border-area clock policies; exact local time varies with state and season
Telephone and domain
International dialing code +52 and country-code web domain .mx, distinct from postal or ISO identifiers

Reference boundaries: UNESCO counts 36 inscribed properties as of the editorial cutoff; tentative-list proposals are excluded. USMCA/T-MEC addresses trade rules but is not a common currency or political federation.

Country Overview

Mexico, officially the United Mexican States, is a federal presidential republic in the southern part of North America. It borders the United States to the north and Guatemala and Belize to the southeast, with coastlines on the Pacific Ocean, Gulf of Mexico, and Caribbean Sea. The country comprises 31 states and Mexico City, a federal entity and the national capital. Its history includes many Indigenous civilizations and continuing Indigenous nations, three centuries of Spanish colonial rule, independence secured in 1821, and the revolutionary settlement embodied in the Constitution of 1917. Historical context: Mexico developed from a landscape of diverse Indigenous societies, including Maya, Mexica, Zapotec, Mixtec, Purépecha, and many others. Spanish colonization brought coercive labor systems, religious institutions, and far-reaching political and demographic changes. The country experienced imperial and republican governments, territorial conflicts, nineteenth-century liberal reforms, and the Mexican Revolution beginning in 1910. No single date captures its entire process of national formation.

Geographic comparability: The 1,964,375 km² figure describes Mexico's continental and island territory, not its exclusive economic zone. The population denominator used in the calculated density is a 2025 international estimate. Population series issued by INEGI and other institutions may use different estimation methods.

Economy and International Trade

Economic system
Upper-middle-income mixed-market economy with substantial manufacturing, services, agriculture, mining, energy production, and international trade
Nominal GDP
Approximately US$1.83 trillion (2025; current US dollars; World Bank). [2]
Nominal GDP per capita
Approximately US$13,889 (2025; current US dollars; World Bank). GDP per capita is national output divided by population; it is not average personal earnings. [2]
Real GDP growth
0.6% (2025, annual percentage change; World Bank series). [2]
Average quarterly household current income
MX$77,864 (2024; INEGI National Household Income and Expenditure Survey, ENIGH; pesos at 2024 prices). This is a mean, not a median; it includes noncash components such as imputed housing rent. [12]
Currency
Mexican peso (MXN); symbol $ (often shown as Mex$ internationally)
Main economic activities
Automotive and transport-equipment production, electrical and electronic equipment, industrial manufacturing, retail and business services, tourism, agriculture, petroleum, natural gas, and mining
Merchandise exports
US$664.837 billion in 2025 (INEGI provisional full-year figures, customs merchandise trade; excludes services). [5]
Merchandise imports
US$664.066 billion in 2025 (same INEGI merchandise series; excludes services). [5]
Merchandise trade balance
Approximately US$0.771 billion surplus in 2025, on the same provisional merchandise-trade basis; subject to revisions. [5]
Manufactured exports
US$608.819 billion, approximately 91.6% of merchandise exports in 2025; automotive products were US$185.791 billion within manufactured exports. [5]
Largest export relationship
The United States is Mexico's dominant merchandise export market; 83.7% of Mexico's nonpetroleum merchandise exports in 2025 were destined for the United States. This is not a share of all goods and services exports. [5]

People and Society

National-language framework
Spanish and Indigenous languages recognized under Mexico's General Law of Linguistic Rights of Indigenous Peoples are national languages with the same legal validity under Article 4. Spanish is the predominant language of national public life; describing it as the country's sole federal official language would obscure the statutory recognition of Indigenous languages. [10]
Indigenous language diversity
INALI's catalogue recognizes 11 Indigenous language families, 68 linguistic groupings, and 364 linguistic variants; these categories are not interchangeable counts. [9]
Languages spoken
Spanish; Indigenous languages including Nahuatl, Yucatec Maya, Mixtec, Zapotec, Otomí, Tzeltal, Tzotzil, Totonac, Mazatec, and many others; additional immigrant and foreign languages
Speakers of Indigenous languages
7,364,645 people aged 3 and older, or 6.1% of that age group, in the 2020 census. This is a language-use measure, not the total Indigenous-identifying population. [19]
Indigenous self-identification
Approximately 23.2 million people aged 3 and older (19.4% of the age group) self-identified as Indigenous in 2020; the definition differs from mother-tongue or language-speaker measures. [19]
Religious composition
2020 census, all ages: Catholic 77.7%; Protestant/Evangelical Christian 11.2%; other religions 0.2%; believers without a religious affiliation 2.5%; no religion 8.1%. Percentages are rounded, and the figures are from 2020 rather than a 2026 religious survey. [11]
Other religious and spiritual traditions
Judaism, Islam, Indigenous religious traditions, Afro-descendant traditions, spiritualist practices, and other faith communities; formal religious affiliation does not capture every syncretic or local practice
Median age
29 years in the 2020 census (not a 2025 or 2026 demographic estimate). [4]
Population diversity
Includes numerous Indigenous peoples, Afro-Mexican and Afro-descendant communities, populations with mixed ancestry, other historically established communities, and domestic and international migrants; categories of identity, language, and ancestry are distinct

Survey interpretation: The 2020 census religion percentages refer to the enumerated population, unlike adult-only sample surveys used in some other countries. Indigenous language speakers and people who self-identify as Indigenous are separate demographic groups and should not be treated as identical.

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Federal presidential constitutional republic; representative democratic system
Foundational law
Political Constitution of the United Mexican States, promulgated February 5, 1917, as amended. [7]
Head of state and government
President Claudia Sheinbaum Pardo (in office since October 1, 2024; term scheduled through September 30, 2030; verified November 4, 2026). [13]
Executive branch
President of the Republic and federal secretariats, agencies, and administrative institutions
Federal legislature
Bicameral Congress of the Union (Congreso de la Unión): Chamber of Deputies (500 members, generally three-year terms) and Senate (128 members, generally six-year terms). [14]
Judiciary
Supreme Court of Justice of the Nation and other federal judicial bodies, alongside state judiciaries; judicial institutions and judicial selection rules have undergone recent constitutional reform
Administrative divisions
32 federal entities: 31 states and Mexico City. [15]
Federalism
Powers distributed among the federal government, states, municipalities, and Mexico City's constitutional institutions under the national constitutional framework
National elections
Presidential elections ordinarily every six years; Chamber of Deputies renewed every three years; Senate renewed every six years. [14]
Presidential re-election
Constitutional prohibition on an individual holding the presidency for a second term

International Profile

United Nations
Admitted November 7, 1945; an original participant in the United Nations founding process. [17]
Major international memberships
United Nations, G20, OECD, WTO, Organization of American States, Community of Latin American and Caribbean States (CELAC), Pacific Alliance, World Bank, and International Monetary Fund
Regional economic arrangement
United States–Mexico–Canada Agreement (USMCA; known as T-MEC in Spanish), in force since July 1, 2020, replacing NAFTA. [18]
Leading trade relationship
The United States is Mexico's principal goods-trade counterpart, linked through highly integrated regional production and supply chains. [18]
Standard time zones
Several zones, including Northwest (UTC−8), Pacific (UTC−7), Central (UTC−6), and Southeast (UTC−5); local and seasonal time rules may vary, particularly near the northern border
International dialing code
+52

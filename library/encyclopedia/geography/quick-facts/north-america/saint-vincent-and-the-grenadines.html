Country Overview

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines is a sovereign, multi-island Caribbean state comprising the mountainous main island of Saint Vincent and numerous northern Grenadine islands and cays. It occupies the southern Windward Islands between Saint Lucia and Grenada and is distinguished by the active La Soufrière volcano, marine environments, fishing communities, agriculture and a growing tourism sector. The country achieved independence from Britain on October 27, 1979 as a parliamentary constitutional monarchy. Its historical development includes Indigenous Kalinago and Garifuna resistance, plantation enslavement and colonial dispossession. The November 2025 general election brought the New Democratic Party under Godwin Friday to government after more than two decades of Unity Labour Party leadership. This profile presents World Bank 2025 figures separately from the 2023 national census and identifies disaster, trade and tourism indicators by their own dates and definitions.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Saint Vincent and the Grenadines; the country name includes both Saint Vincent and its northern Grenadine islands
Capital city
Kingstown on southwest Saint Vincent, hosting central government administration and port functions [2]
Continental and regional classification
North America in the MGU index and Caribbean subregion in United Nations M49 statistical geography [13]
Indigenous settlement
Indigenous societies including Kalinago groups lived in the island chain long before European colonization
Garifuna heritage
Garifuna or Black Carib communities emerged through complex African and Indigenous histories, with profound importance in regional identity
European colonial contest
French and British claims and settlements developed amid active Indigenous resistance and rivalry over island control
Treaty of Paris 1763
Britain gained formal imperial authority through eighteenth-century settlements, but local resistance continued and sovereignty was contested on the ground
Carib Wars
Colonial campaigns against Kalinago and Garifuna communities culminated in coercive dispossession, deaths and deportations in the late eighteenth century
1797 deportations
Thousands of Garifuna were forcibly deported from Saint Vincent to Roatán and Central America, a formative episode in the wider Garifuna diaspora
Plantation economy
Enslaved African labor was central to colonial sugar production and generated lasting inequalities and cultural change
Emancipation
British abolition measures in the 1830s ended legal enslavement, without immediately eliminating economic and political exclusion
Post-emancipation society
Agricultural tenancy, migration, religious institutions and unionization shaped the transition from plantation structures to electoral politics
British colonial arrangements
Saint Vincent participated in shifting colonial federations and regional institutions rather than a single unchanged constitutional order
West Indies Federation
The islands participated in the Federation of the West Indies, 1958–1962, before its dissolution
Associated statehood
Internal self-government under a British associated-state arrangement began in 1969 and preceded national independence
Independence date
October 27, 1979; country became sovereign while continuing the monarchy as its national constitutional framework [12]
Independence Day
October 27 is the principal national commemoration of independent statehood
1980s political development
Parliamentary competition and regional diplomacy developed alongside agricultural change and public-sector expansion
Unity Labour Party period
Ralph Gonsalves became prime minister in 2001 and served until the 2025 general election
2025 electoral transition
The New Democratic Party led by Godwin Friday won 14 of 15 elected seats on November 27, 2025, ending the Unity Labour Party's long rule [6]
2025 accession
Godwin Friday was sworn in as prime minister November 28, 2025; parliamentary majority and ministerial assignments followed [6]
National flag
Vertical blue, gold and green bands with three green diamond motifs evoke island geography and national symbolism
United Nations membership
Saint Vincent and the Grenadines joined the United Nations September 16, 1980 [2]
ISO identifiers
VC (alpha-2), VCT (alpha-3), 670 (numeric); .vc internet domain [13]

Constitutional chronology: October 27, 1979 is the independence date. The 1969 associated-state arrangement was an earlier transition, and the November 2025 electoral change installed a new prime minister without changing the state’s independence or monarchical constitution.

Geography and Population

Location
Archipelagic Windward Island state south of Saint Lucia and north of Grenada, with Caribbean Sea to the west and Atlantic Ocean to the east
National surface area
389 km² in United Nations geographic reporting; some publications round to 390 km² [2]
Main island
Saint Vincent contains the capital, most of the population, the highest mountains and extensive agriculture and urban services
Grenadine islands
Bequia, Mustique, Canouan, Mayreau, Union Island and other smaller islands and cays are part of the state, not separate sovereign countries
Grenadines boundary
Carriacou and Petite Martinique belong to Grenada, although they lie within the broader Grenadine island chain
La Soufrière
Active volcano on northern Saint Vincent rising to about 1,234 metres; a principal geological and environmental hazard
April 2021 eruption
Explosive activity at La Soufrière displaced residents, disrupted farming and air travel and covered extensive areas in ash [9]
Historical eruptions
La Soufrière has experienced other major eruptions, including events in 1902 and 1979, making long-term monitoring necessary
Volcanic ash impacts
Ashfall can affect livestock, health, roofs, water supplies, roads and farming even beyond formal volcano hazard zones
2024 Hurricane Beryl
Beryl caused severe damage especially in the southern Grenadines, including Union Island, Mayreau and Canouan [10]
Tropical cyclone risks
Hurricane season storms threaten roofs, fishing vessels, harbors, coastal tourism and essential transport between islands
Climate
Tropical maritime weather with a generally wetter season and variation between mountain slopes, coastlines and the smaller Grenadine islands
Rainforest and streams
Steep forested uplands and rivers support biodiversity and water resources on Saint Vincent, while many small islands have limited freshwater
Coral and seagrass ecology
Reefs, mangroves and nearshore habitats support fisheries, beaches and coastal protection
Tobago Cays
Protected marine environments in the Grenadines are important for reef conservation, yacht tourism and fisheries management
Kingstown metropolitan area
Administrative, economic and transport functions concentrate along the southern and southwestern coast of Saint Vincent
Transport geography
Ferries, small aircraft, cargo vessels and marinas connect the capital with Grenadine communities; weather can interrupt travel
Argyle International Airport
Saint Vincent's principal international airport supports commercial access and expands logistics and tourism connectivity
2025 World Bank population
99,924 in the 2025 annual World Bank series, reflecting the international population definition and model vintage [1]
2023 national census
CARICOM's 2020-census-round portal reports a 2023 census total of 108,764, distinct from the 2025 World Bank estimate [3]
2012 census comparison
The preceding 2012 population census reported 109,991 people; the 2023 enumeration must not be confused with 2025 international model estimates [4] [3]
Population-series mismatch
The 2023 national census and World Bank 2025 numbers use different reference dates and methodological assumptions; their difference is not a measured two-year exodus [3] [1]
Approximate density
About 257 persons per km² using World Bank 2025 population divided by UN 389 km², but distribution varies sharply across islands [1] [2]
Environmental pressures
Shoreline erosion, reef degradation, volcanic slopes, waste disposal and freshwater supply pose different problems on the main island and smaller cays
Disaster relocation
Volcanic emergencies and cyclone damage may require evacuation between districts and islands, with difficult inter-island logistics
Conservation geography
The La Soufrière and Grenadines Island Group are on UNESCO's tentative list but are not inscribed World Heritage properties [11]

Population method: CARICOM identifies 108,764 enumerated residents in the 2023 census, while the World Bank estimates 99,924 for 2025. This apparent gap combines differing statistical definitions and vintages; it must not be interpreted as a measured post-2023 population exodus without a reconciliation study.

Economy and International Trade

Economic classification
Small, open Caribbean island economy combining tourism, agriculture, fisheries, construction, public services and smaller manufacturing industries
2025 nominal GDP
Approximately US$1.2552 billion in the World Bank annual current-dollar series, presented as US$1.26 billion in the country panel [1]
2025 GDP per capita
US$12,561.8 in current dollars; this national-output ratio does not measure median wages or household living standards [1]
2025 real GDP growth
About 3.4% in the World Bank annual real-output growth series, subject to later statistical revisions [1]
2025 consumer inflation
0.8% in the World Bank annual consumer-price indicator [1]
2025 modeled unemployment
18.0% of the labor force in the World Bank ILO-modeled series, not necessarily the same as a national survey figure [1]
Remittances as share of GDP
World Bank reports about 7.0% for 2025 personal remittances received, a household-transfer category distinct from exports [1]
Currency
Eastern Caribbean dollar (XCD), used throughout the monetary union administered by the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank [14]
Exchange rate
The EC dollar operates around the common official fixed rate of XCD 2.70 per US$1 through the currency-union system [14]
Tourism
Hotels, guesthouses, marinas, private yacht services, small cruises and excursions support services trade and local employment
Grenadines tourism
Mustique, Bequia, Canouan, Union Island and Tobago Cays attract different forms of yachting, luxury lodging and nature-based tourism
Volcano-economic disruption
The 2021 La Soufrière eruption damaged crops, roads and livelihoods and required emergency public spending [9]
Hurricane-economic disruption
Hurricane Beryl in 2024 damaged southern island housing, hotels, boats, electricity and public infrastructure, delaying some economic activity [10]
Agriculture
Bananas, root crops, vegetables, fruits and other local farm products contribute to employment and food supply
Banana-industry change
Tropical storms, disease, market changes and competition reduced the historical export dominance of bananas
Arrowroot
Saint Vincent has a historical arrowroot starch industry and distinctive agro-processing traditions, though its present trade shares vary
Fisheries
Small-scale and commercial fishery operations supply food and exports, with pressure from costs, marine protection and weather exposure
Flour and food processing
Milling, food preparation, beverages and selected manufactures can contribute to merchandise exports and regional supplies
2024 goods exports
Approximately US$57 million in UNCTAD merchandise-trade data for 2024, an actual reported year rather than a 2025 estimate [5]
2024 goods imports
Approximately US$506 million in the corresponding 2024 UNCTAD series [5]
2024 merchandise trade deficit
Roughly US$449 million using the paired UNCTAD goods figures; excludes services earnings [5]
2024 services exports
About US$366 million in UNCTAD's international services trade series [5]
2024 services imports
About US$196 million in UNCTAD's services trade series [5]
2024 services surplus
Around US$170 million; this arithmetic difference does not equal the nation's full current-account balance [5]
Travel-services composition
Travel accounted for around 84.8% of UNCTAD 2024 service exports, consistent with the economic significance of visitor spending [5]
Imported energy and food
Fuel, vehicles, construction materials and many consumer products are purchased from abroad, creating shipping and foreign-exchange exposure
External investment
Hotel development, transport projects and reconstruction spending contribute to jobs while creating pressure on public finances and imported inputs
Climate-related fiscal risk
Storm recovery, coastal defenses and volcanic preparedness require investment whose annual costs fluctuate by event and funding arrangements
International trade measurement
UNCTAD values refer to 2024; remittances, foreign aid, construction receipts and tourism services are distinct from customs merchandise sales

People and Society

Official language
English is the official language of schools, parliament and formal administration
Vincentian Creole
English-lexifier Vincentian Creole speech is common in daily communication and reflects African, Indigenous and European linguistic histories
Garifuna cultural legacy
The history of Black Carib and Garifuna communities links Saint Vincent with living communities in Belize, Honduras, Guatemala and Nicaragua
Kalinago history
Indigenous political, settlement and resistance histories form part of the national historical record and regional heritage
African diaspora
Enslaved and freed African-descended populations shaped religious life, agriculture, language, festivals and modern society
Population definitions
The 2023 census total and 2025 international population estimate differ and do not by themselves establish direct migration totals [3] [1]
Religious traditions
Anglican, Methodist, Seventh-day Adventist, Pentecostal, Roman Catholic and other Christian communities are historically significant
Religious-measurement caveat
Contemporary religion shares are not invented from historic denominational prominence or assumed attendance
Education
Government and private schools, community colleges and regional examination systems support primary, secondary and postsecondary education
Tertiary education
Saint Vincent and the Grenadines Community College and overseas universities provide academic, vocational and technical pathways
Health
Hospitals, clinics and island health centers serve residents, with specialized services and emergency referrals requiring transport arrangements
Island access disparities
Residents on outer islands may face greater expense and travel requirements for healthcare, education and administrative services
Social development priorities
Employment, affordable housing, youth services, disaster recovery and climate resilience remain major public policy concerns
Carnival
Vincy Mas combines music, costumes, street performance and competition, reflecting local popular culture and its evolving forms
Music
Calypso, soca and steelpan music are central to festival and regional artistic networks
Cuisine
Breadfruit, roast fish, callaloo, root crops, plantains and seafood illustrate island foodways and farming traditions
National dish
Roasted breadfruit and fried jackfish is widely identified as a national dish, though families and communities prepare many variations
Sport
Cricket links Saint Vincent to West Indies regional sport, while football, athletics and sailing also have strong followings
Marine recreation
Sailing, diving and boating are prominent in the Grenadines, with tensions between tourism development, access and conservation
Built and archaeological heritage
Fort Charlotte, colonial-era churches and Indigenous rock art provide evidence of changing political and cultural life
Disaster response
Voluntary organizations, schools, shelters and public agencies assisted with the eruption in 2021 and hurricane damage in 2024
Diaspora
Migration links with the United Kingdom, the United States, Canada and regional Caribbean states support families, remittances and cultural exchange
Public commemorations
Independence and emancipation observances express distinct historical events and should not be treated as interchangeable

Cultural and population evidence: Garifuna history, Christian traditions and local Creole speech are substantive cultural topics but do not authorize a current numerical ancestry, language or religion distribution in the absence of compatible census tables.

Government and Institutions

Form of state
Sovereign unitary parliamentary democracy and constitutional monarchy under the 1979 independence Constitution [8]
Head of state
King Charles III in his separate legal capacity as King of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines [7]
Governor-General
Dame Susan Dougan, representing the monarch and carrying out constitutional appointment and ceremonial functions [7]
Prime Minister
Dr. Godwin Friday, sworn in November 28, 2025 after the New Democratic Party won the general election [6]
Previous prime minister
Ralph Gonsalves headed Unity Labour Party governments from 2001 to November 2025; his departure followed electoral defeat, not a change to republican government [6]
2025 election result
New Democratic Party won 14 of 15 directly elected seats and Unity Labour Party won one in the November 27, 2025 election [6]
House of Assembly
The national legislature includes fifteen directly elected members, six appointed senators and the Speaker under national parliamentary arrangements [6]
Election method
Constituency-based first-past-the-post voting chooses the fifteen directly elected seats, with appointed senators representing separate constitutional categories
Cabinet
Prime minister and ministers lead executive departments, including finance, education, health, tourism, agriculture and national security [7]
Constitutional monarch
The Crown is a national constitutional institution and does not make the country subordinate to the British Parliament
Judicial system
Eastern Caribbean Supreme Court arrangements support the High Court and Court of Appeal with access to final appellate arrangements under applicable law
Constitutional rights
Written constitutional protections address liberty, political participation, equality and due process, subject to interpretation by courts
Local administration
Parish and island-level service structures coexist with national ministries; smaller-island delivery poses distinct logistical demands
Grenadines administration
Grenadine communities form part of the same country and elect national representatives rather than exercising separate external sovereignty
Political competition
National Democratic Party and Unity Labour Party have been major parties in recent decades, reflecting changes in electoral alignment
Constitutional reform debate
Discussions about republicanism and parliamentary modernization have occurred without altering the monarchy as of November 2026
Public finances
Revenue, spending, debt management and reconstruction appropriations are supervised through executive departments and the House
Public security
Police, coast-guard and other agencies address domestic crime, border management and maritime security
Disaster management
Emergency and volcanic authorities oversee preparedness, evacuation, shelter operations and assistance across several islands
Foreign policy
The national government signs agreements and represents the country internationally, while the governor-general acts within constitutional rules
Legislative oversight
Opposition and committee scrutiny operate within parliamentary conventions even when one party controls a large majority
Electoral chronology
The 2025 election changed the head of government but did not change the 1979 independence date or the sovereign legal status of the state

Parliamentary count: 15 directly elected House seats are separate from appointed senators and the Speaker. The 14–1 NDP/ULP election split describes the directly elected seats, not the entire legislative membership.

International Profile

United Nations
Member since September 16, 1980; independence had occurred in October 1979 [2]
CARICOM
Member of the Caribbean Community for regional diplomacy, trade and institutional coordination [3]
OECS
Member of the Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States and associated regional integration institutions [16]
Eastern Caribbean Currency Union
Uses the common regional currency and participates in ECCB monetary arrangements [14]
Commonwealth
Independent Commonwealth realm with its own domestic constitutional Crown
World Trade Organization
Participates in WTO multilateral market-access, trade-policy and dispute-settlement frameworks [17]
Regional maritime connections
Nearby Saint Lucia and Grenada share interests in ferry transport, marine resources, aviation and climate risk
Grenadine geography
Southern islands such as Carriacou are Grenadian territory, while northern Grenadines belong to Saint Vincent and the Grenadines
United States relations
Visitor spending, trade, investment, remittances and diplomatic relations involve the United States and its Caribbean region
United Kingdom relations
Historical, education, migration, Commonwealth and development links continue following independence
Climate diplomacy
Small-island exposure to cyclones, sea-level rise, volcanic hazards and costly infrastructure shapes international negotiations
Disaster financing
Caribbean and international partners provide emergency relief and resilience assistance following eruption and tropical cyclone impacts
UNESCO World Heritage properties
Zero inscribed World Heritage properties as of the 2026 World Heritage State Party listing [11]
UNESCO tentative sites
Tobago Cays-associated Grenadines landscapes, La Soufrière National Park and rock art appear on the tentative list, not the inscribed list [11]
Marine protection
Fisheries, moorings, turtle habitats and reef conservation require regional and national cooperation and consistent enforcement
Diaspora diplomacy
Consular services assist Vincentians living abroad and address migration, travel and educational mobility
International time zone
Atlantic Standard Time UTC−4 with no regular nationwide seasonal daylight-saving change
International calling and internet
+1 784 telephone country code within the North American numbering plan and .vc country-code domain

World Heritage status: No properties are currently inscribed for Saint Vincent and the Grenadines. Tentative nominations for rock art, the Grenadine island group and La Soufrière are proposals, not UNESCO inscriptions.

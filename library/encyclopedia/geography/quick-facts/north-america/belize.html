Country Overview

Belize is an independent Commonwealth realm on the Caribbean coast of Central America, adjoining Mexico to the north and Guatemala to the west and south. Its mainland includes tropical forests, wetlands, Maya archaeological landscapes and an extensive offshore barrier-reef complex. Indigenous Maya civilizations, British colonial administration, African-descended and Caribbean societies, and later migration have all shaped the population. Formerly known as British Honduras, the territory gained independence in 1981. English is the sole official language, but Kriol, Spanish, Maya languages and Garifuna are important in daily life. Tourism, agriculture and marine exports support the economy, while coastal hazards, biodiversity protection and the long-running territorial dispute with Guatemala remain important national concerns.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Belize, a sovereign independent Commonwealth realm [7]
Common name
Belize; the English-language official country name has been used since the territorial renaming in 1973
Capital city
Belmopan; developed inland as the national capital following the destruction caused by Hurricane Hattie in 1961 [2]
Continent / UN M49 subregion
North America in the MGU library; Americas / Central America in United Nations M49 coding [11]
Indigenous history
Maya civilization established cities, trade networks and political communities long before European arrival; surviving Maya peoples remain part of Belizean society
Early European contact
Spanish expeditions reached the region in the sixteenth century, but durable Spanish administration was limited compared with neighboring territories
British settlement and colonial rule
English-speaking settlers developed commercial timber extraction; enslaved African labor was central to the colonial economy, and British authority gradually expanded
Battle of St. George's Caye
Traditionally commemorated on September 10 for the 1798 conflict between British settlers and a Spanish expedition; national remembrance should not be mistaken for an independence date
Formal colonial designation
British Honduras became a Crown colony in 1862, though the territory had an earlier history of British settlement and administration
Emancipation
Slavery was abolished in the British Empire in the 1830s; legal emancipation did not eliminate economic inequality or colonial landholding patterns
Internal self-government
1964 constitutional development transferred substantial responsibility for internal affairs to locally elected ministers
Territorial renaming
June 1, 1973: British Honduras was renamed Belize, preceding constitutional independence
Independence
September 21, 1981: Belize became independent from the United Kingdom [10]
Independence Day
September 21; the September 10 public holiday commemorates a different historical episode
Constitutional foundation
Belize Constitution of 1981 establishes the parliamentary monarchy, protected rights and national institutions [6]
United Nations admission
September 25, 1981, shortly after independence [10]
ISO 3166-1 codes
BZ (alpha-2), BLZ (alpha-3), and 084 (numeric) [11]
Internet domain and nationality
.bz country-code domain; citizens and nationals are called Belizeans
National symbols
National flag combines blue and red with a central coat of arms depicting the country's historic mahogany industry; national motto is Sub Umbra Floreo

Historical interpretation: Belize’s 1973 renaming, 1981 independence and 1981 United Nations admission are separate milestones. British Honduras was the earlier colonial name; it does not describe the current sovereign state.

Geography and Population

UN statistical region
Central America, within the Americas; the MGU North America category includes Central America and the Caribbean [11]
Total area
Approximately 22,965 km² in UNData; other sources occasionally round the figure to 22,966 km² [2]
2025 population
422,924 people in the World Bank annual country series; an estimate rather than a 2025 national census [1]
2022 census
The most recent decennial census was conducted in 2022, with a different reference date, coverage and methodology from the 2025 World Bank series [5]
Population density
Approximately 18.4 persons per km², calculated from World Bank 2025 population and UN total area; actual settlement is concentrated in selected districts [1] [2]
Population growth
Around 1.4% annually in 2025 in the World Bank population series [1]
Administrative geography
Six districts: Corozal, Orange Walk, Belize, Cayo, Stann Creek and Toledo
Largest urban area
Belize City remains the principal commercial urban centre and major port; it is not the country's constitutional capital
Other settlements
Belmopan, San Ignacio–Santa Elena, Orange Walk Town, Corozal Town, Dangriga, Punta Gorda and San Pedro
Land neighbors
Mexico to the north and Guatemala to the west and south; the Caribbean Sea lies to the east
Coast and islands
Mangrove-fringed mainland coastline, numerous cayes and offshore atolls, including Ambergris Caye and Turneffe Atoll
Barrier-reef system
The Belize Barrier Reef Reserve System is a UNESCO World Heritage property that protects reef, atoll and marine ecosystems [9]
Physical regions
Northern lowlands, coastal plain, wetlands, Maya Mountains and forested southern uplands
Highest elevation
Doyle's Delight in the Maya Mountains, approximately 1,124 metres, is commonly reported as the country's highest point
Principal rivers
Belize River, New River, Hondo, Sibun, Monkey River and Sarstoon River are important regional drainage systems
Climate
Tropical, with a wetter season and a drier season; rainfall patterns vary substantially between northern and southern districts
Natural hazards
Atlantic hurricanes, coastal floods, intense rainfall, drought and coral-reef stress are major environmental and infrastructure risks
Protected landscapes
Marine reserves, reef habitat, tropical forest and Maya archaeological landscapes support biodiversity, heritage and tourism

Population methodology: The 2022 national census and the World Bank 2025 population estimate are not interchangeable observations. The calculated density is a national average and is not representative of Belize City, island settlements or sparsely populated forests.

Economy and International Trade

Economic model
Small open economy supported by tourism, domestic services, agriculture, fisheries, financial activities, construction and international commerce
Nominal GDP
US$3.3265 billion in 2025, in current-dollar World Bank accounting [1]
GDP per capita
US$7,865.5 in 2025 (current US dollars); this is calculated output per resident, not a wage or median household income [1]
Real GDP growth
2.7% in 2025 according to the World Bank country series [1]
Consumer inflation
1.1% in 2025, World Bank annual consumer price indicator [1]
Modeled unemployment
8.9% for 2025 in the ILO-modelled series shown by the World Bank; not necessarily comparable with Belize's national labor-force measure [1]
Currency
Belize dollar (BZD), commonly written BZ$, rather than the U.S. dollar
Currency arrangement
Official fixed exchange rate of BZ$2 per US$1; actual retail conversion fees or transaction terms can differ
Central bank
Central Bank of Belize, responsible for monetary regulation and the country's currency arrangements
Principal productive sectors
Tourism and accommodation, agriculture, agroprocessing, marine fisheries, trade, logistics, construction and public services
Domestic merchandise exports
BZ$390.0 million for calendar 2025 in the Statistical Institute of Belize's December 2025 release; this is a domestic-export series [3]
Recorded merchandise imports
BZ$2.911 billion for calendar 2025 in the same Statistical Institute of Belize release; imports exceed domestic merchandise exports [3]
Goods-account comparison
The preceding imports and domestic exports imply a large goods-trade gap, but the calculation is not a full current-account balance and excludes service exports [3]
WTO/UN Comtrade goods exports
A separate 2025 reported merchandise dataset shows approximately US$242.3 million in exports; it differs from Belize's domestic-export series in trade scope and classification [4]
Traditional export products
Raw cane sugar, bananas, citrus preparations, marine products and molasses are important merchandise lines [4] [3]
Main goods export partners
United Kingdom and United States are major partners in the WTO-reported 2025 dataset; regional Caribbean and Central American destinations also matter [4]
Import requirements
Fuels, machinery, vehicles, processed food, manufactured goods and construction materials make the country dependent on external supply chains
Tourism services
Hotels, tours, reef diving, archaeological sites and cruise-related activities generate service receipts that cannot be added to or confused with merchandise export totals
External economic vulnerability
Hurricane damage, fluctuations in tourism demand, agricultural weather shocks, and dependence on imported fuels and manufactures affect public finance and investment
Economic policy concerns
Infrastructure reliability, diversification, environmental sustainability, export competitiveness, fiscal management, and investment in education and transport

Trade-statistics qualification: The Statistical Institute of Belize reports domestic merchandise exports and recorded imports in Belize dollars; the WTO/UN Comtrade dataset gives a different export measure in U.S. dollars. Differences in customs scope, re-exports and processing mean the two export totals should not be treated as a simple exchange-rate conversion. Tourism is a service export and is excluded from the goods figures.

People and Society

Official language
English is the sole official language, used in legislation, formal government administration and much of the education system
Other major languages
Belizean Kriol (Creole), Spanish, Yucatec Maya, Mopan Maya, Q'eqchi' Maya and Garifuna are important in different communities [5]
Multilingual practice
English-language legal status does not mean residents use only English; multilingual communication is widespread in households and commerce [5]
Population ancestry and identities
Mestizo, Creole, Maya, Garifuna, Mennonite and other communities contribute distinct histories and cultural identities; census categories should not be equated with language or religion
Maya communities
Mopan, Q'eqchi' and Yucatec Maya populations have distinct cultural, linguistic and territorial traditions
Garifuna cultural heritage
Garifuna communities, especially in coastal towns, preserve language, music, culinary and commemorative traditions
Roman Catholic affiliation
31.8% in the 2022 census, compared with 40.1% in the 2010 census; percentages refer to reported religious identification [5]
Protestant affiliation
Approximately 29.8% in the 2022 census when Protestant groupings are combined; different denominations are recorded separately [5]
No religious affiliation
Approximately 31.0% reported none in the 2022 census; not all non-affiliation has the same meaning as atheism [5]
Other religious communities
Mennonite and Seventh-day Adventist groups are prominent within the diverse Christian landscape; smaller non-Christian groups are also represented
Cultural expressions
Kriol speech and traditions, Maya material culture, Garifuna music and dance, Afro-Caribbean heritage, regional cuisine and national September celebrations
Education
Public and privately supported institutions provide primary, secondary and tertiary education; geographic access remains unequal between urban centers and rural districts
Health and social policy
Maternal and child health, chronic disease, rural service access, disaster preparedness and affordability are recurring policy concerns
Community distribution
Coastal communities, interior agricultural districts, Maya villages, island settlements and border towns have different livelihoods and infrastructure needs

Religion and language: The 2022 census supplies actual dated answers to religion and language questions. The 2022 percentages must not be presented as the composition of the estimated 2025 population, and religious identification is not evidence of religious practice.

Government and Institutions

Constitutional form
Parliamentary constitutional monarchy and independent Commonwealth realm under the 1981 Constitution [6]
Head of state
King Charles III, in his separate constitutional capacity as King of Belize [7]
Governor-General
Dame Froyla Tzalam, the monarch's representative in Belize as listed in the verified 2026 leadership records [7]
Head of government
Prime Minister John Briceño, returned to office following the March 2025 general election [8] [7]
Cabinet
Ministers are responsible for national executive government under the parliamentary constitutional framework and political leadership of the prime minister [6]
Legislature
National Assembly with an elected House of Representatives and an appointed Senate [6]
House of Representatives
31 elected constituency members under the constitutional and parliamentary framework; the Speaker's position is governed separately [6]
Senate
Appointed upper chamber with 13 senators under the prevailing institutional arrangement, representing government, opposition and designated social interests
Judiciary
Supreme Court and Court of Appeal within Belize's court system, with the Caribbean Court of Justice serving as the final appellate court
Electoral arrangements
Competitive multiparty parliamentary elections, generally held in single-member House constituencies
Local administration
Six districts, municipal councils and village councils; locally managed issues differ from responsibilities of central ministries
Constitutional rights
Constitution protects fundamental rights and liberties while providing mechanisms for their adjudication [6]
Legal tradition
Predominantly common-law system shaped by British colonial legal history and post-independence legislation
Current executive context
John Briceño's administration continued after the 2025 general election; officeholder information has been checked for the November 2026 edition [8]
National security
Belize Defence Force, Coast Guard and Police Department discharge distinct defense, maritime and domestic policing functions

Constitutional distinction: Belize is not part of the United Kingdom. King Charles III is the Belizean monarch under a separate national legal order, while the elected House and cabinet exercise domestic democratic powers.

International Profile

United Nations
Member since September 25, 1981, shortly after independence [10]
Commonwealth of Nations
Member as an independent Commonwealth realm, sharing a monarch constitutionally without surrendering sovereignty
Caribbean Community (CARICOM)
Participates in Caribbean regional economic and political cooperation [12]
Central American institutions
Participates in Central American regional integration frameworks while maintaining strong Caribbean diplomatic and historical links
World Trade Organization
Member of the WTO, with trade statistics reported through international customs-data classifications [4]
UNESCO World Heritage
One inscribed property: Belize Barrier Reef Reserve System, first inscribed in 1996 [9]
Global environmental interests
Reef conservation, forest protection, ocean governance, climate adaptation and biodiversity finance are especially important for a small coastal state
Territorial issue with Guatemala
The long-standing bilateral territorial, insular and maritime dispute is before the International Court of Justice; readers should distinguish the existence of the case from a final adjudicated settlement
Principal economic relationships
United States, United Kingdom, Mexico, Guatemala and Caribbean partners influence trade, tourism, transport and investment [4]
Diplomatic identity
Belize belongs geographically to Central America and culturally and institutionally also participates in Caribbean cooperation
Standard time
UTC−6 year-round, with no regular daylight-saving transition
International dialing code
+501; country-code domain is .bz

The Belize Barrier Reef Reserve System is one UNESCO property composed of multiple protected components; its seven component areas are not seven separate inscriptions.

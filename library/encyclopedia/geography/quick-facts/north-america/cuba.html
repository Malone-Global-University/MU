Country Overview

Cuba is an independent island republic in the northern Caribbean, consisting of the largest island in the Caribbean, the Isla de la Juventud, and smaller islands and cays. Indigenous Taíno and other communities preceded Spanish colonization, which began in the early sixteenth century and established plantation agriculture dependent on enslaved African labor. Cuba became formally independent in 1902 after the Spanish–American War and a period of United States military occupation. The 1959 revolution brought a socialist government to power; its current political institutions are organized under the 2019 Constitution. Havana is the capital and primary metropolitan center. Cuba combines major medical and educational institutions and a distinctive cultural heritage with prolonged economic shortages, international migration, energy-system stress, and disagreements over the economic and political effects of United States sanctions.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Cuba (República de Cuba) [4]
Common name
Cuba; Spanish: Cuba; official adjectival form Cuban
Capital city
Havana (La Habana), the largest city and seat of the central government [2]
UN M49 geographic region
Americas / Latin America and the Caribbean / Caribbean; grouped under North America in MGU's index [5]
Early inhabitants
Taíno, Ciboney and other Indigenous communities inhabited the archipelago before European colonial expansion; local histories and archaeological evidence are more complex than a single-origin account
Spanish conquest
After Columbus reached Cuba in 1492, conquest and permanent colonial settlement advanced from 1511; violence, forced labor and epidemics devastated Indigenous populations [4]
Colonial economic formation
Sugar cultivation, tobacco, ports and slave-based plantations connected Cuba to Atlantic trade and the Spanish imperial economy
Wars of independence
The Ten Years' War began in 1868; the Little War followed in 1879–1880; the final independence struggle began in 1895 and included José Martí and other revolutionary leaders [4]
Spanish–American War
In 1898 Spain relinquished sovereignty; the United States administered Cuba until the establishment of the republic in 1902 [4]
Republic established
May 20, 1902 marks the inauguration of the independent republic; the Platt Amendment framework initially constrained Cuban sovereignty and permitted U.S. intervention [4]
Revolutionary transition
Fidel Castro's revolutionary movement overthrew Fulgencio Batista on January 1, 1959; subsequent land, economic and political transformations established socialist government
Current constitutional order
The Constitution was approved in a February 2019 referendum and entered into force in April 2019; it reorganized national state offices while retaining Communist Party leadership [9]
National day / commemorations
January 1 is Triumph of the Revolution Day; May 20, 1902 is the older republic-independence milestone and should not be confused with the present principal public holiday
United Nations membership
Founding UN member; admitted October 24, 1945, when Cuba was already an independent republic [12]
ISO 3166-1 codes
CU (alpha-2), CUB (alpha-3), 192 (numeric)
National flag
Five alternating blue and white horizontal stripes, a red triangle at the hoist and a white five-pointed star
National emblem
Shield featuring a rising sun, a key, and Cuban landscape and stripes; framed by branches
National anthem
La Bayamesa (Himno de Bayamo)
Independence distinction
The 1895 armed struggle, the 1898 end of Spanish rule, the 1902 republic and the 1959 revolution mark different events and should never be collapsed into one date

Geography and Population

Total surface area
109,884 km², encompassing the main island, Isla de la Juventud and thousands of smaller islands and cays; this is a national archipelago figure [2]
Land-area convention
Land area is slightly smaller than total surface area; do not substitute the main island's area for the entire republic
Geographic setting
Northern Caribbean, south of Florida, east of Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula, north of Jamaica and west of Hispaniola [15]
Coasts
Caribbean Sea, Gulf of Mexico and Atlantic Ocean; complex shallow banks, extensive coastal wetlands and offshore coral reefs
Principal island
The island of Cuba is elongated east–west and contains the great majority of population, cities and farmland
Other major island
Isla de la Juventud is administered separately from the 15 provinces as a special municipality [15]
Relief
Mountain areas include the Sierra Maestra in the southeast and the Escambray massif; plains and rolling karst uplands occupy much of the interior
Highest mountain
Pico Turquino, approximately 1,974 m, in the Sierra Maestra
Principal river
Río Cauto is commonly identified as Cuba's longest river; other streams are shorter and variable seasonally
Climate
Tropical maritime climate with a wetter season generally from May to October and a drier season during northern-hemisphere winter
Natural hazards
Atlantic hurricanes, coastal surge, heavy rains, drought and occasional earthquakes, particularly in eastern Cuba
2025 World Bank population
10,937,203 persons, 2025 modeled international series; this is not the same concept or estimate as Cuba's official effective resident population [1]
2025 Cuban effective residents
9,434,593 persons at the end of 2025, as reported by officials drawing on the national ONEI demographic release; reflects residence and migration adjustments [3]
Population-data divergence
The 2025 World Bank series exceeds the year-end national effective-resident count by about 1.50 million; methods, timing and treatment of overseas migration differ, so neither number is a recent population census [1] [3]
Population change
The national effective-resident count fell during 2025 amid outward migration, low fertility and excess deaths; point-in-time totals differ from midyear estimates [3]
Major cities
Havana, Santiago de Cuba, Camagüey, Holguín, Santa Clara, Guantánamo and Cienfuegos; urban population concentrates near ports and provincial centers
Administrative geography
15 provinces and the Special Municipality of Isla de la Juventud; provinces are subdivided into municipalities [15]
Environmental ecosystems
Mangroves, dry forests, coral reefs, wetlands, limestone landscapes and mountain forests; coastal and reef environments face climate and pollution pressures
UNESCO natural heritage
Alejandro de Humboldt National Park and Desembarco del Granma National Park are among Cuba's inscribed natural World Heritage properties [8]

Population comparison: 9.435 million is the national effective-residence count at end-2025, whereas 10.937 million is an international 2025 World Bank series. The two measures should never be presented as interchangeable.

Economy and International Trade

Economic organization
Predominantly state-led socialist economy with public enterprises, cooperatives, limited private enterprise and regulated self-employment, plus international tourism and professional services
Most recent World Bank nominal GDP
US$107.35 billion in 2020, current US dollars; World Bank's Cuba profile has no 2025 observation for this indicator [1]
Most recent World Bank GDP per capita
US$9,605.3 in 2020, current US dollars; not a contemporary 2025 standard-of-living estimate [1]
Other GDP publications
UN publications may present newer current-US$ national-account estimates, but exchange-rate conventions and statistical revisions can make them noncomparable with World Bank's last published 2020 figure [2]
Real GDP growth
-1.1% in 2024 in the World Bank country series; this is a volume-growth measure, not nominal-dollar GDP [1]
Population growth
-0.4% in 2025 in the World Bank international population series; the national effective-residence count shows a different rate and denominator [1] [3]
Unemployment rate
1.7% in 2025, modeled ILO estimate in World Bank data; incomplete economic activity and measurement differences limit interpretation [1]
Inflation and purchasing power
Inflation, exchange-market fragmentation and chronic availability constraints affect household purchasing power; no comparable recent World Bank CPI observation is provided on its Cuba country summary [1]
Currency
Cuban peso (CUP); the former convertible peso (CUC) was removed from ordinary circulation after the 2021 monetary reform
Exchange-rate caveat
Official administered exchange rates vary by transaction channel or segment and may diverge sharply from informal-market prices; a single published rate should not be treated as universally obtainable
Central bank
Central Bank of Cuba (Banco Central de Cuba), responsible for monetary regulation and authorized exchange arrangements
Major productive sectors
Healthcare and education services, tourism, nickel mining, sugar-related activity, food processing, tobacco, beverages, pharmaceutical production and transportation
Fuel and electricity
Imported fuel requirements, aging generation assets and grid constraints have contributed to power shortages and economic disruption
Merchandise exports
Approximately US$1.607 billion in 2024 in UNCTAD's estimated general-profile merchandise trade series; goods only [6]
Merchandise imports
Approximately US$8.757 billion in 2024 in the same UNCTAD series; goods only [6]
Goods trade balance
Approximately US$7.15 billion deficit for 2024 in that UNCTAD series; does not incorporate professional-service or travel receipts [6]
Goods export classifications
Tobacco products, nickel and other ores/metals, rum, seafood and smaller industrial goods feature prominently; category rankings vary by year and reporting system [7]
Older matched product dataset
WTO's UN Comtrade 2022 merchandise series totals US$2.170 billion and gives nickel products, rum and tobacco as large categories; do not treat those shares as 2024 data [7]
Services exports
Tourism and overseas professional services are important but not equivalent to the sale of physical merchandise; reporting and valuation are more difficult than in the goods series
Import dependence
Food, petroleum, capital machinery and medicines require external purchasing capacity, while the trade and finance environment constrains supply
Trade and sanctions
The U.S. economic embargo and additional bilateral sanctions restrict most U.S. transactions, with specified licenses and exemptions; Cuba also trades with other countries [14]
Principal partners
China, Spain, Venezuela and other European and American economies are significant trading and financing connections; partner rankings depend on commodity scope and year
Economic interpretation
Historical official-dollar GDP should not be divided by an independently reported 2025 resident count to manufacture an apparently current GDP-per-capita figure

People and Society

Official language
Spanish is the official language of the republic, with distinct Cuban pronunciation, vocabulary and regional varieties
Other languages
Haitian Creole, English and other languages are present in migrant, tourism, educational and diaspora communities; Lucumí survives principally in religious and cultural contexts
Population backgrounds
The population reflects Indigenous, European, African and other ancestry and cultural formation; historical colonial color categories are not precise contemporary identity measures
Religious landscape
Roman Catholicism, Protestant communities, Afro-Cuban religions, nonaffiliation and smaller faith traditions coexist; the country lacks a recent authoritative religion census [13]
Catholic identity estimates
Church and external surveys have published varying estimates, including an approximately 60% Catholic identification estimate in a 2023 U.S. government report; it is not an official 2026 census figure [13]
Afro-Cuban religious traditions
Lucumí / Regla de Ocha (often called Santería), Ifá, Palo and other practices may coexist with Catholic identification; overlapping practices should not be summed as mutually exclusive groups [13]
Other faiths
Protestant denominations, Jehovah's Witnesses, Judaism, Islam, Buddhism and other communities have a smaller recorded presence [13]
National culture
Son, rumba, danzón, mambo, salsa-related traditions, trova, jazz, ballet and literature contribute to Cuba's cultural prominence
Cuisine and agriculture
Rice, beans, cassava, plantains, pork, coffee and regional seafood characterize varied food practices, although availability fluctuates under economic constraints
Education
Long-established public schooling and universities have supported widespread literacy; resource shortages have strained school facilities and staffing
Healthcare
National public healthcare and biomedical institutions operate amid severe shortages of supplies, medicines and specialist staffing in recent years
Population aging
Declining births, outward migration and increased shares of older people create pressure on pensions, health services and the labor force [3]
Urban and rural settings
Havana and other cities have dense neighborhoods and multifamily housing, while agriculture and smaller settlements remain important in central and eastern provinces
Diaspora
Cuban communities abroad, particularly in the United States, Spain and other countries, influence migration networks, remittances and cultural exchange
Heritage
Nine UNESCO World Heritage properties as listed by the World Heritage Convention; seven cultural and two natural [8]

Religious affiliations overlap: a person may identify as Catholic while also observing Afro-Cuban religious practices. Lack of a current comprehensive religious census rules out a single definitive percentage breakdown.

Government and Institutions

Constitutional form
Unitary socialist republic governed under the 2019 Constitution; government is organized through the National Assembly, Council of State, president, prime minister and Council of Ministers [9]
Governing political system
The Communist Party of Cuba is constitutionally designated the superior leading political force; national elections do not operate as competitive multiparty parliamentary contests [9]
Head of state
President Miguel Díaz-Canel Bermúdez, in office as president since October 2019; re-elected by the National Assembly in April 2023 [11] [9]
Head of government
Prime Minister Manuel Marrero Cruz, appointed in December 2019 and continuing in office in the 2026 reference [11]
National legislature
Unicameral National Assembly of People's Power (Asamblea Nacional del Poder Popular) [10]
Assembly statutory membership
470 statutory seats following the 2023 change; current occupied seats can vary with vacancies, so membership is distinct from the statutory number [10]
Most recent national assembly election
March 26, 2023; all 470 approved candidates were elected for 470 seats; the IPU notes the constitutional role of the Communist Party [9]
Presidential selection
President elected by the National Assembly, not directly through a nationwide competitive presidential ballot [9]
Executive ministries
Council of Ministers led by the prime minister with the president exercising constitutionally significant authority
Justice system
People's Supreme Court and subordinate courts, with a judicial framework defined by the constitution and subsequent legislation
Administrative divisions
15 provinces and the special municipality of Isla de la Juventud, with local organs of People's Power [15]
Local governance
Municipal assemblies and local executives operate within the national constitutional and administrative system
Civil and political rights
Constitutional guarantees, restrictions on organized political opposition, protest and press freedoms, and international human-rights criticism require treatment as distinct legal and empirical questions [16]
Communist Party chronology
The revolutionary movement consolidated the one-party political order during the 1960s; the present constitution codifies the Party's leading function
State and religion
Cuba is not constitutionally a confessional state; religious practice is protected formally while independent observers report regulatory and practical restrictions [13]

Description of the governing system follows its constitutional structure. Formal electoral procedures should be distinguished from the absence of multiparty competition.

International Profile

United Nations
Founding member admitted October 24, 1945 [12]
Geographic integration
Caribbean, Latin American and broader Global South diplomacy; island location gives strategic importance to shipping and U.S.–Caribbean relations
Regional organizations
Participates in the Association of Caribbean States, Community of Latin American and Caribbean States (CELAC), and other regional forums
Caribbean Community relationship
Cuba is not a CARICOM full member; it maintains cooperation and summit-level relations with CARICOM governments
Non-Aligned Movement
Long-standing member; chaired the movement in 1979–1983 and 2006–2009
International trade framework
WTO member since the organization's founding in 1995; participates in multilateral goods-trade rules [7]
United States relations
Bilateral diplomatic relations were restored in 2015, but the U.S. embargo and related restrictions remain in force; licensed trade is a separate category [14]
Overseas medical cooperation
Cuban public-sector medical missions operate abroad; some arrangements are politically and legally disputed, including labor rights and compensation practices [16]
Major overseas connections
Relations with China, Russia, the European Union, Latin American states and Caribbean governments shape finance, energy and diplomatic activity
Migration and diaspora
International emigration significantly affects population reporting, remittances and foreign relations; domestic effective-resident and international estimates diverge [3]
Climate cooperation
Hurricane forecasting, coastal adaptation and ecosystem protection are major public-policy and regional-science priorities
UNESCO membership and sites
Nine inscribed World Heritage properties, including Old Havana and its Fortification System, Trinidad and the Valley de los Ingenios, and two national parks [8]
Geopolitical caveat
Economic policy, sanctions impact and human-rights conditions are contested; factual descriptions should distinguish government claims, official statistics, and external evaluations

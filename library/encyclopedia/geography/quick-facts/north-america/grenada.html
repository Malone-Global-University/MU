Country Overview

Grenada is a small sovereign island state in the southeastern Caribbean consisting of the main island of Grenada, Carriacou, Petite Martinique and several smaller islands. Longstanding Indigenous Caribbean societies were followed by French and British colonial regimes and a plantation economy dependent on enslaved African labor. The state became independent in 1974 as a parliamentary constitutional monarchy. Its later history includes the 1979 revolution, a violent political crisis and foreign military intervention in 1983, and a subsequent restoration of elected parliamentary government. Modern livelihoods rely on tourism, construction, services, agriculture and specialty spices, particularly nutmeg and mace. Grenada is especially exposed to hurricanes, demonstrated by Hurricane Beryl’s severe damage to Carriacou and Petite Martinique in 2024. A new general election on November 5, 2026 yielded a preliminary 14-of-15 seat victory for Prime Minister Dickon Mitchell’s National Democratic Congress.

Identity and State Formation

Official country name
Grenada, independent Commonwealth realm comprising Grenada, Carriacou and Petite Martinique [8]
Capital city
St. George's on the southwestern coast of the largest island; the metropolitan and port center is distinct from the smaller islands [2]
Regional classification
Americas / Caribbean in UN M49; classified in the MGU North America regional index [3]
Indigenous historical foundations
Indigenous communities including Kalinago and earlier Arawakan-language populations occupied the islands before European colonization; terminology varies across historical sources
European colonial competition
French settlement expanded during the seventeenth century; sovereignty passed to Britain under the 1763 Treaty of Paris with later conflicts
Plantation development
Sugar and later agricultural estates relied heavily on enslaved African labor, shaping population, wealth and land-use systems
Emancipation
British imperial abolition of slavery in the 1830s transformed legal status but did not remove plantation-era social and property inequalities
Associated state
Internal self-government expanded under British-associated-state arrangements in 1967, preceding sovereign independence
Independence
February 7, 1974, from the United Kingdom; Grenada retained the Crown in a separate Grenadian constitutional capacity [14]
National Independence Day
February 7, an annual observance marking constitutional independence rather than the 1979 revolutionary change
Grenadian Revolution
March 1979 brought the New Jewel Movement to power under Maurice Bishop, displacing the administration of Eric Gairy [14]
October 1983 crisis
Bishop and other leaders were killed following an internal coup; a United States-led military intervention followed later that month amid regional controversy [14]
Return to electoral government
Parliamentary elections resumed in 1984, and subsequent governments have changed through elections under the constitutional system
Constitutional instrument
The Grenada Constitution Order 1973 provided the legal framework in force at independence and remains central to the parliamentary state [6]
National flag
Red-bordered field with yellow stars, green and yellow quarters and a central star; nutmeg symbolism reflects the island's historical spice trade
National anthem
Hail Grenada, adopted for the independent state and performed at civic ceremonies
United Nations admission
September 17, 1974, after the February independence declaration [2]
ISO identifiers
GD (alpha-2), GRD (alpha-3), 308 (numeric); country-code top-level internet domain .gd [3]

Historical dates distinguish February 1974 independence, the 1979 revolutionary change, the 1983 crisis and subsequent parliamentary elections. These events are not different dates for the same act of sovereignty.

Geography and Population

Surface area
345 km² in the UNData country statistical profile, including the principal island and associated islands; land-only tables may report a different rounded figure [2]
Caribbean position
Southern Windward Islands north of Trinidad and Tobago and southwest of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, along the eastern Caribbean island arc
Principal landmasses
Island of Grenada, Carriacou and Petite Martinique; smaller nearby islets form part of the territorial setting
Main-island relief
Volcanic mountains, forested ridges and short river valleys dominate the interior, while settlements cluster on lower coastal slopes
Highest point
Mount Saint Catherine reaches approximately 840 m, above the mountainous interior and watershed areas
Geothermal origins
Ancient volcanic activity produced crater lakes, fertile soils and rugged terrain, although there is no continuously erupting volcano on the main island
Grand Etang
Crater lake and surrounding forest reserve inland from St. George's, important for biodiversity, tourism and highland water catchments
Major settlements
St. George's, Grenville, Gouyave, Victoria, Sauteurs and Hillsborough on Carriacou, with smaller villages along coastal roads
Administrative parishes
Six parishes on the main island: Saint Andrew, Saint David, Saint George, Saint John, Saint Mark and Saint Patrick
Carriacou and Petite Martinique
A separate dependency and local administrative area, not a seventh main-island parish; communities maintain strong maritime cultural ties
Climate
Tropical marine conditions with a wetter season, warmer coastal areas and appreciably wetter uplands; annual storm exposure varies greatly
Hurricane Beryl impact
July 2024 storm damage was especially severe on Carriacou and Petite Martinique, affecting housing, medical facilities, power and transport [11]
2025 international population
117,303 in the World Bank 2025 annual country series, not the same statistical product as a full national enumeration [1]
2022 preliminary census
109,021 people in the 2022 preliminary population and housing count published in CARICOM's statistical census archive [4]
Population change
Approximately 0.1% in the 2025 World Bank annual population-growth series; modest net migration shifts materially affect small-island totals [1]
Population density
Approximately 340 people per km² by dividing the World Bank 2025 population by the UN 345 km² total area; a national average, not a parish-level statistic [1] [2]
Life expectancy
Approximately 75 years at birth in the World Bank 2024 national series, a period measure that predates the 2026 editorial cutoff [1]
Coastal systems
Beaches, bays, coral reefs, seagrass beds and mangrove areas support visitor activities and artisanal fisheries
Natural resources
Agricultural soils, marine living resources, scenic landscapes and geothermal potential; natural-resource valuations depend strongly on environmental management
Environmental exposure
Tropical cyclones, landslides, drought, flooding, coral bleaching and rising coastal vulnerability threaten tourism facilities, housing and utilities
UNESCO inscription status
Grenada has zero inscribed World Heritage properties as of November 2026; nominated or tentative properties are not listed inscriptions [12]

Population and area: the preliminary 2022 census count, 2025 international modeled estimate, UN surface-area measure and land-only area figures have distinct origins. The calculated density should not be treated as a measured parish statistic.

Economy and International Trade

Economic model
Small, open island economy where tourism, construction, real estate, education services, government, retail, fisheries and farming interact across seasonal cycles
Nominal GDP
Approximately US$1.42 billion in 2025 in World Bank current-dollar country data; the 2024 UNCTAD figure belongs to a different period [1]
GDP per capita
US$12,106.9 in 2025 in the World Bank series, which should not be interpreted as median household income [1]
Real GDP growth
4.4% in 2025 according to the World Bank annual volume-growth series; other contemporaneous World Bank economic commentary uses a different 2025 estimate [1]
2025 consumer-price inflation
0.6% in the World Bank annual consumer-price series, not a measure of every imported-food price or household basket [1]
2025 modeled unemployment
No current World Bank headline ILO modeled rate is displayed for Grenada; avoid substituting an unverified yearless percentage [1]
Remittance ratio
Personal remittances received were approximately 3.3% of GDP in 2025 according to the World Bank series [1]
National currency
East Caribbean dollar (XCD), the shared money of the Eastern Caribbean Currency Union, rather than a Grenada-issued independent dollar [13]
Exchange-rate parity
The East Caribbean dollar is officially pegged at EC$2.70 to US$1; individual cash, card and bank transactions may include fees or spreads [13]
Monetary policymaker
Eastern Caribbean Central Bank (ECCB) manages regional monetary policy and the currency arrangement for participating member states [13]
Tourism economy
Stayover visitors, cruise passengers, resorts, marine recreation, restaurants, transport and cultural attractions generate revenue, employment and service exports
Construction and property
Hotel investments, reconstruction and public capital works support local construction and related service demand but also raise import needs
Higher education and health services
The St. George's University complex and associated services attract international students, instructors and related economic activity
Agricultural identity
Nutmeg, mace, cocoa, tropical fruit, root crops and other specialty produce link small growers and processors to export and domestic markets
Nutmeg and mace
Distinct products from the same spice tree, historically central to Grenada's Spice Isle reputation; volumes fluctuate with cultivation and storm impacts
Fisheries
Tuna and other fish products contribute to domestic food supply and limited export sales; marine-resource rules and weather affect landed quantities
2024 merchandise exports
About US$33 million in 2024 under UNCTAD's general profile for merchandise trade; not the value of service receipts [5]
2024 merchandise imports
About US$633 million in the same UNCTAD merchandise series, reflecting dependence on foreign fuels, food, machinery and manufactured products [5]
2024 merchandise deficit
Approximately US$600 million in rounded 2024 UNCTAD goods totals; this is not the full balance-of-payments current-account deficit [5]
2024 services exports
About US$789 million of exports of services in 2024, a far larger foreign-receipts category than goods exports [5]
2024 services imports
Approximately US$429 million in 2024 under the corresponding UNCTAD services series, creating a services surplus on rounded figures [5]
Travel share of service exports
Travel accounted for about 85% of 2024 service exports in UNCTAD classifications, reflecting Grenada's tourism orientation [5]
Merchandise export composition
Spices, cocoa, fish products, agricultural produce and some manufactured and re-exported goods contribute to a relatively narrow goods basket [5]
Import requirements
Refined petroleum, construction materials, equipment, food and consumer manufactures create a large merchandise import requirement
Energy and resilience investments
Fuel-import exposure supports policy interest in renewable electricity, grid modernization and storm-resilient infrastructure
Citizenship-by-investment
Approved investment-linked citizenship programs can generate fiscal and real-estate activity, alongside international due-diligence and governance concerns
Development constraints
Storm-related reconstruction, tourism concentration, household costs, public debt management and climate adaptation affect development strategy

People and Society

Official language
English is the language of state administration, legislation, courts and the public education system
Everyday speech
Grenadian English Creole is widely used in informal conversation alongside standard English; older French Creole influences remain part of local heritage
Historical French-language influence
Place names and vocabulary reflect French colonization, religious traditions and long-standing Antillean networks
Population and ancestry
Many residents identify with African-Caribbean heritage, alongside mixed-heritage, South Asian and other family histories; self-identification is not a single racial category
2022 census reference
The preliminary 2022 count was 109,021 residents; it should not be treated as a 2025 or 2026 population projection [4]
Historical Protestant shares
The 2011 government religious estimate reported 49.2% Protestant denominations collectively, including Pentecostal, Adventist and Anglican communities [10]
Historical Roman Catholic share
Approximately 36% reported Roman Catholic affiliation in the same 2011 estimate; the figure is not a verified current share [10]
Other religious identities
Rastafarian, Jehovah's Witness, Hindu, Muslim and other small communities are present; 2011 reporting also included no affiliation [10]
Religious methodology
Affiliation records identification, not levels of worship attendance; the 2011 composition should not be applied to the 2025 population denominator
Schooling
Public and private schools operate across the islands; higher education includes domestic tertiary pathways and internationally oriented institutions
Healthcare and access
Public hospitals and clinics serve the population, while small-island transport and specialist capacity can make access difficult across the island group
Cultural festivals
Spicemas carnival, music competitions, traditional masquerade, dance and community celebrations remain important forms of cultural expression
Musical traditions
Soca, calypso, steelpan, drumming and other Caribbean music traditions form part of national and diasporic cultural exchange
Cuisine
Oil down, a one-pot dish featuring breadfruit, coconut milk and other ingredients, is strongly identified with Grenadian food culture
Maritime culture
Fishing, sailing, inter-island transport and Carriacou's distinctive music, boat-building and festival traditions link the islands
Diaspora networks
Grenadian communities overseas maintain family ties, philanthropic associations and return-migration connections, particularly with the United Kingdom, United States and Canada
Disaster recovery
The 2024 Beryl destruction created major demands for housing recovery, education continuity, health support and community-led rebuilding [11]
Sports
Cricket, athletics, football and marine sports are prominent; international accomplishments coexist with grassroots school and community participation

Religion caution: the cited 2011 affiliation breakdown is historical. The 2022 census preliminary population is a newer count but is not evidence that 2011 religious percentages remained unchanged.

Government and Institutions

System of government
Parliamentary democracy and constitutional monarchy under Grenada's independence-era constitution, with the Crown constitutionally separate from the United Kingdom [6]
Head of state
King Charles III, in his independent capacity as King of Grenada, represented domestically by a governor-general [8]
Governor-general
Dame Cécile La Grenade, serving as the monarch's representative as of the November 2026 editorial cutoff [8]
Prime minister
Dickon Mitchell, leader of the National Democratic Congress, first assumed office in June 2022 and led the party into the November 2026 election [8] [9]
Constitutional executive
Prime minister and Cabinet exercise government leadership, with appointments and parliamentary responsibilities governed by the Constitution [6]
Parliament
Crown, appointed Senate and elected House of Representatives constitute the national Parliament [6]
House representation
Fifteen elected constituency seats, with a Speaker as a distinct parliamentary office; the government website describes 16 House members including the Speaker [7]
Senate membership
Thirteen appointed senators, chosen under constitutionally specified advice and consultation arrangements rather than a nationwide Senate election [6]
General election of 2022
National Democratic Congress obtained nine of the fifteen elected seats and formed the government under Dickon Mitchell [9]
General election date 2026
Election held Thursday, November 5, 2026, before the previously anticipated 2027 deadline [9]
2026 preliminary outcome
Early consolidated results show NDC winning 14 of 15 constituencies and New National Party winning one; the tally remains expressly preliminary at this editorial cutoff [9]
Result certification caution
Election-night reporting and provisional constituency returns must not be confused with every later legal certification, appointment or parliamentary swearing-in [9]
Constituency election system
House members are chosen in single-seat constituencies; composition of the next Senate follows constitutional appointment rules
Local administration
Six main-island parishes and Carriacou–Petite Martinique form the principal administrative geography, with central ministries providing much service delivery
Courts and law
Eastern Caribbean Supreme Court structures and common-law traditions operate within the regional judiciary; appellate arrangements are distinct from national executive offices
Constitutional rights
Legal protections address fundamental rights and freedoms, subject to constitutional procedures and judicial review [6]
Emergency governance
Natural-disaster management involves national agencies, local community systems and regional disaster-response cooperation [11]
Political competition
The NDC and NNP have dominated recent national elections, but party competition and legislative oversight must be assessed with dated results

Election status as of November 6, 2026: local reporting describes the 14–1 result as preliminary. The elected lower house and appointed Senate are different institutions. Updated cabinet posts and final certified returns require separate subsequent verification.

International Profile

United Nations membership
Admitted September 17, 1974, following constitutional independence earlier that year [2]
CARICOM
Participates in Caribbean Community political, trade, educational and diplomatic cooperation
Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States
Member of the OECS, coordinating economic, legal and administrative policies across participating eastern Caribbean territories
Eastern Caribbean Currency Union
Uses the East Caribbean dollar with a shared central bank and fixed exchange-rate regime [13]
Commonwealth of Nations
Independent Commonwealth member; Crown status is a national constitutional arrangement and does not subordinate Grenada to British domestic rule
Organization of American States
Participates in regional inter-American diplomacy and democratic-governance institutions
International trade governance
Member of the WTO and relevant international financial institutions; small size affects negotiating priorities and statistical coverage
Climate diplomacy
As a small island developing state, Grenada advocates for resilience finance, adaptation and reduced disaster vulnerability
Hurricane response cooperation
Caribbean disaster agencies, neighboring governments and international organizations contributed to the response following Hurricane Beryl [11]
UNESCO World Heritage
Zero inscribed UNESCO World Heritage properties; the St. George Historic District and other tentative entries are proposals, not inscriptions [12]
Regional mobility
Connections to Trinidad and Tobago, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, Barbados and other island states support education, work and trade
Standard time zone
Atlantic Standard Time UTC−4 year-round, without a normal seasonal clock change
International dialing code
+1 473 under the North American Numbering Plan; internet country-code domain is .gd
Foreign policy challenges
Disaster exposure, access to concessional financing, tourism market conditions and international financial scrutiny shape diplomatic engagement

Country Overview

Saint Kitts and Nevis, formally the Federation of Saint Christopher and Nevis, is a two-island sovereign state in the Leeward Islands of the eastern Caribbean. The volcanic islands lie across the Narrows strait, and their coastlines, towns, agricultural landscapes and protected heritage sites reflect centuries of Indigenous occupation, European colonization, enslavement, emancipation and migration. The federation achieved independence from the United Kingdom on September 19, 1983. Saint Kitts hosts the federal capital, Basseterre, while Nevis has its own island administration and elected assembly within the federation. Tourism, financial and professional services, construction, manufacturing and citizenship-by-investment receipts influence its economy. The profiles below distinguish the World Bank annual population series from the separately counted 2022 national census and report merchandise exports separately from travel and other services.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Federation of Saint Christopher and Nevis; Saint Kitts and Nevis is the widely used common name [5]
Capital and principal centers
Basseterre on Saint Kitts is federal capital; Charlestown on Nevis is the island's principal administrative town [5]
Continental classification
North America within the MGU continent index; Caribbean under the UN M49 regional classification [12]
Indigenous foundations
Kalinago and earlier Indigenous communities inhabited and traversed the Lesser Antilles before European colonization
Early European encounter
Columbus encountered the islands during his 1493 Caribbean voyage; later European names and claims did not erase existing Indigenous settlements
English colonial foothold
English colonization of Saint Kitts began in 1623 and became a foundation for subsequent English settlement in the region
French and English competition
Colonial forces competed for Saint Kitts, and the island experienced repeated imperial struggles and partitions in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries
Sugar plantation society
Sugar estates relied heavily on enslaved Africans and transformed landownership, labor, demography and the islands' export economy
Colonial Nevis
Nevis developed as a separate plantation island with distinctive social institutions, port connections and later territorial political interests
Emancipation
Slavery was abolished under British imperial legislation in the 1830s, followed by contested changes in labor and plantation relations
British Leeward Islands
The islands participated in shifting British colonial structures and regional administration, rather than forming an unchanged independent entity throughout colonial rule
Associated-state period
Saint Christopher–Nevis–Anguilla obtained associated-state status in 1967; Anguilla separated politically from the arrangement and is not part of the independent federation
Independence date
September 19, 1983: the federation became independent with a written Constitution and its own sovereign Commonwealth realm [6]
National day
Independence Day, September 19; cultural commemorations and annual observances are separate from the formal 1983 statehood date [5]
Federal constitutional identity
The 1983 Constitution established national institutions and legally distinctive arrangements for the island of Nevis [6]
1998 Nevis referendum
A Nevis secession proposal received majority support but failed to meet the constitutionally required two-thirds threshold; the island remained within the federation
Contemporary federal government
Prime Minister Terrance Drew heads the administration formed after the August 2022 general election [7]
National flag and symbols
The flag adopted at independence has green, red and black fields divided by a yellow-edged diagonal bearing two white stars
Demonym
Kittitian refers especially to Saint Kitts; Nevisian to Nevis; both are citizens of the federation
UN membership
Admitted to the United Nations September 23, 1983, shortly after independence [2]
ISO codes
KN (alpha-2), KNA (alpha-3), 659 (numeric), country-code web domain .kn [12]

Independence and federation: September 19, 1983 established a sovereign federation of two islands, but it did not make Nevis a separately sovereign country. The associated-state history and Anguilla’s political separation preceded independence.

Geography and Population

Location
A twin-island state in the northern Lesser Antilles and Leeward Islands, east of the Caribbean Sea and west of the Atlantic
National surface area
261 km² in United Nations country-area reporting, with other sources sometimes rounding to about 260 km² [2]
Saint Kitts
Larger and more populous island; coastal settlements encircle a volcanic interior and the southeastern peninsula contains important tourism and marine areas
Nevis
Smaller island southeast of Saint Kitts with a central volcanic cone, lowland villages and protected and cultivated land
Inter-island passage
The Narrows separates the islands; ferries and small craft are important for movement, commerce and government service delivery
Highest elevation
Mount Liamuiga on Saint Kitts rises approximately 1,156 metres and dominates the island's central volcanic landscape [5]
Nevis volcanic cone
Nevis Peak rises about 985 metres; steep slopes create localized rainfall, catchments, erosion and settlement patterns [5]
Climate
Tropical maritime climate with prevailing trade winds, seasonal rains and temperatures moderated near the coast
Hurricane season
Exposure to North Atlantic tropical cyclones generally extends from June through November, though storms do not occur on a fixed annual schedule
Geological hazards
Both islands have volcanic origins; monitoring and disaster planning must address severe rainfall, landslides and other geological processes
Marine ecology
Coral reefs, sea-grass beds, fisheries, mangroves and coastal habitats support tourism, marine livelihoods and shoreline protection
Rainforest and watersheds
Forested upper slopes act as catchment zones and hold biodiversity important for water supply and landscape management
Settlement geography
Basseterre is the federal capital and major port, while Charlestown is the principal town on Nevis
Parish administration
The federation comprises fourteen parishes, nine on Saint Kitts and five on Nevis; parishes are not equivalent to sovereign islands
World Bank population
46,922 residents in the 2025 World Bank annual population series, a modelled or compiled international estimate [1]
2022 national census
National 2022 census enumerated 51,320 persons, with 38,138 on Saint Kitts and 13,182 on Nevis, using national census definitions [3]
2011 census comparison
The 2022 census report compares 51,320 with 47,195 in 2011, describing an 8.7% increase between enumerations [3]
Population-series mismatch
The 2025 World Bank series is lower than the 2022 national enumeration; reporting vintages, coverage and methodology differ and the two must not be presented as interchangeable [3] [1]
Approximate density
Roughly 180 people per km² using the 2025 World Bank annual population divided by the UN 261 km² surface area; census-based density would be higher [1] [2]
Urban concentration
Much settlement and economic activity lies around lower coastal belts, while central slopes have more restrictive terrain
Transport infrastructure
Robert L. Bradshaw International Airport serves Saint Kitts, Vance W. Amory International Airport serves Nevis, and ports connect the islands internationally [5]
Environment and land use
Housing and tourism development compete for coastal land, while watershed, waste-disposal and marine protection require island-scale planning
Regional proximity
Nearby island jurisdictions include Saint Eustatius, Saba, Antigua and Barbuda and Montserrat; no international land border exists
UNESCO heritage landscape
Brimstone Hill Fortress National Park is the federation's single inscribed UNESCO World Heritage property, recognized in 1999 [11]

Population scope: The 2022 national census reported 51,320 noninstitutional private-household residents, while the World Bank estimated 46,922 for 2025. These series have different population conventions and reference periods; the difference should not be described as a direct measured population decline.

Economy and International Trade

Economic classification
Small-island services-oriented economy dependent on external demand and sensitive to global travel, energy prices and climate hazards
Nominal GDP
Approximately US$1.1835 billion in 2025, World Bank current-price U.S.-dollar series [1]
GDP per capita
US$25,223.0 in 2025 (current U.S. dollars); this is economy-wide output divided by population, not average wages or median income [1]
Real GDP growth
2.7% during 2025 in the World Bank annual series; this differs from some regional GDP estimates with other data vintages [1]
Consumer-price data vintage
World Bank country summary's displayed consumer inflation is 3.6% for 2023, not an independently verified 2025 or 2026 inflation rate [1]
Currency
Eastern Caribbean dollar (XCD), issued by the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank for participating currency-union economies [13]
Exchange-rate arrangement
The XCD is maintained at an established fixed relationship of EC$2.70 per US$1, with policy coordinated regionally [13]
Service-sector importance
Accommodation, restaurants, transportation, professional services, public administration and domestic retail are important contributors to output and jobs
Tourism
Cruise visits, overnight accommodation, heritage tourism and marine recreation produce external receipts, but visitor arrivals are not the same statistic as tourism earnings
Cruise concentration
Basseterre's port infrastructure draws calls from major Caribbean cruise itineraries; volumes vary with schedules, season and weather
Nevis tourism profile
Smaller accommodation properties, heritage estates, beaches and local attractions support a distinct island visitor economy
Citizenship by investment
The federation operates a citizenship-by-investment framework whose fiscal and investment implications are separate from merchandise export receipts
Financial and professional services
Domestic banking, insurance and offshore-facing professional activities operate within supervisory, tax and compliance frameworks
Manufacturing
Selected electronics, fabricated goods and smaller industrial facilities generate exports despite the limited domestic market
Historic sugar transformation
The closure of the state sugar industry in 2005 marked a major restructuring away from large-scale traditional cane production
Agriculture
Fruit, vegetables, root crops and animal husbandry support local consumption and small-scale commerce, with limited export quantities
Fisheries
Marine harvesting and related small businesses operate within resource-management constraints and vulnerability to storm damage
2024 merchandise exports
About US$31 million in the UNCTAD 2024 international merchandise-trade series; not a 2025 result [4]
2024 merchandise imports
About US$368 million in the same UNCTAD 2024 series; imports greatly exceeded goods exports [4]
2024 merchandise trade balance
Approximate US$337 million deficit calculated within UNCTAD's paired 2024 merchandise series; excludes services and capital flows [4]
2024 services exports
Approximately US$471 million in the UNCTAD 2024 services-trade series, reflecting the economic importance of international services [4]
2024 services imports
Approximately US$239 million in UNCTAD's services series; net services earnings differ from the goods-trade balance [4]
External trade partners
The United States, neighboring Caribbean economies and other international markets are important, although country rankings depend on data series and year
Food and fuel imports
The limited land base means substantial reliance on imported food products, refined fuels, vehicles, construction materials and machinery
Foreign exchange and fiscal risk
Tourist seasonality, external shipping costs, capital inflows and fiscal receipts make annual economic performance sensitive to international conditions

People and Society

Official language
English is the official language of government, law and much formal schooling [5]
Creole speech
English-lexifier Caribbean Creole varieties are widely used informally and coexist with standard English across communities
Historical population origins
African-descended communities, European colonial influences and later Caribbean and other migration streams have shaped the islands' demographic history
Slavery and social structure
The legacies of enslaved plantation labor, land concentration and post-emancipation struggles influence historical memory and social institutions
Census interpretation
National household enumeration and World Bank population estimates are separate instruments and cannot be used to infer identical age or gender shares [3]
Religious traditions
Protestant denominations, Roman Catholic communities and other Christian groups have long-standing roles, with other faiths and non-affiliation also present
Religion measurement
No undated denominational percentages are treated as a current 2026 religious census or a measure of worship attendance
Schooling
Primary and secondary educational institutions operate within national and Nevis-based administrative arrangements, with regional examination links
Tertiary opportunities
Clarence Fitzroy Bryant College and regional higher-education pathways support academic, technical and professional study
Health care
Hospitals, clinics and public health services serve both islands; inter-island access and specialized care can require transport arrangements
Federation and identity
Kittitian and Nevisian community identities coexist with federal nationality and distinct island political institutions
Carnival traditions
Sugar Mas on Saint Kitts and Culturama on Nevis feature different local calendars, performance traditions and community participation
Music
Calypso, soca, steelpan and other Caribbean musical forms contribute to festivals and public life
Cuisine
Dishes use fish, rice, green bananas, breadfruit, saltfish, goat, tropical produce and locally distinctive culinary preparations
Cricket
Cricket remains a major social sport with local competition and broader West Indies regional representation
Other sports
Athletics, football, netball and school-level sports complement cricket and community recreation
Built heritage
Plantation structures, churches, fortifications and Georgian-era urban buildings reflect colonial and postcolonial histories
Public commemorations
Emancipation, independence and national-hero commemorations have different historical meanings and public calendars
Employment and training
Service-sector employment requires hospitality, languages, digital skills, health professions, construction and small-business capabilities
Migration links
Family and professional ties extend to the United Kingdom, United States, Canada and neighboring Caribbean states
Demographic aging
A small population and external migration create planning challenges for labor supply, housing, pensions and older-person care
Disaster resilience
Schools and communities participate in emergency preparedness for severe storms, storm surge and other hazards

Cultural evidence: Denominational traditions and public festivals document social life but do not supply a current percentage distribution of religion, ancestry or language use. Census-based shares should retain their survey questions and date.

Government and Institutions

State system
Sovereign federal parliamentary democracy and constitutional monarchy governed by the Constitution of 1983 [6]
Monarch
King Charles III serves as King of Saint Christopher and Nevis, a constitutional position distinct from the UK government [10]
Governor-General
Dame Marcella Liburd, sworn in February 1, 2023, represents the sovereign in federal constitutional functions [8]
Prime Minister
Dr. Terrance Drew, head of the federal government following the August 2022 election [7]
Cabinet
Prime minister and ministers direct federal executive portfolios, including finance, health, infrastructure, foreign affairs and justice [7]
National Assembly
Unicameral federal legislative chamber with elected representatives and appointed senators under the constitutional framework [6]
Federal electoral seats
Eleven elected constituencies are assigned across the federation, eight on Saint Kitts and three on Nevis, alongside appointed legislative positions [6]
Election system
Westminster-derived constituency voting and government confidence shape the selection of the parliamentary administration
Constitution and rights
Constitutional provisions address personal liberties, legislative authority, judicial organization and the distinctive position of Nevis [6]
Nevis Island Assembly
The island has its own assembly and autonomous administration for designated domestic responsibilities [9]
Premier of Nevis
Mark Brantley leads the Nevis Island Administration, whose island government does not amount to a separate internationally recognized sovereign state [9]
Division of powers
Federal institutions control specified national functions, while Nevis exercises constitutionally recognized authority over certain island matters [6]
Nevis autonomy debate
The constitution contains special Nevis-related provisions, and debates about federal balance and possible separation have continued since independence
1998 constitutional vote
A Nevis referendum on separation failed the two-thirds constitutional approval threshold despite a majority of valid votes favoring the proposal
Judiciary
The Eastern Caribbean Supreme Court structure supports the regional High Court and appellate system for participating states
Legal tradition
Common-law legal structures operate alongside written constitutional provisions and statutes shaped by colonial and independent legislation
Local administration
Parishes and island-level services structure some domestic administration; the separate island of Nevis requires special federal coordination
Public finance
Federal budgetary and debt policy interacts with Nevis administration, tourism receipts and citizenship-by-investment revenues
Electoral administration
Electoral authorities supervise voter registration and general elections under statutes and constitutional requirements
Defense and security
National security, police, border management and disaster agencies perform responsibilities without the scale of a major-state military
Foreign relations
The federation maintains its own embassies, treaty relations and voting rights in international organizations, independent of the United Kingdom
Constitutional distinction
The governor-general represents the monarch, the prime minister leads government, and the Nevis premier is not federal prime minister

Federal architecture: Constitutionally recognized Nevis autonomy and the island Premier coexist with the federal National Assembly and Prime Minister. A Nevis proposal in 1998 did not reach the legally required secession threshold.

International Profile

United Nations membership
Joined September 23, 1983 following independence [2]
Commonwealth membership
Independent Commonwealth realm with its own crown and parliamentary institutions, not a dependency of the United Kingdom
Caribbean Community
CARICOM membership supports regional diplomatic coordination, economic cooperation and development programs [14]
Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States
OECS participation supports regional policy coordination, integration and common institutions [15]
Eastern Caribbean Currency Union
ECCU membership links monetary policy and currency issuance to the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank [13]
World Trade Organization
Participates in multilateral trade frameworks and associated reporting and market-access rules [16]
Regional justice
Eastern Caribbean judicial arrangements provide links between domestic courts and regional appellate institutions
United States relationship
Tourism, investment, migration, diplomatic and financial-integrity questions shape bilateral dealings
United Kingdom relationship
Historical links, migration and Commonwealth institutions remain important without British sovereignty over the independent federation
Neighboring island cooperation
Marine protection, transport, fisheries and hurricane preparedness require cooperation with adjacent Caribbean jurisdictions
Climate diplomacy
Small-island exposure to sea-level rise, coastal storms and costly imported fuel informs international climate negotiations
UNESCO World Heritage
One inscribed cultural property, Brimstone Hill Fortress National Park, inscribed in 1999 [11]
Heritage-list distinction
Proposed Basseterre and other tentative-list sites are not additional inscribed UNESCO World Heritage properties [11]
Citizenship regulation
International visa access and financial due-diligence discussions can affect the country's citizenship-by-investment administration
Standard time
Atlantic Standard Time, UTC−4; no routine nationwide seasonal daylight-saving adjustment
International telephone
+1 869 under the North American Numbering Plan; internet country-code top-level domain .kn
Overseas representation
Diplomatic posts and regional representations cover consular services, investment promotion and intergovernmental negotiation

Heritage method: Brimstone Hill Fortress National Park is one inscribed UNESCO property. Separate tentative-list candidates do not increase the World Heritage count.

Country Overview

Canada is a federal parliamentary democracy and constitutional monarchy extending from the Atlantic to the Pacific and Arctic oceans. It is the world’s second-largest country by total area, with ten provinces, three territories, and a population concentrated mainly in its southern regions. First Nations, Inuit and Métis peoples have histories extending long before European settlement and remain distinct political and cultural communities. Modern Canada began with Confederation in 1867, evolved toward legislative autonomy through the twentieth century, and patriated its Constitution in 1982. Its diverse economy includes services, energy, mineral resources, advanced manufacturing, agriculture and technology. The federal government operates in English and French, while regional, Indigenous and immigrant languages add to the national linguistic landscape.

Identity and State Formation

Official country name
Canada; the constitutional state uses Canada as its formal short name
Capital city
Ottawa, Ontario, seat of the federal Parliament and central executive institutions [2]
Continent / UN M49 subregion
North America / Northern America [11]
Indigenous foundations
First Nations, Inuit and Métis peoples have distinct nations, histories, legal traditions and political relationships that predate the Canadian federal state
French colonial history
French settlement, trade and colonial institutions developed along the St. Lawrence and parts of the Great Lakes and Atlantic regions from the seventeenth century
British colonial transformations
British victory in the Seven Years' War and the 1763 settlement preceded changes in colonial administration and the later formation of British North America
Confederation
July 1, 1867: the British North America Act established a Dominion initially comprising Ontario, Quebec, Nova Scotia and New Brunswick [10]
Expansion of the federation
Other provinces and territories joined or were created over time; Newfoundland entered Confederation in 1949 and Nunavut was established in 1999 [10]
Statute of Westminster
1931: expanded Canada's legislative autonomy, although certain constitutional amendments still involved the British Parliament
Canadian citizenship
Separate Canadian citizenship was established by legislation effective in 1947; citizenship is distinct from the date of Confederation
Constitutional patriation
April 17, 1982: the Constitution Act, 1982 took effect, incorporating the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms and a domestic constitutional amendment procedure [10]
National Day
Canada Day, July 1, commemorates Confederation; it is not an independence day in the single-event sense applicable to some postcolonial states
Constitutional head of state
King Charles III is King of Canada, under a legally separate Canadian Crown [6]
National emblems
Red maple leaf flag, adopted in 1965; the maple leaf is a prominent national symbol, alongside the national anthem O Canada
Official federal languages
English and French; their legal standing at the federal level does not negate Indigenous languages or region-specific language provisions
United Nations membership
Founding member of the United Nations, admitted November 9, 1945 [2]
ISO 3166-1 codes
CA (alpha-2), CAN (alpha-3), 124 (numeric) [11]
Internet domain
.ca country-code domain; Canada participates in North American telephone-numbering arrangements

Constitutional chronology: Confederation in 1867, the Statute of Westminster in 1931 and patriation in 1982 mark different stages of Canada’s constitutional development. None should be substituted uncritically for a single universally agreed independence date. Indigenous political histories precede these federal milestones.

Geography and Population

Surface area
9,984,670 km² including inland waters, consistent with United Nations country data; the land-only figure is smaller [2]
Rank by total area
Second-largest sovereign country in the world by commonly reported total surface area, after Russia
World Bank population
41,651,653 in the 2025 annual World Bank series, an international statistical estimate [1]
Latest national population estimate
41,798,407 as of July 1, 2026, in Statistics Canada's quarterly estimates, released September 23, 2026 [3]
Population methods
World Bank annual estimates and quarterly national estimates have different reference dates, vintages and updating procedures [1] [3]
Population density
Approximately 4.2 people per km² when the 2025 World Bank population is divided by the UN total area; large areas are sparsely populated [1] [2]
Provincial and territorial structure
Ten provinces and three territories, with distinct constitutional and statutory powers
Provinces
Alberta, British Columbia, Manitoba, New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia, Ontario, Prince Edward Island, Quebec and Saskatchewan
Territories
Northwest Territories, Nunavut and Yukon; northern governance arrangements differ from provincial constitutional status
Largest metropolitan regions
Toronto, Montréal and Vancouver; other important urban centers include Calgary, Edmonton, Ottawa–Gatineau, Winnipeg and Québec City
Bordering country
United States, including the Alaska boundary; Canadian coastlines face three ocean basins
Ocean frontiers
Atlantic to the east, Pacific to the west and Arctic to the north, with extensive offshore islands and archipelagos
Major physical regions
Canadian Shield, Great Lakes–St. Lawrence Lowlands, Interior Plains, Appalachian region, Cordillera, Arctic lands and Hudson Bay Lowlands
Mountain ranges
Canadian Rockies, Coast Mountains, Mackenzie Mountains and other Cordilleran systems
Highest summit
Mount Logan in Yukon, approximately 5,959 metres above sea level
Principal inland waters
Great Lakes, Hudson Bay, Great Bear Lake and Great Slave Lake; extensive rivers include the Mackenzie, St. Lawrence, Fraser and Saskatchewan systems
Climate zones
Arctic, subarctic, continental and coastal maritime climates, with considerable variation in temperature and precipitation
Natural resources
Petroleum, natural gas, uranium, potash, hydroelectric potential, timber, fisheries and a wide range of minerals
Environmental pressures
Wildfires, changing sea ice, floods, permafrost thaw, climate-sensitive ecosystems and biodiversity conservation are major national policy issues
Demographic distribution
Most residents live within several hundred kilometres of the United States border; northern settlement is much sparser
Life expectancy
Approximately 82 years at birth in the World Bank's latest available annual series, which predates the 2026 editorial cutoff [1]

Population comparison: The 2025 World Bank estimate and Statistics Canada’s July 1, 2026 quarterly figure are two different series and reference dates. The geographic total includes significant inland water, so density calculated using total area differs from density calculated using land area.

Economy and International Trade

Economic classification
High-income, advanced mixed economy with substantial private activity and federal and provincial public-service sectors
Nominal GDP
US$2.3199 trillion in 2025 in the World Bank current-dollar series [1]
GDP per capita
US$55,697.7 (2025, current U.S. dollars); national output per resident is not the same as median earnings or household disposable income [1]
Real GDP growth
1.7% for calendar 2025 in the cited World Bank annual series [1]
Inflation
Consumer prices rose approximately 2.1% in 2025 in the World Bank annual series [1]
Modeled unemployment
6.9% in 2025 in the ILO-modeled series reported by the World Bank; national survey estimates use related but not identical data concepts [1]
Currency
Canadian dollar (CAD), with a floating exchange rate against the U.S. dollar and other currencies
Central banking
Bank of Canada sets monetary-policy instruments within its legislative mandate; the federal government operates fiscal policy
Economic structure
Large service sector accompanied by energy, resources, construction, agriculture, transportation, aerospace, manufacturing, digital technology and finance
Energy trade
Crude oil, refined petroleum, natural gas and electricity are major export categories; regional production and environmental impacts vary
Mineral and agricultural trade
Gold, aluminum, potash, canola, wheat, lumber and pulp contribute to merchandise trade
Automotive industry
Integrated North American vehicle and parts supply chains link Canadian industrial plants with producers and customers in the United States and Mexico
Merchandise exports
C$779.0 billion in 2025 on the customs-based figures presented by Global Affairs Canada; goods only [4]
Merchandise imports
C$789.0 billion in 2025 on the same customs-based series; goods only [4]
Merchandise trade balance
About C$10.0 billion deficit in 2025 by subtraction of the paired customs-based export and import totals; excludes services [4]
Leading export market
United States is Canada's dominant single-country market for goods; its importance varies by sector and by year [4]
Other trade relationships
European Union, China, Mexico, Japan and other Asia-Pacific economies are important in varying goods and service segments
Services trade
Banking, transportation, education-linked services, tourism, technology and professional services generate international receipts beyond merchandise totals
Free-trade framework
Canada–United States–Mexico Agreement (CUSMA/USMCA) governs much of the North American trade relationship; Canada also has other agreements
Economic policy priorities
Productivity, housing affordability, infrastructure, energy transition, Indigenous economic participation and trade diversification remain prominent debates
Trade measurement
Customs-based merchandise totals differ from balance-of-payments goods figures and must not be combined with U.S.-dollar World Bank GDP without conversion

People and Society

Federal official languages
English and French are the federal government's two official languages, with protections in legislation and constitutional law
Regional language regimes
Quebec has distinctive French-language legislation; New Brunswick is officially bilingual at the provincial level; territorial and Indigenous-language provisions vary
Indigenous peoples
First Nations, Inuit and Métis are constitutionally recognized distinct Aboriginal peoples; identities, treaties and rights are not interchangeable
Indigenous languages
Inuktut, Cree, Ojibwe, Dene languages, Mi'kmaq and many others are spoken or revitalized in different regions
Immigration and diversity
Immigration has shaped Canadian population growth and cities, with large communities tracing origins to Asia, Europe, Africa, the Caribbean and elsewhere
Christian religious identification
53.3% of persons in the 2021 Census reported Christianity; this is not a 2026 estimate of active religious practice [8]
No religious affiliation
34.6% reported no religious affiliation or a secular perspective in the 2021 Census [8]
Other religious traditions
2021 census shares included Muslim 4.9%, Hindu 2.3%, Sikh 2.1%, Buddhist around 1.0%, and Jewish around 0.9% [8]
Census interpretation
Religion, ancestry, language and ethnic identity are distinct census measures and cannot be used as proxies for one another
Education
Primary and secondary education is largely administered by provincial and territorial authorities; universities and colleges operate within regional regulatory frameworks
Health care
Publicly funded provincial and territorial systems deliver medically necessary hospital and physician services under national principles, with important coverage differences
Social development issues
Housing, reconciliation, rural and northern services, affordability, health-system capacity and demographic aging are recurring priorities
Literature and creative sectors
English-, French- and Indigenous-language cultural production includes literature, cinema, broadcasting, visual arts and music
Regional cultures
Atlantic Canada, Quebec, Ontario, the Prairies, Pacific regions and the North have distinct histories, traditions and economic experiences
Demographic aging
Statistics Canada estimated median age at 40.9 years on July 1, 2026; regional age profiles vary [3]

Census method: The 2021 percentages measure reported religious identification, not attendance or belief strength. Demographic statements about Indigenous peoples should preserve distinct nations, languages, treaty histories and governance systems.

Government and Institutions

Constitutional system
Federal parliamentary democracy and constitutional monarchy under a written and unwritten body of constitutional law
Head of state
King Charles III, in his separate capacity as King of Canada [6]
Governor General
Louise Arbour, sworn in June 8, 2026 as the thirty-first Governor General since Confederation, representing the Crown federally [6]
Prime Minister
Mark Carney, Canada's twenty-fourth prime minister, sworn in during March 2025 [5]
Executive authority
Prime minister and cabinet exercise government leadership through the parliamentary system, subject to confidence of the elected House of Commons
Parliament
Bicameral federal Parliament: the elected House of Commons and appointed Senate, together with the Crown [7]
House of Commons
343 constituencies and seats under the representation established for the 2025 federal election; temporary vacancies should not be confused with statutory seats [7]
Senate
105 ordinarily authorized Senate seats, with senators appointed under the constitutional rules, rather than elected nationally [7]
Federal administration
National institutions exercise constitutionally assigned functions including defense, currency, criminal law and certain interprovincial and international matters
Provincial responsibilities
Provinces administer major sectors including education, health services and many local matters under constitutional powers
Territorial administration
Territorial powers derive principally from federal legislation and negotiated devolution arrangements, unlike provincial constitutional jurisdiction
Judiciary
Supreme Court of Canada is the final court of appeal; lower federal, provincial and territorial courts exercise different jurisdictions
Charter of Rights and Freedoms
Entrenched within the Constitution Act, 1982, governing fundamental freedoms and specified constitutional rights [10]
Indigenous constitutional rights
Section 35 of the Constitution Act, 1982 recognizes and affirms existing Aboriginal and treaty rights, with continuing legal and political interpretation [10]
Elections
Parliamentary elections use single-member constituencies and first-past-the-post voting at the federal level
2026 officeholder update
Louise Arbour succeeded Mary Simon as Governor General in June 2026; older online directories naming Simon as current are no longer up to date [6]

Officeholder verification: On June 8, 2026, Louise Arbour became Governor General. The governor general is not the head of government, and the monarch and governor general must not be conflated with the prime minister.

International Profile

United Nations
Founding member, with UN membership dated November 9, 1945 in UNData [2]
G7 and G20
Participates in major international economic coordination and diplomacy through both groupings
North Atlantic Treaty Organization
NATO founding member since 1949; defense and collective-security obligations form part of international policy
Commonwealth
Member of the Commonwealth, while the Canadian Crown remains constitutionally independent from the United Kingdom
OECD
Member of the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development, engaged in comparative policy and economic statistical work
WTO
World Trade Organization member, participating in international trade rules and dispute settlement
North American economy
Canada–United States–Mexico trade framework is central to manufacturing, energy, agriculture and supply chains
UNESCO World Heritage
22 inscribed World Heritage properties in the 2026 UNESCO State Party listing, including cultural, natural and mixed sites [9]
Arctic cooperation
Arctic governance, Indigenous engagement, maritime access and the environmental effects of warming are strategic concerns
Bilateral priority
United States relationship spans a long land border, major two-way trade, energy grids, security and environmental systems
International development
Canadian government and nongovernmental institutions participate in humanitarian, development, climate and peacebuilding activities
Foreign-policy themes
Trade diversification, sovereignty, democratic partnerships, climate policy, human rights and regional security remain important political questions
Time zones
Multiple standard time zones, conventionally UTC−8 through UTC−3:30 in most settled provinces, with some local seasonal and regional exceptions
International dialing code
+1 within the North American Numbering Plan; .ca country-code internet domain

The 22 UNESCO properties include sites associated with Indigenous heritage, geological history, natural ecosystems and transboundary conservation. UNESCO World Heritage inscription is different from designation under Canadian national parks law.

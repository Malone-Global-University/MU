Country Overview

Canada is a federal parliamentary democracy and constitutional monarchy in northern North America. It extends from the Atlantic to the Pacific and Arctic oceans and is organized into ten provinces and three territories. The country developed from Indigenous homelands, French and British colonial territories, and a federal union established on July 1, 1867. Canadian independence evolved through constitutional changes, particularly the Statute of Westminster (1931) and the patriation of the Constitution (1982). English and French are its two federal official languages. [4]

Confederation
July 1, 1867: the British North America Act, 1867 (now the Constitution Act, 1867) established the Dominion of Canada as a federation of four provinces: Ontario, Quebec, Nova Scotia, and New Brunswick. [4]
Legislative autonomy
December 11, 1931: the Statute of Westminster recognized broad legislative autonomy; constitutional amendment authority was not fully transferred at that time. [4]
Constitution patriated
April 17, 1982: the Constitution Act, 1982 came into force, establishing a domestic constitutional amendment framework and entrenching the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms. [14]
National day

Historical context: First Nations, Inuit, and Métis peoples have distinct histories, institutions, and continuing rights in the territories now comprising Canada. European colonization, treaties, the expansion of settler government, the dispossession of Indigenous peoples, and the residential-school system are major parts of Canadian history. Confederation is Canada's central federal founding milestone, but it did not constitute a single, instantaneous declaration of complete independence.

Geographic comparability: The total-area estimate includes inland freshwater; the 2021 Census land-area statistic is a separately defined measure. People and Society

Federal official languages
English and French; both have official status in federal institutions. [7]
Other languages spoken
Indigenous languages, including Cree, Inuktitut, Ojibwe, Dene languages, and others; as well as Punjabi, Mandarin, Cantonese, Arabic, Spanish, Tagalog, and many additional immigrant languages
Knowledge of an official language
98.1% could conduct a conversation in English or French (2021 Census). [7]
Languages used at home
92.9% spoke English or French at home at least regularly (2021 Census); languages may be spoken together, so categories are not mutually exclusive. [7]
Religious composition
Christian affiliation 53.3%; no religious affiliation 34.6%; remaining population affiliated with non-Christian religions or other spiritual traditions (2021 Census). [8]
Major Christian tradition
Roman Catholic affiliation 29.9% of total population (2021 Census); other Christian traditions include United Church, Anglican, Orthodox, Baptist, and Pentecostal communities. [8]
Other religious traditions
Islam (4.9%), Hinduism (2.3%), Sikhism (2.1%), Buddhism (1.0%), Judaism (0.9%), and Indigenous spiritual traditions, among others (2021 Census). [8]
Indigenous population
1,807,250 people (5.0%) identified as Indigenous in the 2021 Census, including First Nations, Métis, and Inuit identities. [10]
Immigrant population
23.0% of the 2021 Census population were immigrants (landed immigrants or permanent residents); this measure does not describe the entire foreign-born or temporary-resident population. [11]
Ethnocultural diversity
Indigenous peoples, descendants of earlier French and British settlements, and communities with origins throughout Europe, Asia, Africa, the Caribbean, Latin America, the Middle East, and the Pacific

Census interpretation: Religious affiliation records self-reported identification and is not necessarily a measure of belief or attendance. Canadian language, immigrant-status, and Indigenous-identity measures use distinct census definitions, which should be retained when comparing countries.

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Federal constitutional monarchy and parliamentary democracy; responsible government under the Westminster parliamentary tradition
Constitutional framework
Constitution Act, 1867; Constitution Act, 1982; and related constitutional laws, principles, and conventions [14]
Head of state
King Charles III, King of Canada (since September 8, 2022). [17]
Governor general
Louise Arbour, representative of the King in Canada (sworn in June 8, 2026; verified November 4, 2026). [9]
Head of government
Prime Minister Mark Carney (in office since March 14, 2025; verified November 4, 2026). [18]
Executive
Prime minister and Cabinet, formally operating within the Crown's constitutional executive authority
Federal Parliament
Bicameral legislature consisting of the Crown, the Senate (105 constitutionally provided seats), and the House of Commons (343 elected constituencies). [19]
Judiciary
Supreme Court of Canada, federal courts, and provincial and territorial courts; common-law and Quebec civil-law traditions
Administrative divisions
10 provinces and 3 territories; municipalities and local governments established under provincial or territorial law [4]
Governmental powers
Constitutional division of federal and provincial powers, territorial self-government arrangements, parliamentary accountability, and constitutional protection of Indigenous and treaty rights
National elections
Federal elections ordinarily follow a fixed-date statutory schedule of every four years, subject to earlier dissolution of Parliament; House of Commons seats are filled by single-member plurality elections.

International Profile

United Nations
Original member; UN Charter entered into force October 24, 1945
Major international memberships
United Nations, NATO, G7, G20, OECD, WTO, Commonwealth of Nations, Organisation internationale de la Francophonie, World Bank, and International Monetary Fund
Regional economic arrangement
Canada–United States–Mexico Agreement (CUSMA; also known as USMCA), in force since July 1, 2020
Major trade relationship
The United States is Canada's largest merchandise export destination; it received 71.7% of Canada's merchandise exports in 2025. [12]
Standard time zones
Pacific, Mountain, Central, Eastern, Atlantic, and Newfoundland; daylight-saving practices differ by province, territory, and locality
International dialing code
+1 (North American Numbering Plan)

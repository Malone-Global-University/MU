Country Overview

The United States of America is a federal constitutional republic occupying much of central North America, with Alaska in the continent's northwest and Hawaii in the Pacific Ocean. It consists of 50 states and the federal District of Columbia, and it exercises sovereignty over several inhabited territories. The country emerged from thirteen British colonies that declared independence in 1776; its present federal constitutional government began operating in 1789. [8]

Historical context: Indigenous peoples inhabited and governed societies across these lands long before European colonization. The United States subsequently expanded across North America through treaties, purchases, war, settlement, and the dispossession of Indigenous nations. Enslavement, abolition, Reconstruction, and struggles over civil and voting rights are central to its institutional history. A single “founding date” cannot capture all stages of state formation.

People and Society

Federal official language
English, designated by presidential executive order on March 1, 2025. This designation is by executive action, not an amendment to the Constitution. [5]
Other languages spoken
Spanish, Chinese languages, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Arabic, French, Korean, and numerous Indigenous and immigrant languages, among others
Language diversity
22.3% of people age 5 and older spoke a language other than English at home (2020–2024 American Community Survey period) [1]
Religious composition
Among U.S. adults in Pew's 2023–2024 survey: Christian 62%; religiously unaffiliated 29%; other religions 7%; remaining approximately 1% did not answer, with percentages subject to rounding. [4]
Christian traditions
Protestant 40% of adults; Catholic 19%; other Christian 3% (Pew, 2023–2024) [4]
Other religious traditions
Judaism, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, Indigenous spiritual traditions, and other faiths; no single faith is established as the national religion by the U.S. Constitution
Population age 65 and older
18.9% (Census QuickFacts, 2025 estimate) [1]
Foreign-born population
14.1% (2020–2024 Census American Community Survey period) [1]
Ethnic and cultural diversity
A multiethnic population including Indigenous peoples; descendants of European, African, Asian, Pacific Islander, and Latin American populations; and immigrant communities from around the world. Race and Hispanic-origin Census categories use distinct, sometimes overlapping definitions.

Survey interpretation: Religious percentages describe surveyed adults, not the entire population or official government registration. U.S. Census race and Hispanic-origin measures should not be summed without accounting for their different definitions.

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Federal presidential constitutional republic; representative democracy
Foundational law
Constitution of the United States, with amendments and constitutional jurisprudence

Executive branch
President, vice president, executive departments, and federal agencies
Legislature
Bicameral U.S. Congress: Senate (100 seats, two per state) and House of Representatives (435 voting seats apportioned among states) [6]
Judiciary
U.S. Supreme Court and lower federal courts; separate state court systems
Administrative divisions
50 states and the federal District of Columbia; inhabited U.S. territories include Puerto Rico, Guam, the U.S. Virgin Islands, American Samoa, and the Northern Mariana Islands
Governmental powers
Federalism, separation of powers, checks and balances, and division of authority among federal, state, local, and territorial governments [6]
National elections
Presidential elections every four years; all voting House seats every two years; Senate seats are elected for staggered six-year terms

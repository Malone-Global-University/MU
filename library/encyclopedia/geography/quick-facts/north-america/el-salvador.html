Country Overview

El Salvador is a republic on the Pacific side of Central America, bordered by Guatemala and Honduras and facing the Gulf of Fonseca. Its densely settled volcanic highlands and coastal plains contain the capital, San Salvador, and the principal commercial and industrial corridors. Indigenous societies, Spanish colonial rule, nineteenth-century coffee production and a twentieth-century civil war shaped the modern state. Independence from Spain was proclaimed with Central America in 1821; a separate national republic consolidated after the federal experiment. The 1992 peace accords ended a prolonged armed conflict. President Nayib Bukele’s government has pursued extensive security and constitutional changes, generating both support for reduced gang violence and international debate over detention, due process and institutional checks. The economy is dollarized and combines manufacturing, trade, services and sizable remittance receipts from Salvadorans overseas.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of El Salvador (República de El Salvador); sovereign state in Central America [2]
Common name and demonym
El Salvador; Salvadoran (salvadoreño or salvadoreña in Spanish)
Capital and seat of government
San Salvador in the metropolitan center of the country; government functions remain separate from the 2023 municipal reorganization [2]
Continent and UN regional code
North America in the MGU geographic index; Central America within the United Nations Americas geographic scheme [3]
Precolonial foundations
Pipil or Nahua-speaking communities, Lenca, Maya-related populations and other Indigenous peoples occupied different regions long before Spanish control
Spanish conquest
Spanish military conquest began during the 1520s amid Indigenous resistance, followed by colonial towns, mission activity and reorganized farming and landholding
Colonial administration
Territory was part of the Spanish Captaincy General of Guatemala and connected through trade and administration to neighboring Central American provinces
Independence from Spain
September 15, 1821, in the joint Central American declaration; this date records the end of Spanish colonial sovereignty, not the founding of the current constitution [11]
Federal period
Joined the Federal Republic of Central America after independence; the federation dissolved through conflicts and withdrawals in the 1830s
Separate republic
The 1841 constitutional moment marked a distinct Salvadoran republic after the collapse of Central American federal union [11]
Coffee economy and social change
Nineteenth-century expansion of export coffee transformed landholding, infrastructure, labor relations and political influence
Twentieth-century authoritarianism
Military-led governments and political repression, including violence against rural communities, shaped the period preceding civil war
Civil war
Armed conflict between state forces and the FMLN guerrilla coalition extended from 1980 through 1992, with major civilian suffering and displacement [11]
Chapultepec Peace Accords
Signed January 16, 1992, establishing major security, political and institutional reforms and pathways for former combatants to participate in electoral life [11]
Present constitutional foundation
Constitution promulgated in December 1983, subsequently amended many times; the 2025–2026 electoral reforms materially changed presidential and electoral rules [5]
National day
Independence Day on September 15, commemorated with other Central American states rather than a distinct post-federal separation date
National flag and emblem
Blue-white-blue national flag with a central coat of arms; the national motto Dios, Unión, Libertad is shown on the emblem
United Nations membership
Original United Nations member, membership dated October 24, 1945 [2]
ISO 3166-1 identifiers
SV (alpha-2), SLV (alpha-3), 222 (numeric); .sv internet country-code domain [3]

Chronology: September 1821 independence from Spain, the post-federal republic in 1841, the 1983 constitutional order and the 1992 peace settlement are different legal and historical milestones. The 2025–2026 reforms are amendments to the present constitutional system, not a new independence event.

Geography and Population

UN total surface area
21,041 km² in the UN statistical profile; land-only figures may differ and should not be interchanged with the total surface measure [2]
Geographic position
Entirely on the Pacific side of Central America, between Guatemala to the west and Honduras to the north and east; the Gulf of Fonseca borders the southeast
Coastal frontage
Pacific Ocean beaches, estuaries, mangroves and the Gulf of Fonseca; El Salvador has no coast on the Caribbean Sea
Administrative departments
Fourteen departments divided into municipalities; changes adopted in 2023 reduced the count to 44 municipalities from 262 [4]
Urban distribution
San Salvador metropolitan area is the most prominent urban concentration, while Santa Ana, San Miguel and Sonsonate are major regional centers
Principal mountain systems
Volcanic Cordillera and adjoining interior highlands separate coastal plains from valleys and northern upland landscapes
Highest summit
Cerro El Pital, approximately 2,730 m on the northern border with Honduras, exceeds the summits of the country's best-known volcanoes
Volcanoes
Santa Ana (Ilamatepec), San Salvador, San Vicente, Izalco and other volcanoes help shape topography, soils, hazard planning and national identity
Volcanic and seismic hazards
Active faults, volcanic systems and destructive historic earthquakes create material housing and infrastructure risks throughout the country
Main river
Río Lempa, with a basin extending across national borders, is central to hydropower, irrigation, water supply and environmental management
Principal lakes
Lake Ilopango, Lake Coatepeque and Lake Güija; volcanic caldera settings distinguish several important inland water bodies
Wetlands and coastal ecosystems
Jiquilisco Bay, coastal mangroves, lagoons and marine habitats support fisheries and biodiversity while facing development and climate pressures
Climate
Tropical conditions with a wet season generally during May to October, a drier season in boreal winter, and strong elevation-related temperature differences
Hurricane and rain hazards
Tropical storms can cause flooding and landslides even when their centers are elsewhere; coastal settlements also face storm surge and erosion
2025 World Bank population
6,365,503 people in the 2025 annual World Bank estimate; it is a modeled population series, not an enumeration on a single census day [1]
Population growth
About 0.4% annually in the 2025 World Bank series, influenced by fertility changes, mortality and migration estimates [1]
Illustrative density
About 303 people per km² by dividing the 2025 World Bank estimate by 21,041 km²; this is calculated from rounded figures rather than an official settlement-level statistic [1] [2]
Migration balance
Net migration of approximately −23,934 in the World Bank 2025 series; measures reflect estimation conventions and should not be interpreted as a literal count of all border crossings [1]
Life expectancy
Approximately 72 years in the World Bank 2024 series; this is a period indicator and not a forecast of lifespan for a particular individual [1]
Land use and agriculture
Coffee thrives at elevations, while sugarcane, maize, beans, pasture and settlements compete for lower-elevation land and water
UNESCO World Heritage
Joya de Cerén Archaeological Site is El Salvador's single inscribed World Heritage property; sites on a tentative list are not already inscribed [10]

Density method: 6,365,503 divided by 21,041 gives approximately 303 residents per km²; that figure does not describe each department or the metropolitan population density. The UN 21,041 km² surface measure should not be mixed with differing land-only definitions.

Economy and International Trade

Macroeconomic structure
Dollarized, open mixed economy based on services, manufacturing, retail trade, construction, farming, communications and external income from emigrant households
Nominal GDP
US$36.71 billion in 2025 (World Bank current-dollar series); estimates from other institutions can differ in vintage and methodology [1]
GDP per capita
US$5,766.7 in 2025, current U.S. dollars; this is output divided by population rather than average wages or household purchasing power [1]
Real GDP growth
3.9% in calendar 2025 in the World Bank national-accounts volume-growth series, distinct from nominal-dollar GDP growth [1]
Consumer-price inflation
0.3% for 2025 in the World Bank annual CPI change indicator; does not describe the price of every household necessity [1]
Modeled unemployment
3.3% in 2025 in the ILO-modeled World Bank headline series; employment, informality and underemployment require different measurements [1]
Personal remittance intensity
Personal remittances received amounted to about 27.5% of GDP in 2025 in World Bank data; remittances are international transfers, not manufactured exports [1]
Principal circulating currency
U.S. dollar (USD) is the unit of account used for prices, wages, taxes and national economic statistics [2]
Former national currency
Salvadoran colón was superseded in everyday circulation following monetary integration in 2001; some legal references retain historical colón terminology
Bitcoin legislation
A 2021 law introduced Bitcoin transactions; 2025 amendments removed mandatory acceptance and made private acceptance voluntary, while tax payments were confined to U.S. dollars [9]
Central bank
Banco Central de Reserva conducts financial statistics, supervision-related functions and aspects of payment infrastructure without independently issuing U.S. dollar currency
Trade-policy environment
Central American regional trade arrangements and the CAFTA–DR framework link manufactured goods and farming with the United States and nearby economies
Manufacturing
Knit apparel, plastic articles, processed foods, paper, metal goods and electrical components are prominent merchandise-export categories [7]
Maquila treatment
Export-processing or maquila activity is included in the central-bank total; figures excluding maquila are not directly comparable with the inclusive headline [7]
2025 merchandise exports
About US$6.43 billion in 2025 in BCR-based annual reporting, goods including maquila; not the value of exports of services [8]
2025 merchandise imports
About US$17.85 billion in 2025 in the same reported merchandise customs framework, reflecting a large goods deficit [8]
Calculated goods deficit
Roughly US$11.42 billion in 2025 from the two rounded customs totals; it does not equal the current account or goods-plus-services balance [8]
Major export destinations
United States, Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua and Costa Rica are central markets; rankings may change by month and reporting basis [7]
Remittances versus exports
Household transfers from relatives abroad support consumption and foreign exchange but should not be added to merchandise exports as if they were goods sales [1]
Service activities
Telecommunications, commerce, finance, tourism, transport and business services contribute national output and produce service receipts outside the merchandise table
Agricultural production
Coffee, sugarcane, maize and livestock remain significant for rural livelihoods, even though industry and services account for larger shares of overall value added
Energy and infrastructure
Hydroelectric and geothermal generation complement other electricity supplies; grid reliability, roads and ports affect industrial competitiveness
Digital policy and finance
Bitcoin and digital-asset frameworks attract policy attention, but cryptoasset regulation is distinct from routine banking access or legal-tender acceptance [9]
Development constraints
Public debt, informal work, infrastructure needs, climate exposure and household income disparities shape fiscal and investment decisions; quantitative claims require year-specific sources

People and Society

Official language
Spanish is the principal language of public institutions, education, national media and most daily interregional communication
Indigenous-language heritage
Nawat or Pipil is a historically important Indigenous language supported by revitalization initiatives; other Indigenous linguistic histories predate colonial assimilation
Education and bilingual interests
School instruction is predominantly Spanish-language, while English language education and bilingual business services have expanded in selected sectors
Religion: general composition
Roman Catholic and Protestant or evangelical Christian affiliations are both important; exact percentages depend on the date, adult sampling frame and survey method
Religious nonaffiliation
A portion of residents report no religion or no formal affiliation; a specific 2026 share cannot be inferred from an older survey
Constitutional religion protections
Religious freedom and public institutions coexist with a strong Christian cultural inheritance; self-reported affiliation is not the same as regular participation
Indigenous peoples and memory
Nahua-Pipil and Lenca-descended communities maintain cultural and historical claims, despite long assimilation pressures and earlier repression
1932 historical trauma
The 1932 repression often known as La Matanza deeply affected Indigenous identity, political memory and public discussion of state violence
Diaspora communities
Large Salvadoran communities reside in the United States and other countries; family ties shape remittance flows, migration and civic identity [1]
Urban social structure
Metropolitan San Salvador combines employment concentrations with differences in housing, income, transport access and local public services
Rural livelihood systems
Smallholder agriculture, seasonal farm work, local commerce and regional commuting remain important outside the main metropolitan corridor
National cuisine
Pupusas and regional corn-based foods form prominent culinary traditions alongside tamales, yuca dishes and Pacific coastal seafood
Music and cultural expression
Cumbia, contemporary popular music, community festivals and visual arts coexist with Indigenous and colonial traditions
Health challenges
Noncommunicable disease, access to specialist services and disaster preparedness are major public-health issues, particularly in lower-income communities
Education priorities
Quality of primary and secondary education, digital skills and pathways to technical employment remain persistent development questions
Historical pluralism
Indigenous, African-descended, European-descended and mixed-heritage histories are part of the national story; simplistic ancestry percentages are not warranted without defined survey evidence
Demographic interpretation
Household surveys, vital registrations, population projections and international estimates are different kinds of evidence and should retain their original dates and definitions

Religious percentages are omitted in the absence of a single recently verified nationally representative source matching the relevant year and population. Survey affiliation, attendance and official registration are distinct measures.

Government and Institutions

Constitutional system
Presidential republic under the 1983 Constitution as extensively amended through 2026; amendments altered electoral procedures and the presidency [5]
Current president
Nayib Bukele, first elected in 2019 and inaugurated for a further term on June 1, 2024; editorial status checked November 6, 2026 [4]
Vice president
Félix Ulloa, serving alongside President Bukele and selected as the governing party's 2027 vice-presidential candidate [6]
Executive branch
President and ministers direct national administration, subject to constitutional provisions and changing legislative oversight arrangements
National legislature
Unicameral Legislative Assembly (Asamblea Legislativa), with 60 elected seats following changes that reduced the earlier 84-seat chamber [4]
2024 legislative election
Nuevas Ideas won 54 of 60 Assembly seats, providing a commanding legislative majority during the subsequent constitutional reforms [4]
2025 reelection reform
Amendments approved in 2025 removed the former bar on indefinite presidential reelection and extended subsequent presidential terms to six years [5]
2026 electoral reforms
Further reforms addressed electoral districts abroad and the use of a single presidential-election round rather than a mandatory runoff [5]
Next election schedule
General elections scheduled for February 28, 2027, after transitional shortening of the presidential term begun in June 2024 [6]
Municipal framework
Fourteen departments and 44 municipalities after the 2023 restructuring; district subdivisions should not be confused with independent municipalities [4]
Judicial system
Supreme Court of Justice and subordinate courts form the judicial branch; judicial appointments and independence are subjects of domestic and international debate [4]
Security emergency framework
A state of exception introduced in March 2022 and repeatedly extended provided extraordinary detention and policing powers amid major antigang operations [4]
Public-safety results and concern
Authorities report sharp reductions in killings and gang control; independent observers raise due-process, detention-condition and oversight concerns [4]
Electoral politics
Presidential, legislative and local representation coexist with significant concentration of governing-party power; analysts disagree about the implications for democratic competition [4]
Constitutional amendment process
2025 changes accelerated the approval process for constitutional revisions, an institutional development separate from any single election result [4]
State finance and accountability
Fiscal policy and international financing arrangements raise questions about budget transparency, debt sustainability and anti-corruption controls [9]
Legal status of Bitcoin
The mandatory-payment features of the 2021 Bitcoin framework were removed under 2025 reforms; private acceptance is voluntary [9]
Civil rights and historic peace settlement
Constitutional civil liberties and the institutions born of the 1992 peace agreement remain relevant reference points in evaluating security and political changes [11]

Governance context: the 2025–2026 constitutional reforms, the exceptional security regime and reported reductions in violent crime should be presented with both official policy objectives and documented civil-liberties concerns, without implying consensus about their consequences.

International Profile

United Nations
Founding member; membership effective October 24, 1945 [2]
Central American Integration System
Participates in SICA and related regional integration frameworks, alongside neighboring Central American republics
Central American economic ties
Trade, transport corridors and customs agreements connect Salvadoran producers with Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua and Costa Rica [7]
United States relationship
Strong connections through trade, diaspora, remittances, security collaboration and development policy [1]
CAFTA–DR
Party to the United States–Central America–Dominican Republic trade agreement, with rules affecting textiles, investment, agriculture and market access
Inter-American system
Member of the Organization of American States and participates in associated regional diplomatic mechanisms
International economic institutions
Member of the IMF, World Bank and WTO, with policy engagement on fiscal stability, trade and payments [9]
UNESCO World Heritage
One site, Joya de Cerén Archaeological Site, inscribed in 1993; tentative-list places are not additional UNESCO inscriptions [10]
Human rights diplomacy
International debate focuses on security strategy, prisoners' rights, judicial safeguards and democratic governance, alongside the state's duty to protect residents [4]
Transnational environmental concerns
Water security, volcanic and seismic risks, drought, storms and cross-border watershed management require cooperation with neighbors
Standard time
Central Standard Time UTC−6 year-round; ordinary daylight-saving changes are not observed nationwide
International telephone dialing
+503; national internet country domain .sv and ISO country identifier SV
Regional geographic convention
UN M49 places El Salvador in Central America, which MGU organizes within its North America section rather than a distinct continent

The international profile distinguishes formal organizational membership from participation in regional trade and security frameworks, and distinguishes UNESCO inscriptions from tentative nominations.

Country Overview

The Commonwealth of The Bahamas is an independent Caribbean and Atlantic archipelagic state north of Cuba and southeast of Florida. It consists of hundreds of islands and thousands of smaller cays spanning broad shallow banks; Nassau on New Providence is the capital and dominant urban center. The islands were inhabited by Indigenous Lucayan peoples before European conquest and were subsequently shaped by British colonial rule, slavery, maritime commerce and migration. Independence was achieved on July 10, 1973. A parliamentary constitutional monarchy, The Bahamas retains the British monarch as its separate sovereign. Tourism, financial services and maritime trade are central economic activities, while hurricanes, coastal inundation and import dependence create persistent vulnerabilities.

Identity and State Formation

Official state name
Commonwealth of The Bahamas [11]
Common name
The Bahamas; the definite article is commonly retained in formal English usage
Capital
Nassau, on New Providence Island [2]
UN M49 region
Americas / Caribbean; grouped under North America in this MGU index [10]
Pre-colonial population
Lucayan (Taíno-related) Indigenous communities lived on many islands before European expeditions, conquest and forced removal
Columbus-era contact
1492: Columbus first landed in the Bahamian archipelago according to traditional identification of Guanahani; the exact island identification is debated
Colonial settlement
Seventeenth-century English settlement developed around Eleuthera and New Providence; colonial administration was periodically destabilized by maritime conflict and privateering [13]
Royal colonial administration
1718 is conventionally associated with establishment of more effective British royal government and suppression of Nassau’s pirate stronghold
Slavery and emancipation
Plantation and maritime economies depended on enslaved labor; abolition of slavery in the British empire took effect in 1834, reshaping Bahamian society
Internal self-government
Major constitutional developments in 1964 established ministerial responsibility and a more advanced system of domestic self-government
Independence
July 10, 1973, from the United Kingdom [13]
Constitution
Constitution of the Commonwealth of The Bahamas, effective with independence in 1973 [5]

Geography and Population

Archipelago location
Western North Atlantic Ocean, southeast of Florida and north of Cuba, geographically part of the Caribbean regional system [2]
Total reported area
13,880 km² under a common CIA-style geographic convention; the UN country profile instead reports 13,940 km² because statistical area definitions vary [2]
Land surface
Approximately 10,010 km² under the cited CIA-style total/land/water convention; inland water accounts for much of the area difference
Population
403,033 (2025 World Bank national population estimate) [1]
Population growth
About 0.4% in 2025 in the World Bank country series [1]
Population density
About 40 persons per km² of reported land area (403,033 divided by 10,010 km²); density calculated from total area is approximately 29 per km² [1]
Island count
Often described as around 700 islands and more than 2,000 cays; counts vary with definitions and scale
Principal populated islands
New Providence, Grand Bahama, Abaco, Eleuthera, Andros, Exuma, Long Island, Cat Island and other Family Islands
Major cities and settlements
Nassau, Freeport, Marsh Harbour, George Town (Exuma) and smaller island communities
Highest natural point
Mount Alvernia on Cat Island, approximately 63 metres above sea level
Landscape
Low-lying limestone islands, extensive shallow banks, blue holes, mangrove wetlands, coastal lagoons and coral reefs
Ocean and banks
Great Bahama Bank and Little Bahama Bank; the deep Tongue of the Ocean lies adjacent to shallower carbonate platforms
Climate
Tropical to subtropical maritime climate; summer rainfall and frequent hurricane risk, with cooler winters than many equatorial Caribbean islands
Major recent disaster
Hurricane Dorian struck Abaco and Grand Bahama in 2019, causing catastrophic loss of life, destruction and displacement
Protected ecosystems
Mangroves, seagrasses, coral habitats, wetlands and national parks support biodiversity, fisheries and the tourism economy
Life expectancy at birth
Approximately 75 years in 2024 (World Bank rounded country indicator) [1]

Area and density conventions: the total area figure of 13,880 km² and land area of approximately 10,010 km² are a single compatible geographic convention; UNData lists 13,940 km² as surface area. The density figures on this page are explicitly calculated and should not be mistaken for a national statistical-office publication.

Economy and International Trade

Economic structure
High-income tourism- and services-intensive island economy supported by finance, aviation, shipping, marine industries, construction, retail trade and real estate [4]
Nominal GDP
US$15.8328 billion, 2024, latest annual GDP value displayed on the World Bank country page as of November 2026 [1]
Nominal GDP per capita
US$39,455.4, 2024; this is output per inhabitant in current dollars, not the average cash income of a Bahamian household [1]
Real GDP growth
3.4% in 2024, the latest annual growth value in the cited World Bank country overview; IMF projected approximately 2.8% for 2025 in its February 2026 review [1] [4]
Consumer-price inflation
1.1% in 2025 in the World Bank annual CPI indicator [1]
Unemployment rate
9.2% for 2025 (World Bank ILO modeled series); the IMF cited a 9.3% domestic rate in the second quarter of 2025; definitions and reference dates differ [1] [4]
Currency
Bahamian dollar (BSD, B$) with the U.S. dollar widely used in travel and commerce [14]
Exchange-rate arrangement
Bahamian dollar maintained at a one-to-one parity with the U.S. dollar; this is a policy arrangement, not evidence that their domestic prices are identical [14]
Monetary authority
Central Bank of The Bahamas [14]
Major tourism services
Hotels, cruise operations, marinas, recreation, restaurants, travel transport and related wholesale and construction activity [4]
Financial services
Banking, trusts, investment and professional services; regulation, financial transparency and international compliance remain key policy areas [4]
Merchandise exports
US$944.3 million in 2024 from WTO/UN Comtrade goods records; an unusually large share of partner destinations is listed under other or unspecified destinations [3]
Merchandise imports
US$4.9440 billion in 2024, heavily concentrated on food, fuel, equipment, vehicles and other imported goods [3]
Merchandise trade balance
Approximately US$3.9997 billion deficit in 2024, based on the two goods figures above; not the current-account balance [3]
Leading goods export destinations
United States 48.7% and other/non-specified countries 46.9% of 2024 exports; many named partners individually account for very small shares [3]
Leading goods import source
United States 85.6% in 2024, followed at a distance by the EU 2.8% and China 2.5% [3]
WTO membership status
Not a WTO member as of the WTO profile consulted; trade and tariff data nevertheless appear in the WTO country portal [3]
Economic policy concerns
Public-debt management, disaster insurance, aviation and tourism investment, skills, infrastructure, energy imports and financial stability [4]

Statistical comparability: GDP and GDP per capita above refer to 2024, population and inflation to 2025, and merchandise trade to 2024. The Bahamian economy exports substantial tourism and financial services that are not captured by the merchandise figures. The unusually large “other countries, not elsewhere specified” partner share should not be assigned to a named country without evidence.

People and Society

Official language
English, used in government, law, education and formal communication [5]
Vernacular languages
Bahamian English and distinctive Bahamian Creole forms of English, varying by island, age and social context
Historical population origins
Lucayan Indigenous societies were displaced through colonization; a majority African-descended population subsequently developed through slavery, emancipation, and later migration
Religious communities
Christian churches including Baptist, Anglican, Catholic, Methodist, Pentecostal and Adventist congregations; other religious and nonreligious communities also present
Religion statistics limitation
No current nationwide harmonized religion estimate is implied by the broad denominational description; percentages in secondary references may come from older enumerations
Urban concentration
The Nassau–New Providence area contains a large share of the national population; other islands have smaller, widely separated settlements
Family Islands and migration
Internal migration, commuting and tourism employment influence settlement and living conditions throughout the archipelago
Culture
Junkanoo street parades, brass and percussion traditions, storytelling, visual arts and varied Bahamian island festivals
Music
Rake-and-scrape, Junkanoo rhythms, gospel, calypso, soca and contemporary music
Cuisine
Conch preparations, boiled and fried fish, peas-and-rice, guava duff and regional seafood dishes
Education
National public and private school systems; University of The Bahamas and technical and vocational institutions
Healthcare
Hospitals and clinics are concentrated on major islands, with inter-island transport an important factor for specialized care
Sport
Sailing, track and field, basketball, swimming, sailing regattas and other marine sports
Diaspora and external links
Strong family and economic networks with Florida and wider North America; visitors and migrant labor influence local economies
Disaster resilience
Hurricanes and flooding can disrupt housing, health services, employment, insurance access and inter-island transportation

Population figures are model-based national estimates rather than a claim that 403,033 people were directly enumerated in a 2025 census. A national average obscures the far greater density of New Providence and the small populations of many Family Islands.

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Independent parliamentary constitutional monarchy / Commonwealth realm [11]
Head of state
King Charles III in his separate capacity as King of The Bahamas [11]
Governor-General
Dame Cynthia Pratt, appointed effective September 1, 2023 and serving in 2026 [11]
Head of government
Prime Minister Philip Edward Davis, re-elected with the Progressive Liberal Party after the May 12, 2026 general election [6] [11]
Constitution
1973 Constitution, effective upon independence and subsequently amended [5]
Parliament
Bicameral Parliament made up of the elected House of Assembly and appointed Senate [6] [7]
House of Assembly
41 elected seats after the May 2026 election; the 2026 IPU result shows PLP 33 seats and FNM eight [6] [15]
Senate
16 appointed senators in the upper chamber under the constitutional arrangements [7]
Electoral system
Single-member districts and first-past-the-post legislative elections; executive authority depends on House confidence [6]
Central administration
Cabinet and ministries under the prime minister; the governor-general performs constitutional functions for the monarch
Judiciary
Domestic courts, Court of Appeal and Judicial Committee of the Privy Council as final appellate tribunal
Administrative division
Local government districts on islands outside New Providence, often grouped under the broader “Family Islands” designation
Public security
Royal Bahamas Police Force and Royal Bahamas Defence Force; marine security and territorial waters are especially important
Current political context
The May 2026 election returned the PLP government; election results are referenced by date to prevent reuse of a superseded 2021 parliamentary configuration [15]

Election and seat-count update: older descriptions of a 39-seat House are superseded by the 41 elected seats contested on May 12, 2026. The 16-seat Senate is appointed rather than elected. Political officeholders have been checked against 2026 external governmental and parliamentary sources.

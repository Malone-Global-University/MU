Country Overview

Antigua and Barbuda is a sovereign twin-island Caribbean state in the eastern Lesser Antilles. Antigua contains the capital and most residents, while the less populated Barbuda has its own elected local council; uninhabited Redonda is also part of the state. Earlier Indigenous communities, European colonization, plantation slavery, emancipation and twentieth-century self-government shaped its formation. Independence from the United Kingdom was attained on November 1, 1981. Tourism, construction and internationally connected services dominate the economy, while the archipelago faces hurricane exposure, water scarcity and climate-related coastal risks. Its constitutional system is a parliamentary democracy within a Commonwealth realm, with a locally represented monarch and elected government.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Antigua and Barbuda; independent sovereign state and Commonwealth realm [5]
Capital city
Saint John's on northwest Antigua; seat of the central government and primary urban centre [2]
Continent and UN M49 subregion
North America in the MGU continental index; Americas / Caribbean in United Nations M49 coding [10]
Historical Indigenous societies
Arawakan- and later Kalinago-associated populations predated European settlement; specific chronology and population histories remain subjects of research [13]
European colonial period
British colonial settlement developed on Antigua during the seventeenth century; plantation sugar production expanded through the labor of enslaved Africans [13]
End of slavery
Emancipation of enslaved people in the British Caribbean came into force in 1834; the social and economic legacy of plantation slavery remained substantial [13]
Universal adult suffrage and labor politics
Twentieth-century labor and constitutional reforms expanded participation in public affairs and supported the move toward self-government
Associated statehood
February 27, 1967: Antigua gained associated-state status with internal self-government while the United Kingdom retained specified external responsibilities
Independence
November 1, 1981: Antigua and Barbuda became independent, with Queen Elizabeth II as the initial post-independence monarch [13]
Constitution
Independence Constitution of 1981; includes provisions for a governor-general, bicameral Parliament, the cabinet and judiciary [5]
National Day
Independence Day is celebrated on November 1 [13]
United Nations admission
November 11, 1981, ten days after independence [9]
Kingdom and national identity
Antigua and Barbuda is independent even though its sovereign is also monarch of other Commonwealth realms; each realm is legally distinct [11]
Island names
Antigua, Barbuda and uninhabited Redonda; surrounding smaller islands and cays also form part of the state [2]
ISO 3166-1 country codes
AG (alpha-2); ATG (alpha-3); 028 (numeric) [10]
Internet country-code domain
.ag; the international telephone code is +1 268
National symbols
Flag with rising sun above blue, white, red and black elements; national anthem “Fair Antigua, We Salute Thee”

Historical interpretation: formal constitutional independence in 1981 should be distinguished from the longer processes of Indigenous history, colonial occupation, emancipation, labor mobilization and parliamentary self-government. Barbuda's local institutions are part of this national history.

Geography and Population

Territorial area
442 km² in the United Nations country profile; the CIA-style figure of 443 km² appears in other geographic references because of differing island and rounding conventions [2]
Land area
A small, predominantly terrestrial island group; approximate land area is close to the territorial total, while lagoons, wetlands and offshore waters must be treated separately [2]
Population
94,209 persons (2025, World Bank series); alternative national and IMF population estimates can be higher and should not be combined without identifying definitions [1] [4]
Annual population growth
Approximately 0.5% in 2025 according to the World Bank [1]
Average density
About 213 people per km² based on 94,209 residents divided by 442 km²; Antigua is far more densely settled than Barbuda [1] [2]
Main island of settlement
Antigua contains the capital, largest resorts, most government institutions and the international airport
Barbuda
Low-lying, relatively sparsely settled coral and limestone island; principal community Codrington, with major wetlands and coastal habitats
Redonda
Small uninhabited rocky island west-southwest of Antigua, with ecological significance and no ordinary residential population
Principal settlements
Saint John's, All Saints, Liberta, Parham and Potters Village on Antigua; Codrington on Barbuda
Highest elevation
Mount Obama (formerly Boggy Peak), approximately 402 metres, in Antigua’s southwestern Shekerley Mountains
Coast and harbors
Caribbean Sea and Atlantic Ocean coastlines; harbors include Saint John’s, English Harbour and Falmouth Harbour
Climate
Tropical maritime climate with seasonal rainfall, trade winds, periodic drought conditions and exposure to Atlantic hurricanes
Hurricane history
Hurricane Irma devastated Barbuda in September 2017, causing major displacement and long-term recovery demands
Freshwater supply
Limited natural freshwater availability; reservoirs, groundwater management, desalination and conservation are significant public priorities
Protected and historic landscapes
Marine reefs, mangrove systems, wetlands, beaches and the Antigua Naval Dockyard cultural landscape [8]
Life expectancy at birth
About 78 years in the 2024 World Bank country data, rounded [1]

Geographic interpretation: density is an arithmetic nationwide average and does not imply uniform settlement across Antigua, Barbuda and Redonda. Storm impacts, especially on Barbuda, also differ sharply from island to island.

Economy and International Trade

Economic structure
Tourism-led upper-middle/high-income small-island economy combining hospitality, construction, wholesale and retail trade, transportation, agriculture and financial and professional services [4]
Nominal GDP
US$2.3382 billion in 2025 at current market exchange rates (World Bank) [1]
Nominal GDP per capita
US$24,819.2 in 2025 (World Bank); average GDP divided by residents, not median household income or take-home wages [1]
Real GDP growth
5.0% in the 2025 World Bank country series; the IMF May 2026 staff estimate for 2025 was 3.0%, a different release/model vintage [1] [4]
Consumer price inflation
1.4% in 2025 in both the cited World Bank series and the IMF 2026 review [1] [4]
Unemployment
A reliable recent annual modeled unemployment rate is not displayed on the cited World Bank country overview; the absence of data is not a claim of zero unemployment [1]
Currency
Eastern Caribbean dollar (XCD, EC$), issued under the Eastern Caribbean Currency Union
Exchange-rate regime
Eastern Caribbean dollar fixed to the U.S. dollar at EC$2.70 per US$1 through the regional monetary arrangement [14]
Monetary authority
Eastern Caribbean Central Bank, headquartered in Saint Kitts and Nevis [14]
Tourism role
International leisure travel, cruise visits, yachting and hospitality are central sources of foreign exchange, employment and demand for local suppliers [4]
Construction and investment
Hotel projects, real estate development, infrastructure investment and public capital projects affect growth and fiscal capacity [4]
Citizenship-by-investment program
Program-related revenue and capital flows are material fiscal and balance-of-payments considerations; they should not be classified as merchandise exports [4]
Merchandise exports
US$33.4 million in 2024 according to WTO/UN Comtrade goods data; reported values include small-volume and re-export categories [3]
Merchandise imports
US$663.1 million in 2024, including fuel, vehicles, machinery, food and other consumer and construction goods [3]
Merchandise trade balance
Approximately US$629.7 million goods deficit in 2024 (exports minus imports); this excludes tourism and other services [3]
Leading recorded goods export markets
European Union 20.4%, United States 19.2%, other/unallocated markets 19.1%, China 9.8%, and Saint Kitts and Nevis 9.5% in the 2024 WTO dataset [3]
Leading recorded goods import sources
United States 54.0%, European Union 8.6%, China 5.2% and Trinidad and Tobago 4.2% in 2024 [3]
Principal vulnerabilities
Storm exposure, global travel and fuel prices, import dependence, public debt, constrained freshwater supplies and limited economies of scale [4]

Interpretation of growth estimates: the 2025 World Bank growth value (5.0%) and the May 2026 IMF 2025 estimate (3.0%) are cited separately, not averaged. A large goods deficit does not by itself establish a correspondingly large overall external deficit, because tourism is exported as a service.

People and Society

Official language
English, used for legislation, government, education and most formal public communication [5]
Everyday speech
Antiguan and Barbudan English-based Creole varieties alongside standard English, with regional differences
Religious traditions
Christian denominations, including Anglican, Methodist, Moravian, Pentecostal, Adventist and Catholic communities; other faiths and nonreligious residents also present
Religion data limitation
Exact contemporary denominational percentages should not be inferred from older census tables or church affiliation claims
Ancestry and historical population
Majority of residents have African Caribbean ancestry rooted partly in the history of enslavement; other populations and mixed heritage contribute to national diversity
Antigua and Barbuda relations
A shared national citizenship encompasses distinct Barbuda local history, land institutions, political representation and community identity
Major festivals
Antigua Carnival and emancipation-period celebrations, independence festivities, sailing events and community music festivals
Music and performance
Calypso, soca, steel-pan ensembles, contemporary Caribbean popular music and religious choral traditions
Cuisine
Fungee and pepperpot, rice and peas, saltfish, local vegetables, fish, seafood and regional Caribbean dishes
Education
Public school network, Antigua State College and higher-education opportunities within and beyond the eastern Caribbean
Healthcare
Public and private medical services concentrated on Antigua; access from Barbuda requires inter-island transport for some specialized care
Sport
Cricket, sailing, football and regional athletics; national cricket traditions overlap with the West Indies team structure
Migration and diaspora
Emigration and return migration connect the country with the United Kingdom, United States, Canada and other Caribbean societies

Social and demographic interpretation: language practice, religious identification, and ethnic or national heritage represent distinct dimensions. Older census observations should not be relabeled as 2025 survey results.

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Parliamentary constitutional monarchy and independent Commonwealth realm; elected Cabinet exercises executive government [5]
Head of state
King Charles III, as King of Antigua and Barbuda, represented in the country by the Governor-General [11]
Governor-General
Sir Rodney Williams, serving as the monarch’s representative as of the November 2026 verification [11]
Head of government
Prime Minister Gaston Browne, in office since June 2014 and continuing after the 2026 electoral period [11]
Constitutional framework
1981 Independence Constitution, as subsequently amended or supplemented by legislation [5]
Legislature
Bicameral Parliament with a House of Representatives and a Senate [5]
House of Representatives
Seventeen elected territorial constituencies; additional non-elected parliamentary roles should not be confused with the elected seat total [6] [5]
Senate
Seventeen statutory appointed seats, with appointments made by the Governor-General under constitutional rules [7] [5]
Cabinet responsibility
Prime minister and ministers are politically accountable to Parliament under the Westminster-derived constitutional arrangement
Judicial system
Eastern Caribbean Supreme Court institutional framework, domestic magistrates’ courts and a final appellate relationship with the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council
Administrative divisions
Six parishes on Antigua, together with the dependencies of Barbuda and Redonda; Barbuda has its own council [5]
Barbuda Council
Local statutory authority on Barbuda, with responsibilities concerning local governance and land-related matters within national law
Political system
Multiparty parliamentary elections and constituency representation; the 2026 political situation should be read in the context of the latest published institutional records [6]
Security institutions
Royal Police Force of Antigua and Barbuda and the Antigua and Barbuda Defence Force

Constitutional note: Antigua and Barbuda shares the person of its monarch with other realms but is not a dependency of the United Kingdom. The 17 elected constituencies should be distinguished from parliamentary seats held ex officio or by the Speaker.

Country Overview

The Commonwealth of Dominica is a small sovereign island republic in the eastern Caribbean, situated between Guadeloupe and Martinique. Often called the Nature Island, its volcanic mountains, rainforests, deep valleys and high rainfall distinguish it from flatter islands with similar names. The Kalinago people retain a distinctive cultural presence, while French and British colonial competition, African enslavement, emancipation, and later self-government shaped its institutions. Dominica gained independence from the United Kingdom on November 3, 1978, adopting a parliamentary republican system. Its economy combines tourism, farming, public works and citizenship-by-investment receipts, with substantial vulnerability to hurricanes and other climate hazards. Dominica is an independent state and should not be confused with the larger Dominican Republic on Hispaniola.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Commonwealth of Dominica (independent republic, not a Commonwealth realm) [5]
Common name
Dominica; pronounced approximately dom-in-EE-ka; distinct from the Dominican Republic
Capital city
Roseau, on the island's west coast, with surrounding communities in Saint George Parish [2]
Continent and UN M49 subregion
North America in MGU indexing; Americas / Latin America and the Caribbean / Caribbean in the UN classification [3]
Indigenous identity
Kalinago inhabitants have a long history on the island and maintain communities and cultural institutions in the Kalinago Territory [4]
Indigenous name
Waitukubuli, often translated 'tall is her body,' is a Kalinago-associated name referring to the island's mountainous form [4]
European contact
Columbus sighted the island on November 3, 1493; the Spanish name Dominica refers to Sunday, but Indigenous societies predate this encounter [4]
Colonial competition
French and British interests contested the island, with changing control and persistent cultural connections to the French-speaking Antilles
British sovereignty milestone
The 1763 Treaty of Paris transferred sovereignty to Britain after a period of French control; military contests and French influence continued afterward
Slavery and emancipation
Plantation agriculture depended on enslaved African labor until British imperial emancipation in the 1830s; formerly enslaved people and descendants shaped political life
Internal self-government
A British-associated state from 1967, with responsibility for internal affairs, before independence
Independence
November 3, 1978, when Dominica became independent from the United Kingdom as a republic [4] [5]
Independence Day
November 3, marked by national celebrations; do not confuse this with the 1493 European sighting of the island
Founding constitutional framework
Constitution effective at independence in 1978; parliamentary institutions and republican presidency are distinct offices [5]
Early post-independence politics
Political instability followed in 1979–1981, after which electoral institutions consolidated
UN membership
Admitted December 18, 1978, shortly after independence [15]
ISO 3166-1 codes
DM (alpha-2), DMA (alpha-3), 212 (numeric)
National flag
Green field, a central circular red disk with the Sisserou parrot, ten green stars and cross stripes in yellow, black and white
National bird
Sisserou (imperial amazon parrot), an endemic and conservation-sensitive species
National anthem
Isle of Beauty, Isle of Splendour
Naming caveat
Dominica and Dominican Republic are two separate UN member states with different capitals, currencies, independence histories and governments

A separate country: Dominica (DM) is not the Dominican Republic (DO). The same root in the two English names does not indicate common present-day sovereignty.

Geography and Population

Total surface area
750 km² in the UN statistical reference; figures near 751 km² sometimes appear due to rounding and land-area conventions [2]
Geographic position
Windward Islands, Lesser Antilles, between the French overseas territories of Guadeloupe to the north and Martinique to the south
Main island
The state consists principally of a single mountainous volcanic island with adjacent smaller offshore rocks
Highest summit
Morne Diablotins, approximately 1,447 m above sea level
Volcanic landscape
Mountain ridges, thermal features, sulphur springs and the Boiling Lake show the island's volcanic origins
Protected natural landmark
Morne Trois Pitons National Park includes boiling and freshwater lakes, forests and geothermal features; inscribed by UNESCO in 1997 [14]
Rivers and water resources
Short, steep rivers, forest watersheds and waterfalls are abundant; heavy rainfall can cause rapid flood events
Principal settlements
Roseau, Portsmouth, Marigot, Mahaut, Grand Bay, and smaller inland and coastal villages
Parishes
Ten civil parishes, including Saint George, Saint John, Saint Andrew, Saint David, Saint Patrick, Saint Paul, Saint Peter, Saint Joseph, Saint Luke and Saint Mark
Kalinago Territory
Legally recognized territory in the northeast; cultural and administrative arrangements are distinct from the ten-parish system
Climate
Humid tropical marine climate with substantial rainfall, especially in windward and upland districts; hurricane season brings greater storm exposure
Severe storm experience
Tropical Storm Erika in 2015 and Hurricane Maria in 2017 inflicted extensive damage, prompting a national focus on disaster resilience [13]
2025 population
65,871 in the World Bank's 2025 population series; small-country estimates can change with migration and revisions [1]
Older religion-census denominator
Dominica's 2011 census religion table records 69,325 persons; it is a different observation from the 2025 modeled population [12]
2025 population change
-0.5% in the World Bank series; small changes in net migration can materially affect rates for an island of this size [1]
Life expectancy
Around 71 years in the World Bank's 2024 indicator, a rounded estimate rather than a current census measurement [1]
Biodiversity
Mountain forests support the endemic Sisserou and Jaco parrots, tropical flora and freshwater habitats
Coastal features
Rocky coastlines, narrow bays and marine ecosystems include the Soufrière–Scotts Head area and coral habitat
Environmental risks
Landslides, flash flooding, hurricanes, coastal erosion and changes in rainfall patterns threaten transport, farming and utilities [13]

Area figures of 750 and 751 km² are common in secondary compilations; this profile consistently uses the UN 750 km² statistical reference.

Economy and International Trade

Economic structure
Small, open island economy combining public administration, construction, tourism, agriculture, trade and citizenship-by-investment-linked revenues [13]
Nominal GDP
US$723.86 million, 2025, World Bank current-US$ country series [1]
GDP per capita
US$10,989 in 2025, World Bank; an economy-wide average rather than household income or median wages [1]
Real GDP growth
3.1% in 2025 in World Bank WDI; other World Bank narrative publications have differing updated growth estimates, so this page states its series explicitly [1]
2025 inflation
2.5% consumer-price inflation in the World Bank country summary [1]
Population growth
-0.5% in 2025 (World Bank); GDP-per-capita change should not be inferred from population alone [1]
Unemployment measurement
The World Bank headline country page has no current modeled ILO unemployment observation for Dominica; do not substitute an undated third-party percentage [1]
Currency
East Caribbean dollar (XCD), also used by other members of the Eastern Caribbean Currency Union [11]
Exchange-rate regime
Fixed at EC$2.70 per US$1 under the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank monetary framework; this is an official conversion rate, not a travel purchase quotation [11]
Monetary institution
Eastern Caribbean Central Bank, the regional monetary authority headquartered in Saint Kitts and Nevis [11]
Tourism and services
Stayover and cruise tourism create demand for hotels, tours, transportation, food services and marine recreation; service exports are separate from goods exports [13]
Agriculture
Bananas, plantains, root crops, citrus, vegetables and other produce support markets and some external sales; harvests are exposed to storm damage
Citizenship-by-investment
Government investment-based citizenship receipts support public finance but create transparency, regulation and external-policy sensitivities [13]
Infrastructure
National airport, roads, ports, electricity and water systems face high maintenance costs because of steep terrain and storm exposure
Renewable energy
Geothermal development and other clean-energy initiatives seek to reduce imported-fuel dependency while requiring significant infrastructure investment
Merchandise exports
US$29 million in 2024 in UNCTAD's general-profile goods series; do not confuse with services exports [8]
Merchandise imports
US$262 million in 2024 in the same UNCTAD goods series; consumer items, energy and equipment contribute to the import requirement [8]
Merchandise trade balance
Approximately US$234 million deficit in 2024 in UNCTAD's rounded annual trade series [8]
Trade in services
UNCTAD records approximately US$191 million in services exports and US$157 million in services imports in 2024; tourism is a large component of exported services [8]
Detailed product reference year
UK government/UN Comtrade top-product categories use 2023 data totaling about US$22 million in goods exports; a different year from UNCTAD's 2024 aggregate [9]
Key merchandise categories
Fruit, vegetables, soap and cleaning goods, machinery-related products, botanical or specialty products and spices appear among recorded exports [9]
Principal import origins
The United States, Trinidad and Tobago, EU members, China and other Caribbean and global suppliers; WTO's detailed reporter-partner table uses 2023 [10]
Economic resilience policy
Climate-resilient housing, road upgrades, ports, emergency planning and diversified tourism products are recurring public-investment priorities [13]

People and Society

Official language
English is the official language of government, education and legislation
Dominican Creole
Kwéyòl (Antillean French Creole) is widely associated with local cultural traditions, oral history, media and everyday communication
Kalinago language heritage
The historical Kalinago language has largely ceased to be a general community language, but community-led efforts preserve knowledge, vocabulary and identity [4]
Other languages
French and other languages appear through education, neighboring islands, tourism and migrant communities
Population backgrounds
Predominantly African-descended society, with Kalinago, mixed-heritage, European-descended, Caribbean and other communities; ethnic and language categories are not interchangeable
Religious census reference
In the 2011 census, 52.7% were Roman Catholic, 19.0% evangelical, 6.7% Seventh-day Adventist and 9.4% reported no religion [12]
Other religious groups
Methodist, Anglican, Rastafarian, Jehovah's Witness and other faith communities appear in the 2011 religion tables [12]
Religious-data limitation
The latest figures above come from the 2011 census; they are not claimed to be a 2025 or 2026 estimate [12]
Cultural traditions
Creole music, bouyon, cadence-lypso, carnival, Kalinago artistry, oral histories and community festivals contribute to national life
Annual celebrations
Independence Season includes Creole Day and Wob Dwiyet observances alongside independence commemorations
Cuisine
Callaloo soup, provisions, fish, ground foods and Creole seasoning form part of varied local diets
Education
Public and private schools and the Dominica State College support education and professional formation; small-island training may include overseas study
Healthcare
Public clinics and hospitals serve the population, with specialized treatment sometimes requiring overseas referral
Housing and migration
Storm rebuilding, tourism jobs, migration and remittances shape household conditions and demographic trends
Land and indigenous affairs
Kalinago Territory supports a distinctive community governance and cultural-representation framework, alongside national citizenship
Natural heritage and identity
Forest landscapes, the endemic parrots and an ecotourism identity have become prominent national emblems
UNESCO heritage
Morne Trois Pitons National Park is Dominica's one inscribed World Heritage property as of this edition [14]

The religious percentages are clearly labeled as belonging to the 2011 census and should not be reused as if they were measured in 2026.

Government and Institutions

Government form
Unitary parliamentary republic within the Commonwealth of Nations; not a monarchy and not governed by the British sovereign [5]
Constitution
Constitution of the Commonwealth of Dominica, effective at independence in 1978, establishes republican head of state and parliamentary government [5]
Head of state
President Sylvanie Burton, inaugurated October 2, 2023, the first woman and first Indigenous person to hold the presidency [6]
Head of government
Prime Minister Roosevelt Skerrit, leader of the Dominica Labour Party and long-serving head of government [5]
President's selection
President chosen through parliamentary constitutional procedures rather than a direct public election to a U.S.-style executive presidency [5]
Executive authority
Prime minister and cabinet direct daily government; the presidency performs constitutional duties and selected appointment functions
Parliament
Unicameral House of Assembly consisting of 21 directly elected representatives and nine appointed senators, with the Speaker and Attorney General included under the 32-member statutory composition [7]
Elections
Parliamentary elections determine the constituency representatives and effective formation of government; terms can run up to five years [7]
Political parties
Dominica Labour Party, United Workers Party, Dominica Freedom Party and other political organizations contribute to electoral life [5]
Judiciary
Eastern Caribbean Supreme Court system includes High Court and Court of Appeal; the Caribbean Court of Justice is Dominica's final appellate court in relevant matters
Administrative structure
Ten parishes, with communities and local councils operating below the central government
Kalinago local leadership
Kalinago Chief and community council have a distinctive recognized role within the Kalinago Territory
Citizenship
Citizenship follows constitutional and nationality-law provisions; a regulated citizenship-by-investment program also exists
Public finance
Appropriations and accountability are exercised through parliament, ministries and statutory audit institutions; extreme-weather rebuilding affects budget priorities
Civil liberties
Constitutional rights, elections and judicial review coexist with policy debates over public integrity, citizenship programs and media institutions

International Profile

United Nations
Member since December 18, 1978 [15]
Commonwealth of Nations
Independent republic and member of the Commonwealth; membership does not make the British monarch its head of state [5]
CARICOM
Member of the Caribbean Community, participating in regional economic and foreign-policy coordination
OECS
Member of the Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States, sharing institutions and cooperating on mobility and integration
Eastern Caribbean Currency Union
Participates in the common East Caribbean dollar monetary framework administered by the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank [11]
WTO
Member of the World Trade Organization with WTO goods-trade commitments and small-economy interests [10]
Regional diplomacy
Relationships with neighboring French Caribbean territories, other Eastern Caribbean states, North America and international development institutions
Foreign exchange
Tourism, official financing, remittances and external investment finance a small, highly import-dependent economy
Disaster diplomacy
Climate finance and reconstruction support are significant priorities after damaging storms in 2015 and 2017 [13]
UNESCO World Heritage
Morne Trois Pitons National Park, inscribed in 1997, is the country's only World Heritage property as of November 2026 [14]
Kalinago cultural heritage
Community-led cultural preservation and wider Indigenous rights are visible elements of national and international heritage discussions
Migration
Diaspora communities abroad, particularly elsewhere in the Caribbean, United Kingdom and North America, maintain social and economic ties
Country-name disambiguation
Dominica's capital is Roseau and it is a parliamentary republic; the Dominican Republic's capital is Santo Domingo and it is a presidential republic
Statistical interpretation
Very small population and economy mean that migration, tourist arrivals or storm damages can cause unusually large year-to-year percentage changes

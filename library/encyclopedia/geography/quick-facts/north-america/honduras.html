Country Overview

Honduras is a Central American republic bordered by Guatemala, El Salvador and Nicaragua, with a long Caribbean coastline and a smaller Pacific frontage on the Gulf of Fonseca. Its mountainous interior, fertile valleys, forests and coral-rich Bay Islands are home to communities whose histories include Maya cities, Lenca and other Indigenous peoples, Afro-Caribbean societies and Spanish colonial rule. Independence was proclaimed in 1821, followed by a period in the Central American federation and the consolidation of a separate republic. The economy spans coffee, bananas, palm oil, manufacturing for export, services, agriculture, tourism and substantial remittances. President Nasry Asfura assumed office in January 2026 following a close and disputed 2025 election. This profile identifies the reporting boundaries of the 2025 central-bank trade figures, including goods made for export through maquila production.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Honduras (República de Honduras); sovereign Central American republic [6]
Common name and nationality
Honduras; Honduran (hondureño or hondureña); the state is distinct from its neighboring former federal partners
Capital and central district
Tegucigalpa and Comayagüela comprise the Distrito Central, the constitutionally defined seat of national government [6]
UN geographical classification
Central America within UN M49 Americas; located in the MGU North America country index [9]
Precolonial Maya history
Maya political and artistic life flourished at Copán, particularly during the Classic period, within a wider network of Central American communities [8]
Other Indigenous nations
Lenca, Miskito, Pech, Tolupán, Tawahka and other communities have distinct regional histories and are not interchangeable with one Maya identity
Spanish invasion and administration
Conquest campaigns began during the 1520s; colonial mining, farms and towns came within the wider Captaincy General of Guatemala
Colonial economic history
Silver mining, livestock, small-scale cultivation and Caribbean coastal exchange developed under systems of colonial extraction and coerced labor
British Caribbean interactions
Coastal settlements and the Bay Islands experienced sustained English-speaking Caribbean and British colonial influence
Independence from Spain
September 15, 1821, proclaimed jointly across Central America; does not mark the end of the subsequent federal period [12]
Federal republic years
Honduras was part of the Federal Republic of Central America after 1823, before separatist consolidation amid federation breakdown
Separate statehood
Honduras became independently organized as the federation dissolved by 1838–1839; the central independence holiday remains September 15 [12]
Banana export era
Foreign-owned railway and plantation businesses became influential in coastal agriculture and politics during the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries
Political change in twentieth century
Military influence, civilian reform, regional conflicts and the evolving party system shaped twentieth-century state institutions
Constitution of 1982
Modern constitutional framework establishes a presidential republic with elected branches and defined rights [6]
2009 constitutional crisis
The removal of President Manuel Zelaya triggered intense domestic and international disagreement over constitutional succession and democratic norms
2010s political institutions
Debate over presidential reelection, security measures, corruption oversight and judicial independence affected subsequent electoral cycles
2021 presidential transition
Xiomara Castro won the 2021 election and became the first woman to hold Honduras's presidency in January 2022
2025 general election
Nasry Asfura defeated Salvador Nasralla by a narrow margin in a disputed count; public disputes over legitimacy must be distinguished from the certified inauguration [5]
2026 presidential inauguration
Nasry Asfura assumed the four-year presidency on January 27, 2026 [5]
National day
Independence Day, September 15, shared as a historical commemoration with other Central American states
National symbols
Blue-white-blue flag with five blue stars representing the historical Central American union; national coat of arms includes mountainous and maritime imagery
United Nations membership
Honduras joined the UN on December 17, 1945 [13]
ISO and internet identifiers
HN, HND and numeric 340; .hn is the country-code internet domain [9]

Political chronology: the shared September 1821 declaration, the 1830s disintegration of the Central American federation, the 1982 Constitution and the January 2026 inauguration mark different kinds of state and institutional milestones. The narrow 2025 electoral outcome generated disputes that should be described without prejudging every allegation.

Geography and Population

Relative location
Lies between Guatemala to the northwest, El Salvador to the southwest and Nicaragua to the southeast, with coastlines on two ocean basins
Surface area
112,492 km² in World Bank surface-area reporting; land-only and total-area figures differ because inland water is treated separately [2]
Caribbean frontage
Northern coastline opens toward the Caribbean Sea and Gulf of Honduras, with bays, river mouths and Atlantic trade ports
Pacific access
Short shoreline on the Gulf of Fonseca faces El Salvador and Nicaragua, with marine resources and complex regional boundary history
Bay Islands
Roatán, Utila and Guanaja are major islands associated with fisheries, tourism, coral reefs and distinct English-speaking Caribbean heritage
Miskito Coast region
Northeastern La Mosquitia contains wetlands, tropical forest, rivers and communities with major cross-border cultural links to Nicaragua
Interior mountain systems
Complex ridges and high valleys shape road corridors, rainfall gradients, agricultural production and settlement
Highest peak
Cerro Las Minas (Pico Celaque), approximately 2,870 metres in western Honduras
Largest urban centers
Central District (Tegucigalpa–Comayagüela) and San Pedro Sula are principal urban and economic concentrations
Major commercial region
Sula Valley and surrounding northern industrial corridor contain factories, logistics facilities and productive agricultural lands
Important rivers
Ulúa, Aguán, Patuca and Coco/Segovia watersheds support livelihoods, hydropower potential and varied ecosystems
Inland lake
Lake Yojoa is the country's largest natural inland lake and a focal region for conservation, fishing and tourism
Protected biodiversity
Río Plátano Biosphere Reserve is a large northeastern forest landscape internationally recognized for biological and cultural value [8]
Climate differences
Tropical coastal lowlands contrast with cooler interior mountains; rainfall is highly influenced by Caribbean winds and regional elevation
Hurricane hazards
Hurricanes Mitch in 1998 and Eta and Iota in 2020 demonstrated the country's vulnerability to heavy rain, flooding and landslides
Drought vulnerability
The Central American Dry Corridor experiences variable rainfall and crop stress that can affect subsistence livelihoods and migration
2025 World Bank population
11,005,850 in the 2025 annual international series; this is not a direct count taken in 2025 [1]
Annual population growth
Approximately 1.7% in the 2025 World Bank series, including estimated natural increase and migration effects [1]
Population density
Approximately 97.8 people per km² by dividing World Bank 2025 population by total surface area, a national calculation that conceals local concentration [1] [2]
2013 population census
Honduras counted 8,303,772 people in the 2013 national census; the census is a different period and enumeration basis from 2025 annual estimates [7]
2013 Indigenous and Afro-Honduran census grouping
The official 2013 self-identification dataset counted roughly 717,620 people in Indigenous or Afro-Honduran categories; categories and wording must be retained [7]
Departments
Eighteen departments with municipalities as principal local government divisions
Life expectancy
Approximately 73 years in the World Bank 2024 life expectancy series, an earlier reference year than the economic statistics [1]
UNESCO properties
Two World Heritage properties: Maya Site of Copán and Río Plátano Biosphere Reserve [8]

Census comparability: Honduras’s 2013 enumeration of 8,303,772 people and the 2025 World Bank estimate of 11,005,850 are not interchangeable. The 2013 Indigenous and Afro-Honduran shares refer to self-identification categories within that census, not to the country’s 2026 ethnic composition.

Economy and International Trade

Economic structure
Open, mixed lower-middle-income Central American economy combining retail and other services, agriculture, export manufacturing, transport and tourism
Nominal GDP
US$39.60 billion in 2025, current U.S. dollars, from the World Bank national-accounts series [1]
GDP per capita
US$3,598.2 in 2025 on a nominal basis; the national average is not typical take-home pay or household consumption [1]
Real GDP growth
3.8% for calendar 2025 in World Bank constant-price growth reporting, separate from nominal currency-value growth [1]
Consumer-price inflation
4.6% during 2025 in the World Bank annual consumer-price indicator; price experiences differ among households [1]
Modeled unemployment
4.9% in 2025 under the ILO-modeled World Bank series, which is not a comprehensive measure of informality [1]
Remittances as GDP share
Personal remittances received equaled about 30.1% of GDP in 2025; they are private transfers, not merchandise exports [1]
National currency
Honduran lempira (HNL), whose exchange rate is managed through the central bank's monetary and foreign-exchange framework [4]
Monetary authority
Banco Central de Honduras publishes the banking and economic statistics and supervises elements of the monetary and foreign-exchange system [4]
2025 total goods exports
US$12,190.9 million, including general merchandise and goods for transformation or maquila, under the central bank's preliminary 2025 report [3]
2025 goods imports
US$20,424.6 million on the same preliminary central-bank merchandise-trade reporting basis [3]
2025 goods deficit
US$8,233.7 million based on the paired national 2025 report; a goods deficit should not be confused with the current-account balance [3]
General merchandise share
56.1% of total 2025 goods exports, while goods for transformation accounted for the remaining 43.9% in the report [3]
Maquila export share
43.9% of 2025 goods exports, largely apparel and assembly-linked goods; excluded from some narrower general-merchandise series [3]
2025 coffee exports
Approximately US$2,329.1 million in the central bank report; a major increase reflected especially higher international coffee prices [3]
2025 palm-oil exports
Approximately US$475.5 million in national reporting; export receipts vary with price and crop cycles [3]
2025 shrimp exports
Approximately US$278.3 million in the report; aquaculture production interacts with coastal environments and market conditions [3]
2025 precious-metal exports
About US$249.0 million, much of it gold; mineral prices can change annual export value significantly [3]
Maquila-related goods
Apparel, textiles and electrical automotive harnesses connect Honduras to North American manufacturers and consumers [3]
2025 maquila export value
Goods for transformation totaled about US$5,347.1 million in the detailed 2025 report, with changes in U.S. clothing demand affecting output [3]
Leading merchandise export destination
United States received 47.2% of Honduras's 2025 goods exports, around US$5,759.4 million [3]
Other export destinations
Central American neighbors and European importers are important, with coffee playing a prominent role in European sales [3]
Agricultural sectors
Coffee, bananas, palm oil, melons, sugarcane, livestock and other produce support rural work and food-processing supply chains
Services and tourism
Bay Islands diving, Maya archaeology, heritage towns, coastal and mountain landscapes create foreign travel receipts that are not counted in goods totals
Maritime ports
Puerto Cortés serves as a primary Caribbean trade gateway; Pacific and regional road connections support cross-border movement
Import needs
Fuels, machinery, manufacturing supplies, medicines, food and vehicles create significant foreign-exchange demand [3]
Export value versus quantity
High coffee prices explain part of the nominal 2025 increase; annual export-value growth does not necessarily mean proportional physical-volume growth [3]
Investment and social conditions
Electricity costs, freight, labor skills, security, climate exposure and government institutions affect investment viability and household opportunity

The central bank’s 2025 figures are preliminary goods-trade totals including both general merchandise (56.1% of exports) and goods for transformation or maquila (43.9%). The US$12.1909 billion export amount must not be mistaken for a general-merchandise-only total. Imports are valued CIF in this report, while exports are FOB; remittances and tourist receipts are excluded.

People and Society

Official language
Spanish is the official national language, used in state institutions, most schooling and national media [6]
Indigenous languages
Miskito, Pech, Tawahka and other Indigenous languages remain integral to community knowledge and rights
Garífuna language
Garífuna belongs to an Arawakan linguistic tradition developed through Caribbean and Central American histories, distinct from Spanish and Miskito
English-speaking communities
Bay Islands and Caribbean coastal communities include English and English-based Creole speech shaped by long-standing maritime connections
Lenca people
Lenca communities are especially significant in western and southwestern Honduras, with differing patterns of cultural and language preservation
Maya Ch’ortí people
Communities in western Honduras connect contemporary Indigenous life with the Copán region's wider historical landscape
Miskito communities
Northeastern peoples have distinct institutions, language and regional linkages across the Nicaragua border
2013 official Indigenous/Afro-Honduran count
About 717,620 persons in the official 2013 census grouping, 8.64% of total enumerated population; not an estimate for 2026 [7]
2013 Lenca identification
Approximately 453,672 persons self-identified as Lenca in the cited 2013 national census classification [7]
2013 Garífuna identification
About 43,111 persons self-identified as Garífuna; census category definitions and self-identification warrant care [7]
2013 Miskito identification
Approximately 80,007 respondents identified as Miskito in the 2013 census category [7]
Ethnic-category limitations
Administrative ethnic counts reflect responses to a specific 2013 census question and cannot substitute for language proficiency or current 2026 identity shares
Religion
Roman Catholic and evangelical or Protestant Christian traditions are prominent, alongside other religions and nonaffiliation; precise up-to-date shares are not established here
Religious method
Religious surveys depend on question wording and sampling year, so no undated denominational percentage is asserted as a 2026 measure
Education
Public and private educational institutions provide elementary, secondary and university instruction; rural access and resources differ from metropolitan services
University system
Universidad Nacional Autónoma de Honduras and other higher education institutions support professional training, research and teacher preparation
Healthcare
Public and private providers serve a geographically diverse population; specialist availability, poverty and transport create service inequalities
Rural living conditions
Subsistence farming, informal commerce and seasonal wage work shape livelihoods in mountain and dry-corridor communities
Urban employment
San Pedro Sula and the northern industrial corridor combine maquila, logistics, retail and services with important urban housing needs
Music and performance
Punta and other Garífuna music, regional folk styles and contemporary Latin American genres coexist in Honduran cultural life
Cuisine
Baleadas, tamales, seafood, beans, maize and coconut-based northern coastal dishes represent different regional food traditions
Sports
Football is widely followed; baseball, cycling and water sports also feature in national and local sporting life
Migration
Family connections abroad, especially with the United States, generate remittances and influence social networks and education [1]
Social development priorities
Violence prevention, school retention, health-service access, gender equality, land rights and disaster resilience are recurrent policy concerns

Indigenous and Afro-Honduran counts use census-specific self-identification and the year 2013. Garífuna is both a living linguistic/cultural tradition and a self-identification category; neither can be summarized by one number for 2026. Religious affiliation data should retain publication and sampling dates.

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Presidential constitutional republic with an elected chief executive and unicameral national legislature [6]
Constitutional foundation
The 1982 Constitution, with subsequent amendments and judicial controversies, is the central written framework [6]
Head of state and government
President Nasry Asfura, sworn in January 27, 2026 for a term ending in January 2030 [5]
Preceding president
Xiomara Castro served from January 2022 to January 2026; reference lists dated before that transition may be obsolete [5]
2025 election
Presidential vote held in November 2025; declared narrow outcome was disputed by political rivals, which does not erase the January 2026 inauguration [5]
Executive ministries
Cabinet secretariats carry out policy, subject to constitutional and legislative controls
Legislature
National Congress is unicameral and conventionally has 128 elected deputies; representation is based on electoral law and departmental constituencies [11]
Legislative elections
Congressional deputies are elected for four-year terms through national electoral procedures
Judiciary
Supreme Court of Justice and subordinate courts handle constitutional and ordinary law, with independence and appointment processes recurring public concerns
Supreme Electoral Council successor
Consejo Nacional Electoral administers general elections and related procedures under electoral legislation [10]
National identity registry
Registro Nacional de las Personas performs civil and identity-registration functions relevant to elections and services
Subnational governance
Eighteen departments and their municipalities, headed by elected municipal authorities and supported by departmental administration
Security sector
National Police and other authorized security institutions operate under laws that require public accountability
Military institutions
Armed Forces are constitutionally recognized with defense functions, and their civilian oversight remains part of institutional debate
Transparency debates
Anti-corruption, public procurement, police accountability and influence over courts have been significant issues across administrations
2009 constitutional disruption
The military removal of Manuel Zelaya and ensuing political crisis continue to inform constitutional and democratic debate
2026 executive priorities
The new administration has emphasized jobs, investment, poverty reduction, public services, security and government efficiency [5]
Regional governance obligations
Participation in inter-American legal and diplomatic institutions shapes Honduras's institutional commitments

Officeholder update: Nasry Asfura assumed the presidency on January 27, 2026. The 2025 count was contentious; the government’s current officeholder, opposition claims and constitutional election procedures are distinct facts.

International Profile

United Nations membership
UN member since December 17, 1945; this is separate from the September 1821 independence commemoration [13]
Central American Integration System
Member of SICA and active participant in regional development, transport and institutional coordination
CAFTA–DR
Party to the Dominican Republic–Central America–United States Free Trade Agreement, important for manufacturing and commercial rules
Central American economic integration
Trade with neighboring states involves customs arrangements and shared industrial and agricultural supply chains
United States relationship
Leading export destination in 2025, purchasing 47.2% of Honduras's goods exports, and a major partner in remittances and security policy [3]
China and Taiwan policy
Honduras recognized the People's Republic of China in 2023; President Asfura indicated an intention to seek renewed Taiwan ties, which should not be presented as a completed reversal without confirmation [5]
Nicaragua relations
Border cooperation and Caribbean coast communities tie Honduras and Nicaragua through trade, river basins and migration
El Salvador and Guatemala relations
Regional commerce, security, river systems and historic federal connections shape neighboring relations
Caribbean coastal cooperation
Maritime shipping, fisheries, coral reef protection and hurricane risk link Honduras to wider Caribbean networks
UNESCO World Heritage sites
Two inscriptions, Maya Site of Copán (1980) and Río Plátano Biosphere Reserve (1982) [8]
Biodiversity diplomacy
Tropical forest preservation and Mesoamerican Barrier Reef cooperation combine international science and environmental policy
World Bank and IMF
Participation in multilateral financial institutions supports national statistics and development lending [1]
OAS
Member of the Organization of American States within the inter-American diplomatic and human-rights framework
Time zone
Central Standard Time (UTC−6) throughout the year under normal national practice
Dialing and domain
Country calling code +504; internet country-code top-level domain .hn

Trade and heritage conventions: the U.S. share of 47.2% concerns Honduran goods exports in 2025, not all sales of services. UNESCO currently lists two World Heritage properties, one cultural and one natural; tentative-list nominations are not additional inscriptions.

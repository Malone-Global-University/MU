Country Overview

Barbados is a sovereign island state at the eastern edge of the Caribbean, situated east of the main Windward Islands. Bridgetown, its capital, anchors the principal metropolitan and commercial area. Indigenous settlements preceded English colonization, which began in the seventeenth century; sugar production and African enslavement profoundly shaped its institutions, population and cultural traditions. Independence from the United Kingdom came on November 30, 1966. Barbados became a parliamentary republic on November 30, 2021, replacing the monarch with a ceremonial president while retaining its Westminster-derived cabinet and legislative system. Tourism, business services, construction and selected manufacturing underpin the modern economy, which is exposed to storm, coastal, climate and external-market risks.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Barbados; republic since November 30, 2021 [5]
Capital
Bridgetown; principal governmental, commercial and historic port city [2]
Geographic classification
North America / Caribbean under this MGU index; Americas / Caribbean under UN M49 [10]
Early communities
Indigenous Amerindian settlement and exchange networks preceded the English colonial presence; the island’s population history cannot begin at the seventeenth-century colonial date
English colonization
Permanent English settlement traditionally dated to 1627; colonial landholding expanded with plantation agriculture [13]
Sugar plantation era
During the seventeenth century, Barbados became an intensive sugar-plantation economy built on enslaved African labor and transatlantic commercial networks
Emancipation and social change
British abolition of slavery took effect in 1834; the subsequent apprenticeship system ended in 1838 across much of the British Caribbean
Move to representative democracy
Twentieth-century labor organizing, constitutional reform and the expansion of suffrage contributed to mass electoral politics
First responsible government reforms
The development of ministerial government and internal autonomy preceded the end of British colonial sovereignty
Independence
November 30, 1966, from the United Kingdom [13]
United Nations membership
December 9, 1966 [9]
Republic inaugurated
November 30, 2021: the republic was established, with the president replacing the Queen as head of state; not a second independence date [5]
2025 presidential transition
Jeffrey Davidson Bostic was elected president in October 2025 and installed November 30, 2025 [15]
Current constitutional framework
1966 Constitution, as amended, including the transition to a republic and later parliamentary provisions [5]
National Day
Independence Day, November 30, now also marking the republic’s inauguration
ISO codes

National symbols

State formation: independence in 1966 and republican constitutional change in 2021 are separate milestones. The island’s history also includes Indigenous societies, plantation slavery, emancipation, Black political and social mobilization, self-government and regional integration.

Geography and Population

Location
Eastern Caribbean region; a small island east of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines and Saint Lucia, fronting the Atlantic Ocean [2]
Territorial area
Approximately 430 km² (166 square miles); inland water accounts for little of the national area [2]
Population
282,623 persons (World Bank 2025 country series) [1]
Population growth
Approximately 0.1% in the World Bank 2025 series [1]
Population density
Approximately 657 inhabitants per km² (World Bank 2025 estimate divided by 430 km²), among the higher national densities in the region [1] [2]
Capital region
Bridgetown and the adjoining southern and western settlement corridor contain a large concentration of residents and commerce
Administrative geography
Eleven historical and administrative parishes: Christ Church, Saint Andrew, Saint George, Saint James, Saint John, Saint Joseph, Saint Lucy, Saint Michael, Saint Peter, Saint Philip and Saint Thomas
Highest point
Mount Hillaby in Saint Andrew, approximately 340 metres above sea level
Landscape
Coral limestone terraces, coastal plains, beaches, gullies and the more rugged Scotland District; the island’s east-coast surf and erosion differ from the west-coast setting
Climate
Tropical maritime environment with a wetter season, year-round trade winds and periodic drought and storm exposure
Water resources
Groundwater aquifers, rainfall variability, water-distribution infrastructure and conservation are core environmental concerns
Coastal exposure
Sea-level rise, erosion, marine heatwaves, coral reef stress and severe weather pose risks to housing, fisheries, infrastructure and visitor facilities
Main urban and resort corridors
Bridgetown, Holetown, Oistins, Speightstown and connected urban communities along the southwestern and western coasts
Natural heritage
Marine reefs, seagrass systems, coastal ecosystems and limestone caves; the Scotland District presents a different geology and conservation context
Historic urban heritage
Historic Bridgetown and its Garrison were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2011 [8]
Life expectancy at birth
About 76 years in 2024 (World Bank, rounded) [1]

Area and density interpretation: 657 people per km² is an arithmetic population-to-area calculation based on separate World Bank and geographic inputs, not an independently published census-density estimate. The physical island differs sharply between low-lying coral-terrace areas and the Scotland District.

Economy and International Trade

Economic structure
Service-oriented small-island economy with tourism, international business, professional and business services, construction, trade, agriculture and selected manufacturing [4]
Nominal GDP
US$8.01655 billion in 2025 (World Bank, current U.S. dollars) [1]
Nominal GDP per capita
US$28,364.8 in 2025; not a measure of median salary, wealth or disposable personal income [1]
Real GDP growth
2.7% for 2025 in the World Bank country series and supported by the IMF 2026 report; visitor services and construction were important contributors [1] [4]
Consumer-price inflation
0.8% in 2025 in the World Bank annual consumer price index series [1]
Unemployment
6.5% in the World Bank 2025 ILO modeled rate; IMF reported a domestic unemployment rate of 7.2% in Q4 2025, using a different measure and observation period [1] [4]
Currency
Barbados dollar (BBD, Bds$), issued under national monetary authority [14]
Currency peg
Fixed at B$2 to US$1 since 1975, distinguishing it from the Eastern Caribbean dollar used by several neighboring island economies [14]
Central bank
Central Bank of Barbados [14]
Tourism and visitor economy
Stay-over and cruise tourism support accommodation, transport, entertainment, restaurants and local employment; demand is sensitive to foreign economic conditions [4]
Business and other services
International business, corporate and professional activities, financial and insurance services and domestic commercial services are significant [4]
Merchandise exports
US$494.0 million in 2024 according to WTO/UN Comtrade; refined petroleum goods and rum are prominent physical-product categories [3]
Merchandise imports
US$2.1642 billion in 2024, including fuel, vehicles, food, machinery and industrial inputs [3]
Merchandise trade balance
Approximately US$1.6702 billion deficit on goods in 2024, calculated as US$494.0 million less US$2,164.2 million; service receipts are outside this calculation [3]
Leading goods export markets
United States 20.7%, Jamaica 6.9%, Trinidad and Tobago 6.0%, United Kingdom 5.4% and other/unallocated markets 30.1% in the 2024 WTO dataset [3]
Leading goods import sources
United States 38.5%, Trinidad and Tobago 21.1%, European Union 10.9% and United Kingdom 4.6% in 2024 [3]
Economic policy concerns
Debt sustainability, long-run growth, external financing, climate and coastal resilience, water security, skills and diversification of tourism and business services [4]
Economic geography
Bridgetown and the southwestern coastal corridor host much of the country’s commerce, finance, tourism and public administration

Economic interpretation: in an island economy with large tourism and business-service receipts, the measured merchandise deficit is not the same as the external current-account position. The goods export profile includes petroleum-related products and should not be misconstrued as evidence that Barbados possesses a major oil-exporting economy comparable to large hydrocarbon producers.

People and Society

Official language
English; government, legislation and formal instruction are predominantly in English [5]
Common spoken language
Bajan (Barbadian English-based Creole) alongside standard English; usage varies by setting and community
Population origins
Predominantly African-descended population, reflecting slavery and subsequent social development, with communities of mixed, European, South Asian and other backgrounds
Religious composition
Christian denominations including Anglican, Pentecostal, Methodist, Seventh-day Adventist, Catholic and other churches; smaller non-Christian and nonreligious groups also present
Religion-data caution
Published religious affiliation shares often rely on older surveys or censuses and are not implied to represent a verified 2025 religious census
Culture
Crop Over festival, calypso and soca, literary and visual arts, oral traditions and a rich community and parish culture
Music and performance
Soca, calypso, tuk bands, gospel, steel pan and contemporary regional and international popular music
Cuisine
Cou-cou and flying fish, fish cakes, pudding and souse, macaroni pie and local seafood and vegetable dishes
Sport
Cricket, road tennis, athletics, surfing, swimming and football; Kensington Oval has regional sporting importance
Education
National school system and tertiary institutions including the University of the West Indies Cave Hill campus, Barbados Community College and technical training providers
Public health
Public and private healthcare networks; national health management faces chronic disease burdens, aging and climate-related risks
Rural and urban pattern
The island is comparatively densely settled, with many parishes linked into a continuous commuter and commercial network
Migration and diaspora
Longstanding diaspora ties with the United Kingdom, Canada, United States and other Caribbean states
Social development priorities
Education and workforce skills, social protection, affordable housing, coastal adaptation and resilient public services

Social interpretation: Bajan language use is not interchangeable with ethnic ancestry. Denominational identity, religious practice and formal church membership also require distinct measurement; no fabricated contemporary percentage breakdown is used.

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Unitary parliamentary republic with a ceremonial president and a prime minister responsible for elected government [5]
Head of state
President Jeffrey Davidson Bostic, formally installed November 30, 2025 [15]
Head of government
Prime Minister Mia Amor Mottley, reappointed February 12, 2026 after the February 11 general election [16]
Constitutional structure
1966 Constitution as amended, including 2021 transition to republican head of state and amendments affecting parliamentary composition [5]
Parliament
Bicameral Parliament comprising the House of Assembly and Senate [6] [7]
House of Assembly
30 directly elected constituency representatives in the February 2026 election; constitutional amendment allows an externally selected Speaker who becomes an additional House member [6] [17]
Statutory House seat clarification
The IPU notes a resulting statutory House total of 31 including a Speaker elected from outside the House; this should not be confused with the 30 elected constituencies [17]
Senate
21 appointed members under the republic’s constitutional arrangements [7]
2026 general election
The Barbados Labour Party secured all 30 directly elected seats on February 11, 2026; the election formed the new legislature [17]
Executive
Prime minister, Cabinet and government departments hold day-to-day executive authority in a Westminster-derived system
President’s role
Ceremonial and constitutional head of state; presidential responsibilities must be distinguished from the prime minister’s political management of government [15]
Judiciary
Supreme Court of Judicature, Court of Appeal and Caribbean Court of Justice as final appellate court in place of the Privy Council
Administrative organization
Eleven parishes; the parish system is geographic and historical rather than a direct equivalent to a fully devolved provincial government
Security institutions
Barbados Police Service and Barbados Defence Force
Government accountability
Regular parliamentary elections, an elected lower chamber, appointed upper chamber and constitutional judicial institutions

2026 House distinction: 30 directly elected seats versus a 31-member statutory House after inclusion of a Speaker chosen externally under the 2025 constitutional amendment. This distinction is documented by the IPU and avoids the common inconsistency between electoral-seat counts and total House membership.

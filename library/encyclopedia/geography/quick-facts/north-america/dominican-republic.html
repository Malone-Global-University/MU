Country Overview

The Dominican Republic occupies the eastern portion of Hispaniola in the Greater Antilles, sharing the island and a land border with Haiti. Santo Domingo is its capital and the historic center of Spanish colonial administration in the Caribbean. Its history includes Indigenous Taíno societies, European conquest, African enslavement, independence from Haiti in 1844, a subsequent Spanish annexation and restoration of sovereignty, and twentieth-century dictatorship and democratic transitions. Today it is a presidential constitutional republic whose economy combines tourism, free-zone manufacturing, mining, agriculture, logistics, construction and financial services. Dominican society and culture draw on Indigenous, African, European and broader Caribbean experiences. This country must not be confused with Dominica, the smaller, independent Windward Islands republic.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Dominican Republic (República Dominicana) [2]
Common name
Dominican Republic; not equivalent to the independent country Dominica
Capital city
Santo Domingo, founded during the earliest Spanish colonial settlement era in the Americas [2] [13]
Continent and UN M49 region
Americas / Latin America and the Caribbean / Caribbean; grouped with North America in the MGU index [3]
Indigenous societies
Taíno communities and regional chiefdoms occupied Hispaniola before the arrival of Spanish settlers; conquest and epidemic disease caused catastrophic demographic disruption
European colonial arrival
Columbus arrived at Hispaniola in 1492; Santo Domingo became a principal Spanish colonial administrative and trading center
Colonial labor and slavery
Indigenous labor coercion was followed by increasing use of enslaved Africans in plantations, households, commerce and imperial transport
Island division
The eastern and western parts of Hispaniola developed different colonial and national histories under Spanish and French imperial rule
Haitian unification period
The eastern territory was governed by Haiti from 1822 until 1844; interpretations of its social and political legacies vary
Independence
February 27, 1844, when the Dominican Republic declared independence from Haiti; associated with the Trinitaria and figures including Juan Pablo Duarte [16]
Principal Independence Day
February 27; not to be confused with the country's 1865 restoration or the separate 1821 independence declaration
Spanish reannexation
The Dominican state was annexed to Spain in 1861 under Pedro Santana, prompting armed resistance
Restoration of sovereignty
The Dominican Restoration War ended Spanish annexation in 1865, a separate constitutional and national milestone [16]
U.S. military occupation
The United States occupied the country in 1916–1924, deeply influencing state institutions and fiscal administration
Trujillo dictatorship
Rafael Trujillo dominated politics from 1930 until his assassination in 1961; the regime used extensive repression and racial-nationalist policies
Civil war and U.S. intervention
Political conflict escalated in 1965, accompanied by a major U.S. military intervention
Democratic transitions
Elections, alternation of power, constitutional reforms and opposition politics strengthened in stages, especially after 1978
Constitutional system
Current constitutional framework, substantially amended in 2010 and again in October 2024 [8]
UN membership
Founding UN member admitted October 24, 1945 [15]
ISO 3166-1 codes
DO (alpha-2), DOM (alpha-3), 214 (numeric)
National flag
Divided by a white cross into blue and red quarters, with national coat of arms at the center
National anthem
Quisqueyanos valientes
Historical interpretation
The Dominican Republic's 1821 declaration, 1844 independence, 1865 restoration and modern constitutional revisions describe different events, not a single founding date

Independent milestones: 1844 independence from Haiti and 1865 restoration after Spanish reannexation must be identified separately.

Geography and Population

Total area
48,671 km² in the UN statistical reference; rounding and measurement conventions produce small differences in other references [2]
Geographic position
Eastern Hispaniola, Greater Antilles, bordered by Haiti to the west, the Atlantic Ocean to the north and Caribbean Sea to the south
Shared island
Hispaniola is divided between the Dominican Republic and Haiti; the Dominican Republic occupies roughly the eastern two-thirds by area
Capital metropolitan area
Greater Santo Domingo includes the National District and surrounding municipalities in Santo Domingo Province
Other major urban centers
Santiago de los Caballeros, La Romana, San Pedro de Macorís, Puerto Plata, San Francisco de Macorís, Higüey and La Vega
Relief
Cordillera Central, Cordillera Septentrional, eastern plains and intermontane valleys create varied land use and microclimates
Highest peak
Pico Duarte, approximately 3,098 m, generally recognized as the highest summit in the Caribbean
Largest lake
Lake Enriquillo, a hypersaline lake below sea level in the southwest; its extent changes with precipitation and environmental conditions
Principal river systems
Yaque del Norte, Yuna, Yaque del Sur, Ozama and Artibonite basin tributaries contribute to agriculture, water supply and flooding risks
Coastal features
Northern Atlantic beaches, southern Caribbean ports, coral reefs, mangroves and offshore islands including Saona
2025 population
11,520,487 people in the World Bank's 2025 international series [1]
2022 population census
10,760,028 residents enumerated in the November 2022 census; the date and methodology differ from the 2025 estimate [4]
2025 annual population growth
Approximately 0.8% in the World Bank series; natural increase and migration jointly affect the total [1]
Life expectancy
Approximately 74 years in the 2024 World Bank country indicator, rounded to the nearest year [1]
Climate
Tropical maritime with variations by elevation and rain-shadow zone; trade winds and the wet season affect temperatures and rainfall
Hurricanes and natural hazards
Atlantic hurricanes, flooding, landslides, drought and earthquake hazards require disaster planning and land-use management
Environmental assets
Los Haitises National Park, Jaragua–Bahoruco–Enriquillo landscapes and important coral and mangrove ecosystems
Administrative territory
31 provinces plus the separate National District (Distrito Nacional); municipalities and municipal districts provide lower administrative tiers
UNESCO heritage
Colonial City of Santo Domingo (inscribed 1990) is the country's one inscribed World Heritage property at the current reference date [13]

The 2022 census count of 10,760,028 and the 2025 World Bank population estimate of 11,520,487 are dated observations from different statistical processes.

Economy and International Trade

Economic structure
Upper-middle-income mixed market economy with growing services, free-zone manufacturing, tourism, mining, construction and agriculture
Nominal GDP
US$127.41 billion in 2025, World Bank current US dollars [1]
GDP per capita
US$11,059.2 in 2025, World Bank current dollars; not equivalent to median individual earnings [1]
Real GDP growth
2.1% in 2025 in the World Bank WDI series, distinct from some preliminary national or UN growth releases [1]
2025 inflation
3.9% annual consumer-price inflation in the World Bank country series [1]
Unemployment
5.1% in 2025 (modeled ILO series published by World Bank), not necessarily the identical national labor-force survey definition [1]
International remittances
Personal remittances were equivalent to about 9.7% of GDP in the World Bank's 2025 series; this is not a share of government revenue [1]
Currency
Dominican peso (DOP), often symbolized RD$ domestically
Exchange rate
Peso value fluctuates against the U.S. dollar; point-in-time rates differ from annual averages and are not fixed at a statutory parity
Monetary authority
Central Bank of the Dominican Republic (Banco Central de la República Dominicana), with monetary-policy and banking-system functions [14]
Primary export zones
Special economic/free-zone manufacturing facilities produce medical devices, electrical products, tobacco goods, garments and other manufactures
Tourism economy
Beach tourism, urban heritage, conferences and cruise travel provide foreign-exchange earnings and employment; these are services, not merchandise exports
Mining
Gold and other metallic products are substantial goods exports and are sensitive to commodity price movements [7]
Agricultural exports
Cacao, bananas, tobacco, sugar-related products and other horticultural goods support rural livelihoods and external markets
Merchandise exports
US$14.6452 billion in 2025, according to ProDominicana's national export-promotion and customs reporting; includes free-zone and national-regime exports [5]
2025 export change
Approximately 13.4% above 2024 in ProDominicana's annual national series; changes reflect value as well as volumes and product mix [5]
National export regime
Approximately US$5.458 billion in 2025 under the national customs regime, as reported in the 2026 national competitiveness review; the remaining exports involve other regimes, principally free zones [6]
Principal merchandise categories
Gold, medical instruments, cigars, electrical apparatus, jewelry, cacao and other manufactured and agricultural products are key exports; categories and ranks vary across years [7] [5]
Main destinations
The United States is the largest destination in the WTO's 2024 reporter data; EU members, Haiti, Switzerland and India are also important buyers [7]
2024 WTO goods baseline
US$13.1994 billion in WTO/UN Comtrade reporter data for 2024, a different international dataset and reporting year from ProDominicana's 2025 figure [7]
Merchandise imports
Approximately US$32.7622 billion in the WTO's 2024 goods dataset, driven by energy, machinery, intermediate goods and consumption needs [7]
Goods trade balance
Approximately US$19.56 billion goods deficit on the WTO's 2024 export/import dataset; travel and other services partly offset the external-goods imbalance [7]
Economic risks
Fuel prices, natural disasters, logistics constraints, external demand, fiscal capacity and unequal access to opportunity affect resilience and inclusive development

People and Society

Official language
Spanish is the official language, with diverse regional Dominican speech patterns and a strong presence in education, administration and media
Other languages
Haitian Creole is spoken by Haitian migrants and some Dominican communities; English and French have roles in tourism, education and international commerce
Population heritage
Dominican national identities reflect African, European, Indigenous and other Caribbean experiences; racial classifications differ across social, census and historical settings
Religious composition
Survey compilations referenced in the 2023 U.S. religious-freedom report indicate Catholics above half the population, evangelicals at roughly 20–30%, and nonreligious identification around 18%; these are not census results [12]
Catholic legal status
A 1954 concordat with the Holy See grants the Catholic Church distinct legal privileges, while constitutional freedom of conscience and worship is recognized [12]
Other Christian groups
Evangelical and Pentecostal denominations, Seventh-day Adventists, Jehovah's Witnesses and other Protestant communities have significant activity [12]
Other religious traditions
Judaism, Islam and smaller religious communities are present; some traditions incorporate Afro-Caribbean practices; no current detailed religion census underlies exact shares [12]
Dominican merengue
Merengue music and dance form a major national cultural tradition; bachata and contemporary popular music also have substantial influence
Literature and visual culture
Dominican poetry, novels, painting, film and contemporary performance reflect Caribbean and diasporic experiences
Cuisine
Rice and beans, plantains, sancocho, mangú, fish and other local specialties vary by region and household
Sports
Baseball is a major national sport, with domestic leagues and strong links to professional baseball in North America; other sports include basketball and volleyball
Education
Public primary and secondary schools, national universities and private providers serve a large and youthful population
Healthcare
Public insurance and hospitals coexist with private clinics; access differs among metropolitan, tourism and rural areas
Migration history
Haitian labor migration, Dominican migration to the United States and elsewhere, remittance flows and return migration contribute to social change
Haiti border relations
Cross-border commerce and migration are economically important and politically contentious; nationality, immigration enforcement and human rights require careful distinction
Social inequalities
Differences in income, neighborhood infrastructure, education and access to services remain prominent alongside expanding tourism and industry
Historical heritage
The Colonial City of Santo Domingo is the sole UNESCO World Heritage inscription as of the present source review [13]

Religious-group percentages are based on older surveys rather than a current religious census, and should be labeled accordingly.

Government and Institutions

Government form
Unitary constitutional presidential republic with separation of executive, legislative and judicial branches
Constitution
Constitution of the Dominican Republic, amended in October 2024; provisions for presidential terms and chamber sizes have specified transition rules [8]
Head of state and government
President Luis Abinader Corona, in office since August 16, 2020 and re-elected in May 2024 [10]
Vice president
Raquel Peña, serving in the administration following the 2024 election [10]
Presidential election method
Direct national election with potential second round if the constitutional majority threshold is not achieved
Presidential term
Four years; 2024 amendments added safeguards around limits on presidential re-election and constitutional change [8]
Legislature
Bicameral National Congress (Congreso Nacional) consisting of Senate and Chamber of Deputies [8]
Senate
32 members, one representing each province and the National District, under direct elections [9]
Chamber of Deputies
190 statutory seats for the 2024–2028 legislature; the October 2024 reform calls for 170 seats beginning with the 2028–2032 legislature [8]
Current parliamentary term
Deputies and senators were elected in May 2024 for the current legislative cycle ending in 2028 [8]
Judicial structure
Supreme Court of Justice, Constitutional Court, Electoral Superior Court and lower courts within the national justice system
Electoral administration
Central Electoral Board (JCE) administers national electoral procedures, with separate electoral judicial review
Administrative divisions
31 provinces plus National District, with municipalities and municipal districts as subnational units
Main political organizations
Modern Revolutionary Party (PRM), People's Force (FP), Dominican Liberation Party (PLD) and other political parties
Local government
Municipalities and the National District have elected governing authorities, alongside provincial administration
Foreign relations ministry
Ministry of Foreign Affairs coordinates diplomacy; Víctor 'Ito' Bisonó took office as foreign minister in August 2026 [11]

A constitutional change adopted in 2024 reduces the Chamber of Deputies to 170 members for the 2028–2032 legislature; 190 remains the statutory number for the current 2024–2028 chamber.

International Profile

United Nations
Founding UN member admitted October 24, 1945 [15]
Geographic and diplomatic region
Caribbean and Latin America; shares Hispaniola with Haiti and maritime connections to Puerto Rico and the wider Antilles
Caribbean regional organizations
Participates in the Association of Caribbean States and the Central American Integration System in different capacities
CARICOM status
Not a full CARICOM member; has substantial trade and diplomatic ties with CARICOM economies
Trade agreement with United States
DR–CAFTA links the Dominican Republic with the United States and five Central American partners in a free-trade framework
WTO
WTO member, subject to multilateral goods and services commitments [7]
United States trade
The United States is the leading WTO-reported merchandise-export destination and an important tourism, remittance and investment partner [7]
Haiti relations
Border management, migration, trade, security and humanitarian concerns affect relations with the neighboring sovereign Republic of Haiti
Diaspora and remittances
Dominican communities abroad contribute substantially to family support, entrepreneurship and transnational cultural and political networks [1]
Tourism diplomacy
Visitor demand from North America, Latin America and Europe shapes aviation links, hotel investment and labor markets
UNESCO World Heritage
Colonial City of Santo Domingo, inscribed in 1990; one inscribed property on the UNESCO State Party list as of the current edition [13]
External growth factors
Regional stability, international shipping, oil prices, investment flows and tourism demand affect national prosperity
Environment and adaptation
Coastal ecosystems and watershed protection are linked to hurricane preparedness, marine tourism and disaster management
Migration-rights interpretation
Migration, citizenship and nationality questions should be assessed using precise legal statuses and documented policies; they are not interchangeable ethnic categories
Country-name distinction
The Dominican Republic (DO) is an independent presidential republic on Hispaniola; Dominica (DM) is a separate eastern Caribbean parliamentary republic

Country Overview

Haiti, officially the Republic of Haiti, occupies the western portion of Hispaniola in the Caribbean and shares the island with the Dominican Republic. The republic emerged from the Haitian Revolution, a struggle by enslaved and free people that defeated French colonial rule and culminated in independence on January 1, 1804. Haiti became the first independent state established by a successful mass revolt against slavery and has sustained a distinctive Creole-speaking culture and powerful artistic and intellectual traditions. Its mountainous terrain, extensive coastlines and agricultural communities coexist with concentrated settlement around Port-au-Prince. As of November 2026, the republic is experiencing a severe security and humanitarian crisis and governing through an unelected transitional executive after the expiration of the Transitional Presidential Council. Economic data and humanitarian estimates must be read in light of displacement, institutional disruption and differing statistical periods.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Haiti (République d’Haïti; Repiblik d Ayiti); sovereign Caribbean republic [3]
Common name and demonym
Haiti; Haitian; the modern state emerged from the former French colony of Saint-Domingue
Capital and constitutional seat
Port-au-Prince is named by constitutional Article 1-1 as the capital and seat of government, subject to force-majeure relocation [3]
Continent / UN subregion
North America in the MGU index; Caribbean in the UN M49 regional statistical classification [12]
Taíno and earlier Caribbean history
Indigenous peoples lived on Hispaniola before the 1492 arrival of Columbus; colonial conquest, coerced labor and epidemic disease transformed their societies
Spanish imperial period
Spanish claims and settlements initially covered Hispaniola, but the western part came increasingly under French settlement and control
French colonial Saint-Domingue
The plantation colony became a major exporter of sugar and coffee by using enslaved African labor under extreme coercion
Revolutionary outbreak
The 1791 uprising of enslaved people initiated a revolutionary process shaped by leaders, military alliances and struggles over abolition and sovereignty
Abolition in French Caribbean
The French National Convention abolished slavery in 1794; Napoleon’s later attempt to restore imperial control intensified the war in Saint-Domingue
Haitian independence
January 1, 1804: Jean-Jacques Dessalines proclaimed independence following the revolutionary victory over French forces [13]
Early state institutions
The first decades included imperial and republican experiments and regional political division; the country was not governed continuously under one constitutional scheme
French recognition and indemnity
France recognized Haitian independence in 1825 while imposing a large indemnity that burdened public finance; recognition was not the same as the 1804 founding [13]
Nineteenth-century development
Rural smallholding, regional political contestation, foreign trade and episodes of foreign pressure shaped the republic after independence
U.S. occupation
U.S. Marines occupied Haiti from 1915 until 1934, reorganizing administration and security while generating resistance and enduring controversy [13]
Duvalier period
François Duvalier and then Jean-Claude Duvalier ruled from 1957 to 1986 under authoritarian governments associated with political repression
1987 constitution
Constitution adopted in 1987 and subsequently amended; it provides the republic’s fundamental legal framework, though present political practice diverges from ordinary arrangements [3]
2010 earthquake
The January 12, 2010 earthquake caused extensive loss of life, displacement and destruction of public buildings and urban infrastructure
2021 presidential assassination
President Jovenel Moïse was assassinated July 7, 2021; an elected successor has not been installed as of November 6, 2026 [4]
2024 transitional council
A nine-member Transitional Presidential Council began in April 2024 with a mandate to steer a security and electoral transition [4]
February 2026 executive transition
The council's mandate ended February 7, 2026; executive responsibility passed to Prime Minister Alix Didier Fils-Aimé and the Council of Ministers [4]
National holiday
Independence Day on January 1; Flag Day, May 18, separately commemorates the historic national flag
Official languages
Haitian Creole and French are both official; Haitian Creole is the everyday national language of the great majority [3]
National flag and motto
Blue-over-red flag with national coat of arms; the arms bear L’Union fait la force, while the constitutional motto is Liberté, Égalité, Fraternité [3]
United Nations entry
Original member of the United Nations as of October 24, 1945 [14]
ISO and internet identifiers
HT (alpha-2), HTI (alpha-3), 332 (numeric), and .ht internet country-code top-level domain [12]

Historical chronology: the 1791 uprising, the 1804 independence declaration, the 1825 French recognition and indemnity, and the 1987 Constitution are separate events. The 2024–2026 transitional council did not replace the constitutional concept of an elected presidency.

Geography and Population

Geographic position
Western portion of Hispaniola, between the Caribbean Sea and Atlantic Ocean; the Dominican Republic occupies the island's eastern portion
International border
Only land border is with the Dominican Republic, encompassing mountain valleys, watersheds and significant cross-border communities
Total surface area
27,750 km² in the World Bank's geographic area series; land-only and water-area measures use different denominators [2]
Offshore islands
La Gonâve, Île de la Tortue, Île-à-Vache and smaller islands are included within Haiti’s coastal geographic system
Main peninsula
The southern peninsula projects westward between the Gulf of Gonâve and Caribbean Sea, creating mountainous transport corridors
Gulf of Gonâve
Large central embayment adjoining Port-au-Prince; maritime access and nearby islands form a major part of the national coastal environment
Northern coastal region
Cap-Haïtien and northern plains connect historic plantations, ports, fortress landscapes and agricultural zones
Highland relief
Massif de la Selle, Massif de la Hotte and ranges in the north create steep slopes and variable elevation-related rainfall
Highest mountain
Pic la Selle, commonly cited at approximately 2,680 m, rises in the southeastern mountain region
Major watercourse
Artibonite River system spans the border and runs through the principal agricultural valley toward the Gulf of Gonâve
Agricultural plains
Artibonite Valley and northern and southern coastal lowlands provide important areas for crops and settlement
Lake systems
Étang Saumâtre (Lac Azuéi) and adjacent border-zone lakes are important freshwater and brackish-water environments
Climate zones
Mainly tropical, with cooler upland settings and large differences in rainfall between windward and leeward slopes
Cyclone exposure
Atlantic and Caribbean hurricanes and tropical storms produce flooding, coastal surge and landslides; vulnerability depends on local terrain and housing conditions
Seismic hazards
Active plate-boundary faults make strong earthquakes a major risk; the 2010 and 2021 earthquakes affected different geographic regions
Soil and forest pressures
Hillside erosion, charcoal dependence, land-use change, watershed degradation and pressures on remaining forests challenge environmental management
2025 World Bank population
11,906,095 residents in the World Bank annual modeled series, not a full directly enumerated contemporary census [1]
2025 population growth
Approximately 1.1% in the World Bank annual series; births, deaths, migration and statistical revisions influence estimates [1]
Calculated density
About 429 persons per km² using 2025 World Bank population divided by 27,750 km² total area; settlement distribution is highly uneven [1] [2]
Urban concentration
Port-au-Prince metropolitan settlements are major population centers, with Cap-Haïtien, Gonaïves, Les Cayes and Jacmel important elsewhere
Administrative departments
Ten departments, subdivided into arrondissements, communes and lower local divisions under constitutional and administrative rules [3]
Displacement and counting
UNFPA estimated nearly 1.5 million internally displaced people in October 2026; internal displacement is not equivalent to departure from Haiti [7]
Humanitarian needs
About 6.4 million people were assessed as needing humanitarian assistance in 2026, reflecting deteriorated access and violent insecurity [7]
Life expectancy
Approximately 65 years at birth in the 2024 World Bank series; the value predates the 2026 humanitarian conditions [1]
UNESCO heritage
One inscribed World Heritage property, National History Park – Citadel, Sans Souci, Ramiers (1982); three elements form one listing [11]

Population and humanitarian comparability: 11,906,095 is the 2025 World Bank modeled national population. Nearly 1.5 million internally displaced people in an October 2026 UNFPA update are people displaced within Haiti, not additional residents to be added to the population denominator.

Economy and International Trade

Economic structure
Lower-income mixed economy built around agriculture, apparel assembly, domestic commerce, transport, services and substantial remittances from Haitians overseas
Nominal GDP
Approximately US$32.08 billion for 2025 in the World Bank current-dollar national accounts; not a measure of household purchasing power [1]
Nominal GDP per capita
US$2,694.2 in 2025 in the World Bank current-dollar measure; this average cannot be substituted for median income [1]
Real GDP change
Economy contracted 2.7% in 2025 in the World Bank annual real-growth series amid severe internal disruption [1]
Consumer-price inflation
Approximately 28.6% in 2025 on the World Bank annual consumer-price measure, illustrating broad erosion of money purchasing power [1]
ILO-modeled unemployment
14.9% for 2025 under the international modeled series; informal livelihoods and insecure working hours are not fully described by this indicator [1]
Personal remittance share
Personal remittances were equivalent to roughly 16.9% of GDP in the World Bank's 2024 series; transfers differ from goods or services exports [1]
Domestic currency
Haitian gourde (HTG); exchange-rate variation means foreign-currency GDP and gourde-denominated wages cannot be compared without period-specific conversion [3]
Monetary authority
Banque de la République d'Haïti regulates currency and banking and publishes payments and monetary statistics [10]
Fiscal and development pressures
Limited tax collection, disruption of customs routes, domestic security operations and high humanitarian needs constrain public and private activity [8]
Agricultural livelihood base
Smallholder farming produces maize, rice, beans, roots, fruit, coffee and livestock, though imported food also plays a major role
Apparel assembly
Knit shirts, trousers, sweaters and related clothing exports link industrial employment to North American retail and tariff arrangements [9]
2024 leading export categories
T-shirts, sweaters, suits and essential oils were among principal exports in the 2024 product data summarized by French Treasury authorities [9]
2024 T-shirt exports
Around US$339 million in the referenced 2024 commodity series, a specific product category rather than all merchandise exports [9]
Other apparel products
Sweaters around US$119 million and suits around US$85 million in the same 2024 summary, showing the concentration of goods exports in clothing [9]
Essential oil exports
Approximately US$47 million in 2024 in the referenced trade summary, associated with Haitian agriculture and processing [9]
2025 partial goods exports
French Treasury summary reports approximately US$520 million for October 2024–June 2025, a nine-month fiscal-year-to-date period, not a full calendar-year total [9]
2025 partial imports
About US$3.858 billion for October 2024–July 2025 in the referenced summary; its ten-month window must not be paired mechanically with the export subtotal [9]
Key export markets
The United States was the largest merchandise export destination in 2024, with Canada, Mexico, France and the Dominican Republic also recorded [9]
Energy and food imports
Refined petroleum, rice and other foodstuffs are significant import categories, raising sensitivity to transport, foreign-exchange and global commodity prices [9]
Coffee and cocoa
Historic cash crops remain important in rural communities, although contemporary national commodity volumes are affected by production and logistics constraints
Mango and specialty agriculture
Fruit, vetiver and other specialty crops participate in local and export supply chains, with standards and transport critical to market access
Services activity
Transport, telecommunications, wholesale trade and personal services generate income; wartime-style security disruption restricts operating hours and geographic access
Banking and external receipts
Overseas remittances support household consumption and foreign-exchange supply even when domestic agricultural and manufacturing activity contracts [8]
Trade-balance interpretation
A merchandise trade deficit is not identical to the current-account balance once remittances, services and investment-income flows are taken into account [9]
2026 outlook
IMF projected another real output contraction in fiscal 2026; projections are scenario-based and should not be mistaken for completed-year estimates [8]
Institutional constraints
Reliable infrastructure, electricity, port and road security and banking access remain central development challenges for businesses and households

People and Society

Official languages
Haitian Creole (Kreyòl ayisyen) and French are constitutionally recognized official languages, with markedly different patterns of daily use [3]
Most widely used home language
Haitian Creole is used by the great majority in everyday speech, media, markets and community life, while access to formal French varies
Creole literary and educational movement
Writers, teachers and publishers have expanded Kreyòl language use in literature, literacy programs, broadcasting and civic communication
Religion and public life
Catholic and numerous Protestant traditions are prominent; precise current population shares are not inferred from older surveys
Vodou
Haitian Vodou is an important religious and cultural tradition developed from African, European and Caribbean histories and may coexist with other affiliations
Religious overlap
Some people practice Vodou in addition to Catholic or Protestant identification, so rigid one-choice categories may conceal lived practice
African-descended historical majority
Enslaved Africans and their descendants shaped the Haitian Revolution, farming systems, religious traditions, language and national institutions
Social diversity
Class, region, urban and rural background, migration and family networks produce differences within the national population
Education and language access
Primary education delivery is uneven; costs, insecurity and French-language instruction can complicate participation for Kreyòl-speaking children
2026 education disruption
UN humanitarian reporting documents school closures, displacement-related access barriers and facilities used as emergency shelters [7]
Healthcare infrastructure
Public, charitable and private providers operate with major shortages and transport problems in violence-affected regions [7]
Maternal and reproductive health
Loss of safe transport and clinical capacity particularly affects pregnancy, childbirth and sexual-violence response services [7]
Urban-rural disparity
Employment, specialist medical access and state-service availability differ substantially between major urban areas and mountain communities
Agrarian landholding
Small farms and local markets are important to food security, though erosion and access to productive land affect rural livelihoods
Traditional music
Rara, compas, twoubadou, sacred drumming and regional music express different historical and social traditions
Visual art
Painting, sculpture, metalwork and architectural heritage have produced internationally recognized Haitian artists and schools
Haitian literature
Literature in French and Kreyòl examines emancipation, sovereignty, migration, dictatorship, faith, popular movements and lived everyday experience
Cuisine
Soup joumou, griot, diri ak pwa, pikliz and varied coastal dishes combine agricultural ingredients and national historical memory
Diaspora
Haitian communities in the Dominican Republic, United States, Canada, France and elsewhere sustain remittances and transnational cultural networks
Cultural memory
January 1 soup joumou traditions and the memory of the revolution link foodways to emancipation and independence
Displacement and social protection
Nearly 1.5 million internally displaced persons in October 2026 faced urgent shelter, safety, school and healthcare needs [7]

Social indicators under crisis: language and religious descriptions are qualitative where reliable current census shares are unavailable. The October 2026 humanitarian figures concern needs and access, not a survey of all residents or a statistical reclassification of religious affiliation.

Government and Institutions

Constitutional form
Republic constitutionally based on the 1987 framework as amended, with a separation of executive, legislative and judicial powers [3]
Constitutional executive
Normal constitutional design provides for a president and a prime minister, but no elected president was in office as of November 6, 2026 [3] [4]
Current transitional head of government
Prime Minister Alix Didier Fils-Aimé leads the Council of Ministers following the council handover in February 2026 [4]
Former Transitional Presidential Council
Nine-member body convened in April 2024; mandate ended February 7, 2026, and it should not be described as the current presidency [4]
Executive authority in transition
Prime minister and Council of Ministers held executive responsibility under an exceptional interim arrangement, distinct from the constitutionally normal presidency [4]
Legislative structure in law
Constitution envisages a bicameral National Assembly comprising Senate and Chamber of Deputies; absence of members means it is not functioning normally [3] [5]
Legislature's operational status
No functioning national legislature since January 2020, according to September 2026 Congressional Research Service review [5]
Presidential vacancy
The elected presidency has remained vacant since Jovenel Moïse's assassination in 2021; the transition has not resolved that constitutional gap [5]
Judicial branch
Court of Cassation and other courts are recognized by constitutional law, though security and personnel constraints affect effective functioning [3]
Local administration
Ten departments subdivided into communes and other local authorities; formal legal structures do not guarantee uniform service provision
Election postponement
October 2026 authorities postponed planned December 2026 voting; by October 29, a first round was announced for July 18, 2027, subject to security and future change [6]
Planned subsequent electoral round
October 4, 2027 was announced for a second round and local elections; these were scheduled dates, not completed democratic outcomes [6]
Security services
Haitian National Police remains the national civilian policing institution, working in a severe environment of armed-gang violence [7]
International security cooperation
UN-backed Gang Suppression Force support and international deployments involve complex mandates and do not constitute an elected Haitian government [6]
Human-rights concerns
Armed-group violence, insecurity, access to courts, civilian casualties and displacement raise major protection and governance issues [7]
Constitutional language rights
Article 5 recognizes Haitian Creole and French as official languages; the gap between formal bilingualism and practical access warrants attention [3]
National policy pressures
Public safety, humanitarian support, constitutional legitimacy, credible elections and rebuilding basic services are central governmental priorities
Distinguishing institutions
A prime minister exercising transitional executive responsibility is not equivalent to a newly elected president or a constitutionally renewed parliament

Transitional governance: the 1987 constitutional design assumes elected branches, whereas the November 2026 reality is an unelected executive and a nonfunctioning elected legislature. The July/October 2027 election dates are provisional plans and not evidence that an election has taken place.

International Profile

United Nations
Founding member, October 24, 1945; UN system agencies provide humanitarian, development and human-rights assistance [14]
CARICOM
Haiti is a full member of the Caribbean Community, which has engaged closely in mediation and political-transition consultations [4]
Organization of American States
Participates in the inter-American institutional system and related diplomatic mechanisms
Trade links
United States is a critical market for apparel and source of imports; Dominican Republic border commerce and regional markets are also significant [9]
Dominican Republic relationship
Shared island entails complex migration, border, citizenship, environmental and economic cooperation and disputes
Haitian diaspora diplomacy
Protection of migrants and international family networks influence relations with North American and Caribbean partners
Multilateral financial cooperation
IMF and World Bank programs address economic stabilization, public finance and essential institutional capacity [8]
UN humanitarian response
UNFPA estimated 6.4 million people needing aid in 2026, highlighting the scale of international humanitarian coordination [7]
2026 peace and security transition
International security mission coordination seeks to support Haiti's own security services under contested operating conditions [6]
World Heritage
One UNESCO inscription, the National History Park – Citadel, Sans Souci, Ramiers, originally listed in 1982 [11]
National sovereignty in history
Haitian independence followed the successful antislavery revolution; the country’s early isolation and foreign indemnity have shaped international historical analysis [13]
Time zone
Eastern Standard Time (UTC−5) in winter; daylight-saving practice can vary by government decision and year
Telephone dialing
International calling code +509; internet domain .ht
Environmental cooperation
Caribbean hurricane and earthquake response, watershed management and public-health programs frequently involve international partnerships
Aid data caution
Humanitarian requirements and displacements are needs-based and operational estimates, not substitutes for a decennial population census

UNESCO counts: the Citadel, Sans Souci and Ramiers together form one World Heritage inscription; tentative-list properties do not increase the inscribed count. Security assistance and humanitarian aid are distinct forms of international engagement.

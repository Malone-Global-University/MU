Country Overview

The Republic of the Marshall Islands is a sovereign Micronesian country of coral atolls and low islands in the central Pacific Ocean, conventionally grouped into the Ratak (Sunrise) and Ralik (Sunset) chains. Marshallese voyaging, navigation, kinship and land traditions predate European encounters, German rule, Japanese administration and the United States-administered United Nations Trust Territory. The republic adopted its constitution in 1979 and entered free association with the United States in 1986. Its modern history includes the devastating legacy of U.S. nuclear weapons tests from 1946 to 1958 and the continuing importance of Kwajalein in strategic cooperation. Majuro hosts national institutions and commercial activity; Ebeye is another densely settled community. Fishing, public services, overseas remittances, foreign assistance and maritime activity support livelihoods. President Hilda Heine heads a parliamentary-elected executive. The Marshall Islands has also implemented ENRA, a nationwide eligible-citizen cash benefit distribution program that commenced in November 2025. Population figures distinguish the 2021 census enumeration from the World Bank’s annual 2025 estimate.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of the Marshall Islands, sovereign Micronesian republic with a parliamentary-elected president [4]
Marshallese name
Aolepān Aorōkin Ṃajeḷ is a Marshallese-language name for the republic
Capital
Majuro, located along a major lagoon in the Ratak Chain [2]
Regional identity
Marshall Islands belongs to the United Nations Micronesia subregion of Oceania [13]
Indigenous settlement
Marshallese communities developed distinctive canoe building, navigation, genealogy and island landholding systems long before European rule
Ratak and Ralik heritage
Eastern Ratak and western Ralik chains formed distinct but interconnected social and chiefly networks
Matrilineal inheritance
Lineages connect rights in land, family descent and chiefly authority through historically matrilineal relationships
Spanish contact
Spanish navigators encountered parts of the archipelago during sixteenth-century Pacific voyages
German protectorate
Germany established colonial rule in the late nineteenth century with copra commerce and changing chiefly relationships
Japanese administration
Japan occupied the islands in 1914 and later governed them under a League of Nations mandate
World War II campaigns
U.S. forces captured important islands and atolls during the Pacific War, including Kwajalein and Enewetak
Trust Territory
After the war, the United States administered the area within the UN Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands
Nuclear testing period
The United States conducted 67 nuclear tests in the Marshall Islands from 1946 to 1958 [7]
Bikini displacement
People were displaced from Bikini in connection with nuclear tests; contamination and resettlement effects endured [6]
Enewetak testing
Enewetak also hosted U.S. nuclear testing and subsequent cleanup, affecting land and community returns [7]
Castle Bravo
The 1954 Bikini thermonuclear test caused widespread fallout, including exposure on neighboring atolls [7]
Constitutional adoption
The republic's constitution took effect in 1979 and provided for an elected Nitijela [4]
Free association
The U.S. Compact of Free Association took effect in 1986, establishing the modern sovereign relationship [4]
Trusteeship closure
The UN trusteeship for the Marshall Islands was formally terminated in 1990, after entry into free association
United Nations admission
Joined the UN on September 17, 1991 [2]
Modern political tradition
Presidents are selected from the legislature, combining republican independence with parliamentary accountability
Hilda Heine presidency
Hilda C. Heine became the first woman president in 2016 and returned to office after the 2023 election [4]
2023 general election
Nitijela elections took place in November 2023, followed by presidential selection [4]
Customary Council
Council of Iroij gives guidance on traditional matters and customary law [4]
Nuclear claims legacy
Survivor welfare, compensation and environmental remediation are subjects of continuing public policy and international dialogue [7]
National holiday
Constitution Day on May 1 marks the 1979 constitutional order
National symbols
A blue flag with orange and white rays and a star reflects a maritime island republic
Currency status
The U.S. dollar is used as the national currency; RMI has no independent floating currency [2]
ISO and domain
MH, MHL, numeric 584; internet country-code top-level domain .mh [13]

Chronology: the 1979 constitution, 1986 entry into free association and 1991 UN admission are distinct milestones. Nuclear tests occurred during U.S. administration, before independence, and have continuing human and environmental consequences.

Island chains
Ratak (Sunrise) chain in the east and Ralik (Sunset) chain in the west

Outer atolls
Arno, Ailinglaplap, Mili, Jaluit, Wotje and other inhabited atolls have diverse local economies
Marine jurisdiction
An extensive exclusive economic zone extends far beyond the small terrestrial territory
Sea level
Most dry land sits only a few meters above mean sea level, increasing coastal exposure
King tides

Groundwater lens
Freshwater can accumulate as thin lenses above saline groundwater, making water quality fragile
Rainwater capture
Storage and rainwater harvesting are essential sources of drinking water in many communities
Drought exposure
El Niño-related rainfall changes can worsen drought in northern atolls
Coral reefs
Fringing and barrier reef systems protect shorelines, support fisheries and harbor high marine biodiversity
Lagoon ecosystems
Reef lagoons sustain nearshore fish, seagrass, coral and shellfish as well as transport routes
Ocean circulation
Pacific currents and tropical weather shape coral habitats, navigation, marine debris and fisheries
Tropical cyclones
The islands can experience damaging winds and storm surges despite variable historical storm frequency
Climate warming
Marine heatwaves and warming seas create coral-bleaching threats
Coastal waste
Dense settlement and limited landfill space create wastewater and solid-waste management problems
Administrative geography
The state has island and atoll electoral constituencies rather than inland provinces [4]

Population definitions: 42,418 people were enumerated in the 2021 national census, compared with 36,282 in the World Bank 2025 annual estimate. The UN Data 181 km² surface figure and alternative government land-area estimates use different geographic scopes.

Tourism profile

Employment market

Remittance dependence

Infrastructure priorities

State system
Independent republic with an elected legislature and a president chosen from legislators [4]
Constitution
The constitution took effect on May 1, 1979, before the 1986 free-association agreement [4]
President
Hilda C. Heine, serving a second presidential term following 2023 general elections [4]

The president performs both constitutional roles, without a separate prime minister [4]

The Nitijela elects its president from among its members after national legislative elections [4]
Nitijela
Unicameral parliament with 33 senators representing inhabited atolls and islands [4]
Electoral constituencies
Twenty-four constituencies select one or more senators depending on population arrangements [4]
Parliamentary terms
The Nitijela is normally elected for four-year terms under national law [4]
Last general election

Cabinet

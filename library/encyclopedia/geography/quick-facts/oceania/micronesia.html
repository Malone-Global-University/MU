Country Overview

The Federated States of Micronesia (FSM) is a Pacific federal republic composed of the geographically separated states of Yap, Chuuk, Pohnpei and Kosrae. Its high volcanic islands and coral atolls contain distinct languages, clan structures, navigational traditions and political histories. European colonial administrations were followed by Japanese rule and the U.S.-administered United Nations Trust Territory. An FSM constitution entered into effect in 1979; the country became fully self-governing in free association with the United States in 1986 and joined the United Nations in 1991. Palikir on Pohnpei serves as the national capital, while the state governments retain substantial powers. President Wesley W. Simina and Vice President Aren B. Palik lead the federal executive elected by Congress. Public administration, fisheries, farming, tourism, services and Compact-related economic assistance are important to livelihoods, with significant outward migration. A major statistical issue is the difference between the 75,817 residents recorded in the 2023 national census and the World Bank’s 113,683-person 2025 annual estimate; both are presented with explicit attribution rather than as interchangeable counts.

Country Overview

The Independent State of Papua New Guinea occupies the eastern half of the island of New Guinea and numerous island groups in Melanesia, including New Britain, New Ireland, Manus and Bougainville. Its communities preserve extraordinary linguistic and ecological diversity, with more than 800 Indigenous languages and traditions of highland agriculture, coastal exchange and maritime navigation. The ancient Kuk agricultural landscape, inscribed by UNESCO, documents independent developments in wetland farming. Colonial partition, German and British/Australian administration, world war and decolonization preceded independence on September 16, 1975. Port Moresby is the capital, and national institutions operate in a parliamentary constitutional monarchy with King Charles III as head of state, Governor-General Sir Bob Dadae and Prime Minister James Marape. Gold, liquefied natural gas, copper, agricultural crops and fisheries dominate merchandise exports, while many households depend on customary land, gardening and local markets. The 2019 Bougainville referendum strongly favored independence, but the constitutional political-status process remained unresolved at the November 6, 2026 cutoff. The national 2024 census, 2025 World Bank population and GDP series, and PNG statistical office 2025 GDP and goods-trade data differ in scope and compilation and are reported separately.

Country Overview

Kiribati is a sovereign Pacific island republic whose islands and atolls are dispersed across the central ocean, spanning both sides of the Equator and the former International Date Line. Its major groups are the Gilbert, Phoenix and Line islands; South Tarawa contains the principal national government institutions, while Banaba is a distinctive raised limestone island with a history of phosphate extraction. I-Kiribati communities maintain strong maritime, land, family and maneaba meeting-house traditions. British colonial institutions and the former Gilbert and Ellice Islands administration preceded independence on July 12, 1979. Kiribati’s economy depends heavily on fisheries access revenue, public services, marine resources, external support, trade and household remittances. The country uses the Australian dollar and has a presidential republican system led by Taneti Maamau. Its low-lying atolls face serious coastal erosion, freshwater, crowding and climate-adaptation pressures. The official 2020 population census, the World Bank’s 2025 population and GDP estimates, and the Kiribati National Statistics Office’s November 2026 preliminary GDP release have different coverage and methodologies and are identified separately.

Kiribati was independent from July 12, 1979. Its republic has an elected president, not a prime minister as head of government. The name “Kiribati” reflects the Gilbertese pronunciation of “Gilberts.”

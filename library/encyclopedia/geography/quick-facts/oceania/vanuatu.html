Country Overview

Vanuatu is an independent Melanesian republic formed by an arc of volcanic and coral islands in the southwest Pacific, east of Australia and west of Fiji. Ni-Vanuatu communities speak many Indigenous languages and maintain distinctive customary land and chiefly systems. From the early twentieth century the New Hebrides were administered under an unusual joint British-French condominium, until independence on July 30, 1980. The capital is Port Vila on Efate, while Luganville on Espiritu Santo is another important urban center. Vanuatu has a parliamentary republican system with President Nikenike Vurobaravu as head of state and Prime Minister Jotham Napat as head of government following the January 2025 election. In December 2024 a destructive magnitude-7.3 earthquake struck near Port Vila, compounding the country's exposure to tropical cyclones, volcanoes and climate shocks. The economy depends on tourism and other services, agriculture, fisheries, kava, coconut-related exports, construction and labor connections abroad. In June 2026 Vanuatu signed the Nakamal Agreement with Australia, while negotiations concerning the disputed Matthew and Hunter Islands remained politically significant. Its 2020 census of 300,019 people is not the same series or date as the World Bank 2025 modeled population of 335,169.

The former Anglo-French condominium of the New Hebrides ended with independence on July 30, 1980. “Vanuatu” is neither a British overseas territory nor a French collectivity. Bislama, English and French have defined national constitutional roles, while many Indigenous vernaculars remain widely used.

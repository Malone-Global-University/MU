Country Overview

Solomon Islands is a Melanesian archipelagic state east of Papua New Guinea, comprising six large island groups and numerous smaller islands, reefs and atolls. Its communities speak many Oceanic and other local languages and maintain distinctive customary land, kinship and maritime traditions. British protectorate administration, World War II fighting around Guadalcanal, and independence in 1978 shaped the modern state. Honiara, on Guadalcanal, is the capital. A constitutional monarchy with King Charles III as sovereign, the country is governed through a 50-constituency Parliament and a prime minister responsible to it. In May 2026, Matthew Cooper Wale succeeded Jeremiah Manele after a no-confidence vote; Governor-General Sir David Tiva Kapu continued to hold the Crown's representative office. Forestry, gold and other mineral exports, tuna, cocoa, coconuts, agriculture and services are important to the economy. The 2019 final census count differs from its earlier provisional announcement, while World Bank 2025 population estimates and IMF trade projections use different methods and periods.

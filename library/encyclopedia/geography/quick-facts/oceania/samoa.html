Country Overview

Samoa is an independent Polynesian island state in the South Pacific, centered on the volcanic islands of Upolu and Savai‘i and extending to several smaller inhabited and uninhabited islands. Longstanding Samoan settlement and chiefly institutions shape the cultural framework known as fa‘a Samoa, while colonial German and New Zealand administration influenced later institutions. Samoa became independent in 1962 and maintains a parliamentary republic whose head of state, the O le Ao o le Malo, is separate from its prime minister. Apia is the capital and principal transport and commercial center. After the 2025 general election, Prime Minister Laaulialemalietoa Leuatea Polataivao Fosi Schmidt formed the government; Tuimalealiifano Vaaletoa Sualauvi II continues as head of state at the November 6, 2026 cutoff. Tourism, agriculture, financial services, household remittances and overseas connections are economically important. The official 2021 census is distinct from modeled 2025 population data, while the latest trade account cited here records a July–June fiscal year rather than a calendar-year headline.

Independence began on January 1, 1962 under the name Western Samoa; the state adopted the name Samoa in 1997. Samoa is separate from American Samoa, a United States territory. Traditional chiefly authority and national constitutional offices are not identical.

Land ownership

Country Overview

Nauru, formally renamed the Republic of Naoero in 2026, is a very small sovereign island republic in the central Pacific. Its single raised-coral island comprises a narrow coastal settlement belt, an elevated interior profoundly transformed by phosphate extraction, and surrounding reef and ocean waters. Indigenous Nauruan society experienced German colonial control, Australian-led administration, Japanese wartime occupation and a postwar United Nations trusteeship before independence on 31 January 1968. Yaren is the seat of government, although the constitution does not establish an official capital. Government, fisheries-access income, phosphate-related activity, external partnerships and public services dominate economic life, while water security, ecological rehabilitation and coastal pressures are persistent challenges. President David Adeang was reelected by the 19-member Parliament in October 2025. A 2026 constitutional naming reform restored Naoero internationally; the established name Nauru is retained in the page title and URL for reader continuity. The World Bank’s 2025 population and national accounts are not a direct contemporary census.

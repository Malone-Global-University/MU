Country Overview

Seychelles, officially the Republic of Seychelles, is an Indian Ocean archipelago northeast of Madagascar and a sovereign member of the African regional system. The country comprises approximately 115 islands, with Victoria on Mahé as its capital. French settlement began in 1770; British colonial rule was established in the Napoleonic era, and the country became independent on June 29, 1976. Seychelles is a multiparty presidential republic led by President Patrick Herminie, who took office in October 2025, and Vice President Sebastien Pillay. Tourism, fisheries, fish processing and marine services underpin its high-income island economy. [8] [9] [6] [11] [12] [2]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Seychelles (French: République des Seychelles; Seychellois Creole: Repiblik Sesel). [10]
Common name
Seychelles.
Demonym
Seychellois (singular and plural); also used adjectivally.
Capital
Victoria, on northeastern Mahé. [5] [6]
Continent
Africa.
United Nations subregion
Eastern Africa, notwithstanding its Indian Ocean island setting. [5]
Geographic country type
Sovereign archipelagic island republic in the western Indian Ocean. [7]
Historical habitation
The islands had no established permanent population before European-led settlement in the eighteenth century. [8] [9]
Early maritime contact
Portuguese charts and early English navigation record knowledge of the islands well before permanent settlement. [8]
French possession
1756: France asserted possession of the islands; French-administered settlement followed. [8]
First permanent settlement
August 27, 1770: French-led settlers, including enslaved Africans and people from India, arrived at Sainte-Anne. [24]
Name origin
The name derives from Jean Moreau de Séchelles, a minister in the French royal government. [8]
Development of Victoria
The French settlement on Mahé became the nucleus of the later capital, Victoria. [8] [9]
British rule
British occupation began in the Napoleonic era; sovereignty passed under the 1814 Treaty of Paris. [9]
Separate Crown colony
August 31, 1903: Seychelles became a British Crown colony separate from Mauritius. [9]
Independence
June 29, 1976, from the United Kingdom. [6] [20]
Independence Day / National Day
June 29, annually. [6]
First president
James R. Mancham, upon independence in 1976. [9]
One-party period
Following the 1977 coup, the country experienced extended one-party rule under France-Albert René. [9] [2]
Modern multiparty constitutional milestone
1993: adoption of the current multiparty constitutional framework and competitive elections. [10] [2]
Democratic change in 2020
Wavel Ramkalawan won the presidency in the 2020 election, marking an opposition-party presidential transition. [2]
Democratic change in 2025
Patrick Herminie defeated incumbent Wavel Ramkalawan and took office October 27, 2025. [11] [2]
Official languages
Seychellois Creole (Kreol Seselwa), English and French; the Constitution calls them national languages. [10]
Currency
Seychellois rupee (SCR); 100 cents. [6]
International telephone code
+248.
ISO country codes
SC (alpha-2), SYC (alpha-3); ISO 3166.
Internet country-code domain
.sc.
Time zone
Seychelles Time (SCT), UTC+4; no seasonal clock change.
Driving convention
Left-hand traffic.
National flag
Five diagonal bands fanning outward: blue, yellow, red, white and green.
Political identity
Sovereign democratic republic, constitutionally governed. [10]

State formation: French settlement in 1770, the transfer to Britain in 1814, independence on June 29, 1976 and the 1993 multiparty constitution are distinct milestones. [8] [9] [6] [10]

Geography and Population

Location
Western Indian Ocean, northeast of Madagascar and east of mainland East Africa. [7]
Archipelago size
Commonly described as approximately 115 islands. [7] [6]
Total land area
About 455 km² in the SADC country profile; international references use slightly different area definitions. [6] [23] [5]
UN statistical surface-area convention
457 km² in the UNData country profile, rather than the 455 km² SADC figure. [5] [6]
Island geography
Granitic inner islands and low-lying coralline outer islands; their coastal and ecological settings differ. [7]
Largest island
Mahé, containing Victoria and the main airport. [7]
Other major inhabited islands
Praslin and La Digue. [7]
Other notable islands
Silhouette, Curieuse, Sainte-Anne, Fregate, Bird and Denis among others. [7]
Remote island groups
Aldabra group, Amirantes, Farquhar group and other outer islands. [7] [18]
Terrain
Coastal plains, granite hills and steep vegetated slopes on the main granitic islands. [6]
Highest point
Morne Seychellois on Mahé, approximately 905 m above sea level.
Ocean setting
A very small terrestrial territory amid a large marine jurisdiction and ecosystem. [21] [2]
Climate
Tropical maritime, moderated by the ocean and seasonal trade winds. [7]
Seasonal winds
Northwest monsoon and southeast trade-wind seasons influence weather and maritime activity. [7]
Rainfall
Generally wetter in the northwest monsoon season; varies substantially by altitude and island.
Natural vegetation
Tropical forests, coastal vegetation and specialized island ecosystems. [18] [21]
Coral reefs
Coral habitats provide fisheries, tourism value and coastal protection but face warming-related stress. [2] [21]
Indigenous wildlife
Aldabra giant tortoises, endemic birds and rare plant species are important conservation features. [21] [18]
World Heritage—Aldabra
Aldabra Atoll, UNESCO-listed natural property since 1982. [18]
World Heritage—Vallée de Mai
Vallée de Mai Nature Reserve on Praslin, UNESCO-listed natural property since 1983. [18]

Victoria and the surrounding settlements on Mahé. [5]

Climate risks
Sea-level rise, coastal erosion, marine heat stress, coral bleaching and extreme rainfall are salient concerns. [2] [21]
Freshwater vulnerability

Protected areas

People and Society

Population identity
A culturally mixed Creole society with historical African, European and Asian influences. [7] [6]
Principal spoken language
Seychellois Creole (Kreol Seselwa), central to everyday conversation. [10] [6]
National languages
Creole, English and French under Article 4 of the Constitution. [10]
English
Used in government, education, law, commerce and international communication. [10]
French
Used in culture, media, religion and other public or private contexts. [10]
Creole literary culture
Kreol Seselwa has written literature, theatre, music and contemporary cultural use. [6]
Religion—Roman Catholic
Approximately 70% according to figures identified as 2022-census data in the 2023 U.S. religious-freedom report. [19]
Religion—Anglican
Approximately 6% in the same reported 2022 census religion data. [19]
Religion—Hindu
Approximately 6% in the same reported 2022 census religion data. [19]
Religion—Muslim
Approximately 3% in the same reported 2022 census religion data. [19]
Other faith communities
Includes other Protestant and Christian communities, Bahá’ís, Buddhists and smaller religious traditions. [19]
Religious freedom
Freedom of conscience and religion protected under the constitutional framework. [10] [19]
Cultural heritage
Creole identity reflects the settlement histories and traditions of formerly enslaved Africans and later migrants. [24] [6]
Cuisine
Seafood, rice, coconut milk, spices and tropical fruits are prominent in Seychellois cooking.
Music and dance
Traditional moutya, sega and other Creole performance forms are associated with the islands.
Major celebration
National Day is celebrated on June 29 to mark independence. [6]
Constitutional milestone commemoration
June 18 is associated with the 1993 constitutional transition. [10]
Education
Public schooling and multilingual instruction are important national services.
Healthcare
Public health services and prevention efforts are part of the country’s social infrastructure.
Youth and labor
A mix of citizen and foreign-resident workers supports tourism, construction and related activities. [3] [4]
2024 citizenship distinction
NBS reported 101,093 Seychellois citizens within 122,038 estimated residents at year-end 2024. [3]
Population statistical categories
All-resident population includes foreign residents; Seychellois-only citizen totals are smaller. [3]
Urban and rural settlement
Settlement clusters on Mahé and other inhabited islands, with many outer islands sparsely populated. [7] [2]
Cultural and environmental tourism
Wildlife, beaches, marine biodiversity and nature reserves are major elements of the visitor economy. [18] [2]
National heritage conservation
Natural and intangible Creole cultural heritage are both important. [21] [6]
Sport and recreation
Football, swimming, athletics, watersports and fishing are prominent recreational activities.

Religious data: The 2023 international religious-freedom report cites the 2022 census for approximate religious proportions, which should not be re-labeled as 2025 or 2026 statistics. [19]

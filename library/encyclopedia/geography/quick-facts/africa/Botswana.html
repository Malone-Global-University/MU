Country Overview

Botswana, officially the Republic of Botswana, is a landlocked Southern African country whose landscapes include much of the Kalahari basin, the Okavango Delta and the Chobe region. It borders South Africa, Namibia, Zimbabwe and Zambia. Formerly the British-administered Bechuanaland Protectorate, Botswana gained independence on September 30, 1966, with Sir Seretse Khama as its first president. The country is a multiparty presidential republic and underwent its first change of governing political party since independence following the October 2024 general election. Diamond mining remains a major economic pillar, alongside government services, tourism, trade and other industries. [5] [8] [3]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Botswana (in Setswana: Lefatshe la Botswana).
Common name
Botswana.
Former colonial designation
Bechuanaland Protectorate under British administration (1885–1966). [5]
Capital
Gaborone; seat of national executive and legislative institutions.
Continent / UN M49 subregion
Africa / Southern Africa; UN M49 country code 072. [11]
Precolonial historical context
Communities including Tswana-speaking chiefdoms, San peoples and other societies occupied and governed parts of the region long before British administration. [5]
British protectorate established
1885; Bechuanaland Protectorate. [5]
Move of government seat
1965: administrative seat transferred from Mafikeng (in present-day South Africa) to Gaborone. [5]
Internal self-government
1965, preceding full independence. [5]
Independence / state formation
September 30, 1966: independent Republic of Botswana, formerly the Bechuanaland Protectorate. [5]
Independence legislation
United Kingdom Botswana Independence Act 1966; independence took effect September 30. [6]
First president
Sir Seretse Khama, 1966–1980. [5]
Current constitution
Constitution of Botswana, originally adopted upon independence in 1966 and subsequently amended. [7]
Democratic transition in 2024
Opposition Umbrella for Democratic Change (UDC) won the October 30, 2024 national election, ending Botswana Democratic Party dominance since independence; Duma Boko was sworn in November 1. [8]
National holiday
Botswana Day / Independence Day, September 30.
ISO 3166-1 identifiers
BW (alpha-2); BWA (alpha-3); 072 (numeric). [11]
Internet country-code domain
.bw.
National emblematic animal
Zebra appears on the coat of arms; wildlife is a prominent national and tourism emblem.

Historical distinction: The modern republic dates from the 1966 independence settlement. Indigenous societies, regional chieftaincies and communities preceded colonial boundary-making; 1885 marked the start of British protectorate administration, not Botswana's founding as an inhabited society.

Geography and Population

Total surface area
581,730 km² (approximately 224,607 sq. mi.), according to the Government of Botswana. [4]
Land area
566,730 km² (2024, World Bank / FAOSTAT series); land excludes inland water. [12]
Geographic location
Landlocked country in Southern Africa, north of South Africa.
Bordering countries
South Africa, Namibia, Zimbabwe and Zambia; the short Zambian border lies by the Kazungula crossing. [15]
Capital city
Gaborone, in southeastern Botswana near the South African border.
Principal urban centers
Gaborone, Francistown, Maun, Selebi-Phikwe, Lobatse and other urban municipalities.
Population estimate
2,562,122 (2025; World Bank series, international estimate). [1]
National census population
2,359,609 (2022 Population and Housing Census, final count; Statistics Botswana). [2]
Population growth
Approximately 1.6% (2025 annual growth, World Bank). [1]
Population density
About 4.5 people per km² of land (illustrative calculation from the 2025 World Bank population estimate divided by 2024 land area; not an official density release).
Principal landscapes
Kalahari sand basin and savannas, northern wetlands, the Okavango Delta, Makgadikgadi salt pans, Chobe region, and southeastern uplands. [4]
Major river and wetland systems
Okavango River and inland delta; Chobe and Linyanti waters; Limpopo-linked drainage in the east; seasonal pans. [13]
Climate
Predominantly semi-arid with strong seasonal variation in rainfall; regional differences between the arid southwest and wetter north.
World Heritage places
Okavango Delta (natural site, inscribed 2014) and Tsodilo (cultural site, inscribed 2001). [13]
Environmental and land-use context
Conservation areas, rangeland and livestock production coexist with water scarcity, wildlife management, tourism and mining.

Measurement note: Total area includes inland water, while land area excludes it. The 2025 international population estimate and the 2022 national census count are different series and should not be treated as simultaneous measurements.

Economy and International Trade

Economic system
Upper-middle-income, mixed market economy in which diamond mining, public services, finance, trade, tourism and cattle-related activities have substantial roles.
Nominal GDP
Approximately US$19.93 billion (2025; World Bank, current U.S. dollars). [1]
Nominal GDP per capita
Approximately US$7,778 (2025; World Bank, current U.S. dollars); national output per resident, not average wages. [1]
Real GDP growth
−0.7% in 2025 (World Bank annual series), reflecting weak economic activity amid difficult diamond markets. [1]
Annual consumer-price inflation
2.7% (2025; World Bank annual series). [1]
Modeled unemployment
24.5% in 2025 (ILO-modelled estimate displayed by World Bank; not directly comparable with all national survey definitions). [1]
Currency
Botswana pula (BWP), divided into 100 thebe. [4]
Central bank
Bank of Botswana, responsible for monetary and currency policy.
Principal industries
Diamond mining and processing, copper mining, business and financial services, government services, cattle and beef production, tourism and retail trade.
Diamond-sector dependence
Diamonds remain a central source of export receipts; weak international demand can materially affect revenue and economic growth. [3] [14]
Merchandise exports
US$3,422.0 million (2024; WTO reported goods trade). [3]
Merchandise imports
US$6,986.8 million (2024; same WTO reported goods-trade series). [3]
Merchandise trade balance
Approximately US$3,564.8 million deficit (2024; calculated as exports minus imports using the WTO series; excludes services). [3]
Largest individual goods-export destination
United Arab Emirates, 26.5% of 2024 WTO-reported merchandise exports. [3]
Other major goods-export markets
South Africa (16.1%), India (12.8%), European Union reported as a bloc (10.2%), Australia (10.1%) and China (9.8%); WTO 2024 shares. [3]
Major merchandise import origin
South Africa accounted for 60.7% of reported merchandise imports in 2024; Namibia was next at 14.2%. [3]
Leading goods-export classifications
Unworked gem diamonds, copper ores and concentrates, worked diamonds, wiring sets, soda ash and beef; WTO 2024 product classifications. [3]
Trade framework
Membership in the Southern African Customs Union (SACU) and Southern African Development Community (SADC); also party to the African Continental Free Trade Area agreement. [15] [16]

People and Society

Demonym
Motswana (singular); Batswana (plural).
Official language
English, used extensively in government and formal institutions. [4]
National language
Setswana, a widely spoken language of public life and everyday communication. [4]
Other languages spoken
Kalanga, Shekgalagari, Shona varieties, Ndebele, Sheyeyi, Sesarwa languages, Thimbukushu, Chisubia, Otjiherero, Afrikaans and others; usage varies by region. [9]
Setswana language prevalence
The 2022 census identifies Setswana as the most widely reported language spoken at home; census language responses should not be interpreted automatically as ethnic identity. [9]
Ethnolinguistic communities
Tswana communities of several historical merafe, Kalanga, San communities of diverse identities, Herero, Wayeyi, Mbukushu and others; classifications should reflect each group’s own identity.
Religious composition: Christian
86.5% of respondents with valid religious data in the 2022 census analytical report. [9]
Religious composition: Badimo
4.6% identifying with Badimo / African traditional religion in the same census religious table. [9]
Religious composition: no religion
7.1% in the same 2022 census religious table. [9]
Other religious affiliation
Muslim 0.6%, Hindu 0.2%, Rastafarian 0.2% and other categories (2022 religious-response dataset). [9]
Religious data denominator
The census religion table reports n = 1,680,964 valid observations, not all 2,359,609 residents; do not apply percentages as an exact total-population registration count. [9]
Constitutional religious protection
Freedom of conscience and religion is protected by the Constitution. [7]
Life expectancy at birth
Approximately 69 years (2024; World Bank country indicator). [1]
2022 census household count
684,844 households (2022 national census analytical report). [10]
2022 average household size
3.3 persons per household (2022 census analytical report). [10]
2022 census population density
Approximately 4.1 persons per square kilometre (2022 census analytical report); differs from the calculated density using 2025 population. [10]
Principal cultural institutions
Traditional kgotla consultation and dikgosi institutions interact with modern parliamentary and local-government structures. [7]
Education system
National public and private education services spanning primary, secondary and tertiary levels; language of instruction varies by level and policy.
Health and social context
Major public health priorities include HIV prevention and treatment, communicable disease control, maternal health and universal access to essential services.

Religious and linguistic survey note: The 2022 census percentages describe valid responses within specified questions and age/coverage definitions. Religious practice may overlap with formal affiliation, and language at home does not determine ethnicity.

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Unitary multiparty presidential republic with a constitutional executive president and a directly elected National Assembly. [4] [7]
Constitutional foundation
1966 Constitution of Botswana, as amended. [7]
Head of state and head of government
President Duma Gideon Boko (in office since November 1, 2024; checked November 4, 2026). [17] [8]
Vice president
Ndaba Nkosinathi Gaolathe, also Minister of Finance (current official leadership listing). [17]
Governing political alliance
Umbrella for Democratic Change (UDC), which won the 2024 general election. [8]
Executive
President, vice president, cabinet ministers and ministries; executive powers established by the Constitution. [7]
Legislature
National Assembly with 61 directly elected members and six specially elected members, plus president ex officio; a speaker elected from outside its membership adds another seat. [7]
Traditional advisory institution
Ntlo ya Dikgosi is an advisory body on customary, cultural, constitutional and land matters; it is not a coequal legislative chamber. [7]
National election cycle
General elections normally held every five years. [4]
2024 electoral transition
UDC won 36 of 61 directly elected constituencies in October 2024, replacing the Botswana Democratic Party after decades of continuous rule. [8]
Judiciary
Independent court system headed by the Court of Appeal and High Court, with subordinate and customary courts. [7]
Chief justice
Justice Gaolapelwe Ketlogetswe (official leadership listing as of editorial cutoff). [17]
Legal system
Roman-Dutch-derived common law alongside statute and customary legal institutions. [4]
Local governance
Local authorities administer services across urban and district jurisdictions; the responsible ministry lists 31 councils (2026 official listing). [18]
National administration
Government ministries and agencies headquartered principally in Gaborone.
Citizenship and elections
The Constitution and electoral laws govern citizenship, franchise, parliamentary elections and appointment of the executive.

Institutional distinction: Ntlo ya Dikgosi offers constitutional and customary advice but is not an independently elected upper house with coequal lawmaking power. The presidential and parliamentary figures refer to the amended Constitution and the government in office as of the editorial cutoff.

Admitted October 17, 1966, shortly after independence. [19]

Southern Africa under UN M49. [11]

Founding member of SADCC (1980), predecessor of SADC; the SADC Secretariat is headquartered in Gaborone. [15]
Southern African Customs Union

Member of the African Union.
Commonwealth
Member of the Commonwealth since independence.
World Trade Organization
Member since May 31, 1995. [16]
African Continental Free Trade Area

UNESCO World Heritage
Two inscribed World Heritage properties: Tsodilo and the Okavango Delta. [13]

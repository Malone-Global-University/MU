Country Overview

Namibia, officially the Republic of Namibia, is a sparsely populated country on the southwestern Atlantic coast of Africa. Its capital is Windhoek, and its landforms range from the dunes of the Namib Desert to interior plateaus and the Kalahari fringes. Following German colonial rule and a prolonged period of South African administration, Namibia became independent on March 21, 1990, following a United Nations-supervised transition. Its economy is marked by uranium, diamonds, gold, fisheries, tourism, and regional trade. [4] [20] [16]

As of November 5, 2026, Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah is President and Elijah Ngurare is Prime Minister. Namibia is a presidential republic with a bicameral legislature and English as its sole constitutional official language, alongside a rich range of widely spoken African and other languages. Landlocked or coastal

Historical societies
Indigenous San communities and diverse pastoral, farming and trading societies, including Damara, Nama, Herero, Ovambo and other communities, long predated the modern state.
European colonial period
German colonial rule over German South West Africa began in 1884 and lasted until 1915.
Colonial-era genocide
German colonial forces committed genocide against the Herero and Nama peoples in 1904–1908; the historical record also encompasses dispossession and forced labor.
South African occupation
South Africa occupied the territory during World War I and administered it under a League of Nations mandate; the territory later remained under its control despite UN objections. [20]
Historical territorial name
South West Africa; “Namibia” was adopted in independence-era political and international usage. [20]
National liberation movement
SWAPO, the South West Africa People’s Organisation, became the dominant independence movement. [20]
Armed struggle milestone
1966 is commonly marked as the start of SWAPO’s armed liberation struggle.
UN Security Council Resolution 435
Adopted in 1978, setting out a UN-supervised independence settlement framework. [20]
UN transition
1989 implementation through the United Nations Transition Assistance Group (UNTAG). [20]
Constituent Assembly election
November 1989, conducted under UN supervision. [20]
Independence
March 21, 1990, from South African rule. [20] [23]

March 21; also the commencement date of the Constitution. [20] [9]
Constitution entered into force
March 21, 1990. [9]

Sam Nujoma, 1990–2005. [20]
Second president

Hage Geingob, 2015–February 2024.

Nangolo Mbumba, February 2024–March 2025.

Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah, inaugurated March 21, 2025. [10]
Walvis Bay transfer
South Africa transferred Walvis Bay and offshore islands to Namibia in 1994; this followed 1990 national independence.

SWAPO has held the presidency since independence, although opposition representation has increased in parliamentary elections. [15]
Historical date convention
The country’s independence (1990), its constitutional commencement (1990), and the separate Walvis Bay transfer (1994) are distinct events. [20] [9]

Historical note: March 21, 1990 is the independence and constitutional commencement date. Colonial takeover, the UN settlement, the 1989 elections and the 1994 transfer of Walvis Bay are different historical milestones. [20] [9]

Sources and Methodology

This MGU Quick Facts country profile draws on Namibia’s national statistics office, the Constitution, official government and parliamentary records, and the World Bank, WTO, United Nations, UNESCO and other identified institutional records. A statistical reference year describes when an observation was measured, not the date this HTML page was created.

Population and geography: The final 2023 census was 3,022,401 persons; the World Bank’s 2025 population estimate is 3,092,816. UNData records a total surface area of 825,229 km², while UNCTAD reports land area of 823,290 km². Other official bodies publish additional surface-area conventions; one figure is not silently substituted for another. [5] [2] [4] [18]

Economic measurements: The World Bank 2025 GDP is nominal gross domestic product in current dollars; GDP per capita, real GDP growth and inflation are separately defined indicators. The WTO 2024 merchandise values (exports US$6,543.8 million and imports US$8,882.1 million) concern goods crossing borders and do not include services. Trade classifications can include processing and re-export movements. [3] [1] [16]

Labor market: The Namibia Statistics Agency reported 36.9% unemployment for 2023 from its census-linked labor-force series. The World Bank country dashboard has a 19.3% modeled 2025 unemployment indicator. The IMF documents measurement and definitional changes in national unemployment data; direct comparisons require methodological reconciliation. [7] [8] [1]

Languages and religion: The Constitution designates English as the official language. Numerous Namibian community languages are widely spoken. Historical estimates of religious affiliation from older surveys are identified as such; no current religious proportions are fabricated. [9] [23] [28]

Government and history: Official government portals identify the incumbent President, Vice President and Prime Minister; parliamentary and IPU sources describe the legislative system and 2024 election. The United Nations supplies the independence history. [10] [11] [12] [13] [14] [15] [20]

Country Overview

Somalia, officially the Federal Republic of Somalia, occupies the eastern Horn of Africa, where the Gulf of Aden meets the western Indian Ocean. Its capital is Mogadishu. Northern and southern territories attained independence from Britain and Italy on June 26 and July 1, 1960, respectively, uniting on July 1. Somalia is a federal republic led by President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud and Prime Minister Hamza Abdi Barre. The country adopted a permanent constitution in 2026, but the federal political system, electoral arrangements and several territorial claims remain contested. Livestock production, agriculture, transport, commerce and services are important activities. [9] [11] [12] [13] [14] [2]

The Horn of Africa; easternmost country on the African mainland. [9]
Historic coastal trading networks
Mogadishu, Zeila and other port cities participated in Red Sea and Indian Ocean trade over many centuries. [31]
Precolonial history
The region has long supported Somali pastoral, mercantile, Islamic and farming societies, alongside other communities.
Colonial division
In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, the north became British Somaliland and most of the south came under Italian administration. [32] [31]
Northern independence
June 26, 1960: the former British Somaliland protectorate became independent. [11]
Southern independence
July 1, 1960: the former Italian-administered Trust Territory of Somaliland became independent. [11]
Formation of the Somali Republic
July 1, 1960: northern and southern entities united to establish the Somali Republic. [11]
National independence and unification day
July 1; June 26 is also commemorated as a key northern independence anniversary. [11]
United Nations admission
September 20, 1960. [3]
1969 political transition
A military coup led by Mohamed Siad Barre ended the first post-independence civilian constitutional period.
Collapse of central government
The fall of the Barre government in 1991 initiated prolonged civil conflict and institutional fragmentation. [32]
Somaliland independence claim
In 1991, Somaliland declared the restoration of its independence and developed separate institutions; its status remains disputed. [20] [18]
Federal government formation
August 2012: a new federal government and provisional constitutional order succeeded earlier transitional institutions. [32] [18]
2024 constitutional amendments
March 2024 amendments addressed the electoral system and five-year terms; implementation remained politically contested. [15] [16]
Permanent constitution approved
March 4, 2026, by the Federal Parliament, according to the presidency. [14]
Permanent constitution signed
March 8, 2026, by President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud. [14]
2026 constitution in force
April 12, 2026, according to the Inter-Parliamentary Union parliamentary chronology. [16]
Constitutional transition
The new permanent constitution replaced the 2012 provisional framework, but implementation and political acceptance remain contested. [14] [16]
National flag
A light-blue field bearing one centered white five-pointed star. [9]
National flag adopted
October 12, 1954, before the 1960 union. [9]
National identity note
The five-pointed star historically symbolizes the idea of Somali unity across five Somali-inhabited territories; it is not a count of current federal states. [9]

State-formation chronology: The 1960 northern and southern independence dates, July 1 union, 2012 provisional federal settlement and March–April 2026 constitution are distinct milestones. [11] [14] [16]

Population identity
A Somali-speaking majority, alongside long-established minority and other ethnolinguistic communities. [9] [32]
Official languages
Somali and Arabic. [9] [10]
Somali language family
Cushitic branch of the Afroasiatic language family.
Somali writing system
A Latin-based national orthography was formally adopted in 1972.
Arabic
Official language with roles in religion, education, scholarship and administration. [9]
English usage
Spoken in some business, education and government settings; not one of the two federal official languages. [10] [9]
Italian usage
Historical and limited contemporary language use connected with former colonial administration. [10]
Somali dialects
Regional variation includes Maay and Maxaa/Mahaa varieties; distinctions differ across linguistic descriptions.
Somali Bantu communities
Long-established communities, particularly near southern river valleys, with distinctive language and cultural traditions. [21]
Other minority communities
Include Benadiri coastal groups, Bajuni, Bravanese and other historically distinct populations.
Dominant religion
Islam; the population is overwhelmingly Muslim. [9] [21]
Religious affiliation estimate
More than 99% Sunni Muslim, according to the federal religious affairs ministry estimate reported in 2023; not a new 2026 census. [21]
Muslim traditions
Sunni Islamic practice includes varied Sufi, legal and devotional traditions. [21]
Religious minorities
Small Christian and other minority communities are reported; robust contemporary shares are not available. [21]
State religion
Islam. [9] [22]
Religious-freedom qualification
Legal and social restrictions on religious conversion and propagation have been documented; constitutional rights and practice should not be conflated. [22] [21]
Community organization
Extended family networks, clans, local customary institutions and faith organizations play important social roles.

Poetry and oral literature
Poetry, storytelling, proverbs and oral history are important forms of cultural expression. [10]
Music and performance
Regional vocal, instrumental, dance and ceremonial traditions coexist with contemporary music.
Staple foods
Rice, sorghum, maize, flatbreads, meat, fish and spiced tea feature in local cuisines, varying by region.

Sorghum and maize, with crop cultivation concentrated where rainfall and irrigation permit. [26]
Pastoral livelihoods
Camels, sheep, goats and cattle support household economies and social traditions. [2]
Schooling priorities
Access, school infrastructure, teacher training and education quality remain development priorities. [2]
Health priorities
Maternal and child health, nutrition, infectious-disease prevention and access to care. [2] [25]

Drought, displacement and interrupted services contribute to malnutrition and public-health risks. [25]
National poverty and society

Popular sports
Football is widely followed; athletics and other sports are also practiced.

Laas Geel rock art is an important prehistoric cultural site in the Somaliland-administered northwest. [10]

Sources and Methodology

Statistical method: World Bank country indicators form the internationally comparable 2025 population and macroeconomic baseline. UNData supplies the 637,657 km² surface-area value; UNCTAD reports a different land-area measure and supplies 2024 international merchandise trade. The Somali National Bureau of Statistics separately publishes a 2025 national accounts series, which is not substituted for World Bank GDP. Somalia has not published a recent complete nationwide census: the last published census dates to the 1970s and UNFPA’s 2014 population estimation survey was a sample survey.

Political and legal method: The presidency and parliament document the permanent constitution adopted in March and brought into force in April 2026. The political standing of federal institutions and future elections remains disputed; statements about anticipated direct elections do not assume an election has taken place. Somaliland’s self-government and 2025 bilateral recognition by Israel are presented distinctly from Somalia’s internationally recognized UN membership and the position of the federal government and African Union.

Other qualifications: Religious affiliation proportions derive from an older attributed government estimate, not from a new 2026 census. Humanitarian needs counts depend on assessment dates and definitions. UNESCO World Heritage inscriptions differ from UNESCO intangible-heritage listings and tentative nominations. GDP per capita is output divided by population, not average personal income. The contemporary institutional reference cutoff is November 5, 2026; each statistic retains its underlying reporting year.

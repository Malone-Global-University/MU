Country Overview

Cabo Verde is a sovereign Atlantic island republic in Western Africa, comprising ten main islands west of the African mainland, nine of them inhabited. Its capital is Praia, on Santiago. Portuguese settlement began in the fifteenth century, and the islands gained independence from Portugal on July 5, 1975. Cabo Verde developed a competitive multiparty constitutional order in the early 1990s. Its service-oriented economy depends heavily on tourism and external connections, while preserved fish dominates recorded merchandise exports. Following the May 2026 parliamentary election, President José Maria Neves serves alongside Prime Minister Francisco Carvalho. [15] [9] [8] [2] [3] [10]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Cabo Verde; República de Cabo Verde. [7]
Common name
Cabo Verde; formerly widely rendered in English as Cape Verde. Nationality: Cabo Verdean.
Capital
Praia, on Santiago Island. [7]
Continent / UN subregion
Africa / Western Africa under the UN M49 geographic classification; an Atlantic archipelago west of mainland Africa. [21] [4]
Portuguese settlement
Portuguese settlement on Santiago began in 1462, following fifteenth-century voyages; the archipelago became part of Portuguese overseas possessions. [9]
Colonial history
The islands were a major Atlantic trade and slave-trading entrepôt. Enslaved Africans and European settlers shaped local language and culture; drought and migration became recurrent historical forces. [9]
Independence movement
The African Party for the Independence of Guinea and Cabo Verde (PAIGC), associated with Amílcar Cabral and other activists, organized against Portuguese colonial rule. [9]
Independence
July 5, 1975, from Portugal; this is the national independence and founding date of the sovereign republic. [8] [9]
First republican constitution
Approved September 5, 1980; the post-independence political framework initially operated under a single-party system. [8]
Transition to multiparty politics
Constitutional revision on September 28, 1990 facilitated political pluralism; competitive legislative and presidential elections followed in 1991. [8]
Current constitutional foundation
Constitution adopted August 5, 1992, subsequently amended, notably in 2010; distinct from the 1975 independence date. [8] [7]
Recent national election
May 17, 2026: PAICV won 37 of 72 National Assembly seats and formed a new government. [10]
National day
Independence Day, July 5. [8]
ISO 3166-1 codes
CV (alpha-2); CPV (alpha-3); 132 (numeric). [21]
Internet country-code domain
.cv
International telephone code
+238

Historical context: Portuguese settlement, Atlantic slavery, the anticolonial independence movement, the end of single-party rule, and the creation of a pluralist constitutional republic are separate stages in Cabo Verde’s history. The independence date should not be mistaken for the date of its current constitutional order. [9] [8]

Geography and Population

Geographic position
Volcanic archipelago in the eastern North Atlantic, west of Senegal and Mauritania; no international land borders. [15]
Total surface area
4,033 km² (approximately 1,557 square miles; UNdata reference). [4]
Archipelago
Ten major islands, nine permanently inhabited; Santa Luzia is uninhabited. Numerous smaller islets. [15]
Northern island group
Barlavento (windward): Santo Antão, São Vicente, Santa Luzia, São Nicolau, Sal, and Boa Vista.
Southern island group
Sotavento (leeward): Maio, Santiago, Fogo, and Brava.
Largest island
Santiago, which includes the capital, Praia. [7]
Highest point
Pico do Fogo, approximately 2,829 metres (9,281 feet), on the volcanic island of Fogo.
World Bank population estimate
527,326 residents in 2025 (World Bank population series). [1]
National statistical projection
513,997 for 2025 (INE 2024 revision of its demographic projection). This is a different population series from the World Bank estimate. [6]
2021 national census, resident population
491,233 residents (final RGPH-2021 count). [5]
2021 census, total persons enumerated
505,044 persons, including nonresident visitors and other categories; not equivalent to the resident count of 491,233. [5]
Estimated population density
Approximately 130.8 people per km² (2025 UNdata model/area basis). [4]
Capital municipality, 2021 census
Praia: approximately 145,378 residents in the municipality; not a 2025 city-proper estimate. [5]
Other major population centre
Mindelo, on São Vicente; the São Vicente municipality recorded 75,845 residents in the 2021 census. [5]
National demographic growth
Approximately 0.5% (2025 World Bank estimate); population development is strongly affected by emigration and migration. [1]
Climate
Predominantly arid to semiarid tropical maritime climate, with erratic rainfall, drought vulnerability and distinctive island microclimates.
Natural hazards
Droughts, flash flooding, coastal erosion and volcanic hazards; activity is particularly associated with Fogo.
Maritime geography
Atlantic maritime territory is central to fisheries, tourism, ocean transport and the country’s external trade.

Geographic and demographic comparability: Area covers the islands’ surface area, not the Atlantic exclusive economic zone. The 2021 census reports usual residents separately from all persons enumerated; INE demographic projections and World Bank international estimates are separate statistical series. [4] [5] [6] [1]

Economy and International Trade

Economic system
Small, service-oriented island economy with major tourism, transport, finance, construction, public administration and related activities. [2]
Nominal GDP
Approximately US$3.0566 billion (2025, current dollars, World Bank; rounded display US$3.06 billion). [1]
Nominal GDP per capita
Approximately US$5,796.5 (2025, current dollars, World Bank); GDP per capita is not average earnings. [1]
Real GDP growth
6.3% in 2025 (World Bank, annual real output growth). [1] [2]
Consumer price inflation
2.3% in 2025 (World Bank annual CPI series). [1]
Currency
Cabo Verdean escudo (CVE); the country does not use the euro as its national currency. [4] [16]
Euro exchange-rate arrangement
Fixed parity of EUR 1 = CVE 110.265, distinct from floating CVE rates against other currencies. [16]
Principal services exports
International tourism and associated travel, hospitality and transport services, together with other services. [2]
Services exports as share of GDP
30.5% of GDP in 2025, as reported in the World Bank 2026 Economic Update; distinct from merchandise-export totals. [2]
Merchandise exports
US$68.2 million in 2024 (WTO / UN Comtrade data as presented by the WTO). [3]
Merchandise imports
US$915.7 million in 2024 on the same WTO trade-data basis. [3]
Goods-trade balance
Approximately US$847.5 million deficit in 2024, calculated from WTO merchandise exports and imports; excludes services. [3]
Leading goods export categories
Prepared and preserved tuna, mackerel and other fish; fish products, selected footwear components and apparel. [3]
Goods export destination
European Union represented 97.6% of recorded 2024 merchandise export value in the WTO country profile; the EU is a trading bloc, not a single country. [3]
Goods import origin
European Union accounted for 71.5% of recorded 2024 goods imports; China, Brazil and other partners supplied smaller shares. [3]
Tourism concentration
Sal and Boa Vista attract a high share of international tourism; European-origin visitors dominate arrivals, creating concentrated external exposure. [2]
External accounts
Current-account surplus estimated at 3.6% of GDP in 2025 in the World Bank 2026 country update, despite a significant goods-trade deficit. [2]
Remittances
Remittances from Cabo Verdeans abroad are an important source of household and external income; World Bank country indicators report about 11.7% of GDP for 2025. [1]
Transport constraint
Inter-island air and maritime connectivity materially affects prices, market access, tourism dispersion and regional development. [2]
Trade interpretation
The recorded merchandise export total measures physical goods; travel spending and other services appear in separate balance-of-payments accounts. [3] [2]

People and Society

Official language
Portuguese, as specified in Article 9 of the Constitution. [7]
Widely spoken language
Cabo Verdean Creole (Kriolu/Krioulu), the everyday first language of much of the population; it is distinct from Portuguese and has multiple island varieties. [7] [9]
Language-law distinction
The Constitution instructs the state to promote conditions for official recognition of the Cabo Verdean mother tongue in parity with Portuguese; it does not itself make Creole an existing second official language. [7]
Other languages
English, French and other languages appear in education, tourism, expatriate communities and international commerce.
Religious profile, census basis
Roman Catholic: about 73% in the 2021 census as summarized by the U.S. State Department; figures refer to a dated census, not a 2026 survey. [14]
Other Christian traditions
2021 census reported about 2% Seventh-day Adventist, 2% Nazarene and 2% Christian Rationalist, alongside other Protestant and independent churches. [14]
Muslim population
About 1% in the reported 2021 census religious breakdown. [14]
No religious identification
About 16% reported no religious affiliation in the 2021 census figures cited by the U.S. State Department. [14]
Freedom of religion
Freedom of conscience, worship and religious choice is protected within Cabo Verde’s constitutional order. [7] [14]
Cultural traditions
Cabo Verdean cultural traditions include morna, coladeira, funaná, batuque, literature, community festivals and island-specific music.
Diaspora
Large Cabo Verdean communities reside abroad, including in Portugal, the United States and other Atlantic and European destinations. [9]
Life expectancy
Approximately 76 years (World Bank, 2024; rounded). [1]
National identity
Cabo Verdean identity developed through Atlantic trade, African and European histories, creole language and long-standing patterns of migration. [9]
2021 census comparison
In 2021, the statistical authority distinguished 491,233 usual residents from the broader 505,044 people enumerated; demographic proportions must specify the population base. [5]

Religion and language conventions: Religion shares above are attributed to the 2021 census as reported in a 2023 State Department report. They are not measures of 2026 religious affiliation. Portuguese is the constitutionally designated official language; the constitutional provision supporting official recognition of Cabo Verdean Creole does not itself amount to that designation. [14] [7]

Government and Institutions

Constitutional form
Unitary multiparty constitutional republic with an elected president, prime minister, and government accountable to the unicameral National Assembly. [7] [10]
Head of state
President José Maria Pereira Neves, in office at the November 5, 2026 editorial cutoff. [12] [10]
Head of government
Prime Minister Francisco Avelino Vieira de Carvalho (Francisco Carvalho), appointed June 19, 2026. [10] [11]
Prime minister’s party
African Party for the Independence of Cabo Verde (PAICV), which returned to government after the May 2026 election. [10]
Legislature
Unicameral National Assembly (Assembleia Nacional), with 72 elected seats. [10]
May 2026 parliamentary election
PAICV won 37 seats; Movement for Democracy (MpD) won 33; UCID won 2, for a 72-seat total. [10]
Speaker of the National Assembly
Janira Hopffer Almada, elected June 18, 2026; first woman to preside over the chamber. [10] [13]
Women in parliament
34 women among the 72 members elected in 2026 (47.2%), according to the Inter-Parliamentary Union. [10]
Government programme
The June 2026 government presented its programme for the 2026–2031 legislature in July 2026. [11]
Next presidential election
Scheduled for November 15, 2026; this date is in the future relative to the page’s November 5 editorial cutoff. No result is assumed. [12]
Judicial order
Constitutionally established judiciary; courts operate separately from the legislature and executive under the constitutional framework. [7]
Administrative divisions
22 municipalities (concelhos) distributed among the islands; smaller local administrative units also exist. [15]
Local administration
Elected local authorities govern municipalities within the unitary republic, subject to national constitutional and statutory law. [7]
Elections and diaspora
Cabo Verde has competitive multiparty elections, with electoral participation from qualified citizens resident abroad. [10] [12]

Election timing: The presidential election is scheduled for November 15, 2026, ten days after this profile’s editorial cutoff. The page identifies the incumbent as of November 5 and does not project an election winner. [12]

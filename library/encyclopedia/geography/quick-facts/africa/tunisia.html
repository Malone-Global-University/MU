Country Overview

Tunisia, officially the Republic of Tunisia (Arabic: al-Jumhūriyya at-Tūnisiyya), is a North African Mediterranean country between Algeria and Libya, with Tunis as its capital. The territory has a history that includes ancient Carthage, Roman Africa, Islamic dynasties, Ottoman-era governance and a French protectorate. Tunisia became independent on March 20, 1956 and a republic on July 25, 1957. It is governed under a presidential constitution promulgated in 2022; Kaïs Saïed is president and Sarra Zaafrani Zenzri heads the government. The modern economy encompasses components manufacturing, olive oil, tourism, agriculture and services. [6] [24] [10] [11] [2]

Ancient foundations
Phoenician settlements, including Carthage, developed before the region became a centre of Punic power. [24] [16]
Roman era
Carthage fell to Rome in 146 BCE; the region later formed an important part of Roman North Africa. [24]
Early Islamic era
Arab-Muslim conquests from the seventh century changed the region’s political, religious and linguistic development. [24]
Medieval and early modern polities
Aghlabid, Fatimid, Zirid and Hafsid rule preceded the Ottoman-era Regency of Tunis. [24]
Ottoman and Husainid legacy
Ottoman suzerainty and the Husainid dynasty shaped the beylik before the French protectorate. [24]
Anticolonial nationalist movement
Habib Bourguiba and the Neo Destour movement played central roles in the independence campaign. [24]
Bourguiba presidency
The first president governed from 1957 until 1987. [24]
Ben Ali period
Zine El Abidine Ben Ali held the presidency from 1987 until the 2011 revolution. [24]
2010–2011 revolution
Protests beginning in December 2010 culminated in President Ben Ali’s departure on January 14, 2011.
2014 constitutional phase
A new constitution established a post-revolution parliamentary-presidential framework, later replaced in 2022. [10]
2021 institutional rupture
President Kaïs Saïed suspended Parliament and asserted exceptional executive powers in July 2021. [21]
Current constitution
Dated July 25, 2022 and promulgated August 17, 2022. [10]
2024 presidential election
Kaïs Saïed was declared reelected in October 2024. [15]

People and Society

Official language
Arabic, as established by the 2022 Constitution. [10]
Everyday spoken Arabic
Tunisian Arabic (Derja) is widely used in ordinary speech and local media.
French language
Widely used in business, administration, technical education and professional settings, but is not a constitutional official language. [2] [10]
Amazigh languages
Amazigh/Tamazight language varieties persist within some communities; do not treat Arabic as the sole spoken mother tongue.
Other foreign languages
English and other foreign languages are taught or used in tourism and business.
Cultural ancestry
Tunisian society reflects longstanding Amazigh, Arab, Mediterranean, African and other historical connections. [24]
Religious majority
Predominantly Sunni Muslim; approximately 99% in the U.S. State Department’s cited mid-2023 demographic estimate. [18]
Religious minority communities
Christian, Jewish, Shia Muslim, Baha’i and nonreligious communities are also present. [18]
Historic Jewish community
Djerba and the Tunis area retain longstanding Jewish community traditions. [18]
Christian communities
Catholic, Protestant and other churches; institutional and membership estimates vary. [18]
Religious-demography caution
The 2023 religious estimates must not be represented as 2024 census results or current 2026 measurements. [18] [4]
Constitution and religion
The 2022 Constitution describes Tunisia as part of the Islamic nation and assigns the state a role in advancing the purposes of Islam; it differs in wording from the 2014 Constitution. [10]
Education system
Public and private primary, secondary and higher education, including technical and vocational training.
2024 school participation
The 2024 census dissemination cites about 97.3% school enrollment for ages 6–14. [5]
Education systems and language
Arabic dominates school administration and much instruction; French has an important role in some secondary and tertiary subjects.
Electricity connection, 2024
97.6% of dwellings connected in the national census housing results. [4]
Drinking-water network, 2024
86.8% of dwellings connected to the potable-water network. [4]
Sanitation network, 2024
61.9% of dwellings connected to sewerage under the census indicator. [4]
Urban and rural differences
Employment, water access and poverty vary by region; national averages mask geographic inequality. [2]
Human Development Index
0.746, 2023 reference year as reported in the UNDP Human Development Report 2025. [23]
Life expectancy
Approximately 77 years at birth, World Bank 2024 series. [1]
Health and medical services
Public and private healthcare and regional healthcare disparities remain important policy concerns. [2]
Common cuisine
Couscous, brik, harissa, olive oil, seafood and date-based preparations are prominent.
Historical and archaeological sites
Carthage, El Jem, Dougga and Kairouan are major heritage and visitor destinations. [16]
UNESCO World Heritage count
10 inscribed properties as of November 2026: nine cultural and one natural. [16]
Newest World Heritage property
Village of Sidi Bou Saïd, inscribed July 25, 2026. [17] [16]
UNESCO natural World Heritage property
Ichkeul National Park, inscribed in 1980. [16]
World Heritage properties in the capital
Medina of Tunis and Archaeological Site of Carthage. [16]
Other UNESCO cultural properties
Amphitheatre of El Jem, Kairouan, Medina of Sousse, Dougga, Kerkuane, Djerba and Sidi Bou Saïd. [16]

The 2026 inscription raises the heritage count from nine to ten; older pages may list only nine. [16] [17]

Religion and heritage: Religious affiliation estimates date from a U.S. government account of 2023; the 2024 census is not cited as a religious count. UNESCO’s list was updated to ten Tunisian properties when Sidi Bou Saïd was inscribed in July 2026. [18] [16]

Government and Institutions

Current governing system
Unitary presidential republic under the 2022 Constitution. [10]
Head of state
President Kaïs Saïed. [12]
Head of government
Prime Minister Sarra Zaafrani Zenzri (also rendered Sara/Sarra Zaâfrani Zenzeri). [11]
Head of government appointment
Prime Minister appointed in March 2025, serving as of the 2026 government portal update. [11]
Presidential election
October 6, 2024, with final results subsequently certified. [15]
2024 official presidential vote share
90.69% of valid votes for Kaïs Saïed, according to the election authority. [15]
2024 presidential turnout
28.79% of registered voters, per the election authority’s detailed report. [15]
Presidential term
Five years under the 2022 Constitution. [10]
Presidential term limit
Two presidential terms under the constitutional text. [10]
Executive structure
The president holds extensive executive authority and appoints the head of government and ministers under the 2022 framework. [10]
Constitutional form
Unitary state; Article 4 establishes territorial unity. [10]
National legislature
Bicameral Parliament with a directly elected lower chamber and a regional-district upper chamber. [10] [13]
Lower chamber
Assembly of the Representatives of the People (Assemblée des représentants du peuple, ARP). [13]
Lower chamber statutory seats
161. [13]
Upper chamber
National Council of Regions and Districts (Conseil national des régions et des districts, CNRD). [10]
Upper chamber statutory seats
77 statutory seats, comprising 72 governorate representatives and five district representatives; actual occupied seats may change. [14]
Upper chamber representation
Indirect selection through 24 regional councils and the five district groupings, not the same election procedure as the lower chamber. [10] [14]
Parliamentary roles
National legislation and budget processes, subject to the distinct roles of each chamber under the 2022 Constitution. [10]
Administrative divisions
24 governorates, each with subordinate delegations and local authorities. [22]
Five districts
Five groupings of governorates for specified regional representation and planning, not five provinces in place of 24 governorates. [22]
National electoral authority
Independent High Authority for Elections (ISIE), publisher of official 2024 presidential data. [15]
Judicial institutions
Ordinary courts and other constitutionally recognized judicial bodies, with institutional arrangements governed by constitutional and statutory rules. [10]
2011 democratic transition
Revolutionary change led to competitive politics and a 2014 Constitution.
2021–2022 governing shift
Extraordinary executive measures beginning in July 2021 preceded the new 2022 constitutional order. [10] [21]
Public debate over constitutional order
The presidency presents the current system as a reconfiguration of public authority; critics have raised concerns about checks and balances and political competition. [12] [21]
Civil-liberties concerns
Human Rights Watch reported restrictions affecting journalists, opposition politicians, lawyers, civic groups and migrants in 2025–2026. These are attributed findings. [21]
Government record on current policy
Official government statements emphasize investment, social development, economic planning, public services and digital transformation. [12]
Political reference cutoff
November 6, 2026; current officeholders and electoral arrangements require verification at later publication dates. [12] [10]

Institutional timeline: The 2022 Constitution, the October 2024 presidential election and the current cabinet’s March 2025 appointment are distinct events. Accounts of Tunisia as a system governed by the 2014 Constitution are not current. Official sources describe the officeholders; human-rights assessments cited here are clearly attributed to their publisher. [10] [15] [11] [21]

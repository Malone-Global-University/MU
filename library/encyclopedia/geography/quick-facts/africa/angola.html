Country Overview

Angola, officially the Republic of Angola, is an Atlantic-facing country of Africa with a continental mainland and the northern exclave of Cabinda. Its capital, Luanda, is a major regional urban and commercial center. The country encompasses diverse languages, communities and ecological regions, from northern tropical forests and the central highlands to the arid southwest. Angola became independent from Portugal on November 11, 1975; its civil war ended in 2002. Petroleum and natural gas dominate merchandise exports, while agriculture, mining, and non-oil services are important to domestic activity. The present constitutional order dates from 2010 and was revised in 2021. [4] [6]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Angola (República de Angola).
Common name
Angola (Angola).
Capital
Luanda, on the Atlantic coast.
Continent / UN M49 subregion
Africa / Middle Africa (UN statistical classification); often described geographically and politically as Southern Africa. [17]
Precolonial historical foundations
The territory included the Kingdom of Kongo, the Ndongo and Matamba polities, and other societies with distinct political and cultural histories. [5]
Portuguese colonial presence
Portuguese contact began in the late fifteenth century; effective colonial control expanded unevenly over succeeding centuries. [5]
Armed anticolonial struggle
Began in 1961; different nationalist movements fought Portuguese rule. [5]
Political transition
Portugal’s April 25, 1974 revolution accelerated decolonization; the Alvor Agreement of January 15, 1975 provided a framework for transition but did not prevent renewed conflict. [5]
Independence proclaimed
November 11, 1975, ending Portuguese colonial rule; proclaimed in Luanda by Agostinho Neto. [5]
National day
Independence Day, November 11.
Civil war and peace
Civil war followed independence and ended with the April 4, 2002 peace memorandum following the death of UNITA leader Jonas Savimbi; the longer conflict involved multiple phases and international intervention. [9]
Current constitutional framework
Constitution approved January 21, 2010 and promulgated February 5, 2010; revised in August 2021. [6] [7]
ISO 3166-1 codes
AO (alpha-2); AGO (alpha-3); 024 (numeric). [17]
Internet country-code domain
.ao

Historical context: Angola's modern national boundaries encompass a wide range of societies and languages rather than a single precolonial state. Portuguese trading and colonial networks, including the Atlantic slave trade and forced labor, profoundly affected the region. The movements most closely associated with the independence struggle were the MPLA, FNLA, and UNITA. Political and military rivalry after independence developed into a prolonged civil war. Contemporary state formation is therefore best understood through distinct precolonial, colonial, independence, constitutional, and postwar phases.

Luanda, accounting for roughly one-quarter of residents in the 2024 national census. [2]

Luanda, Huambo, Lobito, Benguela, Lubango, and Cabinda; city versus metropolitan definitions differ.
Bordering states
Democratic Republic of the Congo, Republic of the Congo (at the Cabinda exclave), Zambia, and Namibia. [4]
Coastline
Approximately 1,650 km along the Atlantic Ocean. [4]
Cabinda
Northern coastal exclave separated from the rest of Angola by a section of the Democratic Republic of the Congo; it borders the Republic of the Congo and the Atlantic Ocean. [4]
Major physiographic features
Coastal plain, central highlands / Bié Plateau, savannas, tropical forest zones in the north, and arid landscapes in the southwest.
Major river systems
Cuanza (Kwanza), Cunene (Kunene), Cubango (Okavango headwaters), and Congo River basin tributaries.
Climate
Tropical in much of the north and interior, cooler in the central highlands, and increasingly arid toward the southwest.

Population methodology: The 2024 INE census is a national enumeration at a specific date; the World Bank's 2025 value is a model-based annual international estimate. They are intentionally shown as distinct series and should not be interpreted as directly interchangeable census releases.

Economy and International Trade

Economic system
Mixed market economy with major petroleum and natural-gas industries, state-owned enterprises, and growing non-oil activities.
Income group
Lower-middle-income economy in the World Bank’s contemporary classification. [1]
Nominal GDP
Approximately US$122.17 billion (2025, current U.S. dollars; World Bank). [1]
Nominal GDP per capita
Approximately US$3,129.5 (2025, current U.S. dollars; World Bank). This is national output per person, not mean wages or median household income. [1]
Real GDP growth
3.1% in 2025 (World Bank/IMF series). [1] [16]
Consumer-price inflation
20.2% annual average in 2025 (World Bank/IMF). [1] [16]
Unemployment
14.1% in 2025 (World Bank / ILO-modeled national unemployment estimate; method differs from some national labor-force surveys). [1]
Currency
Angolan kwanza (AOA); symbol Kz. [4]
Principal economic sectors
Oil and gas extraction, diamond mining, construction, trade, transport, agriculture, fisheries, and services.
Merchandise exports
US$39.2503 billion in 2024 (WTO / UN Comtrade reporting). [3]
Merchandise imports
US$13.9453 billion in 2024 (same trade dataset). [3]
Merchandise trade balance
Approximately US$25.31 billion surplus in 2024, calculated from the cited export and import figures; excludes services. [3]
Principal merchandise export destination
China, 41.4% of Angola’s 2024 merchandise export value; the European Union (as a bloc) and India were also major destinations. [3]
Major merchandise import suppliers
European Union (as a bloc), China, United Kingdom, India, and United States were among the leading suppliers in the 2024 WTO dataset. [3]
Oil dependence
Crude oil and liquefied natural gas together accounted for about 85.5% of reported merchandise exports in 2024. The economy and public finances remain sensitive to petroleum prices and production volumes. [3] [16]

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Presidential constitutional republic with a multiparty political system. [6]
Constitution
Constitution of 2010, with the first partial revision enacted August 16, 2021. [6] [7]
Head of state and executive
President João Manuel Gonçalves Lourenço (in office since September 26, 2017; latest status checked November 4, 2026). [9]
Vice president
Esperança Maria Eduardo Francisco da Costa (in office since September 2022; latest status checked November 4, 2026). [10]
Executive branch
President exercises executive power and is assisted by a vice president, ministers of state, ministers, and other executive institutions. [6]
Presidential selection
The candidate heading the national list of the party or coalition receiving the most votes in a general election becomes president; this differs from a separate presidential ballot. [6] [11]
Legislature
Unicameral National Assembly (Assembleia Nacional) with 220 statutory seats. [11]
Legislative term
Five-year general-election cycle under the constitutional electoral system. [6]
Judiciary
Constitutional Court, Supreme Court, and other courts within the national judiciary. [6]
Administrative divisions
21 provinces, 326 municipalities, and 378 communes under Law No. 14/24; system in force since January 1, 2025. [8]
Administrative reorganization
The 2025 division added Icolo e Bengo, Cuando, and Moxico Leste while reorganizing former territorial units; older references listing 18 provinces are outdated. [8]
Main political parties
Popular Movement for the Liberation of Angola (MPLA) and National Union for the Total Independence of Angola (UNITA), alongside smaller parties represented in the assembly. [11]
Last completed general election
August 24, 2022; MPLA won a legislative majority, with UNITA the largest opposition party. [11]

Government-reference convention: Officeholders reflect the editorial cutoff, not a permanent attribute. This profile reports the constitutional method of selecting the president rather than calling the office a separately elected direct presidency. The updated 21-province framework supersedes legacy references still visible on some institutional websites.

International Profile

United Nations
Admitted December 1, 1976. [12]
African Union
Member; Angola joined the Organization of African Unity after independence and participates in its successor, the African Union.
Southern African Development Community (SADC)
Founding participant in the Southern African Development Coordination Conference of 1980, predecessor of SADC. [13]
World Trade Organization
Member since November 23, 1996; member of GATT from April 8, 1994. [18]
Community of Portuguese Language Countries
Member of the Community of Portuguese Language Countries (CPLP).
Other international affiliations
African Continental Free Trade Area, African Union, United Nations, SADC, WTO, and other multilateral and regional institutions.
OPEC membership (historical)
Withdrew from the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries effective January 1, 2024; not an OPEC member as of this reference date. [14]
Key external economic relationships
China is the leading recorded merchandise export destination; European Union markets and India are also important. [3]
Geopolitical setting
Atlantic-facing Central / Southern African state with strong cross-border links to the Congo Basin and southern African regional institutions.
Standard time
UTC+1 (West Africa Time; no seasonal daylight saving time).
International dialing code
+244.

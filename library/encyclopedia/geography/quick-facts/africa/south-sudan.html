Country Overview

South Sudan, officially the Republic of South Sudan, is a landlocked state in Eastern Africa with Juba as its capital. The country declared independence from Sudan on July 9, 2011, following the 2005 Comprehensive Peace Agreement and a January 2011 referendum. Its economy relies heavily on crude oil exports, while millions of people depend on agriculture, livestock and local trade. President Salva Kiir Mayardit remains head of state; on September 22, 2026, the transitional government and legislature were dissolved and replaced by a caretaker administration ahead of elections scheduled for December 22. South Sudan contains the Sudd wetland and the Boma–Badingilo Migratory Landscape, which became its first UNESCO World Heritage property in 2026. [8] [7] [4] [16] [11] [12] [24]

Multiethnic society with numerous Nilotic, Central Sudanic and other cultural-linguistic communities. [9]

Dinka, Nuer, Shilluk, Bari, Zande and numerous other communities; no reliable contemporary census-based percentage shares supplied here. [4]

English, designated for government use. [4]

Dinka, Nuer, Bari, Zande, Shilluk, Juba Arabic and many others. [4]

English is official, but fluency and everyday communication vary across communities and regions. [4]
Juba Arabic
An Arabic-based regional lingua franca, especially in and around Juba; distinct from designation as the official national language. [4]
Religious communities
Christian churches, indigenous religious traditions, Muslim communities and other affiliations.
Religious percentage caution
No contemporary census-based national shares are presented as authoritative; surveys may differ in definitions and coverage.
Christian traditions
Catholic, Anglican/Episcopal, Presbyterian, Pentecostal and other Protestant communities.

Locally rooted spiritual practices and customary beliefs coexist with Christian and Muslim practices.

Patterns of language, religion, kinship and livelihood differ significantly among regions and communities. [9]

Small-scale crop production, fishing, livestock raising and informal trade. [20]

Insecurity, displacement, poverty, infrastructure deficits and limited school sanitation constrain learning. [23] [22]

Conflict, limited facilities, displaced populations, disease outbreaks and difficult road access impair service delivery. [22] [20]

Humanitarian needs
UN humanitarian assessments projected assistance needs for around 10 million people in 2026. [28] [22]
Displacement
Conflict, flooding and the neighboring war in Sudan have created major internal and cross-border movements. [22]
Return movements
South Sudanese returnees from Sudan add demand for food, housing, health services and local employment. [22]
Food production hazards
Flooding, dry spells, disruptions to planting and high fuel prices compound food insecurity. [21]
Cultural traditions
Oral history, storytelling, cattle-centered ceremonies, communal music, dance and regional cuisines are significant in many communities.
Heritage and archaeology
Archaeological landscapes, historic settlement sites and continuing customary institutions contribute to national heritage.

Sources and Methodology

Population: The summary’s 12,188,788 people are the World Bank harmonized 2025 estimate. The latest conventional nationwide census baseline commonly cited by South Sudan’s National Bureau of Statistics was conducted in 2008, before independence. Humanitarian agencies use operational planning populations that need not match the World Bank model. An approximately 19.7 persons/km² density is calculated from the displayed population divided by the EAC area and is not published as a separate census result.

National accounts and prices: The country is an exception to the quick-facts practice of using World Bank 2025 nominal GDP: World Bank country data currently show 2015 as the last available GDP observation. Therefore the snapshot instead uses the United Nations Statistics Division’s nominal GDP and GDP-per-capita figures (US$4.629 billion and US$403 respectively); although displayed in the 2025 column, both are marked with footnote e meaning 2023. These data do not use the World Bank’s 2025 population denominator; do not divide one source by the other to infer a corrected value. The IMF’s 2026 US$6.07 billion GDP and 4.1% real-growth projections are forecasts from the April 2026 World Economic Outlook. Inflation comparisons retain years and different forecast/observation statuses.

Trade: The East African Community report’s 2024 US$1.823 billion merchandise exports and US$0.983 billion imports are one regional statistical series. The National Bureau of Statistics’ product-category total of US$1.694 billion is a distinct national breakdown. Product categories, reporters, coverage and vintages can vary. No WTO merchandise product data are imputed when the official WTO member profile lists none.

History, territory and political institutions: The independence referendum was in January 2011, independence was July 9, 2011 and UN membership began July 14. References to a 2018-power-sharing transitional executive with multiple vice-presidents and a seated national assembly were superseded by the September 22, 2026 dissolution and caretaker arrangement. The December 22, 2026 vote was scheduled, not completed, at this publication cutoff. Abyei is administratively listed by South Sudan but has an unresolved international status; it is not treated as an uncontested border settlement.

Social and environmental context: Language and religion lists describe known communities, without inventing a national 2026 percentage breakdown. Food-security and displacement counts are humanitarian estimates for their own reference periods. UNESCO records one inscribed South Sudan World Heritage property, Boma–Badingilo, added in July 2026 and listed as in danger.

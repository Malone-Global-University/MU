Country Overview

The Kingdom of Eswatini, formerly known as Swaziland, is a landlocked country in Southern Africa, bordered by South Africa and Mozambique. Mbabane is its administrative and judicial capital, while Lobamba serves as the royal and legislative center. The kingdom became independent from British rule on September 6, 1968, and adopted the name Eswatini in 2018. Its monarchy operates alongside a bicameral parliament and a Tinkhundla constituency system. The economy is closely connected to South Africa, with important manufacturing, sugar and beverage-related exports. [16] [15] [9] [17]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Kingdom of Eswatini (in siSwati, Umbuso waseSwatini). [15]
Common name
Eswatini (also written eSwatini in some contexts); formerly Swaziland. [15]
Capital: administrative and judicial
Mbabane. [17]
Capital: royal and legislative
Lobamba, seat of important royal institutions and Parliament. [17]
Continent / UN M49 subregion
Africa / Southern Africa; country numeric code 748. [2]
Traditional state history
Swazi (Ngwane) communities and royal institutions developed before the modern state; the monarchy evolved through the nineteenth century. [14]
British protectorate period
British administration followed the South African War in the early twentieth century and continued until independence in 1968. [14] [16]
Independence
September 6, 1968, from the United Kingdom as the Kingdom of Swaziland. [16]
Independence Day
September 6, commemorating independence in 1968. [16]
Constitutional milestones
A post-independence constitution was set aside in 1973; the present Constitution, Act No. 001/2005, came into force on July 26, 2005. [9] [10]
Current monarch accession
King Mswati III acceded to the throne on April 25, 1986. [12]
Name change
Renamed from the Kingdom of Swaziland to the Kingdom of Eswatini, effective April 19, 2018. [15]
ISO 3166-1 codes
SZ (alpha-2), SWZ (alpha-3), 748 (numeric). [2]
Internet country-code domain
.sz.
Demonym
Swazi (commonly); Liswati (singular) and Emaswati (plural) in siSwati usage. [15]
National classification
Sovereign, landlocked member state of the United Nations; one of the monarchies of southern Africa. [21] [9]

Historical context: The Swazi monarchy and communities predate British protection and colonial administration. Independence in 1968, the 2005 constitutional framework and the 2018 change of official name are distinct stages; none alone describes the origins of the people or the state. [14] [16] [9] [15]

Economy and International Trade

Economic classification
Lower-middle-income economy, closely linked to the South African market and regional customs/monetary arrangements. [8]
Nominal GDP
US$5.16 billion (2025, World Bank series; current US dollars). [1]
Nominal GDP per capita
US$4,108.3 (2025, World Bank; this is output per resident, not average wages). [1]
Real GDP growth
4.2% (2025, World Bank series). National, UN and IMF published vintages may not match exactly. [1]
2024 nominal GDP benchmark
US$4.904 billion (2024, UNCTAD); not a 2025 comparison unless the reporting year is shown. [5]
Currency
Lilangeni (plural: emalangeni), ISO 4217 SZL. [2] [29]
Currency arrangement
The lilangeni is pegged at one-to-one parity to the South African rand under the Common Monetary Area; the rand is also used in Eswatini. [27] [18]
Central economic sectors
Manufacturing, agriculture, wholesale and retail trade, transportation, financial services, government services and tourism. [8]
Manufacturing
Food and beverage concentrates, sugar processing, chemicals and related products, wood processing, textiles and apparel. [6]
Agricultural production
Sugarcane, maize, citrus and other horticulture, with livestock and smallholder farming; production varies by rainfall and irrigation. [8]
Merchandise exports
US$2.327 billion (2024, UNCTAD series; goods only). [5]
Merchandise imports
US$2.072 billion (2024, UNCTAD series; goods only). [5]
Goods-trade balance
US$255 million surplus (2024, UNCTAD series). [5]
Services exports
Approximately US$221 million (2024 UNCTAD estimate), recorded separately from merchandise exports. [5]
Services imports
Approximately US$938 million (2024 UNCTAD estimate). [5]
Principal exports
Food-industry aromatic mixtures, specialty industrial chemical preparations, raw sugar, timber, coal, and other manufactured goods. [6] [7]
Leading export destination
South Africa is by far the largest merchandise destination, followed by other African and global markets; rankings depend on reporter and year. [7]
Regional customs system
Southern African Customs Union (SACU), an important channel for customs revenue and regional economic integration. [18]
Unemployment indicator
34.2% (2025, World Bank/ILO modeled unemployment rate; distinct from a national labor-force-survey result). [1]
Structural challenges
High unemployment, inequality, exposure to drought, fiscal pressure and reliance on regional demand and customs revenue. [8]
Currency and trade comparability
GDP is expressed in U.S. dollars at current exchange rates; local-currency aggregates and trade shipments are separately measured. [1] [5]

People and Society

Official languages
siSwati and English. [17] [29]
siSwati language
Bantu language of the Nguni branch; prominent in households, cultural life and public discourse. [17]
English language
Used in public administration, parts of education, commerce and judicial proceedings. [17]
Other languages
Some residents also use Zulu, Tsonga and other neighboring or immigrant languages; prevalence varies locally.
Population composition
Predominantly Emaswati, alongside smaller communities with diverse regional origins.
Religious composition, Christianity
89.25% of residents identified as Christian (2017 national census). [4]
Religious composition, Islam
0.33% of residents identified as Muslim (2017 national census). [4]
Religious composition, traditionalist
0.45% reported traditionalist religious affiliation (2017 census); cultural practices may coexist with Christian identification. [4]
Religious composition, other beliefs
Hinduism, Bahá’í Faith, Judaism and other faiths were recorded in small proportions in the 2017 census. [4]
No religion / not stated
7.40% reported no religion and 2.19% did not state an affiliation (2017 census). [4]
Christian denominations
Includes Zionist, Evangelical, Pentecostal, Roman Catholic, Methodist, Nazarene, Anglican and other traditions. [4]
Religious measurement
2017 figures are census self-identifications, not contemporary 2025/2026 estimates, nor a measure of frequency of practice. [4]
Life expectancy
About 64.3 years at birth (2024, World Bank series); figures vary between publishers and methods. [25]
2023 life expectancy, WHO series
WHO reports 56.2 years for males and 61.9 for females (2023); not directly interchangeable with the World Bank 2024 figure. [24]
Public health context
Communicable diseases, particularly HIV and tuberculosis, remain major public-health considerations alongside noncommunicable disease burdens. [24]
Population distribution
The 2017 census showed rural residence substantially more common than urban residence. [4]
Cultural traditions
The monarchy, royal ceremonies including Umhlanga (Reed Dance) and Incwala, and regional crafts and performing arts have important cultural roles. [14]
Education
Primary, secondary and tertiary institutions operate in siSwati- and English-speaking settings; indicators should be reported with dates rather than assumed constant. [8]
Diaspora and movement
Historical labor migration and ongoing mobility connect Eswatini closely to South Africa. [18]
Cultural diversity caveat
Religious, linguistic, ethnic and national categories are different concepts and should not be combined into a single percentage distribution. [4]

Census interpretation: Religious figures above are from the 2017 census of all residents. They do not represent a newly measured 2026 distribution, and religious self-identification does not necessarily exclude participation in traditional cultural observances. [4]

Government and Institutions

Government form
Hereditary monarchy under the 2005 Constitution and a Tinkhundla-based political system; the monarch exercises extensive executive and appointment powers. [9] [12]
Political characterization
The government describes a constitutional system blending monarchical institutions and constituency elections; independent assessments characterize Eswatini as an absolute monarchy. [9] [23]
Head of state
King Mswati III, on the throne since April 25, 1986. [12]
Queen Mother (Ndlovukazi)
Queen Mother Ntombi, a prominent royal and traditional institution. [23] [14]
Head of government
Prime Minister Russell Mmiso Dlamini, appointed November 3, 2023. [11]
Foundational law
Constitution of the Kingdom of Swaziland Act No. 001/2005 (the country was renamed Eswatini in 2018). [10] [15]
Executive authority
The King holds executive authority, directly or through Cabinet; the King appoints the prime minister and ministers under constitutional arrangements. [12] [10]
Legislature
Bicameral Parliament: Senate and House of Assembly, with royal appointment roles and elected representation. [9]
Senate
Constitutional ceiling of 31 senators; a combination of royal appointees and senators elected by the House of Assembly. [9]
House of Assembly
Constitutional ceiling of 76 members, including elected Tinkhundla constituency members and others provided for by law. [9]
Electoral organization
Elections use the 59 Tinkhundla constituencies, with candidates generally contesting as individuals. [13] [9]
Political-party elections
Political parties do not contest parliamentary elections as party lists under the Tinkhundla system; independent assessments describe material restrictions on political organization. [23]
Latest general parliamentary election
2023; parliamentary terms ordinarily span five years. [23]
Traditional governance
Chiefdoms, local Tinkhundla institutions and royal customary bodies coexist with constitutional ministries and administrative offices. [13] [14]
Judicial organization
Superior and lower courts operate under a mixed Roman-Dutch-derived legal system, statutory law and customary law, as provided for by the Constitution. [10]
Administrative organization
Four regions; regional administration and 59 Tinkhundla centers. [13]
Regional Tinkhundla distribution
Hhohho 15, Manzini 18, Shiselweni 15 and Lubombo 11. [13]
Capital institutions
Mbabane houses central administrative and judicial functions; Lobamba is the royal and parliamentary center. [17]
Constitutional caution
The existence of constitutional provisions does not mean that electoral participation or the distribution of practical political power matches competitive multiparty systems. [9] [23]

Institutional distinction: The Constitution establishes state organs and elected representation, while the king retains significant powers. The Tinkhundla system is not equivalent to competitive party-list parliamentary elections; government and independent assessments use different political terminology. [9] [10] [23]

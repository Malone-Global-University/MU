Country Overview

Ghana, officially the Republic of Ghana, lies on the Gulf of Guinea in Western Africa, bordered by Côte d’Ivoire, Burkina Faso and Togo. Its capital is Accra. The country emerged from the former British Gold Coast and associated territories, gaining independence on March 6, 1957 and becoming a republic in 1960. Ghana is governed under the 1992 Constitution and has an economy encompassing services, agriculture, manufacturing, gold, cocoa and petroleum. [6] [19] [20] [8]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Ghana. [19]
Common name
Ghana. [19]

Capital
Accra, on the Gulf of Guinea. [6]
Major urban centers
Accra, Kumasi, Sekondi–Takoradi, Tamale and other regional cities. [17] [6]
Continent / UN M49 subregion
Africa / Western Africa. [22]
Historical polities
The territory now forming Ghana contains histories of Akan states including Asante, coastal polities, northern kingdoms including Dagbon, and many other communities; modern Ghana was not created from a single precolonial state. [25] [19]
Colonial-era name
Gold Coast, the principal British colonial administration over much of the territory. [19]
British Togoland transition
The UN trust territory of British Togoland voted in 1956 for integration with the future independent Ghana. [19]
Independence
March 6, 1957, from the United Kingdom; Ghana initially retained the British monarch as head of state. [19]
Republic proclaimed
July 1, 1960; Kwame Nkrumah became the first president of the republic. [19] [20]
Early post-independence president
Kwame Nkrumah was Ghana’s first prime minister following independence and first president when the republic began. [19]
1966 political transition
Military coup removed President Nkrumah; subsequent decades included civilian and military administrations. [20]
1981 political transition
The Provisional National Defence Council period followed a December 1981 military takeover and preceded the Fourth Republic. [20]
Constitutional referendum
April 28, 1992 referendum endorsed a new constitution for a return to constitutional civilian government. [20]
Present constitutional order
1992 Constitution, effective January 7, 1993, inaugurating the Fourth Republic; as subsequently amended. [20]
National day
Independence Day, March 6. [19]
ISO 3166-1 codes
GH (alpha-2); GHA (alpha-3); 288 (numeric). [6]
Internet country-code domain
.gh.
National currency code
GHS (Ghana cedi). [6]

Historical context: Ghana’s modern state formation includes precolonial societies and kingdoms, Atlantic commerce and enslavement, British colonial incorporation, self-government, independence in 1957, republican status in 1960 and successive constitutional periods. The currently operative Fourth Republic began in 1993, not in 1957. [19] [25] [20]

Geography and Population

West African Atlantic littoral; Gulf of Guinea to the south. [6] [7]

238,537 km² (United Nations country-profile series); Ghana’s Ministry of Food and Agriculture uses 238,533 km². [6] [7]
Land area
About 227,533 km², using Ghana Ministry of Food and Agriculture geographic convention. [7]

About 11,000 km² in the same Ministry of Food and Agriculture classification. [7]
Coastline
Approximately 539 km on the Gulf of Guinea (government geographic reference). [7]
Neighboring countries
Côte d’Ivoire to the west, Burkina Faso to the north, Togo to the east. [7]

Coastal plains, forested and farmed hills, the Volta Basin, and the northern savanna; low-lying to moderately elevated landscapes. [7]

Lake Volta, an extensive reservoir created by the Akosombo Dam. [7]

Tropical, with wetter southwestern and coastal sectors, comparatively drier northern conditions, and marked seasonal rainfall variation. [7]

Population and area conventions: The 2021 census is an enumerated resident count, while the World Bank population figure is an estimate for 2025. UN total area (238,537 km²) differs slightly from the agriculture ministry’s geographical reference (238,533 km²); land area and water area are shown using the latter methodology. [3] [2] [6] [7]

English; used in state administration, formal education and much national public communication. [18]
Major indigenous languages
Akan varieties (including Twi and Fante), Ewe, Ga, Dangme, Dagbani, Dagaare, Gurene, Nzema and many others. [18]
Language diversity
Multilingualism is common; ethnicity and language ability are distinct categories and should not be treated as identical census measurements. [18] [5]
Largest ethnic category
Akan: approximately 45.7% in the 2021 census classification. [5]
Mole-Dagbani population share
Approximately 18.5% (2021 census ethnic classification). [5]
Ewe population share
Approximately 12.8% (2021 census ethnic classification). [5]
Ga-Dangme population share
Approximately 7.1% (2021 census ethnic classification). [5]
Other major ethnic categories
Gurma, Guan, Grusi and Mande, in addition to other categories and communities. [5]
Christian religious affiliation
About 71.3% (2021 census), across Pentecostal/Charismatic, Protestant, Catholic and other Christian classifications. [4]
Muslim religious affiliation
About 19.9% (2021 census). [4]
Traditional religious affiliation
About 3.2–3.3% in the 2021 census-derived reporting (rounding and microdata versions differ). [4]
No religious affiliation
About 4.5% in the relevant 2021 census microdata category. [4]
Other religious affiliation
About 1.1% in the 2021 census microdata category, including various minority faiths. [4]
Religious coexistence
Christianity, Islam and Indigenous traditions have long-standing histories in Ghana; religious affiliation does not fully describe all personal practices. [4] [18]
Life expectancy
Approximately 66 years at birth (World Bank, 2024). [24]
Population age composition
The 2021 census documents a relatively youthful population; demographic proportions change and should be assigned a census year. [3]
Population growth and urban development
Greater Accra and other urban areas face housing, transport and service-delivery pressures associated with internal migration and population expansion. [3]
Principal traditional arts
Kente textiles, Adinkra symbols, carved stools, gold weights, ceremonial arts and diverse oral and musical traditions. [25]
Music and performance
Highlife, hiplife, traditional drumming and dance, and contemporary Ghanaian popular music. [25]
Major public observances
Independence Day, Constitution Day, Christian and Muslim holidays, and regional cultural festivals. [19] [18]
World Heritage sites

Health and education systems
Public and private provision operate alongside local and community institutions; indicators should be cited by year rather than assumed stable. [24]

Demographic interpretation: Religious and ethnic shares refer to the 2021 census and associated microdata categories. They are not measurements of current 2026 affiliation, and ethnic identity is not equivalent to everyday spoken language. Rounding and microdata extracts can create small differences in reported percentages. [4] [5] [18]

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Unitary presidential constitutional republic with competitive multiparty elections. [20]
Foundational law
Constitution of 1992, effective January 7, 1993, as amended. [20]
Current republic
Fourth Republic (inaugurated January 7, 1993). [20]
Head of state
President John Dramani Mahama (current term began January 7, 2025). [13] [14]
Head of government
President John Dramani Mahama; the president directs the executive branch. [13]
Vice president
Professor Naana Jane Opoku-Agyemang, inaugurated January 7, 2025. [13] [14]
Last completed presidential election
December 7, 2024; won by John Dramani Mahama. [14]
Presidential term
Four years, with a constitutional limit of two elected terms. [20]
Executive branch
President, vice president, ministers and agencies exercising executive functions under the Constitution. [13] [20]
Legislative branch
Unicameral Parliament of Ghana. [15] [16]
Parliamentary seats
276 statutory seats from the December 2024 legislative configuration (IPU); older country descriptions listing 275 seats are dated. [16]
Current legislative body
Ninth Parliament of the Fourth Republic, inaugurated in January 2025. [15]
Speaker of Parliament
Alban Sumana Kingsford Bagbin, serving as Speaker of the Ninth Parliament. [15]
Legislative terms
Four-year electoral cycles; constituencies elect representatives by single-member plurality voting. [15] [16]
Judiciary
Supreme Court, Court of Appeal, High Court and lower courts within the judicial system. [20]
Judicial independence
Constitutionally recognized judiciary, alongside separate constitutional and statutory powers assigned to other institutions. [20]
Local government
Metropolitan, Municipal and District Assemblies under Ghana’s decentralized local administrative arrangements. [17]
Regional administration
16 administrative regions and 261 Metropolitan, Municipal and District Assemblies in the cited official overview. [17]
Current 16 regions
Ahafo, Ashanti, Bono, Bono East, Central, Eastern, Greater Accra, North East, Northern, Oti, Savannah, Upper East, Upper West, Volta, Western and Western North. [17]
2018–2019 regional reorganization
Creation of six additional regions raised the total from 10 to 16, following regional referendums and subsequent implementation. [17]
Electoral framework
President and Members of Parliament are elected in separate national polls; the Electoral Commission administers elections. [20] [16]
Political-party system
Multiparty competition; the National Democratic Congress (NDC) and New Patriotic Party (NPP) have been leading national parties during the Fourth Republic. [15] [20]
Traditional authorities
Chieftaincy institutions continue to play recognized roles in Ghanaian public and cultural life alongside elected government. [18] [25]
Current political cutoff
Leadership and legislature checked as of November 5, 2026; officeholders and seat totals can change after the reference date. [13] [15] [16]

Contemporary government: The 1992 Constitution governs the Fourth Republic. President John Dramani Mahama and Vice President Naana Jane Opoku-Agyemang were inaugurated in January 2025; the present unicameral Parliament has 276 statutory seats, rather than the 275 stated in some older summaries.

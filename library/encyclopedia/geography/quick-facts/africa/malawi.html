Country Overview

Malawi, officially the Republic of Malawi, is a landlocked country in southeastern Africa between Tanzania, Mozambique and Zambia. Its capital is Lilongwe, and the Rift Valley’s Lake Malawi dominates the national geography. Formerly known as Nyasaland, it gained independence from the United Kingdom on July 6, 1964 and became a republic on July 6, 1966. Agriculture remains central to livelihoods, while tobacco, tea, legumes and other products are important exports. [7] [17] [16] [11]

Arthur Peter Mutharika is president, having won the September 2025 presidential election and taken office October 4, 2025. Jane Ansah serves as first vice president and Enoch Chihana as second vice president. The National Assembly has 229 constituency seats under the 2025 framework. [4] [13] [6]

Geography and Population

Geographic position
Landlocked country in southeastern Africa along the western side of the East African Rift. [7]
Neighboring countries
Tanzania to the north and northeast, Mozambique to the east, south and southwest, and Zambia to the west. [7]
Coastline
No ocean coastline; Lake Malawi provides inland shorelines and fisheries.
Total surface area
118,484 km² in the SADC and Malawi government geographic convention. [7]
Alternative UN surface series
UNData reports 117,726 km² for its own statistical series; not a change in sovereignty or country boundaries. [8] [7]
Land-only area
About 94,280 km² in the UNCTAD country profile; inland-water area is excluded by this measure. [12]
Area-unit distinction
Total surface area includes inland water; land area does not. Different reference agencies may report slightly different surface totals. [7] [8] [12]
Lake Malawi
Large rift-valley freshwater lake extending along much of Malawi’s eastern side, shared with Mozambique and Tanzania. [22] [7]
Lake Malawi dimensions
The lake stretches approximately 587 km along the eastern part of the country, according to the environment ministry. [22]
Lake Malawi ecosystem
Extraordinary fish diversity, especially endemic cichlids, supports conservation and local fisheries. [22] [14]
Principal river system
Shire River drains Lake Malawi southward to the Zambezi basin.
Other important water bodies
Lake Malombe, Lake Chilwa and numerous reservoirs, wetlands and seasonal watercourses.
Highest mountain
Sapitwa Peak in the Mulanje Massif, approximately 3,002 m.
Northern plateau
Nyika Plateau, with montane grassland and highland ecosystems. [22]
Highland and escarpment zones
Viphya and Dedza highlands, Shire Highlands and steep escarpments.
Shire Valley
Lower-elevation, often warmer valley in southern Malawi.
Climate
Predominantly tropical; temperature and rainfall vary substantially by altitude and region. [3]
Rainy season
Typically November to April, with regional and annual variation.
Dry season
Generally May to October, cooler in elevated districts.
Climate hazards
Droughts, intense rainfall, flood and tropical-cyclone impacts threaten agriculture and infrastructure. [16]
Cyclone Freddy legacy
Severe cyclone impacts in 2023 remain part of recent disaster and reconstruction history. [13]
National parks
Nyika, Kasungu, Liwonde, Lengwe and Lake Malawi National Park. [22]
Major protected wetland / lake
Lake Malawi National Park was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1984. [14]
Regional organization
Three major geographic-administrative regions: Northern, Central and Southern. [9]
District organization
28 administrative districts; local councils administer district and urban services.
2025 population estimate
22,216,120 people (World Bank, 2025). [1]
Most recent national census used here
17,563,749 enumerated residents (2018 census). [9]
Population growth
2.6% annual growth, World Bank 2025 (rounded). [1]
Approximate population density
About 187.5 residents/km² using the 2025 World Bank population and SADC 118,484 km² total surface area; calculated reference, not a census density. [1] [7]
2018 regional population distribution
Southern Region 44%, Central Region 43%, Northern Region 13% (2018 census; rounded). [9]
2018 urbanization
About 16% of the enumerated 2018 population was urban; the figure is not a 2025 forecast. [9]
2018 ruralization
Approximately 84% of the enumerated population lived in rural areas in 2018. [9]
City settlement
Lilongwe is the national political and administrative center, while Blantyre remains a major commercial center. [3]
Time zone
Central Africa Time (CAT), UTC+2; no regular daylight-saving change.

Geographic scope: SADC uses 118,484 km² for surface area and UNData lists 117,726 km². Land-only figures are smaller. The 2018 census count and 2025 World Bank estimate measure different reference years and methodologies; density here is a simple calculated value. [7] [8] [12] [9] [1]

People and Society

Population and community history
Malawi contains numerous communities with long regional histories, cultural practices and language traditions.
Major ethnolinguistic communities
Chewa, Tumbuka, Yao, Ngoni, Lomwe, Sena and others; identities are regionally diverse, not an exhaustive enumeration. [7]
Official language in government portal
English is identified as the official language of government and administration. [3]
National and widely spoken language
Chichewa is identified by the government as a common language; SADC lists both English and Chichewa as official languages. [3] [7]
Other languages spoken
Chitumbuka, Chiyao, Chilomwe, Chisena, Chitonga and other Malawian languages. [7]
Language-family context
Many widely spoken Malawian languages belong to the Bantu branch of the Niger-Congo family.

Country Overview

Burkina Faso is a landlocked country in Western Africa and the Sahel, bordered by Mali, Niger, Benin, Togo, Ghana and Côte d’Ivoire. Formerly known as Upper Volta, it gained independence from France on August 5, 1960, and adopted its present name in 1984 during the government of Thomas Sankara. Ouagadougou is its capital and largest city. Agriculture and gold mining are central to its economy. As of November 2026, Captain Ibrahim Traoré heads a military-led transitional government operating under a 2026 Revolution Charter. The country’s population is estimated at more than 24 million, and continuing insecurity and displacement affect public life. [1] [8] [7]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Burkina Faso (the state’s official short and full name in international use).
Previous national name
Republic of Upper Volta (French: République de Haute-Volta) until August 4, 1984. [5]
Capital
Ouagadougou; also the country’s largest city.
Continent / UN M49 subregion
Africa / Western Africa; UN M49 numeric country code 854. [9]
Precolonial societies
Mossi kingdoms and other societies of the Volta basin and Sahel, including Gur-speaking, Mande-speaking and Fulani communities, long predate modern colonial boundaries. [5]
French colonial rule
Territory incorporated into French colonial West Africa; the Upper Volta colony was established in 1919, dissolved in 1932, and reconstituted in 1947. [5]
Autonomous republic
December 11, 1958, as the Republic of Upper Volta within the French Community. [5]
Independence
August 5, 1960, from France, as the Republic of Upper Volta. [5]
First president
Maurice Yaméogo (independence-era president). [5]
Renaming as Burkina Faso
August 4, 1984, under President Thomas Sankara; the name is commonly rendered “land of upright people.” [5]
1983–1987 revolutionary period
Thomas Sankara led the revolutionary government from 1983 until his death during the October 1987 coup. [5]
1991 constitutional framework
A constitution was adopted in 1991 and subsequently amended; current political arrangements also operate under the 2026 Revolution Charter. [6] [7]
Change of power in 2014
A popular uprising ended President Blaise Compaoré’s long rule; a subsequent political transition led to elected civilian government. [5]
2022 coups and transition
Two military coups occurred in January and September 2022; Captain Ibrahim Traoré emerged as head of state after the second. [8]
2026 Revolution Charter
Adopted March 27, 2026, replacing the earlier Transition Charter and restructuring the interim legislature. [7]
Independence Day
August 5.
National Day / Republic Day
December 11, associated with the 1958 proclamation of a republic.
National motto
La Patrie ou la Mort, nous Vaincrons (“Homeland or Death, We Shall Overcome”); restored by constitutional revision in 2024. [6]
ISO 3166-1 identifiers
BF (alpha-2); BFA (alpha-3); 854 (numeric). [9]
Internet country-code domain
.bf.

State-formation distinction: The 1960 date marks sovereign independence, 1984 marks the adoption of the current national name, and 2026 marks a new interim political charter. These are separate events. The history of peoples and institutions in the region extends well before French colonial rule.

Geography and Population

Total surface area
274,200 km² (about 105,870 sq. mi.); includes water as well as land. [9]
Land area
273,600 km² (2024, World Bank / FAOSTAT land-area series). [10]
Geographic setting
Landlocked country in the western Sahel, lying between the semi-arid northern Sahel and more humid southern savanna.
Neighboring sovereign states
Mali, Niger, Benin, Togo, Ghana, and Côte d’Ivoire (six land neighbors).
Capital and principal urban centers
Ouagadougou, Bobo-Dioulasso, Koudougou, Banfora, Ouahigouya, Kaya, Dori and others.
2025 population estimate
24,074,580 (World Bank international population series, 2025). [1]
2019 national census population
20,505,155 residents, fifth general population and housing census (RGPH 2019; final results published in 2022). [3]
Annual population growth
2.2% (2025; World Bank series). [1]
Population density
About 88 people per km² of land; illustrative calculation using 2025 population and 2024 land area.
2019 rural population
73.9% of 2019 census residents; 26.1% urban, reflecting the 2019 census’s urban/rural definitions. [3]
2019 census households
3,908,847 households, about 5.2 people per household on average. [3]
Administrative divisions
17 regions, 47 provinces and 350 departments following the July 2025 administrative reorganization. [4]
Regional names
Regions were renamed using locally grounded names in the 2025 reform; earlier names remain common in older maps and statistical tables. [4]
Principal landscapes
Central plateau, Sahelian plains, savanna and wooded southern uplands; the country is traversed by major Volta basin drainages.
Main river systems
Mouhoun (Black Volta), Nakambé (White Volta), Nazinon (Red Volta), and tributaries of the Niger River basin.
Climate
Tropical wet-and-dry to semi-arid Sahelian; strong north–south variation and seasonal rainfall.
Land and environmental issues
Drought and rainfall variability, land degradation, watershed management, deforestation pressures and climate risk.
World Heritage sites
Ruins of Loropéni; Ancient Ferrous Metallurgy Sites; Royal Court of Tiébélé; and the transboundary W-Arly-Pendjari Complex. [17]
Geographic reporting convention
Water-inclusive total area and land-only area refer to different measurements; international population estimates do not replace the 2019 enumeration.

Geographic and population comparability: The 2025 World Bank estimate and the 2019 national census count come from different statistical series and dates. The July 2025 territorial map supersedes the former arrangement of 13 regions and 45 provinces; older census tables retain their original geographic definitions.

Economy and International Trade

Economic system
Low-income mixed economy with major agricultural, mining, informal trade, service and public-sector activities. [8]
Nominal GDP
Approximately US$27.63 billion (2025; World Bank, current dollars). [1]
Nominal GDP per capita
US$1,147.6 (2025; World Bank current dollars); total national output divided by population, not typical wages. [1]
Real GDP growth
5.3% (2025, World Bank annual real GDP growth series). [1]
Consumer price inflation
−0.6% annual CPI change (2025, World Bank series); a negative result denotes a fall in the index, not a fall in all prices. [1]
Modeled unemployment
3.5% (2025, ILO-modeled estimate published by World Bank). This measure does not capture all informal work or underemployment. [1]
Currency
West African CFA franc (XOF), divided into 100 centimes. [9]
Central bank
Central Bank of West African States (BCEAO), shared within the West African Economic and Monetary Union (WAEMU/UEMOA). [18]
Major economic activities
Gold mining, cotton production, food cropping and livestock, trade, transport, government services, construction, finance, and small-scale manufacturing. [8]
Key crops and agricultural products
Cotton, sorghum, millet, maize, rice, sesame, cashews, groundnuts and livestock products; significance varies by growing season and location.
Gold dependence
Gold accounted for most recorded merchandise export receipts in 2024; dependence on international gold prices and mining conditions increases external exposure. [2]
Merchandise exports
US$5,642.3 million (2024; WTO/UN Comtrade customs-based reported goods exports). [2]
Merchandise imports
US$6,428.8 million (2024; same WTO/UN Comtrade goods-trade series). [2]
Merchandise trade balance
Approximately US$786.5 million deficit (2024; WTO exports minus imports, calculated; excludes services). [2]
Largest goods export destinations
Switzerland (56.2%) and United Arab Emirates (26.2%) of 2024 reported merchandise export value. [2]
Other leading goods export destinations
European Union as a bloc (3.2%), Côte d’Ivoire (2.9%), Mali (2.3%), Ghana (1.5%) and Togo (1.1%), according to WTO’s 2024 profile. [2]
Major goods import origins
European Union as a bloc (17.2%), China (15.8%), Côte d’Ivoire (15.3%), Russia (11.7%) and Ghana (8.9%) in 2024. [2]
Leading merchandise export categories
Gold, cotton, cashew nuts, sesame seeds, cement, petroleum preparations and fruit; detailed 2024 categories below. [2]
External economic risks
Security disruptions, climate shocks, commodity prices and transport constraints influence production, access to markets and public finance. [8] [16]

People and Society

Demonym
Burkinabè (in English also Burkinabe; commonly unchanged for singular and plural).
Constitutional official-language provision
Following the 2023 revision, national languages formally designated by law are the official languages; the Constitution does not itself enumerate every designated language. [6]
Working languages
French and English are designated working languages under revised Article 35; French remains widely used in formal administration. [6]
Widely spoken national languages
Mooré, Fulfulde (Fula), Jula/Dioula, Gulmancema (Gourmanchéma), Bissa, Bwamu, San and many other languages; linguistic distribution varies by locality. [11]
Historical linguistic communities
Mossi, Fulani/Peul, Gurma, Bissa, Bobo, Gourounsi, Lobi, Senufo, Mande-speaking communities and many others, with overlapping language and identity affiliations.
Muslim religious affiliation
63.8% in the 2019 census religion tabulation. [12]
Roman Catholic affiliation
20.1% in the 2019 census religion tabulation. [12]
Protestant affiliation
6.2% in the 2019 census religion tabulation. [12]
Indigenous traditional religion
9.0% identifying with traditional/animist religion in the 2019 census tabulation. [12]
Other or no religion
Less than 1% collectively in other or no-religion classifications in the 2019 tabulation; survey question and response denominator matter. [12]
Religious practice qualification
Traditional religious practices can coexist with Muslim or Christian affiliation; reported single-category percentages are not a complete measure of practice. [12]
Urbanization
26.1% living in urban places by the 2019 census definition, not a 2025 estimate. [3]
Life expectancy
Approximately 61 years at birth (2024; World Bank latest series). [1]
Education and livelihoods
School access, literacy, livelihoods, and public services vary across urban and rural areas; the security and displacement crisis has disrupted provision in some regions. [15]
Cultural heritage and arts
Mossi and other cultural traditions, visual arts, film, literature, music and artisanal production; Ouagadougou is known for the FESPACO pan-African cinema festival.
Displacement and security
Armed violence and displacement have substantially affected communities; IOM’s 2026 response plan estimates 4.5 million people require humanitarian assistance, a needs estimate distinct from population or displacement totals. [15]

Language and religion qualification: Under the constitutional wording, national languages require formal designation by law to hold official status; French and English are working languages. The religious figures describe categories in the 2019 census and may not capture combined traditional and other religious practices.

Government and Institutions

Present political system
Military-led transitional government; not presently an elected multiparty constitutional republic in ordinary operation. [7] [8]
Head of state
Captain Ibrahim Traoré, president / head of state since the September–October 2022 change of power; status checked November 2026. [8] [13]
Head of government
Prime Minister Rimtalba Jean Emmanuel Ouédraogo, in office as of the November 2026 editorial cutoff. [13]
Executive authority
President, prime minister, Council of Ministers, ministries and security institutions within transitional arrangements. [13]
Foundational constitutional text
Constitution adopted in 1991 and amended, read in conjunction with the 2026 Revolution Charter governing the transition. [6] [7]
2024 transition timetable
A 60-month period starting July 2, 2024 was established in the 2024 amended Transition Charter and retained in the March 2026 Revolution Charter. [7]
Legislature as of 2026
Unicameral People’s Legislative Assembly (French: Assemblée législative du peuple), renamed under the March 27, 2026 Revolution Charter. [7]
Legislative composition
71 seats under the 2026 Charter, filled through designated constituencies and appointment mechanisms rather than a conventional nationwide multiparty legislative election. [7]
Members designated by head of state
24 of the 71 People’s Legislative Assembly seats under Article 15 of the 2026 Charter. [7]
Other legislative allocation
17 seats designated by regional stakeholders, 16 by defense and security forces and 14 by civil society, per the 2026 Charter. [7]
Political-party status
The Council of Ministers adopted dissolution of political parties and formations on January 29, 2026, following the suspension of party activity in 2022. [14]
National election status
The 2026 Charter envisages future elections at the end of the transition; their precise date was not established in the IPU’s 2026 account. [7]
Judicial system
Court institutions established under the constitutional and legal order, including higher courts, with their operation affected by transitional legal arrangements. [6]
Territorial administration
17 regions, 47 provinces and 350 departments after the 2025 reorganization. [4]
Local administration
Regional, provincial and departmental authorities operate through national and local administrative systems; their responsibilities are influenced by security conditions.
Government and security context
Security operations and the fight against armed extremist groups are prominent issues shaping institutions, fiscal planning and public-service delivery. [8] [15]

Political-status qualification: Do not classify Burkina Faso’s 2026 transitional leadership as the product of a new national election. The IPU catalogues the March 27, 2026 legislative reconstitution in its election database, but its account identifies an appointed or designated chamber and no completed nationwide multiparty election that day. The military-led transition should be described separately from the design of the standing 1991 Constitution.

International Profile

United Nations
Member since September 20, 1960. [9]
UN regional designation
Western Africa under UN M49; also part of the wider Sahel geographical region. [9]
Major international participation
United Nations, World Trade Organization, International Monetary Fund and World Bank Group; also participates in African regional institutions. [2] [16]
West African monetary union
Continues to use the West African CFA franc and participate in WAEMU/UEMOA monetary institutions; this is distinct from ECOWAS. [18]
Alliance of Sahel States
Founding participant with Mali and Niger in the Alliance of Sahel States (AES), established in September 2023; confederation treaty adopted July 6, 2024. [19]
ECOWAS membership
Withdrawal from the Economic Community of West African States became effective January 29, 2025, with transitional trade and movement arrangements announced. [20]
African Continental Free Trade Area
A participant in the African Continental Free Trade Area framework; practical implementation and concessions are distinct from the country’s separate ECOWAS status.
International trade profile
Main 2024 merchandise trade partners included gold markets Switzerland and UAE; the WTO data do not account for all cross-border services. [2]
Time zone
Greenwich Mean Time / UTC+0 (no seasonal daylight-saving time change).
International dialing code
+226.
Common international identifiers
BFA (ISO alpha-3), BF (ISO alpha-2), and country internet domain .bf.

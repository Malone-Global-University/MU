Country Overview

Kenya, officially the Republic of Kenya, is an Eastern African country with an Indian Ocean coast and a capital in Nairobi. British colonial rule ended when Kenya gained independence on December 12, 1963; it became a republic on December 12, 1964. Its present constitutional order dates from August 2010, combining a presidential national government with 47 devolved county governments. [10] [7]

Kenya’s economy combines agriculture, tourism, manufacturing, finance, transportation and digital services. As of November 5, 2026, President William Samoei Ruto remains both head of state and head of government, with Kithure Kindiki serving as Deputy President. [3] [9]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Kenya. [7]
Common name
Kenya.
Demonym
Kenyan.
Capital
Nairobi, the seat of national government and a major commercial and diplomatic center in Eastern Africa. [12]
Principal cities
Nairobi, Mombasa, Kisumu, Nakuru, Eldoret and Thika; rankings depend on whether administrative or continuously built-up populations are used. [12]
Continent / UN geographic subregion
Africa / Eastern Africa.
Indigenous and precolonial history
Present-day Kenya encompasses a long history of pastoralist, agricultural, coastal trading and urban communities, and many distinct peoples; modern national boundaries do not represent a single precolonial state. [12]
Swahili Coast
Indian Ocean trading networks connected Mombasa, Lamu and other coastal communities to the wider East African, Arabian and Indian Ocean worlds.
British East Africa Protectorate
Established in 1895, after earlier chartered-company administration of parts of the region. [12]
Kenya Colony
British colonial rule was reorganized into the Colony of Kenya in 1920; some coastal territories retained separate protectorate legal arrangements. [12]
Anticolonial resistance
Political organization, land conflicts, labor activism and the Mau Mau uprising of the 1950s shaped the movement against British colonial rule. [12]
Internal self-government / Madaraka Day
June 1, 1963; distinct from full independence. [10]
Independence from the United Kingdom
December 12, 1963; Kenya initially remained a Commonwealth realm under a constitutional monarchy. [10]
Republic established
December 12, 1964; Jomo Kenyatta became the first President of the Republic. [10]
Current constitutional order
Constitution promulgated August 27, 2010, establishing a strengthened bill of rights, bicameral Parliament and devolved county governments. [7]
Independence / republic observance
Jamhuri Day, December 12; Madaraka Day, June 1; Mashujaa Day, October 20. [7]
Flag
Horizontal black, red and green stripes separated by narrow white stripes, with a central Maasai-style shield and crossed spears. [7]
National anthem
“Ee Mungu Nguvu Yetu” (“O God of All Creation”), in the national language Kiswahili. [7]
ISO 3166-1 codes
KE (alpha-2); KEN (alpha-3); 404 (numeric).
Internet country-code domain

International dialing code

State formation: The three frequently cited nationhood dates—internal self-government (June 1963), independence (December 1963) and republic status (December 1964)—mark different legal milestones. The 2010 Constitution marks a later institutional reorganization. [10] [7]

Geography and Population

Location
Eastern Africa, on the western Indian Ocean; extends from coastal plains to highlands, lake basins and arid northern regions. [12]
Total area
580,367 km² (approximately 224,081 sq. mi.), using the archived CIA World Factbook geographic convention; other datasets report different surface-area totals. [11] [2]
Land area
569,140 km² under the archived Factbook convention. [11]
Inland water area
11,227 km² under the same archived Factbook convention. [11]
Indian Ocean coastline
Approximately 536 km. [12]
Bordering countries
Ethiopia, Somalia, South Sudan, Tanzania and Uganda. [12]
Highest elevation
Mount Kenya, approximately 5,199 m (17,057 ft) above sea level according to the archived Factbook. [12]
Lowest elevation
Indian Ocean coast at sea level. [12]
Major landforms
Kenyan Highlands, the East African Rift system, Mount Kenya massif, coastal lowlands, arid north and northeast, and western Lake Victoria basin. [12]
Major lakes
Lake Victoria (shared with Tanzania and Uganda), Lake Turkana, Lake Naivasha and several other Rift Valley lakes. [12]
Major rivers
Tana and Athi–Galana–Sabaki rivers, with other river basins contributing to agriculture and hydropower. [12]
Climate
Tropical maritime conditions along the coast, temperate highland conditions, and semi-arid or arid conditions across much of the north and east. [12]
Rainfall seasonality
Long rains often occur around March–May and short rains around October–December in many regions, with significant local variation. [14]
Main natural hazards
Recurring drought, local flooding, landslides, heat and climate-related disruptions; exposure varies substantially by region. [14]
Important ecosystems
Savanna grasslands, montane forests, dryland landscapes, wetlands, coral and mangrove coasts, lakes and inland freshwater systems. [13] [12]
2019 enumerated population
47,564,296 people in the 2019 Kenya Population and Housing Census; an enumerated count, not a 2025 estimate. [5]

57,532,493 people (World Bank, 2025 series). [1]
2025 population growth

Urbanization
Urban and rural shares depend on national urban-unit definitions and statistical observation years; the country has rapidly expanding metropolitan regions. [5]
Major population centers
Nairobi metropolitan area; Mombasa coast; Nakuru and Eldoret highlands; Kisumu on Lake Victoria. [5]
Life expectancy
Approximately 63.8 years at birth, both sexes, 2024 World Bank series. [2]
Total fertility
Approximately 3.17 births per woman in the 2024 World Bank series. [2]
Education and health
National services include public and private schools and health facilities, with meaningful access disparities between metropolitan and remote counties.

Lower-middle-income economy under the World Bank income-group framework; principally agriculture, commerce, transport, manufacturing and a diversified service sector. [1] [3]

US$135.94 billion, World Bank current-US-dollar series. [1]

US$2,362.9 per resident, World Bank current-US-dollar series; not a measure of median household income. [1]
Real GDP growth, calendar 2025
4.6%, in the KNBS 2026 Economic Survey and World Bank country data. [3] [1]
Consumer-price inflation, 2025
Approximately 4.1% annual average (World Bank/KNBS). [1] [4]
Unemployment, 2025
About 5.4% of the labor force using the World Bank’s ILO-modeled comparable series; not equivalent to youth unemployment or broader underemployment. [1]

Agriculture and food processing, transport and logistics, financial and digital services, tourism, wholesale and retail trade, construction and light manufacturing. [3]

Tea, coffee, maize, dairy, cut flowers, vegetables, fruit and diverse smallholder and commercial crops. [4]

Financial and digital economy
Major Nairobi-based financial services and mobile-money systems serve domestic and regional markets.
Power system
Geothermal, hydro, wind and solar provide a high share of electricity generation; petroleum-based thermal generation also remains part of the mix. [14]

About 91% of electricity generated from renewable sources in the IEA’s 2026 assessment, with geothermal accounting for about 43% of 2024 generation. [14]

People and Society

National language
Kiswahili, according to Article 7 of the 2010 Constitution. [7]
Official languages
Kiswahili and English under Article 7 of the Constitution. [7]
Kenyan Sign Language
Recognized for parliamentary proceedings and promotion under the Constitution; national language and official languages have separate legal designations. [7]
Other widely spoken languages
Kikuyu (Gikuyu), Luhya languages, Kalenjin languages, Dholuo, Kamba (Kikamba), Somali, Maasai, Meru, Giriama and numerous others.
Main language families
Bantu, Nilotic and Cushitic language groups, alongside English and other languages used in education, trade and migration.
Linguistic diversity
Kenya has dozens of indigenous languages; Kiswahili functions as a national lingua franca, while English is extensively used in higher education, law and commerce. [7]
Main religious traditions
Christianity and Islam predominate, alongside Hinduism, traditional religious practices, other religions and people reporting no religious affiliation. [6]
Christians, 2019 census
Approximately 85.5% of residents, in the official 2019 census classification. [6]
Muslims, 2019 census
Approximately 11% of residents, from the same 2019 census. [6]
Christian census subgroups
The 2019 census classified about 33.4% as non-evangelical Protestant, 20.6% Catholic and 20.4% evangelical; additional Christian categories account for the remainder. [6]
Other religions, 2019
Smaller communities include Hindus, Sikhs, Bahá’ís and traditional-religion adherents, each reported below 2% in the State Department census summary. [6]
No religious affiliation, 2019
Approximately 755,000 persons identified as atheist or having no religion in the 2019 census. [6]
Religious status under law
No state religion under Article 8 of the Constitution; freedom of conscience, religion, belief and opinion is protected. [7]
Religious-data methodology
2019 religion shares are census measurements and should not be represented as shares recalculated for 2025 or 2026. [6] [5]
Cultural and ethnic communities
Kikuyu, Luhya, Kalenjin, Luo, Kamba, Somali, Maasai, Mijikenda, Meru, Kisii and many other communities; identities are diverse and not interchangeable with language categories. [5]
Major festivals and holidays
Jamhuri Day, Madaraka Day, Mashujaa Day, Christian and Muslim holidays, and diverse county-level and cultural celebrations. [7]
Cuisine and foodways
Regional cuisines include ugali, sukuma wiki, nyama choma, rice dishes, coastal Swahili food traditions and many local dishes.
Music and literature
Rich traditions of Swahili poetry, local-language storytelling, contemporary literature, gospel, benga, taarab and popular music.
Sports
Athletics—especially long-distance running—is internationally prominent, alongside football, rugby, volleyball and other sports.
UNESCO World Heritage sites
Eight sites on UNESCO’s list at the 2026 editorial cutoff, including Lamu Old Town, Fort Jesus, Gedi, Mount Kenya National Park/Natural Forest, and the Lake Turkana national parks. [13]
Education system
Public and private schooling, universities and technical/vocational institutions, with evolving curricula and uneven local access.
Public health and welfare
Life expectancy was about 63.8 years (2024), while the challenges of maternal and child health, water access, regional inequality and urban services vary by county. [2]

Religion methodology: The 2019 census percentages describe people enumerated in that year. The World Bank 2025 population estimate should not be multiplied by these shares and represented as a current religious census. The constitutional national-language designation also differs from official and parliamentary language provisions. [5] [6] [7]

Government and Institutions

Government form
Unitary presidential constitutional republic with constitutionally devolved county governments, not a federal state. [7]
Constitution
Constitution of Kenya, promulgated August 27, 2010. [7]
Head of state and head of government
President William Samoei Ruto, both head of state and government, in office since September 13, 2022; verified against the presidential cabinet roster at the cutoff. [9]
Deputy president
Kithure Kindiki, as listed on the current presidential cabinet page. [9]
Prime Cabinet Secretary
Musalia Mudavadi, a Cabinet coordination office; the title does not create a parliamentary prime minister. [9]
Executive branch
President, Deputy President, Cabinet Secretaries and other constitutional/executive institutions; Cabinet Secretaries are appointed under constitutional procedures. [7] [9]
Parliament
Bicameral national legislature composed of the National Assembly and Senate. [7] [8]
National Assembly membership
290 constituency-elected members, 47 county-elected women representatives and 12 nominated members, plus the ex officio Speaker under Article 97. [7]
Senate membership
47 elected county senators, 16 nominated women, 4 other nominated representatives of youth and people with disabilities, plus ex officio Speaker under Article 98. [7] [8]
Parliamentary terms
Five-year election cycle for directly elected national legislators under constitutional provisions. [7]
Most recent general election
August 9, 2022 (presidential and general elections); the next ordinary cycle was due in 2027 as of the cutoff. [12]
Presidential election method
Direct popular election subject to the constitutional national-vote majority and geographic county thresholds. [7]
Presidential term limit
Two terms, each five years, under the 2010 Constitution. [7]
Devolved government
47 county governments, each with an elected governor and county assembly, exercising constitutionally assigned functions. [7]
County administrative scope
County governments coexist with national administration; Kenya’s devolved system is not legally described as 47 federal states. [7]
Judicial branch
Supreme Court, Court of Appeal, High Court and other courts established under the Constitution. [7]
Constitutional review
Independent judicial interpretation and constitutional adjudication operate under Chapter Ten of the Constitution. [7]
Election authority
Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC) is constitutionally responsible for elections and specified boundaries-related functions. [7]
Official and parliamentary languages
Kiswahili and English are national-level official languages; Kenyan Sign Language may also be used in the parliamentary proceedings specified by Article 120. [7]
Bill of rights
Constitutional protections include equality, dignity, association, religious freedom, political rights, social and economic rights and access to justice. [7]
Public accountability
Constitutional commissions and public bodies provide audit, anti-corruption and oversight functions; the legal design should not be equated with uniform outcomes. [7]
Contemporary institutional context
County finance, public debt, cost of living, rights protections, security and public services are continuing issues in political debate; institutional practice may differ from formal provisions.

Institutional classification: Kenya is a unitary presidential republic with constitutional devolution, not a federal parliamentary republic. The President serves simultaneously as head of state and government; the Prime Cabinet Secretary is not the prime minister of a parliamentary government. Officeholders are verified to the November 2026 editorial cutoff. [7] [9]

International Profile

United Nations
Member since December 16, 1963, following independence.
African Union
Member, participating in continental political and development initiatives.
East African Community
Founding partner state of the revived East African Community (2000), hosting significant regional transport and trade infrastructure. [16]
Common Market for Eastern and Southern Africa
COMESA member.
African Continental Free Trade Area
Kenya has ratified the AfCFTA agreement; implementation proceeds through continental, regional and domestic mechanisms. [16]
World Trade Organization
Member since January 1, 1995; previously a member of the GATT system. [15]
Commonwealth
Member since independence in 1963.
Intergovernmental Authority on Development
IGAD member in regional Horn of Africa cooperation.
Major regional role
Mombasa is a maritime gateway for East and Central Africa; Nairobi hosts numerous multilateral, private-sector and civil-society institutions. [4]
UN institutional presence
Nairobi is a major United Nations office location, including UNEP and UN-Habitat.
Diplomatic priorities
Regional security, economic integration, trade corridors, climate adaptation, investment and diplomatic engagement across Africa and the Indian Ocean region.
UNESCO heritage
Eight inscribed World Heritage sites as of the editorial cutoff. [13]
Currency code
KES (Kenyan shilling).
International dialing code
+254.
Internet country domain
.ke.
Time zone
East Africa Time (EAT), UTC+3; no regular daylight-saving adjustment.
Geographic trade access
Indian Ocean coastline with the Port of Mombasa and inland transport networks linking to neighboring countries. [12] [4]

Country Overview

The Gabonese Republic (République gabonaise), generally known as Gabon, is an Atlantic coastal state in equatorial Central Africa. Its capital and largest city is Libreville. Gabon became independent from France on August 17, 1960. Its contemporary political system is governed by a new constitution promulgated in December 2024, following a 2023 military coup and elections held in 2025. Gabon has extensive tropical forests and a largely urban population; petroleum, manganese and timber are central to its export economy. [11] [10] [6] [9]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Gabonese Republic (République gabonaise). [10] [9]
Common name
Gabon. [9]
Demonym
Gabonese; Gabonais and Gabonaise in French.
Capital
Libreville, on the Atlantic coast and the Gabon Estuary. [10] [2]
Continent / UN M49 subregion
Africa / Middle Africa; UN numeric code 266. [28] [2]
Indigenous and precolonial histories
Many communities and polities developed in the region before European colonial rule; Gabon has no single historically sufficient date of original state formation. [11] [25]
French colonial control
French rule expanded in the nineteenth century; the colony was incorporated into French Equatorial Africa in 1910. [11]
Autonomous republic
1958–1959: political autonomy and a republican constitutional framework within the French Community; distinct from sovereignty. [12]
Independence
August 17, 1960, from France. [11] [12]
First post-independence institutions
A 1960 constitutional law established the first independent institutions; Léon Mba became president in 1961. [12] [11]
Major constitutional periods
Constitutions and revisions in 1961, 1991 and subsequent years; the new constitution was approved by referendum November 16, 2024 and promulgated December 19, 2024. [10] [11]
Current constitutional order
The Fifth Republic under the 2024 Constitution, followed by national elections in 2025. [10] [17]
National day
August 17, commemorating independence in 1960. [10]
Additional constitutional commemoration
August 30 is designated the liberation holiday under the 2024 Constitution; this is a distinct state commemoration. [10]
Official motto
Union, Travail, Justice (Unity, Work, Justice). [10]
National anthem
La Concorde. [10]
ISO 3166-1 codes
GA (alpha-2); GAB (alpha-3); 266 (numeric). [28]
Internet country-code domain
.ga.

Historical context: Gabonese societies and communities existed long before French colonial rule. Autonomy within the French Community in 1958–1959, independence in 1960, and the new constitutional order of 2024–2025 are separate institutional milestones. The Bongo family dominated the presidency from 1967 until the 2023 military takeover; the current Fifth Republic followed a new constitution and elections. [11] [12] [10] [17]

Geography and Population

Total surface area
267,668 km² (UNData); official French diplomatic listing rounds to 267,667 km². [2] [9]
Land area
Approximately 257,670 km², excluding inland water, per UNCTAD. [4]
World Bank resident population
2,593,130 people (2025 estimate). [1]
2013 population census
1,811,079 residents (2013 census, UN Demographic Statistics Database final figure). [3]
Population growth
Approximately 2.1% in 2025 (World Bank). [1]
Population density
Approximately 10.1 residents per km² of land (2025 population divided by 2024 UNCTAD land area); a derived approximation. [1] [4]
Urbanization
A strongly urban country; sources report roughly four-fifths or more in urban places, depending on definition and reference year (World Bank overview about 80%; French diplomatic profile about 90%). [7] [9]
Capital population
UNData lists about 823,900 for Libreville in 2025, under its source-specific urban boundary definition. [2]
Largest urban centers
Libreville, Port-Gentil and Franceville; other significant centers include Oyem, Lambaréné, Mouila and Tchibanga. [9] [19]
Location
On the equatorial Atlantic coast of west-central Africa; the Equator crosses the country. [2]
Land borders
Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea and the Republic of the Congo. [7]
Coastline
Faces the Atlantic Ocean and the Gulf of Guinea. [7]
Primary river system
The Ogooué River and tributaries dominate much of the drainage system; other watercourses connect to the coastal estuary and surrounding basins. [25]
Relief
Coastal lowlands, interior uplands and plateaus, and forested mountains including the Cristal and Chaillu massifs.
Climate
Predominantly equatorial tropical: high humidity, substantial rainfall and regional wet and drier seasons.
Forest coverage
About 91.4% of land classified as forest in 2024 in the World Bank environmental series; definitions can change. [1]
Protected landscape
Tropical rainforest, inland wetlands, savanna mosaics and important protected ecosystems across the Congo Basin. [6] [24]
World Heritage properties
Lopé-Okanda cultural and natural landscape (2007); Ivindo National Park (2021). [24]
Environmental challenges
Deforestation and land-use pressures, conservation financing, wildlife protection and climate-related infrastructure risks. [6] [26]
Administrative geography
Nine provinces, subdivided into departments and local government units; the published Interior Ministry register lists 48 departments and 52 communes. [19]
Nine provinces
Estuaire; Haut-Ogooué; Moyen-Ogooué; Ngounié; Nyanga; Ogooué-Ivindo; Ogooué-Lolo; Ogooué-Maritime; Woleu-Ntem. [19]
Regional concentration
Libreville and the coastal Estuaire region contain a large proportion of population, administration and services; Port-Gentil is a major petroleum and port center. [7] [9]

Geographical and demographic conventions: Total surface area includes inland water; land area does not. The 2025 population is a World Bank estimate, not a national 2025 census. Urbanization measurements differ by statistical definitions. For departmental totals, the Gabonese Interior Ministry’s own published administrative breakdown is preferred to conflicting secondary datasets. [2] [4] [1] [3] [19]

Economy and International Trade

Economic system
Upper-middle-income, resource-dependent mixed economy with hydrocarbon extraction, mining, forestry, public services and private enterprise. [6] [7]
Nominal GDP
Approximately US$21.43 billion (2025, World Bank, current US dollars). [1]
Nominal GDP per capita
Approximately US$8,263 (2025, World Bank, current US dollars); not average household earnings. [1]
Real GDP growth
Approximately 2.5% in 2025 (World Bank annual series); preliminary estimates can differ across World Bank publications. [1] [7]
GDP source comparability
UNData lists approximately US$20.06 billion for 2025; this differs from the World Bank series because datasets and revision cycles are not identical. [2] [1]
Currency
Central African CFA franc, ISO code XAF (FCFA), issued by the Bank of Central African States (BEAC). [20] [2]
Currency exchange regime
Fixed euro parity: EUR 1 = XAF 655.957 under BEAC monetary arrangements. [20]
Major sectors
Oil and natural gas; manganese mining; timber and processed wood; construction, public administration, transport and trade. [6] [8]
Commodity dependence
Oil, manganese and wood together accounted for roughly 97% of Gabonese exports in the World Bank’s discussion of 2024 trade. [6]
Merchandise exports
US$13.10 billion (2024, UNCTAD estimate; goods only). [4]
Merchandise imports
US$4.00 billion (2024, UNCTAD estimate; goods only). [4]
Merchandise trade balance
US$9.10 billion surplus (2024, UNCTAD estimate); excludes services. [4]
Services exports
US$38 million (2024, UNCTAD estimate; services data are particularly sparse and may be revised). [4]
Services imports
US$901 million (2024, UNCTAD estimate). [4]
Principal merchandise exports
Crude petroleum, manganese ores, wood products and other commodities; products and rankings depend on reporting year. [5] [6]
2023 leading goods product
Crude petroleum accounted for about 51.8% of WTO/UN Comtrade 2023 merchandise exports. [5]
2023 manganese ore exports
Manganese ores and concentrates represented about 23.3% of WTO/UN Comtrade 2023 merchandise exports. [5]
Principal export destinations
The WTO 2023 goods profile lists the European Union and China as the two largest destinations; separate destination records are not directly comparable to 2024 UNCTAD totals. [5]
Manganese sector
One of the country’s principal sources of mineral export receipts and an important industrial activity outside oil. [8]
Wood processing
Forest products and manufactured timber provide export earnings; long-term policy seeks more local processing and sustainable forestry. [6]
Petroleum sector
Oil production and prices strongly influence government revenues, foreign-exchange receipts and investment. [6] [8]
Unemployment
Approximately 20.2% (2025 World Bank/ILO modeled unemployment estimate); not a stand-alone national household-survey observation. [1]
Inflation
Approximately 1.8% (2025 annual consumer-price inflation, World Bank series). [1]
Fiscal pressures
Public finance vulnerabilities include debt servicing, oil-revenue volatility and the need to improve non-oil revenue collection. [8]
Economic development priorities
Diversification, jobs, transport and electricity infrastructure, industrial processing, education and forest-based value creation. [6] [8]
Statistical caution
2025 GDP and 2024 merchandise trade are not same-year measurements; export goods shares from 2023 cannot be presented as 2024 percentages. [1] [4] [5]

People and Society

Language of official public business
French, designated by the 2024 Constitution as the official working language (langue officielle de travail). [10]
National-language policy
The Constitution provides for the protection, promotion and teaching of national languages. [10]
Major languages spoken
Fang, Myene, Nzebi, Punu/Shira and Teke, with Kota, Tsogo and many other Gabonese languages alongside French. [27] [10]
French in everyday life
Used extensively in education, public administration, journalism and urban communication, while language use varies among households. [9]
Linguistic diversity
The country includes numerous language communities; no fixed list of languages should be interpreted as exhaustive or mutually exclusive. [27]
Religious composition: Christianity
The French diplomatic country profile estimates Christianity at about 85% (updated October 2026); this is a broad summary, not a new census-based distribution. [9]
Religious composition: Islam
The same diplomatic profile estimates Islam at roughly 10%; populations and estimates vary between sources and survey methods. [9]
Traditional belief and practice
Indigenous spiritual practices, including forms associated with Bwiti, coexist with Christianity and other religious affiliations. [9] [27]
Religious freedom and state
The 2024 Constitution defines Gabon as a secular republic with separation of state and religions. [10]
Religious statistics caveat
Self-identification, religious observance and syncretic practice are different measures; approximate percentages should not be presented as 2026 administrative counts. [9] [27]
Cultural and community identities
Includes Fang, Punu/Shira, Nzebi, Myene, Teke, Kota and numerous other communities, as well as residents of foreign origin. [27]
Population measurement
The 2013 population census and 2025 World Bank estimate refer to different dates and methodologies. [3] [1]
Urban and rural populations
A large majority reside in urban settings; published proportions depend on the settlement definition and population series. [7] [9]
Life expectancy
Approximately 69 years at birth (2024, World Bank). [1]
Adult literacy
Approximately 89% (2021 estimate cited in the French Foreign Ministry’s October 2026 profile); this is not a newly completed 2026 census. [9]
Public health context
Healthcare access and outcomes vary by urban-rural location; access to primary care, maternal care and infectious-disease services remain development priorities. [8]
Education
French-medium primary and secondary education, public and private tertiary institutions, and professional training systems serve a young, heavily urban population. [9] [8]
Cultural heritage
Oral histories, music, performance, art and ceremonial practices are significant across ethnic and linguistic communities. [24] [27]
Heritage landscape
Lopé-Okanda connects archaeological records of long human occupation with the river corridor, forests and savanna environments. [25]
Economic and social inequality
Oil and mineral income per resident does not guarantee broad household prosperity; employment opportunities and access to services vary considerably. [6] [8]

Language and religion interpretation: French is the constitutionally designated official working language, while national languages are protected. Religious percentages are approximate estimates from the cited country profile, not results of a 2026 religious census. Cultural and religious identifications can overlap in lived practice. Government and Institutions

Form of government
Unitary, decentralized republic with a presidential executive under the Constitution of December 19, 2024 (Fifth Republic). [10]
Constitution in force
Referendum of November 16, 2024, followed by promulgation on December 19, 2024; the older 1991 constitution is superseded. [10]
Head of state
President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema, inaugurated May 3, 2025. [15] [14]
Head of government
President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema; the 2024 constitutional arrangement places this role with the president. [16] [9]
Vice President of the Republic
Hugues Alexandre Barro Chambrier (office-holder listing updated October 16, 2026). [15]
Vice President of the Government
Hermann Immongault (office-holder listing updated October 16, 2026). This is distinct from the Vice President of the Republic. [15] [16]
Prime minister
No separate prime minister in the current presidential cabinet framework; the presidency combines head-of-state and head-of-government functions. [16] [10]
Presidential election
April 12, 2025, followed by constitutional court proclamation on April 25; Oligui Nguema was officially declared elected. [13]
Certified presidential result
94.85% of valid votes for Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema (official 2025 constitutional court proclamation); the percentage is not an independent evaluation of electoral competition. [13]
Presidential term
Seven years under the 2024 constitution, with a maximum of two presidential terms. [14] [10]
Executive ministries
Ministers responsible for finance, foreign affairs, security, justice, energy, industry, infrastructure and other portfolios; cabinet composition may change by decree. [16] [15]
Legislature
Bicameral parliament: National Assembly (lower chamber) and Senate (upper chamber), reconstituted after the 2023–2025 transition. [10] [17]
National Assembly
145 seats contested in the 2025 legislative election; new assembly convened in November 2025. [17]
Speaker of the National Assembly
Michel Régis Onanga Mamadou Ndiaye, elected November 17, 2025. [17]
Senate
70 seats, filled through indirect election by local elected bodies under the 2025 institutional system. [17] [18]
President of the Senate
Huguette Nyana Ekoume, elected December 17, 2025. [18]
Legislative terms
Both parliamentary chambers operate under five-year terms in the Fifth Republic framework. [17] [10]
Latest national legislative elections
2025, following the military-led transition; parliamentary institutions resumed with elected members. [17]
Judicial system
Constitutional jurisdiction and courts of the Republic, with constitutional guarantees for judicial institutions. [10]
Territorial administration
Nine provinces and 48 departments in the official Interior Ministry territorial register; the ministry also lists 52 communes. [19]
Provincial administration
Provinces include Estuaire, Haut-Ogooué, Moyen-Ogooué, Ngounié, Nyanga, Ogooué-Ivindo, Ogooué-Lolo, Ogooué-Maritime and Woleu-Ntem. [19]
Political transition, 2023
Military officers removed President Ali Bongo following a disputed presidential election announcement on August 30, 2023. [11] [17]
Constitutional transition, 2024–2025
A new constitution and subsequent presidential, parliamentary and local elections established the Fifth Republic institutions. [10] [17] [14]
Elected assembly party balance
The Democratic Union of Builders won more than 100 of the 145 seats in the 2025 National Assembly election, according to the IPU election summary. [17]
Governmental description caveat
The formal constitutional and institutional description is distinct from an independent assessment of political competition, civil rights or executive influence. [10] [17]

Constitutional source caution: Gabon’s 2024 constitution replaced the older 1991 constitutional text still indexed on some government websites. The Vice President of the Republic and Vice President of the Government are separate offices; there is no separate prime minister in the current constitutional cabinet. Legislature figures correspond to the 2025 elections and subsequent institutional organization. [10] [15] [16] [17]

International Profile

United Nations
Member since September 20, 1960. [21]
African Union
Member of the African Union and the earlier Organization of African Unity; post-2023 suspension measures were subsequently lifted. [9]
Central African Economic and Monetary Community
Member of CEMAC, sharing the XAF currency and BEAC with Cameroon, the Central African Republic, Chad, Equatorial Guinea and the Republic of the Congo. [20]
Economic Community of Central African States
Member of ECCAS/CEEAC and regional Central African cooperation initiatives. [9]
Commonwealth
Joined June 2022; partially suspended after the 2023 coup; suspension lifted July 2025; removed from the Commonwealth Ministerial Action Group formal agenda on November 1, 2026. [23]
World Trade Organization
Member since January 1, 1995; previously a GATT contracting party from May 3, 1963. [22]
Major international financial institutions
Member of the World Bank and International Monetary Fund; engaged in economic surveillance and development lending. [8]
Regional monetary system
Uses XAF under the Central African monetary union; the BEAC manages currency issuance and monetary arrangements. [20]
Important external economic partners
European Union and China among the two largest destination markets in the WTO’s 2023 merchandise profile; other trade relationships vary annually. [5]
UNESCO World Heritage
Two inscribed properties: Lopé-Okanda (2007) and Ivindo National Park (2021). [24]
Atlantic and regional setting
Coastal member of the Congo Basin region with regional ecological, migration, trade and transport connections. [7] [24]
Time zone
West Africa Time (WAT), UTC+01:00; daylight-saving time is not generally observed.
International dialing code
+241.

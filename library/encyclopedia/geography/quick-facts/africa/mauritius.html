Country Overview

Mauritius, officially the Republic of Mauritius, is an island country in the southwestern Indian Ocean, conventionally classified within Eastern Africa. Its capital is Port Louis, and the republic includes the main island of Mauritius, autonomous Rodrigues and smaller outer islands. Independence from the United Kingdom came on March 12, 1968; the country became a republic on March 12, 1992. Its economy combines tourism, finance, trade, manufacturing and agricultural activities. Mauritius is a parliamentary republic led by Prime Minister Navinchandra Ramgoolam, with Dharambeer Gokhool as President. The Chagos Archipelago remains a significant territorial and diplomatic question: although Mauritius and the United Kingdom signed a treaty in May 2025, the agreement had not entered into force by the October 2026 official status update. Sovereign Commonwealth realm with a parliamentary constitutional monarchy, 1968–1992. [9] [10]

Early history
The islands had no established permanent Indigenous population before European settlement; sailors from different maritime traditions knew of the region before colonization. [25]
Dutch settlement
Dutch colonization began in the seventeenth century; the island was named for Maurice of Nassau. [25]
French period
French control began in 1715; the island became known as Île de France. Enslaved labor was central to plantation development. [25] [9]
British period
British forces captured the island in 1810; British rule preceded the transition to independence. [25] [9]
Labor migration history
After British abolition of slavery, indentured workers, many from the Indian subcontinent, became integral to the sugar economy and modern population. [9] [21]
1967 constitutional milestone
The August 1967 legislative election was followed by a resolution requesting independence. [10]
National flag
Four horizontal stripes: red, blue, yellow and green. [25]

Geography and Population

Total area
Approximately 2,040 km² (788 sq. mi.), for the commonly reported territory; Chagos is excluded from the administered-area total. [16] [25]
Reported land area
2,007 km² used by Statistics Mauritius for its 2022 population-density calculation; differs from the commonly cited 2,040 km² country-area measure because datasets use different geographic definitions. [4] [16]
Main island
Mauritius, the largest island, with Port Louis as capital; volcanic in origin and fringed by reefs. [25]
Other administered islands
Rodrigues, Agaléga, and the Cargados Carajos Shoals (Saint Brandon), alongside smaller islets. [19] [25]
Rodrigues
Autonomous outer island approximately 560 km east of Mauritius, with its own Regional Assembly. [19] [26]
Agaléga
Small inhabited outer islands to the north of the main island; included in population and territorial administration. [1]
Geographic position
Southwestern Indian Ocean east of Madagascar and northeast of Réunion; no land borders. [16] [25]
Physical landscape
Volcanic plateaus, uplands, basaltic terrain, river valleys, low coastal plains, and coral-fringed lagoons. [16]
Climate
Tropical maritime, with a warmer, wetter summer and cooler, drier winter; influenced by southeast trade winds. [16]
Natural hazards
Tropical cyclones, intense rainfall, flash floods, storm surges, coastal erosion and sea-level rise are important risks.

People and Society

Everyday lingua franca
Mauritian Creole (Kreol Morisien) is widely used in ordinary conversation. [4]
Language of the National Assembly
English is constitutionally designated for the Assembly’s official business; members may address the chair in French. This is narrower than claiming the Constitution declares a single nationwide official language. [27] [14]
Other widely used languages
French, English and Bhojpuri are prominent; Hindi, Urdu, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi and Chinese languages also have cultural, educational or community roles. [4]
2022 Creole home-language share
90.0% reported speaking only Creole at home in the 2022 census home-language classification. [4]
2022 Bhojpuri home-language share
5.1% reported Bhojpuri only at home. [4]
2022 French home-language share
4.4% reported French only at home. [4]
2022 English home-language share
0.6% reported English only at home; this does not measure proficiency or ability to use English elsewhere. [4]
Religious traditions
Hinduism, Christianity (including Roman Catholicism and Protestant churches), Islam, Buddhism and other spiritual or nonreligious affiliations. [18]
Religious composition: Hindu
Approximately 48% of the population in the 2011 census, as summarized in the U.S. Department of State’s 2023 report. [18]
Religious composition: Christian
Approximately 26% Roman Catholic and 6% other Christian in the same 2011 census-based summary. [18]
Religious composition: Muslim
Approximately 17% in the 2011 census-based summary. [18]
Other faiths or nonaffiliation
The remaining approximately 3% of the rounded 2011 religious-affiliation figures includes other traditions and people without a recorded religious affiliation. [18]
Population origins
A diverse population shaped by African, Malagasy, Indian, European and Chinese ancestry and historical movements across the Indian Ocean. [9]
Literacy
91.9% in the 2022 population census, using that publication’s age and measurement definition. [3]
Average household size
3.3 persons in the 2022 census. [3]
Education
Public and private educational institutions include primary, secondary, vocational and tertiary systems; educational instruction uses multiple languages.
Cultural heritage
Aapravasi Ghat (inscribed 2006) and Le Morne Cultural Landscape (inscribed 2008) are UNESCO World Heritage properties. [21]
Historical memory
Aapravasi Ghat documents indentured migration; Le Morne is associated with the history and memory of slavery and resistance. [21]
Social diversity
Mauritian national identity encompasses multiple linguistic, religious and cultural communities; present-day nationality is not reducible to a single ethnic group.
Religious data qualification
Religious percentages shown here are specifically from the 2011 census, not a presumed 2025–2026 update. [18]

Language and religion methodology: The 2022 home-language results describe what respondents most commonly reported speaking at home, not the share fluent in any language. Religious affiliation figures here derive from the older 2011 census, as explicitly labeled by the source; no more recent national religious composition is presumed. [4] [18]

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Sovereign democratic parliamentary republic under a written Constitution. [9] [14]
Head of state
President Dharambeer Gokhool; assumed office December 7, 2024. [11]
Head of government
Prime Minister Dr. Navinchandra Ramgoolam; returned to office following the November 2024 general election. [12] [15]
Deputy prime minister
Marie Arianne Navarre-Marie (named in the official Cabinet list dated May 6, 2026). [13]
Executive structure
The Prime Minister leads the government and Cabinet; the President performs the constitutional functions of head of state. [14] [12]
Constitutional history
The 1968 constitutional order was amended to establish a republic effective March 12, 1992. [10] [9]
National legislature
Unicameral National Assembly, within a Parliament consisting constitutionally of the President and National Assembly. [14]
Elected seats
62 directly elected members: 60 from 20 three-member constituencies on Mauritius and two from Rodrigues. [14] [15]
Additional representation
Up to eight seats may be allocated under the constitutional best-loser system; the Attorney-General may sit ex officio. [14] [15]
Assembly composition reported by Parliament
The National Assembly website describes 67 sitting members after the 2024 election: 62 elected, four additional allocated seats, and one ex-officio Attorney-General (site-reported composition). [14]
Latest national general election
November 10, 2024; the Alliance du Changement won 60 of 62 directly elected seats. [15]
Prime minister sworn in
November 13, 2024, following the 2024 election. [15]
Speaker of the National Assembly
Shirin Aumeeruddy-Cziffra elected when the new Assembly first met in November 2024 (as recorded in the IPU election account). [15]
Judicial institutions
Independent courts, including the Supreme Court and subordinate courts; appeals in prescribed cases may reach the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council.
Local geographic divisions
Nine traditional districts on Mauritius island, alongside the distinct regional arrangements for Rodrigues and the outer islands. [19]
Autonomous region
Rodrigues has a Regional Assembly and Executive Council under the Rodrigues Regional Assembly framework. [19] [26]
Other outer-island administration
Agaléga and Cargados Carajos are administered under national outer-island arrangements rather than as ordinary mainland districts. [19]
Political rights
Multiparty parliamentary electoral competition under the Constitution, with general elections and local/regional institutions. [15] [14]
Current-office verification
President, Prime Minister and Cabinet names were checked against official government pages for the November 5, 2026 editorial cutoff. [11] [12] [13]

Legislative terminology: The Constitution provides 62 directly elected Assembly seats plus a variable additional representation allocation. As of the National Assembly’s published description of the post-2024 chamber, the count is 67 including the ex-officio Attorney-General; describing the chamber as permanently fixed at 70 seats would be misleading. [14] [15]

International Profile

United Nations membership
Admitted April 24, 1968, shortly after independence. [20]
African Union
Member of the African Union and participant in continental cooperation. [9]
Southern African Development Community
Joined SADC in 1995. [16]
Common Market for Eastern and Southern Africa
Member of COMESA, a major regional trade grouping. [9] [7]
Commonwealth of Nations
Member since independence, remaining in the Commonwealth after becoming a republic. [9]
Francophonie
Participant in the International Organisation of La Francophonie. [9]
Indian Ocean Commission
Participates in the Indian Ocean Commission with neighboring island states and territories. [9]
Indian Ocean Rim Association
Member of the Indian Ocean Rim Association, focused on regional cooperation. [9]
World Trade Organization
WTO member and participant in multilateral trading rules. [6]
Strategic location
Maritime location links African, Asian and European commerce through the southwestern Indian Ocean.
Chagos Archipelago claim
Mauritius claims sovereignty over the Chagos Archipelago, including Diego Garcia; the archipelago has been administered by the United Kingdom as the British Indian Ocean Territory. [22] [23]
2025 Chagos agreement
Mauritius and the United Kingdom signed a treaty on May 22, 2025 addressing sovereignty and future arrangements for the Diego Garcia base. [22]
Chagos status at editorial cutoff
The treaty had not entered into force by the October 2026 Mauritian government statement; related UK implementation legislation had lapsed in 2026. It is inaccurate to describe the handover as completed as of November 5, 2026. [23] [24]
Separate maritime claims
Mauritius’s constitutional and diplomatic claims include areas not covered by the conventional inhabited-island statistical reporting area; territorial claims should not be silently added to the standard area figure. [23] [25]
Major export relationships
France, South Africa, the United States, United Kingdom and Madagascar were prominent in the 2025 export-destination distribution. [7]
Climate and international policy
The country participates in international climate discussions as a small island developing state vulnerable to cyclone and coastal risks.

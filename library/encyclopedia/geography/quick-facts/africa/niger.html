Country Overview

Niger, officially the Republic of Niger, is a large, landlocked state in West Africa spanning the southern Sahara and the Sahel. Its capital is Niamey. The nation became independent from France on August 3, 1960. Its diverse societies and economies encompass agriculture, pastoralism, oil production, uranium resources and regional trade, with insecurity and climate variability posing major development challenges. Niger is distinct from the neighboring state of Nigeria. [4] [15] [14]

After a July 26, 2023 military coup, Niger entered a transitional governing period. As of November 5, 2026, Abdourahamane Tiani serves as president and Ali Mahaman Lamine Zeine as prime minister. The March 26, 2025 Refoundation Charter is the country's transitional fundamental law; it designates Hausa as the national language, with English and French as working languages. [9] [7] [6]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Niger (République du Niger). [7]
Short name
Niger.
Demonym
Nigerien (distinct from Nigerian, referring to Nigeria).
Capital
Niamey, on the Niger River. [4]
Continent
Africa.
UN geographic subregion
Western Africa. [4]
Broader geographic setting
Landlocked central Sahel and southern Sahara; Niger is geographically distinct from neighboring Nigeria. [4] [15]
Precolonial historical societies
Hausa states and sultanates, Songhay and Bornu influences, Tuareg communities and other regional trading and pastoral societies long predate modern Niger.
Agadez history
A major Saharan caravan and urban trading center associated with the Sultanate of Aïr and wider trans-Saharan networks. [20]
Colonial era
France consolidated control of Niger in the early twentieth century; the territory was constituted as a French colony in 1922.
Pre-independence political status
Niger became an autonomous republic within the French Community on December 18, 1958.
Independence
August 3, 1960, from France.
Independence Day holiday
August 3.
UN admission
September 20, 1960. [23]
First president
Hamani Diori (1960–1974).
1974 political rupture
A military coup brought Seyni Kountché to power.
National Conference
The 1991 Sovereign National Conference advanced a transition toward multiparty political competition.
1993 electoral transition
Multiparty democratic elections followed the National Conference process.
1996 coup
Ibrahim Baré Maïnassara seized power in a military coup.
1999 transition
Following the assassination of Maïnassara, the military organized a transition to civilian elected institutions.
2010 coup and constitutional transition
A military coup removed Mamadou Tandja; a new Constitution was approved in 2010.
2011 elected administration
Mahamadou Issoufou became president after the post-2010 transition.
2021 transfer of power
Mohamed Bazoum succeeded Issoufou after the 2020–21 election process.
July 26, 2023 coup
Presidential guards detained President Bazoum and military authorities took control; the 2010 Constitution and elected institutions were suspended. [8] [15]
2025 transition charter
The Refoundation Charter was promulgated March 26, 2025 and functions as the transitional fundamental law. [6] [7] [10]
2025 political party measure
Existing political parties were dissolved by an ordinance of March 26, 2025. [7] [24]
Historical government distinction
A prior multiparty constitutional republic and the present military-led transitional system are separate phases; the 2010 Constitution should not be described as currently operative. [8] [7]
Country flag
Three horizontal orange, white and green bands with an orange disk centered in the white band. [7]
National motto
Fraternité – Travail – Progrès (Fraternity – Work – Progress). [7]
National anthem
L’Honneur de la Patrie, as named in the 2025 Refoundation Charter. [7]
National day calendar distinction
December 18 marks the historical proclamation of the republic (1958); August 3 marks independence (1960).

Historical distinction: Republic status in 1958, full independence in 1960, the 2010 constitutional order, the 2023 coup and the 2025 transitional Charter are separate milestones. [7] [8] [6]

Population scale
Nearly 27.92 million people in the World Bank’s 2025 annual estimate. [2]

2012 census recorded 17.14 million; 2025 population is a later estimate, not an additional census. [5] [2]

3.2% in 2025, among the world’s faster-growing national populations. [1]

Large youth population, with substantial demand for schools, health services and employment. [22]

Hausa (Haoussa), designated the national language by Article 12 of the Refoundation Charter. [7]
Working languages, 2025 charter
English and French are working languages in Article 12, not designated the sole constitutional official languages. [7]

French was official under the suspended 2010 Constitution; the March 2025 Charter changed the formal formulation. [7]

Arabic, Buduma, Fulfuldé, Gurmancema, Hausa, Kanuri, Tagdal, Tamajaq, Tassawaq, Tubu and Zarma-Sonrai. [7]

Widely used in southern and central Niger, cross-border markets and everyday communication. [7]
Zarma-Songhay
Important western language grouping around Niamey and Niger River communities. [7]

Spoken by Fulani communities associated with diverse pastoral and settled livelihoods. [7]

Tuareg language varieties, especially relevant to northern and central regions. [7]

Major religious affiliation

Sunni Islam; Maliki jurisprudence is prominent in historic regional practice. [21]

Christian denominations, Baha’i and Indigenous religious practices in comparatively small communities. [21]

Sources and Methodology

MGU's Niger Quick Facts entry relies on identified records from the World Bank, WTO, United Nations, IMF, UNESCO, Nigerien state institutions and statistical reports, supplemented by diplomatic and historical background sources. Every dated statistical entry specifies the underlying observation year. The publication or verification date is not the measurement date.

Population and area: The World Bank's annual population estimate for 2025 is 27,917,831; the official fourth national population and housing census found 17,138,707 in 2012. The UNData total surface area reference is 1,267,000 km². The calculated density of approximately 22 residents per km² uses the 2025 estimate and UN surface-area convention. [2] [5] [4]

National accounts: World Bank 2025 nominal GDP is US$21.65 billion and GDP per capita US$775.4 in current dollars. Real GDP growth of 7.0% and average consumer-price inflation of –4.5% in 2025 measure other concepts. The World Bank's 2026 growth figure is a forecast and subject to revision. [3] [1] [16]

Goods trade: WTO / UN Comtrade detail reports US$467.4 million recorded exports in 2023 and US$1,501.2 million recorded imports in 2024. Because those years differ, this page deliberately does not calculate a merchandise trade balance. Product classifications and reporting coverage may omit flows, and 2023 export shares should not be used to describe the 2025 oil-export economy. IMF reports oil pipeline disruptions and disrupted uranium shipments in 2024–25. [13] [14] [18]

Legal and institutional status: The March 2025 Refoundation Charter operates as the transitional fundamental law after the suspension of the 2010 Constitution in 2023. The Charter identifies Hausa as national language and English and French as working languages. Its 60-month transition period is not an unconditional fixed election deadline. The proposed May 2026 increase to 19 regions is described as a proposal, not as the established boundary system. [7] [10] [8] [12] [11]

Religion and human development: Religion estimates are from a historically dated diplomatic report and are not misrepresented as a modern census. The World Bank's 2024 life expectancy and electricity-access indicators are separate from 2025 population/GDP estimates. UNESCO maintains the World Heritage list, which has three inscribed Niger properties. [21] [1] [20]

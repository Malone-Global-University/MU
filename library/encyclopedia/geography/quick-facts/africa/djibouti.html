Country Overview

Djibouti is a small republic in the Horn of Africa at the southern approaches to the Red Sea, bordering Eritrea, Ethiopia and Somalia and opening onto the Gulf of Aden. Its capital and principal port share the name Djibouti. The country gained independence from France on June 27, 1977 and operates under a 1992 constitutional system revised in 2010 and 2025. Its hot, largely arid landscape and small domestic market contrast with the strategic role of its ports, transport corridors, and services in the wider Ethiopian and Red Sea economies. [16] [9] [7]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Djibouti; République de Djibouti; Arabic: جمهورية جيبوتي. [8]
Common name
Djibouti; the capital city shares the country’s name.
Capital
Djibouti City (Djibouti-Ville), the principal port and largest urban concentration. [4] [3]
Continent / UN M49 subregion
Africa / Eastern Africa (part of the Horn of Africa). [18]
Early historical setting
Afar and Somali-speaking communities, pastoralist networks, and commerce across the Gulf of Aden and Red Sea long predated European colonial rule. [16]
French establishment at Obock
A French treaty with an Afar ruler in 1862 was an early colonial foothold on the Gulf of Tadjourah; subsequent agreements expanded French influence. [16]
French Somaliland
The territory was administered under the name French Somaliland from the late nineteenth century until 1967. [16]
French Territory of the Afars and the Issas
Renamed in 1967, after a territorial referendum held under French rule. [16]
Independence referendum
A May 1977 referendum endorsed independence from France; the political process preceded proclamation of the new republic. [16]
Independence from France
June 27, 1977; the principal national independence and founding date. [16] [17]
First president
Hassan Gouled Aptidon, who led the republic from independence in 1977 until 1999. [16]
Post-independence political change
A 1992 constitutional order formalized a multiparty framework; conflict between government forces and Afar-led insurgents in the 1990s shaped subsequent political negotiations. [8] [16]
Current constitutional foundation
1992 Constitution, revised in 2010 and again in November 2025; subsequent electoral legislation implemented candidate-eligibility changes in 2026. [8] [9]
2025 constitutional revision
Removed the former upper age limit for presidential candidacy, introduced a five-year residence rule with exceptions, and altered the composition of the Constitutional Council. [9]
National day
Independence Day, June 27. [16]
National motto
Unité, Égalité, Paix (Unity, Equality, Peace). [8]
ISO 3166-1 codes
DJ (alpha-2); DJI (alpha-3); 262 (numeric). [18]
Internet country-code domain
.dj

Geography and Population

Total surface area
23,200 km² (approximately 8,958 sq. mi.), UN country profile and national statistical reference. [4] [2]
Land area
Approximately 23,180 km² (UNCTAD 2024 reference); land area excludes inland water and therefore differs slightly from total area. [5]
World Bank population estimate
1,184,076 residents in 2025 (annual modeled estimate; not a census enumeration). [1]
National census (RGPH-3)
1,066,809 residents as of May 20, 2024, according to the final national census results released in 2025. [2] [3]
Population methodological difference
The 2025 World Bank series and the 2024 nationally enumerated census use different years and methods. Do not describe the difference as one-year natural population growth. [1] [3]
Population density
Approximately 51.1 people per km² of land using the World Bank 2025 estimate and UNCTAD land area (calculated); approximately 46.0 per km² of total surface area using the 2024 national census and 23,200 km². [1] [2] [5]
Djibouti City population
767,250 residents (2024 national census); approximately 71.9% of the country’s census population. [3]
2024 census sex distribution
536,447 female residents (50.3%) and 530,362 male residents (49.7%). [2] [3]
Median age
23 years (2024 national census summary published by INSTAD). [2]
Neighboring countries
Eritrea, Ethiopia and Somalia; its short southeastern boundary adjoins Somaliland, which is internationally generally treated as part of Somalia.
Maritime geography
On the Gulf of Aden and the Gulf of Tadjourah, near the Bab el-Mandeb strait and southern entrance to the Red Sea. [7]
Terrain
Volcanic plateaus and ranges, faulted basins, low-lying salt depressions, and a coastal plain in a predominantly arid environment. [20]
Lake Assal
A highly saline lake in the Afar Depression; its shoreline lies about 155 m below sea level, among the lowest exposed land points on Earth.
Lake Abbe
Saline lake near the Ethiopian border, recognized for mineral formations, wetlands and unique geological scenery.
Climate
Extremely hot, arid to semi-arid lowlands with limited and irregular rainfall; some highlands are cooler. [7]
Principal environmental pressures
Water scarcity, drought, high temperatures, flash floods, and coastal/infrastructure exposure to climate change. [20]
Principal settlements
Djibouti City, Ali Sabieh, Dikhil, Tadjourah, Obock and Arta; the capital dominates the urban population. [3]
Time zone
East Africa Time (EAT), UTC+03:00 throughout the year.

Population and area conventions: The nationally validated 2024 census and World Bank’s 2025 population series have different statistical scopes and methods. The UN and national total surface area (23,200 km²) differs slightly from UNCTAD land-only area (23,180 km²). [2] [3] [4] [5] [1]

Economy and International Trade

Economic system
Service-led market economy with significant port, freight, logistics, transit and public-sector activity; heavy reliance on imported food and many manufactured goods. [7]
Nominal GDP
US$4.62 billion (2025, World Bank; current U.S. dollars). [1]
Nominal GDP per capita
US$3,905.6 (2025, World Bank; current U.S. dollars); this is output per resident, not average personal income. [1]
Real GDP growth
6.5% (2025, World Bank annual series). [1] [7]
Currency
Djiboutian franc (DJF; also FDJ in French-language materials). [15]
Exchange-rate regime
Currency board with a long-established fixed parity of approximately DJF 177.721 for US$1. [15]
2025 modeled unemployment
26.0% (2025 World Bank presentation of ILO modeled estimate, not a direct national unemployment census). [1]
2025 consumer inflation
−0.3% annual change (World Bank 2025 series; a small decline in the price index rather than a decline in nominal GDP). [1]
2024 merchandise exports
US$5.169 billion (UNCTADstat; goods including substantial re-export/transit-linked transactions; not domestically produced output alone). [5] [6]
2024 merchandise imports
Approximately US$5.546 billion (UNCTADstat estimate, goods only). [5]
2024 merchandise trade balance
Approximately US$0.38 billion deficit using UNCTAD’s rounded totals; the publisher reports roughly US$376 million deficit. [5]
2024 services exports
US$1.173 billion (UNCTADstat, services); 60.5% classified as transport services. [5]
2024 services imports
Approximately US$730 million (UNCTADstat); services balance around US$443 million surplus. [5]
2023 WTO merchandise comparator
US$4.7847 billion in goods exports (WTO/UN Comtrade 2023). Product and destination rankings below refer to 2023, not UNCTAD’s 2024 total. [6]
Leading 2023 export market
Ethiopia accounted for 83.5% of WTO-recorded merchandise exports (US$3.995 billion in 2023), illustrating the importance of trade routed through the Djibouti corridor. [6]
Strategic trade role
Ports and logistics form an essential gateway for landlocked Ethiopia and regional commerce at the Red Sea–Gulf of Aden maritime intersection. [7] [20]
Domestic production
Modest agriculture, livestock activity, fishing, salt and mineral-related activities; very limited arable land and severe water constraints. [7]
Principal transport assets
Port facilities in and near Djibouti City and Doraleh, the Addis Ababa–Djibouti railway, roads to Ethiopia and associated free-zone and cargo infrastructure. [7] [20]
Economic vulnerabilities
Dependence on foreign transit demand, maritime stability, imported food and energy, external financing and infrastructure resilience. [7] [20]

People and Society

Official languages
Arabic and French, as defined by the Constitution. [8]
Widely spoken languages
Somali and Afar are major indigenous and everyday languages; Arabic and French also feature in religion, administration, education and public life. [8]
Other languages
Additional languages occur in expatriate, migrant and refugee communities, including regional Horn of Africa languages.
Ethnolinguistic communities
Somali-speaking communities (including Issa) and Afar communities are the two major historical population groups; precise contemporary proportions are not asserted without a comparable census. [16]
State religion
Islam is identified as the religion of the state by Article 1 of the Constitution; the text also states respect for all beliefs. [8]
Religion: indicative population share
Approximately 94% Sunni Muslim in the U.S. government’s 2023 estimate; this is not a new 2024 or 2025 census measurement. [14]
Other religious communities
Small communities of Shia Muslims, Roman Catholics, Protestants, Orthodox Christians, Hindus, Baha’is and other affiliations are reported; figures vary by definition. [14]
Religion and nationality
Religious population estimates may encompass citizens, migrants, expatriates and refugees; nationality and religion are not interchangeable categories. [14]
Life expectancy
Approximately 66 years at birth (World Bank, 2024 country-data series). [1]
Population age structure
Young national population; the 2024 census reported a median age of 23 years. [2]
Urban concentration
About 71.9% of the 2024 census population lived in the Djibouti-Ville region; this should not be conflated with an international standardized urbanization rate. [3]
Education languages
French and Arabic appear in formal education, with Somali and Afar widely used in home and community contexts. [8]
Electricity access
69.2% of population (World Bank 2024 indicator); not necessarily a measure of supply reliability. [1]
Refugees and regional mobility
Location between the Horn of Africa and Arabian Peninsula has long created migration, asylum and cross-border labor and family networks. [7] [14]
Cultural life
Afar and Somali poetic, oral and musical traditions; regional cuisines, Islamic festivals and coastal trading influences.
Agriculture and food security
Food supply depends substantially on imports because rainfall and arable land are limited. [7]
Health and living conditions
Heat, water access and climate-related stresses affect public health and community services; conditions vary substantially between Djibouti City and rural regions. [20]

Religious data caution: The 94% Sunni Muslim figure is a 2023 U.S. government estimate and not an enumeration from Djibouti’s 2024 population census. Official-language status does not measure how many residents speak each language in daily life. [14] [8]

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Unitary presidential republic, with a president as head of state and head of the executive and an appointed prime minister. [8] [11]
Foundational law
Constitution of 1992, as amended in 2010 and November 2025; 2026 organic election legislation also governs eligibility. [8] [9]
President
Ismaïl Omar Guelleh, president since 1999; re-elected in April 2026 for a sixth term. [10] [21]
April 2026 presidential election
Constitutional Council final proclamation on April 21, 2026 confirmed 97.01% of valid votes for Guelleh; earlier preliminary reporting cited a different figure. [10]
Prime minister
Abdoulkader Kamil Mohamed, appointed in 2013 and reappointed by presidential decree on May 14, 2026. [11] [21]
Executive branch
President, prime minister, Council of Ministers and ministries; the president has central authority under the constitutional system. [8] [11]
Presidential candidate age
Under the 2025 amendment, a candidate must be at least 40; the previous maximum age of 75 was removed. [9]
Presidential term
Five years; changes to term-limit rules occurred through the 2010 constitutional revisions. [8] [9]
National legislature
National Assembly (Assemblée nationale), currently unicameral; the constitution contemplates a future Senate that has not been constituted. [12] [9]
National Assembly membership
65 members; the most recent legislative election identified by the Inter-Parliamentary Union was February 2023. [12]
Constitutional review
Constitutional Council; the November 2025 amendment expanded its designed composition to nine members. [9]
Judiciary
Supreme Court and other judicial institutions under constitutional provisions for separation of powers and judicial independence. [8] [9]
First-order administration
Five regions—Ali Sabieh, Arta, Dikhil, Obock and Tadjourah—plus the capital Djibouti City with a separate territorial status. [13]
Djibouti City communes
Ras-Dika, Boulaos and Balbala; governed within the city’s special local-government arrangement. [13]
Regional administration
Regional and municipal institutions coexist with central state administration; official classifications distinguish regions from the city’s special status. [13]
Political parties and elections
Multiparty electoral institutions exist; political participation, opposition conditions and election procedures should be assessed separately from formal constitutional provisions. [8] [10]
Security forces
National armed forces, gendarmerie, police and other security institutions under the republic’s executive and legal framework.
National symbols
Blue, green and white flag with a red five-pointed star; national motto Unity, Equality, Peace. [8]

Political and constitutional status: The final certified presidential-election share is 97.01%, distinct from early media counts. The 2025 constitutional revision envisages a Senate when enabling conditions are met, but the operational parliament remains a 65-member unicameral National Assembly in the IPU record. [10] [9] [12]

International Profile

United Nations
Admitted September 20, 1977, following independence on June 27 of the same year. [17]
African regional institutions
Member of the African Union and participates in regional Horn of Africa and African trade and diplomatic structures.
World Trade Organization
Member since May 31, 1995. [19]
International financial institutions
Member of the World Bank Group and International Monetary Fund; an important recipient of infrastructure and development finance. [1] [7]
Strategic maritime position
Situated near Bab el-Mandeb, linking the Red Sea route to the Gulf of Aden; international shipping and maritime security are important foreign-policy concerns. [7] [20]
Relationship with Ethiopia
Regional commercial gateway for landlocked Ethiopia, linked by roads and the Addis Ababa–Djibouti railway; Ethiopia accounted for 83.5% of recorded 2023 goods export destinations. [7] [6]
Regional neighbors
Eritrea, Ethiopia and Somalia; relations also extend across the Gulf of Aden to Yemen and the Arabian Peninsula.
Foreign military presence
Djibouti hosts military facilities or detachments operated by several foreign partners, including France, the United States, China and Japan; arrangements and personnel levels change over time.
Maritime and climate cooperation
National and international infrastructure adaptation addresses port, railway, coastal and freshwater risks. [20]
Communications infrastructure
International submarine cables and landing facilities give Djibouti a role in regional digital connectivity. [20]
International dialing code
+253
International time standard
UTC+03:00 (East Africa Time), with no seasonal daylight-saving switch.
Country-code internet domain

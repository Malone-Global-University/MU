Country Overview

Eritrea, officially the State of Eritrea, is a country in the Horn of Africa on the Red Sea, bordered by Sudan, Ethiopia and Djibouti. Its capital is Asmara. A former Italian colony later federated with and annexed by Ethiopia, Eritrea gained control of its territory in 1991 and became formally independent on May 24, 1993 following a UN-supervised referendum. The economy includes agriculture, services, mining and maritime trade. The country is governed by President Isaias Afwerki; the constitution ratified in 1997 has not been implemented. [10] [12] [13]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
State of Eritrea; Tigrinya: ሃገረ ኤርትራ; Arabic: دولة إريتريا. [23] [15]
Common name
Eritrea.
Capital
Asmara (also written Asmera in some older English-language sources). [15]
Continent / UN M49 subregion
Africa / Eastern Africa; UN statistical code 014 for the subregion. [23]
Historical and cultural setting
The northern Horn of Africa and Red Sea littoral, with highland, lowland and island societies connected to African and Arabian trading networks.
Precolonial political history
Parts of modern Eritrea were connected with Aksum and later highland and coastal polities; no single modern founding date covers this history. [10]
Italian colony
Italian colonial rule consolidated beginning in 1890; Italian Eritrea was one of Italy’s African colonies. [10]
British administration
British military administration followed the defeat of Italian forces during World War II, beginning in 1941. [10]
Federation with Ethiopia
Eritrea became an autonomous federated unit under the Ethiopian crown in 1952, following a United Nations decision. [10]
Annexation by Ethiopia
Ethiopia terminated the federation and annexed Eritrea as a province in 1962. [10]
Independence struggle
An armed independence struggle continued over approximately three decades, with major roles for Eritrean liberation movements. [10]
Liberation of Asmara
Eritrean People’s Liberation Front (EPLF) forces took control of Asmara in May 1991; this preceded formal international independence. [10]
Independence referendum
April 23–25, 1993, under United Nations observation; voters overwhelmingly supported independence. [10]
Early international recognition
The United States recognized Eritrea on April 27, 1993, following the referendum; the official national independence date is May 24, 1993. [25] [10]
Official independence and national day
May 24, 1993, commemorated as Independence Day. [10] [19]
United Nations admission
May 28, 1993. [11]
Constitutional milestone
A constitution was ratified in May 1997, but it has not entered into practical operation; do not conflate ratification with implementation. [13] [14]
Border war with Ethiopia
1998–2000; the post-war boundary dispute and subsequent regional relations have been central to the modern state. [13]
Peace process with Ethiopia
Diplomatic rapprochement occurred in 2018, followed by later regional tensions; the current context should be assessed with dated sources. [13] [24]
ISO 3166-1 codes
ER (alpha-2); ERI (alpha-3); 232 (numeric). [23]
Internet country-code domain

Geography and Population

Geographic position
Horn of Africa; western shore of the Red Sea between Sudan and Djibouti, with Ethiopia to the south. [15]
Total area
121,144 km² in the UNData statistical profile; other international publications use slightly different area totals. [4]
Land area
120,800 km² (2024 World Bank/FAO land-area series); measurement conventions differ from UNData surface area. [5]
2025 population
3,607,003 (World Bank midyear estimate; not a direct recent census count). [2]
2024 population
3,535,603 (World Bank modeled midyear estimate), compared with 2025 growth of approximately 2.0%. [2]
Population data limitation
Census coverage is limited; external estimates differ substantially in some published references. The same estimation series is used here for comparability. [1] [14]
Population density
Approximately 29.9 persons per km², derived from 2025 World Bank population and 2024 World Bank/FAO land area; rounded, not an official density publication. [2] [5]
Capital city
Asmara, in the central highlands. [15]
Other major cities
Keren, Massawa, Assab, Mendefera and Barentu; population rankings are not asserted without comparable recent municipal estimates.
Land borders
Sudan (west and northwest), Ethiopia (south), and Djibouti (southeast). [15]
Maritime setting
Red Sea coast, including the Dahlak Archipelago; ports and islands are integral to the geographic and trade profile.
Major landforms
Eritrean Highlands, western lowlands, eastern escarpment, coastal plains and Danakil lowlands.
Highest elevation
Emba Soira, approximately 3,018 meters above sea level (commonly reported topographic value).
Principal ports
Massawa and Assab on the Red Sea.
Main climatic patterns
Cooler and more seasonal rainfall in the highlands; hotter, drier conditions along much of the Red Sea coast and lowlands.
Major environmental pressures
Rainfall variability, recurrent drought exposure, soil degradation, land-use constraints, and water-resource stress.
Agricultural landscapes
Rain-fed highland cultivation and livestock pastoralism in parts of the lowlands, subject to seasonal precipitation.
Administrative geographic regions
Six first-level regions (zobas): Anseba, Debub, Gash-Barka, Maekel, Northern Red Sea and Southern Red Sea. [7]
Time zone
East Africa Time (UTC+03:00), without regular daylight-saving changes.

Economy and International Trade

Economic model
Strongly state-directed economy with public enterprises and restricted private activity; agriculture, services, mineral extraction and trade contribute to output. [14]
Nominal GDP, recent estimate
Approximately US$2.535 billion in 2024 (UNCTAD profile; estimated, current-dollar value). [3]
Why GDP is not listed as a World Bank 2025 figure
World Bank’s latest displayed nominal GDP observation for Eritrea is from 2011; using it as a contemporary annual total would be misleading. [1]
Nominal GDP per capita, calculated
Approximately US$717 for 2024, computed from UNCTAD’s US$2.535 billion GDP and approximately 3.536 million population; not average earnings or household income. [3] [2]
Real GDP growth
Approximately 2.8% in 2024 in the UNCTAD country-profile series; data are estimates and vintages vary. [3]
National currency
Eritrean nakfa (ERN), introduced after independence; domestic currency used for official accounts. [4]
Banking and currency conditions
Public-sector banking and foreign-exchange controls play a major role; official exchange quotes may not represent all available market transactions. [14]
Merchandise exports
Approximately US$576 million in 2024 (UNCTAD country-profile estimate; goods only). [3]
Merchandise imports
Approximately US$562 million in 2024 (same UNCTAD estimate and methodology). [3]
Merchandise trade balance
About US$14 million surplus in 2024, using UNCTAD exports and imports; not a goods-plus-services balance. [3]
Alternative partner-reconciled trade series
CEPII BACI estimates US$573.9 million in 2024 merchandise exports, close to—but not identical with—the UNCTAD estimate; its import values differ more materially. [9] [3]
Principal export category
Copper and zinc ores and concentrates dominate the recorded 2024 goods export mix in CEPII BACI partner data. [9]
Zinc ore exports
Approximately US$277.5 million in 2024 in the CEPII BACI partner-based series; not a separately verified national customs declaration. [9]
Copper ore exports
Approximately US$258.0 million in 2024 in the same CEPII BACI series. [9]
Other mineral exports
Precious-metal ores and concentrates, associated gold and silver mineral production; product accounting varies across mine and customs datasets. [9] [8]
Leading export destinations
China and South Korea were the largest destinations in the 2024 CEPII BACI partner-reconciled goods series. [9]
Mining-sector products
Zinc, copper, gold and silver from operating mines; USGS estimated physical output in 2024, not the value of all exported finished metal. [8]
Nonmineral export categories
Apparel and smaller goods categories appeared in 2024 partner-reconciled customs records, far below ore exports. [9]
Agricultural activities
Sorghum, millet, cereals, horticultural crops and livestock husbandry support rural livelihoods; variable rainfall affects output.
Other economic activities
Port services, fisheries, wholesale and retail commerce, construction, small-scale industry and public administration.
Service-trade comparability
Recent 2024 UNCTAD services export totals are not supplied in the cited country profile; do not infer a combined goods-and-services export number. [3]
Statistical reliability
Limited public national-account and trade reporting makes triangulation essential. UNCTAD, BACI and World Bank series may use different sources, reference years and conventions. [3] [9] [1]

People and Society

Official-language status
No single legally designated official language. The 1997 constitution provides for equality of Eritrean languages, but has not been implemented. [15] [16] [13]
Working languages
Tigrinya, Arabic and English commonly used in government, education or public administration; calling all three constitutionally official would overstate their status. [15] [16]
Major indigenous languages
Tigrinya, Tigre, Saho, Afar, Bilen, Kunama, Nara and Beja/Hedareb, with Arabic also present in community and wider regional use. [16]
English-language use
English plays an important role in secondary and higher education and parts of official communication. [16]
Tigrinya writing system
Commonly written in the Geʽez-derived script; language use differs by location and community. [16]
Ethnolinguistic communities
Tigrinya and Tigre groups are among the largest; Saho, Afar, Kunama, Nara, Bilen, Hedareb and Rashaida communities form part of Eritrea’s national diversity. [16]
Principal religious traditions
Christianity and Islam are the two major faiths; the precise national proportions are uncertain in the absence of a recent comparable census. [15] [17]
Christian denominations
Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Christianity, Roman Catholicism, Lutheranism and other Christian communities. [17]
Muslim communities
Sunni Islam is prominent, particularly in parts of the coastal and lowland areas; local distributions vary. [17]
Government-recognized faith communities
Four officially registered religious categories: Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church, Roman Catholic Church, Evangelical Lutheran Church and Sunni Islam. [17]
Other religious practices
Other Protestant, minority-faith and nonreligious communities exist, but legal recognition and freedom to organize or worship are restricted. [17]
No current official religion census percentage
A modern, nationally representative religious census series was not confirmed; avoid assigning precise 2025 percentages to Christian, Muslim or other communities. [17] [14]
Population younger than 15
Approximately 38% in 2025 (UNFPA/UN population model). [6]
Working-age population
Approximately 58% aged 15–64 in 2025 (UNFPA model). [6]
Population age 65 and older
Approximately 4% in 2025 (UNFPA model). [6]
Total fertility rate
Approximately 3.6 births per woman (2025 UNFPA modeled estimate). [6]
Life expectancy at birth
Approximately 69 years in 2024 (World Bank series); male–female values vary. [1]
Adult literacy
UN country annual results reporting gives an estimated adult literacy rate of about 76.6%; assessment and reference year differ from census-based education measures. [7]
Religious and linguistic coexistence
Christian and Muslim communities and multiple indigenous languages form part of national social life, with regional variation. [15] [16]
Cultural heritage
Asmara’s modernist architectural heritage is internationally recognized; oral literature, poetry, music, food traditions and highland-lowland identities reflect a diverse social history. [18]
Population statistics caution
Most contemporary demographic data are modeled rather than direct results from a recent comprehensive national census. [6] [1]

Language and religion caveat: Tigrinya, Arabic and English are important working languages, but no single language is designated as constitutionally official in the operative legal order. Government recognition of four religious groups is not a population survey and cannot be converted into contemporary religious percentages. [15] [16] [17]

Government and Institutions

Government system
Authoritarian one-party presidential system in practice; formal constitutional arrangements are not fully implemented. [13] [14]
Head of state
President Isaias Afwerki, serving since independence in 1993; confirmed as incumbent in the 2026 diplomatic directory. [12]
Head of government
President Isaias Afwerki simultaneously performs head-of-government functions; there is no separately serving prime minister. [12] [13]
Foreign minister
Osman Saleh Mohammed (Osman Saleh), as listed in current diplomatic directories. [12]
Governing political party
People’s Front for Democracy and Justice (PFDJ), the only legally permitted political party. [14]
Predecessor liberation movement
Eritrean People’s Liberation Front (EPLF), transformed into the PFDJ after independence. [10] [14]
Independence-era presidency
Isaias Afwerki became president in 1993 following the independence transition. [12] [13]
Foundational constitutional text
A constitution was ratified on May 23, 1997, but has never been implemented. [13] [14]
Written constitutional framework versus practice
Rights, powers and representative institutions set out in the unimplemented text should not be presented as functioning institutions. [14] [13]
National presidential elections
No national presidential election has taken place since independence; the presidency has not been renewed by a popular national vote. [13] [14]
National legislative elections
No national parliamentary elections since independence. [13] [14]
National Assembly status
An assembly existed during the post-independence transition but has not convened for many years; a normally sitting elected national legislature is not functioning. [14]
Political opposition
Independent political party competition and assembly are severely restricted. [14] [13]
Executive decision-making
Authority is concentrated in the president, governing-party leadership and associated military and administrative structures. [14]
Judicial system
State courts and administrative institutions exist; independent reports describe limited judicial independence and weak procedural safeguards. [13] [14]
Administrative divisions
Six zobas (regions): Anseba, Debub, Gash-Barka, Maekel (Central), Northern Red Sea and Southern Red Sea. [7]
Regional and local administration
Zobas are subdivided into local administrative areas; regional governance operates under centrally appointed or controlled officials. [14]
National service
Compulsory national service is established in law; UNHCR reports that service frequently extends beyond the nominal 18-month framework in practice. [13]
Civil liberties
UNHCR and independent monitoring report severe restrictions on speech, assembly, detention safeguards and mobility. [13] [14]
Religious freedom regulation
Only four religious categories are registered; the USCIRF 2026 report documents continuing constraints and persecution affecting other faith communities and members of recognized groups. [17]
2026 political status
No verified general election, operative constitution or new head of state had superseded this structure by the editorial cutoff. [12] [14] [13]

Constitution and elections: Eritrea’s 1997 constitution is ratified but unimplemented. An inactive assembly and the lack of national elections are documented separately from the institutions the constitutional text envisioned; the page does not imply these institutions function normally. [13] [14]

International Profile

United Nations
Member since May 28, 1993 (General Assembly Resolution 47/230). [11]
African Union
Member of the African Union; joined the Organization of African Unity on May 24, 1993. [19]
Intergovernmental Authority on Development
Not a current member: IGAD acknowledged Eritrea’s withdrawal on December 12, 2025, after its 2023 return and subsequent nonparticipation. [20]
African Continental Free Trade Area
As of September 2026, Eritrea remained the only African Union member state not to have signed the AfCFTA agreement; this is distinct from ratification status. [21]
International Monetary Fund
Member since July 6, 1994; the IMF country page identifies 2019 as the latest Article IV Executive Board consultation. [22]
World Bank Group
Member of the World Bank Group since 1994; availability of modern annual GDP figures remains limited. [1]
Key bilateral relationships
Regional diplomacy, security and trade are especially shaped by relations with Ethiopia, Sudan, Djibouti and countries across the Red Sea. [13]
Red Sea strategic position
Ports at Massawa and Assab place the country along major Red Sea maritime routes and near the Bab el-Mandeb approach.
Relations with Ethiopia
The 1998–2000 border war, 2018 rapprochement and later security tensions all remain important context; reporting about active forces and control can be contested. [13] [24]
October 2026 regional security concern
The UN Secretary-General expressed concern about renewed northern Ethiopia fighting and reports of Eritrean forces; the statement was a concern about reports, not independent verification of specific troop positions. [24]
UNESCO World Heritage
One inscribed property: Asmara: A Modernist African City, listed in 2017. [18]
UNESCO Tentative List
The Qoahito Cultural Landscape appears on the country’s tentative list; tentative status is not a World Heritage inscription. [18]

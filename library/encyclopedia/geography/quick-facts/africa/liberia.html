Country Overview

Liberia, officially the Republic of Liberia, is a coastal country in West Africa bordered by Sierra Leone, Guinea, Côte d’Ivoire and the Atlantic Ocean. Its capital and largest metropolitan center is Monrovia. Indigenous societies preceded coastal settlements established in the nineteenth century through the American Colonization Society; the modern republic declared independence on July 26, 1847. Liberia’s current Constitution dates to 1986. [9] [15] [6]

Liberia is a presidential republic led by President Joseph Nyuma Boakai and Vice President Jeremiah Kpan Koung. Its economy depends on mining, agriculture, trade and services, with gold, iron ore, rubber and palm oil prominent among exported commodities. The country continues long-term development and institutional reconstruction after the civil wars of the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries. [7] [2] [10]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Liberia. [6]
Common name
Liberia.

National capital
Monrovia, on the Atlantic coast. [14] [5]

Monrovia and its surrounding metropolitan settlements. [9]

Africa; Western Africa. [5]
State type
Independent, unitary sovereign republic. [6]
Before the modern republic
The territory was home to numerous indigenous African peoples and societies before nineteenth-century Atlantic colonization. [15] [14]
American Colonization Society
Organized in 1816 in the United States; sponsored settlement of free and formerly enslaved Black Americans on the West African coast. [15]
Nineteenth-century coastal settlements
Settlements associated with the American Colonization Society began in the early 1820s; Americo-Liberian communities interacted and often conflicted with indigenous polities. [15]
Independence
July 26, 1847: independence declared by the Republic of Liberia. [15]
Independence Day
July 26, commemorating the 1847 declaration. [15]
First president
Joseph Jenkins Roberts; elected after independence and inaugurated in 1848. [15]
Founding vs. constitutional dates
1847 independence and original constitutional order; January 6, 1986, date of the current Constitution following the 1980 coup and subsequent political transition. [15] [6]
Long one-party era
The True Whig Party dominated national politics from the late nineteenth century until the 1980 coup; political power was concentrated among Americo-Liberian elites. [15] [18]
1980 political rupture
Military coup in April 1980 overthrew the government and suspended the 1847 Constitution. [6]
Civil-conflict era
Two interrelated civil wars affected Liberia between 1989 and 2003, generating immense human and economic losses. [18]
Postwar electoral transition
National elections in 2005 led to a new civilian government in January 2006; later peaceful transfers of presidential power followed. [18]
National flag
Eleven alternating red and white horizontal stripes, with one white star in a blue canton; the stripes represent the eleven independence signatories. [21]
National anthem
“All Hail, Liberia, Hail!” [18]

Historical interpretation: Indigenous Liberian societies existed before nineteenth-century settlement. Coastal colonization, the 1847 declaration of independence, and the Constitution of 1986 are distinct events; none should be conflated with the origin of the peoples of Liberia. [15] [6]

People and Society

National diversity
A multiethnic and multilingual society including many indigenous communities and descendants of settlers from the African diaspora. [3] [15]
Largest recorded ethnic group, 2022
Kpelle, 20.2% in LISGIS final census summary. [3]
Second-largest recorded ethnic group, 2022
Bassa, 13.6% in LISGIS final census summary. [3]
Other significant ethnolinguistic communities
Grebo, Gio/Dan, Mano, Kru, Loma/Lorma, Gola, Vai, Kissi, Mandingo, Gbandi and others. [3] [14]
Americo-Liberian history
Communities descended from formerly enslaved and free Black settlers from the United States and Caribbean played a defining role in nineteenth- and twentieth-century state institutions. [15]
Official language
English. [14] [9]
Everyday English varieties
Liberian English and Liberian Kreyol varieties are widely used alongside indigenous languages. [14] [9]
Indigenous languages
More than twenty are spoken, including Kpelle, Bassa, Vai, Grebo, Kru, Mandingo and other languages. [14] [9]
Vai literacy tradition
The Vai language has an indigenous syllabary developed in the nineteenth century; its continued use is an important African writing tradition.
Language distinction
English is the sole nationally designated official language; widespread indigenous languages are not thereby made legally official. [14] [9]
Main religion
Christianity is the majority religion under the 2022 population census. [3] [19]
Christian, 2022 census
84.9% of population. [3]
Muslim, 2022 census
12.0% of population. [3]
Traditional African religions, 2022 census
0.5% of population reported as primary affiliation. [3]
Other religions, 2022 census
0.1% of population reported in the other-religion category. [3]
No religion, 2022 census
2.6% of population. [3]
Religion reporting caveat
Census affiliations cannot fully represent overlapping indigenous practices or the diversity within Christianity and Islam. [3] [19]
Christian denominations
Includes Methodist, Baptist, Roman Catholic, Pentecostal and many other Christian traditions. [19]
Muslim traditions
Predominantly Sunni communities, with smaller groups and varied religious practices. [19]
Traditional institutions
Poro, Sande and other local community institutions remain culturally important in some regions. [19]
Life expectancy, 2024
Approximately 62 years at birth (World Bank). [1]
Education
Primary, secondary, technical and higher education coexist with inequalities in access and completion. [2]
Higher-education institutions
University of Liberia, Cuttington University and other institutions contribute to university and professional training.
Health priorities
Maternal and child health, infectious disease prevention, workforce capacity and access to care remain important concerns. [2]
Urbanization
Substantial urban concentration around greater Monrovia; 2022 urban share 54.5%. [3]
Cuisine
Rice, cassava, greens, stews, palm-butter dishes and fish are prominent foods, with regional variation.
Music and arts
Traditional drumming, dance, storytelling and contemporary Liberian popular music reflect multiple communities.
National holiday
Independence Day, July 26. [15]
Sports
Football is widely followed; basketball, athletics and other sports are also played.
Historical national challenge
Long-term reconstruction after civil wars, public-health emergencies and economic shocks remains significant. [2]

Religion and ethnicity methodology: Religious percentages and recorded ethnic shares are derived from the 2022 census, not annual 2025 estimates. Religion figures are self-reported affiliations; overlapping practices and differences in community-based estimates require caution. [3] [19]

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Unitary presidential constitutional republic. [6]
Head of state
President Joseph Nyuma Boakai, Sr., inaugurated January 22, 2024. [7] [8]
Head of government
President Joseph Nyuma Boakai, Sr.; the President is both head of state and head of government. [6] [7]
Vice president
Jeremiah Kpan Koung. [7] [8]
Constitution in force
Constitution of the Republic of Liberia dated January 6, 1986. [6]
Sovereign structure
Unitary state, not a federation; divided administratively into counties. [6]
Government branches
Executive, legislative and judicial branches under constitutional separation of powers. [6]
President’s term
Six years; constitutional maximum of two presidential terms. [6]
Most recent presidential election
2023 presidential election and November 14 runoff, followed by the January 2024 inauguration. [20] [8]
Next regular presidential election
Scheduled on the ordinary six-year cycle for 2029, unless law or constitutional events alter timing. [6] [20]
National legislature
Bicameral Legislature: House of Representatives and Senate. [6]
House of Representatives
73 elected seats in the 2023 electoral apportionment; membership can change through vacancies or future legislation. [20]
Representatives’ terms
Six years, subject to constitutional rules and by-elections. [6]
Senate
30 senators, with two elected from each of the 15 counties. [6] [9]
Senate term
Nine years under the Constitution. [6]
Senate presiding role
Vice President is President of the Senate, with a Senate president pro tempore presiding in relevant circumstances. [6] [8]
Legislative elections
Electors vote in county and constituency contests under Liberian electoral law. [6] [20]
Supreme judicial body
Supreme Court of Liberia. [6]
Other courts
Circuit courts, magistrates’ courts and other courts provided by law. [6]
Legal traditions
Constitutional law and statutory/common-law institutions coexist with customary-law practices in legally relevant areas. [6]
County government
County and sub-county administrative institutions support implementation of national government policy. [6] [9]
Constitutional rights
Includes expression, assembly, due process, equality and other guarantees, subject to constitutional provisions. [6]
Religious status of the state
Constitutional freedom of religion; Liberia is not classified here as a religious state. [6] [19]
Public administration
Cabinet ministries, executive agencies and county-based public administration. [8] [9]
Current policy direction
Boakai administration’s development priorities include agriculture, roads, rule of law, education, sanitation and tourism (ARREST agenda). [8]
Contemporary governance concerns
Fiscal resilience, governance quality, education, infrastructure and post-conflict institutional strengthening. [2]

Constitutional classification: Liberia is a presidential republic. Unlike parliamentary systems, the President is both head of state and head of government. Senate seats are apportioned two per county; the House seat count is tied to the referenced 2023 electoral apportionment. [6] [20]

Country Overview

The Union of the Comoros is an island republic in the Indian Ocean, between southeastern Africa and Madagascar. Moroni is its capital. The state administers Ngazidja (Grande Comore), Ndzuwani (Anjouan), and Mwali (Mohéli), and claims Mayotte, which remains under French administration. Independence was declared on July 6, 1975; the 2018 Constitution defines the present union's core institutions. Its economy combines agriculture, small-scale trade, services, diaspora remittances and specialized exports such as cloves, vanilla and ylang-ylang oil. [4] [5] [17] [19]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Union of the Comoros; Union des Comores; الاتحاد القمري. [4]
Common names
Comoros; the Comoro Islands; Comores in French; Ngazidja and related native-language designations vary by island.
Capital
Moroni, on Ngazidja (Grande Comore). [4] [2]
Continent / UN M49 region
Africa / Eastern Africa; an island state in the western Indian Ocean and northern Mozambique Channel. [18] [2]
Early settlement and regional history
The islands developed through migration and maritime exchange linking East Africa, Madagascar, the Arabian Peninsula and the wider Indian Ocean; historic sultanates emerged in several islands. [10]
Historic political systems
Islamic sultanates and Swahili-influenced urban centers predate modern colonial borders; the current republic has no single precolonial founding date. [10]
French colonial rule
France gradually established colonial authority across the islands in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries; institutions and island treatment evolved over time. [5]
Self-government before independence
The Comoros gained increasing territorial autonomy during the late French colonial period; this was not equivalent to sovereign independence. [5]
Independence referendum
December 22, 1974, archipelago-wide referendum in which an overall majority supported independence; support varied sharply by island, particularly on Mayotte. [5] [17]
Independence proclaimed
July 6, 1975, from France, with independence proclaimed by representatives of the islands that became the modern Comorian state. [5]
Mayotte territorial issue
Mayotte remained under French administration following the independence process. Comoros claims the fourth main island; the present republic administers Ngazidja, Ndzuwani and Mwali. [4] [17]
United Nations admission
November 12, 1975. [6]
Federal Islamic Republic era
Political institutions were reorganized after independence; the Federal Islamic Republic of the Comoros was the state name from 1978 until the 2001 union settlement. [4]
Union constitutional settlement
The Union of the Comoros was established under the December 2001 constitutional framework, following island-level separatist crises and negotiations. [4]
Current constitutional framework
A new constitutional text was approved in a July 2018 referendum; the 2018 text remains the principal baseline for this profile. [4]
2024 presidential election
Azali Assoumani was returned to the presidency in the January 2024 vote; opposition parties disputed aspects of the election and its administration. [15] [16]
2025 parliamentary election
National Assembly elections were held in January 2025; the ruling party won 31 of 33 seats according to the African Commission’s report. [14]
National day
Independence Day, July 6. [5]
National motto
Unité – Solidarité – Développement (Unity – Solidarity – Development). [4]
National anthem
Umodja Wa Massiwa (Unity of the Great Islands). [4]
ISO 3166-1 codes
KM (alpha-2); COM (alpha-3); 174 (numeric). [18]
Internet country-code domain
.km

Geography and Population

Geographic position
Volcanic archipelago off southeastern Africa, between northern Madagascar and the Mozambique coast. [2]
Main islands administered
Ngazidja (Grande Comore), Ndzuwani (Anjouan) and Mwali (Mohéli); each has an autonomous island administration under the Union. [4]
Island claimed but not administered
Maore (Mayotte), administered by France and claimed by the Comorian state; the territorial question should not be mistaken for current administrative control. [4] [17]
Area: administered three-island territory
Approximately 1,861 km² (718 sq. mi.), the land-area convention used in UNCTAD’s 2024 maritime profile. [3]
Area: four-island claim / UN table
2,235 km² (863 sq. mi.) in UNdata’s territorial surface-area entry, reflecting a different territorial convention; this number must not be presented as the area of the administered three islands. [2] [17]
Resident population
882,847 (World Bank, 2025 population estimate; not a census count). [1]
UN population estimate
Approximately 883,000 (UNdata, 2025), rounded to thousands. [2]
Most recent cited census
758,316 in the December 15, 2017 census, as tabulated in the UN Demographic Yearbook; historical enumeration. [12]
Population growth
About 1.9% (World Bank estimate for 2025). [1]
Estimated density
Approximately 474 residents per km² of the three administered islands, calculated as 882,847 divided by 1,861 km²; not an official independent estimate. [1] [3]
Principal urban center
Moroni; Mutsamudu and Fomboni are important island towns. [2] [4]
Island geography
Ngazidja has young volcanic terrain; Ndzuwani is more deeply dissected by valleys; Mwali combines uplands, coasts and protected marine environments. [19]
Highest peak
Mount Karthala, an active volcano on Ngazidja, approximately 2,361 m above sea level. [19]
Geological setting
Volcanic island chain shaped by eruptions, steep uplands, coastal erosion and reefs. [19]
Coastal environment
Indian Ocean reefs, beaches, coastal lagoons and fisheries; marine habitats are vulnerable to warming, pollution and overexploitation. [19]
Rivers and water
Short, steep catchments and limited freshwater storage; seasonal rainfall, springs and groundwater are important, with island-level differences. [19]
Climate
Tropical maritime climate with humid rainy and relatively drier seasons, influenced by monsoon winds. [19]
Natural hazards
Tropical cyclones, intense rain, coastal flooding, landslides, drought and volcanic hazards on Ngazidja. [19] [17]
Land borders
None; all international connections are maritime or air connections, while Mayotte is separated by sea.
Time zone
East Africa Time (EAT), UTC+03:00; no regular daylight-saving time change.

Economy and International Trade

Economic classification
Small, import-dependent island economy with agriculture, fishing, trade, construction, public services and a significant overseas diaspora. [19]
Nominal GDP
Approximately US$1.81 billion (2025; World Bank, current U.S. dollars; rounded from the online series). [1]
GDP per capita
Approximately US$2,055.8 (2025; World Bank, current dollars). This is national output per person, not average wages or median income. [1]
Real GDP growth
3.8% in 2025 (World Bank annual series; subject to revision). [1]
Consumer price inflation
3.2% in 2025 (World Bank annual CPI inflation series). [1]
Currency
Comorian franc, ISO 4217 code KMF (symbol commonly FC); not the Central African CFA franc XAF or West African CFA franc XOF. [2] [11]
Exchange-rate arrangement
Conventional peg to the euro; approximately KMF 491.97 per €1 under the IMF-reported arrangement. [11]
Main economic sectors
Smallholder farming, fishing, retail and wholesale trade, transportation, construction, public administration and remittance-supported consumption. [19]
Agricultural products
Cloves, vanilla, ylang-ylang, coconuts, bananas, cassava and other crops; production patterns vary by island and season. [19] [3]
Role of remittances
Transfers from Comorians working abroad are an important source of household and foreign-exchange income; World Bank series report personal remittances around 20.8% of GDP in 2023. [1]
Merchandise exports
Approximately US$30 million in 2024 (UNCTAD maritime profile; goods only; not combined goods and services). [3]
Merchandise imports
Approximately US$358 million in 2024 (UNCTAD; goods only). [3]
Merchandise trade balance
Approximately US$328 million deficit for 2024 (exports minus imports; goods only). [3]
Primary merchandise exports
Cloves, aromatic/essential oils including ylang-ylang, and vanilla; values fluctuate with prices and harvest conditions. [3] [19]
Key imported goods
Food staples, refined fuels, vehicles, machinery, medicines and other manufactured consumer and capital goods. [19]
Export partners
Partner-reported 2024 merchandise-data ranking lists Tanzania, India, France, the United States and the United Kingdom; ranking depends on reporting basis and may change by year. [20]
Services sector
Trade, transport, tourism and government activity contribute to domestic services; the monetary figures for goods above must not be interpreted as services trade. [19]
Tourism
Nature, beaches, marine habitats, volcanic landscapes and island heritage offer tourism potential, constrained by connectivity and infrastructure. [19] [9]
Development challenges
Limited market size, dependence on imported staples and fuel, energy and transport costs, climate vulnerability and exposure to external financing and remittance conditions. [19]

People and Society

Official languages
Shikomor (Comorian), French and Arabic under Article 9 of the 2018 Constitution. [4]
National language
Shikomor, a Bantu language related to the Swahili language continuum, with island-linked speech varieties. [4]
Local language varieties
Shingazidja on Ngazidja, Shindzuani on Ndzuwani, Shimwali on Mwali; Shimaore is associated with Mayotte, which is French-administered. [4] [17]
French usage
Used in parts of administration, education, law, media and external relations, alongside Comorian and Arabic. [4]
Arabic usage
Used especially in Islamic education and worship, and in some formal or cultural contexts; fluency and everyday use differ from Shikomor. [4] [7]
State religion
Islam, with Sunni and Shafi‘i principles specified in Article 97 of the 2018 Constitution. [4]
Religious composition
Approximately 98% Sunni Muslim in commonly cited estimates; this is not a new 2026 census result. Smaller non-Sunni Muslim, Christian and other communities are present. [7]
Religious diversity
Small Shia Muslim and Christian communities and other religious minorities exist, with legal and social restrictions on public non-Sunni observance described in 2023 U.S. State Department reporting. [7]
Religious freedom framework
The constitution recognizes equality while prescribing Sunni Islam as the state religion; constitutional text and practical restrictions need to be distinguished. [4] [7]
Population age profile
About 36.9% under age 15 and 6.7% aged 60 or older in 2025 UNdata series; not derived from a 2025 census. [2]
Fertility rate
Approximately 3.8 births per woman (2025 UN demographic estimate). [2]
Life expectancy
Approximately 67 years at birth (World Bank 2024 series; rounded), with a female–male gap. [1] [2]
Urbanization
UNdata reports approximately 29.2% urban in its 2020 series; later values and settlement definitions may differ. [2]
Diaspora
Large overseas communities, particularly in France and elsewhere around the Indian Ocean, help connect families through migration and remittances. [19]
Culture
Comorian traditions combine East African, Malagasy, Islamic, Indian Ocean and French-influenced elements, varying substantially by island. [10]
Social practices
Marriage ceremonies, music, family networks, Islamic celebrations and local age-grade institutions play important roles in community life. [10]
Historic built heritage
Swahili-influenced medinas, palaces, public squares, mosques and stone architecture reflect sultanate history. [10]

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Republican union with a powerful directly elected presidency and autonomous island governments; constitutional arrangements should be distinguished from the quality of electoral competition. [4] [15]
President
Azali Assoumani, in office since May 2016 and returned in the January 2024 presidential election (status reviewed November 5, 2026). [13] [15]
Head of state
The president of the Union of the Comoros. [4] [13]
Head of government
The presidency leads the national executive; the UN protocol list identifies no prime minister in its current government entry. [13] [4]
Constitutional text
2018 Constitution of the Union of the Comoros, following the 2001 union settlement; this profile reports the 2018 constitutional baseline. [4]
Presidential election cycle
Five-year presidential term under the 2018 framework, with the constitutional rotation and reelection rules reorganized by that reform. [4]
Executive cabinet
The president and Council of Ministers exercise executive authority under the constitution. [4]
National legislature
Unicameral Assembly of the Union (Assemblée de l’Union). [4] [14]
Assembly seats
33 elected members for the legislature chosen in the 2025 parliamentary elections. [14]
2025 ruling-party result
The Convention for the Renewal of the Comoros won 31 of 33 seats in the 2025 election according to the African Commission; some opposition parties boycotted the vote. [14] [15]
Judiciary
Supreme Court and lower judicial authorities; the 2018 Constitution assigns the Supreme Court judicial, administrative and constitutional responsibilities. [4]
Constituent island governments
Ngazidja, Ndzuwani and Mwali have distinct local governmental institutions, including elected island governors under the constitutional framework. [4]
Municipal institutions
Local communes and municipal councils provide another level of government; their powers are prescribed by law and island arrangements. [4] [14]
Territorial governance limitation
Mayotte is constitutionally claimed by Comoros but is not represented as an island governed by the Union in this administrative profile. [17] [4]
Electoral context
The 2024 presidential vote and 2025 legislative elections were criticized by sections of the opposition; official electoral results and these criticisms should not be conflated. [15] [16]
Defense and security
National defense and internal security forces operate under national authorities; maritime surveillance and coastal security are important to the island state. [4]

International Profile

United Nations
Member since November 12, 1975. [6]
African Union
Member of the African Union; geographically part of the Eastern Africa statistical region. [2] [18]
World Trade Organization
Became the 165th WTO member on August 21, 2024. [8]
Other international organizations
Participates in the Arab League, Organisation internationale de la Francophonie, Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, Indian Ocean Commission, IMF and World Bank. [4] [19]
African continental trade cooperation
Participates in African regional economic and trade initiatives, with the WTO accession marking an additional integration milestone. [8] [19]
Mayotte dispute
Comoros claims Mayotte as part of the archipelago, while France continues to administer it; this is a longstanding contested sovereignty issue, not a current Comorian administrative district. [4] [17]
Principal maritime relationships
Regional connections with Madagascar, Tanzania, Mozambique, Mauritius and other Indian Ocean countries. [19]
Trade and travel connectivity
International trade depends on ports and aviation; maritime geography and shipping costs strongly affect food, fuel and manufactured-goods supply. [19] [3]
2026 UNESCO World Heritage inscription
The Medinas of the Historic Sultanates of the Comoros was inscribed in 2026; it includes six historic urban sites on Ngazidja and Ndzuwani. [9] [10]
World Heritage Convention
Ratified September 27, 2000. [9]

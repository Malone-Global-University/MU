Country Overview

The Republic of the Congo (also called Congo-Brazzaville) is a sovereign state in Central Africa, bordering the Atlantic Ocean and the Congo River basin. Its capital is Brazzaville; Pointe-Noire is its major seaport and commercial center. The country gained independence from France on August 15, 1960. It is distinct from the Democratic Republic of the Congo, whose capital is Kinshasa. [2] [16] [17]

Its political system operates under the 2015 constitution, as amended in 2022. Denis Sassou-Nguesso was re-elected president in March 2026, with Anatole Collinet Makosso serving as prime minister. Oil dominates merchandise exports, while broad rainforest and peatland ecosystems make conservation central to the country's international profile. [5] [9] [7] [11] [19]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of the Congo (French: République du Congo). [5]
Common alternative name
Congo-Brazzaville, used to distinguish the country from the Democratic Republic of the Congo (Congo-Kinshasa). [2]
Demonym
Congolese; context is needed because the name is also used for citizens of the neighboring Democratic Republic of the Congo.
Capital
Brazzaville, on the Congo River. [5]
Principal commercial and port city
Pointe-Noire on the Atlantic coast. [17]
Continent
Africa.
United Nations geographic subregion
Middle Africa (Central Africa). [2]
Geographic identity
A Central African country with an Atlantic coastline and an extensive interior of rainforest, river basins and savanna. [19]
Ancient and precolonial history
The region was inhabited by diverse communities, including ancestors of Kongo, Teke, Loango and forest-dwelling societies. [15]
Historical political formations
Kingdom of Kongo, Teke polity and Loango Kingdom influenced parts of the area before colonial conquest. [15]
French colonial rule
Became part of French colonial Central Africa and French Equatorial Africa during the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. [16]
Brazzaville in World War II
Brazzaville was a major administrative and symbolic center for Free French forces in Equatorial Africa. [16]
Autonomous republic
November 28, 1958, within the French Community; a separate milestone from sovereign independence.
Independence
August 15, 1960, from France. [15] [16]
National Day
August 15, marking independence. [17]
First president
Fulbert Youlou, who led the country at independence. [15]
Early political turning point
August 1963 political crisis led to the fall of President Youlou and major institutional change.
People’s Republic period
The People’s Republic of the Congo was established in 1969 and lasted until 1991; it followed a Marxist-Leninist political orientation. [15]
Return to multiparty politics
National Conference of 1991 and subsequent constitutional/electoral changes opened a multiparty transition. [15]
1992 election
Pascal Lissouba won the presidential election and became president of a multiparty republic.
1997 civil war
Conflict ended in the return to power of Denis Sassou-Nguesso, who had previously governed from 1979 to 1992.
Current constitution
Adopted by referendum on October 25, 2015, subsequently amended in 2022. [5]
Current presidential tenure
Denis Sassou-Nguesso re-elected in March 2026 after earlier post-1997 terms. [9] [10]
Flag
Green and red triangles separated by a diagonal yellow band. [5]
National motto
Unité – Travail – Progrès (Unity – Work – Progress). [5]
National anthem
La Congolaise. [5]
Official language
French. [5]
National vehicular languages
Lingala and Kituba, expressly recognized by the constitution. [5]
Country identification caution
Congo-Brazzaville (CG/COG) is not the Democratic Republic of the Congo (CD/COD). World Bank and WTO series used here are for CG. [1] [2] [11]

Historical milestones: the 1958 autonomous republic, sovereign independence in 1960, the 1969 People’s Republic, the 1991 multiparty transition and the current 2015 constitution refer to separate political eras. [15] [16] [5]

342,000 square kilometers, inclusive of inland-water area as reported by UNData. [2]
Land area

Continent and subregion
Africa; Middle Africa in United Nations classifications. [2]

West-central Africa, reaching the Atlantic Ocean near Pointe-Noire and the lower Congo River. [2] [19]

Gabon, Cameroon, Central African Republic, Democratic Republic of the Congo and Angola (Cabinda exclave).

2.4% annual growth, World Bank. [1]

19.0 residents per km² of total surface area (derived from 2025 World Bank population and UN total area). [1] [2]
Urban concentration
Brazzaville and Pointe-Noire account for a large share of national residents and economic activity. [4] [19]
Largest river
Congo River, forming much of the eastern boundary with the Democratic Republic of the Congo.
Other principal waterways
Ubangi, Sangha, Likouala and Kouilou-Niari river systems; names vary by reach and local convention.
Major landscape zones
Atlantic coastal plain, Mayombe hills, Niari basin, Batéké Plateau and northern Congo Basin lowlands.
Climate
Predominantly tropical; humid equatorial conditions in northern forests and varying wet/dry seasons farther south.
Vegetation
Extensive tropical rainforest, secondary forest, wetlands, peatlands, wooded savannas and coastal ecosystems. [19]
Forest cover, 2024
Approximately 64.0% of land area in the World Bank series. [1]
Tropical peatlands
Northern Congo Basin peat ecosystems are important carbon stores and sensitive environments. [19]
Key protected landscapes
Odzala-Kokoua National Park, Nouabalé-Ndoki National Park and Conkouati-Douli National Park. [14]
Odzala-Kokoua World Heritage
Forest Massif of Odzala-Kokoua, inscribed by UNESCO in 2023. [14]
Sangha World Heritage
Sangha Trinational, a cross-border natural property inscribed in 2012. [14]
Environmental pressures
Forest conversion, resource extraction, illegal wildlife exploitation and potential damage to peatlands; scale varies by region. [19] [14]
Natural hazards
Seasonal river flooding and localized erosion are relevant; extreme weather exposure varies by region.
Geographic measurement caution
Total surface area (342,000 km²) is not identical to World Bank land-only area (341,500 km²). [2] [3]

Measurement caution: the official 2023 national census and the World Bank 2025 population estimate are distinct datasets. Surface area includes water, whereas land area excludes it. [4] [1] [2] [3]

Economy, Industry and International Trade

Economic system
Market-oriented economy with a large hydrocarbons sector and significant state involvement. [19] [12]
Nominal gross domestic product, 2025
US$16.31 billion in current US dollars, World Bank series. [1]
Nominal GDP per capita, 2025
US$2,514.7, current US dollars, World Bank series. [1]
Real GDP growth, 2025
3.1% in the World Bank’s current annual series. [1]
Consumer price inflation, 2025
2.4% annual CPI change in the World Bank series. [1]
ILO-modeled unemployment, 2025
19.9% of the labor force; modeled estimate, not a complete local unemployment census. [1]
Currency
Central African CFA franc (XAF), issued in the BEAC monetary area. [2] [17]
Currency union
Central African Economic and Monetary Community (CEMAC), sharing the XAF currency with other member states. [17]
Exchange-rate relationship
The XAF is pegged to the euro under the regional monetary arrangement; 1 EUR = 655.957 XAF (fixed parity). [17]
Petroleum sector
Crude petroleum is the dominant export category, creating vulnerability to oil-price and output fluctuations. [11] [19]
Petroleum production geography
Offshore Atlantic production near the Pointe-Noire / Kouilou region is a major source of oil revenue.
Natural gas
Associated gas and new gas infrastructure are part of hydrocarbon and power sector development. [12]
Other natural resources
Forestry resources, iron ore and potash deposits; commercial utilization varies widely by project. [19]
Timber industry
Tropical hardwood logs and sawn timber contribute to non-oil merchandise trade. [11]
Agriculture
Cassava, plantains, maize, groundnuts and horticulture support food production and livelihoods.
Forest-sector importance
Forest-dependent communities and environmental services contribute to the economy beyond measured wood exports. [19]
Transport and logistics hub
Pointe-Noire port and associated railway/road systems connect maritime trade to Central African markets.
Principal service industries
Wholesale and retail trade, transport, communications, public administration and financial services.
Industrial diversification challenge
Policy aims to expand agriculture, processing, tourism and non-oil industry. [12] [19]
Fiscal vulnerability
Public finances face high debt, arrears, oil revenue exposure and liquidity risks. [12]
Public debt, end-2025 estimate
97.2% of GDP, IMF March 2026 assessment; methodology and vintage differ from GDP observation rows. [12]

2023 census: 6,142,180 people; 2025 World Bank estimate: 6,484,437 people. [4] [1]
Demographic growth
2.4% estimated annual population growth in 2025, World Bank. [1]

Approximately 70% of census residents were classified urban. [4]
Most populous cities
Brazzaville and Pointe-Noire, with 2023 census counts published separately. [4]
Life expectancy at birth, 2024
Approximately 66 years, World Bank country-data indicator. [1]
Official language
French; constitutionally designated for government and formal administration. [5]
National vehicular languages
Lingala and Kituba are expressly named in the constitution. [5]
Other widely spoken languages
Kikongo-related varieties, Teke languages, Mbochi and other regional and community languages. [17]

Major cultural groups
Kongo-related communities, Teke, Mbochi and many other groups; no exhaustive ethnicity percentages are asserted.
Religious landscape
Christian denominations predominate; Muslim and Indigenous religious traditions are also represented. [13]
Religious demographic reference year
The U.S. State Department cites different 2012 census and survey distributions rather than a 2025 religion census. [13]
2012 census religious composition
U.S. reporting quotes a 2012 census estimate for native-born people of approximately 55% Catholic, 32% Protestant and 2% Muslim. [13]
Alternative 2012 religious survey
A separate ministry survey quoted by the same report places Protestant and Catholic shares at approximately 55% and 32%, respectively. [13]
Religious data caveat
Different 2012 source classifications conflict; this entry does not arbitrarily designate a single official current national distribution. [13]
Indigenous religious practice
Traditional beliefs and local cosmologies coexist with Christianity, Islam and other affiliations. [13]
Secular state
The constitution declares the Republic secular. [5]

HDI global rank, 2023 data
138th of 193 countries in the UNDP 2025 report. [18]

53.0% of the population in the World Bank data series. [1]
Urban-rural service divide
World Bank analysis highlights weaker electricity, water and learning outcomes outside large cities. [19]
Public-health priorities
Malaria control, maternal and child health, primary care, sanitation and access to essential services.
Education
Public, private and community education at primary, secondary, technical and tertiary levels.
Higher education
Marien Ngouabi University in Brazzaville is a major national public university.
Principal economic household challenges
Unemployment, poverty, urban service pressures and exposure to oil-driven economic cycles. [19] [12]
Literature and music
French- and Congolese-language literature, visual arts and Congolese rumba are prominent cultural expressions.
Common foods
Cassava, plantains, fish, rice and stews feature in diverse local cuisines.
National holidays
Independence Day on August 15, with additional civic, religious and commemorative observances. [17]
National heritage

Media and communications
Broadcast and digital media operate alongside an uneven distribution of connectivity and infrastructure.

Government and Institutions

Political form
Unitary republic with a president and a prime minister, under the 2015 constitution as amended. [5]
Constitutional territorial character
Sovereign, unitary, indivisible, decentralized and secular state in the constitutional text. [5]
Head of state, November 2026
President Denis Sassou-Nguesso. [8] [9]
Head of government, November 2026
Prime Minister Anatole Collinet Makosso. [7] [8]
Latest presidential election
Military/security personnel voted March 12, 2026, and civilian voters March 15, 2026; incumbent re-elected. [9] [10]
Official announced presidential result
94.90% for Denis Sassou-Nguesso, confirmed by the Constitutional Court March 28, 2026. [9] [10]
Electoral-context qualification
Major opposition parties boycotted the 2026 contest; opposition and civil society raised concerns over competitiveness and turnout. [10]
Presidential inauguration
April 16, 2026, following re-election. [9]
Presidential term
Five years under the current constitutional framework; subject to legal provisions and amendments. [5]
Prime minister
Appointed head of government within the constitutional executive system. [5] [7]
Legislature
Bicameral parliament: National Assembly and Senate. [5]
Court system
Constitutional Court plus ordinary and specialized judicial institutions under Congolese law. [5]
Judicial role
Constitutional Court reviews election outcomes and constitutionality within its defined jurisdiction. [5] [10]
Main governing party
Congolese Labour Party (Parti congolais du travail, PCT) associated with the president’s governing coalition. [15]
Administrative divisions, 2025 onward
15 departments, as evidenced by March 31, 2025 government appointments. [6]

Congo-Oubangui, Djoué-Léfini and Nkéni-Alima appear in the 2025 official prefecture list; do not rely on older 12-department maps. [6]
Departments, first group
Brazzaville, Pointe-Noire, Bouenza, Congo-Oubangui, Cuvette, Cuvette-Ouest, Djoué-Léfini and Kouilou. [6]
Departments, second group
Lékoumou, Likouala, Niari, Nkéni-Alima, Pool, Plateaux and Sangha. [6]
Regional government
Departmental prefects and decentralized institutions operate within national legal authority. [6] [5]
Capital administration
Brazzaville is both the national capital and one of the listed departments. [5] [6]
Coastal administration
Pointe-Noire is listed as a department in the current government reference. [6]
Voting principles
The constitution provides for voting subject to statutory rules, political pluralism and national sovereignty. [5]
Official state flag
Green, yellow and red diagonal arrangement. [5]
State motto
Unité – Travail – Progrès. [5]
Constitutional official language
French; Lingala and Kituba have separate national vehicular status. [5]
State religion
None; the constitution defines a secular republic. [5]
International constitutional context
Different legal periods (1969 People’s Republic, 1992 multiparty transition, current 2015 Constitution) must not be conflated. [15] [5]

Institutional update: the March 2025 government prefecture list recognizes 15 departments. The 2026 presidential election result was officially confirmed but opposition parties and observers contested aspects of the process. [6] [10] [9]

Sources and Methodology

Figures were selected from primary and institutional publications accessed for this November 5, 2026 edition. The page distinguishes observational year, reporting organization, statistical definition and website verification date. Future revisions to national accounts or census releases may change published figures. Each footnote resolves to the source register.

Population and area: World Bank estimates 6,484,437 residents in 2025, while the government’s consolidated 2023 census counted 6,142,180. UNData total area is 342,000 km²; World Bank land area is 341,500 km² (2024). They represent different definitions. [1] [4] [2] [3]

Macroeconomics: The World Bank reports 2025 nominal GDP US$16.31 billion, GDP per capita US$2,514.7, real growth 3.1%, CPI inflation 2.4% and ILO-modelled unemployment 19.9%. IMF statements may have different forecast vintages, and UNData employs another economic series. No values are silently harmonized. [1] [12] [2]

Government: The constitution was adopted in 2015 and revised in 2022. The March 2026 presidential vote, the July 2026 reference to cabinet officeholders and March 2025 establishment of 15 named departmental prefectures are described separately. The confirmed 94.90% result is reported alongside information about opposition and civil-society objections. [5] [10] [8] [7] [6]

Social and environmental series: 2012 religious estimates conflict by survey method; World Bank electrification (53%) and forest-cover (64.0%) measures refer to 2024; UNDP HDI 0.649 and rank 138 of 193 describe 2023 as reported in 2025.

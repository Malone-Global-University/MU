Country Overview

Ethiopia, officially the Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia, is a landlocked state in Eastern Africa and the Horn of Africa. Its capital is Addis Ababa. Ethiopian state traditions extend back to Aksum and later highland kingdoms; no single modern independence date captures this history. The country defeated Italy at the Battle of Adwa in 1896 but experienced Italian occupation from 1936 to 1941. The monarchy ended in 1974, and the present federal constitutional order dates to 1995. [16] [5]

As of November 2026, President Taye Atske-Selassie is head of state and Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed heads the government. Ethiopia is highly diverse linguistically and religiously, with a large agricultural economy and substantial investment in energy and infrastructure. Its national statistical series, fiscal-year trade figures and older census data require careful date labeling. [13] [1] [9]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia (FDRE). [5]
Common name
Ethiopia; historically also known internationally as Abyssinia, although the terms are not fully interchangeable. [16]
Demonym
Ethiopian.
Capital
Addis Ababa, which also hosts the headquarters of the African Union. It has its own city administration. [2] [4]
Other major cities
Dire Dawa, Mekelle, Gondar, Bahir Dar, Hawassa, Jimma, Adama and Dessie; urban rankings vary with administrative and built-up-area definitions. [4]
Continent / UN M49 subregion
Africa / Eastern Africa (Horn of Africa). [2]
Single independence date
Not applicable in the usual decolonization sense. Ethiopian state traditions extend back many centuries; twentieth-century sovereignty was interrupted by Italian occupation from 1936 to 1941. [16]
Ancient state traditions
The Kingdom of Aksum flourished in antiquity in the northern Horn of Africa; subsequent Ethiopian polities had changing rulers and territorial boundaries. [16]
Medieval and early modern states
The Zagwe and later Solomonic dynastic traditions form part of Ethiopia’s long political and religious history; they do not define the borders of the modern republic. [16]
Modern territorial consolidation
During the late nineteenth century, the empire under Menelik II expanded and consolidated much of the territory of the modern state. [16]
Battle of Adwa
March 1, 1896: Ethiopian forces defeated an Italian invasion; the victory became a major symbol of African resistance to imperial conquest. [16]
Italian occupation
1936–1941, following Italy’s 1935 invasion; Ethiopian resistance and Allied forces helped end occupation in 1941. [16]
Monarchy abolished
1974: the military Derg overthrew Emperor Haile Selassie; the imperial constitutional order ended. [16]
Derg government ended
1991: rebel forces overthrew the Derg government, followed by transitional arrangements and a new constitutional process. [16]
Present federal republic
The 1995 Constitution established the current Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia as a federal parliamentary constitutional order. [5] [16]
Present Constitution
Constitution of the Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia, adopted in 1994 and effective in 1995, as amended and implemented. [5]
National flag
Green, yellow and red horizontal bands with a central blue disc bearing a yellow star and rays under Article 3 of the Constitution. [5]
ISO 3166-1 codes
ET (alpha-2); ETH (alpha-3); 231 (numeric).
Country-code top-level domain
.et.
International dialing code
+251.
Calendar conventions
The Ethiopian calendar, with a different year count and year boundary from the Gregorian calendar, is used in domestic life and administration; this page uses Gregorian dates for international comparability.

State-formation note: An Ethiopian national history cannot be reduced to a conventional postcolonial independence date. The 1896 defense of sovereignty, 1941 end of occupation, 1974 fall of monarchy, 1991 transition and 1995 federal constitution are separate historical events. [16] [5]

Geography and Population

Total surface area
Approximately 1,104,300 km² (426,372 sq. mi.), using the archived CIA World Factbook measure; source methodologies differ. [3] [2]
Land area
Approximately 1,096,570 km² (423,388 sq. mi.) in the same Factbook series; inland water is approximately 7,730 km². [3]
Location
Landlocked eastern Africa in the Horn of Africa; no marine coastline or direct sea access. [3]
Neighboring states
Eritrea, Djibouti, Somalia, Kenya, South Sudan and Sudan. [3] [4]
Geographic center
Broadly the Ethiopian Highlands and associated rift and lowland systems; terrain differs markedly between uplands and arid border regions.
Major physical regions
Ethiopian Highlands, northern and central mountain systems, the Great Rift Valley, the Afar Depression, Ogaden/Somali plateau and western lowlands. [3]
Highest mountain
Ras Dejen (Ras Dashen), approximately 4,550 m above sea level in the Simien Mountains. [3]
Lowest land
Afar/Danakil Depression, including locations below sea level. [3]
Principal river systems
Blue Nile (Abay), Awash, Omo, Wabe Shebelle and other tributaries feeding the Nile, Rift Valley lakes and Horn of Africa drainage basins.
Major lakes
Lake Tana, source region of the Blue Nile; Lake Abaya; Lake Chamo; Lake Ziway and other Rift Valley lakes.
Climate
From tropical and arid lowlands to temperate and cool highlands; rainfall is highly seasonal and differs across regions.
Environmental pressures
Droughts, floods, soil erosion, variable rains, food insecurity and pressure on forests, rangelands and water resources.
Estimated population
135,472,051 residents (World Bank 2025 estimate; not a census enumeration). [1] [2]
Population growth
2.6% in 2025 (World Bank population-growth series). [1]
Population density
Approximately 124 people per km² of 2025 World Bank estimated population divided by the archived 1,096,570 km² land-area measure; a derived mixed-source approximation, not an official published density. [1] [3]
Last full national census
2007. Contemporary population statistics are projections or estimates rather than a newer full national headcount. [7] [4]
Population ranking in Africa
Second most populous country in Africa after Nigeria under the World Bank’s 2025 estimate. [1]
Capital-city population
Estimates vary depending on city and metropolitan definitions; UN Data reports about 4.592 million in its 2025 city series. [2]
Life expectancy at birth
About 68 years (2024, World Bank measure; rounded). [1]
Access to electricity
56.6% of the population (2024 World Bank series), indicating substantial remaining access gaps. [1]
Administrative geography
12 regional states and two self-governing city administrations (Addis Ababa and Dire Dawa) by 2026. [4]
New regional-state divisions
Sidama (2020), Southwest Ethiopia Peoples (2021), Central Ethiopia and South Ethiopia (2023) were separated from the former Southern Nations, Nationalities and Peoples arrangement. [4]
Map interpretation
Administrative boundary depictions and disputed border segments can vary by source; geographic areas are approximations rather than settled legal border determinations. [3] [4]

Statistical convention: The 2025 population is an international estimate. The 2007 census is a separate historical enumeration. Country-area totals differ slightly across datasets, and the calculated density above is explicitly derived, not presented as an official 2025 published density. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Economy and International Trade

Economic profile
Large developing mixed economy in which agriculture, trade, services, construction, manufacturing, and transport all play significant roles. [1] [2]
Nominal GDP
US$126.36 billion (2025, World Bank; current US dollars, rounded). [1]
GDP per capita
US$932.7 (2025, World Bank; current US dollars). GDP per capita is output per resident, not mean wages or household earnings. [1]
Real GDP growth
9.8% (2025, World Bank annual series; subject to revisions and different fiscal-calendar reporting conventions). [1]
Inflation
13.2% annual consumer-price inflation in 2025 (World Bank series). [1]
Unemployment
3.3% in 2025 (World Bank/ILO-modeled labor-force estimate; not a direct count of everyone lacking adequate employment). [1]
Currency
Ethiopian birr (ETB); subdivided into 100 santim. [2]
Monetary authority
National Bank of Ethiopia (NBE). [9]
Major economic activities
Smallholder and commercial agriculture; coffee production and processing; wholesale trade; transport and logistics; manufacturing; financial and public services; construction and electricity.
Merchandise export revenue, national fiscal series
US$8.3472 billion in Ethiopian fiscal 2024/25 (NBE report; classification and reporting period differ from calendar-year WTO data). [9] [10]

US$3.4809 billion in 2024/25, approximately 41.7% of the NBE merchandise-export total. [10]
Coffee exports, national fiscal series
US$2.6651 billion in 2024/25, approximately 31.9% of total NBE merchandise exports. [10]
Cut flowers, national fiscal series
US$489.7 million in 2024/25 (NBE, flowers category). [10]
Oilseeds, national fiscal series
US$332.7 million in 2024/25 (NBE category). [10]
Pulses, national fiscal series
US$273.7 million in 2024/25 (NBE category). [10]
Electricity exports, national fiscal series
US$336.4 million in 2024/25 in the NBE accounting; cross-border energy sales are shown separately from coffee and gold. [10]
Calendar 2024 merchandise exports
US$3.6501 billion reported in the WTO country trade profile for calendar 2024; not directly comparable with the subsequent 2024/25 national fiscal series. [11]
Leading 2024 export products, WTO basis
Unroasted coffee, fresh cut roses, sesame seed, dried beans and other agricultural products were prominent in the WTO’s 2024 product listing. [11]
2024 export destinations, WTO basis
European Union (reported collectively, 28.0%); Saudi Arabia (11.4%); United States (8.4%); United Arab Emirates (7.6%); plus other markets. [11]
Main imports
Fuel and petroleum products, industrial machinery, electrical equipment, transport equipment, pharmaceuticals, fertilizers and consumer goods.
Primary trade and logistics gateway
Ethiopia is landlocked and depends heavily on regional transport routes, notably connections to ports in Djibouti, for international cargo.
Hydroelectric investment
The Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam (GERD) on the Blue Nile was officially inaugurated September 9, 2025; power generation and Nile-basin diplomacy remain major regional issues. [19]
Economic reform
Foreign-exchange, monetary and investment reforms are ongoing; inflation and external financing remain important macroeconomic constraints. [1] [9]
WTO status
Accession candidate, not a WTO member, as of November 5, 2026. [12]

People and Society

Language recognition
Article 5 of the 1995 Constitution gives all Ethiopian languages equal state recognition; this is distinct from specifying the language used for a given government function. [5]
Federal working-language policy
Amharic is specified in the Constitution; the federal government announced an expansion in 2020 recognizing Afaan Oromo, Tigrinya, Somali and Afar alongside it for federal working-language purposes. [5] [6]
Widely spoken languages
Afaan Oromo, Amharic, Somali, Tigrinya, Sidama, Wolaytta, Gurage varieties, Afar, Hadiyya, Gamo, and many others; the country has extensive linguistic diversity. [4] [7]
Languages by mother tongue (2007)
Oromo 24.93 million; Amharic 21.63 million; Somali 4.61 million; Tigrinya 4.32 million in 2007 census records (historical, not estimates for 2026). [7]
Language families
Mainly Afro-Asiatic languages (Cushitic, Semitic and Omotic groupings), as well as Nilo-Saharan languages and others. [4]
Literacy and school languages
Regional languages are used in parts of primary education; Amharic and English serve important instructional, administrative and interregional roles; policy and implementation vary by region. [4]
Principal religious traditions
Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Christianity, Sunni Islam, Protestant and evangelical Christianity, Catholic churches, Indigenous religious practices and smaller communities. [8]
Orthodox Christian share, 2007
About 43.5–44% in the 2007 census, depending on presentation and rounding; primarily Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church. [8] [7]
Muslim share, 2007
About 33.9–34% (2007 national census, mostly Sunni Muslim in the U.S. State Department summary). [8] [7]
Protestant share, 2007
Approximately 18.6–19%, including evangelical and Pentecostal communities under the 2007 census classification. [8] [7]
Catholic share, 2007
Approximately 0.7% of the 2007 census population; smaller than the Protestant and Orthodox categories. [7]
Indigenous religious traditions, 2007
Approximately 2.6% in the 2007 census; these figures are broad administrative classifications, not complete descriptions of lived religious practice. [7]
Other religious communities
Ethiopian Jewish historical communities, other Christian denominations, Indigenous faith traditions and smaller religious groups. [8]
Religious-data limitation
The 2007 census remains the last full national religious enumeration widely used in official reporting; avoid treating those percentages as 2025–2026 survey results. [8]
Population and cultural diversity
Oromo, Amhara, Somali, Tigrayan, Sidama, Afar and many other communities contribute to the country’s cultural and linguistic diversity; nationality, language and ancestry are not identical measures. [4]
Religious history
Ethiopia’s northern highland kingdoms adopted Christianity in antiquity; Islam became deeply rooted in the Horn of Africa from the early centuries of Islamic history. [16]
Cultural traditions
Coffee ceremonies, distinctive manuscript and church traditions, oral storytelling, regional foodways, music and festivals, and diverse local artistic practices.
Important heritage
UNESCO properties include Aksum, the Rock-Hewn Churches of Lalibela, Harar Jugol, Simien National Park, and Bale Mountains National Park; the inscription list had 12 properties at the editorial cutoff. [18]
Public health and development
Life expectancy was about 68 years in 2024; uneven access to reliable electricity, rural services, and conflict-affected areas remain important development considerations. [1] [20]

Language and religion methodology: The 2007 census describes reported mother tongues and religious affiliations in that year, not Ethiopia’s 2026 religious or language composition. The constitutional recognition of Ethiopian languages and later federal working-language policy should be read together rather than collapsed into the claim that Ethiopia has one sole official language. [5] [6] [8] [7]

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Federal parliamentary republic under a written constitution; constitutional design and political practice should be distinguished. [5]
Foundational law
1995 Constitution of the Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia. [5]
Head of state
President Taye Atske-Selassie (in office as of November 5, 2026). [13]
Head of government
Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed; reappointed and sworn in on October 5, 2026 at the opening of the Seventh House of Peoples’ Representatives. [13] [14]
Executive authority
Prime Minister and Council of Ministers exercise executive functions; the President has largely ceremonial constitutional functions. [5]
National legislature
Bicameral federal parliamentary institutions: House of Peoples’ Representatives and House of Federation. [5]
House of Peoples’ Representatives
Federal legislative chamber whose members are elected from constituencies; appoints the Prime Minister from the parliamentary political majority. [5] [14]
House of Federation
Upper federal constitutional body representing nations, nationalities and peoples; handles constitutional interpretation and matters concerning federal arrangements. [5]
Speaker of the House of Peoples’ Representatives
Ahmed Shide, selected October 5, 2026 at the opening of the seventh parliamentary term. [14]
Speaker of the House of Federation
Muferihat Kamil, selected October 5, 2026. [14]
Latest national election
Seventh general election held June 1, 2026, followed by the establishment of new federal parliamentary leadership in October. [14]
Government-forming political party
Prosperity Party secured a parliamentary majority at the 2026 election according to the Ethiopian election/government report; political representation and competition vary regionally. [14]
Constitutional election cycle
House of Peoples’ Representatives general elections ordinarily occur every five years under the constitutional framework. [5]
Federal regional states
Twelve regional states: Afar, Amhara, Benishangul-Gumuz, Central Ethiopia, Gambella, Harari, Oromia, Sidama, Somali, South Ethiopia, Southwest Ethiopia Peoples, and Tigray. [4]
Self-governing city administrations
Addis Ababa and Dire Dawa, in addition to the 12 regional states. [4]
Local administrative units
Regions are subdivided, as appropriate, into zones, woredas (districts) and kebeles (local units). [4]
Judicial institutions
Federal courts and regional courts operate within constitutional arrangements; the Federal Supreme Court is the senior federal court. [5]
Political and security conditions
Conflict, displacement and uneven administration in parts of the country mean that formal institutional rules do not necessarily describe local political or security conditions throughout Ethiopia. [20] [4]
Pretoria agreement
A cessation-of-hostilities agreement between the federal government and Tigrayan representatives was signed on November 2, 2022; this did not resolve every political or security problem. [20]
Federal language administration
Federal policy includes Amharic, Afaan Oromo, Tigrinya, Somali and Afar as working languages, while regional governments may designate their own working languages. [5] [6]

Institutional qualification: Constitutional arrangements, current officeholders, the outcome of the 2026 parliamentary process and actual local administrative conditions are distinct. The 12-state count reflects the creation of new states through 2023 and supersedes older nine-state descriptions. [4] [5] [14] [20]

International Profile

United Nations
Original UN founding participant: signed the Charter June 26, 1945; ratification recorded November 13, 1945. [15]
African Union
Founding participant in the Organization of African Unity tradition and a member of the African Union; Addis Ababa hosts the AU Commission headquarters. [16]
Intergovernmental Authority on Development
IGAD member participating in Horn of Africa regional cooperation. [17]
Common Market for Eastern and Southern Africa
COMESA member; participates in regional economic integration. [17]
African Continental Free Trade Area
Participant in the AfCFTA framework, with implementation shaped by national and continental trade rules. [17]
World Trade Organization
Not yet a member at the editorial cutoff; negotiations for WTO accession remain ongoing. [12]
Regional diplomatic hub
Addis Ababa hosts numerous embassies, African Union institutions and international diplomatic missions.
Transport and maritime access
A landlocked state that relies substantially on neighboring countries and regional transport corridors for port access. [3]
Nile basin diplomacy
The Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam on the Blue Nile is central to electricity planning and diplomatic discussions with downstream countries. [19]
2025 hydroelectric milestone
GERD was inaugurated on September 9, 2025; its commissioning is a date-specific fact, not a measure of national household electricity access. [19] [1]
UNESCO heritage
Twelve UNESCO World Heritage properties were listed under Ethiopia at the November 2026 editorial cutoff; World Heritage recognition is not an international political membership. [18]
International dialing code
+251.
Internet country-code domain
.et.
Time zone
East Africa Time (EAT), UTC+3; no regular seasonal daylight-saving adjustment.
Standard currency code
ETB, Ethiopian birr. [2]
Foreign-policy priorities
Regional peace and security, economic corridors, development finance, Nile basin arrangements, trade integration and access to international markets.

Membership distinction: Ethiopia is a founding UN member and a participant in major African regional organizations, but it was still negotiating accession to the WTO as of the November 5, 2026 editorial cutoff. It should not be labeled an existing WTO member. [15] [12] [17]

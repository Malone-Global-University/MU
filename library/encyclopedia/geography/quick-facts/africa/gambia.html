Country Overview

The Gambia, officially the Republic of The Gambia, is a narrow country in Western Africa extending inland along the Gambia River from the Atlantic coast. Banjul is the capital, while the larger western urban corridor includes Kanifing and Brikama. The country is surrounded on its landward sides by Senegal. It became independent from the United Kingdom on February 18, 1965, established a republic in 1970 and is governed by the 1997 Constitution, as amended. Agriculture, commerce, tourism and overseas remittances are central to its contemporary economy. [9] [7] [11] [1]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of The Gambia. [9]
Common name
The Gambia; Gambia (many international datasets and country lists). [9] [4]
Demonym
Gambian. [9]
Capital
Banjul, on St. Mary’s Island at the mouth of the Gambia River. [9]
Largest continuous urban area
Greater Banjul, including Kanifing and adjacent communities; the municipality of Banjul itself is much smaller than the metropolitan settlement. [4] [20]
Continent / UN M49 subregion
Africa / Western Africa; UN country numeric code 270. [28] [4]
Precolonial historical setting
Settlements and political communities developed within the wider Senegambian region before British colonization, with long-standing trade, Islamic, and Atlantic connections. [11]
Colonial administration
The river corridor became a British colonial possession and protectorate, with periods of overlapping regional European imperial interests. [11]
Internal self-government
1963, before complete independence from the United Kingdom. [11] [27]
Independence
February 18, 1965, from the United Kingdom, initially as a Commonwealth realm. [11]
Republic established
April 24, 1970, following a referendum; Dawda Jawara became the first president. [11]
Current constitutional order
1997 Constitution, introduced following a 1996 referendum, as amended; later replacement drafts have not displaced it. [12] [14] [15]
Draft constitutional reform
The proposed 2024 Constitution Bill was rejected by the National Assembly at second reading on July 7, 2025. [15]
Senegambia Confederation
Confederation with Senegal existed from 1982 to 1989 while the two states retained their separate identities. [11]
Military takeover
July 1994 coup removed President Dawda Jawara; Yahya Jammeh governed until the 2016 election and January 2017 transition. [26] [7]
Political transfer of power
Adama Barrow won the December 2016 election and took office in January 2017 after the disputed transition. [7] [26]
National day
Independence Day, February 18. [11]
ISO 3166-1 codes
GM (alpha-2); GMB (alpha-3); 270 (numeric). [28]

Historical context: The Gambia’s modern constitutional history has multiple distinct benchmarks: British colonial control, independence in 1965, republican status in 1970, the 1994 coup, the 1997 constitution and the 2017 transfer of power. Its present constitutional framework was not replaced by the unsuccessful 2024 draft bill. [11] [12] [15] [7]

Narrow West African state following both banks of the Gambia River inland from the Atlantic coast. [7]

4,361 sq. mi., using the UNData total surface-area measure. [4]

Atlantic Ocean coast along the western end of the country. [7]

Gambia River, a navigable estuary and major inland corridor. [7]
Coastal geography
Low-lying beaches, estuaries, mangroves and riverine wetlands. [7]

Predominantly low plains and gently rolling uplands along the river basin. [7]

Tropical with a marked seasonal division between rains and a drier period. [7]

Coastal and riverine ecology
Mangroves, wetlands, estuarine fish habitats, cultivated lowlands and savanna landscapes. [7]
Natural-environment concerns

Landlocked status

Area and census conventions: Total area includes inland waters; land area excludes them. The May–June 2024 census is a provisional enumeration while the World Bank 2025 figure is a model-based estimate. Some of the eight statistical local-government areas are not coterminous with the five regional administrations. [4] [5] [2] [20] [1]

Economy and International Trade

Economic system
Small, open, lower-income mixed economy dependent on domestic trade, agriculture, remittances and tourism. [7] [8]
Nominal GDP
Approximately US$2.59 billion (World Bank, 2025, current dollars). [1]
Nominal GDP per capita
Approximately US$919 (World Bank, 2025, current dollars), not average wages or household income. [1]
Real GDP growth
5.9% (World Bank, 2025 annual growth). [1] [8]
Currency
Gambian dalasi (GMD), symbol D; 1 dalasi = 100 bututs. [4] [27]
National monetary authority
Central Bank of The Gambia. [27]
Dalasi introduced
1971, replacing the pre-decimal Gambian pound system. [27]
Principal economic activities
Wholesale and retail commerce, agriculture, construction, transport, public services, financial activities and tourism. [7] [8]
Main agricultural production
Groundnuts, rice, millet, maize, vegetables and horticultural crops; agriculture is sensitive to rainfall. [7] [8]
Fisheries
Artisanal and industrial fishing and seafood processing operate in coastal and riverine waters. [7] [6]
Visitor economy
Tourism generates foreign exchange through accommodation, hospitality, transport and cultural and coastal experiences. [8] [5]
Remittances from abroad
Personal remittances received were about 23.8% of GDP in the World Bank 2025 series, a major source of external household income. [1]
Employment pressure
Employment quality, youth labor opportunities and women’s economic participation are prominent development challenges. [8]

Key economic vulnerabilities
External financing conditions, fuel and food import costs, coastal climate risks and employment constraints. [8] [7]

People and Society

Official language
English, used in central government, national administration and formal education. [10]
Widely spoken Gambian languages
Mandinka, Wolof, Pulaar/Fula, Jola, Serahule/Soninke and several other local languages. [10]
Language practice
Multilingual communication is common; home language, ethnic identification and official-language competence are not interchangeable statistics. [10]
Major ethnolinguistic communities
Mandinka, Fula, Wolof, Jola and Serahule, alongside other Gambian and immigrant communities. [10]
Religious affiliation: Islam
Approximately 96.4% of the population in the U.S. State Department’s 2023 country estimate, principally Sunni Muslim; not a 2026 census count. [19]
Religious affiliation: Christianity
Approximately 3.5% of the population in the 2023 U.S. State Department estimate; Catholic and Protestant traditions are represented. [19]
Other religious traditions
Smaller Baha’i, Hindu, Ahmadiyya and other communities; Indigenous beliefs may coexist with organized religious affiliations. [19]
Freedom of religion
The Constitution protects religious practice and disallows establishment of an official state religion. [19] [14]
Religious estimate caveat
Percentages are approximate external demographic estimates, not results of the 2024 preliminary population census. [19] [2]
Demographic age structure
A youthful population, with about 39.7% aged 0–14 in the UN population projection used for the 2025 UNData profile. [4]
Population sex ratio
About 99.2 males per 100 females in the 2025 UNData projection. [4]
Life expectancy
About 66 years at birth (World Bank, 2024). [1]
Population and housing census
2024 preliminary digital census collected household, settlement, fertility, migration and other demographic characteristics. [2]
Urban-rural distinction
Urbanization is concentrated in the western corridor, while significant populations in other regions depend on rural and market-town economies. [2] [7]
Schooling
English-language formal schooling coexists with religious, community and technical learning traditions. [10] [8]
Education and labor market
Recent education gains have not eliminated disparities in workforce participation and employment outcomes. [8]
Public health
Healthcare services face geographic and resource constraints, with maternal and child health and infectious disease control among persistent priorities. [4] [7]
Gambian music
The kora, balafon, traditional percussion, singing and praise traditions are significant to Gambian musical cultures. [10]
Cultural and oral heritage
Griots/jalis maintain musical and oral-history traditions within multiple communities. [10]
Festivals and celebrations
Ramadan and Eid celebrations, Christian holidays and diverse community events shape public cultural life. [10] [19]
UNESCO-recognized heritage
Kunta Kinteh Island and Related Sites, and the transboundary Stone Circles of Senegambia. [25]

Religious and demographic conventions: The religion percentages shown are 2023 secondary estimates; they are not a census of all residents in 2026. The 2024 preliminary population count and later population estimates are also distinct. [19] [2] [1]

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Unitary presidential republic under the 1997 Constitution, as amended. [14] [12]
Head of state
President Adama Barrow; assumed office in January 2017. [17] [7]
Head of government
President Adama Barrow; the president heads the executive government. [17]
Vice president
Muhammed B. S. Jallow, listed by the Office of the President; spellings of the given name vary in official materials. [18]
Current constitutional foundation
1997 Constitution remains in force; the proposed 2024 replacement bill was defeated in 2025. [14] [15]
Current constitutional reform status
A replacement draft did not obtain the required legislative threshold in July 2025; institutional reforms remain under public discussion. [15] [7]
Executive branch
President, vice president, cabinet ministries, administrative departments and statutory agencies. [17] [18]
Legislature
Unicameral National Assembly with 58 statutory members. [12] [13]
Directly elected legislators
53 members elected from single-member constituencies. [12] [13]
Nominated legislators
Five members appointed by the president. [12] [13]
Legislative term
Five years. [12]
Current legislative cycle
Sixth Legislature inaugurated after the April 9, 2022 parliamentary election. [12] [13]
Speaker of the National Assembly
Fabakary Tombong Jatta (elected during the 2022 Sixth Legislature). [12]
Last completed presidential election
December 2021, when President Barrow was reelected for a further five-year term. [7] [13]
Next scheduled presidential election
December 5, 2026, according to the Independent Electoral Commission; future event as of this profile cutoff. [16]
Next scheduled National Assembly election
April 10, 2027, according to the IEC published calendar. [16]
Electoral authority
Independent Electoral Commission (IEC). [16]
National judiciary
Supreme Court and other courts operating under the constitutional and statutory legal system. [14]
Regional and local administration
Five named administrative regions with separate Banjul and Kanifing urban authorities; GBoS uses eight LGAs for census reporting. [20]
Five principal regions
West Coast, Lower River, North Bank, Central River, Upper River. [20]
Eight statistical LGAs
Banjul, Kanifing, Brikama, Mansakonko, Kerewan, Kuntaur, Janjanbureh and Basse. [20]
National political transition (1994)
Military takeover overthrew President Dawda Jawara and began the Yahya Jammeh period. [26] [11]
National political transition (2016–2017)
Electoral defeat of Jammeh and his eventual departure enabled Barrow to take office in January 2017. [7] [26]
Truth and reconciliation record
The Truth, Reconciliation and Reparations Commission investigated abuses committed from July 1994 through January 2017. [26]
Truth commission final report
Delivered November 25, 2021, containing 265 recommendations; later implementation and legal proceedings are separate developments. [26]
2026 electoral status
Campaign and nomination arrangements underway before the scheduled December 5, 2026 poll; the result must not be presumed. [16] [7]

Constitution and elections: The proposed constitutional replacement failed in 2025, leaving the 1997 Constitution operative. The December 5, 2026 presidential election is a scheduled future event relative to the November 5, 2026 editorial cutoff; no result is implied. The official National Assembly and IPU figures are 53 elected plus five nominated members, not an older 48-plus-five description. [15] [16] [12] [13]

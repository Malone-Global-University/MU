Country Overview

Sierra Leone, officially the Republic of Sierra Leone, is a coastal country in Western Africa, bordered by Guinea, Liberia and the Atlantic Ocean. Its capital is Freetown. The area has long been home to diverse communities, and Freetown became a major settlement for freed and resettled Africans during the late eighteenth century. Sierra Leone gained independence from the United Kingdom on April 27, 1961, and became a republic in 1971. Today it is a constitutional presidential republic led by President Julius Maada Bio and Vice President Mohamed Juldeh Jalloh. Agriculture, mining, commerce and services form its economy, with iron ore a major goods export. [15] [8] [9] [2] [7]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Sierra Leone. [14] [4]
Common name
Sierra Leone.
Demonym
Sierra Leonean.
Capital
Freetown, on the Atlantic coast of the Western Area. [4] [15]
Continent
Africa.
United Nations subregion
Western Africa. [4]
Geographic setting
Atlantic-facing coastal republic between Guinea and Liberia. [6] [4]
Name origin
Portuguese-language historic reference to lion-like mountains near the coast; conventionally rendered “Lion Mountains”. [15]
Precolonial history
Temne, Mende, Limba, Kono, Sherbro, Vai and other communities maintained diverse political and trading networks before British colonial expansion.
Transatlantic slave-trade history
Coastal sites including Bunce Island were used in the transatlantic trade in enslaved Africans. [15] [17]
Early Freetown settlement
1787: an organized settlement for freed Black people was established on the Sierra Leone Peninsula. [15]
Nova Scotian settlers
1792: Black Loyalists from Nova Scotia established a major new community at Freetown. [15]
Jamaican Maroons
1800: Maroon migrants from Jamaica joined the growing Freetown community. [15]
Crown Colony
1808: Freetown and the coastal settlement became a British Crown Colony. [15]
British protectorate
1896: Britain proclaimed a protectorate over much of the interior, distinct from the coastal Crown Colony. [15]
Independence
April 27, 1961, from the United Kingdom. [15] [22]
Independence Day
April 27, observed as the national independence anniversary. [15]
First prime minister at independence
Sir Milton Margai. [15]
Republic proclaimed
April 19, 1971; Sierra Leone replaced the monarchy with a republican head of state. [15]
Period of single-party rule
The 1978 constitution created a one-party state, subsequently superseded by multiparty constitutional arrangements. [15] [14]
Current constitutional foundation
Constitution of 1991, with subsequent legislation and amendments. [14]
Civil war
1991–2002, involving armed groups, state forces and regional intervention; the conflict had grave human consequences. [15] [2]
Formal end of civil war
January 18, 2002, when the government declared the conflict over. [15]
Ebola emergency
Sierra Leone was severely affected by the West African Ebola outbreak of 2014–2016. [25]
Recent constitutional politics
The 2023 national elections returned President Bio and an SLPP majority to office; the results were disputed by the opposition. [8] [13] [26]
National flag
Three horizontal stripes: green, white and blue. [14]
National motto
Unity, Freedom, Justice.
Contemporary state status
Sovereign multiparty presidential republic. [14] [22]

Historical milestones: Independence (1961), the republic (1971), the 1991 constitutional framework and the end of civil war (2002) are distinct historical events. [15] [14]

Other major cities
Bo, Kenema, Makeni and Koidu.
Coastal region
Estuaries, mangroves, sandy beaches and the mountainous Sierra Leone Peninsula. [6]
Inland physical regions
Coastal plains transition to interior plateaus, forest, savanna and uplands.
Highest mountain
Mount Bintumani (Loma Mansa), commonly listed at about 1,945 metres.
Major rivers
Rokel (Seli), Sewa, Moa, Mano and Great Scarcies.
Southern river system
The Moa River borders and surrounds the Tiwai Island wildlife landscape. [18]
Offshore and coastal islands
Sherbro Island, the Turtle Islands and Banana Islands, among others.
Climate
Tropical; generally a wet season from roughly May to November and a drier season from December to April.
Rainfall
Especially heavy near Freetown and the coastal peninsula; interior rainfall also supports tropical forests.
Harmattan winds
Dry northeasterly seasonal winds may affect inland areas during the dry season.
Forest landscapes
Upper Guinean rainforest and forest–savanna mosaics, including the Gola landscape. [18]
Key forest wildlife
Western chimpanzees, pygmy hippopotamuses and African forest elephants occur in protected forest ecosystems. [18]
Land-use focus
Smallholder agriculture, plantation crops and forest-resource management are significant. [6]
Agricultural regions
Rice and other crops are grown in inland valleys, lowlands and upland farming systems. [6] [24]
Environmental pressures
Deforestation, land degradation, flooding, coastal erosion and urban slope instability.
Freetown hazard exposure
Steep slopes, intense rainfall and urban development increase localized landslide and flash-flood risks.
Protected natural heritage
Gola Rainforest National Park and Tiwai Island Wildlife Sanctuary together form the Gola-Tiwai Complex. [18]
Time zone
Greenwich Mean Time / UTC+00:00 throughout the year.
International dialing code
+232.
Internet country code

People and Society

Population composition
A multilingual society with numerous ethnolinguistic communities. [16] [3]
Official language
English, used in constitutional, administrative, legal and formal education contexts. [22] [14]
Widely spoken lingua franca
Krio, an English-lexifier Creole widely understood across ethnic and regional lines. [15]
Major indigenous languages
Mende, Temne, Limba, Kono, Kuranko, Loko, Fula, Mandinka, Kissi, Sherbro and others. [16]
Mende
Major ethnolinguistic group, especially in southern and eastern areas. [16]
Temne
Major ethnolinguistic group, especially in northern and northwestern areas. [16]
Krio community
Creole cultural heritage is strongly associated with Freetown and histories of liberated and resettled Africans. [15]
Other ethnic communities
Include Limba, Kono, Fula, Loko, Koranko, Mandinka, Kissi, Sherbro and Vai. [16]
Religion—Muslim
About 77%, government estimate reported in the 2023 U.S. religious-freedom report. [16]
Religion—Christian
About 22%, same government-estimate reporting period. [16]
Religion—traditional and other beliefs
Approximately 2% reported under animist/traditional or other categories; approximate religious proportions are rounded and need not sum to 100%. [16]
Islamic communities
Predominantly Sunni Muslim; other Muslim traditions also present. [16]
Christian communities
Includes Methodist, Pentecostal, other Protestant and Catholic traditions. [16]
Interfaith relations
Muslim and Christian communities and inter-religious institutions cooperate in civic life. [16]
Constitutional religious freedoms
Freedom of conscience and religion is provided for by the constitutional and legal framework. [14] [16]
Historical education institution
Fourah Bay College was founded in 1827 and became a major centre of higher learning in West Africa. [15]
Education system
Primary, junior-secondary, senior-secondary, technical and tertiary education are principal levels.
Education policy
Free Quality School Education is a major programme of the Bio administration. [8]
University and tertiary centres
University of Sierra Leone and Njala University are major institutions.
Healthcare
Public health services operate alongside private, faith-based and community providers.
Public health history
The 2014–2016 Ebola epidemic sharply tested health institutions and shaped subsequent surveillance priorities. [25]
Staple foods
Rice, cassava, palm-oil sauces, fish and leafy vegetables feature in everyday cuisine.
Music traditions
Traditional drumming, dance, palm-wine music and contemporary popular styles.
National celebration
Independence Day, April 27. [15]
Sport
Football is widely followed; athletics and other sports also have national associations.
Cultural history
Freetown’s settlement history intersects with Black Atlantic histories, abolition and the transatlantic slave trade. [15]
Cultural landmarks
Bunce Island, historic Freetown and Fourah Bay College are significant historical reference sites. [15] [17]
Social development priorities
Food security, education, health systems, youth opportunity and women’s participation. [24] [8] [12]
Labor-market interpretation
Informal work, household farming and underemployment are not fully conveyed by the modeled unemployment figure. [1] [6]
Heritage and biodiversity
The Gola-Tiwai natural property was recognized in 2025 for globally important forest biodiversity. [18]

Religion methodology: The 2023 religious-freedom report cites government approximations for religious groups, not a 2026 census; percentages are rounded. [16]

Sources and Methodology

Statistical method: World Bank country indicators provide internationally comparable population and macroeconomic data for 2025. Statistics Sierra Leone supplies the 2021 mid-term census, and UNData supplies the 72,300 km² surface-area convention. National official 2024 trade receipts are from the Ministry of Finance/Bank of Sierra Leone annual bulletin. These values refer to different statistical years and are not assumed to describe a single moment.

Data qualifications: The census and the World Bank population series are different measures; published area conventions vary by organization. GDP per capita is not mean or median personal income. Religion percentages are approximations from an earlier reporting period and may exceed 100% when rounded. The 2025 annual inflation rate must not be confused with an August 2026 point-in-time inflation reading. Trade tables exclude international services and use the ministry’s own reporting series; other international data compilers may differ. Current officeholders and memberships were checked against institutional sources available by November 5, 2026.

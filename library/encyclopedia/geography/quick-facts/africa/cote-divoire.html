Country Overview

Côte d’Ivoire, also known in English as Ivory Coast, is a West African republic on the Gulf of Guinea. It borders Liberia, Guinea, Mali, Burkina Faso and Ghana. Yamoussoukro is the political capital, while Abidjan is its largest city and principal commercial and administrative center. The country gained independence from France on August 7, 1960. Its current constitutional framework dates from 2016, with amendments in 2020 and 2023. A major cocoa-growing and trading economy, Côte d’Ivoire also exports gold, natural rubber, refined petroleum and cashews. [5] [11] [2]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Côte d’Ivoire; République de Côte d’Ivoire. [5]
Common name
Côte d’Ivoire; Ivory Coast (traditional English rendering). The country requests use of “Côte d’Ivoire” as its international name.
Political capital
Yamoussoukro, designated national capital in 1983; some principal government institutions remain in Abidjan. [5] [18]
Administrative and economic center
Abidjan, the largest urban center, principal port, and continuing seat of many national administrative and diplomatic functions. [5] [20]
Continent / UN M49 region
Africa / Western Africa. [17]
Precolonial history
The territory was home to diverse Akan, Kru, Mandé and Gur-speaking peoples, kingdoms, communities and trading networks; no single modern-style founding date applies to the precolonial societies. [3]
French colonial incorporation
A French colony was formally established in 1893; colonial administration and territorial organization continued to change through the twentieth century.
French Community and republican status
The Republic of Côte d’Ivoire was proclaimed on December 4, 1958, within the then-French Community, before full sovereignty.
Independence
August 7, 1960, from France; this is the nationally commemorated independence date. [5] [18]
United Nations admission
September 20, 1960. [10]
First president
Félix Houphouët-Boigny, president from 1960 until his death in 1993.
Political transition of 1999
A military coup ended the presidency of Henri Konan Bédié in December 1999; subsequent elections and conflict disrupted political institutions.
2002 conflict
An armed rebellion in September 2002 divided the country, producing years of political and military crisis and later peace negotiations.
2010–2011 post-election crisis
Disputed presidential-election results led to violent confrontation and a political crisis, followed by Alassane Ouattara’s assumption of the presidency in 2011.
Current constitutional order
Third Republic Constitution promulgated November 8, 2016, and amended on March 19, 2020, and July 25, 2023. [11]
2025 presidential election
Alassane Ouattara was returned to office following the October 25, 2025 election; the electoral commission reported approximately 89.8% in its provisional first-round result. [12] [18]
National holiday
Independence Day, August 7. [5]
National motto
Union – Discipline – Travail (“Unity – Discipline – Work”). [7]
ISO 3166-1 codes
CI (alpha-2); CIV (alpha-3); 384 (numeric). [17]
Internet country-code domain
.ci

Geography and Population

Geographic location
Coastal West African country on the Gulf of Guinea, with its Atlantic coastline in the south. [15]
Total surface area
322,463 km² (approximately 124,504 sq. mi.; includes inland waters). [16] [5]
Land area
318,003 km² (approximately 122,782 sq. mi.); reference land-area definitions vary among publishers. [16]
Inland water area
4,460 km², as reported in the archived geographic reference. [16]
2025 estimated population
32,711,547 (World Bank); an estimate, not a census enumeration. [1]
2025 national estimate
32,757,857 (ANStat). The national series differs slightly from the World Bank estimate and is given for comparison, not averaged with it. [4]
2021 national census
29,389,150 residents, definitive census count. [3]
Population growth
2.4% in 2025, World Bank annual population-growth series. [1]
Population density
Approximately 103 residents per km² of land using the 2025 World Bank population and the 318,003-km² land-area measure; calculated reference figure.
Urbanization
52.5% urban and 47.5% rural in the 2021 national census; not a 2025 urbanization estimate. [3]
Largest city
Abidjan, the principal metropolitan, commercial and port center. [5]
Other major cities
Bouaké, San-Pédro, Korhogo, Daloa, Gagnoa and Yamoussoukro. [5]
Land neighbors
Liberia and Guinea to the west; Mali and Burkina Faso to the north; Ghana to the east. [15]
Atlantic coastline
Approximately 515 km under one commonly used geographical measurement; coastal definitions can vary. [16]
Major rivers
Bandama, Comoé, Sassandra and Cavally river systems.
Relief and highest point
Mostly low plateaus and plains, with western highlands; Mount Nimba reaches approximately 1,752 m at the Guinea–Liberia–Côte d’Ivoire frontier region. [15]
Climate
Humid tropical conditions in the south, transitional climate in the center, and drier tropical savanna conditions toward the north; rainfall and dry-season patterns vary regionally. [15]
Vegetation
Southern forest and coastal lagoon systems transition toward woodland and savanna in the north. [15]
Natural resources
Gold, petroleum and natural gas, manganese and other minerals, water resources, forests and agricultural land. [16] [14]
Time zone
Greenwich Mean Time, UTC+00:00; no regular daylight-saving time.

Economy and International Trade

Economic system
Market-oriented mixed economy with substantial commercial agriculture, trade, transport, finance, telecommunications, construction, energy and public investment. [20]
Nominal GDP
Approximately US$99.77 billion in 2025, current US dollars (World Bank series). [1]
Nominal GDP per capita
Approximately US$3,050.1 in 2025 (World Bank); GDP per capita is not average earnings or median household income. [1]
Real GDP growth
6.5% in 2025 (World Bank annual real GDP growth series). [1]
Currency
West African CFA franc, issued by BCEAO; ISO 4217 code XOF and conventional symbol CFA or FCFA. [19] [5]
Currency arrangement
XOF is pegged to the euro at EUR 1 = XOF 655.957, under the existing monetary arrangement. [5] [19]
Central bank
Central Bank of West African States (BCEAO), shared with other West African Economic and Monetary Union members. [19]
Main economic sectors
Agriculture and agro-processing, services, manufacturing, mining, oil and gas, construction, logistics, banking and communications. [20]
Principal agricultural products
Cocoa, cashew nuts, rubber, coffee, palm oil, bananas and other food and cash crops. [14]
Major export industries
Cocoa beans and processed cocoa, gold, natural rubber, refined petroleum, crude oil and cashew nuts. [2] [14]
Merchandise exports
US$20.5405 billion in 2024 (WTO profile, based on UN Comtrade); goods only. [2]
Merchandise imports
US$17.2152 billion in 2024 (WTO profile, based on UN Comtrade); goods only. [2]
Merchandise trade balance
Approximately US$3.3253 billion surplus in 2024, calculated as merchandise exports less imports from the same WTO series; not a current-account balance. [2]
Largest export markets (2024)
European Union counted as a bloc (33.7%); Switzerland (13.4%); Mali (8.1%); United States (4.5%); Viet Nam (4.0%), followed by other partners. [2]
Principal transport and trade hubs
Abidjan and San-Pédro ports, road freight networks and connections to landlocked West African economies. [5] [20]
Cocoa and value addition
Côte d’Ivoire is a major global cocoa producer; raw beans, cocoa paste and cocoa butter are distinguishable export products, while international cocoa prices affect export receipts. [2] [14]
Goods-versus-services distinction
The US$20.54-billion 2024 trade figure covers merchandise exports and does not include transportation, tourism or other service exports. [2]
Different trade-reporting systems
Government export values in CFA francs and WTO/UN Comtrade values in US dollars can differ due to source revisions, exchange rates, classification and valuation methods; they are not interchangeable. [2] [14]

People and Society

Official language
French, used in national administration, schooling and formal state business. [5]
Widely used Ivorian languages
Dioula/Jula, Baoulé, Bété, Senufo languages, Agni/Anyin, Dan/Yacouba and many other Indigenous languages; use differs by region and community.
Languages in commerce
Dioula is an important regional lingua franca for commerce and intercommunity communication; French is widely used in formal and urban settings.
2021 religious composition
Muslim 42.5%; Christian 39.8%; no religion 12.6%; animist 2.2%; other religions approximately 0.7%. These are 2021 census figures, not 2026 estimates. [3] [13]
Christian denominations
The 2021 census includes Catholics, Protestant/Methodist groups, Harrists and other Christian churches, including evangelical congregations. [13]
Islamic communities
Predominantly Sunni Islam, with diverse religious and Sufi traditions and smaller groups; religious affiliation cannot be inferred from ethnicity alone. [13]
Traditional belief systems
Indigenous spiritual practices and traditions persist; some residents also integrate customary practices with Christianity or Islam. [13]
Constitutional religion policy
The state is formally secular, with religious liberty guaranteed by the constitutional system. [11]
Cultural and ethnic diversity
Akan, Gur/Voltaic, Northern Mandé, Southern Mandé and Kru population categories appear in national census reporting; those statistical groupings encompass many distinct communities. [3]
Migration and nationality
A substantial resident population has familial and migratory ties across West Africa; census totals cover residents, not just citizens. [3]
National demonym
Ivorian; Ivoirien or Ivoirienne in French.
Adult literacy
Approximately 50% in the 2021 figure reproduced by Côte d’Ivoire’s foreign ministry; literacy measures depend on definition, ages and survey source. [5] [18]
Life expectancy
Approximately 62 years at birth in 2024 (World Bank series). [1]
Demographic profile
A relatively young and growing population; the 2021 census documents marked urban growth and extensive regional mobility. [3]
Cultural and social life
Music, oral traditions, contemporary literature, cinema, football, festivals and regional cuisines reflect diverse communities and urban cultural exchange.
Largest metropolitan center
Abidjan is the principal hub for universities, media, creative industries and finance; Yamoussoukro remains the statutory capital. [5]

Religious and linguistic interpretation: Religious percentages derive from the 2021 resident-population census and are not government endorsements of any faith. Listed languages are examples, not an exhaustive ranking by native speakers or by total fluency. [3] [13]

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Unitary presidential constitutional republic with a prime minister and bicameral parliament, under the Third Republic. [11] [9]
Fundamental law
Constitution of November 8, 2016, as amended March 19, 2020, and July 25, 2023. [11]
Head of state
President Alassane Ouattara; re-elected in the October 2025 presidential election, status checked November 5, 2026. [7] [18]
Vice president
Tiémoko Meyliet Koné, vice president since April 2022; status verified in October 2026 official communications. [7]
Head of government
Prime Minister Robert Beugré Mambé, reappointed January 21, 2026, following the government’s resignation earlier that month. [6]
President’s principal functions
Head of state and central executive authority under the presidential constitutional system; presidential powers and appointment rules are set by the Constitution. [11]
Council of Ministers
Prime minister and ministers implement the government program under the constitutional and presidential framework. [11] [6]
Legislative structure
Bicameral Parliament: National Assembly (Assemblée nationale) and Senate (Sénat). [9] [11]
National Assembly
255 seats, chosen through legislative elections; last general legislative election held December 27, 2025. [9]
Senate
Upper chamber combining indirectly elected members and presidential appointees under constitutional and statutory rules; chamber size and vacancies should be checked from Senate records. [11] [9]
Judicial institutions
Judicial branch with ordinary courts and higher judicial institutions; constitutional adjudication and the electoral framework involve the Constitutional Council. [11]
Administrative structure (2026)
31 regions and two autonomous districts, Abidjan and Yamoussoukro, in current government mapping. Older lists of 14 autonomous districts reflect a superseded organization. [8]
Other subnational divisions
Regions are divided into departments and sub-prefectures; communes and local councils perform municipal and local functions. [8]
Main political and administrative locations
Yamoussoukro is the political capital by law; Abidjan remains the main administrative, economic and diplomatic center. [5]
Presidential elections
Direct presidential elections under constitutional rules; the most recent election took place October 25, 2025. [12] [18]
2025 presidential result
The Independent Electoral Commission’s provisional results reported Alassane Ouattara with 89.77% of valid votes; official provisional returns are not a substitute for a full assessment of electoral conditions. [12]
2025 legislative election
General National Assembly election held December 27, 2025; Parliament’s lower-house composition was updated for the new legislature. [9]
Public administration
National ministries, deconcentrated prefectural administration and local government operate alongside specialized public institutions. [8]
Defense and security
Armed forces, internal security institutions, police and gendarmerie support national defense, public safety and border security.

2026 territorial revision: The current official youth-program map identifies 31 regions and two autonomous districts (Abidjan and Yamoussoukro). Sources created before the January 2026 district reform may still describe 14 autonomous districts. [8]

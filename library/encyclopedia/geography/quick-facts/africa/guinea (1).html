Country Overview

Guinea, officially the Republic of Guinea (République de Guinée), is a West African republic on the Atlantic coast. Its capital, Conakry, serves as the main political and commercial center. Guinea became independent of France on October 2, 1958, following a September referendum that rejected membership in the proposed French Community. Its economy is anchored by mineral extraction, agriculture and services. Following a 2021 military takeover, a new constitution was promulgated in September 2025, a presidential election took place that December, and a newly elected National Assembly was seated in July 2026. Indigenous and precolonial history
The modern territory includes the histories of Mande communities, Futa Jallon, coastal polities and Forest Guinea societies; the republic did not emerge from one single precolonial state. [23]

Leading bauxite producer globally in 2024, estimated at 33.2% of world output excluding the United States according to USGS. [19]

Large iron-ore development and transport corridor; iron-ore exports began in the 2025–2026 period, creating new diversification potential and infrastructure requirements. [22]

An important source of rural income and employment; farming includes rice, other staple crops, livestock and cash-crop production. [8] [22]

People and Society

Constitutional official languages
The 2025 Constitution recognizes national languages and French as official languages; the French language is specifically the working language. [6]
French in administration
French is the working language of state administration, formal documentation and much of schooling. [6] [14]
Prominent national languages
Pular (Fula), Maninka, Susu, Kissi, Kpelle (Guerzé) and Toma (Loma), alongside many others. [14]
Language use: Pular
Approximately 35% of the population in the 2014 census-language summary; the proportion is historical, not a new 2025 language census. [14]
Language use: Maninka
Approximately 25% in the cited 2014 census-language summary. [14]
Language use: Susu
Approximately 18% in the cited 2014 census-language summary. [14]
Language geography
Pular is particularly important in Middle Guinea, Maninka in Upper Guinea, and Susu in Coastal Guinea; multilingualism crosses these zones. [14]
Religious affiliation: Muslim
89.1% in a 2014 census-based series summarized in the archived World Factbook; not a 2025 measurement. [15]
Religious affiliation: Christian
6.8% in the same 2014 census-based series. [15]
Religious affiliation: traditional / animist
1.6% in the same 2014 census-based series. [15]
Religious affiliation: other / none
0.1% other and 2.4% no religion in the 2014 census-based series; combined totals are subject to rounding. [15]
Constitutional religious order
The republic is constitutionally secular and protects religious liberty. [6]
Major ethnolinguistic communities
Fulani/Peul, Malinké/Mandinka, Susu, Kissi, Kpelle/Guerzé, Toma and other communities; terminology and denominators vary across surveys. [14]
Rural share of population
61.3% in 2025 preliminary national census results. [2]
Urban share of population
38.7% in 2025 preliminary national census results. [2]
Sex composition
51.8% women and 48.2% men in 2025 preliminary national census results. [2]
Population youth
43% under 15 in 2025 preliminary census results. [2]
Life expectancy at birth
61 years in the latest World Bank indicator, referring to 2024; cross-dataset values can differ. [1]
Cultural practices
Diverse Mande, Atlantic and forest-region musical, storytelling, craft and culinary traditions; local cultural forms differ across communities. [14]
Legal language implementation
Article 5 of the 2025 Constitution envisages an organic law setting the modalities for national-language implementation and official-text translation. [6]

Language-law update: Many older international fact sheets list French as Guinea’s only official language. Article 5 of the September 2025 Constitution instead identifies national languages and French as official, with French serving as the working language. Likewise, the religious figures above refer to 2014, not a current religious census. [6] [15] [14]

Government and Institutions

Form of state
Unitary, secular republic organized under the September 2025 Constitution. [6]
Present constitutional framework
September 2025 Constitution; implementation of some new institutions has proceeded through 2026. [6] [10]
Head of state
President Mamadi Doumbouya; elected December 28, 2025 and inaugurated January 17, 2026. [8] [9]
Head of government
Prime Minister Amadou Oury Bah, reappointed January 26, 2026. [9]
Executive branch
President, prime minister and government under constitutional arrangements. [6]
Presidential term
Seven years, renewable once, under Article 44 of the 2025 Constitution. [6]
Presidential electoral system
Direct popular election with a two-round majority system under the 2025 Constitution. [6]
Legislature: constitutional design
Bicameral Parliament composed of a National Assembly and a Senate under the 2025 Constitution. [6]
National Assembly
147 elected deputies; elections held May 31, 2026 and members seated July 17, 2026. [7] [11]
National Assembly term
Five years under the constitutional framework. [6]
Senate
Upper chamber provided for in the 2025 Constitution; its implementation and selection procedures are distinct from the already seated July 2026 National Assembly. [6] [10] [11]
Senate term
Six years under the 2025 Constitution. [6]
Transition legislature
Appointed National Transition Council served after the 2021 military takeover and ceased performing its legislative role after the new elected National Assembly was installed. [10] [11]
Judicial system
Courts and constitutional review institutions organized by the Constitution and applicable laws. [6]
Administrative regions
10 administrative regions following the August 20, 2026 creation of Beyla and Siguiri regions; older references may list eight. [12] [3]
Prefectures
44 following the August 20, 2026 creation of 11 new prefectures; older references may list 33. [13] [3]
Major administrative units
Regions, prefectures, subprefectures and decentralized local authorities; Conakry has special administrative significance. [3]
2026 legislative election
May 31, 2026, followed by final results and the installation of deputies July 17, 2026. [7] [11]
2026 executive appointments
The government composition was revised in July 2026; verify officeholder-specific changes against the official government register. [9]
Political pluralism context
A March 2026 decision dissolved numerous political parties under a regulatory review; opposition groups disputed its effects on political competition. [8]
Constitutional rights
The 2025 Constitution declares fundamental rights, electoral principles, state secularism and a minimum 30% women’s participation quota in national, regional and local elected or decision-making offices. [6]

Institutional status: The Constitution establishes a two-chamber Parliament, but the post-transition National Assembly is specifically documented as seated in July 2026. The Senate should not be described as fully operational without separate evidence of its formation. Also, legislative chamber counts published by different organizations may refer to constitutional plans or proposed organic-law arrangements rather than seated members. [6] [10] [11]

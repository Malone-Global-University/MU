Country Overview

Mauritania, officially the Islamic Republic of Mauritania, is a large, sparsely populated Saharan and Sahelian country on the Atlantic coast of northwest Africa. Its capital is Nouakchott. It connects the western Sahara and Maghreb with Sahelian societies and the Senegal River basin. It gained independence from France on November 28, 1960. Its main traditional export sectors are gold, iron ore, fisheries and copper, with offshore liquefied natural gas exports beginning in 2025. [3] [9] [12] [14]

President Mohamed Ould Cheikh El Ghazouani began a second elected five-year term in August 2024. El Moctar Ould Djay remained prime minister following a cabinet renewal announced in October 2026. Mauritania has a unicameral National Assembly and Arabic as its constitutional official language. [7] [8] [18] [6]

Geography and Population

Country location
West-central Sahara and Sahel on the Atlantic coast of northwestern Africa. [2] [3]
Total surface area
1,030,700 km² (UNData/ANSADE). [3] [5]
Approximate surface in square miles
About 398,000 square miles (rounded conversion from square kilometres). [3]
Area measurement convention
The national surface-area figure is a geographic statistic, not an assertion about unresolved neighboring territorial claims. [3]
Western coastline
Atlantic Ocean; fisheries, ports and nearshore ecological habitats are important national features. [16] [2]
Neighboring sovereign states
Algeria to the northeast, Mali to the east/southeast, and Senegal along the southwest border.
Northwestern boundary
Western Sahara is a disputed territory immediately northwest of Mauritania; this entry does not resolve its sovereignty.
Southern river border
The Senegal River forms much of the boundary with Senegal and supports irrigated agriculture.
Dominant physical environment
Sahara Desert covers most of the territory; the southern margin transitions toward the semi-arid Sahel. [2]
Desert landforms
Ergs (sand dunes), rocky plateaus, escarpments, gravel plains and oases are widespread.
Central desert highlands
Adrar plateau and surrounding escarpments are characteristic landforms.
Northern mining zone
Zouerate and the Tiris Zemmour area are major iron-ore mining and transport locations. [5] [12]
Coastal nature reserve
Banc d’Arguin National Park, recognized by UNESCO for extensive habitats for migratory and resident birds. [16]
Important inland oasis settlements
Atar, Chinguetti, Ouadane and other desert towns historically linked caravan routes. [16] [5]
Typical climate
Hyper-arid in much of the north and centre; hotter seasonally rainy Sahelian conditions in the south.
Rainfall concentration
Most rainfall occurs in the southern seasonal wet months, especially summer to early autumn.
Major environmental risks
Drought, desertification, flash flooding and coastal pressures; climate volatility affects pastoral and river communities. [2]
Arable land context
World Bank estimates arable land at approximately 0.4% of the country’s land area. [2]

Capital region is organized into Nouakchott Nord, Nouakchott Ouest and Nouakchott Sud wilayas. [5]

4,927,532 residents (final RGPH5 enumeration). [4]

Nouakchott, Nouadhibou and the Senegal River valley have comparatively concentrated settlement. [2] [5]

People and Society

Historical-cultural composition
Arab and Hassaniya-speaking Moorish communities, Haratin communities, and several West African ethnolinguistic communities; these labels overlap and must not be treated as rigid census categories. [20] [6]
Hassaniya Arabic
Widely used vernacular Arabic variety, particularly among Moorish communities.
Other cultural communities
Pulaar-speaking, Soninke-speaking and Wolof-speaking communities are important parts of the country. [6]
National languages
Arabic, Pulaar (also spelled Poular), Soninke and Wolof under Article 6 of the constitution. [6]
Official language
Arabic is the sole official language under Article 6; the other three national languages are not individually designated official in that article. [6]
French language
French is used in parts of administration, education, business and diplomacy, but Article 6 does not list it as an official language. [6]
Religion of the state
Islam (Article 5 of the constitution). [6]
Predominant religious tradition
The population is overwhelmingly Muslim, primarily Sunni; there is no basis here for a precise current percentage estimate. [19]
Non-Muslim residents
Small Christian and other non-Muslim communities are present; migration status and legal classifications complicate precise quantification. [19]
Freedom of belief legal context
Constitutional protections coexist with legal restrictions on apostasy and blasphemy reported by the U.S. Department of State. [6] [19]
National identity and diversity
Constitutional preamble recognizes cultural diversity within national unity. [6]
Slavery abolished
1981 abolition measure; later law criminalized slavery in 2007. [20]
Antislavery law strengthened
2015 legislation increased penalties and created specialized institutional mechanisms. [20]
Contemporary social-rights issue
Human-rights reporting documents continuing discrimination and difficulties enforcing slavery-related protections; abolition does not imply all abuses ended. [20]
2023 census households
834,352 ordinary households, mean household size 5.9, in national final RGPH5 results. [4]
2024 life expectancy
Approximately 69 years at birth in World Bank WDI (rounded). [1]
WHO life expectancy series
WHO estimates 70.9 years at birth for 2023; its methodology differs from the World Bank WDI value. [21] [1]
2024 access to electricity
52.2% of population in World Bank WDI. [1]
2024 forest cover
About 1.0% of land area in World Bank WDI. [1]
Urbanization
Around 61% of residents lived in urban areas in 2025–2026, according to the World Bank country overview. [2]
Education systems
Public and private education includes Arabic-language instruction and bilingual / French-language streams.
Traditional Islamic learning
Mahadra communities transmit knowledge, oral traditions and Islamic scholarship recognized by UNESCO. [17]
Music and oral literature
Moorish epic T’heydinn is UNESCO-recognized intangible heritage. [17]
Heritage of trade-route scholarship
Chinguetti and other historic ksour retain distinctive Saharan manuscript, mosque and settlement traditions. [16]
Traditional foods
Couscous, dates, rice, grain preparations, milk and tea reflect Saharan and Sahelian foodways. [17]
Couscous heritage
Included in a multinational UNESCO intangible-heritage inscription (2020). [17]
Date-palm traditions
UNESCO-listed knowledge and practices relating to date palm cultivation and use (2022, multinational). [17]
Henna practices
Henna rituals, aesthetics and social practices included in UNESCO intangible heritage (2024, multinational). [17]
Wedding traditions
Zaffa in the traditional wedding inscribed by UNESCO in 2025. [17]
World Heritage: Banc d’Arguin
Natural World Heritage property, inscribed 1989. [16]
World Heritage: Ancient Ksour
Cultural World Heritage property covering Ouadane, Chinguetti, Tichitt and Oualata, inscribed 1996. [16]
Total UNESCO World Heritage properties
Two inscribed properties as of November 2026. [16]
Environmental heritage challenge
Maintaining fragile marine ecosystems and desert architectural heritage under coastal and climate pressures. [16] [2]
Identity caution
No single 2026 ethnic-percentage table has been used: community identities and definitions vary, and unsupported percentages would be misleading. [6] [20]

Language, religion and rights: Mauritania’s constitution differentiates the official language (Arabic) from other national languages. The country is overwhelmingly Muslim, but this entry intentionally does not assign unsupported contemporary religious or ethnic percentages. Antislavery legal milestones and ongoing enforcement concerns are distinct. [6] [19] [20]

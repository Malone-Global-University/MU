Country Overview

Algeria, officially the People's Democratic Republic of Algeria, is a North African country bordering the Mediterranean Sea and extending southward across the Sahara. It is Africa's largest country by area. Its population is concentrated predominantly in the northern coastal and highland regions, while its desert interior connects Algeria with the Sahel. The modern republic emerged from a prolonged struggle against French colonial rule, culminating in independence in 1962. Algeria is a republic governed under a constitution revised in 2020, with substantial executive authority vested in the president. [3] [5]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
People's Democratic Republic of Algeria (الجمهورية الجزائرية الديمقراطية الشعبية; République algérienne démocratique et populaire)
Common name
Algeria (الجزائر; Algérie)
Capital
Algiers, on the Mediterranean coast
Continent / UN M49 subregion
Africa / Northern Africa [12]
Beginning of French conquest
1830; French colonial rule expanded across the territory over succeeding decades. [4]
Independence war began
November 1, 1954, conventionally marking the beginning of the Algerian Revolution / War of Independence. [3]
Évian Accords and ceasefire
Accords signed March 18, 1962; ceasefire took effect March 19, 1962. [3]
Independence referendum
July 1, 1962: voters approved self-determination and independence. [3]
Recognition and transfer of sovereignty
July 3, 1962: France formally recognized Algerian independence and sovereignty was transferred to the Algerian Provisional Executive. [4]
Independence Day
July 5, 1962: commemorated as the national independence date; not identical to the July 3 formal recognition date. [3]
Republic proclaimed
September 25, 1962: proclamation of the People's Democratic Republic of Algeria. [3]
Constitutional history
First post-independence constitution in 1963; present constitutional framework reflects the 2020 constitutional revision and subsequent implementing law. [5]
National commemorations
Independence Day, July 5; Anniversary of the Revolution, November 1. [2]
ISO 3166-1 codes
DZ (alpha-2); DZA (alpha-3); 012 (numeric). [12]
Internet country-code domain
.dz

Historical context: The region has a long history encompassing Amazigh (Berber) societies and polities, Phoenician and Carthaginian settlements, Roman-era Numidia, successive Islamic dynasties, Ottoman-period governance, and French colonial rule. The anti-colonial struggle transformed Algerian politics and society, while independence did not end debates over language, identity, political representation, and the relationship between state and society. Its history cannot be reduced to a single founding date.

Geography and Population

Total area
2,381,741 km² (approximately 919,595 sq. mi.). This is the commonly reported territorial area; measurement conventions for inland water may vary. [2]
Land area
Approximately 2.38 million km²; the reported total is overwhelmingly land, with relatively limited inland-water area. [2]
Population
47,435,312 (2025, World Bank population series). [1]
Annual population growth
Approximately 1.3% (2025, World Bank). [1]
Population density
Approximately 20 people per km², calculated by dividing the 2025 World Bank population by the reported national area; the distribution is highly uneven. [1] [2]
Population distribution
Most settlement and agricultural activity is in northern coastal zones, the Tell Atlas and nearby highlands; the Sahara is sparsely populated, with oasis towns and energy-producing centers
Major cities
Algiers, Oran, Constantine, Annaba, Blida, Sétif, Tlemcen, and Batna; population rankings depend on the definition of city versus metropolitan area
Adjacent countries and territories
Tunisia, Libya, Niger, Mali, Mauritania, and Morocco; also borders Western Sahara, a territory whose international status is disputed
Coastline and sea access
Mediterranean Sea along the northern coast; major commercial ports include Algiers, Oran, Béjaïa, and Skikda
Principal physical regions
Tell Atlas, High Plateaus, Saharan Atlas, and the Algerian Sahara, including the Ahaggar (Hoggar) and Tassili n'Ajjer regions
Highest mountain
Mount Tahat in the Ahaggar massif, approximately 2,908 metres above sea level (published elevations vary slightly)
Climate
Mediterranean along parts of the coast; semi-arid on the inland plateaus; arid and hyper-arid across much of the Sahara
Principal water systems
Seasonal wadis and northern river systems, including the Chélif; groundwater and Saharan aquifers are especially important inland
Life expectancy
Approximately 76 years at birth (World Bank 2024 series, rounded). [1]

Geographic comparability: Population density uses the 2025 international population estimate and the territorial area, not a separate official density survey. Algeria's vast desert area means this national average does not describe population concentration along the northern coast.

Economy and International Trade

Economic system
Upper-middle-income, mixed economy with significant state involvement, especially in oil and natural gas, alongside private industry, agriculture, manufacturing, trade, and services. [7]
Nominal GDP
Approximately US$287.03 billion (2025, current US dollars; World Bank). [1]
Nominal GDP per capita
Approximately US$6,051 (2025, current US dollars; World Bank). This is national output divided by population, not average salary or household income. [1]
Real GDP growth
3.8% (2025, World Bank series); the IMF's September 2026 review estimated 3.9% for the same year, reflecting differing release vintages. [1] [7]
Consumer-price inflation
1.4% (2025, annual consumer-price percentage change; World Bank). [1]
Unemployment
11.6% (2025, ILO modeled estimate displayed by the World Bank; not necessarily identical to a national labour-force survey result). [1]
Currency
Algerian dinar (DZD); common domestic abbreviation DA
Monetary authority
Bank of Algeria (Banque d'Algérie)
Principal economic activities
Petroleum and natural-gas extraction, refining, petrochemicals, power generation, construction, transportation, food production, manufacturing, agriculture, and services
Merchandise exports
US$51.857 billion (2024, WTO/UN Comtrade product-and-partner dataset; goods only). [6]
Merchandise imports
US$47.376 billion (2024, same WTO/UN Comtrade goods dataset). [6]
Merchandise trade balance
Approximately US$4.482 billion surplus (2024, calculated as goods exports minus goods imports within the same WTO dataset; not the full goods-and-services current account). [6]
Export dependence
Hydrocarbon products dominate merchandise exports by value: natural gas, crude oil, liquefied natural gas, refined petroleum, and liquefied petroleum gases. [6]
Leading goods export destination
European Union as a bloc, approximately 63.8% of 2024 merchandise exports; other destinations include Türkiye, the United States, China, Tunisia, and the United Kingdom. [6]
Leading goods import sources
European Union as a bloc (31.4% in 2024) and China (22.3%), followed by Brazil, Türkiye, and other suppliers. [6]
Economic policy priorities
Diversification beyond hydrocarbons, investment in agriculture, mining and manufacturing, and management of fiscal and external vulnerabilities. [7]

People and Society

National and official languages
Arabic and Tamazight (Amazigh), both recognized as national and official languages under the 2020 Constitution. [5]
Languages spoken in daily life
Algerian Arabic (Darja), Standard Arabic in formal contexts, Tamazight varieties, and French; other languages are also spoken within immigrant and educational communities
Major Tamazight varieties
Kabyle (Taqbaylit), Chaoui (Tachawit), Mozabite (Tumzabt), Tuareg varieties (including Tamasheq/Tamahaq), and other regional varieties; these are not a single uniform spoken dialect
Role of French
Widely used in commerce, some education, administration, and media, but not designated a national or official language by the Constitution. [2] [5]
State religion
Islam, under Article 2 of the Constitution. This is a statement about constitutional status, not a measure of individual religious identity. [5]
Religious composition
Overwhelmingly Muslim, predominantly Sunni; the Mzab region includes an established Ibadi Muslim community. Estimates of religious percentages vary and are not based on a recent comprehensive religion census. [2] [15]
Other religious communities
Smaller Christian, Jewish, Ahmadi, and Shia Muslim communities, alongside other religious and nonreligious individuals; published population estimates are uncertain. [15]
Principal cultural traditions
Amazigh and Arab cultural heritages, Saharan and Tuareg traditions, Andalusi musical forms, raï music, Islamic and regional architecture, and varied local cuisines
Urban and rural society
Large metropolitan populations in the north coexist with smaller highland settlements, oasis communities, and sparsely populated Saharan districts
Education and healthcare
National public education and healthcare systems are supplemented by private providers; access and service provision differ among urban, rural, and remote regions
Population identities
Algerian national identity encompasses multiple Amazigh, Arab, regional, linguistic, and other backgrounds; linguistic use is not a straightforward measure of ancestry or identity

Demographic interpretation: Algeria's linguistic, cultural, and religious classifications are not interchangeable. Arabic and Tamazight are constitutionally official, while spoken varieties and actual language use are more diverse. Precise percentages for religious groups should not be presented as current census results without an appropriately dated source.

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Unitary republic with a president and prime minister; commonly characterized as a presidential or presidential-parliamentary system. [5] [10]
Official constitutional designation
People's Democratic Republic of Algeria; Article 1 describes the republic as one and indivisible. [5]
Foundational law
Constitution of Algeria, as revised by the 2020 constitutional referendum and promulgation, with later legislation governing its implementation. [5]
Head of state
President Abdelmadjid Tebboune (in office since December 19, 2019; re-elected in September 2024; status verified November 4, 2026). [2]
Head of government
Prime Minister Sifi Ghrieb (in office since August 2025; status verified November 4, 2026). [2]
Executive branch
President, prime minister, Council of Ministers, ministries, and national executive agencies operating under constitutional and statutory provisions
Parliament
Bicameral parliament: National People's Assembly (Assemblée populaire nationale) and Council of the Nation (Conseil de la nation). [10] [11]
National People's Assembly
407 statutory seats; members are directly elected, with five-year parliamentary terms. [10]
Council of the Nation
174 statutory seats in the parliamentary reference data consulted, including indirectly elected members and presidential appointees; the chamber's makeup may require updating as the 2026 wilaya reorganization is implemented. [11]
Judiciary
Supreme Court, Council of State, Constitutional Court, and lower courts within the constitutional judicial framework
Administrative divisions
69 wilayas (provinces) and 1,541 communes under territorial-division legislation published in April 2026; transfers of responsibilities from predecessor wilayas were scheduled through December 31, 2026. [9]
Political organization
Multiparty elections and representative institutions operate within a system featuring extensive presidential authority; the formal constitutional model should be distinguished from assessments of political competition

Administrative transition: The 2026 law specifies 69 wilayas, replacing the former 58-wilaya structure. Parliamentary reference figures for the upper chamber may reflect its pre-transition statutory size. The two figures should not be assumed to have been updated in tandem. Source [9]; Source [11].

International Profile

United Nations
Member since October 8, 1962. [13]
African Union
Member; Algeria was among the founding participants in the Organization of African Unity on May 25, 1963, the AU's predecessor. [14]
Major international affiliations
United Nations, African Union, League of Arab States, Organization of Islamic Cooperation, OPEC, Arab Maghreb Union, and African Continental Free Trade Area framework
Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries
OPEC member since 1969; hydrocarbon production and exports remain important to Algeria's international economic relationships. [16]
World Trade Organization status
Not a WTO member as of the reference date; WTO trade-statistics pages include nonmember economies. [6]
Regional geographic setting
Maghreb and Mediterranean North Africa; also a Saharan and Sahel-bordering state
Principal external trade relationship
European Union is the leading destination bloc for recorded merchandise exports in the 2024 WTO dataset. [6]
Regional diplomatic issues
Relations with Mediterranean and Sahel neighbors, energy trade, migration, security, and the disputed status of Western Sahara are recurrent international policy concerns
Standard time
UTC+1 (Central European Time, used year-round without seasonal clock changes)
International dialing code

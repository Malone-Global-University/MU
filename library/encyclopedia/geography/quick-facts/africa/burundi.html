Country Overview

Burundi is a landlocked republic in East Africa’s Great Lakes region, bordered by Rwanda, Tanzania, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Gitega serves as the political capital, while Bujumbura is the main commercial center. A precolonial kingdom, it came under German and later Belgian rule before gaining independence on July 1, 1962. It became a republic in 1966. Burundi’s economy depends heavily on agriculture, local commerce, and export products such as gold, coffee, and tea. President Évariste Ndayishimiye heads the country under its 2018 constitutional system. The 2025 administrative reorganization established five provinces. [16] [13] [1] [8] [15]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Burundi; République du Burundi; Republika y’u Burundi. [7]
Common name
Burundi; residents are called Burundians.
Capital
Gitega, the political and administrative capital since the December 2018 decision; Bujumbura remains the major economic and commercial center. [7] [19]
Continent / UN M49 subregion
Africa / Eastern Africa; country code 108 in UN M49. [6]
Historical state
The Kingdom of Burundi existed before European colonization, with a mwami (king) and long-established regional institutions. [13]
German colonial period
Incorporated into German East Africa in the late nineteenth century; Belgian forces occupied the territory during the First World War. [20]
Belgian administration
Administered as part of Ruanda-Urundi under the League of Nations mandate system and later a United Nations trusteeship, before independence. [20]
Independence
July 1, 1962, from Belgian trusteeship; Burundi became independent as a kingdom. [13]
Republic established
November 28, 1966, when the monarchy was abolished and a republic proclaimed. [20]
Civil-war and peace-process milestones
The 1993 assassination of elected president Melchior Ndadaye preceded prolonged violence; the Arusha Peace and Reconciliation Agreement was signed in August 2000. [13]
2005 constitutional and institutional transition
The 2005 constitutional framework and the peace process supported a new political order after years of civil war. [13]
Current constitutional text
Constitution promulgated June 7, 2018, following the May 2018 referendum; subsequent legislation governs the territorial organization. [12]
2020 presidential transition
Évariste Ndayishimiye became president in June 2020 after the election and death of outgoing president Pierre Nkurunziza. [9]
2025 legislative election
National Assembly election held June 5, 2025; CNDD-FDD won all 100 directly elected seats, according to the Inter-Parliamentary Union. [10]
National day
Independence Day, July 1.
ISO 3166-1 codes
BI (alpha-2); BDI (alpha-3); 108 (numeric). [7] [6]
Internet country-code domain
.bi
International dialing code
+257

Historical context: The monarchy, colonial administration, independence, military rule, mass violence, civil war, peace settlements and constitutional transitions are different stages of the country’s state formation. Its national founding should therefore not be reduced to an undifferentiated date. [13]

Borders
Rwanda to the north, Tanzania to the east and south, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo to the west; Lake Tanganyika lies along the southwest. [16]
Landlocked status
No ocean coastline; Lake Tanganyika supports domestic and regional water transport. [16]
Principal physical geography
Highlands and a rolling central plateau, western Rift Valley lowlands, Lake Tanganyika shore, river valleys and wetlands. [16]
Major water systems
Lake Tanganyika, the Rusizi River, the Ruvubu River, and tributaries connecting to the wider Nile and Congo drainage regions.
Climate
Tropical highland conditions across much of the country, with warmer lake-shore lowlands and alternating wetter and drier seasons. [19]
Major urban centers
Bujumbura, Gitega, Ngozi, Rumonge and Muyinga; city rankings and boundaries vary by reference year. [19]
Administrative divisions
Five provinces and 42 communes in the reorganized system effective July 2025, replacing 18 provinces and 119 communes. [15] [2]
Provinces
Buhumuza, Bujumbura, Burunga, Butanyerera and Gitega. [2]
Population by province (2024)
Buhumuza 2,052,261; Bujumbura 3,353,555; Burunga 2,118,551; Butanyerera 2,530,206; Gitega 2,278,215. [2]
Environmental pressures
High settlement density, soil erosion, deforestation, land scarcity, watershed stress and rainfall variability affect agriculture and livelihoods. [15]

Economy and International Trade

Economic system
Low-income mixed economy with extensive smallholder agriculture, informal trade, domestic services, public activity and a developing mining sector. [15]
Nominal GDP
Approximately US$3.365 billion (2025, World Bank; GDP at current US-dollar prices). [1]
Nominal GDP per capita
US$233.8 (2025, World Bank, current dollars); GDP divided by population, not the average wage or median income. [1]
Real GDP growth
4.2% (2025, World Bank annual real GDP series). [1]
Consumer price inflation
34.1% (2025 annual consumer price inflation, World Bank series). [1]
Currency
Burundian franc (BIF, often abbreviated FBu); notes and coins issued by the Banque de la République du Burundi. [18]
Central bank
Banque de la République du Burundi (BRB). [18]
Economic sectors
Agriculture, trading and other services, mining, construction and industry; World Bank overview reports services as the largest GDP component. [15]
Agriculture and livelihoods
Subsistence and commercial farming are central to employment, with food crops and export cash crops; sector shares differ between employment and GDP. [15]
Main food crops
Cassava, bananas and plantains, maize, beans, sweet potatoes, rice and other crops, with regional differences in production.
Important cash crops
Coffee and tea, complemented by other commercial crops. [15] [5]
Mining and mineral exports
Gold is a major recorded export product; broader mining development faces investment, infrastructure and foreign-exchange constraints. [5] [15]
Merchandise exports
US$174 million (2024, UNCTADstat merchandise trade series). [4]
Merchandise imports
US$1.023 billion (2024, same UNCTADstat merchandise trade series). [4]
Merchandise trade balance
Approximately US$849 million deficit (2024, merchandise exports minus imports; excludes services). [4]
Services exports
Approximately US$126 million (2024, UNCTADstat estimate). [4]
Services imports
Approximately US$379 million (2024, UNCTADstat estimate). [4]
Reported product-level merchandise exports
Gold, unroasted coffee, black tea, cigarettes and wheat flour were among leading products in WTO/UN Comtrade’s 2023 breakdown. [5]
Leading 2023 merchandise destinations
United Arab Emirates (37.0%), Democratic Republic of the Congo (20.4%), European Union as a bloc (7.3%), Switzerland (6.4%) and Sudan (4.0%). [5]
Trade statistics qualification
The 2023 WTO product/destination breakdown and 2024 UNCTAD merchandise totals come from different reporting years and are not one combined ranking. [5] [4]
Principal macroeconomic constraints
Foreign-exchange shortages, fuel scarcity, high inflation, agriculture’s exposure to weather, and transport costs on landlocked trade routes. [15]

People and Society

Demonym
Burundian.
National language
Kirundi (Rundi), a Bantu language widely spoken throughout the country; the 2018 Constitution names it as the national language. [12]
Official-language status
Kirundi and French are consistently identified by official regional and foreign-government sources; a 2025 UK reference also lists English as official following a 2014 change. The 2018 Constitution permits languages to be designated by statute, so listings vary by authority. [12] [7] [16]
Additional languages used
English and Kiswahili (Swahili), especially in regional commerce or education; language use varies across urban and rural communities. [7] [16]
Major communities
Hutu, Tutsi and Twa/Batwa peoples, as well as other residents; avoid treating historical ethnic categories as fixed measures of individual identity. [13]
Christian religious communities
Roman Catholic, Protestant, Seventh-day Adventist, Anglican and other Christian communities are present. [14]
Roman Catholic affiliation (historical census)
62.0% in the 2008 national census, as reproduced in the U.S. State Department’s 2023 report; not a current 2026 estimate. [14]
Protestant affiliation (historical census)
21.6% Protestant, plus 2.3% Seventh-day Adventist recorded separately in the same 2008 census summary. [14]
Muslim affiliation (historical census)
2.5% in the 2008 census; the State Department also notes some Muslim community estimates are higher. These are not contemporary measurements. [14]
Indigenous religious affiliations (historical census)
3.7% in the 2008 census, with other spiritual practices potentially overlapping Christian or Muslim identities. [14]
No religious affiliation (historical census)
6.1% in the 2008 census summary; survey categories and interpretation can change over time. [14]
Constitutional religious status
Secular republic with formal constitutional protections relating to religion and belief. [12]
Life expectancy
Approximately 64 years at birth (2024, World Bank; rounded). [1]
Age structure
Young population; INSBU’s 2024 census reports a substantial share under 15, with figures sensitive to source population definitions. [2]
Settlement pattern
Predominantly rural in the 2024 census, with strong concentration in agrarian highlands and an urban commercial hub in Bujumbura. [3]
Livelihoods
Smallholder agriculture, local trade and services support many households; land access and productivity are central to the development challenge. [15]
Education and language
Education operates in a multilingual environment in which Kirundi is central and French and English have roles in schools, administration or regional communication. [7]
Cultural traditions
Kirundi-language oral literature, drumming, dance, woven and other artisanal traditions, and shared Great Lakes cultural influences.
Religion data limitation
The percentages above deliberately retain the source year 2008; do not use them as shares of the population estimated for 2025. [14]

Religious data limitation: Religion shares are dated 2008 because that is the national census underlying the cited 2023 State Department report. They should not be represented as 2025 or 2026 proportions. Official-language descriptions also vary by authority; the constitutional rule and the relevant reference differences are identified rather than silently reconciled. [14] [12] [7]

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Unitary presidential constitutional republic; the 2018 Constitution formally provides the foundational governing framework. [12]
Head of state
President Évariste Ndayishimiye, in office since June 2020; confirmed on official and international government references available in 2026. [9] [19]
Vice president
Prosper Bazombanza (CIA Chiefs of State listing updated May 5, 2026). [9]
Prime minister
Nestor Ntahontuye, appointed August 5, 2025 and listed by the Burundian presidency in 2026. [8] [9]
Executive branch
President, vice president, prime minister and cabinet, with executive authority distributed under the constitutional order. [12] [8]
Legislature
Bicameral Parliament: National Assembly and Senate. [10] [11]
National Assembly (2025 election cycle)
111 seats reported by the Inter-Parliamentary Union: 100 directly elected members, eight CNDD-FDD co-opted members and three co-opted Twa members; 108 total CNDD-FDD. [10]
June 2025 Assembly election
CNDD-FDD won all 100 directly elected seats according to the IPU; this is a descriptive result, not an endorsement of electoral conditions. [10]
Senate (2025 election cycle)
13 members: ten indirectly elected senators (two from each of five provinces) and three co-opted Twa senators, per the IPU’s August 2025 summary. [11]
Governing political party
National Council for the Defense of Democracy–Forces for the Defense of Democracy (CNDD-FDD). [10] [15]
Judiciary
Constitutional Court, Supreme Court and other courts under the constitution and national legal framework. [12]
Territorial administration
Five provinces and 42 communes effective July 2025, with further local levels including zones and collines (hills). [15] [12]
2025 administrative reform
Replaced the former 18-province and 119-commune structures with a consolidated five-province, 42-commune organization. [15]
Constitutional political status
The constitution describes the country as independent, sovereign, secular, democratic and unitary; this is the legal formulation, distinct from assessments of electoral competitiveness. [12]
National election cycle
The 2025 legislature was elected for a five-year cycle; the World Bank country overview describes the next presidential election as scheduled for 2027. [10] [15]
Government reference cutoff
Leadership identified from official presidency pages, dated CIA cabinet listing and October 2026 France Diplomatie; personnel may change between reviews. [8] [9] [19]

Country Overview

South Africa, officially the Republic of South Africa, occupies the southernmost part of the African continent, with coasts on the Atlantic and Indian Oceans. It has three traditional capital functions: Pretoria (executive), Cape Town (legislative) and Bloemfontein (judicial), although the Constitutional Court sits in Johannesburg. The Union of South Africa formed in 1910, a republic was established in 1961, and universal-suffrage democratic elections in 1994 ended the apartheid system. South Africa is governed under the 1996 Constitution by President Cyril Ramaphosa and Deputy President Paul Mashatile. Its diversified economy includes finance, mining, services, automotive manufacturing, agriculture and tourism. [8] [10] [11] [12] [14] [15] [24]

Sources and Methodology

Population and macroeconomics: The six-card summary uses the World Bank’s internationally harmonised 2025 population, GDP and GDP-per-capita figures. South Africa’s own Statistics South Africa mid-year estimates and its 2022 census are shown separately because reference dates and estimation systems differ. Annual real GDP growth, inflation and the modeled labor-force unemployment rate are explicitly dated 2025; Q2 2026 national unemployment comes from the Quarterly Labour Force Survey. GDP per capita is output divided by a population denominator, not median or average wages.

Territory and political history: The headline land area follows the country’s 1,220,813 km² provincial administrative-area series; other official publications use approximately 1,219,602 km² or different totals. The 1910 formation of the Union, the 1961 establishment of a republic, the first inclusive democratic national election in 1994, and the effective date of the 1996 Constitution in 1997 are different milestones. Pretoria, Cape Town and Bloemfontein have conventionally distinct capital functions, while the Constitutional Court sits in Johannesburg.

Government, society, and trade: The current president and deputy president are checked against October 2026 executive announcements. South African Sign Language became the twelfth official language by constitutional amendment in 2023. Census 2022 religious-response denominators exclude certain not-applicable or unspecified answers; published percentage series can differ depending on denominator. Detailed 2025 merchandise trade uses WTO/UN Comtrade USD values and a distinct SARS ZAR-denominated preliminary series with different geographic coverage. World Heritage inscriptions are not identical to a tentative list or other UNESCO heritage programs.

Editorial cutoff: Contemporary officeholders and institutions are described as verified on November 5, 2026. Each statistical record retains its own observation date; dates of web publication and census enumeration are not silently substituted for those dates.

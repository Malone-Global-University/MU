Country Overview

Senegal, officially the Republic of Senegal, is a coastal country in West Africa with its capital at Dakar. The country emerged from French colonial rule through the short-lived Mali Federation in 1960. It marks April 4 as Independence Day, while its separate national sovereignty following withdrawal from the federation dates to August 20, 1960. Senegal is a secular presidential republic. President Bassirou Diomaye Faye has served since 2024; Prime Minister Ahmadou Alhaminou Mohamed Lo took office in May 2026. Senegal’s economy includes agriculture, commerce, transport, fishing, gold and phosphate-related production, alongside crude oil and natural-gas exports. [9] [10] [8] [7] [5]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Senegal (French: République du Sénégal). [11]
Common name
Senegal.
Demonym
Senegalese.
Capital
Dakar, on the Cap-Vert peninsula on the Atlantic coast. [15]
Continent
Africa.
United Nations subregion
Western Africa. [15]
Geographical position
The Atlantic-facing westernmost mainland country of Africa. [19]
Name origin
The country takes its name from the Senegal River, which forms much of its northern border.
Precolonial historical setting
Wolof, Serer, Fulani, Mandinka and other societies developed diverse political and commercial traditions across the region. [20]
Historical polities
Ghana and Mali imperial networks influenced the region; Jolof and other Senegambian kingdoms shaped local history. [20]
Atlantic trade period
Coastal commercial posts and international trade expanded from early European contact; the slave trade profoundly affected coastal societies. [20] [16]
French colonial rule
France consolidated its colonial administration over the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, with Dakar an important regional center. [20]
Autonomous republic milestone
November 15, 1958: Senegal became a republic within the French Community. [9]
Independence-transfer agreement
April 4, 1960: agreements concerning the transfer of sovereignty to the Mali Federation; later adopted as Senegal’s Independence Day. [9] [20]
Mali Federation independence
June 20, 1960: the Mali Federation, combining Senegal and French Sudan, obtained independence from France. [20] [10]
Separate Senegalese independence
August 20, 1960: Senegal withdrew from the Mali Federation and established separate sovereignty. [9] [10]
National Independence Day
April 4, annually; distinct from the August 20 separation of the independent Republic from the Mali Federation. [9]
First president
Léopold Sédar Senghor, 1960–1980. [9] [20]
Subsequent presidents
Abdou Diouf (1981–2000), Abdoulaye Wade (2000–2012), Macky Sall (2012–2024), and Bassirou Diomaye Faye (from 2024). [9] [8]
Multiparty electoral transitions
Peaceful presidential transfers of power occurred in 2000, 2012 and 2024. [9] [19]
National flag
Three vertical green, yellow and red bands with a green five-pointed star centered on the yellow band.
Official language
French. [11]
Currency
West African CFA franc, ISO code XOF. [15]
International telephone code
+221.
Country-code top-level domain

ISO codes
SN (alpha-2), SEN (alpha-3), 686 (numeric). [15]
Time zone
Greenwich Mean Time (GMT), UTC+0; no ordinary daylight-saving change.

Important dates: April 4, 1960 is the national independence commemoration. The Mali Federation became independent June 20, and the separate Republic of Senegal emerged August 20, 1960. [9] [10]

Geography and Population

196,722 km² (approximately 75,955 sq. mi.), Senegalese government geographic series. [14]

Land area versus total area
Land-only measurements are smaller than the total-area figure and should be labeled explicitly.
Atlantic coastline
Approximately 531 km, depending on mapping scale and coastal measurement method.
Land borders
Mauritania to the north, Mali to the east, Guinea and Guinea-Bissau to the south, and The Gambia within the west-central region. [19]
Relationship to The Gambia
The Gambia is a separate sovereign state, bordered by Senegal except for its Atlantic outlet; it is not a Senegalese region.
Regional location
The Sahel–Sudanian transition with Atlantic littoral zones, Senegal River valley, central savannas and more humid southern Casamance.
Principal rivers
Senegal River, Gambia River, Casamance River and Sine–Saloum estuarine waterways.
Major lake
Lac de Guiers, a major freshwater reservoir in northwestern Senegal.
Northern physical setting
Semi-arid Sahelian lands and Senegal River valley.
Southern physical setting
More humid woodlands, agricultural landscapes and Casamance wetlands.
Climate
Primarily tropical with pronounced wet and dry seasons; rainfall generally increases southward.
Rainy season
Roughly June to October in much of the country, with regional variation.
Major climate hazards
Drought, heat, urban flooding, coastal erosion, saltwater intrusion and changing rainfall patterns.

People and Society

Official language
French, under Article 1 of the Constitution. [11]
Constitutionally named national languages
Diola/Jola, Malinké/Mandinka, Pular/Pulaar, Sérère/Serer, Soninké and Wolof, plus other languages subsequently codified. [11]
Wolof
Widely used as a common language for everyday communication, commerce and urban life.
Pulaar
Used especially among Fulani and Toucouleur communities across northern and inland Senegal.
Serer
Spoken in parts of west-central Senegal and associated with Serer cultural traditions.
Jola (Diola)
Spoken in parts of the Casamance region and neighboring Guinea-Bissau.
Mandinka (Malinké)
Used in sections of southeastern Senegal and across the wider Manding-speaking region.
Soninke
Spoken in communities historically associated with the Senegal River valley and Sahelian trade networks.
Other regional languages
Balanta, Manjak, Bassari, Bedik and others; language names and spelling vary by standardization system.
Language status distinction
French is the single constitutional official language; the recognized national languages are not all constitutionally official. [11]
Religious profile
Islam predominates, alongside Christian and other communities. [17]
Reported Muslim population share
About 97.2%, as quoted in the U.S. State Department 2023 report from government statistics; not a new 2025 religious census. [17]
Reported Christian population share
About 2.7% in the same cited demographic estimate; mainly Catholic and Protestant communities. [17]
Major Islamic traditions
Most Muslims are Sunni, and Sufi brotherhoods play a significant role in social and religious life. [17]
Major Sufi orders
Muridiyya, Tijaniyya, Qadiriyya and Layene traditions.
Touba
A major religious center especially associated with the Muridiyya brotherhood.
Religious status of state
Constitutionally secular; freedom of belief and equal protection are guaranteed. [11] [17]
Historical religious coexistence
Muslim and Christian communities have longstanding shared civic and social traditions.
Ethnolinguistic communities
Wolof, Fulani/Peul/Toucouleur, Serer, Mandinka, Jola and many other communities; identity labels are not interchangeable with language proficiency.
Life expectancy at birth
Approximately 69 years (2024, World Bank). [1]
Fertility context
National fertility has fallen over recent decades; ANSD reports about 4.2 children per woman for 2023 in its census-derived series. [2]
Education system
Public and private schools provide primary, secondary and tertiary education, with French prominent in formal instruction.
Major higher-education institution
Université Cheikh Anta Diop de Dakar (UCAD).
Other major institutions
Gaston Berger University (Saint-Louis) and universities in other regions.
National culture
Music, oral literature, poetry, cinema, religious scholarship and textile traditions are central cultural expressions.
Music
Mbalax, sabar drumming, kora traditions and contemporary popular music.
Cuisine
Ceebu jën (thieboudienne), yassa, mafé and other dishes featuring rice, fish, vegetables and sauces.
Ceebu jën cultural importance
Rice-and-fish cuisine associated with Saint-Louis and Senegalese culinary heritage.
Traditional clothing
Boubou and other tailored, embroidered West African garments.
Popular sport
Football (soccer), with national and club competitions widely followed.
Wrestling
Laamb (Senegalese wrestling) is a prominent sporting and cultural tradition.
Historical social concepts
Teranga, commonly associated with hospitality, is an important expression in national cultural identity.
UNESCO World Heritage properties
Seven inscribed sites in the UNESCO country listing: five cultural and two natural. [16]
UNESCO cultural—Island of Gorée
Historical Atlantic island associated with the memory of slavery and exchange (1978 inscription). [16]
UNESCO cultural—Island of Saint-Louis
Historic colonial and commercial urban landscape (2000). [16]
UNESCO cultural—Stone Circles
Stone Circles of Senegambia, shared with The Gambia (2006). [16]
UNESCO cultural—Saloum Delta
Cultural landscape of estuarine waterways, shell mounds and communities (2011). [16]
UNESCO cultural—Bassari Country
Bassari, Fula and Bedik Cultural Landscapes (2012). [16]
UNESCO natural—Djoudj
Djoudj National Bird Sanctuary (1981). [16]
UNESCO natural—Niokolo-Koba
Niokolo-Koba National Park (1981). [16]
Heritage-count caution
The UNESCO state-party total is seven, including the transboundary Senegambian stone-circle site; national parks alone do not exhaust the list. [16]
Social-development priorities
Skills, education quality, youth employment, healthcare access, resilient agriculture and urban infrastructure. [19]

Language and religion: French is the constitutional official language, whereas Wolof, Pulaar, Serer, Jola, Mandinka and Soninke are among the nationally recognized languages. The quoted religious percentages reflect dated source estimates. [11] [17]

Sources and Methodology

The profile prioritizes Senegalese government agencies, international statistical bodies, current official government records, and the UNESCO World Heritage Centre. Population estimates, census enumerations, GDP and trade data are dated by their respective source years. The page's verification date does not mean every statistic describes 2026. The 2025 ANSD trade release and 2024 WTO profile use different reporting methods and currencies.

For independence, this profile distinguishes the April 4 national holiday, June 20, 1960 independence of the Mali Federation, and Senegal's withdrawal from that federation on August 20, 1960. For population, it distinguishes 2023 final census data, ANSD 2025 demographic projections and World Bank 2025 estimates. Reported 2023 religion figures are not represented as a new 2025 census.

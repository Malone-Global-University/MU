Country Overview

Zimbabwe, officially the Republic of Zimbabwe, is a landlocked country in Southern Africa between the Zambezi and Limpopo river systems. Harare is its capital. Independent since 18 April 1980, the republic is governed under a constitution adopted in 2013. Its society recognizes 16 national languages under the Constitution, and its economy combines mining, commercial and smallholder agriculture, tourism, services and industry. Great Zimbabwe, the Matobo Hills and Victoria Falls are among five UNESCO World Heritage properties. President Emmerson Mnangagwa and Vice Presidents Constantino Chiwenga and Kembo Mohadi are listed by Parliament at the edition date. [11] [7] [8] [10] [2]

Official name
Republic of Zimbabwe. [3] [7]

Zimbabwe. [3]
Continent
Africa; a landlocked state in Southern Africa. The United Nations statistical classification places Zimbabwe in Eastern Africa. [11] [3]
Capital
Harare. [3]

Harare; the capital is also the principal metropolitan center. [3] [4]
Former colonial name
Southern Rhodesia; the name Rhodesia was used by the internationally unrecognized white-minority administration after its 1965 unilateral declaration of independence. [11] [7]
Independence
18 April 1980, following negotiations, an internationally supervised transition and liberation struggles against minority rule. [11] [18]

18 April (national public holiday). [11]
State system
Unitary sovereign republic with a presidential executive under the constitutional framework. [7]

Emmerson Dambudzo Mnangagwa, in office since November 2017 and elected for the current presidential term in 2023. [8] [11]
Vice presidents
Constantino G. D. N. Chiwenga and Kembo C. D. Mohadi as listed by Parliament at the November 2026 review. [8]

Constitution adopted in 2013; subsequent amendments have affected institutional and electoral arrangements. [7] [9]

16 under section 6 of the Constitution, including English, Shona, Ndebele and sign language. [7]

Zimbabwean. [3] [11]

National flag
Seven horizontal bands of green, gold, red, black, red, gold and green, with a white hoist triangle bearing the Zimbabwe Bird and red star. [15]
National emblem

National motto

National anthem

The country’s modern name refers to Great Zimbabwe, a major precolonial stone-built urban and political center. [10]
Historical framing
Zimbabwe’s history extends well before colonial rule, including Great Zimbabwe and Khami, followed by settler colonialism, anticolonial resistance, and post-1980 independent statehood. [10] [11]

Geography and Population

Total surface area
390,757 km² in the UN statistical and ZIMSTAT convention; national publications sometimes use a different area calculation. [3] [4]
Landlocked status
No sovereign seacoast; access to ports depends on transport corridors across neighboring countries. [11]
Land neighbors
Zambia (north), Mozambique (east), South Africa (south) and Botswana (west). [11]
Major rivers
Zambezi along much of the northern boundary; Limpopo near the southern boundary; Save/Sabi, Runde and Manyame in the interior. [11] [3]
Major lake and reservoir
Lake Kariba, a large reservoir on the Zambezi shared with Zambia. [11] [2]
Major waterfall
Victoria Falls on the Zambezi at the Zambia–Zimbabwe border, part of the transboundary UNESCO property Mosi-oa-Tunya / Victoria Falls. [10]
General terrain
A central plateau or highveld, flanked by lower-lying lowveld areas and mountain terrain in the east. [11]
Eastern Highlands
Mountain belt along the Mozambique boundary, including the Nyanga and Chimanimani ranges. [11]
Climate
Generally tropical to subtropical, moderated by elevation; a summer wet season and relatively dry, cooler winter. [11] [12]
Rainfall seasonality
Most seasonal rain falls approximately November–March; conditions vary sharply by elevation and region. [12]
Drought exposure
El Niño and other climate variations can sharply affect maize, pasture, water supply and hydropower reliability. [2] [12]
Population estimate, 2025
16,950,795 (World Bank); an estimate for 2025, not a census count. [1]
Final census population, 2022
15,178,957 (ZIMSTAT final census, as reflected in the UN demographic database). [4] [21]
Preliminary census count, 2022
15,178,979 was published in the earlier preliminary release; the final result is 22 lower. [4] [21]
2022 census reference night
Night of 20 April 2022; the census used a de facto enumeration approach. [4]
2022 census sex distribution
7,287,922 males (48.0%) and 7,891,035 females (52.0%). [4]
2022 census private households
3,818,734 private households; average private-household size about four. [4]
2022 rural–urban distribution
61.4% rural and 38.6% urban in the final census thematic reporting. [4]
2022 census population density
Approximately 39 people per km² based on national area and census population. [4]
Population growth, 2025
1.9% annual growth (World Bank estimate). [1]
Net migration, 2025
Approximately −54,555 (World Bank estimate; a net flow, not total emigrants). [1]
Most populous province in 2022
Harare, 2,427,231 people, 16.0% of the final census total. [4]
Second-most populous province in 2022
Manicaland, 2,037,703 people (13.4%). [4]
Provincial units
10 provinces, including the two metropolitan provinces of Harare and Bulawayo. [4] [7]
Province names
Bulawayo, Harare, Manicaland, Mashonaland Central, Mashonaland East, Mashonaland West, Masvingo, Matabeleland North, Matabeleland South and Midlands. [4]
Other major cities
Bulawayo, Mutare, Gweru, Masvingo, Kwekwe and Chitungwiza are important urban centers. [4]
Protected landscapes
Mana Pools and the Zambezi valley, Matobo Hills, and the Victoria Falls landscape are among internationally recognized protected or cultural landscapes. [10]
Forest cover
35.7% of land area in the World Bank 2024 forest-area indicator. [17]

Economy and International Trade

Economic structure
Mixed economy with major mining, agriculture, wholesale and retail trade, financial services, manufacturing, tourism and public services. [2] [12]
Nominal GDP, 2025
US$51.22 billion (World Bank, current US dollars, rounded). [1]
GDP per capita, 2025
US$3,021.4 (World Bank, current US dollars; not a median wage measure). [1]
Real GDP growth, 2025
8.1% in the World Bank indicator dataset; its country narrative gives 8.3% from a different reporting vintage. [1] [2]
Growth drivers in 2025
Rebound in agriculture after drought and higher mining output/prices. [2] [12]
2026 GDP-growth outlook
IMF April 2026 WEO projected around 5% real GDP growth for 2026; a forecast, not a final outcome. [19]
Current monetary unit
Zimbabwe Gold, generally called ZiG (ISO currency code ZWG); a domestic monetary unit alongside permitted foreign currencies. [3] [6] [23]
Foreign currencies in use
The US dollar and other permitted foreign currencies remain legal tender under the multicurrency framework. [6]
Multicurrency-system period
Zimbabwe’s 2025 Reserve Bank guidance cites legal permission for foreign and local currency use through 31 December 2030; policy may be amended. [6]
Currency transition
ZiG was introduced on 5 April 2024 to replace the prior Zimbabwe-dollar unit; the ZWG currency code became effective on 25 June 2024 and superseded ZWL. [23] [3]
Currency-pricing caution
US-dollar and ZiG inflation, exchange rates and balance measures differ; figures should be identified by currency and measurement period. [6] [2]
Price stabilization, August 2026
World Bank overview reports year-on-year local-currency inflation of 2.9% in August 2026; it is not the 2026 annual-average blended inflation figure. [2]
Modeled unemployment, 2025
9.3% under the World Bank/ILO modeled labor-force series. [1]
Zimbabwe statistical unemployment metric
ZIMSTAT reports 20.7% unemployment among persons aged 16+ in its second-quarter 2025 Quarterly Labour Force Survey; definitions and samples differ from the ILO modeled series. [22] [1]
2024 goods exports
US$7,434.4 million (WTO/UN Comtrade, merchandise only). [5]
2024 goods imports
US$9,533.5 million (WTO/UN Comtrade, merchandise only). [5]
2024 goods-trade balance
Approximately US$2,099.1 million merchandise deficit (exports minus imports); excludes service and financial flows. [5]

South Africa (39.5%) and China (13.9%) in the recorded WTO goods series. [5]
Major 2024 imports
Petroleum products, maize, soya-bean oil, electricity, vehicles, pharmaceuticals and other manufactured goods. [5]
Mineral and metal sectors
Gold, platinum-group metals, nickel, chrome, diamonds, coal and lithium are important to the broader resource economy. [2] [11]
Mining export mix
Gold, nickel mattes, nickel ores, ferrochrome and industrial diamonds all appear among the leading 2024 HS product categories. [5]
Agricultural staples
Maize, sorghum, millet and wheat, together with livestock and vegetables. [12]
Major agricultural cash crops
Tobacco, cotton, sugar, horticultural crops and other market-oriented agricultural production. [12] [5]
2025 maize rebound
FAO reported a marked recovery after the 2024 drought, with about 1.82 million tonnes of maize in the 2025 assessment. [12]
2026 maize production assessment
FEWS NET cites a government estimate near 2.35 million tonnes for 2026; differing crop assessments are dated separately. [13]
Drought-related import volatility
The 2024 drought substantially increased staple-food import demand, including maize. [12] [5]
Electricity generation
Important sources include Kariba hydropower and Hwange thermal generation, alongside developing solar and other resources. [2] [17]
Electricity access, 2024
62.0% of the population in the World Bank access-to-electricity series. [17]
Electricity constraints
Power-system reliability is affected by hydrology, aging infrastructure, generation availability and maintenance investment. [2]
Forest-area indicator, 2024
35.7% of land area, subject to World Bank dataset definitions. [17]
Transport corridors
Trade flows connect with South Africa, Mozambique, Botswana and Zambia via road and rail routes serving regional ports and markets. [11] [5]
Tourism activities
Victoria Falls, wildlife and national parks, archaeological heritage and highland landscapes are major attractions. [10] [11]
Economic development concerns
Public debt, arrears, large informal activity, poverty, infrastructure investment and climate exposure remain key constraints. [2]
2025 public-debt context
World Bank overview reports total public debt around 38% of GDP in 2025, while also characterizing debt sustainability and arrears as material problems. [2]
Economic-data comparability
World Bank and UN GDP estimates for the same year may use different vintages, conversions or statistical bases; do not mix source series to compute growth. [1] [3]

People and Society

Constitutional languages
Sixteen officially recognized: Chewa, Chibarwe, English, Kalanga, Koisan, Nambya, Ndau, Ndebele, Shangani, Shona, sign language, Sotho, Tonga, Tswana, Venda and Xhosa. [7]
Widely spoken languages
Shona and Ndebele are major languages in everyday life; English is widely used in national administration and formal business. [7] [11]
Language rights
The Constitution requires equitable treatment of officially recognized languages and consideration of people’s language preferences. [7]
Language policy caution
“Officially recognized” in constitutional text includes 16 languages; limiting the list to English, Shona and Ndebele is incomplete. [7]
Religion, 2022 census
Christian affiliations jointly accounted for about 85.3% of the population in the final ZIMSTAT census report. [4]
Apostolic affiliations, 2022
40.3% in the 2022 census religion classification. [4]
Pentecostal affiliations, 2022
17.0% in the 2022 census religion classification. [4]
Other Christian affiliations, 2022
Protestant 13.8%, Roman Catholic 6.4%, and other Christian 7.8% within the ZIMSTAT categories. [4]
African traditional religion, 2022
5.0% in the ZIMSTAT reported religious categories. [4]
No stated religious affiliation, 2022
8.3% in the ZIMSTAT census breakdown. [4]
Other religious communities
Muslim, Jewish, Hindu and other religious communities are also recorded; individual small categories should not be conflated. [4]
Major ethnolinguistic communities
Shona-speaking groups, Ndebele and multiple other communities with distinct histories and local identities. [7] [4]
Life expectancy, 2024
Approximately 63 years at birth in the World Bank series. [1]
Education structure
Primary, secondary, vocational and tertiary education; public and independent institutions operate nationwide. [2]
Leading public universities
University of Zimbabwe (Harare), National University of Science and Technology (Bulawayo), Midlands State University and other institutions. [2]
Health-service priorities
Primary health services, HIV treatment, maternal and child health, malaria prevention and rural access. [2]
Urban–rural divide
The final 2022 census reports about 38.6% urban and 61.4% rural residents, with major infrastructure and income differences. [4] [2]
Household cooking fuels, 2022
The census records extensive use of wood and other solid fuels, underscoring urban–rural energy inequalities. [4]
Household internet access, 2022
The census reported 34.3% of households had internet access at home, using its own household definition. [4]
Handset ownership, 2022
The census summary reported mobile-phone ownership among about 87% of households under its published measure. [4]
Signature staple
Sadza, a thick maize-meal preparation, commonly served with vegetables, legumes, meat or other relishes.
Other common foods
Millet, sorghum, groundnuts, leafy vegetables, beef and regional traditional foods. [12]
Cultural production
Shona stone sculpture, music, dance, storytelling, crafts and visual art are important cultural forms. [10] [15]
Major sports
Football, cricket, rugby, athletics and netball are important organized sports.
Cultural heritage landmarks
Great Zimbabwe, Khami and the Matobo Hills connect archaeology, living traditions and historical memory. [10]
World Heritage properties
Five UNESCO-inscribed sites: three cultural and two natural properties, including a cross-border site shared with Zambia. [10]
Great Zimbabwe National Monument
UNESCO World Heritage cultural site inscribed in 1986. [10]
Khami Ruins National Monument
UNESCO World Heritage cultural site inscribed in 1986. [10]
Matobo Hills
UNESCO World Heritage cultural landscape inscribed in 2003. [10]
Mana Pools National Park, Sapi and Chewore Safari Areas
UNESCO World Heritage natural site inscribed in 1984. [10]
Mosi-oa-Tunya / Victoria Falls
UNESCO World Heritage natural property inscribed in 1989 and jointly associated with Zimbabwe and Zambia. [10]

Government and Institutions

Constitutional form
Presidential, unitary republic; the president combines head-of-state and head-of-government functions. [7]
President
Emmerson Dambudzo Mnangagwa (as listed by the Parliament of Zimbabwe at verification). [8]
Vice president, first
Constantino G. D. N. Chiwenga. [8]
Vice president, second
Kembo C. D. Mohadi. [8]
Political system
Multiparty electoral system with an executive presidency and bicameral legislature. [7] [9]
National legislature
Parliament of Zimbabwe: National Assembly and Senate. [7] [8]
National Assembly statutory size
280 members under 2023 electoral arrangements, including 210 constituency seats, 60 reserved women’s seats and 10 youth seats. [9]
National Assembly constituency seats
210 seats elected from single-member constituencies. [9]
National Assembly reserved seats
60 additional seats reserved for women and 10 for youth aged 21–35 under the relevant constitutional amendments. [9]
Senate
Upper chamber with 80 statutory seats under the constitutional design, including elected senators and representatives of traditional leadership and persons with disabilities. [7] [8]
Speaker of National Assembly
Jacob Francis Mudenda (Parliament’s published roster). [8]
President of Senate
Mabel Memory Chinomona (Parliament’s published roster). [8]
Most recent national general election
Harmonized presidential and parliamentary elections held in August 2023. [9] [16]
2023 presidential result
Emmerson Mnangagwa was declared the presidential winner; the opposition challenged the electoral process and its credibility. [8] [16]
2023 observation findings
The SADC observer mission described polling as generally calm but found aspects of the elections inconsistent with national legal and SADC standards. [16]
2023 electoral criticism
Regional observers identified problems such as delayed ballot delivery, aspects of voter administration and transparency; electoral assessments should be distinguished from official results. [16]
Judicial branch
Constitutionally established judiciary, including Constitutional Court, Supreme Court and High Court. [7]
Constitutional rights
Guarantees of citizenship rights, expression, association, assembly, language, culture and religious liberty, subject to lawful limitations. [7]
Local governance
Provincial and metropolitan councils plus urban and rural local authorities are recognized tiers beneath national government. [7]
Provinces
10 provincial/metropolitan units. [7] [4]
Harare administration
Metropolitan provincial unit containing the capital. [4]
Bulawayo administration
Metropolitan provincial unit in southwestern Zimbabwe. [4]
Local-government responsibilities
Urban councils and rural district councils perform service and infrastructure responsibilities within the constitutional framework. [7]
Central bank
Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe, responsible for monetary and exchange-control regulation. [6]
National statistics agency
Zimbabwe National Statistics Agency (ZIMSTAT). [4] [20]
Government verification caveat
Incumbent names, portfolios and election rules can change after 6 November 2026; official rosters and current legal consolidations should be rechecked before later republication. [8] [7]

Management of transboundary waterfalls, protected wildlife areas and archaeological World Heritage sites. [10]
Important international tourism attractions
Victoria Falls, Great Zimbabwe and wildlife-protected regions. [10]

Sources and Methodology

Editorial cutoff: November 6, 2026 (Pacific Time). This fact sheet combines authoritative but non-identical periods: ZIMSTAT final census results (2022), merchandise goods trade (2024), World Bank national accounts and demographic estimates (2025), country institutional rosters (checked November 2026) and selected 2026 projections or developments. Where original sources have different values for a particular indicator, the figure is attributed to its own source and year rather than silently reconciled.

Interpretation: The final census total (15,178,957) supersedes the preliminary release (15,178,979). World Bank GDP in current dollars is not directly interchangeable with the UN statistical profile’s different published GDP series. The modeled ILO unemployment measure and the ZIMSTAT labor-force survey are not the same indicator; Zimbabwe’s ZiG and US-dollar price measures are also not interchangeable. The HS 2022 merchandise code describes a product nomenclature and does not imply goods were exported in 2022. Official officeholders and currency rules should be reconfirmed at later updates.

Country Overview

Mozambique, officially the Republic of Mozambique, is a southeastern African country facing the Indian Ocean, with its capital at Maputo. Its coastline, Zambezi River basin, major ports and transport routes connect the interior of Southern Africa to the Indian Ocean. The country became independent from Portugal on June 25, 1975, and developed a multiparty constitutional system after the 1990 reforms and 1992 peace agreement. Coal, natural gas, aluminium and electricity are among its major exported products. [5] [15] [6] [11]

As of November 5, 2026, Mozambique’s president is Daniel Francisco Chapo and its prime minister is Maria Benvinda Delfina Levi. The country has a unicameral 250-seat Assembly of the Republic and Portuguese as its sole constitutionally designated official language. The contested 2024 elections and northern Cabo Delgado conflict remain important elements of its contemporary political and humanitarian context. [5] [14] [6] [16] [17]

Portuguese maritime contact
Portuguese navigators reached the East African coast around the end of the fifteenth century; coastal contact preceded durable inland control.
Portuguese colonial administration
Portuguese control expanded unevenly; the territory formed part of Portugal’s colonial empire until 1975. [15]
Liberation movement
The Frente de Libertação de Moçambique (FRELIMO) emerged in 1962 as a leading independence organization.
Armed liberation war began

New constitutional framework introduced multiparty democracy and major political reforms. [6]
General Peace Agreement

Portuguese (constitution Article 10). [6] [5]

A predominantly youthful population places major demand on education, health and employment systems. [1] [12]

Tertiary education
Universities and higher-education institutions include the Eduardo Mondlane University in Maputo.
Cultural heritage
Makonde sculpture, southern African musical traditions, coastal architecture and varied oral histories.

Marrabenta is strongly associated with Mozambique, alongside regional song and dance styles.
Food culture
Cassava, maize, seafood, coconut, peri-peri seasoning and matapa (leaf-based dish) are found in local cuisines.

Public-health priorities
Maternal and child health, malaria prevention, sanitation and resilient access to care remain major public priorities.

Sources and Methodology

The figures use country-specific national publications, the World Bank, United Nations, World Trade Organization, Inter-Parliamentary Union, UNESCO, constitutional texts and other primary or identified institutional records. Statistics have different reference years; a more recent page-update date does not convert an old census result into a new observation.

Population: The World Bank’s 2025 estimate of 35,631,653 differs from the 2017 de jure final census count of 26,899,105, as well as from adjusted national census and projection series. Both are explicitly labeled. [2] [7]

Economics: World Bank current-dollar GDP, GDP per capita and growth indicators are presented with their own years. WTO/UN Comtrade 2024 merchandise values are distinct from GDP, services trade and other agencies’ compiled trade totals. Shares and product codes are based on the WTO 2024 breakdown. [1] [11]

Religion and language: Percentages come from the Mozambique National Statistics Institute’s 2017 census. The official language is Portuguese in Article 10; Article 9 addresses the development of other national languages. The English constitutional translation is informative and identifies the Portuguese original as controlling. [8] [6] [9]

Institutions and political context: Contemporary incumbents were checked against the official Mozambique government website. The 2024-election protest record and Cabo Delgado displacement are documented through attributed rights and UNHCR reporting; the numerical situations can evolve. [5] [16] [17]

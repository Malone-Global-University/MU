Country Overview

Zambia, officially the Republic of Zambia, is a landlocked country in south-central Africa with eight neighbors, Lusaka as its capital and copper mining as a central export industry. Formerly British-administered Northern Rhodesia, it became independent on October 24, 1964. Zambia is a multiparty presidential republic. President Hakainde Hichilema and Vice President Mutale Nalumango were inaugurated for a second term on September 1, 2026, after an election whose outcome was contested by the principal opposition. Zambia's natural environment includes the Zambezi River and the transboundary Mosi-oa-Tunya / Victoria Falls World Heritage property. [17] [2] [9] [11] [15]

People and Society

Official language
English under Article 258 of the Constitution. [13]
Indigenous language diversity
Numerous indigenous languages and dialects are used across provinces and communities. [17]
Commonly spoken languages
Bemba, Nyanja/Chewa, Tonga, Lozi, Kaonde, Lunda and Luvale, among others. [17] [22]
Seven widely used Zambian languages
Bemba, Nyanja, Tonga, Lozi, Kaonde, Lunda and Luvale are often identified as seven major vernacular languages. [17]
Official-language distinction
The seven major local languages are not seven additional constitutional nationwide official languages; English is the official language. [13] [17]
Language policy
Other languages may be used in education and certain official proceedings as prescribed; the Constitution protects linguistic diversity. [13]
Ethnolinguistic communities
Bemba, Tonga, Lozi, Chewa/Nyanja, Kaonde, Lunda, Luvale and many other groups form Zambia’s diverse society. [17]
Religious profile
Christianity is the largest religion, alongside Muslim, Hindu, Baha’i, Indigenous religious and other communities. [6] [27]
National constitutional religious statement
The constitutional preamble describes Zambia as a Christian nation while expressly recognizing religious freedom. [13]
Religious freedom
The Constitution upholds freedom of conscience, belief and religion; constitutional self-description does not eliminate other faiths. [13]
Religious statistics source
ZamStats issued its final 2022 census religion descriptive tables in April 2026. [6]
Religious-data denominator caution
The published religion descriptive table displays 18,340,343 classified observations, not the full 19,693,423 counted in the revised census; percentages must identify their base. [6] [4]
Older religion estimate
The U.S. State Department cited a ZamStats estimate of 95.5% Christian and 2.7% Muslim in its 2022 report; it is not a recalculation from the 2026-issued census table. [27] [6]
Population age pattern
The national median age was 18.3 years in 2022, reflecting a large youth population. [5]
Rural–urban differences
2022 census: 55.3% rural and 44.7% urban, using the revised national total. [5]
Life expectancy
About 67 years in 2024 (World Bank), with national and international series subject to revision. [1]
Education system
Primary, secondary and tertiary education, with universities, technical colleges and vocational institutions. [2]
Major universities
University of Zambia in Lusaka, Copperbelt University and other public and private universities.
Healthcare delivery
Public and private clinics and hospitals serve cities and rural communities; health access remains uneven. [2]
HIV and public health
HIV prevention, maternal and child health, malaria control and rural health-service delivery are important policy priorities. [2]
Social development pressures
Employment, education, infrastructure, housing and health demands are shaped by a rapidly growing young population. [2]
Common foods
Nshima (maize-meal staple) eaten with vegetables, beans, fish, meat or relish; local dishes differ by region.
Agricultural cuisine
Cassava, groundnuts, leafy vegetables and seasonal crops have important roles in regional food systems.
Major sports
Football, athletics, boxing and other community and competitive sports. [21]
Cultural traditions
Traditional ceremonies, music, dance, crafts and oral histories are diverse across regional communities.
Prominent traditional ceremony
Kuomboka, associated with the Lozi and seasonal movement on the Barotse floodplain.
Tourism centers
Livingstone and Victoria Falls, South Luangwa and other national parks, Kafue and the Lower Zambezi. [15]
UNESCO World Heritage total

Elephants, large mammals, birdlife and habitats in national parks are central to conservation and ecotourism. [15]
Social inequality

Sources and Methodology

Reviewed November 6, 2026. Six summary indicators use the World Bank's latest displayed 2025 country series where available and United Nations Statistics Division area and currency conventions. Zambia's national population enumeration is the revised final 2022 census published in February 2025; its demographic reference date is September 8, 2022. The economic tables use the WTO/UN Comtrade calendar-year 2024 goods series. Political offices and electoral institutions reflect the post-August 2026 election and October 2026 parliamentary sitting.

Definitions and cautions: The World Bank 2025 population is an estimate, not a 2025 census. Zambia's national poverty line and international US$3.00/day (2021 PPP) poverty measure are distinct. The 2025 modeled ILO unemployment estimate and Zambia's 2022 household-survey unemployment rate likewise differ in method and period. The ZamStats 2022 religion tabulation published in 2026 has a narrower tabulation denominator than the revised census total. Goods exports exclude service exports; the HS 2022 label specifies product-classification version, not year of export. Executive and legislative details require renewed review after the page-verification date.

Country Overview

Lesotho, officially the Kingdom of Lesotho, is a highland, landlocked country in Southern Africa that is entirely surrounded by South Africa. Its capital is Maseru. The modern Basotho kingdom took shape under Moshoeshoe I in the nineteenth century; after a period of British control as Basutoland, the country became independent on October 4, 1966. Its current constitutional framework dates from 1993, with later amendments. [6] [10]

Lesotho is a parliamentary constitutional monarchy. King Letsie III is head of state and Samuel Ntsokoane Matekane is prime minister. The economy includes apparel production, diamond mining, public and private services, agriculture, and exports of water to South Africa; regional trade and remittances play major roles. [8] [2] [11]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Kingdom of Lesotho. [6]
Common name
Lesotho.
Local name
Lesotho; formerly known as Basutoland during the colonial period. [10]
Demonym
Mosotho (singular); Basotho (plural); also Lesotho citizen.
Capital and largest city
Maseru, on the western border with South Africa. [10]
Region
Africa / Southern Africa. [16]
Country classification
Independent, landlocked sovereign kingdom completely surrounded by South Africa. [10] [6]
Historical national formation
In the early nineteenth century, Moshoeshoe I unified communities into a Basotho kingdom in a period of regional conflict and migration. [10]
Thaba Bosiu stronghold
Moshoeshoe I established the prominent defensible mountain settlement in 1824; it is central to Basotho state memory. [10]
British protection
Basutoland came under British protection in 1868 following conflict with neighboring settler polities. [10]
Colonial status
Basutoland was governed as a British colony after 1884, separate from South Africa. [10]
Independence from the United Kingdom
October 4, 1966; the independent state retained a constitutional monarchy. [6] [10]
Independence Day
October 4; the principal national independence observance. [10]
Present constitutional framework
Constitution brought into operation in 1993; subsequent amendments, including provisions reflected in the revised 2025 text. [6]
Restoration of electoral government
Multiparty constitutional government was restored in 1993 after periods of one-party rule and military government. [10]
National flag
Blue, white and green horizontal bands with a black mokorotlo (traditional Basotho straw hat) at the center; present design adopted in 2006. [10]
National anthem
“Lesotho fatše la bo-ntat’a rona” (“Lesotho, Land of Our Fathers”). [10]
National emblem
Basotho hat (mokorotlo), also represented on the national flag. [10]
ISO 3166-1 codes
LS (alpha-2); LSO (alpha-3); 426 (numeric).
Country-code domain
.ls.
International telephone code
+266. [13]
Time zone
South Africa Standard Time (SAST), UTC+2, year-round.

Historical date distinction: The nineteenth-century Basotho kingdom, British protection in 1868, formal independence on October 4, 1966, and the 1993 constitutional restoration represent different historical stages; the independent country was not first founded in 1993. [6] [10]

Geography and Population

Location
Southern Africa; fully enclosed by the territory of South Africa, with no coastline. [10]
Total area
30,355 km² (approximately 11,720 square miles), under the archived World Factbook and UN statistical conventions. [10] [16]
Land area
30,355 km² under the same archived Factbook accounting convention. [10]
Inland-water area
0 km² in the archived Factbook area partition; this accounting figure does not mean the country has no rivers, reservoirs or lakes. [10]
Land boundary
Approximately 1,106 km, all with South Africa. [10]
Coastline
0 km; a landlocked country. [10]
Highest point
Thabana Ntlenyana, 3,482 m above sea level. [10]
Lowest elevation
Approximately 1,400 m, at the Orange–Makhaleng river junction, by the archived Factbook convention. [10]
Typical elevation
Highland country; its lowest point is itself well above sea level, and the mean elevation is approximately 2,161 m. [10]
Main relief regions
Maloti Mountains and Drakensberg highlands, foothills, western lowlands and the Senqu River valley. [10]
Principal river system
Senqu (Orange) River and its tributaries; vital for surface water, hydropower and interbasin transfers. [10]
Water infrastructure
Lesotho Highlands Water Project exports water to South Africa and supports hydroelectric generation. [2]
Important reservoirs
Katse and Mohale reservoirs, associated with the Lesotho Highlands Water Project.
Climate
Temperate highland climate; generally cool to cold, drier winters and warmer, wetter summers. [10]
Natural hazards
Drought, severe winter weather, soil erosion and variable precipitation; floods can occur during intense rainfall. [10]
Natural resources
Water, diamonds, grazing lands and construction materials. [10]
Land use
Pasture is extensive; arable farming occupies a smaller share, with cropland concentrated in accessible zones. [10]
Administrative geography
Ten districts: Berea, Butha-Buthe, Leribe, Mafeteng, Maseru, Mohale’s Hoek, Mokhotlong, Qacha’s Nek, Quthing and Thaba-Tseka. [10]
Population, 2025
2,363,325 (World Bank annual estimate; not a completed census count). [1]
Population, 2016 census
2,007,201 persons counted by the Lesotho Bureau of Statistics. [3] [4]
2026 census status
A national Population and Housing Census is being conducted in 2026; a new final census count is not substituted for the 2025 World Bank estimate. [5]
Population growth
Approximately 1.1% in 2025 (World Bank annual population-growth series). [1]
Population distribution
Settlement is denser in the western lowlands and near Maseru than in much of the mountainous east. [10]
Urban population, 2016 census
34.2% (685,938 of 2,007,201 residents) in the 2016 census summary; not a 2025 observation. [4]
Major population centers
Maseru, Teyateyaneng, Hlotse/Leribe, Maputsoe, Mafeteng, Mohale’s Hoek and Butha-Buthe. [10]
Life expectancy at birth
About 58 years (World Bank, 2024 reference year). [1]
International migration
Cross-border labor, household networks and mobility with South Africa are longstanding characteristics of the economy. [2]

Data methodology: The 30,355 km² area is the archived Factbook/UN convention. The Bureau of Statistics 2016 figure is a census count; the World Bank 2025 figure is an estimate, not a census. The Bureau is conducting a 2026 census, but its final population count should not be inferred from the announcement. [10] [3] [4] [5] [1]

Maize, sorghum, wheat, livestock and smallholder production, constrained by limited arable land and climate variability. [10]

Lesotho loti (plural: maloti), code LSL. [13]

Loti pegged at parity to the South African rand (1:1). [13]

Regional trade arrangements
Southern African Customs Union (SACU), Southern African Development Community (SADC) and Common Monetary Area (CMA). [2] [13]
Infrastructure constraint
Landlocked and mountainous terrain makes road access, logistics and maintenance relatively costly. [2]
Tourism
Mountains, trails, highland landscapes, cultural heritage and winter recreation are notable attractions. [15]
Economic risks
Exposure to external textile demand, diamond prices, South African economic performance, customs receipts and climatic shocks. [2]

People and Society

Main national community
Basotho, alongside smaller populations and communities with varied historical backgrounds. [10]
Official languages under revised constitutional text
Sesotho, English, isiXhosa, isiPhuthi and sign language (Constitution, revised 2025, Article 3). [6]
Predominant spoken language
Sesotho is the principal language of everyday communication and Basotho public life. [10]
English use
English is used in government, law, education and business alongside Sesotho and the other constitutionally recognized languages. [6]
Other linguistic traditions
isiXhosa, isiPhuthi and other languages spoken in localized communities; sign language has constitutional status. [6]
Language data caveat
Older reference works list only Sesotho and English as official; the revised 2025 constitutional text expands the list to five. [6]
Predominant religion
Christianity is the majority religion; there are Protestant, Catholic, other Christian, indigenous and minority faith traditions. [10]
Protestant affiliation, historical estimate
47.8% (archived Factbook estimate referring to 2014). [10]
Roman Catholic affiliation, historical estimate
39.3% (archived Factbook estimate referring to 2014). [10]
Other Christian affiliation, historical estimate
9.1% (archived Factbook estimate referring to 2014). [10]
Other religions and no affiliation
Minority non-Christian traditions, religious syncretism and people without affiliation; the available shares refer to a historic estimate. [10]
Religious freedom
Freedom of conscience and religious practice is protected subject to constitutional limits. [6]
Education system
Primary, secondary and postsecondary education, including institutions around Maseru and other urban centers. [2]
Health profile
Communicable disease burdens, maternal and child health, and access to services remain important public-health priorities. [2]
Life expectancy
About 58 years in 2024 (World Bank), reflecting substantial long-term health challenges. [1]
Employment
Formal industrial employment and public service coexist with agriculture, informal work and labor migration. [2]
Rural livelihoods
Agriculture, herding, household transfers and seasonal incomes remain important in many areas. [2]
Traditional clothing
The Basotho blanket is a prominent symbol of national and cultural identity.
Traditional headwear
The mokorotlo Basotho straw hat is used in national symbolism. [10]
Cultural practices
Sesotho oral traditions, praise poetry, music, dance, festivals and communal celebrations form part of the country’s cultural heritage.
Cuisine
Typical foods include maize porridge (papa), vegetables, legumes and meat dishes, varying by household and region.
Major sports
Football (soccer), athletics, horse-related traditions and other national and community sports.
Cultural and natural heritage
Maloti-Drakensberg Park includes Sehlabathebe National Park and important San rock-art heritage. [15]
National identity
The Basotho kingdom, Moshoeshoe I, Thaba Bosiu and the landscape of the highlands are recurring elements of public memory. [10]

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Unitary parliamentary constitutional monarchy. [6] [7]
Head of state
King Letsie III (constitutional monarch). [8] [9]
Head of government
Prime Minister Samuel Ntsokoane Matekane, in office since October 28, 2022. [8] [9]
Deputy Prime Minister
Nthomeng Majara, listed in the June 2026 CIA World Leaders roster. [9]
Government formation
The Prime Minister commands the confidence of the National Assembly, with multiparty coalition government an important feature. [6] [8]
Constitution
Constitution of 1993, as amended and represented by the revised 2025 text. [6]
Sovereign character
The Constitution describes Lesotho as a sovereign democratic kingdom. [6]
Legislature
Bicameral Parliament composed of the King, the Senate and the National Assembly. [7] [6]
National Assembly
120 elected members under a mixed-member proportional electoral arrangement. [7]
National Assembly constituency seats
80 elected in geographic constituencies. [7]
National Assembly proportional seats
40 assigned by proportional representation within the mixed system. [7]
Senate
33 senators: 22 principal chiefs and 11 other appointed senators under constitutional procedures. [7]
Parliamentary term
Ordinarily five years, subject to constitutional provisions on dissolution and elections. [7]
Last general election before cutoff
October 7, 2022; next general election expected under the applicable electoral timetable. [8]
Political system
Multiparty parliamentary system; parties form coalition arrangements to govern. [8]
Constitutional role of monarch
The King performs head-of-state and constitutional functions; executive governance is primarily exercised through the Prime Minister and Cabinet. [6] [2]
Judiciary
Court of Appeal, High Court, magistrates’ courts and other constitutionally or statutorily established judicial institutions. [6]
Legal tradition
Mixed legal system including Roman-Dutch law, English common law and aspects of customary law. [10]
Local government
District and local governance institutions alongside customary chieftainship structures. [10]
Administrative districts
Ten districts, not provinces or federal states. [10]
Constitutional rights
Protections for life, personal liberty, expression, association, equality, conscience and other civil and social rights. [6]
Language policy
Five official languages in the revised 2025 constitutional text; translation and accessibility provisions also apply. [6]
Electoral framework
Mixed-member proportional representation for the National Assembly, with constituency and compensatory seats. [7]
Civil-military history
The post-independence period included one-party and military interventions; the current constitutional order dates to 1993. [10]
Political context
Governance reform, economic diversification, fiscal sustainability and public-service delivery remain major policy issues. [2]

Governmental classification: Lesotho is a constitutional monarchy, not a presidential republic. The King is head of state; a prime minister leads the elected parliamentary government. The legislature’s 120 elected National Assembly seats should not be confused with its 33-member Senate. [6] [7] [8]

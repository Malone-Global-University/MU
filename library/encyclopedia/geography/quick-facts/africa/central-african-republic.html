Country Overview

The Central African Republic is a landlocked state in the heart of Africa, with its capital at Bangui. Its modern state formation followed French colonial rule over Ubangi-Shari, autonomy in December 1958 and independence in August 1960. It remains culturally and linguistically diverse, with Sango and French as official languages. Agriculture, forestry and mineral resources are important to an economy affected by prolonged conflict and infrastructure constraints. Faustin-Archange Touadéra continues as president following a December 2025 election validated in January 2026, with Félix Moloua serving as prime minister. [7] [8] [6] [11] [10]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Central African Republic; République centrafricaine (French); commonly abbreviated CAR. [7]
Common and local names
Central African Republic; Centrafrique in French; national language Sango. [7]
Capital
Bangui, on the Ubangi (Oubangui) River near the border with the Democratic Republic of the Congo. [3] [7]
Continent / UN subregion
Africa / Middle Africa (commonly Central Africa). [22]
Precolonial communities
A diverse history of Ubangi-basin, savanna and forest societies, long-distance exchange, local political systems and population movements predating French rule; the modern borders do not define a single precolonial polity.
French colonial formation
France established colonial control over Ubangi-Shari (Oubangui-Chari) during the late nineteenth century; it became part of French Equatorial Africa in 1910. [8]
Self-government and republic proclaimed
December 1, 1958, establishment of the Central African Republic as a self-governing republic within the French Community; distinct from full independence. [8] [7]
Founding political figure
Barthélemy Boganda was a leading advocate of self-government and nationhood and headed the first autonomous government; he died in March 1959. [8]
Independence
August 13, 1960, from France, under David Dacko; celebrated as Independence Day. [8] [7]
First president
David Dacko, who led the new independent republic after Boganda’s death. [8]
Bokassa period
Jean-Bédel Bokassa seized power in the 1965–1966 coup; the country was renamed the Central African Empire in 1976, until the republic was restored in 1979. [8]
Multiparty transition
Presidential elections in 1993 brought Ange-Félix Patassé to office; subsequent coups and insurgencies repeatedly disrupted governance. [6]
2013 political and security crisis
The overthrow of President François Bozizé amid the Séléka rebellion was followed by extensive violence and competing armed-group mobilizations; the effects continued into later years. [6]
Post-conflict constitutional milestone
A 2015 constitution established a new republican framework, following a transitional period; the 2023 Constitution later superseded it. [6] [7]
Current constitution
Adopted following the July 30, 2023 referendum and promulgated August 30, 2023; it reshaped executive institutions and presidential tenure. [7] [9]
National days
December 1 (Republic Day, 1958) and August 13 (Independence Day, 1960), both designated in the 2023 Constitution. [7]
National motto
Unité, Dignité, Travail — Unity, Dignity, Work. [7]
National anthem
La Renaissance. [7]
ISO 3166-1 codes
CF (alpha-2); CAF (alpha-3); 140 (numeric). [22]
Internet country-code domain
.cf
International calling code
+236

Historical context: December 1, 1958 marked the proclamation of the autonomous republic; August 13, 1960 marked independence. The empire of 1976–1979, the political crisis of 2013, and constitutional reforms in 2015 and 2023 are separate milestones. [8] [7] [6]

Geography and Population

Location
Landlocked country in central Africa, with borders extending across the Congo Basin and northern savanna belt. [6]
Total surface area
622,984 km² (approximately 240,535 sq. mi.), from UNdata. [3]
Land area
Approximately 622,980 km² (UNCTAD reference); different area datasets round or classify inland water differently. [5]
Neighboring sovereign countries
Cameroon, Chad, Sudan, South Sudan, Democratic Republic of the Congo, and Republic of the Congo. [6]
Coastline
None; access to maritime trade relies on corridors through neighboring countries, particularly Cameroon. [6]
Capital and principal cities
Bangui; other important population and administrative centers include Berbérati, Bambari, Bouar, Bossangoa, Bria and Bangassou.
Resident population — World Bank
5,513,282, 2025 modeled midyear estimate. [1]
Resident population — national statistical institute
6,656,269, provisional 2025 RGPH-4 census results. ICASEES explicitly identifies the count as not yet validated; it should not be labeled final or substituted uncritically for the World Bank series. [2]
Population density — World Bank / UN basis
Approximately 8.8 persons per km² using the 2025 World Bank estimate and UNdata surface area. [1] [3]
Population density — provisional national basis
10.7 persons per km² in ICASEES provisional 2025 results. [2]
Population growth
3.4% annual growth (2025, World Bank model). [1]
Main river systems
Ubangi (Oubangui), a major Congo River tributary; Mbomou, Sangha, Kotto and Chari tributary systems.
Physical regions
Broad central plateau, river valleys, northern savannas, southwestern Congo Basin forests, and eastern and western uplands.
Climate
Predominantly tropical, with pronounced wet and dry seasons; more humid forest conditions in the south and longer dry seasons in the north.
Protected ecosystems
Dzanga-Ndoki National Park in the southwest forms part of the UNESCO-listed Sangha Trinational forest conservation complex. [21]
Natural-resource endowments
Gold, diamonds, timber, water resources and agricultural land; resource wealth has not consistently translated into broad-based prosperity. [6]

Population comparison: The 2025 World Bank midyear estimate and the ICASEES provisional 2025 RGPH-4 census count employ different collection methods and are not numerically reconcilable from these published summaries. The national result remains explicitly unvalidated; avoid presenting it as a final census. [1] [2]

Economy and International Trade

Economic classification
Low-income, predominantly agrarian and resource-dependent economy with a substantial informal sector and large development needs. [6]
Nominal GDP
US$3.07 billion (2025, World Bank current-dollar series; approximately US$3,066 million). [1]
GDP per capita
US$556.1 (2025, World Bank current U.S. dollars), a measure of average output per resident, not average personal earnings. [1]
Real GDP growth
4.5% (2025, annual percentage change, World Bank country indicator; estimates and vintages can be revised). [1]
Consumer price inflation
1.0% (2025, World Bank consumer-price annual rate). [1]
Currency
Central African CFA franc (XAF), issued by the Bank of Central African States (BEAC) within the CEMAC currency union. [3] [17]
Currency standard and regime
The CFA franc is the operative monetary unit. The short-lived 2022 cryptoasset legal-tender provisions were revised in April 2023 to remove cryptoassets’ legal-tender status. [20]
Main economic activities
Smallholder agriculture, forestry, livestock raising, mining, construction, commerce, transport and public services. [6] [5]
Major agricultural products
Cassava, maize, sorghum, millet, groundnuts, vegetables, livestock products, coffee and cotton in relevant producing areas.
Natural resources in trade
Gold, diamonds, and timber are important export activities; official merchandise accounting does not capture all informal flows. [6] [4]
Merchandise exports (WTO / UN Comtrade)
US$204.5 million in 2024, according to the WTO profile; not directly comparable to UNCTAD’s alternative estimate. [4]
Merchandise imports (WTO / UN Comtrade)
US$779.5 million in 2024, according to the same WTO profile. [4]
Merchandise trade balance (WTO / UN Comtrade)
Approximately US$575.0 million deficit in 2024, calculated as exports less imports from one consistent WTO dataset. [4]
Alternative export compilation (UNCTAD)
US$121 million merchandise exports in 2024; UNCTAD reported US$533 million imports and a US$411 million merchandise deficit. Data series have different methodologies and coverage. [5]
Officially reported leading export destination markets
WTO 2024 profile reports Pakistan, Tunisia, Sri Lanka, Egypt and other categories among leading recorded export destinations; such rankings are sensitive to customs records and unusual product flows. [4]
Largest recorded import origins
Cameroon and China were leading suppliers in the WTO’s 2024 imports-by-partner series. [4]
Merchandise trade data limitation
The WTO’s 2024 product ledger includes a large aircraft-and-spacecraft-parts entry. It is a customs-trade classification, not evidence of a major domestic aircraft-manufacturing sector; reexports and reporting anomalies require caution. [4]
Trade geography
As a landlocked state, imports and exports depend heavily on regional road, border, river and port connections. [6]
Banking and monetary framework
BEAC is the shared central bank for CEMAC; monetary policy is coordinated regionally rather than run through a separate CAR-only currency. [17] [3]

People and Society

Official languages
Sango and French; both are specified as official languages in Article 1 of the 2023 Constitution. [7]
National language
Sango, explicitly designated as the national language; also widely used as a lingua franca. [7]
Other languages
Gbaya, Banda, Mandjia, Zande, Sara languages, Fulfulde and many other languages and varieties are spoken by different communities.
Language and public life
Sango serves broad everyday cross-community communication, while French is important in state administration, higher education and formal written uses.
Religion — principal traditions
Christianity (including Protestant and Roman Catholic traditions), Islam, and Indigenous religious practices; some individuals combine practices or identify in more than one way. [14]
Religion — survey estimate
The U.S. Department of State’s 2023 report cites 2019 Pew-derived estimates of about 61% Protestant, 28% Catholic and 9% Muslim, with other groups about 2%; these are older estimates, not an official 2026 religion census. [14]
Religion — estimation limits
Other estimates, including Oxfam’s reported Muslim share up to 15%, differ by data collection, definition and displacement; no single current nationwide percentage should be presented as certain. [14]
Constitutional religious framework
The Constitution describes the republic as a secular state and protects religious exercise subject to constitutional and legal conditions. [7] [14]
Community and ethnic diversity
Includes Gbaya, Banda, Mandjia, Sara, Zande, Fulani and many other communities; ethnic and linguistic identities are diverse and cannot be reduced to a single ranking.
Cultural practices
Rich local traditions of music, storytelling, crafts, dance, religious observance and community markets, with considerable regional variation.
Population age structure
Young age structure with a substantial share of children and adolescents; detailed age-group percentages depend on the particular source and year. [6]
Life expectancy
Approximately 58 years at birth (2024, World Bank series). [1]
Human-development context
Persistent challenges in schooling, health access, infrastructure and income, compounded in some areas by conflict, displacement and isolation. [6] [15]
Internally displaced people
416,615 (UNHCR/Government figure as of August 31, 2026); not a population census count and not interchangeable with refugees abroad. [15]
Refugees present in CAR
53,308 refugees in the country as of October 5, 2026, according to UNHCR/Government reporting; distinct from asylum-seekers and internally displaced persons. [15]
CAR refugees and asylum-seekers abroad
737,705 according to UNHCR regional aggregation, last updated September 30, 2026; host-country datasets may have different reference dates. [16]
Major humanitarian pressures
Insecurity, displacement, constrained basic services and the effects of neighboring crises including Sudan. [15] [16]

Survey interpretation: Religious percentages cited here derive from older source estimates and should not be misrepresented as 2026 observations. Displacement counts and host-country reports use differing dates and populations. [14] [15] [16]

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Unitary republic with a president, prime minister, unicameral National Assembly and constitutional judicial institutions; executive authority is strong under the 2023 Constitution. [7] [12]
Foundational law
Constitution of August 30, 2023, replacing the previous constitutional order. [7]
Head of state
President Faustin-Archange Touadéra, in office since 2016; inaugurated for a new term on March 30, 2026 following the December 2025 vote. [10] [9]
Head of government
Prime Minister Félix Moloua, reappointed in May 2026. [10] [9]
Executive branch
President, prime minister, cabinet and executive institutions, with powers allocated under the 2023 Constitution. [7]
Legislature
Unicameral National Assembly (Assemblée nationale) with 144 statutory directly elected seats, according to the Inter-Parliamentary Union. [12]
Assembly membership reference
IPU reported 139 members holding seats in May 2026, fewer than the 144 statutory seats; vacancies or reporting updates can change this figure. [12]
Presidential term
Seven years under the 2023 Constitution; the new constitutional order removed the former presidential term limit. [7] [9]
Presidential election
The latest presidential election was held December 28, 2025; the Constitutional Council proclaimed Touadéra reelected January 19, 2026 with 77.9% of the valid vote in the final official result. [11] [9]
Electoral disputes
Opposition candidates challenged the presidential result; the Constitutional Council rejected the appeals and confirmed the official outcome. Institutional validation and objections should be distinguished. [11]
Legislative and local elections
Held in December 2025 with further voting and runoffs in April 2026, supported operationally by the UN mission MINUSCA. [9]
Judiciary
Ordinary courts and higher judicial organs operate under the constitution; the Constitutional Council adjudicates constitutional and electoral matters. [7] [9]
Administrative regions
Seven: Plateaux, Équateur, Yadé, Kagas (Kaga in some constitutional spelling), Fertit, Haut-Oubangui and Bas-Oubangui. [7] [13]
Prefectures
20 prefectures in the contemporary administrative framework, including Bangui’s special prefectural position; older 16-prefecture figures are outdated. [13]
Subprefectures
85 according to the territorial portal’s 2024 decree-based listing; earlier materials may report 84. [13]
Administrative status of capital
Bangui is the capital and the seat of national institutions, associated with the Bas-Oubangui administrative region. [7] [13]
State and religion
The Constitution declares a secular state and requires separation of government and religion. [7]
Public administration challenges
Institutional and territorial service provision have been affected by recurring armed conflict and resource constraints; formal institutions do not imply equal state reach across the territory. [6]
Peace and reconciliation framework
The February 2019 Political Agreement for Peace and Reconciliation (APPR-RCA) forms part of the country’s continuing disarmament and stabilization efforts. [6]

Contemporary context: Official election validation and opposition challenges are distinct facts. The 2023 Constitution specifies seven regions; contemporary administrative sources count twenty prefectures and eighty-five subprefectures. Older country factbooks may retain obsolete subdivisions. [11] [7] [13]

International Profile

Member of the African Union and participant in continental political cooperation.
Economic Community of Central African States (ECCAS)
Member of the Central African regional organization for political and economic cooperation. [6]
Economic and Monetary Community of Central Africa (CEMAC)
Member of the six-state customs and monetary community with Cameroon, Chad, Congo, Equatorial Guinea and Gabon. [17]

Shares the regional central bank and the Central African CFA franc (XAF). [17] [3]

Participates in IMF and World Bank Group programs and statistical systems; external financial cooperation is important to reconstruction and public-sector capacity. [6] [20]

MINUSCA (United Nations Multidimensional Integrated Stabilization Mission in the Central African Republic) has supported stabilization, civilian protection and electoral logistics. [9]
Regional security partnerships
Security cooperation and regional diplomacy involve the African Union, neighboring states and bilateral partners; the situation varies across regions. [6] [9]
Regional trade relationships
Landlocked trade connections with Cameroon and other neighbors are strategically important; 2024 import origin data identify Cameroon as a major supplier. [4] [6]
Refugee and humanitarian cooperation
UNHCR coordinates cross-border humanitarian responses, voluntary returns and protection activities related to displacement. [16] [15]
Environmental cooperation
Participates in Sangha Trinational conservation with Cameroon and the Republic of the Congo. [21]
Geographic / UN regional classification

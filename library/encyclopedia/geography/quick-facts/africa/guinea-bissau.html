Country Overview

Guinea-Bissau, officially the Republic of Guinea-Bissau (República da Guiné-Bissau), is a West African country on the Atlantic Ocean, bordering Senegal and the Republic of Guinea. Bissau is its capital. Its deeply indented coast and the Bijagós Archipelago include internationally significant mangrove and marine ecosystems. Guinea-Bissau declared independence from Portugal on September 24, 1973; Portugal recognized its independence on September 10, 1974. Cashew farming is central to the rural economy and merchandise exports. Since the November 2025 military takeover, the country has been governed under transitional institutions. A constitutional referendum took place in August 2026, but presidential and legislative elections scheduled for December 2026 were postponed without a replacement date announced as of this edition. Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Guinea-Bissau (República da Guiné-Bissau). [13]
Common name
Guinea-Bissau (Guiné-Bissau in Portuguese); distinct from the neighboring Republic of Guinea and from Equatorial Guinea. [13]
Demonym
Bissau-Guinean; Guinea-Bissau citizen. [13]
Capital
Bissau, a port city on the Atlantic estuarine coast. [16]
Other important towns
Bafatá, Gabú, Cacheu, Bissorã, Farim, Catió, Buba and Bolama; settlement ranks depend on administrative boundaries. [13]
Continent / UN M49 subregion
Africa / Western Africa. [17]
Precolonial societies
The territory encompassed diverse coastal, riverine and inland communities, including Balanta, Fula, Mandinka, Papel and Manjaco societies; it did not originate from a single precolonial polity. [13]
Colonial name
Portuguese Guinea (Guiné Portuguesa), a Portuguese colonial territory prior to independence. [12]
National liberation movement
African Party for the Independence of Guinea and Cape Verde (PAIGC), associated with Amílcar Cabral and the struggle against Portuguese colonial rule. [12] [27]
Armed independence struggle
PAIGC armed struggle began in 1963; decolonization accelerated after Portugal’s April 1974 revolution. [12]
Unilateral declaration of independence
September 24, 1973, proclaimed during the liberation struggle. [12]
Independence recognized by Portugal
September 10, 1974; formal recognition recorded in the Portuguese Official Gazette on September 11, 1974. [14] [12]
First head of state
Luís Cabral, first president of independent Guinea-Bissau. [12]
1980 military takeover
João Bernardo Vieira overthrew Luís Cabral; the previously linked Guinea-Bissau–Cabo Verde political project was disrupted. [12]
First multiparty national elections
1994, after a transition from one-party rule. [12]
1998–1999 civil war
A military rebellion and civil conflict disrupted national institutions and economic activity. [26] [12]
2012 military takeover
Military intervention contributed to another interruption of constitutional political life. [7]
November 2025 military takeover
Military forces seized power on November 26, 2025, before presidential and parliamentary election results were released. [5]
2026 constitutional reform
A transitional council approved a revised text in January 2026; voters then approved a proposed new constitutional order in an August 30 referendum (provisional results announced September 1). [10] [8]
National independence day
September 24, commemorating the 1973 declaration of independence. [12]
ISO 3166-1 country codes
GW (alpha-2); GNB (alpha-3); 624 (numeric). [16]
Internet country-code domain
.gw.

Geography and Population

World region
West Africa on the Atlantic coast between Senegal and the Republic of Guinea. [13]
Total surface area
36,125 km² including inland waters, in the archived World Factbook geographic series. [18] [16]
Land area
28,120 km² (UNCTAD/archived Factbook definition). [2] [18]
Inland water area
8,005 km² (the total-minus-land measure in the archived Factbook series). [18]
Land borders
Senegal to the north and Guinea to the east and south. [18]
Atlantic coastline
Approximately 350 km in the archived Factbook definition; coastal-length measures vary with mapping scale. [18]
Population: World Bank
2,249,515 (2025 modeled estimate). [1]
Population growth
2.2% in 2025 (World Bank annual population growth series). [1]

Approximately 80 residents per km² of land, calculated from 2025 World Bank population and 28,120 km² land area; consistent with the UN Data rounded figure. [1] [16] [18]

Approximately 578,800 in the UN Data 2025 capital-city estimate; metropolitan and administrative urban boundaries are not necessarily identical. [16]

Major rivers and estuaries
Geba River estuary, Cacheu River, Corubal River and extensive tidal channels. [23] [18]
Terrain
Low-lying coastal plains, estuaries and mangroves transitioning to somewhat higher savanna landscapes inland. [18] [23]
Highest point
Dongol Ronde, about 277 m in the archived Factbook geographic series. [18]
Climate
Tropical, with a rainy season generally around June–November and a drier season around December–May; periods vary geographically and annually. [18]
Coastal ecosystems
Mangroves, tidal mudflats, sandbanks, lagoons and biologically important marine habitats. [21] [23]
Internationally protected heritage
Coastal and Marine Ecosystems of the Bijagós Archipelago – Omatí Minhô, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage property in 2025. [21]
Biosphere conservation
Bolama-Bijagós Biosphere Reserve, recognized under UNESCO’s Man and the Biosphere programme. [22]
Environmental pressures
Coastal erosion, pressure on fisheries and mangroves, altered rainfall patterns, and risks to island and wetland habitats. [21] [23]
Administrative geographic layout
Eight regions plus the autonomous sector of Bissau. [13]

Area definitions: The archived total-area measure of 36,125 km² includes 8,005 km² of inland waters; the 28,120 km² land-area measure is used for the population-density calculation. Country profiles using different GIS boundaries may report different area totals. [18] [2] [16]

Economic system
Small mixed economy in which cashew agriculture, domestic trade, services, fishing and public-sector activity are important. [7] [3]
Nominal GDP
Approximately US$2.53 billion (2025; World Bank, current U.S. dollars). [1]
GDP per capita
Approximately US$1,124 (2025, World Bank current U.S. dollars); this is output divided by population, not an average wage. [1]
Real GDP growth: World Bank
5.8% (2025 annual change in constant-price output). [1]
Real GDP growth: IMF estimate
5.5% for 2025 in IMF Country Report 26/83, reflecting a separate reporting vintage from the World Bank series. [3]
Annual consumer price inflation
0.9% (2025, World Bank series; also reported by IMF). [1] [3]
Currency
West African CFA franc (XOF), shared by member countries of the West African Economic and Monetary Union. [16] [25]
Monetary institution
Central Bank of West African States (BCEAO), the regional monetary authority. [25]
Currency adoption date
May 2, 1997, replacing the Guinea-Bissau peso when the country joined the West African monetary union. [25]
Principal income-generating crop
Cashew nuts, especially unprocessed raw nuts for export. [3] [7]
Other crops and food production
Rice, peanuts, palm products, maize, cassava and horticulture; smallholders are central to rural livelihoods. [7]
Fisheries and marine activity
Artisanal fishing, seafood resources and coastal economic activity. [7] [21]

US$487 million in the same UNCTAD series. [2]

Main export goods
Raw cashew nuts dominate; smaller exports include fish and seafood, selected agricultural products and limited wood products. [3] [2]
Main import needs
Food staples, fuel, machinery, vehicles and manufactured consumer goods; precise rankings change by year and source. [7] [3]
Regional market integration
Member of the West African Economic and Monetary Union (WAEMU/UEMOA), using the common CFA franc. [25]
Economic development constraints
Limited diversification, transport and electricity infrastructure needs, fiscal and financial-sector risks, and exposure to agricultural shocks. [3] [7]
2026 economic outlook
IMF anticipated growth around 5–6% for 2026 in its 2026 review; a forecast rather than a completed annual observation. [3]

People and Society

Official language
Portuguese; used in the legal system, central government and formal education. [19]
National lingua franca
Guinea-Bissau Creole (Kriol, a Portuguese-based creole) is widely used for communication across linguistic communities. [19] [13]
Other major languages
Fula/Pular, Mandinka (Mandingo), Balanta, Papel, Manjaco and other indigenous languages. [19] [13]
Ethnolinguistic diversity
Numerous communities maintain distinctive languages, cultural institutions and regional histories. [13]
Religious composition: archived estimate
Muslim 46.1%; Indigenous/folk religions 30.6%; Christian 18.9%; other or unaffiliated 4.4% in a CIA World Factbook 2020 estimate. These are historical estimates, not a contemporary census. [19]
Alternative religious estimates
The U.S. State Department cited an earlier Pew estimate of approximately 45% Muslim, 31% Indigenous religious practices and 22% Christian; the estimates use differing years and definitions. [20]
Religious coexistence
Muslim, Christian and Indigenous spiritual traditions are represented, and some customary practices overlap with affiliation categories. [20]
Islamic traditions
Predominantly Sunni Muslim communities, with other traditions present; religious affiliation varies by region and community. [20]
Christian traditions
Catholic and Protestant denominations, particularly visible in Bissau and some coastal areas. [20]
Population structure
Young population; archived CIA Factbook-based data estimate a median age of 18.5 years (2025 model), not a measured census result. [19]
Life expectancy at birth
64.3 years (2024; World Bank series, rounded). [1]
Urban and rural settlement
Population concentrated in the capital and other towns, with substantial rural communities dependent on agriculture and fishing. [13] [18]
Education language environment
Portuguese-language formal schooling coexists with a multilingual everyday environment, including Kriol and community languages. [19] [13]
Cultural expression
Oral traditions, music, dance, diverse communal ceremonies and West African–Atlantic cultural exchange. [13]
National identity
The independence movement and anticolonial political thought associated with Amílcar Cabral are major parts of public national memory. [12] [27]
Population data qualification
A World Bank modeled population series is used for 2025; no unverified or undated national census count is substituted for that observation. [1]

Religious-data caution: Percentages shown are archived estimates, not a 2026 census. Local practice and categories of religious affiliation can overlap; different surveys should not be combined into a single artificial distribution. [19] [20]

Government and Institutions

Form of state
Republic; currently governed through military-led transitional institutions after the November 2025 takeover. [5] [7]
Constitutional description
The 2026 constitutional reform describes a unitary, secular republic and arrangements for a president, government and national legislature; implementation must be distinguished from the current transition. [10] [8]
Current transition president
Major-General Horta Inta-A (also rendered Horta Nta Na Man in some reports), sworn in November 27, 2025. [4] [5]
Current prime minister
Ilídio Vieira Té, appointed following the November 2025 military takeover; also listed as finance minister in the CIA’s June 2026 leadership record. [4] [3]
Pre-coup president
Umaro Sissoco Embaló, in office 2020–November 2025; ousted during the military takeover. [7] [5]
November 2025 election status
Presidential and legislative voting occurred November 23, 2025, but the military takeover on November 26 prevented publication of the final results and suspended the process. [5]
Political Transition Charter
Adopted December 8, 2025, establishing an intended 12-month transitional period. [5]
Transitional legislature
Transitional National Council (Conselho Nacional de Transição, CNT), appointed and first seated December 12, 2025. [6] [5]
Current Transitional National Council membership
66 members in IPU records: 65 designated members plus the speaker. [6]
Transitional legislative speaker
Major-General Tomás Djassi (since December 12, 2025, IPU record). [6]
CNT membership composition
Designated members include nominees of the transition president, high military command, political parties, civil society and regions. [5]
Earlier elected legislature
People’s National Assembly, formerly a 102-seat elected chamber; it must not be conflated with the 66-member transitional council. [6] [8]
Transitional National Council constitutional action
CNT approved a revised constitution on January 13, 2026. [5] [10]
Constitutional referendum
Held August 30, 2026; provisional results indicated approximately 70% support and nearly 60% turnout. [8] [11]
Constitutional reform, executive
Approved reforms enlarge presidential authority, including appointment and dismissal of the prime minister and cabinet. [8]
Constitutional reform, legislature
Proposed new elected parliament of 65 seats rather than the earlier 102; this is not the same body as the existing 66-member appointed CNT. [8] [6]
Transitional election date originally set
December 6, 2026, in a January 2026 decree. [5]
Election timetable as of editorial cutoff
The December 6, 2026 presidential and legislative elections were postponed in October 2026; no new date had been announced by November 5, 2026. [9]
Transition candidate restrictions
December 2025 Political Transition Charter stipulated that the transition president and prime minister would not contest the next elections. [5]
Judicial institutions
Courts and constitutional procedures are provided for under national law, but their operation is affected by the transitional political context. [10] [5]
Administrative regions
Eight regions: Bafatá, Biombo, Bolama-Bijagós, Cacheu, Gabú, Oio, Quinara and Tombali. [13]
Special capital-sector status
Bissau forms an autonomous sector, administratively separate from the eight regions. [13]
Civil liberties and institutional context
Political participation and electoral implementation are contested during the current military-led transition; formal constitutional provisions do not by themselves establish competitive civilian governance. [7] [8] [9]

Constitutional and electoral chronology: A January 2026 draft and legal publications, the August 30 referendum, the existing transitional council and the later postponement of elections describe distinct stages. The 65-seat elected-parliament design proposed by the constitutional reform is not the same as the currently appointed 66-member Transitional National Council. Some institutional databases still list the original December 2026 election date; Reuters reported its postponement on October 8. [10] [6] [8] [9]

International Profile

United Nations membership
Admitted September 17, 1974. [15]
African Union membership and status
Member state; participation suspended by the AU Peace and Security Council on November 28, 2025, pending restoration of constitutional order. [24]
ECOWAS
Regional member; following the 2025 coup, Guinea-Bissau was suspended from ECOWAS decision-making bodies (IMF 2026 account). [3]
West African Economic and Monetary Union
Member of WAEMU/UEMOA since May 1997; uses the regional CFA franc (XOF). [25]
Community of Portuguese Language Countries
Member of the CPLP, reflecting Portuguese language ties and Lusophone cooperation. [13]
International financial relationships
Member of the IMF and World Bank, with continuing macroeconomic, fiscal and development programs. [3] [7]
Historical relationship with Cabo Verde
Shared PAIGC liberation history before separate postcolonial political developments. [12]
Principal immediate neighbors
Senegal and the Republic of Guinea; the country is not the same state as Guinea or Equatorial Guinea. [13]
International heritage
The UNESCO-inscribed Coastal and Marine Ecosystems of the Bijagós Archipelago – Omatí Minhô (2025). [21]
Marine conservation significance
Important migratory shorebird, sea turtle and mangrove habitats within the eastern Atlantic coastal migration and ecosystem network. [21] [23]
International dialing code
+245. [18]
Time zone
Greenwich Mean Time (GMT; UTC+00:00), without seasonal daylight-saving adjustment. [16]
International country code
ISO 3166-1: GW / GNB / 624. [16]
Current foreign-affairs caveat
Regional membership should not be confused with active participation: AU and ECOWAS suspensions concern decision-making and institutional participation rather than the state’s geographical affiliation. [24] [3]

Membership and participation: Guinea-Bissau remains geographically and historically part of the cited regional institutions, but the 2025 coup triggered suspensions in AU activity and ECOWAS decision-making; do not read membership alone as unrestricted active participation. [24] [3]

Country Overview

Rwanda, officially the Republic of Rwanda, is a densely populated, landlocked state in the Great Lakes region of East-Central Africa. Its capital is Kigali, and its four neighbors are Uganda, Tanzania, Burundi and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. The country became independent of Belgian administration on July 1, 1962. Its contemporary political institutions developed after the 1994 genocide against the Tutsi, in which moderate Hutu and other opponents of the genocidal regime were also killed. [2] [6] [25]

Rwanda operates a presidential-led republic under its amended constitution. Paul Kagame is president and Justin Nsengiyumva is prime minister as of November 2026. Services, agriculture, industry, mining and tourism contribute to its economy; the country is particularly associated with coffee, tea, mineral trade and conservation tourism. Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Rwanda (Kinyarwanda: Repubulika y’u Rwanda). [8]
Common name
Rwanda; no alternate independent state is denoted by the name.
Demonym
Rwandan.
Capital
Kigali, the national capital and largest urban center. [8] [3]
Continent
Africa.
United Nations geographic subregion
Eastern Africa; also described geographically as the East-Central African Great Lakes region. [2] [6]
Geographic identity
A small, densely populated, landlocked country of highlands, lakes and volcanic mountains. [6] [2]
Precolonial political history
A centralized Kingdom of Rwanda developed before European colonization; numerous communities and lineages formed its social fabric. [25]
Precolonial monarchy
Rwandan kings bore the title mwami; the institution predates Belgian and German colonial administration. [25]
German colonial period
Rwanda fell under German East Africa around the end of the nineteenth century until World War I. [25]
Belgian colonial administration
Belgium administered Ruanda-Urundi after World War I under a League of Nations mandate, and later as a UN trust territory. [25]
Social transformation and political violence
Political conflict and violence in 1959–1961 displaced many Tutsi and contributed to the collapse of the monarchy. [25]
Republican transition
A 1961 referendum and political changes replaced the monarchy with a republican order before full independence. [25]
Independence
July 1, 1962, when Rwanda became sovereign after Belgian administration of Ruanda-Urundi. [25] [2]
Independence Day
July 1, annual national commemoration of sovereignty. [25]
First president of independent Rwanda
Grégoire Kayibanda. [25]
1973 coup
Juvénal Habyarimana overthrew the government and established a new authoritarian political system. [25]
1990 civil war
The Rwandan Patriotic Front (RPF) offensive began a civil war with the government. [25]
1994 genocide against the Tutsi
During roughly 100 days in 1994, extremist-led mass killings targeted Tutsi people; moderate Hutu and others were also murdered. This was a genocide, not merely a civil-war casualty event. [25] [23]
End of the genocide
RPF military victory in July 1994 ended the genocidal regime; displacement, reconstruction and regional conflicts followed. [25]
Post-1994 government
The RPF-led political order oversaw reconstruction, national reconciliation policies and subsequent constitutional change. [25] [10]
2003 constitution
A new constitutional framework came into force in 2003. [8]
2015 constitutional revision
Changed presidential term structures and rules, including transitional provisions. [8] [1]
2023 constitutional edition
Constitution published in the August 4, 2023 Official Gazette; the national language clause and institutional rules are cited from this text. [8]
Most recent presidential election
July 2024; Paul Kagame was re-elected. [13]
Head of state, November 2026

NISR provides quarterly 2025 and 2026 merchandise trade with domestic exports, re-exports and imports distinguished. Services, including tourism, are not in customs merchandise tables. [16] [17] [18] [19]

Social, political and heritage data: Religious affiliation is from the 2022 census, adult literacy is the 2022 NISR census measure, and UNDP HDI 0.578 with rank 159 of 193 refers to 2023 data reported in 2025. The 2024 official election result and Freedom House 2026 political assessment are attributed separately. UNESCO lists two inscribed World Heritage properties, both inscribed in 2023.

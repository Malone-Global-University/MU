Country Overview

Benin, officially the Republic of Benin, is a West African country bordering the Gulf of Guinea, with Porto-Novo as its official capital and Cotonou as its principal administrative and commercial center. Its territory encompasses coastal lagoons, wooded and agricultural landscapes, northern savannas, and communities speaking many languages. The independent state was established on August 1, 1960, as the Republic of Dahomey and adopted the name Benin in 1975. Its present political order developed from the Constitution of 1990 and subsequent amendments, including major institutional reforms in 2025 that introduced a Senate. Agricultural exports, including cotton and processed oilseeds, are central to recorded merchandise trade, while port logistics connect Benin with regional markets. [9] [8] [3]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Benin (République du Bénin).
Common name
Benin (Bénin).
Former state name
Republic of Dahomey (at independence in 1960); People’s Republic of Benin (1975–1990). [9]
Capital
Porto-Novo (constitutional / official capital); national executive institutions and most major ministries operate in Cotonou.
Continent / UN M49 subregion
Africa / Western Africa (UN M49). [12]
Precolonial states and societies
The Kingdom of Dahomey, centered at Abomey, was a major regional power; other historical polities and communities inhabited lands now within Benin. The modern republic is not simply identical to the historic kingdom. [15]
French colonial period
The territory was incorporated into French colonial rule in the late nineteenth century and was administered as French Dahomey in French West Africa.
Republic proclaimed
December 4, 1958, as a self-governing republic within the French Community. [9]
Independence achieved
August 1, 1960, from France, under the name Republic of Dahomey. [9]
National day
Independence Day, August 1.
Name change
Renamed the People’s Republic of Benin on November 30, 1975, during the revolutionary period; formally became the Republic of Benin again on March 1, 1990. [9]
National Conference
February 1990 National Conference helped inaugurate a transition to multiparty constitutional government. [9]
Constitutional foundation
Constitution of December 11, 1990, following the December 2 referendum; subsequently amended, including in 2019 and 2025. [9] [8]
2025 constitutional revision
A major revision was adopted in November 2025 and promulgated in December 2025; it introduced the Senate and extended specified elected terms to seven years. [8] [4]
ISO 3166-1 codes
BJ (alpha-2); BEN (alpha-3); 204 (numeric). [12]
Internet country-code domain
.bj

Historical context: Benin’s borders contain peoples, kingdoms, trading communities, and religious traditions whose histories precede French rule. The historic Kingdom of Dahomey and the Atlantic slave trade are important to the region’s history but do not exhaust the histories of the peoples within present-day Benin. After independence, the country experienced military interventions and a one-party revolutionary period before the National Conference and constitutional transition of 1990. Its 2025 constitutional reform is a separate milestone from the original founding of the postcolonial state.

Geography and Population

Total surface area
114,760 km² (World Bank surface-area series, 2023; approximately 44,309 sq. mi.). [2]
Land area
112,760 km² (World Bank / FAOSTAT land-area series, 2024); excludes inland water counted in surface-area figures. [18]
Population (2025 international estimate)
14,814,460 residents (World Bank, 2025 annual series). [1]
Population growth
2.4% annually in 2025 (World Bank). [1]
Population density
Approximately 131 residents per km² of land, calculated from 2025 World Bank population and 2024 land area; not an official published figure.
Capital and commercial center
Porto-Novo is the official capital; Cotonou is the principal economic center, port, and seat of the central government.
Principal cities
Cotonou, Porto-Novo, Parakou, Abomey-Calavi, Abomey, and Natitingou; rankings depend on census and urban-area definitions.
Bordering countries
Togo to the west; Burkina Faso and Niger to the north; Nigeria to the east.
Maritime setting
South-facing Atlantic shoreline on the Bight of Benin, part of the Gulf of Guinea.
Coastal zone
Low-lying coastal plains, lagoons, wetlands, and estuaries, including Lake Nokoué and the Cotonou area.
Northern landscapes
Savanna and woodland landscapes, with hills and highlands of the Atakora region.
Principal river systems
Ouémé, Mono, Pendjari, and northern waterways associated with the Niger River basin.
Climate
Predominantly tropical; humid in the south, with a more pronounced wet/dry seasonal cycle and generally drier conditions toward the north.
Protected natural landscapes
Pendjari and W national parks form part of the wider transboundary W-Arly-Pendjari protected-area complex. [15]
Largest administrative subdivisions
12 departments, further subdivided into 77 communes. [16]
Regional geography
Benin is a coastal West African country connecting Atlantic commerce with inland Sahelian markets.

Comparability: The World Bank’s 2025 population series is an international estimate, not a newly completed national census. Some Beninese institutional publications use different projection series and consequently report different totals. Surface and land area also use different definitions; dates and sources are retained to avoid false precision.

Economy and International Trade

Economic system
Mixed economy with agriculture, trade, manufacturing, processing, services, and port-related logistics.
Nominal GDP
Approximately US$24.57 billion (2025, current U.S. dollars; World Bank). [1]
Nominal GDP per capita
Approximately US$1,658.3 (2025, current U.S. dollars; World Bank); this is output per person, not average wages or personal income. [1]
Real GDP growth
Approximately 8.1% in 2025 (World Bank annual series). [1]
Consumer-price inflation
Approximately 1.1% in 2025 (World Bank annual CPI series). [1]
Modeled unemployment
Approximately 1.6% in 2025 (World Bank / ILO modeled measure). This does not capture all underemployment or informal work. [1]
Currency
West African CFA franc (XOF), issued by the Central Bank of West African States (BCEAO). [14]
Currency exchange regime
The XOF is fixed to the euro at 655.957 CFA francs per €1; not independently floated by Benin. [14]
Principal economic activities
Agriculture (especially cotton and food crops), cashew and soybean processing, construction, wholesale trade, transport, and services.
Port and logistics
Cotonou serves as a maritime trade and transit gateway for Benin and inland regional markets.
Merchandise exports
US$1,112.8 million in 2024 (WTO / UN Comtrade reporting basis). [3]
Merchandise imports
US$3,366.5 million in 2024 (same reporting basis). [3]
Merchandise trade balance
Approximately US$2,253.7 million deficit in 2024, calculated as exports minus imports using the WTO series; goods only, excluding services. [3]
Top merchandise export destination
Bangladesh (43.2% of reported 2024 exports); the European Union (reported as a bloc), India, Pakistan, and the United States were other principal destinations. [3]
Major merchandise import origins
European Union (reported as a bloc), India, China, Togo, and United States in the WTO 2024 series. [3]
Principal export product
Uncarded/uncombed cotton accounted for 45.0% of the 2024 WTO merchandise export value. [3]
International trade context
Regional transit, re-export, and informal cross-border trading activity complicate interpretation of simple import/export totals.
Economic statistical caveat
National GDP and monetary measures are not comparable with merchandise-export figures without accounting for methodology, coverage, and reporting year.

People and Society

Official language
French, used in national administration, public institutions, and much formal education. [11]
Widely spoken languages
Fon, Yoruba, Baatonum (Bariba), Aja, Gun, Dendi, Fulfulde, Yom, and numerous other languages; patterns vary by region. [11]
Linguistic diversity
More than 68 languages were recorded in the 2013 census as summarized by CLEAR Global; most residents use more than one language. [11]
Fon first-language share
Approximately 20.2% in the 2013 first-language dataset; a language-share figure, not an ethnic-population estimate. [11]
Baatonum first-language share
Approximately 8.7% in the same 2013 dataset. [11]
Borgu Fulfulde first-language share
Approximately 8.4% in the same 2013 dataset. [11]
Christian affiliation
48.5% of residents in the 2013 census, as cited in the U.S. Department of State religious-demography report. [10]
Muslim affiliation
27.7% in the 2013 census (largely Sunni); affiliation is not equivalent to uniform practice. [10]
Vodun affiliation
11.6% in the 2013 census identified with Vodun; traditional religious practices can also coexist with Christian and Muslim identification. [10]
Other religious responses
Indigenous religions other than the separately reported Vodun category 2.6%; other religions 2.6%; no religious affiliation 5.8% (selected 2013 census categories). [10]
Christian traditions
Roman Catholic, Protestant, Evangelical, and African-initiated churches, including the Celestial Church of Christ. [10]
Religious status of the state
Secular constitutional framework providing freedom of religious thought, expression, and practice. [10]
Life expectancy at birth
Approximately 61.0 years in 2024 (World Bank series). [2]
Total fertility rate
Approximately 4.48 births per woman in 2024 (World Bank series). [2]
Adult literacy
Approximately 51.4% of persons age 15+ in 2022 (World Bank series; literacy definitions and survey coverage matter). [2]
Ethnolinguistic communities
Includes Fon, Aja, Yoruba, Baatonum, Fulani, Dendi, Yom, and other communities; language and ethnicity should not be treated as identical categories.
Cultural heritage
Notable traditions include Vodun religious and artistic practices, textile and craft production, historical royal architecture at Abomey, and diverse music, dance, and oral traditions. [15]
UNESCO World Heritage
Royal Palaces of Abomey; Koutammakou (transboundary extension); W-Arly-Pendjari Complex (transboundary natural property). [15]

Survey interpretation: The detailed religious figures above come from the 2013 census, not a 2025–2026 religious count. The figures are not a complete mutually exclusive accounting of contemporary belief or practice; some people maintain Vodun or other customary religious practices while reporting Christian or Muslim affiliation. Language estimates also refer to specified first-language categories, rather than all languages understood or spoken.

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Presidential constitutional republic with a multiparty system and a bicameral legislature following the 2025 constitutional revision. [8] [4]
Foundational constitutional order
Constitution of December 11, 1990, amended in 2019 and 2025. [9] [8]
Head of state and government
President Romuald Wadagni, inaugurated May 24, 2026; status checked November 4, 2026. [6] [7]
Vice president
Mariam Chabi Talata, elected on the 2026 presidential ticket with Romuald Wadagni. [7]
Executive branch
The president is both head of state and head of government and directs the executive with appointed ministers and related institutions. [6]
Presidential election
The most recent presidential election was held April 12, 2026; the Wadagni–Talata ticket was declared elected by the Constitutional Court. [7]
Presidential term
Seven years, renewable once, under the 2025 constitutional revision; the constitutional rules bar more than two presidential mandates in a lifetime. [8]
Parliament
Bicameral: National Assembly (lower chamber) and Senate (upper chamber); an upper chamber was established by the 2025 reform. [4] [5]
National Assembly membership
109 directly elected seats in the National Assembly (2026). [4]
National Assembly election
January 11, 2026; 60 seats won by Progressive Union for Renewal and 49 by Republican Bloc. No other party won a seat under the electoral allocation rules. [4]
National Assembly term
Seven years for members elected in 2026 under the amended constitution. [4]
Senate
25-member upper chamber constituted July 30, 2026, with ex officio and appointed membership categories rather than a direct general-election ballot. [5]
Senate term
Five years under the new upper-chamber arrangement; first session recorded July 30, 2026. [5]
Judiciary
Constitutional Court, Supreme Court, and other courts in the national judicial system. [8]
Territorial administration
12 departments and 77 communes; principal cities with special local administrative status include Cotonou, Porto-Novo, and Parakou. [16]
Political rights and participation
Constitutional multiparty institutions coexist with electoral eligibility thresholds and other rules affecting parties’ ability to obtain parliamentary representation. [4]
Religious constitutional status
The constitution establishes a secular republic with protections for religious practice. [10]

2026 institutional update: Older profiles that describe Benin’s legislature as unicameral or give five-year presidential and National Assembly terms are outdated following the 2025 constitutional revision and the July 2026 establishment of the Senate. The National Assembly and Senate follow different methods of selection and term lengths.

International heritage

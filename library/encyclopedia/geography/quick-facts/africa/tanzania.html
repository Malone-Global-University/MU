Country Overview

Tanzania, officially the United Republic of Tanzania, lies in eastern Africa along the Indian Ocean and includes the mainland territory formerly known as Tanganyika and the semi-autonomous Zanzibar islands. Tanganyika gained independence in 1961, Zanzibar in 1963, and the two united on April 26, 1964. Dodoma is the national capital, while Dar es Salaam is the principal commercial city. Tanzania’s landscapes encompass Mount Kilimanjaro, the Serengeti, Great Lakes shorelines and major Swahili coastal heritage sites. Agriculture, gold mining, tourism, manufacturing and transport are important economic activities. As of November 2026, President Samia Suluhu Hassan leads the Union government, alongside Vice President Deogratius Ndejembi and Prime Minister Mwigulu Nchemba; President Hussein Mwinyi leads the government of Zanzibar. The October 2025 elections were accompanied by deadly protests and serious concerns reported by African Union observers. [9] [10] [11] [7] [15] [19]

December 9, 1962. [9]

December 10, 1963, from British protectorate arrangements. [10]

January 12, 1964, which displaced the sultanate and produced a revolutionary republican government. [9]
Union of Tanganyika and Zanzibar
April 26, 1964, creating the United Republic. [10] [9]

United Republic of Tanzania, adopted November 1, 1964 according to the United Nations. [10]
National holiday — Union Day
April 26. [9]
Mainland Independence Day
December 9. [9]
National historical figure
Julius Kambarage Nyerere, leading independence figure and first president; commonly called Mwalimu (teacher). [9]
Precolonial historical setting
Home to diverse indigenous societies, Great Lakes trade networks, Indian Ocean ports and Swahili coastal cultures. [9]
Coastal historical networks
Swahili-speaking communities engaged in long-distance Indian Ocean commerce, including contact with Arabian, Persian and South Asian traders. [9]
Zanzibar historical economy
Cloves, trade and Indian Ocean shipping; political history includes the sultanate and British protectorate. [9]
German colonial period
Much of the mainland was incorporated into German East Africa during the late nineteenth century. [9]
British administration
Tanganyika became a League of Nations mandate and later a UN trust territory administered by Britain. [10] [9]
Post-independence economic history
The 1967 Arusha Declaration promoted socialism and self-reliance (ujamaa), followed by subsequent market-oriented reforms. [9]
Multiparty political restoration
Multiparty politics was restored in the 1990s after decades of single-party dominance. [18]
United Nations membership
Tanganyika was admitted December 14, 1961; Zanzibar December 16, 1963; the union continued as one UN member. [10]

People and Society

Primary national lingua franca
Kiswahili (Swahili), widely used across regions in government, mass media and public life. [11]
English
Used in international business, some higher education, law and government functions. [11]
Official languages as reported by EAC
Kiswahili and English (regional institutional reference). [11]
Language-policy caveat
The distinction among national language, official languages and sector-specific instruction varies across legal and institutional sources; Kiswahili is the principal shared public language. [11]
Ethnolinguistic diversity
More than 120 ethnic groups, with many indigenous languages and local traditions. [21]
Selected community languages
Sukuma, Nyamwezi, Chaga, Haya, Gogo, Hehe, Makonde, Maasai and other Bantu and non-Bantu languages. [21]
Zanzibar language heritage
Kiswahili is dominant; Arabic has historical and religious significance in the islands. [17]
Religious composition
Christian and Muslim communities are the largest nationally; estimates are survey-derived rather than a current religious census. [17]
Christian population estimate
About 63% in the cited 2020 Pew survey, as summarized by the U.S. State Department in 2023. [17]
Muslim population estimate
About 34% in the same survey summary; shares are approximate and methodology-dependent. [17]
Religious traditions beyond Christianity and Islam
Indigenous religious practices, Hinduism, Buddhism, Sikhism, Bahá’í communities and non-affiliation are also represented. [17]
Zanzibar religion
Predominantly Muslim; the U.S. Department of State separately cited an estimate of about 99% Muslim. [17]
Christian traditions
Roman Catholic, Lutheran, Anglican, Pentecostal and other Protestant and independent church communities. [17]
Islamic traditions
Predominantly Sunni, with additional Shia, Ibadi and other communities. [17]
Religious geography
Muslim communities are prominent along the coast and on Zanzibar; inland religious composition varies substantially. [17]
Social and cultural heritage
Swahili coastal culture, numerous mainland traditions, oral literature, music, crafts, literature and dance. [21]

Primary, secondary, vocational and tertiary sectors, with Mainland and Zanzibar administrative responsibilities. [22]

Public and private facilities; national priorities include maternal/child health, disease prevention and health-service coverage. [22]

Urbanization
Increasing growth of urban settlements, especially Dar es Salaam and regional cities. [4]
Tourism and natural heritage

President Samia Suluhu Hassan. [6] [7]

Mwigulu Lameck Nchemba (official government and CIA leadership listings). [7] [8]
President of Zanzibar
Hussein Ali Mwinyi. [7] [8]

Parliament (Bunge), with directly elected, reserved, appointed and other legally constituted seats. [18]

CCM secured 270 out of 272; ACT-Wazalendo won two, according to the Inter-Parliamentary Union. [18]
2025 election assessment
African Union observers identified serious irregularities and stated that the election did not meet its democratic-election standards. [19]
Election-related violence
Protests and lethal violence accompanied the October 2025 elections; exact casualty totals remain contested. [19] [20]
Political rights and accountability
Human Rights Watch reported unresolved accountability for abuses around the 2025 election as of October 28, 2026. [20]
Electoral assessment caveat
Official results identify officeholders and seat distributions; they do not by themselves resolve the observer concerns about electoral fairness. [18] [19]

Judiciary

Electoral competition
Multiparty electoral system, but the 2025 process faced significant restrictions and contested legitimacy. [18] [19]
Women in senior executive leadership
Samia Suluhu Hassan became Tanzania’s first woman president in March 2021. [7]

Country Overview

Equatorial Guinea, officially the Republic of Equatorial Guinea, is a Central African state comprising the Río Muni mainland and islands in the Gulf of Guinea, notably Bioko and Annobón. It gained independence from Spain on October 12, 1968, and developed an oil-and-gas-centered economy after major hydrocarbon discoveries in the 1990s. The capital is Ciudad de la Paz in Djibloho, designated by decree in January 2026; government offices are being transferred from the former capital, Malabo, in phases. [5] [6] [7] [10]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Equatorial Guinea; Spanish: República de Guinea Ecuatorial. [4]
Common name
Equatorial Guinea; Spanish: Guinea Ecuatorial.
Current capital
Ciudad de la Paz, Djibloho Province; designated January 2, 2026, with institutional relocation proceeding during 2026. [6] [7]
Former capital
Malabo, on Bioko Island; some ministries and services have continued operating there during the transition. [7]
Largest mainland city
Bata, a leading commercial center and port on the mainland. [13]
Continent / UN M49 subregion
Africa / Middle Africa (UN subregion 017). [4]
Indigenous societies before colonization
Fang and other mainland communities, Bubi societies of Bioko, Annobonese communities and coastal Ndowé peoples have histories predating European rule. [21]
Early European imperial claims
Portugal and later Spain exercised colonial claims over islands and mainland areas; the emergence of the present territory occurred over a long colonial period.
Colonial name
Spanish Guinea (Guinea Española), comprising mainland and island possessions under Spanish administration. [5]
Autonomous colonial administration
Limited self-government was introduced in the 1960s as decolonization advanced.
Independence from Spain
October 12, 1968; the national Independence Day. [5]
First post-independence president
Francisco Macías Nguema, first president following independence in 1968.
1979 political transition
Teodoro Obiang Nguema Mbasogo seized power in a military coup on August 3, 1979; he remains president in 2026. [19] [14]
Modern fundamental law
Constitutional reforms in 1982, 1991 and 2011–2012 shaped the present political framework; always distinguish formal law from subsequent political practice. [17]
Major resource turning point
Large-scale offshore oil and gas production expanded during the 1990s and 2000s, transforming export revenue and public finances. [10]
Capital designation in 2026
The capital moved by law from Malabo to Ciudad de la Paz on January 2, 2026, with a staged government transfer. [6] [7]

Geography and Population

Total surface area
Approximately 28,052 km² (10,831 sq. mi.), including the mainland and island territory; geographic measurement and rounding vary slightly by source. [3] [13]
Mainland region
Río Muni (also called Mbini), lying between Cameroon to the north and Gabon to the east and south. [13]
Principal islands
Bioko, Annobón, Corisco and smaller islands and islets in the Gulf of Guinea. [13]
Bioko Island
Volcanic Gulf of Guinea island containing Malabo and Pico Basilé; separated from the continental portion of the country.
Annobón
Remote volcanic island south of the equator, geographically separated from Bioko and the mainland.
Bordering countries
Cameroon and Gabon (land borders on mainland Africa).
Maritime setting
Atlantic Ocean and Gulf of Guinea, with important offshore hydrocarbon production areas. [10]
2025 population
1,938,431 (World Bank annual midyear population estimate). [1] [2]
2024 population
1,892,516 (World Bank midyear estimate). [2]
Latest widely cited national census
2015 national census, approximately 1.2 million residents. Its method and reference year differ from internationally modeled 2025 estimates. [20] [2]
Annual population growth
Approximately 2.4% in 2025 (World Bank). [1]
Population density
Approximately 69 persons per km² using 2025 population and UN surface-area conventions (UN country profile). [3]
Population distribution
Settlement is concentrated in the mainland towns and rural communities, Bioko’s urban centers, and coastal and transport corridors.
Principal urban centers
Malabo, Bata, Ebebiyín, Mongomo, Luba, Evinayong and Ciudad de la Paz.
Climate
Equatorial and tropical, generally warm and humid, with wet and dry seasons varying by location and altitude.
Physical landscape
Mainland tropical rainforests and river systems; mountainous volcanic terrain on Bioko; coastal plains and small offshore islands.
Highest mountain
Pico Basilé on Bioko (approximately 3,011 m); an extinct or dormant volcanic highland.
Natural ecosystems
Lowland and montane rainforest, mangroves, coastal and marine habitats, and island biodiversity. [26]
Protected-area examples
Monte Alén National Park, Pico Basilé National Park, Luba Caldera Scientific Reserve and Annobón Nature Reserve appear in 2026 UNESCO tentative nominations. [26]
Administrative geography
Eight provinces, including the newer Djibloho province where Ciudad de la Paz is situated. [6] [8]

Population comparability: The 2015 national census and 2025 World Bank midyear estimate use different periods and methods. The World Bank 2025 figure is the standardized contemporary benchmark used for cross-country comparison. [2] [20]

Economy and International Trade

Economic structure
Small, hydrocarbon-dependent economy with petroleum and natural gas playing a leading role in exports, fiscal receipts and investment. [10]
Nominal GDP
US$12.82 billion (World Bank, 2025 current-dollar series; rounded). [1]
GDP per capita
US$6,615 (World Bank, 2025 current dollars). This is output per person, not median income or average wages. [1]
Real GDP growth
−5.8% in 2025 (World Bank annual real growth measure); differs from projections published by the IMF before the year ended. [1] [10]
Inflation
2.9% in 2024, consumer-price inflation (World Bank), subject to subsequent release revisions. [1]
Modeled unemployment
8.3% in 2025 under the World Bank/ILO modeled unemployment series, not a direct household-census observation. [1]
Currency
Central African CFA franc (XAF; often written FCFA), issued by the Bank of Central African States (BEAC). [12]
Monetary union
Member of the Economic and Monetary Community of Central Africa (CEMAC). [12]
Merchandise exports
US$2.979 billion in 2024 (UNCTAD country profile, goods only; methodology differs from partner mirror data). [9]
Merchandise imports
US$1.517 billion in 2024 (UNCTAD country profile, goods only). [9]
Merchandise trade balance
Surplus of about US$1.461 billion in 2024 in UNCTAD’s goods series; exports minus imports can differ slightly due to rounding. [9]
Major export sectors
Crude petroleum, natural gas and liquefied natural gas, hydrocarbon-linked chemical products, and smaller timber and agricultural exports. [10] [11]
Crude oil export markets
2024 trading-partner data confirm substantial crude exports to the European Union (including Spain and Italy) and China; partner observations must not be summed indiscriminately across EU and individual countries. [11]
Natural gas industry
Gas production and liquefaction support energy exports and related industrial activity; output and prices affect public revenues. [10]
Other goods and agriculture
Cocoa, timber and agricultural products remain significant to parts of the economy but are much smaller export categories than hydrocarbons. [10]
Domestic economic activities
Public administration, construction, logistics, commercial services, small-scale farming, fisheries, forestry and transport.
Economic diversification
A continuing policy objective given declining hydrocarbon production, concentrated export revenues and constraints on private-sector development. [10]
Public finance sensitivity
Hydrocarbon prices, production volume and sector-tax receipts strongly influence government budgets and external accounts. [10]
Trade-series comparability
UNCTAD’s 2024 country-reported goods totals are not directly comparable with summed partner mirror imports or later European-Commission trade estimates; no mixed-basis trade balance is calculated. [9] [11]
Service exports
Recent services trade series may be unavailable or incomplete; no unverified figure is supplied for a specific year. [9]

People and Society

Official languages
Spanish, French and Portuguese. [13] [21]
Main administrative language
Spanish, the principal language of central government, formal schooling and national administration. [21]
Other official-language use
French and Portuguese have formal status, but their everyday use is much less widespread than Spanish. [21]
Major indigenous languages
Fang, Bubi, Ndowé/Kombe, Kwasio and other local languages; community distribution varies geographically. [21]
Annobonese language
Fa d’Ambô (Annobonese), a Portuguese-lexifier creole spoken on Annobón and in diaspora communities. [21]
Other languages
English-based Pidgin and community languages are used in some urban, coastal and island settings. [21]
Ethnolinguistic composition
Fang people are the largest ethnolinguistic group; Bubi, Ndowé, Annobonese and other communities have distinct cultural traditions. [21]
Religious composition, dated survey
The most recently cited government religious estimate, from 2015, was about 88% Roman Catholic, 5% Protestant, 2% Muslim and 5% other; these are not percentages from a 2025 or 2026 census. [20]
Alternative religious estimate
World Religion Database estimates for 2020 place Christians near 90%, Muslims near 4%, and other or no religious beliefs near 6%; methods differ from the 2015 government figures. [20]
Largest Christian tradition
Roman Catholic Christianity; other Protestant and evangelical churches also operate. [20]
Other religious communities
Muslim communities and adherents of Indigenous religious traditions; affiliations and customary practices may overlap. [20]
State religion
No constitutionally established national religion; the legal and practical treatment of religious organizations is not uniform. [20]
Life expectancy at birth
Approximately 64 years in 2024 (World Bank); revised datasets may change the value. [1]
Education and languages
Spanish is central to the formal education system, while children may speak local languages at home. [21]
Urbanization and internal mobility
Growth in Malabo, Bata and other centers is associated with public-sector work, energy-related services and migration.
Cultural traditions
Regional linguistic and artistic traditions, oral history, music, dance and community practices reflect island and mainland diversity.
National historical memory
Spanish rule, 1968 independence, the Macías presidency, the 1979 coup and the petroleum era remain major reference points. [5] [19]
Religious data caveat
Religious percentages come from dated estimates rather than a regularly published national population census; do not imply they measure all residents in 2026. [20]

Religious-survey limitation: The cited government composition is from 2015. It is included with its date as a historical statistical reference, not as a verified 2026 breakdown. Spanish, French and Portuguese share official status, but this does not imply equal usage. [20] [21]

Government and Institutions

Form of government
Constitutional presidential republic in formal law; political power is highly concentrated in the presidency in practice. [17] [19]
Head of state
President Teodoro Obiang Nguema Mbasogo, in office since taking power in August 1979 and confirmed as incumbent in 2026. [14] [19]
Head of government / coordination
President heads the state and executive; Prime Minister Manuel Osa Nsue Nsua is responsible for administrative coordination under presidential authority. [14] [15]
Prime minister
Manuel Osa Nsue Nsua, reappointed June 19, 2026 and sworn in June 22 after the cabinet’s collective resignation. [15]
Vice president
Teodoro Nguema Obiang Mangue, who has held the vice presidency and substantial security responsibilities. [19]
June 2026 executive reorganization
The outgoing cabinet collectively resigned June 2026; a new government was constituted that month, with the same prime minister reappointed. [15] [16]
Foundational constitutional law
Fundamental Law of Equatorial Guinea, with major revisions adopted through 2011 and implemented in 2012; subsequent enactments and decrees must also be considered. [17]
Presidential term
Seven-year term under the revised constitutional text, which specifies a two-consecutive-term rule; application and incumbent tenure require separate political assessment. [17] [19]
Most recent presidential election
November 20, 2022; President Obiang was declared reelected. [18]
Election context
International rights monitors report major constraints on opposition, press and electoral competition, distinct from the written constitutional electoral rules. [19]
Legislature
Bicameral parliament comprising the Chamber of Deputies and Senate. [17] [18]
Chamber of Deputies
100 elected seats in the standard parliamentary structure; latest general election cycle in November 2022. [18]
Senate
70 seats in the standard constitutional/electoral structure, including 55 elected and 15 presidentially appointed senators; some source tallies include ex officio members separately. [18]
Political party structure
The Democratic Party of Equatorial Guinea (PDGE) dominates the governing political coalition and legislature. [19]
Executive branch
President, vice president, prime minister, cabinet and executive departments, with ministers appointed under presidential decrees. [17] [16]
Judicial institutions
Supreme Court and other national courts operate under the Fundamental Law; independent assessments identify pronounced limits on judicial autonomy. [17] [19]
Administrative provinces
Eight provinces: Annobón, Bioko Norte, Bioko Sur, Centro Sur, Djibloho, Kié-Ntem, Litoral and Wele-Nzas. [6]
Capital change
Ciudad de la Paz designated the capital in January 2026; government institutions are transferring there in stages from Malabo. [6] [7]
Provincial jurisdiction in 2026
The 2026 capital-related law also adjusted Djibloho’s provincial territory. [8]
Civil rights and governance
The Constitution formally recognizes rights and institutions; independent monitors assess restrictions on speech, assembly, political opposition and government accountability. [17] [19]

Formal institutions versus political conditions: Presidential terms, parliamentary seats and legal rights describe constitutional arrangements. The separate Freedom House 2026 assessment addresses electoral competition and government practice; the two categories of information are not interchangeable. [17] [19]

International Profile

United Nations
Member since November 12, 1968, shortly after independence. [22]
African Union
Member of the African Union and former Organization of African Unity; admitted to the OAU on October 12, 1968. [23]
Regional economic organizations
CEMAC and the Economic Community of Central African States (ECCAS); an important part of the Central African regional economy. [12]
Monetary cooperation
Uses the XAF Central African CFA franc issued by BEAC, in a six-country monetary union. [12]
Community of Portuguese Language Countries
Full CPLP member since July 23, 2014, despite Portuguese being a minority-use official language. [24] [21]
Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries
OPEC member since 2017. [27]
World Trade Organization
Accession negotiations in progress, rather than confirmed full membership, under a working party established in 2008. [25]
Key external economic relationships
Hydrocarbon export markets link the country with buyers in Europe and Asia; destination rankings depend on customs and partner-reporting definitions. [11]
Neighboring countries
Cameroon and Gabon by land, with Atlantic and Gulf of Guinea maritime connections.
UNESCO World Heritage Convention
State party since 2010; no properties inscribed on the World Heritage List as of November 2026. [26]
UNESCO Tentative List
Six sites submitted in April 2026, including Monte Alén, Pico Basilé, Annobón and Corisco; tentative status is not UNESCO inscription. [26]
National time zone
West Africa Time (UTC+01:00), without a regular seasonal daylight-saving change.
International telephone code
+240.
Internet country-code top-level domain
.gq.
International statistical identifiers
ISO 3166-1 GQ / GNQ / 226; UN M49 geographic classification: Middle Africa. [4]

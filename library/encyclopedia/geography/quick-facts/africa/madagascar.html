Country Overview

Madagascar, officially the Republic of Madagascar, is an island nation in the southwestern Indian Ocean east of mainland Africa. Its capital is Antananarivo, and its total surface area is approximately 587,041 km². The island has an exceptional mix of ecosystems and endemic species, and its economy combines agriculture, mining, manufacturing, tourism and other services. It became independent from France on June 26, 1960. [3] [2] [17]

Madagascar is undergoing a political transition following mass protests and a military seizure of power in October 2025. As of November 5, 2026, Michaël Randrianirina serves as President of the Refoundation and Mamitiana Rajaonarison serves as prime minister. On November 4, 2026, the High Constitutional Court held that a referendum on a new constitution could legally precede a presidential election; the ruling did not itself adopt a new constitution or hold an election. [5] [6] [8]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Madagascar (Repoblikan’i Madagasikara; République de Madagascar). [5]
Short name
Madagascar; in Malagasy, Madagasikara.
Demonym
Malagasy (people, language or adjective); Madagascan also appears in English usage.
Capital
Antananarivo (historically Tananarive). [3] [5]
Principal cities
Antananarivo, Toamasina (Tamatave), Antsirabe, Fianarantsoa, Mahajanga, Toliara and Antsiranana. [5]
Continent / UN subregion
Africa / Eastern Africa; geographically an Indian Ocean island country. [22] [3]
National Day
June 26, anniversary of independence in 1960. [5] [17]
Independent state established
June 26, 1960, following negotiations with France. [17] [20]
October 1958 statehood milestone
Malagasy Republic proclaimed October 14, 1958, with self-government inside the French Community; full independence followed in 1960. [17]
Precolonial peoples and societies
Austronesian and African population histories, with diverse Malagasy communities, kingdoms and coastal trading networks.
Historic long-distance connections
Indian Ocean travel linked Madagascar with eastern Africa, South Asia, Southeast Asia and Arab–Swahili commercial networks.
Merina kingdom
A central highlands monarchy expanded over parts of the island in the nineteenth century; the degree of royal control differed by region.
French colonization
France annexed Madagascar in 1896 and removed the reigning monarchy. [20]
1947 uprising
A major anti-colonial uprising against French rule began in 1947 and was suppressed with severe violence. [20]
Independence-era first president
Philibert Tsiranana led the independent republic beginning in 1960. [20]
1972 political transition
Social unrest and military intervention ended the Tsiranana administration. [20]
Second Republic / socialist period
Didier Ratsiraka led a socialist-oriented political order established in 1975; the system later underwent multiparty reforms. [20]
1990s democratization
Constitutional and electoral reforms followed the end of the one-party era, while periods of instability continued.
2009 political crisis
Andry Rajoelina came to power after a contested transition; the 2009–2013 period disrupted constitutional politics. [5]
2010 constitutional framework
The Fourth Republic framework was established by the 2010 Constitution; subsequent events created a transitional context. [8] [9]
2018 / 2023 presidential milestones
Andry Rajoelina was elected in 2018 and again in the contested 2023 presidential election. [5]
October 2025 change of government
Large protests, linked partly to electricity and water disruption, preceded the military seizure of power and political transition. [14] [6]
October 2025 transitional investiture
Colonel Michaël Randrianirina became President of the Refoundation on October 17, 2025. [5]
Transition timeframe
Authorities announced a 24-month refoundation roadmap aiming at constitutional reform and elections by 2027; this is a plan, not proof of completion. [5]
November 2026 constitutional opinion
On November 4, 2026 the High Constitutional Court allowed, in principle, a referendum on a replacement constitution before a presidential election. [8]
Current political characterization
A republic undergoing a contested post-2025 transition under a president of the refoundation; not equivalent to an ordinary completed five-year electoral mandate. [5] [8]
Traditional flag
A vertical white band at the hoist and horizontal red (upper) and green (lower) fields on the fly side.
National identity note
Malagasy cultural identities are diverse; historical kingdoms and ethnic communities do not map neatly onto a single uniform origin story.

Historical dating: 1958 marks the autonomous Malagasy Republic; June 26, 1960 marks independence. The 2025 political transition did not create a different sovereign country, and November 4, 2026 was an opinion on referendum procedure, not a new constitutional founding. [17] [5] [8]

Geography and Population

Island setting
Madagascar is situated in the western Indian Ocean, southeast of mainland Africa, across the Mozambique Channel. [3]
Main island
Madagascar is among the world’s largest islands; the sovereign state also includes smaller offshore islands. [2]
Total surface area
587,041 km² (UNData geographical statistical series). [3]
Land area comparison
Do not treat total surface area, land-only area and coastal-island enumeration as equivalent measures. [3]
Area rank context
The main island is often described as the world’s fourth-largest island after Greenland, New Guinea and Borneo.
Land boundaries
None with another sovereign state; Madagascar is an island country.
Maritime context
Indian Ocean to the east and the Mozambique Channel to the west; neighboring island states and territories include Comoros, Mauritius and Réunion.
Relative location
Off the coast of Mozambique in southeastern Africa.
Major highland zone
Central Highlands, including the Antananarivo area and mountain chains.
Highest mountain
Maromokotro, approximately 2,876 m, within the Tsaratanana Massif.
Eastern escarpment
A steep, wetter eastern side leading into forested coastal lowlands.
Western landscapes
Broad sedimentary lowlands, dry forest zones, savannas and river basins.
Southern landscapes
Semi-arid to arid environments, especially portions of the far south.
Climate pattern
Tropical conditions with substantial regional differences; wetter east, cooler central highlands and generally drier south. [2]
Wet / cyclone season
Tropical cyclones most commonly affect Madagascar during the southern summer, particularly November–April.
Notable 2026 hazard
Cyclone Gezani compounded infrastructure and reconstruction challenges in 2026. [6]
Major rivers
Betsiboka, Tsiribihina, Mangoky and Onilahy, among others.
Major lakes
Lake Alaotra is a major freshwater lake and wetland area.
Biogeographic significance
Long island isolation fostered high endemism, including distinct lemurs and other endemic plant and animal groups. [15]
Protected landscapes
Dry forests, eastern rainforests and limestone formations have globally recognized conservation importance. [15]
UNESCO natural properties
Andrefana Dry Forests and Rainforests of the Atsinanana are recognized under UNESCO World Heritage designations. [15]
Forest share of land
17.3% (World Bank indicator, 2024); reflects the source’s forest definition and land-area denominator. [1]
Population
32,740,678 people, World Bank estimate for 2025. [1]
Most recent completed census
25,674,196 enumerated residents in the 2018 RGPH-3 final census. [4]
2025 population growth
Approximately 2.4% (World Bank annual indicator). [1]
Population density (simple)
About 55.8 people per km² using 2025 World Bank population divided by 587,041 km²; not an official census density. [1] [3]
Urban / rural census
The 2018 census reported approximately 80.7% of residents living in rural areas; this is not a 2025 estimate. [4]
Settlement concentration
Dense settlement in parts of the Central Highlands and major urban corridors contrasts with sparsely settled areas elsewhere. [4]
Major economic port city
Toamasina, on the eastern coast, provides a major seaport.
Regional organization
24 regions after the 2021 Vatovavy split and 2023 creation of Ambatosoa from Analanjirofo. [18]
Administrative heritage
Six historical provinces remain part of the inherited territorial framework, though 24 regions organize much day-to-day administration. [18]
Time zone
East Africa Time (UTC+3), without seasonal daylight-saving adjustment.

Geographic and demographic scope: 587,041 km² is the UNData total surface measure. The finalized 2018 census enumerated residents; 32,740,678 for 2025 is a World Bank demographic estimate. Older geography sources listing only 22 or 23 regions are outdated; 24 regions are reflected in the latest administrative classification. [3] [4] [1] [18]

People and Society

Population identity
Malagasy communities have distinct regional histories and share forms of Malagasy language and cultural heritage.
Major regional communities
Examples include Merina, Betsileo, Betsimisaraka, Sakalava, Antandroy, Antaimoro, Sihanaka, Tsimihety and other communities; not an exhaustive list.
Austronesian and African heritage
The Malagasy language and cultural history reflect both Austronesian and African connections across the Indian Ocean.
Official languages
Malagasy and French under Article 4 of the Constitution as confirmed in a 2026 High Constitutional Court decision. [9]
Everyday national lingua franca
Malagasy is widely spoken in numerous regional varieties; official French is particularly relevant in administration, education and some business settings. [9] [5]
Language family
Malagasy belongs to the Austronesian language family.
Regional Malagasy varieties
Distinctive language varieties occur across the central highlands, east, north, west and south; social use is not uniform.
French-language status
French remains a constitutional official language; an August 2026 effort to elevate Malagasy at its expense was ruled unconstitutional. [9]
Religion: Christianity
Roman Catholic, Protestant (including FJKM and Lutheran), Anglican, evangelical and other Christian traditions are practiced. [19]
Religion: Islam
Islam is practiced in Madagascar, including coastal and immigrant communities, with varied local histories. [19]
Religion: indigenous practice
Ancestral veneration and local religious practices continue, sometimes interwoven with Christian or Muslim identification. [19]
Religion-data caution
The widely repeated 1993 census religion percentages do not constitute a valid contemporary 2025/2026 religious breakdown. [19]
Population census context
The 2018 RGPH-3 is an enumerated count; World Bank 2025 figures are demographic estimates, not a new census. [4] [1]
2018 female census count
13,015,251 women recorded in the 2018 final census. [4]
2018 male census count
12,658,945 men recorded in the 2018 final census. [4]
2018 household size
An average of around 4.2 people per household in the 2018 final census. [4]
2018 age profile
Median age around 18 years in the 2018 census, reflecting a young population; not a current median-age estimate. [4]
Life expectancy
Approximately 64 years at birth (World Bank, 2024, rounded). [1]
Electricity access
42.5% of population with access to electricity in 2024 (World Bank series). [1]
Internet use
19% of individuals using the internet in 2024 (World Bank series). [1]
Safely managed sanitation
13% of population using safely managed sanitation services in 2024 (World Bank series). [1]
Education
Formal schooling uses Malagasy and French in varying roles; educational access and quality vary across regions. [5]
Adult literacy comparison
France Diplomatie gives a 77% adult literacy indicator for 2022; interpretation depends on definition and reporting year. [5]
Health and public services
Geographic disparities in healthcare, water and electricity supply are major development concerns. [6] [2]
Important cultural forms
Music, weaving, oral histories, ancestral commemorations and regionally distinctive food traditions are important but vary locally.
Staple foods
Rice plays a central role in many diets alongside cassava, tubers, legumes, vegetables and regional fish or livestock products.
UNESCO cultural property
Royal Hill of Ambohimanga is recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage cultural property. [15]
World Heritage total
Three UNESCO World Heritage properties are listed for Madagascar in the source register; the Andrefana Dry Forests property includes the earlier Tsingy de Bemaraha designation. [15]

Demographic caution: Avoid presenting 1993 religious-affiliation estimates as contemporary population shares. The nation has longstanding Christian, Muslim and indigenous practices, sometimes overlapping. The constitution continues to recognize Malagasy and French as official languages following the court’s August 2026 ruling. [19] [9]

Government and Institutions

State form
Unitary republic, functioning under a political transition following the October 2025 change of leadership. [5] [8]
Current head of state
Colonel Michaël Randrianirina, President of the Refoundation since October 17, 2025. [5] [7]
Current head of government
Prime Minister Mamitiana Rajaonarison, appointed March 2026. [6] [5]
Head-of-state basis
Transition and investiture followed the October 2025 political crisis; do not describe the incumbent as having been popularly elected in a new presidential election. [5] [14]
Constitutional framework
The 2010 Fourth Republic Constitution remains the point of reference for 2026 High Constitutional Court decisions during institutional change. [9] [8]
Judicial constitutional authority
High Constitutional Court (Haute Cour Constitutionnelle, HCC). [8]
Current cabinet
Prime minister and ministers operate under the President of the Refoundation; line-up changed in March 2026. [7] [5]
Ministerial government
Council of Ministers and ministries cover economic, security, justice, social and infrastructure affairs; officeholders may change rapidly. [7]
Legislative structure (constitutional design)
The 2010 framework includes a National Assembly and Senate, but this does not describe uninterrupted functioning of both houses during the transition. [10] [8]
National Assembly
The National Assembly exercised legislative power alone after the Senate mandate expired, pending a new upper chamber; it also has a role in a proposed referendum procedure. [11] [8]
Senate mandate expiry
The HCC ruled that senators’ and the standing bureau’s mandates expired January 18, 2026 without possible extension. [10]
Senate interim affairs
Following mandate expiry, designated Senate officials could handle routine administrative and financial affairs. [10]
Constitutional referendum status
As of November 5, 2026 a new-constitution referendum had not been completed; HCC Opinion 06-HCC/AV addresses its legal permissibility. [8]
Constitutional referendum sequencing
HCC ruled that a referendum adopting a new Constitution could precede the next presidential election in the transitional context. [8]
Approval threshold for proposed constitution
Court opinion calls for approval by three-quarters of the National Assembly before referral to a referendum. [8]
Multiple-choice referendum options
HCC said multiple alternatives could be permitted subject to adapting relevant electoral and referendum legislation. [8]
Presidential election schedule
The 2026 court opinion states that a new presidential election calendar had not yet been set; transitional targets are not an official completed election. [8] [5]
Anti-corruption priority
Government and IMF discussions identified governance reforms and accountability as development priorities. [6]
Public utility issue
Electricity and water interruptions helped precipitate 2025 protests; utility JIRAMA reform remained a policy priority in October 2026. [6]
Legal language status
Malagasy and French remain constitutionally co-official; a 2026 law on Malagasy primacy failed constitutional review. [9]
Local government divisions
24 regions recognized as subnational units, including Ambatosoa and Vatovavy. [18]
National elections: historical baseline
The last pre-transition presidential election took place in 2023; 2025 leadership change was not an ordinary presidential vote. [5]
Executive / legislative separation
The transitional executive and the National Assembly retain distinct constitutional roles subject to evolving HCC interpretations. [8]
AU response to 2025 takeover
The African Union Peace and Security Council condemned the 2025 military seizure of power and suspended participation in AU organs. [14]
International recognition caution
Recognition of Madagascar as a sovereign state is distinct from recognition of or participation by its transitional authorities in particular organizations. [14] [16]
Governance status as of cutoff
November 5, 2026: interim refoundation administration, no enacted replacement constitution and no new presidential election described by cited official sources. [5] [8]

Political-status caution: Madagascar’s 2025 leadership change, its 2026 Senate mandate expiry and the November 4, 2026 constitutional-court opinion make static older government profiles materially misleading. The HCC opinion permitted a possible new-constitution referendum prior to the presidency election but did not mean either vote had already occurred. [5] [10] [8]

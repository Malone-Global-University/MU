Country Overview

São Tomé and Príncipe, officially the Democratic Republic of São Tomé and Príncipe, is a small equatorial island country in the Gulf of Guinea, off the west-central African coast. Its national capital is São Tomé. Formerly a Portuguese colony organized around Atlantic plantations, it became independent on July 12, 1975. Since constitutional reforms in 1990–1991 it has maintained a competitive multiparty political system. [2] [22] [6]

President Carlos Manuel Vila Nova won reelection in July 2026. Prime Minister Américo Ramos was the latest independently confirmed head of government in sources reviewed for this edition; government-formation negotiations followed the inconclusive September 2026 parliamentary election. Cocoa, palm products, fishing, services and tourism contribute to the economy. [8] [9] [11] [14]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Democratic Republic of São Tomé and Príncipe (Portuguese: República Democrática de São Tomé e Príncipe). [6]
Common short name
São Tomé and Príncipe; also written Sao Tome and Principe without diacritics.
Demonym
São Toméan; São Toméan and Príncipean in descriptive English; Santomense in Portuguese.
Capital
São Tomé, on the northeast coast of the larger island. [2]
Continent
Africa.
United Nations geographical subregion
Middle Africa, also commonly described as Central Africa and the Gulf of Guinea. [2]
Physical setting
An Atlantic archipelago of two principal volcanic islands and neighboring islets, just north of the Equator. [5]
Indigenous settlement before Portuguese arrival
The islands are generally understood to have been uninhabited before Portuguese maritime colonization in the late fifteenth century. [22]
Early Portuguese contact
Portuguese navigators reached the islands during the 1470s; settlement developed in the late fifteenth century. [22]
Colonial authority
Portugal; the islands became plantation colonies in the Portuguese Atlantic empire. [22]
Historical plantation economy
Sugar production was prominent in the sixteenth century; coffee and cocoa became especially important in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. [22] [18]
Enslavement and coerced labor
Colonial agricultural production depended historically on slavery and, later, forced or coercive migrant labor. [22] [18]
Independence
July 12, 1975, from Portugal. [22]
First president after independence
Manuel Pinto da Costa, leader of the Movement for the Liberation of São Tomé and Príncipe (MLSTP). [22]
Original republican constitution
Approved November 5, 1975, following independence. [6]
Multiparty constitutional reforms
Constitutional changes in 1990 opened the political system to opposition parties and competitive elections. [6] [22]
First multiparty national elections
Held in 1991 after the 1990 political reforms. [22]
2003 constitutional revision
Constitutional Law No. 1/2003 revised the democratic constitutional framework. [6] [7]
Modern state form
Sovereign, multiparty, semi-presidential democratic republic. [6]
National Independence Day
July 12; public commemoration of independence in 1975.
National currency name
Dobra, ISO 4217 currency code STN following the 2018 redenomination. [2] [5]
International dialing code
+239.
Country ISO codes
ST (alpha-2), STP (alpha-3), 678 (numeric).
Country-code top-level domain
.st.
Time zone
Greenwich Mean Time / UTC+0 year-round; no routinely observed daylight-saving time.
Location of national population
Population is concentrated on São Tomé Island; Príncipe has an autonomous administrative region. [4]

Geography and Population

Geographic location
Gulf of Guinea in the equatorial eastern Atlantic, offshore from west-central Africa. [5]
Principal islands
São Tomé and Príncipe, with a number of smaller inhabited and uninhabited islets. [5]
Archipelago status
Island sovereign state with no land borders.
Nearest continental coast
West-central Africa, with Gabon and Equatorial Guinea across the Gulf of Guinea.
National area—domestic source
1,001 km² in the national government and INE geographic reference. [5] [3]
United Nations area series
964 km² in the UN Statistics Division country profile; differing published geographic reference figures are kept separately attributed. [2]

Island Príncipe—reported area
Approximately 142 km² in official 2024 census material and government summaries. [5] [4]
Ocean
Atlantic Ocean.
Terrain
Steep volcanic mountains, densely vegetated slopes, coastal lowlands and deeply incised valleys. [20] [21]
Highest point
Pico de São Tomé, about 2,024 metres above sea level.
Distinctive landform
Pico Cão Grande, a prominent volcanic plug on São Tomé Island.
Volcanic origin
Islands formed along the Cameroon volcanic line in the Gulf of Guinea. [21]
Climate
Humid tropical maritime climate, varying with rainfall, elevation and exposure to trade winds. [5] [20]
Seasonality
Rainier and relatively drier periods; local exposure and elevation change rainfall patterns.
Forest ecosystems
Lowland and montane tropical rainforests with high numbers of endemic species. [20] [21]
Marine ecosystems
Coastal fisheries, beaches, nearshore habitats, turtles and seabirds. [20] [21]

Economic structure
Small island economy based on services, agriculture, trade, tourism, fishing and public activity. [1] [5]

US$981.29 million, World Bank current-dollar series. [1]

International GDP-series distinction
The United Nations pocketbook and UNCTAD may publish different GDP values due to publication vintage and national-accounts revisions; these are not alternative currencies. [2] [1] [15]
Currency
São Tomé and Príncipe dobra (STN). [5] [2]
Currency redenomination
STN replaced the former dobra code STD in 2018, with 1 new dobra equal to 1,000 old dobras.
Exchange-rate arrangement
Currency officially pegged to the euro at 24.5 STN per EUR. [5]
Central bank
Banco Central de São Tomé e Príncipe (BCSTP).
Principal cash crops
Cocoa and coffee, with palm and coconut-derived products also important. [5] [14]
Cocoa economy
Cocoa beans and processed cocoa supply a major share of merchandise-export earnings. [14]
Palm oil trade
Crude palm oil was a major recorded product in the WTO 2023 merchandise-export breakdown. [14]
Fisheries
Artisanal and commercial fishing contribute to livelihoods and food supply. [20]
Tourism
Nature-based, heritage, beach and ecotourism centered on the islands’ landscapes and coastal environment. [20] [21]
Oil sector status
Offshore hydrocarbon exploration has been pursued, but the country is not a substantial established crude-oil exporter as of this edition.
Energy supply
Diesel-fired generation and renewable-energy development are important to electricity planning.
Infrastructure constraints
Island shipping logistics, costly fuel imports and power reliability affect private-sector development. [23] [1]
Principal import necessities
Rice and other foodstuffs, petroleum products, power-generation equipment, vehicles and construction materials. [14]

People and Society

Official language
Portuguese, used in public administration, schools and formal media. [6] [16]
Common home and community languages
Forro, Angolar and Lung’ie (Principense), Portuguese-lexifier creole languages with distinct histories.
Forro language
A Portuguese-based creole especially associated with São Tomé Island.
Angolar language
A Portuguese-based creole associated particularly with communities of the island of São Tomé.
Lung’ie language
A Portuguese-based creole associated with Príncipe Island.
Other spoken languages
Cabo Verdean Creole and immigrant languages, alongside some French and English.
Official-language distinction
Regional creoles are culturally important but Portuguese is the constitutionally official language. [6]
Language survey caveat
Language proficiency statistics frequently derive from 2012-era material and are not a 2025 language census. [16]
Population background
Society developed from African, European and creole communities linked to Atlantic plantation migration and trade. [22]
Major historical communities
Forros, Angolares, descendants of contracted laborers and mixed-heritage families; categories carry distinct local histories. [22]
Principal religion
Christianity, with Roman Catholicism historically the largest single denomination. [16]
Catholic identification—2012 census
55.7% in the religious breakdown reproduced by the U.S. State Department; it is a 2012, not 2025, census percentage. [16]
Seventh-day Adventists—2012
4.1% according to the cited 2012 distribution. [16]
Assemblies of God—2012
3.4% according to the cited 2012 distribution. [16]
New Apostolic Church—2012
2.9% according to the cited 2012 distribution. [16]
Other Christian communities
Methodist, evangelical, Pentecostal and other denominations and churches. [16]
Islam
A small Muslim minority; 2012 and church-affiliated estimates are not directly comparable with recent household counts. [16]
No religious affiliation—2012
21.2% recorded in the commonly cited 2012 breakdown. [16]
2024 census religion reporting
The new national census report publishes counts by denomination, including many unknown or unclassified responses; no single 2012 percentage should be treated as the 2024 share. [4]
Religious freedom
The constitution provides for freedom of religion and conscience. [6] [16]
Education language
Portuguese is the principal language of formal instruction.
Education policy priorities
School access, teacher capacity, learning outcomes and secondary education opportunities.
National cultural traditions
Tchiloli performance, dança-congo, musical and dramatic traditions, festivals and local storytelling.
Food culture
Fish, plantains, breadfruit, cassava, bananas, cocoa products and palm-derived ingredients.
Celebrated cultural heritage
Historic roças preserve built landscapes and memories of plantation agriculture and labor migration. [18]
National heritage listing—2026
The Roças of São Tomé and Príncipe became the country’s first UNESCO World Heritage property in 2026. [17]
Roças component sites
Monte Café, Água Izé, Diogo Vaz, São João, Sundy and Belo Monte. [18]
Natural heritage
Endemic flora and fauna, oceanic birds, turtles and volcanic forests protected through conservation programs. [20] [21]
Health-system priorities
Primary healthcare, child and maternal health, communicable-disease prevention and reliable health infrastructure.
Sport
Football (soccer) is among the most widely followed sports.

Religion-year caution: The percentages above are explicitly from the 2012 religious composition series. The 2024 INE census report provides newer counts, but a large unclassified/undetermined category makes unqualified percentage comparisons misleading. [16] [4]

Government and Institutions

Semi-presidential, multiparty democratic republic with president, prime minister and National Assembly. [6] [10]
Constitutional basis
1975 constitution with major multiparty reforms in 1990 and a significant 2003 revision. [6] [7]
Head of state—November 2026
President Carlos Manuel Vila Nova. [8] [9]
Presidential election—2026
Carlos Vila Nova won reelection on July 19, 2026. [8]
Second presidential term
Inaugurated in September 2026 after the July presidential vote. [8]
President’s constitutional functions
Head of state with constitutional powers including the appointment of a prime minister following consultation with parliamentary parties. [6]
Head of government—latest confirmed
Américo d’Oliveira dos Ramos, prime minister since January 2025; official government-formation updates after September 2026 should be checked before publication revisions. [9] [12]

Parliamentary negotiations followed the September 27 election; the election result alone does not appoint a successor prime minister. [11] [12]

55 elected seats. [10]

Cabinet

Judiciary

Country Overview

Togo, officially the Togolese Republic (République togolaise), is a narrow West African country on the Gulf of Guinea, bordered by Ghana, Burkina Faso and Benin. Its capital and largest metropolitan centre is Lomé. The territory passed through German colonial rule, League of Nations mandates and UN trusteeship before French-administered Togoland became independent on April 27, 1960. The economy combines agriculture, phosphate mining, manufacturing, services and transport through the Port of Lomé. In May 2025, following a 2024 constitutional reform, the country moved to a parliamentary system: Jean-Lucien Savi de Tové serves as president and Faure Gnassingbé as President of the Council of Ministers and executive leader. Present political form

Precolonial political setting
A diverse area with Ewe, Mina, Kabye, Tem and other communities; coastal trade connected local polities with Atlantic commercial networks.
Coastal settlement history
Lomé, Aného and adjoining coastal settlements developed as regional trading centres.
German protectorate
German Togoland (Togoland) was established in 1884, within the colonial partition of West Africa. [18]
World War I turning point
Allied forces occupied German Togoland in 1914; the territory was subsequently partitioned between French and British administrations. [18]
League of Nations mandates
After World War I, the territory was divided into French and British mandates. [18]
United Nations trusteeship era
Following World War II, French Togoland and British Togoland became UN trust territories. [18]
British Togoland outcome
Most of British Togoland was incorporated into independent Ghana in 1957, following a 1956 plebiscite. [18]
French Togoland outcome
French-administered Togoland became independent as the Republic of Togo in April 1960. [18]
First post-independence president
Sylvanus Olympio; served as president until his assassination during the 1963 coup. [18]
1967 military coup
Gnassingbé Eyadéma came to power after a military takeover and remained president until his death in 2005. [7]
Leadership succession in 2005
Faure Gnassingbé, Eyadéma’s son, assumed the presidency amid a contested transition and political violence. [7]
Multiparty constitutional era
A 1992 constitution introduced a multiparty constitutional framework, subsequently revised. [18]
Fifth Republic constitutional reform
Constitutional revision promulgated in May 2024 replaced the previous executive-presidential model with a parliamentary system. [8]
Institutional change in May 2025
Jean-Lucien Savi de Tové became president and Faure Gnassingbé became President of the Council of Ministers on May 3, 2025. [7]

People and Society

Official language
French. [7]
Widely spoken national languages
Ewe and Kabiyè (Kabye), among a much broader indigenous-language landscape.
Additional languages
Tem (Kotokoli), Mina (Gen), Moba, Gurma, Akposso, Ifè and other regional languages.
Linguistic diversity
Multiple Gbe, Gur and other language communities, with local multilingualism common.
French-language functions
Formal administration, legislation, much secondary and higher education and public institutions. [7]
Indigenous-language functions
Family life, community communication, local trade, radio, culture and informal education.
Religious composition note
No single contemporaneous nationwide religious census is used here; survey and administrative estimates differ. [15]
Christian population estimate
Approximately 42.3% in the figures cited by the 2023 U.S. State Department report. [15]
Traditional religious practice estimate
Approximately 36.9% in the same source’s demographic summary. [15]
Muslim population estimate
Approximately 14.0% in the same source’s summary. [15]
Other and unspecified affiliations
The cited figures do not provide an exhaustive category breakdown; avoid assigning the remaining share without its methodology. [15]
Christian denominations
Roman Catholic, Protestant and Pentecostal congregations, among others. [15]
Islamic communities
Muslim communities across the country, with varying local concentrations. [15]
Traditional and blended practice
Indigenous religious traditions are important, and practices may overlap with Christian or Muslim identities. [15]
Cultural communities
Ewe, Mina, Kabye, Tem, Moba and many other groups contribute to cultural life.
Food and agriculture in daily life
Maize preparations, cassava dishes, yams, beans, sauces and locally grown vegetables.
Cultural expression
Music, drumming, dance, craft production, oral histories and regional ceremonies.
Public health indicator
Life expectancy at birth is about 63 years in the World Bank’s 2024 series. [1]
Health-system context
Public and private health services operate with urban–rural access challenges. [2]
Education landscape
Primary and secondary schooling, vocational education and university institutions, with French a major language of instruction.
Literacy indicator limitation
Different sources use different age bands and survey definitions; this profile does not manufacture a current literacy rate.
Population age structure
A youthful population, contributing to demand for schools, housing and jobs. [5]
Urbanization (2022 census)
Around 42.9% urban according to national census classification. [4]
Urban–rural planning challenge
Expansion of sanitation, electricity, roads and services in fast-growing settlements. [2]
Cultural heritage site
Koutammakou, the Land of the Batammariba, recognized for landscape, settlement and architectural traditions. [14]
UNESCO World Heritage inscriptions
One inscribed World Heritage property on the UNESCO Togo list. [14]
World Heritage inscription years
Koutammakou inscribed in 2004 and extended across the Benin border in 2023. [14]
Heritage interpretation
Koutammakou is a living cultural landscape; it should not be treated only as an archaeological ruin. [14]
Religious freedom and diversity
Constitutional and administrative approaches to religious registration and practice are discussed in the 2023 report. [15]

Government and Institutions

Government form (November 2026)
Parliamentary republic; the earlier presidential-system description is outdated. [7] [8]
Principal constitutional change
The 2024 constitution introduced a president selected through Parliament and a President of the Council of Ministers as executive leader. [8] [7]
Constitution promulgation
May 6, 2024, according to the U.S. Library of Congress. [8]
Start of current institutional arrangement
May 3, 2025. [7]
President of the Republic
Jean-Lucien Savi de Tové, the head of state. [7]
President of the Council of Ministers
Faure Essozimna Gnassingbé, head of government and effective executive leader. [7]
Executive authority
The President of the Council directs governmental policy and exercises substantial executive functions, including defence and foreign affairs. [7]
Presidency role
The head of state has primarily representative and constitutional functions under the reformed system. [8] [7]
Head of state selection
Parliamentary election rather than direct popular presidential election under the new framework. [8]
Head of state term
Six years, one term under the 2024 constitutional reform described by the Library of Congress. [8]
National legislature
Bicameral Parliament: National Assembly and Senate. [8] [7]
National Assembly elected membership
113 members, as at the 2024 parliamentary election. [9]
Last National Assembly election
April 29, 2024. [9]
2024 election turnout
61.0% according to the Inter-Parliamentary Union. [9]
Ruling party
Union for the Republic (Union pour la République, UNIR). [9]
UNIR seats in 2024 election
108 of 113 National Assembly seats on the election-night result summary; later caucus membership may change. [9]
Other seats in 2024 election
Five National Assembly seats distributed among other parties. [9]
Senate establishment
The upper chamber became operational during the 2025 constitutional transition. [7] [8]
Legislative representation
Parliamentary composition is distinct from popular endorsement of the constitutional reform. [9] [7]
Opposition and civic protests
Opposition groups and civil society protested the constitutional changes and governance issues in mid-2025. [7]
2025 constitutional controversies
Critics opposed the transfer of executive authority and questioned prospects for leadership alternation. [7] [8]
Historical presidency
Gnassingbé Eyadéma ruled from 1967 until 2005; Faure Gnassingbé then governed under the former presidency until 2025. [7]
Government continuity
Faure Gnassingbé retained principal executive leadership following the 2025 transition, but under a different office. [7]
Judiciary
Courts operate within the national constitutional and legal system; judicial institutions have roles in constitutional interpretation. [8]
Subnational administration
Regional, prefectural and local authorities administer territorial affairs. [4]
Territorial census organization
Grand Lomé is a separate reporting unit in 2022 census tables, alongside the surrounding five-region structure. [4]
Government reference cutoff
November 5, 2026; political appointments must be reverified in later editions. [7]

Current-system distinction: The 2024 constitutional reform and May 2025 institutional transition mean that Faure Gnassingbé is now President of the Council of Ministers and the executive leader, not the current President of the Republic. Jean-Lucien Savi de Tové is the current head of state. [7] [8]

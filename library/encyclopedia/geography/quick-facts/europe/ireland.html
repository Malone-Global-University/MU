Country Overview

Ireland is a parliamentary republic in northwestern Europe occupying most of the island of Ireland, while Northern Ireland remains part of the United Kingdom. The modern independent Irish Free State was established in 1922, and the current constitution dates from 1937. Ireland joined the European Communities in 1973 and adopted the euro in 1999. Its multinational-intensive economy requires particular care when interpreting GDP and GDP per capita. [6]

Ireland is a northwestern European parliamentary republic with historical, constitutional, and commercial connections across the island of Ireland, Britain, Europe, and a worldwide diaspora. Modern statehood developed through revolution, partition, the 1922 Irish Free State, the 1937 Constitution, and the 1949 declaration of republic status. The state occupies most, but not all, of the island; Northern Ireland is part of the United Kingdom under a peace framework shaped by the 1998 Good Friday Agreement. Ireland's highly internationalized economy has substantial pharmaceutical, technology, and business-services industries, yet national-accounting measures can be strongly affected by foreign-owned intellectual property and multinational balance sheets. For that reason, this profile pairs conventional GDP with Ireland's modified national-income methodology.

Identity and State Formation

The history of Ireland includes Gaelic polities, Christian monasteries, Norman settlement, and extended British rule, as well as the Great Famine and mass emigration. The 1916 Easter Rising, War of Independence, 1921 Treaty, and 1922 Free State were distinct moments. Partition separated Northern Ireland from the state that became modern Ireland. The 1937 Constitution established the present constitutional name; 1949 legislation clarified the state's republican status in external relations. The 1998 Good Friday Agreement later provided key institutions and principles for the Northern Ireland peace settlement.

Historical context: Ireland developed through Gaelic polities, Norman settlement and centuries of English and British rule. The Easter Rising of 1916 and subsequent War of Independence led to the Anglo-Irish Treaty and the establishment of the Free State in 1922. Partition left six northeastern counties in Northern Ireland as part of the United Kingdom. The 1937 constitution reshaped the state, and the 1949 republic legislation altered its external status. The 1998 Good Friday Agreement underpins institutions associated with the Northern Ireland peace process. [9]

Geography and Population

The sovereign state covers about five-sixths of the island, so island-wide figures must not be attributed to the Republic alone. The Atlantic-facing west and the more urbanized east differ in land use, transport, settlement, and exposure to coastal weather. Dublin dominates many population and employment measures, while Cork, Limerick, Galway, and Waterford anchor other regional systems. The CSO's 2026 annual estimate of 5,525,600 refers to usual residence; the 2022 census uses a different reference concept and date.

Economy and International Trade

Ireland's internationally connected firms are major exporters of pharmaceuticals, medicines, chemicals, technology-related products, and services. Its 2025 provisional merchandise exports reached €260.3 billion, according to the CSO; pharmaceutical and medical products accounted for more than half of that sum. However, the location of intellectual property, company domiciles, and contract manufacturing can cause GDP to depart sharply from domestic household living standards. In 2025 current-price GDP was €602.4 billion and modified GNI* €334.0 billion, a crucial distinction for public-policy analysis.

People and Society

Irish is constitutionally the first official language and remains central to national cultural life, while English predominates in everyday communication. The Gaeltacht, Irish-language education, and broadcasting sustain different forms of language use. Migration has diversified religious, cultural, and linguistic communities, while historic Catholic institutions remain important in the analysis of schools, welfare and social change. Census religion identification differs from faith observance, and housing, healthcare capacity, and regional transport increasingly shape debates about inclusion and prosperity.

Government and Institutions

The Oireachtas comprises the President, Dáil Éireann, and Seanad Éireann. The Taoiseach is chosen through parliamentary processes, while the directly elected president has a separate constitutional role. Catherine Connolly became president in November 2025; Micheál Martin serves as Taoiseach at this cutoff. Ireland's Supreme Court and other courts uphold law and constitutional adjudication. The state shares an island with Northern Ireland, and cross-border cooperation must be understood alongside the separate jurisdiction and institutions of the United Kingdom.

International Profile

Ireland joined the European Communities in 1973 and uses the euro, but it remains outside the Schengen Area and participates in the Common Travel Area with the United Kingdom. Its policy of military neutrality is distinct from political or economic nonalignment: Ireland participates in EU institutions and international peacekeeping. The Good Friday Agreement supports north–south and east–west institutions, which became particularly significant after Brexit. Trade with the United States and European partners, diaspora networks, and multilateral development engagement are major dimensions of Irish international policy.

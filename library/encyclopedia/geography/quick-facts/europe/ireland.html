Country Overview

Ireland is a parliamentary republic in northwestern Europe occupying most of the island of Ireland, while Northern Ireland remains part of the United Kingdom. The modern independent Irish Free State was established in 1922, and the current constitution dates from 1937. Ireland joined the European Communities in 1973 and adopted the euro in 1999. Its multinational-intensive economy requires particular care when interpreting GDP and GDP per capita. [6]

Historical context: Ireland developed through Gaelic polities, Norman settlement and centuries of English and British rule. The Easter Rising of 1916 and subsequent War of Independence led to the Anglo-Irish Treaty and the establishment of the Free State in 1922. Partition left six northeastern counties in Northern Ireland as part of the United Kingdom. The 1937 constitution reshaped the state, and the 1949 republic legislation altered its external status. The 1998 Good Friday Agreement underpins institutions associated with the Northern Ireland peace process. [9]

Irish GDP and domestic income: Multinational profit flows, foreign-owned intellectual property and aircraft leasing distort comparisons based solely on national GDP. Ireland’s Central Statistics Office publishes modified gross national income (GNI*) as a complementary indicator of domestic activity. The 2025 GDP per capita shown above is not a measure of typical Irish household income. [10]

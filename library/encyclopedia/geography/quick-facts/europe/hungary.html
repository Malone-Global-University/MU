Country Overview

Hungary is a landlocked Central European country that the United Nations M49 system classifies within Eastern Europe. Budapest is its political and principal economic center. Medieval Hungarian statehood dates to the kingdom associated with Stephen I, while the modern state emerged from the dissolution of Austria-Hungary in 1918 and underwent major constitutional transitions in 1949, 1989 and 2012. Hungary belongs to the European Union and NATO but retains the forint rather than using the euro. [6]

Medieval statehood
Christian Kingdom of Hungary conventionally dated to the coronation of Stephen I around 1000–1001
Modern independence and transitions
1918 dissolution of Austria-Hungary; 1920 Treaty of Trianon reshaped territory; 1949 socialist constitution; 23 October 1989 proclamation of the republic
Current constitutional order

National day
20 August (State Foundation Day); 15 March and 23 October are additional national holidays

Historical context: The medieval Kingdom of Hungary grew into a major central European polity, later enduring Ottoman and Habsburg rule. The 1867 Austro-Hungarian Compromise established the Dual Monarchy. The post-1918 state experienced territorial losses, the Second World War, a communist regime after 1949, the 1956 uprising and the 1989 democratic transition. The 2012 Fundamental Law superseded the heavily amended 1949 constitutional text. [9]

People and Society

Official language
Hungarian (Magyar), a Uralic language
Recognized national minorities
Roma, German, Slovak, Romanian, Croatian, Serbian and other constitutionally recognized minorities
Religious affiliations, 2022 census
About 60% answered the voluntary religion question. Among respondents, around 46% identified as Roman Catholic, 16% as Reformed/Calvinist, and 27% as having no religion; other traditions were also reported [8]
Religious census limitation
Because approximately 40% did not answer, percentages of respondents are not population-wide percentages
Major faith communities
Roman Catholic, Hungarian Reformed, Lutheran, Greek Catholic, Jewish and other communities
Demographic context
Long-term low fertility and population aging; many residents live in Budapest and other regional centers

Demographic interpretation: The religion categories refer to 2022 census respondents answering an optional question, not all residents. Distinguish denominational self-identification from worship practice. [8]

Government and Institutions

President of Hungary, elected by the National Assembly for a five-year term
Head of government
Prime minister, chosen through the parliamentary system and directing the cabinet
Legislature
Unicameral National Assembly (Országgyűlés), 199 members
Administrative divisions
19 counties (vármegyék) plus the capital Budapest, which has special administrative status
Constitution
Fundamental Law adopted in 2011, effective 1 January 2012, subsequently amended
Judiciary and institutions
Constitutional Court, ordinary courts and independent state institutions within the national legal system

Institutional descriptions refer to the legal and constitutional design; incumbents and cabinet composition may change between updates. [9]

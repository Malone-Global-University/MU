Country Overview

San Marino is a small landlocked republic entirely enclosed by Italy in the Apennine foothills around Mount Titano. It is traditionally described as having been founded in 301 CE, although this is a founding tradition rather than a continuous record of modern state institutions. The modern republic has an unusual dual head of state: two Captains Regent, elected for six-month terms. San Marino is not a European Union member but uses the euro under a monetary agreement and maintains close economic and cultural relations with Italy. [9]

Historical context: Tradition associates San Marino’s beginning with the Christian stonecutter Marinus in 301 CE. Medieval communal institutions evolved into a distinct self-governing republic around Mount Titano. The Captains Regent, documented by the thirteenth century, provide a distinctive system of paired six-month heads of state. The 1600 statutes and the 1974 Declaration of Citizens’ Rights underpin the modern constitutional framework. The republic maintained its separate identity while surrounding Italian states consolidated, joined the United Nations in 1992 and entered into European monetary arrangements allowing it to use the euro.

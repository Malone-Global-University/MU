Country Overview

Estonia is a parliamentary republic on the eastern Baltic Sea in Northern Europe. The Republic of Estonia declared independence in 1918, lost effective independence under Soviet occupation and annexation in 1940, and restored independence in August 1991. It is known for electronic public administration, information-technology services and close economic connections to Nordic and Baltic neighbors. Estonia joined the European Union and NATO in 2004 and adopted the euro in 2011. [6]

Sovereign republic in the interwar years, later annexed by the Soviet Union in 1940 after military occupation

20 August 1991; restoration of the pre-1940 republic’s independence, not formation of an unrelated successor state

Historical context: Estonia’s medieval lands came under several Baltic powers before incorporation into the Russian Empire. Independence was proclaimed in 1918 and consolidated following the War of Independence. Soviet occupation and annexation in 1940 interrupted sovereignty; Estonia reasserted its constitutional continuity and restored independence in 1991, subsequently integrating into European and Atlantic institutions. People and Society

Official language
Estonian, a Finnic language in the Uralic family
Other widely spoken languages
Russian is widely spoken; Ukrainian, Finnish and English are also used in different communities
Religious affiliation, 2021 census
Among residents aged 15 and over, 29% reported a religious affiliation, 58% none, and 13% did not answer; these are religion-identification responses, not church-membership counts [8]
Religious traditions
Orthodox Christianity was the most prevalent declared religion; Lutheran, other Christian, Muslim and other groups are also represented
Demographic context
Population includes Estonian and Russian-speaking communities; the country has significant urban concentration in Tallinn and Tartu
Digital society
Widely used online public services and digital identity systems characterize the contemporary state

Demographic interpretation: The 2021 census religion percentages apply to respondents aged 15 and older and include a 13% nonresponse category. They must not be applied without adjustment to children or treated as church-administrative membership. [8]

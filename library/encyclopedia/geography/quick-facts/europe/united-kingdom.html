Country Overview

The United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland is a constitutional monarchy and parliamentary democracy comprising England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. London is the national capital and seat of the UK government. The modern UK took shape through unions in 1707 and 1801 and the 1922 separation of most of Ireland, with later devolution to Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. The UK left the European Union on 31 January 2020 and continues as a member of NATO, the G7 and the UN Security Council. [10]

Historical context: Successive waves of settlement and political change shaped Britain and Ireland: Roman rule in parts of Britain, Anglo-Saxon and Norse kingdoms, the Norman conquest of England in 1066, medieval Scottish and Welsh state traditions, and English involvement in Ireland. England and Scotland united in a new sovereign kingdom in 1707; the union with Ireland followed in 1801. Industrialization and British imperial expansion profoundly shaped global commerce and warfare, including colonial conquest and participation in Atlantic slavery. Irish partition followed independence movements and conflict in the early twentieth century. The UK played a major role in both world wars and became a postwar welfare state. Decolonization, European integration and later Brexit reshaped foreign relations. Devolution introduced legislatures and executives in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, while Northern Ireland’s power-sharing framework rests on the 1998 Belfast/Good Friday Agreement. [10]

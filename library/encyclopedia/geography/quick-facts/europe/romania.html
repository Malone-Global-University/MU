Country Overview

Romania is a republic in southeastern Europe, extending from the Carpathian Mountains to the Danube Delta and Black Sea. Bucharest is its capital. The modern state emerged from the nineteenth-century union of Moldavia and Wallachia and declared independence from Ottoman suzerainty in 1877. Romania ended communist dictatorship in 1989 and joined NATO in 2004 and the European Union in 2007. It remains outside the euro area but became fully integrated into the Schengen area in January 2025. [6]

Romania is a southeastern European republic with a varied landscape ranging from the Carpathian arc to the plains of Wallachia and the Danube Delta on the Black Sea. Its modern state grew from the nineteenth-century union of Moldavia and Wallachia, independence from Ottoman suzerainty, and twentieth-century territorial reorganization. The 1989 revolution ended communist dictatorship, and Romania joined NATO in 2004 and the European Union in 2007. It is outside the euro area and retains the leu, but its remaining internal Schengen land-border checks were lifted on January 1, 2025. The presidential election of 2025 brought Nicușor Dan to office. After an extended 2026 government-formation crisis, parliament confirmed a minority cabinet under Prime Minister Luca Niculescu on October 20, 2026. Its economic structure includes manufacturing, IT, agriculture, energy and external trade.

Identity and State Formation

The historical regions of Wallachia, Moldavia and Transylvania followed different medieval, early modern and imperial political trajectories, even as Romanian-speaking communities developed cultural and linguistic connections. The principalities of Moldavia and Wallachia elected Alexandru Ioan Cuza in 1859, creating a union consolidated through administrative reform. Independence from Ottoman suzerainty was proclaimed in 1877 and internationally recognized in 1878; the kingdom dates from 1881. The First World War and the 1918 union decisions produced a much larger interwar state. World War II brought dictatorship, persecution, alliance with Nazi Germany and later reversal of wartime alignments. Communist rule consolidated after 1945, with Nicolae Ceaușescu leading an increasingly authoritarian system. His overthrow in December 1989 opened a difficult path to electoral democracy and a market economy. The constitution dates to 1991, with major amendments in 2003. [21]

Geography and Population

The Carpathian Mountains form an arc across central Romania and help divide Transylvania, Moldavia and Wallachia into distinct physical and historical landscapes. The Transylvanian Plateau, the Danube basin, the Wallachian Plain and the Moldavian hills support different agriculture, settlement and transport systems. The Danube flows along much of the southern border before reaching the Black Sea through a biodiverse delta shared hydrologically with Ukraine. Constanța is a major seaport, while Bucharest anchors administration and higher-value services; Cluj-Napoca, Timișoara, Iași, Brașov and Craiova have different research and industrial strengths. National land area excludes inland waters in certain statistical tables, whereas full area includes them. The 2021 census and subsequent population estimates require separate reference dates. Environmental issues include flood risk, drought, land degradation and forest conservation alongside energy and tourism development. [9]

Economy and International Trade

Romania produces vehicles, automotive components, electrical equipment, machinery, chemical products, food and agricultural commodities while exporting an expanding range of technology and business services. The national FOB merchandise export total for 2025 was about €96.6 billion, compared with imports of €129.35 billion measured CIF. These are not identical valuation bases: freight and insurance treatment partly explains why merchandise balances must be labeled carefully. Automotive factories, the Port of Constanța, Black Sea infrastructure and agricultural output connect the country to European markets and beyond. Romania has experienced debates over public deficits, taxation, inflation, pension policy and investment financing, intensified by political instability during 2026. Its membership in the EU provides common-market opportunities and regulatory obligations, but the currency remains the leu. The World Bank's dollar GDP per capita is not an average wage and can change with exchange rates. [8]

People and Society

Romanian is the official language and is used in state institutions, while recognized minority languages, notably Hungarian and Romani, play important local and cultural roles. Romania has an historically diverse population including Romanian, Hungarian, Roma, German, Ukrainian, Turkish and Tatar communities, with regional concentrations and different language practices. The 2021 census reported Romanian Orthodox affiliation as the largest religious category; shares calculated for all residents differ from proportions among respondents who declared a religion. Other Christian traditions and minority faith communities are also present. Emigration and return migration interact with population aging, educational provision and labor shortages, and Romanian communities abroad have strong links to EU economies. Universities and technical schools support the technology workforce; healthcare and social services experience substantial regional disparities. Cultural heritage encompasses rural craftsmanship, Transylvanian towns, historical churches, music, film and the long history of the Danube corridor. [10]

Government and Institutions

Romania's constitutional system combines a directly elected president with a prime minister and cabinet responsible to a bicameral parliament. The Chamber of Deputies and Senate act through lawmaking and scrutiny, while the president nominates the prime ministerial candidate subject to parliamentary confidence. President Nicușor Dan took office in May 2025 after a rerun presidential election; the previous 2024 presidential process had been annulled. The four-party cabinet headed by Ilie Bolojan lost confidence in May 2026. Several proposed replacements failed, and a minority cabinet led by nonpartisan former diplomat Luca Niculescu won parliamentary confirmation on October 20, 2026, by 280 votes. Its support arrangements are conditional and do not represent an automatic stable majority. The country has 41 counties plus Bucharest municipality, and independent courts and the Constitutional Court exercise distinct competences. Electoral events must be dated to avoid confusing caretaker government with a confirmed cabinet. [14]

International Profile

Romania joined NATO in 2004 and the European Union in 2007 and entered the Schengen system in stages, with full lifting of internal land checks in January 2025. It is not a euro-area member and issues the Romanian leu. The Black Sea, lower Danube and borders with Ukraine, Moldova, Hungary, Serbia and Bulgaria place Romania at major transport, energy and security intersections. EU institutions influence regulations and funding, but national budgeting and many public-service responsibilities remain domestic. The country has supported security and humanitarian responses related to Russia's invasion of Ukraine; bilateral relationships also include questions of language, minority rights and trade. The Danube Delta is a major UNESCO-recognized natural area, while Romanian cultural listings preserve medieval churches, industrial landscapes and sites of Roman history. International organizations, UNESCO inscriptions and trade treaties are separate legal frameworks, each requiring accurately dated membership or designation. [19]

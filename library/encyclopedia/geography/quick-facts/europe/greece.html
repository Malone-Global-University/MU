Country Overview

Greece, officially the Hellenic Republic, is a parliamentary republic in southeastern Europe at the southern end of the Balkan Peninsula. Its mainland, Aegean and Ionian islands connect the Mediterranean, Balkans and Anatolian regions. The modern state emerged from the Greek War of Independence beginning in 1821 and received international recognition in 1830. Its present parliamentary republican order was consolidated following the restoration of democracy in 1974 and the 1975 constitution. [6]

War of independence
Greek uprising began in 1821; 25 March is commemorated as Independence Day, but the revolution unfolded over multiple dates
Recognition of independence
1830 London Protocol recognized an independent Greek state; monarchical settlement followed in 1832
Modern political milestones
Military dictatorship 1967–1974; restoration of democracy 1974; republican constitution adopted 1975

Historical context: Ancient Greek city-states, Hellenistic kingdoms, Byzantine territories and centuries of Ottoman rule form distinct historical periods. The 1821 struggle for independence led to the 1830 recognition of an independent Greek polity and the 1832 monarchy. The modern republic’s present constitutional framework followed the fall of military rule in 1974 and the adoption of the 1975 constitution. Greece joined the European Communities in 1981. [8]

People and Society

Official language
Greek
Other languages and communities
Albanian, Turkish, Romani and many migrant languages are spoken; Muslim minority communities exist in Western Thrace
Religious traditions
Eastern Orthodox Christianity plays a historically prominent role; the Constitution recognizes Eastern Orthodoxy as the prevailing religion while protecting freedom of religious conscience
Other communities
Muslim, Roman Catholic, Protestant, Jewish and other religious communities
Religious statistics caveat
No modern comprehensive religious-affiliation percentages are included: administrative church counts are not equivalent to a population-wide census of belief
Social geography
Mainland urban centers and island communities have distinct patterns of residence, seasonal work, tourism and migration

Demographic interpretation: The status of the “prevailing religion” is a constitutional provision, not proof that a certain percentage of the population currently attends church or identifies as Orthodox. [9]

Government and Institutions

System
Unitary parliamentary republic
Head of state
President of the Hellenic Republic, elected by parliament for a five-year term
Head of government
Prime Minister, supported by a cabinet responsible to Parliament
Legislature
Unicameral Hellenic Parliament, normally 300 members
Administrative structure
13 administrative regions and decentralized administrations; municipalities handle local government
Constitution
Constitution of 1975, amended subsequently; parliamentary democracy after the 1974 Metapolitefsi
Judiciary
Independent judiciary with Council of State, Court of Cassation (Areios Pagos) and Court of Audit

Institutional descriptions refer to the legal and constitutional design; incumbents and cabinet composition may change between updates. [9]

Country Overview

France, officially the French Republic, is a European and overseas constitutional republic. Metropolitan France lies in Western Europe, while overseas regions and other territories extend French jurisdiction across the Caribbean, South America, Indian Ocean and Pacific. Its current Fifth Republic constitutional system dates from 1958. France is a founding member of European integration, a member of the euro area, and a permanent member of the United Nations Security Council. [6]

Identity and State Formation

Official and common names
French Republic (République française); France
Capital
Paris
UN M49 classification
Europe / Western Europe (UN statistical classification) [3]
Historic monarchy and revolution
The medieval French monarchy preceded the French Revolution of 1789; the First French Republic was proclaimed in September 1792
Modern state transitions
Successive republics and empires; Fourth Republic established in 1946, Fifth Republic in 1958
Current constitution
Constitution of 4 October 1958; subsequently amended
National day
14 July (Fête nationale), commemorating revolutionary and national-unity traditions
ISO 3166-1 identifiers
FR (alpha-2), FRA (alpha-3), 250 (numeric)
Country-code top-level domain
.fr

Historical context: France developed from medieval kingdoms into a centralized early modern monarchy and a major European power. The Revolution beginning in 1789 transformed its political institutions; nineteenth-century republics, monarchies and empires followed. The Fifth Republic was established in 1958 amid the Algerian crisis. These dates should not be reduced to a single modern “independence” date, because French statehood has a long and discontinuous constitutional history. [10]

People and Society

Official language
French; the Constitution describes French as the language of the Republic
Regional and community languages
Breton, Occitan, Corsican, Basque, Alsatian, regional creoles, Arabic, Berber and many immigrant languages are used alongside French
Religious affiliation, metropolitan survey 2019–2020
Among ages 18–59 in metropolitan France, 51% reported no religion, 29% Catholic affiliation and 10% Muslim affiliation; other affiliations included other Christian traditions [9]
Religious data limitations
France does not collect religious affiliation in its routine population census; the 18–59 metropolitan survey cannot represent every age group or overseas population
Public institutions and religious freedom
Constitutional secularism (laïcité) and freedom of conscience frame the relationship between religion and the state
Demographic context
Population spans metropolitan and overseas communities; large cities are multilingual and marked by migration and regional diversity

Demographic interpretation: The TeO2 religious affiliation percentages are from 2019–2020 respondents aged 18–59 in metropolitan France, not a 2025 population census or an all-ages national breakdown. [9]

Government and Institutions

System
Unitary semi-presidential republic under the 1958 Constitution
Head of state
President of the Republic, directly elected for a five-year term
Head of government
Prime Minister appointed by the President; cabinet answerable to the National Assembly under constitutional rules
Legislature
Bicameral Parliament: National Assembly (577 deputies) and Senate (348 senators)
Territorial organization
18 administrative regions (13 metropolitan and 5 overseas); 101 departments, plus overseas collectivities and special-status areas
Constitutional institutions
Constitutional Council, Council of State, Court of Cassation, and specialized administrative and judicial courts
Political principle
Indivisible, secular, democratic and social republic under Article 1 of the Constitution

Institutional descriptions refer to the legal and constitutional design; incumbents and cabinet composition may change between updates. [10]

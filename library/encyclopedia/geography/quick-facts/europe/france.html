Country Overview

France is a European and overseas republic whose territory, population and constitutional arrangements extend beyond metropolitan Europe. Its state has developed through medieval monarchy, revolution, empire, colonial expansion, republican settlements and the Fifth Republic established in 1958. Paris serves as the political and cultural capital, while cities such as Marseille, Lyon, Toulouse, Bordeaux and Lille anchor other industrial, commercial and cultural regions. Overseas regions and collectivities give the republic a presence in the Caribbean, South America, Indian Ocean and Pacific, under constitutionally different regimes.

Its diversified economy includes aerospace, agriculture and food processing, pharmaceuticals, automotive and transport industries, tourism, nuclear-generated electricity, finance and professional services. France is a founding participant in European integration, a member of NATO and the G7, and one of five permanent members of the UN Security Council. Present-day political institutions include a directly elected president and a government accountable to the National Assembly. Because national population totals, territorial areas and international trade series use varying geographic coverage and accounting methods, the figures below identify sources and data years rather than assuming that all France-related indicators describe identical territory.

Identity and State Formation

France cannot be assigned a single modern independence date. The republic claims institutional and cultural continuities from multiple political regimes, but the Revolution of 1789, First Republic of 1792, republican consolidation in the nineteenth century and the Fifth Republic in 1958 are different constitutional milestones. [8] [7]

Official designation
French Republic (République française), also referred to as France; the French-language state name appears in legislation and international agreements.
Capital
Paris, the seat of the presidency, central executive institutions, both parliamentary chambers and major judicial authorities.
UN geographic category
Western Europe under UN M49; French overseas territories also occupy several non-European geographic regions.
Early historical territories
Pre-Roman Gaul included many peoples and languages; Roman conquest and provincial institutions profoundly transformed settlement and exchange.
Frankish and medieval kingdoms
Frankish dynasties and subsequent Capetian monarchy formed foundations of a centralized kingdom through political changes over centuries.
Capetian era
The Capetian monarchy beginning in 987 provided dynastic continuity while royal authority expanded unevenly relative to nobles and local institutions.
Medieval economy
Agriculture, towns, universities, religious institutions and regional trade structured life long before industrial-era national uniformity.
Hundred Years' War
Prolonged conflicts with England in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries altered taxation, warfare, dynastic politics and territorial authority.
Early modern monarchy
Bourbon-era centralization and absolutist claims developed alongside provincial privileges, parlements and persistent local inequalities.
French Enlightenment
Intellectual debates on sovereignty, rights, religion, science and political legitimacy helped shape European and Atlantic political thought.
1789 Revolution
Revolution began in 1789, transforming monarchy, representative institutions, social estates and ideas of national citizenship.
Declaration of Rights
The 1789 Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen is a foundational constitutional text, interpreted through later law.
First Republic
The monarchy was abolished and a republic proclaimed in September 1792, during war and political upheaval.
Revolutionary violence
The Terror, counterrevolution and wars of the 1790s involved coercion, mobilization and extensive suffering as well as institutional reforms.
Napoleonic rule
Consulate and First Empire reshaped administration, warfare and legal systems; the Napoleonic Code influenced many jurisdictions.
Nineteenth-century regimes
Restoration monarchies, July Monarchy, Second Republic, Second Empire and Third Republic followed one another after 1814.
1848 Revolution
Republican government and universal male suffrage emerged amid European revolutionary upheaval; women's political exclusion persisted.
Third Republic
Established in 1870 and consolidated under subsequent laws; republican schooling and secular public institutions expanded.
Colonial expansion
French power in the Caribbean, Africa, Asia and Pacific relied on unequal legal regimes, extraction, coercion and varied colonial relationships.
Slavery and abolition
French colonial slavery was abolished in 1794, restored under Napoleon in 1802 in relevant territories, and definitively abolished in 1848.
Dreyfus Affair
Late-nineteenth-century prosecution and campaigns exposed antisemitism and debates about military authority, republican law and public accountability.
1905 secularism law
The law separating churches and state established core principles of laïcité, with historically differentiated arrangements in some territories.
World War I
France endured combat, occupation in some regions and enormous casualties; postwar reconstruction changed demographics and politics.
World War II
German occupation, Vichy collaboration and French resistance are distinct parts of wartime history; liberation led to political reconstruction.
Fourth Republic
The constitutional regime established in 1946 governed reconstruction and decolonization amid unstable parliamentary coalitions.
Decolonization
Independence movements and wars in Indochina and Algeria challenged French colonial power; overseas territories followed differing constitutional paths.
Fifth Republic
Constitution of 4 October 1958 created the current regime amid the Algerian political crisis, later amended many times.
Direct presidential election
Constitutional reform in 1962 instituted presidential election by direct universal suffrage, altering political incentives and executive legitimacy.
May 1968
Student and worker movements challenged education, labor and social authority, with political effects extending beyond the immediate protests.
European integration
France helped establish the European Economic Community and later shaped European Union institutions and monetary integration.
National holiday
July 14 commemorates revolutionary-era events and national unity; it is distinct from the date of the Fifth Republic constitution.
Flag and symbols
Blue-white-red tricolor, Marianne, the national motto and La Marseillaise are republican civic symbols with layered historical origins.
ISO and internet
FR (alpha-2), FRA (alpha-3), 250 (numeric); .fr internet domain and +33 telephone code for metropolitan France.

Interpretation: A timeline of 1789, 1792 and 1958 describes revolution, the first republican proclamation and the current constitution respectively. They should not be collapsed into one national founding or independence date. [8] [7]

Geography and Population

France has an Atlantic, Channel and Mediterranean European setting but also overseas territories in several oceans. This makes geography, climate risk, biodiversity, statistical coverage and maritime jurisdiction unusually varied. The total area on the EU country page is not the same as the land-only FAO series or the area of metropolitan France. [2] [3]

Total geographical area
Approximately 638,475 km² in the EU/Eurostat reference used by the uploaded page, covering a wider scope than metropolitan-only geography.
Land-area comparison
World Bank/FAO gives about 538,950 km² of land in a differently defined series; it must not be subtracted mechanically from EU total area. [22]
Metropolitan setting
European France connects Atlantic, Mediterranean and continental transport corridors, bordering eight sovereign neighbors on mainland Europe.
European neighbors
Belgium, Luxembourg, Germany, Switzerland, Italy, Monaco, Spain and Andorra border metropolitan France.
Overseas land frontier
French Guiana borders Brazil and Suriname, making the republic's land boundaries extend into South America.
Atlantic coastline
Brittany, the Bay of Biscay and western estuaries support fisheries, shipping, tourism and major port complexes.
English Channel
Northern maritime frontier with the United Kingdom; ports and the Channel Tunnel are major transport links.
Mediterranean coast
Provence, Côte d'Azur and Gulf of Lion connect metropolitan France to the western Mediterranean economy and ecosystem.
Alps
Southeastern alpine ranges contain high mountains, glaciers, hydroelectric resources, winter tourism and transboundary transport corridors.
Mont Blanc
Mont Blanc is among Europe's highest peaks; exact elevation and national-boundary characterizations depend on surveying and boundary conventions.
Pyrenees
Southwest mountain barrier with Spain and Andorra, including pastoral regions, biodiversity and cross-border routes.
Massif Central
Central uplands include volcanic and plateau landscapes, rural settlements and water-source regions.
Other mountain systems
Jura and Vosges ranges shape eastern land use, ecosystems and historic transportation routes.
Seine basin
River and tributaries support the Paris basin's transport, agriculture, urban development and flood-risk management.
Loire basin
Long river system includes agricultural valleys, wetlands, towns and recognized cultural landscapes.
Rhône valley
Major river corridor connects northern Europe to the Mediterranean; navigation, vineyards and hydroelectric infrastructure are significant.
Garonne basin
Southwestern river system linked to Bordeaux, agriculture, transport and Atlantic flood-risk management.
Overseas departments
Guadeloupe, Martinique, French Guiana, Réunion and Mayotte are overseas departments/regions with specific legal and geographic conditions.
Other overseas collectivities
French Polynesia, Saint-Pierre and Miquelon, Wallis and Futuna, Saint-Martin and Saint-Barthélemy have differentiated governing statuses.
New Caledonia
The Pacific territory has a distinct constitutional and political process; sovereignty and autonomy questions require careful dated description.
French Southern and Antarctic Lands
Remote territories and claimed Antarctic areas have special administrative and treaty contexts and should not be treated as ordinary resident regions.
Corsica
Mediterranean island with distinctive historical and cultural institutions and a special territorial administrative framework.
Administrative regions
Thirteen metropolitan regions and five overseas regions are commonly counted as eighteen regions; special statuses complicate simplistic comparisons.
Departments
One hundred one departments in the standard metropolitan-plus-five-overseas framework, separate from other overseas collectivities.
Major cities
Paris, Marseille, Lyon, Toulouse, Nice, Nantes, Strasbourg, Bordeaux and Lille anchor diverse urban economic systems.
Population INSEE 2026
Provisional national estimate is 69,081,996 residents on 1 January 2026; approximately 66.8 million are in metropolitan France. [2]
Overseas population
INSEE estimated about 2.3 million residents across five overseas departments on 1 January 2026, within its published population field.
World Bank comparison
The original uploaded file gives 68,720,337 for 2025 in the World Bank series; it is not a 1 January 2026 INSEE count. [1]
Natural population change
INSEE's 2025 demographic review recorded approximately 645,000 births and 651,000 deaths, making the natural balance slightly negative. [3]
Ageing
About 22% of the population was at least 65 in early 2026, affecting public spending, labor markets and regional care provision.
Population density
Simple density using 69.082 million and 638,475 km² is about 108 per km², but that mixes administrative area conventions and masks regional disparities. [2]
Climate zones
Atlantic oceanic, Mediterranean, continental, mountain and tropical overseas climates produce very different agricultural and risk profiles.
Natural hazards
Floods, heatwaves, drought, storms, wildfire, Alpine avalanches and overseas tropical cyclones require place-specific adaptation plans.
Overseas biodiversity
French Guiana rainforests, Pacific reefs and Indian Ocean ecosystems contribute to exceptional biodiversity and conservation responsibilities.
Maritime jurisdiction
Overseas territories create extensive exclusive economic zones; maritime entitlements are distinct from reported land and surface area.
Environmental policy
Agriculture, industrial emissions, transport, nuclear energy, coastal pressures and ecosystem preservation intersect with EU climate rules.

Interpretation: INSEE’s 1 January 2026 figure of approximately 69.082 million includes metropolitan France and five overseas departments. The EU/Eurostat area, FAO land-only area and overseas maritime zones refer to different geographic concepts. [2] [3] [7]

Economy and International Trade

France has one of Europe’s largest diversified economies and combines advanced industry with large consumer services, public institutions, tourism, finance and agriculture. Its export base reaches far beyond wine and luxury goods: commercial aircraft, medicines, machinery, metals, vehicles, chemicals and food products all matter. Distinguishing goods, services, domestic output and re-exports is essential to interpreting the economy. [4] [5]

Economic system
Advanced high-income mixed market economy with private enterprise, significant public services and extensive EU regulatory integration.
Nominal GDP
Approximately US$3.366 trillion in the original World Bank 2025 current-dollar series, sensitive to national-account revisions and currency translation. [1]
GDP per capita
About US$48,986 in the World Bank 2025 series; the statistic does not measure average individual earnings or regional purchasing power. [1]
Real GDP growth
Around 0.8% in the uploaded World Bank 2025 annual series, subject to retrospective revisions. [1]
Currency
Euro (EUR); monetary union entry in 1999 and euro banknotes and coins in 2002 replaced the franc.
Bank of France
Banque de France belongs to the Eurosystem, provides financial stability expertise and produces monetary and economic research.
2025 goods exports
French Customs reported €614.7 billion in 2025 merchandise exports, increasing 2.5% on its comparable annual series. [4]
2025 goods imports
Customs reported roughly €703.6 billion of merchandise imports on a CAF/CIF valuation basis in its February 2026 annual release. [4]
2025 customs trade deficit
Customs' comparable FOB/FOB goods trade balance improved to a deficit of €69.2 billion in 2025; basis differs from raw import/export difference. [4]
INSEE trade series
INSEE also publishes a 2025 merchandise series with about €616.5 billion exports using its own national-account adjustments. [5]
Trade-series caution
€614.7bn Customs export headline, €616.5bn INSEE adjusted series and €604.2bn comparative compilation are not directly interchangeable. [4]
Aerospace
Toulouse and other industrial clusters specialize in commercial aircraft, engines, avionics and components, with volatile delivery timing.
Aerospace trade dynamics
Customs attributed much of the 2025 merchandise-export rebound to increased aerospace deliveries rather than uniform expansion across all sectors.
Pharmaceuticals
Prescription medicines, vaccines, medical research and manufacturing contribute to exports and healthcare systems.
Chemicals
Industrial chemicals, specialty materials and coatings serve European manufacturing supply chains, although export values can decline with prices or demand.
Automotive industry
Vehicle assembly, components, electrical systems and engineering employ workers across several regions and international company networks.
Transport machinery
Rail equipment, industrial vehicles, shipbuilding, and engineering services complement automotive and aerospace sectors.
Luxury products
Perfumes, cosmetics, fashion, leather goods and jewelry form globally recognized markets, combining manufacturing and branding.
Agriculture
Cereals, dairy, wine grapes, livestock, vegetables and specialty foods reflect varied climates and intensive EU agricultural integration.
Food exports
Wines, spirits, cheeses, processed foods and other agro-food categories are prominent exports; product values vary with harvest and demand.
Food trade balance
Customs noted that the agricultural and agro-food surplus narrowed sharply in 2025; an export sector can remain large while its net balance weakens.
Tourism
Hotel accommodation, restaurants, cultural travel and visitor spending generate services receipts, which are outside merchandise-export statistics.
Transport services
Aviation, maritime trade and logistics contribute to services accounts and can change net external balances independently of goods.
Financial services
Paris is a significant European center for banking, insurance, asset management and professional business services.
Digital industries
Software, data infrastructure, cybersecurity, telecommunications and start-ups form part of the innovation ecosystem, with uneven regional access.
Nuclear electricity
Nuclear power provides a major share of domestic electricity and is central to energy security, pricing and transition debates.
Renewable power
Wind, solar, hydro and other renewables are developed under national and EU climate-policy frameworks.
Energy imports
Oil, gas and refined products affect external balances; lower energy costs contributed to improvement in the 2025 customs goods deficit.
EU single market
EU rules underpin much intra-European trade, while national and EU statistics use different treatment of some cross-border movements.
Major trade partners
Germany, Italy, Spain, Belgium, the United States and the United Kingdom are significant partners; rankings depend on year and products.
Industrial geography
Île-de-France leads finance and headquarters; Occitanie has aerospace; northern and eastern areas host automotive and industrial networks.
Agricultural geography
Bordeaux, Champagne, Burgundy, Loire, Normandy and other regions differ in crops, production systems and protected geographical designations.
Ports
Marseille Fos, Le Havre, Dunkirk and other major ports connect France to global shipping, domestic manufacturing and distribution.
Labor institutions
Collective bargaining, employment law, social contributions and national wage rules shape employer costs and worker protections.
Public finances
Taxation supports pensions, healthcare, education, defense and public infrastructure; debt and budget balances require separate dated data.
Productivity and competitiveness
Research capability, investment, skill formation, energy costs and regulatory frameworks affect international performance.
2025 measurement
Full-year values are retrospective; preliminary monthly 2026 observations should not be represented as confirmed 2026 annual totals.

People and Society

French society includes metropolitan regional cultures, overseas communities, long-established minority languages and varied migration histories. The constitutional principle of laïcité shapes public institutions, but it does not erase freedom of conscience or the diversity of religious and nonreligious belief. National averages often conceal marked class, territorial and age differences. [6] [3]

Official language
Article 2 of the Constitution establishes French as the language of the Republic, with public administration conducted principally in French.
Regional languages
Breton, Basque, Corsican, Occitan, Alsatian and other varieties retain cultural and educational significance; legal protections vary.
Overseas languages
Caribbean and Indian Ocean creoles, Polynesian languages, Kanak languages and other locally established languages are central to regional identities.
Immigrant languages
Arabic, Berber languages, Portuguese, Turkish, Chinese languages and others are spoken in communities with varied migration histories.
Census principle
Routine French population censuses do not enumerate religion as a population characteristic; surveys use different samples and questions.
TeO2 religion survey
In a 2019–2020 metropolitan study of adults aged 18–59, 51% declared no religion, 29% Catholic affiliation and 10% Muslim affiliation.
Religion survey scope
TeO2 does not represent all ages or the overseas departments; reported proportions are not national 2026 religious census figures.
Laïcité
State secularism and freedom of conscience provide the legal context for public institutions, with specific historical arrangements in some regions.
Catholic heritage
Churches, religious festivals, architecture and charitable institutions have long historical importance without indicating current universal affiliation.
Other religious communities
Protestant, Orthodox, Muslim, Jewish, Buddhist and additional traditions participate in varied civic and cultural settings.
Public schooling
Compulsory education and public schools operate under national law, with significant local implementation and private education alongside.
Baccalauréat
General, technological and vocational pathways lead toward different qualifications and higher-education or labor-market options.
Universities
Universities, grandes écoles and research organizations structure postsecondary education, with varied admission and funding models.
Research institutions
CNRS, INSERM, national universities and applied research bodies are central to scientific production and innovation networks.
Healthcare
Statutory health insurance and complementary coverage support a mixed network of public hospitals and private providers.
Public health inequality
Access to physicians, specialist care and long-term services differs between dense metropolitan areas and underserved rural or overseas places.
Social protection
Family benefits, pensions, unemployment insurance and assistance programs have separate eligibility and financing rules.
Population ageing
Ageing brings pressures on pensions, care services and the distribution of medical resources between urban and rural communities.
Fertility trend
INSEE estimated a total fertility rate of approximately 1.56 children per woman for 2025, a period measure rather than completed family size. [3]
2025 birth/death balance
About 645,000 births and 651,000 deaths created a slightly negative natural balance in 2025; net migration is a separate component. [3]
Urbanization
Paris dominates metropolitan services, but cities such as Marseille, Lyon, Toulouse and Lille maintain distinctive populations and economic systems.
Housing
High rents in major centers, social housing, suburban expansion and rural vacancies reflect very different housing markets.
Migration and citizenship
Migration status, place of birth, nationality, ancestry and citizenship are separate statistical concepts in French reporting.
Overseas inequality
Living costs, youth employment, infrastructure and exposure to climate hazards differ substantially among overseas departments and collectivities.
Cultural literature
Molière, Hugo, Balzac, Césaire, Duras and many other authors illustrate a diverse body of literary and intellectual traditions.
Visual arts
French museums, contemporary art, architecture and historical movements influence cultural institutions and tourism worldwide.
Film industry
Film production and public cultural-support institutions connect domestic cinema with European and international distribution networks.
Music
Classical, regional, African-diasporic, electronic, popular and contemporary genres form a multilingual artistic landscape.
Cuisine
Regional food practices and protected-origin products range from cheeses and bread to seafood, vegetables and distinctive overseas foods.
Sports
Football, rugby, cycling, tennis and athletics have substantial amateur and professional ecosystems with varied regional strength.
Gender and equality
Formal equal-rights guarantees coexist with debates over pay gaps, representation, discrimination and violence.
Labor and protest
Unions, demonstrations and collective bargaining are prominent civic institutions but vary greatly by sector and region.
Digital access
Broadband, public digital platforms and media services coexist with concerns over privacy, misinformation and unequal access.
Regional diversity
Brittany, Alsace, Occitanie, Corsica, the Basque area and overseas territories have distinct cultural histories and institutions.
Statistical interpretation
Surveys of adults, administrative registration and household census measures cannot be combined into one definitive ethnoreligious percentage.

Interpretation: The INSEE TeO2 religion figures are for metropolitan residents aged 18–59 in 2019–2020, not a population census of France or a 2026 observation. The republic’s overseas territories and older age groups are not covered by that headline estimate. [6] [3]

Government and Institutions

The Fifth Republic uses a dual executive: an elected president and a prime minister whose government must be able to operate within parliamentary confidence rules. Presidential appointments, votes of no confidence, dissolution powers and constitutional review explain why a formal description of the system is not equivalent to any particular cabinet majority. [9] [10]

State system
Unitary semi-presidential republic under the Constitution of 4 October 1958, with decentralized local authorities.
President
Emmanuel Macron was the sitting President of the Republic as of 6 November 2026; he was reelected in April 2022. [9]
Prime minister
Sébastien Lecornu was serving as prime minister at the editorial cutoff, confirmed on the French government's official roster. [10]
Presidential term
Five years through direct universal suffrage, with the constitutional rule limiting consecutive presidential terms.
Prime-ministerial appointment
President appoints the prime minister; government must remain able to withstand confidence and censure mechanisms in the National Assembly.
Government responsibility
Cabinet determines and conducts national policy within constitutional allocations, while Parliament can dismiss it through specified votes.
National Assembly
Lower house comprises 577 deputies, elected under national electoral legislation from domestic and overseas constituencies.
Senate
Upper house consists of 348 senators elected indirectly through an electoral college reflecting territorial communities.
Bicameral legislation
Ordinary laws pass through parliamentary procedures; in specified circumstances the National Assembly can have the final word.
Government formation
Coalition and minority-support negotiations may be central when no party controls a majority of deputies.
2024 parliamentary context
Dissolution and early legislative elections in 2024 produced a fragmented Assembly that affected later cabinet stability.
Confidence motions
Article 49 contains procedures including government responsibility and votes of censure, with differentiated constitutional thresholds.
Presidential dissolution
Under Article 12 the president can dissolve the National Assembly subject to procedural and timing restrictions.
Constitutional Council
Conseil constitutionnel reviews constitutionality of certain laws and elections under rules that include referral mechanisms.
Question prioritaire
The priority constitutional question mechanism allows constitutional challenges to enacted statutory provisions during eligible litigation.
Council of State
Conseil d'État is a top administrative judicial body and advises government on specified draft legal instruments.
Court of Cassation
Cour de cassation is the highest ordinary judicial court for civil and criminal cassation proceedings.
Administrative courts
Administrative tribunals and courts of appeal review many disputes involving public authorities, separate from ordinary courts.
Judicial independence
Constitutional guarantees and appointment structures govern the judiciary; institutional safeguards remain subjects of legal reform debates.
Territorial regions
Eighteen administrative regions in the standard count: thirteen metropolitan and five overseas departments/regions.
Departments
101 departments in the standard metropolitan/overseas framework, with local authorities exercising statutory responsibilities.
Municipalities
Communes provide local public services and elected councils; intermunicipal cooperation is important for planning and infrastructure.
Overseas arrangements
Article 73 departments and Article 74 collectivities have differentiated legislative-administrative regimes and adaptation mechanisms.
New Caledonia
Governed under special constitutional arrangements that require separate treatment from ordinary departments or collectivities.
Corsica
Collectivité de Corse has a distinctive territorial governance arrangement within the French Republic.
European Parliament
French voters elect representatives to the European Parliament; EU institutions exercise separate competences from the national legislature.
Budget process
Annual budget laws and social security financing laws follow constitutional and statutory requirements and scrutiny mechanisms.
Independent institutions
Cour des comptes, rights-protection bodies and sector regulators carry separate audit and accountability responsibilities.
Local elections
Municipal, departmental and regional elections occur on distinct calendars under different electoral systems.
Defense and security
President has constitutional defense responsibilities, while ministries, military forces and internal-security bodies carry different operational roles.
Constitutional rights
Equality before law, secularism, rights and freedoms draw on the 1958 Constitution's preamble and referenced rights instruments.
Public administration
Civil service, prefects and local authorities implement national and decentralized policy with administrative-law guarantees.
EU legal order
EU law applies within its competence, interacting with national legislation and judicial interpretation rather than replacing all domestic law.
State secularism
Constitutional laïcité shapes public institutions and neutrality while freedom of conscience remains protected.
Citizenship
Nationality, voting rights and social entitlements arise under separate legal conditions, including rules applicable overseas.

Interpretation: The president and prime minister are distinct offices. Emmanuel Macron and Sébastien Lecornu are the incumbents at the stated cutoff; descriptions of constitutional powers do not establish that a particular cabinet commands an absolute parliamentary majority. [9] [10] [8]

International Profile

France’s international profile reflects its European position, overseas geography, permanent UN Security Council seat, nuclear deterrent, diplomatic network and participation in the Francophonie. The territorial scope of EU and Schengen rules is not identical for every French overseas territory, a distinction important for travel and economic comparisons. [18] [19]

United Nations
Founding member in 1945 and permanent member of the Security Council with veto powers under the UN Charter.
European integration
Founding participant in the European Economic Community, which began in 1958, and a central actor in later European Union treaties.
European Union
EU member through France's national participation; the application of some EU rules differs across overseas categories.
Euro area
Founder of monetary union in 1999; euro currency circulates in metropolitan France and specified overseas areas.
Schengen
France participates in Schengen, while some overseas territories lie outside its geographic free-movement area.
NATO
Founding member in 1949; nuclear forces and national command structures have separate strategic roles.
G7
Member of the Group of Seven advanced economies, participating in collective policy discussions without the group having supranational legislative power.
G20
Participates in the Group of Twenty and broader international financial governance discussions.
OECD
Member of the OECD, providing and using comparative education, economic, fiscal and social-policy analysis.
WTO
Member of the World Trade Organization; common external EU tariff policy applies to relevant EU customs territory.
Council of Europe
Participates in regional human-rights treaties and judicial oversight under the European Convention on Human Rights.
Francophonie
Organisation internationale de la Francophonie connects member states and governments around language and varied educational and diplomatic programs.
Africa relations
Historical colonial relationships, migration, trade, military presence and changing diplomatic priorities shape varied bilateral ties.
Germany relationship
Franco-German cooperation has been important to European institutions, while economic competition and policy disagreements persist.
United Kingdom relationship
Defense, Channel transport, migration and trade connect France with Britain despite changed European institutional relations after Brexit.
Mediterranean diplomacy
France engages with North African and Mediterranean states on migration, energy, security and development issues.
Indo-Pacific strategy
Overseas French territories and resident populations give France substantial Indian and Pacific Ocean responsibilities and maritime claims.
New Caledonia diplomacy
Pacific political questions and self-determination discussions require separate treatment from the status of metropolitan regions.
Caribbean and Guiana
France participates in Caribbean and South American regional issues through overseas communities and adjoining maritime space.
Climate diplomacy
France hosted the 2015 COP21 conference leading to the Paris Agreement, while domestic implementation and emissions trends are separate measures.
Development finance
French Development Agency and multilateral institutions fund programs under different eligibility and financial conditions.
Peace and security
French foreign policy includes Security Council diplomacy, defense partnerships, sanctions implementation and humanitarian interventions.
International cultural policy
French language programs, educational exchanges, museums, research and media form major soft-power channels.
UNESCO headquarters
UNESCO is headquartered in Paris; this institutional host role is distinct from France's own listed heritage properties.
UNESCO World Heritage
UNESCO listed 56 inscribed properties for France in the original 2026 reference, including shared transnational serial sites.
Mont-Saint-Michel
Abbey and its bay demonstrate historic architecture, pilgrimage and management of a dynamic tidal environment.
Versailles
Palace and Park of Versailles embody royal architecture, landscape design and subsequent heritage management.
Paris Seine
Banks of the Seine World Heritage property integrates urban form, monuments and the river's historical role.
Carnac monuments
Megalithic sites added to the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2025 strengthened recognition of prehistoric heritage.
Overseas heritage
French World Heritage listings include overseas landscapes and ecosystems, requiring care about site and territory coverage.
UNESCO counting
Transnational and serial sites count as one property per State Party listing; tentative proposals are not part of inscription totals.
Time zone
Metropolitan France generally uses CET (UTC+1) and CEST (UTC+2); overseas departments and territories occupy other time zones.
International dialing
Metropolitan France uses +33; overseas areas may have distinct international codes and dialing arrangements.
Trade diplomacy
EU trade policy structures many agreements, while French export promotion operates through national agencies and business networks.
Migration and asylum
European and French legal rules operate alongside international refugee and human-rights commitments; arrangements differ by territory.
Science diplomacy
Aerospace, climate research, health, nuclear technology and international scientific institutions are prominent fields of cooperation.

Interpretation: The 56-site UNESCO count follows the 2026 State Party reference and includes transnational properties as listed for France. EU, euro-area and Schengen geographical coverage are not identical for every overseas French territory. [18] [19] [7]

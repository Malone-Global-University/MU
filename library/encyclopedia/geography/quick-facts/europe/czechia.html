Czech Republic (Czechia)

Quick Facts — Contemporary Country Profile • Europe

Country Overview

Czechia, formally the Czech Republic, is a landlocked parliamentary republic in Central Europe. Its historic regions include Bohemia, Moravia, and part of Silesia. The independent Czech Republic was established on 1 January 1993 by the negotiated dissolution of Czechoslovakia; the state also observes the creation of independent Czechoslovakia on 28 October 1918. Czechia belongs to the European Union and NATO but continues to use the Czech koruna rather than the euro. [6]

Official and common names
Czech Republic (Česká republika); Czechia (Česko) is the recognized short-form name

UN M49 classification
Europe / Eastern Europe (UN statistical classification; geographically often described as Central Europe) [3]
Historic state traditions
Medieval Bohemian lands and the Crown of Bohemia; later lands of the Habsburg monarchy and Austria-Hungary
Czechoslovak founding milestone
28 October 1918: declaration of the independent Czechoslovak state
Post-communist milestone
1989 Velvet Revolution, followed by transition to pluralist democracy
Contemporary state independence
1 January 1993: Czech Republic and Slovakia became separate sovereign states
Constitution
Constitution adopted 16 December 1992, effective 1 January 1993
National day
28 October (establishment of Czechoslovakia, 1918); 1 January is the day of the restoration of the independent Czech state
ISO 3166-1 identifiers

Country-code top-level domain

Historical context: The medieval Czech lands were an important part of Central European political and cultural history. They became constituent lands of Czechoslovakia in 1918, endured Nazi occupation during World War II, and entered the communist period after 1948. The peaceful 1989 Velvet Revolution preceded the consensual 1993 separation from Slovakia. The 1918 national commemoration must not be mistaken for Czechia’s sovereign-state founding date in 1993. People and Society

Official language
Czech
Other languages and communities
Slovak, Ukrainian, Polish, Romani, Vietnamese, German, and other minority and migrant languages; Czech sign language has legal recognition
Religious belief, 2021 census
Of persons who answered the voluntary religion question, 68.3% reported no religious belief and 18.7% reported adherence to a church or religious society; the latter excludes believers without church affiliation
Nonresponse, 2021 census
30.1% of residents left the voluntary religion question unanswered; response percentages must not be stated as shares of every resident
Societal context
Historically Catholic and Protestant traditions coexist with large religiously unaffiliated populations; modern society also includes growing migrant communities

Demographic interpretation: The Czech 2021 census religion question was voluntary. Answer-based percentages differ substantially from percentages of the full census population and should be interpreted with nonresponse included. [8]

Government and Institutions

System
Unitary parliamentary republic with directly elected head of state
Head of state
President, directly elected for a five-year term
Head of government
Prime minister, leading a cabinet responsible to the Chamber of Deputies
Legislature
Bicameral Parliament: Chamber of Deputies (200 seats) and Senate (81 seats)
Administrative divisions
14 regions (kraje), including the capital Prague; municipalities constitute local government
Constitutional order
Constitution effective 1 January 1993, together with the Charter of Fundamental Rights and Freedoms within the constitutional order
Judiciary
Constitutional Court and independent general court system

Institutional descriptions refer to the legal and constitutional design; incumbents and cabinet composition may change between updates. [10]

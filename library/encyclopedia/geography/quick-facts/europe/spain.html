Country Overview

Spain is a parliamentary constitutional monarchy occupying much of the Iberian Peninsula, with the Balearic and Canary Islands and the autonomous North African cities of Ceuta and Melilla. Madrid is the capital. Spain’s contemporary constitutional framework emerged from the democratic transition after Francisco Franco’s dictatorship and the Constitution of 1978. Spain has been an EU member since 1986 and participates in the euro area, Schengen area and NATO. [6]

Capital

UN M49 classification
Europe / Southern Europe [3]
State formation
Dynastic union of the crowns of Castile and Aragon in the late fifteenth century; modern centralized state evolved gradually rather than through a single independence declaration
Present constitutional era
1978 Constitution, approved by referendum 6 December 1978 and entered into force 29 December 1978
Democratic transition
Transition after the death of Francisco Franco in November 1975; parliamentary elections in June 1977
National day
12 October (Fiesta Nacional de España)
ISO 3166-1 identifiers

Country-code top-level domain
.es

Historical context: The Iberian Peninsula has a long history of prehistoric settlement, Roman rule, Visigothic kingdoms, Islamic polities beginning in 711, and Christian kingdoms. The dynastic union of Ferdinand of Aragon and Isabella of Castile and the end of the Granada war in 1492 helped consolidate the Spanish monarchy. Spain built a far-reaching global empire that depended on conquest, settlement, coercion and Atlantic slavery. Constitutional conflict, empire loss and political turmoil marked the nineteenth century. The Second Republic began in 1931, followed by the Civil War of 1936–1939 and Francisco Franco’s dictatorship until 1975. Spain’s democracy developed through the 1978 Constitution and integration with European institutions. [12]

People and Society

Official state-wide language
Spanish (Castilian)
Co-official territorial languages
Catalan/Valencian, Galician, Basque and Aranese/Occitan have differing official status in their autonomous territories
Additional linguistic traditions
Asturian, Aragonese and other local linguistic varieties are protected to varying degrees; extensive migrant languages
Religious composition, June 2026 survey
CIS barometer: 17.4% practicing Catholic and 37.4% non-practicing Catholic, among survey respondents aged 18+ [9]
Religious minorities, same survey
3.2% identified as believers of another religion; Muslim, Protestant, Orthodox, Jewish and other communities are present
Nonreligious, same survey
13.0% agnostic, 12.0% indifferent or nonbeliever and 15.1% atheist (June 2026 CIS survey)
Migration and demographics
Immigration has been an important contributor to recent population growth; Spain also faces aging and low fertility
Cultural diversity
Strong regional and local identities; regional parliaments exercise statutory self-government

Demographic interpretation: The CIS June 2026 figures are self-reported in an adult opinion survey (4,024 interviews), not a religious census and not a measure of religious attendance. Catholic practicing/non-practicing categories, other belief and nonreligious categories should not be confused with legal membership in a religious institution. [10]

Country Overview

Spain is a parliamentary constitutional monarchy occupying much of the Iberian Peninsula, with the Balearic and Canary Islands and the autonomous North African cities of Ceuta and Melilla. Madrid is the capital. Spain’s contemporary constitutional framework emerged from the democratic transition after Francisco Franco’s dictatorship and the Constitution of 1978. Spain has been an EU member since 1986 and participates in the euro area, Schengen area and NATO. [6]

Spain is a constitutional monarchy and parliamentary democracy occupying most of the Iberian Peninsula as well as the Balearic and Canary Islands, Ceuta and Melilla. Madrid is the capital, but extensive constitutional autonomy is exercised through 17 autonomous communities and two autonomous cities. Spain's history includes Roman and Visigothic kingdoms, Islamic and Christian medieval polities, global colonial expansion, the nineteenth-century constitutional struggle, the 1936–39 Civil War, Francisco Franco's dictatorship and the transition to democracy embodied in the Constitution of 1978. King Felipe VI serves as head of state and Pedro Sánchez as president of the Government at the November 6, 2026 editorial cutoff; a cabinet reshuffle was announced November 4. Spain participates in the EU, euro area, Schengen and NATO. The national statistics office estimated 49,801,559 residents on July 1, 2026, while the World Bank's 2025 series is separately presented for cross-country comparison. The economy combines manufacturing, agricultural exports, tourism, logistics, digital services and renewable energy, and its trade balances must distinguish physical goods from service receipts.

Identity and State Formation

Spain's formation is not reducible to one independence date. Roman rule, Visigothic government, al-Andalus, Christian principalities and multiple religious and linguistic communities shaped the peninsula. The late-fifteenth-century dynastic union of Castile and Aragon joined crowns without instantly abolishing all separate laws and institutions. The Granada conquest and expulsion policies of 1492 were followed by transatlantic empire building through conquest, settlement, forced labor, slavery and missionary projects. Portugal and Spain maintained distinct dynastic and maritime histories despite temporary composite monarchy during 1580–1640. Bourbon rule and eighteenth-century reforms increased central state capacity, but the nineteenth century brought constitutional conflicts, empire loss, civil wars and industrial transformation. The Second Republic of 1931, the 1936–39 Civil War and Franco's dictatorship made the twentieth century particularly consequential. After Franco died in 1975, parliamentary elections and the December 1978 constitution established the present democratic order. This new framework safeguards rights, recognizes autonomous institutions and retains a constitutional monarch. The history of empire, civil conflict and democratization remains a topic of scholarship and public memory, not a single celebratory founding narrative. [12]

Historical context: The Iberian Peninsula has a long history of prehistoric settlement, Roman rule, Visigothic kingdoms, Islamic polities beginning in 711, and Christian kingdoms. The dynastic union of Ferdinand of Aragon and Isabella of Castile and the end of the Granada war in 1492 helped consolidate the Spanish monarchy. Spain built a far-reaching global empire that depended on conquest, settlement, coercion and Atlantic slavery. Constitutional conflict, empire loss and political turmoil marked the nineteenth century. The Second Republic began in 1931, followed by the Civil War of 1936–1939 and Francisco Franco’s dictatorship until 1975. Spain’s democracy developed through the 1978 Constitution and integration with European institutions. [12]

Geography and Population

Spain's geographic diversity is unusually pronounced. The Meseta Central and surrounding mountain systems shape the inland plateau, while the Pyrenees border France, the Ebro basin links northeastern agriculture and industry, and the Guadalquivir basin supports Andalusian settlements and irrigation. Wet Atlantic northern landscapes contrast with drier Mediterranean environments, leading to differing water-demand and flood-management challenges. The Balearic Islands have a Mediterranean setting; the Canary Islands lie off northwestern Africa, with volcanic relief, separate ecosystems and a special EU territorial context. Ceuta and Melilla are autonomous cities on the African coast with specific international-border questions. Spanish administrative geography consists of 17 autonomous communities and two autonomous cities, plus provinces, municipalities and other local units. Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia, Seville, Bilbao and Málaga have distinct economic functions. INE's provisional 1 July 2026 resident estimate of 49,801,559, including 7,437,543 foreign citizens, must be distinguished from 2025 World Bank estimates and from the number of foreign-born residents. Climate change, wildfire risk, drought, coastal erosion and population pressure on urban housing require different regional responses. [18]

Economy and International Trade

Spain has one of Europe's larger diversified economies, with manufacturing, finance, business services, transport, retail, construction, energy, agriculture and tourism. Automobile plants and parts suppliers connect to European production chains, while agri-food exporters supply fruit, vegetables, olive oil, wine, meats and processed foods to global markets. Pharmaceutical and chemical industries, capital goods and industrial machinery are important in merchandise trade. The government's February 2026 release reported preliminary 2025 goods exports of €387.092 billion and imports of €444.146 billion, a merchandise deficit of approximately €57.054 billion. Tourism is not an additional merchandise category; visitor expenditure appears in the services account and supports regional employment from the Balearics and Canaries to major cities. Renewable electricity and electric-grid projects, housing affordability, labor-market participation, public debt and regional productivity are central policy issues. Euro membership supplies a common currency but does not prevent differences in wages, consumer prices or regional unemployment. GDP per capita in U.S. dollars is also sensitive to the euro–dollar exchange rate and cannot be treated as a national median salary. Gross exports are not equal to value added produced solely in Spain. [20]

People and Society

Spain's official state-wide language is Castilian Spanish, while Catalan/Valencian, Galician, Basque and Aranese/Occitan have co-official status in their relevant autonomous territories under constitutional and statutory rules. Languages such as Asturian and Aragonese enjoy other forms of protection; migration has expanded the range of languages used in cities. The June 2026 CIS adult survey reported self-identification among practicing and non-practicing Catholics, believers in other religions, agnostics, nonbelievers and atheists. Those survey percentages do not quantify adherence among children or religious service attendance. Immigration contributes to recent population growth and labor supply, with people from Latin America, North Africa, the rest of Europe and other regions maintaining varied cultural ties. Education and public health responsibilities are distributed between the state and autonomous communities, creating service-delivery differences. Spain's cultural heritage encompasses ancient, Islamic, Christian, Jewish, regional and modern traditions, including UNESCO-listed landscapes and sites. Housing costs, labor conditions, aging, family structures, urbanization and inequalities differ substantially between regions and demographic groups and should be measured with appropriately dated national statistics. [9]

Government and Institutions

The 1978 Constitution defines Spain as a parliamentary monarchy whose government is led by the president of the Government, commonly called prime minister, responsible to the elected Congress of Deputies. King Felipe VI is constitutional head of state; his acts and political responsibilities differ from those of the elected executive. The Cortes Generales consists of a 350-member Congress and a Senate whose membership depends partly on designated regional representation. Pedro Sánchez continued as prime minister in November 2026. The official La Moncloa cabinet roster records a November 4 ministerial reshuffle, a personnel change that did not create a new prime minister or erase the XV Legislature. The state of autonomies assigns significant education, health, transport and other responsibilities to 17 autonomous communities and the autonomous cities of Ceuta and Melilla, although specific competences vary under statutes and national law. Spain has a Supreme Court and a separate Constitutional Court. Public administration, electoral law, regional national identities, judicial appointments, corruption investigations and coalition bargaining are recurring political topics. Legislative membership and constitutional sovereignty must be distinguished from the number of autonomous governments and local municipalities. [15]

International Profile

Spain is a member of the UN, EU, euro area, Schengen, NATO, WTO, OECD and Council of Europe under separate legal frameworks. Its EU membership began in 1986, euro accounting in 1999, euro cash in 2002 and NATO membership in 1982. Bilateral relationships with Portugal and France involve cross-border infrastructure, energy, water and migration. Spain also maintains extensive diplomatic, educational, linguistic and business relationships with Latin American countries, while the Spanish-speaking world's diversity cannot be equated with Spain's sovereignty. Mediterranean and Atlantic maritime interests include shipping, fisheries and environmental management. Relations with Morocco involve migration, commerce and unresolved competing positions over Ceuta and Melilla; the status of Gibraltar is separately contested between Spain and the United Kingdom, with treaty-based and local arrangements affecting travel and work. The Canary Islands have a distinct Atlantic location that shapes nearby African cooperation. UNESCO currently lists 50 World Heritage properties for Spain, including architectural monuments, archaeological sites, transnational cultural landscapes and natural heritage. Heritage counts, European membership and the number of overseas diplomatic relationships measure different forms of international engagement. [21]

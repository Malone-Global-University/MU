Country Overview

Sweden is a Nordic constitutional monarchy and parliamentary democracy on the Scandinavian Peninsula. Stockholm is the capital. The country combines a manufacturing, technology and services economy with extensive forests, lakes and northern Arctic territories. Sweden joined the European Union in 1995 and NATO in 2024, while retaining the Swedish krona instead of adopting the euro. In November 2026 it is governed by a caretaker cabinet following the September parliamentary election. [6]

Sweden is a parliamentary constitutional monarchy whose economy combines specialized manufacturing, digital services, forestry and a large public welfare sector. Following the 13 September 2026 Riksdag election, the outgoing government became a caretaker cabinet on 17 September. The legislature rejected Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist's prime-ministerial candidacy on 3 November by 324 votes to 25; the Speaker subsequently tasked Ulf Kristersson with exploring a new government, with a report due by 10 November. Thus on 6 November 2026 Kristersson remained caretaker prime minister, not the confirmed leader of a newly installed administration. The national statistical office reported a 2025 year-end population of 10,605,529, distinct from the earlier World Bank 2025 annual series and a later August 2026 monthly count. Goods trade also uses a preliminary 2025 publication with different scope from services exports. Sweden joined NATO in 2024, an important departure from its preceding longstanding nonalignment, but remains outside the euro area.

Identity and State Formation

Swedish statehood emerged through medieval kingdoms, aristocratic conflicts, church organization and the late medieval Scandinavian union, rather than through a single modern independence vote. Gustav Vasa's election in 1523 marked the effective end of Sweden's participation in the Kalmar Union and became a key national commemoration. The sixteenth-century Reformation transformed religious and royal administration, while seventeenth-century campaigns made Sweden a major Baltic military power. The Great Northern War and subsequent constitutional experiments changed Sweden's geopolitical position. The union with Norway from 1814 to 1905 brought a dynastic relationship without merging the countries' national institutions. Nineteenth-century urbanization, industrialization and migration preceded the development of strong labor organizations and democratic parties. During the twentieth century, Swedish policy emphasized neutrality in major wars and expanded public services, collective bargaining and social insurance. EU accession in 1995 and NATO accession in 2024 belong to distinct stages of integration. Historical continuity must not obscure the transition from royal political authority to the present ceremonial monarchy under the 1974 Instrument of Government. [6]

Historical context: Swedish polities developed during the Viking and medieval periods, culminating in a consolidated kingdom and northern European maritime power. Sweden entered the Kalmar Union in 1397 and left the union following Gustav Vasa’s elevation in 1523. In the seventeenth century the Swedish state expanded across the Baltic region, but its great-power position declined after the Great Northern War. Sweden and Norway were united under one crown from 1814 to 1905. Sweden avoided direct combat in both world wars, built a comprehensive welfare state during the twentieth century, and entered the EU in 1995. The Russian invasion of Ukraine and a reassessment of neutrality contributed to Sweden’s NATO accession on 7 March 2024. [6]

Geography and Population

Sweden runs from the southern Baltic coastal and agricultural regions to northern forests, mountains and sub-Arctic communities. This large north-south span gives the country differing growing seasons, winter conditions, settlement densities and public-service challenges. The Scandinavian Mountains form the long western border zone adjoining Norway; Sweden also borders Finland and has wide maritime connections to Denmark and other Baltic states. Lake Vänern, Lake Vättern, major river catchments and the northern coast influence hydropower, biodiversity and transport. Stockholm's archipelago and the islands of Gotland and Öland form distinct maritime environments with heritage and defense implications. Major population centers are concentrated in southern and central urban regions, while the far north supports mining, hydropower, Indigenous Sámi culture and extensive forestry. County governments, elected regions and 290 municipalities perform different statutory duties. Total surface area including inland waters must be distinguished from the smaller land-only indicator. Swedish national population registers specify particular dates; World Bank year estimates should not silently replace the official year-end or monthly observations. [17]

Economy and International Trade

Sweden's economy includes passenger and heavy vehicles, machinery, telecommunications equipment, pharmaceuticals, mining, paper, wood products, renewable energy and sophisticated business services. International corporations, export supply chains and domestic research institutions connect industrial regions to global markets. Statistics Sweden's preliminary 2025 goods exports totaled SEK 2,042.8 billion and imports SEK 1,968.7 billion, recording a goods surplus of SEK 74.1 billion; the numbers do not include international services such as digital consulting, transport or intellectual-property-related transactions. Different goods statistics can be revised after initial publication. Technology-intensive manufacturing requires imported components and skilled labor, so export receipts are not identical to domestic value added. The krona remains Sweden's currency although Sweden is an EU member, with monetary policy outside the Eurosystem. Forestry policy involves environmental and land-use decisions as well as export revenue. Electrification, mining investment, energy distribution and industrial decarbonization depend on permitting and infrastructure capacity. Welfare spending and household disposable income should be analyzed with their own statistics rather than inferred from current-dollar GDP-per-capita comparisons. [18]

People and Society

Swedish is the principal language defined in the Language Act, while Finnish, Meänkieli, Sámi, Romani Chib and Yiddish enjoy recognized national-minority-language status. Indigenous Sámi communities and the Sámi Parliament have cultural and political functions, while Sámi lives and languages extend across Nordic borders. The Church of Sweden reports member registrations, but those are not a census of belief, attendance or all religious affiliation. Muslim, Catholic, Orthodox, free-church, Jewish, Buddhist, secular and other communities have different histories and institutional forms. Schools, universities and labor markets reflect both longstanding domestic cultural practices and immigration. Sweden's social-policy model includes parental leave, child services, healthcare, unemployment insurance and pensions, each managed through different programs and eligibility rules. Challenges of housing, regional service access, aging, skills training, public safety and social integration vary by municipality and cohort. National population figures are annual register counts rather than complete counts of all language, ethnicity or religious affiliations. The 2025 year-end population and the August 2026 monthly register data measure different points in time and should not be presented as contradictory simply because they differ. [19]

Government and Institutions

Sweden's system is a parliamentary monarchy where King Carl XVI Gustaf has a representative, nonexecutive role and the Riksdag controls the choice and continued tolerance of a prime minister. The unicameral Riksdag has 349 elected members and operates under the constitution's four fundamental laws. Unlike a presidential system, a speaker proposes prime-ministerial candidates and a proposed candidate can take office unless an absolute majority of members vote against the appointment. The September 2026 election created complex coalition negotiations; Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson was discharged on 17 September but continued to lead the caretaker government. On 3 November the Riksdag rejected Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist, with 324 votes against and 25 for; the Speaker then assigned Kristersson to explore formation options and report by 10 November. As of the November 6 editorial date, those developments did not constitute an installed new cabinet. Parliamentary committees, independent judicial administration, county authorities and municipalities have separate roles. Civil rights, budget votes and government formation depend on constitutional and statutory procedures, not merely on the party receiving the most seats. [15]

International Profile

Sweden was an original postwar UN participant after joining in November 1946 and became a member of the European Union in 1995. Its membership of the Schengen area started in 2001, yet the country retains its currency, the Swedish krona, instead of using the euro. On 7 March 2024 Sweden joined NATO following a major reassessment of national security after Russia's invasion of Ukraine. NATO membership should not be confused with EU membership, Schengen cooperation or the Nordic Council's regional programs. Sweden participates in the Council of Europe, OECD, OSCE and WTO under distinct charters, and in Nordic cooperation on research, labor mobility, public policy and culture. A maritime Baltic setting makes shipping, seabed infrastructure and environmental protection important security and economic issues. Sweden's development assistance and international diplomacy have varied over time and government policy. UNESCO sites encompass urban, industrial, archaeological and natural heritage in multiple regions. Its International links must also distinguish signed treaties, implemented obligations and political proposals: a new caretaker cabinet does not alter membership of alliances unless formal procedures change.

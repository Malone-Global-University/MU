Country Overview

Finland is a Northern European republic shaped by the Baltic Sea, forests and lakes, Arctic conditions and a long eastern frontier with Russia. Its democratic institutions and economy developed through Swedish rule, a nineteenth-century autonomous grand duchy in the Russian Empire, independence in December 1917 and difficult wars in the twentieth century. Helsinki leads a network of cities that includes Tampere, Turku, Oulu and other industrial, educational and regional centers. The population is small relative to the country's extensive territory, and settlement is concentrated in the south and major transport corridors.

The present state combines parliamentary government, a directly elected president, nationally recognized Finnish and Swedish languages, constitutional autonomy for Åland, and special protection for the Indigenous Sámi. A high-income economy includes advanced machinery, forest industries, metals, chemicals, digital services, clean-energy technologies and extensive public services. Finland joined the European Union in 1995, adopted the euro and became a NATO member in April 2023. Because census-like register counts, World Bank annual estimates and customs exports have different reference dates and concepts, each major statistical figure below identifies its reporting basis.

Identity and State Formation

Finland has no founding date that captures its entire history: state formation, autonomy, sovereign independence and present constitutional design belong to different centuries. Its political culture developed amid Finnish-, Swedish- and Sámi-speaking communities whose histories are not reducible to a single national narrative. [12] [8]

Official state name
Republic of Finland; Suomen tasavalta in Finnish and Republiken Finland in Swedish, both national languages of the republic.
Short name
Finland (Suomi in Finnish); the Finnish and Swedish forms appear in legislation, official notices and many public services.
UN regional classification
Northern Europe under the UN M49 statistical system; Finland is also a Nordic state and part of the European Union.
Capital's historical role
Helsinki became the grand duchy's capital in 1812; its ministries, courts, universities and harbor shaped nineteenth-century administrative centralization.
Early societies
Archaeological settlement developed after the last glaciation; hunter-fisher communities, later cultivation and long-distance exchange predate modern nationhood.
Sámi historical presence
Sámi peoples have long inhabited northern Fennoscandia; contemporary national borders divide a region with older languages, livelihoods and kinship networks.
Swedish-era political history
Finland's territories became integrated into the Swedish kingdom; institutions, law, Lutheran religion and Swedish-language elites left enduring legacies.
1808–1809 war
Sweden ceded Finland to Russia after the Finnish War, transforming the lands into a grand duchy with institutional autonomy.
Grand Duchy period
From 1809 to 1917 Finland maintained particular administrative, legal and monetary institutions while remaining under Russian imperial sovereignty.
Language revival
Nineteenth-century Finnish-language movements expanded publishing, education and administration, challenging exclusive Swedish predominance without erasing bilingual histories.
Kalevala
Elias Lönnrot's nineteenth-century compilation of oral poetry became influential in cultural nationalism, although it is an edited literary work.
1863 language reforms
Imperial language measures initiated a longer process through which Finnish gained administrative standing alongside Swedish.
National parliamentary reform
Universal and equal suffrage in 1906 allowed women both to vote and to stand for parliamentary election, an internationally significant early reform.
Independence declaration
Parliament approved Finnish independence on 6 December 1917 during the collapse of Russian imperial and revolutionary authority.
International recognition
Soviet Russia recognized independence around the turn of 1917–1918, followed by other states; recognition and parliamentary declaration are distinct events.
1918 civil war
Conflict between Red and White forces caused deaths, prison-camp suffering and enduring social divisions, followed by political reconciliation efforts.
1919 republican settlement
The 1919 form of government established a republican constitutional order after debates about monarchy and the distribution of executive power.
Interwar society
Land reform, industrialization, education and party politics expanded opportunities while rural and class inequalities persisted.
Winter War
The Soviet invasion in November 1939 opened the Winter War, ending with the March 1940 Moscow Peace Treaty and territorial losses.
Continuation War
Finland fought the Soviet Union again in 1941–1944 in conjunction with Germany's war against the USSR; motives, alliance and military decisions remain historically debated.
Lapland War
Finland fought to expel German forces from the north in 1944–1945, and retreating troops destroyed substantial infrastructure.
Postwar agreements
Peace obligations, resettlement of evacuees and relations with the Soviet Union shaped the immediate post-1945 state.
Cold War neutrality
Finland maintained multiparty democracy and market institutions while pursuing a cautious policy toward Moscow, distinct from Warsaw Pact membership.
Nordic welfare development
Public education, pensions, healthcare and municipal social services expanded after World War II in successive reform waves.
European integration
EU accession occurred on 1 January 1995 after a national referendum, reflecting changes in the post-Cold War European order.
Euro adoption
Finland entered the euro area in 1999 and introduced euro notes and coins in 2002, replacing the Finnish markka.
NATO membership
Finland joined the North Atlantic Treaty Organization on 4 April 2023, ending its longstanding position outside military alliances.
Current constitution
Constitution of Finland entered into force on 1 March 2000; it consolidated constitutional statutes and strengthened parliamentary government.
Independence Day
December 6 commemorates the 1917 independence decision, not the 1919 constitution or the 2000 constitutional revision.
Flag and symbols
Blue Nordic cross on white; the coat of arms bears a crowned lion, and national symbols appear in civic ceremonies.
ISO codes
FI (alpha-2), FIN (alpha-3), 246 (numeric); country-code internet domain .fi and international dialing code +358.

Interpretation: Historical chronology differentiates Finnish independence in 1917, republican constitutional formation in 1919 and the operative consolidated Constitution of 2000. The Sámi experience and the autonomy of Åland require separate treatment from the national majority narrative. [12] [8]

Geography and Population

Finland is not merely a forested land of lakes. The southern coastal archipelagos, glaciated interior, northern fells, Baltic maritime routes and Arctic river systems support markedly different patterns of settlement, industry and Indigenous land use. Its long Russian border has also made geography central to security planning. [2] [13]

Geographic area
Approximately 338,363 km² in the European Union's comparative surface-area series; methods differ from land-only international estimates.
Land-only area
Around 304,000 km² in the World Bank/FAO land series, excluding inland waters that form a substantial share of Finland's surface. [13]
Borders
Land boundaries with Sweden, Norway and Russia; Baltic maritime approaches link Finland to Estonia and other coastal states.
Gulf of Finland
Southern arm of the Baltic Sea separates the Helsinki region from Estonia and provides important passenger and cargo routes.
Gulf of Bothnia
Western marine zone shared with Sweden; coastal shipping, fishing and industry depend on navigation and winter ice conditions.
Lakeland
Central and eastern Finland contain dense networks of lakes and waterways shaped by glaciation, important for recreation and inland transport.
Lake Saimaa
Extensive southeastern lake system with interconnected basins; conservation efforts include protection of the endangered Saimaa ringed seal.
Major rivers
Kemijoki, Oulujoki, Tornionjoki and other river systems support energy, ecosystems and fishing, with historical alteration by dams.
Northern Lapland
Northernmost region has boreal forests, fells and open tundra-like environments; winter length and seasonal light differ sharply from the south.
Highest point
Halti's Finnish summit area reaches approximately 1,324 meters; the mountain's highest summit lies across the border in Norway.
Forests
Forest covers much of the country and supports commercial timber, biodiversity, recreation and carbon-storage policy; forest condition varies widely.
Wetlands and peatlands
Bogs and peatlands influence water quality, greenhouse-gas accounting, conservation and energy history; drainage has modified many sites.
Glacial legacy
Ice sheets shaped bedrock exposure, eskers, lakes, moraines and landforms; postglacial land uplift continues along parts of the coast.
Archipelago Sea
Southwest coastal region holds numerous islands and skerries, where ferry access and island livelihoods require specialized services.
Åland geography
Autonomous archipelago between Finland and Sweden, distinct for its Swedish language, legislature, maritime links and demilitarized status.
Sámi homeland
Designated northern area in parts of Lapland; statutory protections concern Sámi language and culture and do not imply a single uniform land tenure regime.
Settlement concentration
Helsinki metropolitan region accounts for a large share of jobs, research and population; other cities serve extensive regional hinterlands.
Major cities
Helsinki, Espoo, Tampere, Vantaa, Oulu, Turku, Jyväskylä and Kuopio are important municipal and economic centers.
National register population
Statistics Finland counted 5,652,881 permanent residents on 31 December 2025, an official end-of-year administrative figure. [2]
World Bank population comparison
The uploaded 2025 World Bank series reports 5,646,436, reflecting a different annual estimation convention rather than a contradictory census. [1]
2025 annual increase
Finland's official resident population rose by 16,910 during 2025, slower than in the two preceding years. [2]
Population density
Approximately 16.7 persons per km² when the 2025 national resident total is divided by the total-area reference; local density is highly uneven.
Ageing
About 23.8% of residents were 65 or older at the end of 2025, posing distinct workforce and care-service challenges.
Migration
Recent population change has been heavily influenced by international movement; migration categories and citizenship should not be conflated.
Births and deaths
Statistics Finland reports 45,832 births and 59,209 deaths in 2025, indicating a negative natural population balance.
Transport corridors
Railways and highways connect the south to western ports and northern mining/tourism areas; sparse settlement raises infrastructure costs.
Winter conditions
Snow, freezing sea passages and darkness affect energy demand, logistics and public safety; warming trends shift conditions unevenly.
Climate trends
Rising temperatures, altered snowfall and changing precipitation patterns influence forests, water management, agriculture and species distributions.
Biodiversity
Boreal habitats, lakes, Baltic coastal waters and northern ecosystems host distinct species subject to habitat fragmentation and climate stress.
Protected areas
National parks such as Koli, Nuuksio and Urho Kekkonen preserve different cultural and ecological landscapes.
Energy geography
Hydropower is associated with river systems, wind development with suitable land and marine sites, and nuclear production with dedicated plant locations.
Statistical interpretation
Geographic area, forest cover, resident population and population density must each use consistent territorial and temporal definitions.

Interpretation: Finland’s 31 December 2025 national register population (5,652,881) is not the same statistical construct as the World Bank 2025 series in the original page. Total area includes extensive inland waters, whereas FAO land area excludes them. [2] [13] [4]

Economy and International Trade

Finland combines export-oriented industrial capacity with technology services, public infrastructure and a skilled workforce. International manufacturing demand, energy prices and trade links affect a relatively small domestic market. The role of forest industries is significant, but Finland is not a single-resource economy: specialized machinery, metals, pharmaceuticals, engineering, software and services all matter. [5] [6]

Economic classification
High-income market economy integrated into the EU single market, euro area and Nordic manufacturing, logistics and research networks.
Nominal GDP
Approximately US$317.04 billion in the uploaded World Bank 2025 current-US-dollar series; revisions and exchange rates affect comparability. [1]
GDP per capita
Approximately US$56,149 in World Bank current dollars for 2025; it is not median wage, disposable income or purchasing-power parity. [1]
Real GDP growth
About 0.2% in the 2025 World Bank series used by the original profile; annual revisions can change the rate. [1]
Currency
Euro (EUR); euro-area membership began in 1999 and physical euro currency began circulation in 2002.
Central bank
Bank of Finland forms part of the Eurosystem and contributes to monetary operations, research and domestic financial stability.
Goods exports 2025
Finnish Ministry for Foreign Affairs trade statistics report €74.261 billion in merchandise exports for 2025, drawn from customs records. [5]
Goods imports 2025
The same trade dashboard records €75.658 billion in goods imports, a distinct series from services flows and national accounts. [5]
Services exports 2025
The ministry dashboard records €39.754 billion in services exports; services are not counted within merchandise-export totals. [5]
Services imports 2025
€44.064 billion in the ministry's services series; service categories include international business and transportation activity.
Total exports by type
The dashboard sums goods and services to €114.015 billion; the calculation should not be mistaken for manufacturing exports. [5]
Forest industry
Pulp, paper, paperboard, sawn timber and engineered wood products form established export chains dependent on global demand and sustainability requirements.
Mechanical engineering
Industrial machinery, engines, cranes, lifting equipment, marine machinery and specialized production systems support export manufacturing.
Metal industries
Stainless steel, refined metals, fabricated metal products and mining-related equipment supply European and international customers.
Chemical manufacturing
Specialty chemicals, refined petroleum products and industrial inputs serve domestic process industries and export markets.
Technology heritage
Telecom innovation and Nokia's industrial history contributed to engineering skills; present ICT activity spans networking, software and services.
Gaming industry
Digital games and other creative software exports demonstrate internationally scalable intangible production and skilled professional work.
Marine industry
Shipyards, ice-class vessels, marine power systems and design services respond to northern shipping needs and global orders.
Energy mix
Nuclear, wind, hydropower, biomass and other sources contribute to electricity supply; generation and final consumption are different measures.
Olkiluoto nuclear
Large nuclear units at Olkiluoto are important to domestic electricity generation and debate about power-system reliability.
Wind development
Installed wind generation has grown, but output varies by weather and requires transmission, flexibility and balancing arrangements.
Biomass distinction
Forest biomass appears in energy and industrial process heating; emissions accounting and sustainability claims depend on methodology.
Exports by destination
Sweden, the United States and Germany are major goods destinations in Finnish Customs 2025 reporting, with rankings subject to valuation methods.
Imports and supply chains
Components, energy commodities, electronics and capital equipment connect Finland to European and global supplier networks.
Russia trade contraction
Goods trade with Russia has declined sharply since the war in Ukraine and ensuing sanctions; historical shares are not current baselines.
Ports
Helsinki, HaminaKotka, Turku, Rauma and other ports support cargo and ferry connections across the Baltic Sea.
Transport and logistics
Winter shipping support, rail gauges, port capacity and Baltic connections create different cost structures from mainland European logistics.
Agriculture
Dairy, cereal production, livestock and specialty food operations operate under short growing seasons and EU agricultural frameworks.
Tourism
Lapland winter activities, Helsinki urban tourism, lakes and cultural destinations produce services receipts rather than goods exports.
Public finance
National and local tax revenues fund welfare, healthcare, defense, infrastructure and education; fiscal balances vary with economic cycles.
Labor market
An ageing workforce, skills needs and regional employment differences interact with migration and productivity policy.
Housing and construction
Housing, infrastructure and interest-rate movements affect domestic demand and municipal development in different ways.
Inflation
Consumer prices are monitored using Finnish and EU harmonized measures; do not treat a monthly rate as the full-year average.
Productivity
Digitalization, research investment, workforce skills and capital stock are central to medium-term competitiveness debates.
Trade-statistics limitation
Customs border values, services surveys and GDP value added are three distinct statistical concepts and must not be added indiscriminately.

People and Society

The Finnish social model includes universal public education, extensive social insurance, national language rights and institutions that serve geographically remote areas. Cultural and linguistic identities remain plural: Finnish- and Swedish-speaking residents, Sámi communities and newer migrants have different histories and legal positions. [3] [18]

National languages
Finnish and Swedish have nationally recognized constitutional standing and are used in state administration under language legislation.
Finnish mother tongue
Statistics Finland recorded 4,719,802 residents registered with Finnish as mother tongue at end-2025, about 83.5% of residents. [3]
Swedish mother tongue
The end-2025 register identified 284,611 Swedish mother-tongue residents, around 5.0%, concentrated partly on coasts and Åland. [3]
Other mother tongues
Approximately 646,392 residents, or 11.4%, had a language other than Finnish, Swedish or Sámi registered as mother tongue at end-2025. [3]
Sámi register language
2,076 people registered a Sámi language as mother tongue in 2025; this is not a census count of all persons of Sámi identity.
Sámi languages
Northern Sámi, Inari Sámi and Skolt Sámi have distinct speakers, rights and revitalization programs, especially in northern communities.
Language-register caveat
Administrative mother-tongue data classify individuals into registered categories; bilingual ability and cultural identity are not fully captured.
Swedish-language institutions
Schools, broadcasting, public services and universities provide Swedish-language provision under Finland's bilingual legal framework.
Åland society
Åland has Swedish as its official regional language and its own elected parliament, with citizenship-like domicile rights in regional law.
School system
Public comprehensive education, later general or vocational secondary pathways and higher education are supplemented by specialized support programs.
Teacher education
University-level teacher preparation and school autonomy have contributed to Finland's international education reputation, although outcomes vary over time.
Higher education
Universities conduct academic research, while universities of applied sciences emphasize professional and regional collaboration.
Major universities
University of Helsinki, Aalto University, University of Turku, Tampere University and University of Oulu contribute to national research capacity.
Healthcare organization
Public services are principally organized through wellbeing services counties and Helsinki, with private and occupational healthcare alongside.
Municipal responsibilities
Municipalities retain important tasks in education, planning, culture and local infrastructure after national social-and-health-service reforms.
Social insurance
Kela administers major statutory social benefits; eligibility depends on residence, employment and specific benefit legislation.
Pensions
Earnings-related occupational pensions and national or guarantee pensions form different pillars of old-age income protection.
Religion and registration
The Evangelical Lutheran Church remains the largest registered religious community, but administrative membership is not a survey of faith.
Lutheran membership
Church reporting places end-2025 membership around 61.1% of residents; membership neither measures worship attendance nor religious belief.
Other religions
Orthodox Christians, Muslims, Catholics, Jews, other religions and persons outside registered communities are part of Finland's social landscape.
Cultural literature
The Kalevala, works of Aleksis Kivi and modern bilingual literature have contributed to shifting conceptions of national culture.
Architecture and design
Alvar Aalto and other designers helped develop distinctive modernist and functionalist traditions with international recognition.
Music
Classical composition, contemporary music, choral traditions, folk forms and popular genres coexist across regions.
Sauna culture
Sauna practices are widespread in domestic and communal life and recognized as intangible cultural heritage, but vary among households.
Food culture
Rye, fish, root vegetables, dairy, berries and regionally distinct dishes reflect agricultural, coastal and forest environments.
Sports
Ice hockey, cross-country skiing, athletics, football and other sports support local associations and national competition.
Public libraries
Library services operate widely through municipal networks and are significant for literacy, cultural access and digital inclusion.
Freedom of press
Strong legal protection for expression coexists with debates on media business models, misinformation and online harms.
Gender equality
The 1906 suffrage reform and later social policies advanced participation; pay differences and care burdens remain policy subjects.
Migration integration
Language education, employment pathways and anti-discrimination measures influence the integration of newly arrived residents.
Regional disparities
Job opportunities and access to specialist services vary between the Helsinki region, regional cities and sparsely populated north and east.
Housing
Owner occupation, rental housing and cooperative or subsidized arrangements coexist, with different market pressures across urban centers.
Demographic challenges
Population ageing, low fertility and workforce recruitment create long-term questions for taxation, care and educational capacity.

Interpretation: End-2025 mother-tongue shares describe population-register categories, not the full range of languages spoken at home. Church membership, self-described belief, attendance and constitutional language status are separate measures. [3] [18] [19]

Government and Institutions

Finland is a parliamentary republic. The president has constitutionally defined responsibilities, especially in foreign policy alongside the government, while the prime minister and cabinet depend on parliamentary confidence. Local democracy and Åland autonomy add distinct legal layers below the national institutions. [10] [8]

State structure
Unitary parliamentary republic with constitutionally protected local self-government and a special autonomous arrangement for Åland.
President
Alexander Stubb assumed the presidency on 1 March 2024; the office is elected directly for a six-year term. [10]
Prime minister
Petteri Orpo headed the national government at the 6 November 2026 editorial cutoff, as documented in official leadership listings. [10]
Parliament
Unicameral Eduskunta/Riksdagen has 200 seats, including a seat reserved for the Åland constituency.
Parliamentary elections
Members are chosen for four-year terms through proportional representation, using constituencies and statutory rules.
Cabinet
Government is headed by the prime minister, with ministers responsible for portfolios and the cabinet requiring parliamentary confidence.
President's foreign-policy role
President directs foreign policy in cooperation with government, while EU affairs lie chiefly within government and parliamentary procedures.
Defense commander
President is commander-in-chief of the Defense Forces under constitutional provisions, subject to legislation and government cooperation.
Legislative authority
Parliament enacts legislation, approves budgets, scrutinizes government and exercises confidence mechanisms.
Government formation
Coalition negotiations generally follow elections; the president formally appoints the prime minister elected by Parliament.
Constitution
The Constitution entered into force 1 March 2000 and consolidated major previous constitutional instruments.
Supreme Court
Supreme Court is the highest ordinary judicial instance; jurisdiction differs from administrative-law appeals.
Supreme Administrative Court
Highest body for many administrative judicial matters, reviewing specified public-authority decisions.
Constitutional review
Parliament's Constitutional Law Committee plays a significant advance-review role; courts may give constitutional priority under specified conditions.
Independent ombudsman
Parliamentary Ombudsman investigates legality of official conduct and safeguards fundamental and human rights.
Chancellor of Justice
Chancellor of Justice supervises legality of government action and public authorities, a separate institution from the Ombudsman.
Municipalities
Municipal councils govern local issues including land use, culture and education; boundaries and responsibilities change by statute.
Wellbeing services counties
Regional bodies administer much of public healthcare and social welfare, with funding and democratic oversight frameworks.
Helsinki exception
Helsinki retains specific organization of health and social services within the framework established by national reform.
Åland parliament
Lagting exercises legislative authority in specified autonomous matters while Finnish national law applies in others.
Åland demilitarization
Demilitarized and neutralized status has roots in international agreements; contemporary security debates require legal precision.
Sámi Parliament
Representative institution for Sámi cultural and language autonomy within the constitutionally defined Sámi homeland.
Election franchise
National voting rights and residence-based local voting rights operate under different eligibility rules.
Political parties
Coalition-based multiparty politics includes center-right, center-left, green, liberal, nationalist and other traditions.
Public administration
Ministries, agencies and municipalities implement statutory obligations with administrative law and public-access guarantees.
Budget process
Government submits fiscal proposals while Parliament determines appropriations and exercises public-spending oversight.
National Audit Office
Independent state audit functions review finances, compliance and performance across government activities.
Security policy
NATO membership since April 2023 has changed alliance planning while retaining national conscription and territorial-defense traditions.
Conscription
Finland maintains a military-service framework with gender-specific statutory obligations and alternative service provisions.
Emergency preparedness
Civil protection, stockpiles and continuity planning reflect a national comprehensive-security approach across agencies and society.
Local language duties
Administrative services must respect Finnish- and Swedish-language rights, with special regimes in Åland and Sámi language areas.
Rights guarantees
Constitution protects equality, freedom of expression, due process, cultural rights and social rights subject to legislative frameworks.
Transparency
Access to public documents and administrative accountability are important elements of legal governance, subject to privacy and security exceptions.

Interpretation: The president and prime minister perform different constitutional functions. Petteri Orpo and Alexander Stubb are incumbents as of the editorial cutoff; general institutional rules should not be confused with descriptions of a particular coalition’s political program. [10] [8] [9]

International Profile

Finland’s international role links Nordic cooperation, the Baltic Sea, Arctic policy and EU institutions to the security of Europe's northeastern frontier. Since 2023, NATO membership has become part of this framework, while trade, climate, Sámi issues and peace diplomacy have separate legal and diplomatic tracks. [16] [17]

United Nations
Admitted on 14 December 1955, participating in multilateral development, human-rights and security institutions.
European Union
Joined on 1 January 1995; participates in EU legislation, internal-market policies, common external relations and EU budgeting.
Euro area
Joined on 1 January 1999; monetary policy is undertaken within the Eurosystem rather than by a purely independent national exchange-rate regime.
Schengen area
Joined the Schengen framework in March 2001, allowing travel under common rules with specified border controls and exceptions.
NATO
Became a member on 4 April 2023; collective defense obligations and joint defense planning became integral to security policy.
Russia frontier
Finland's long land boundary with Russia is strategically significant; border regulation and bilateral political relations have shifted since 2022.
Sweden relations
Defense, labor, business and culture links connect the two neighbors; Swedish accession to NATO in 2024 deepened joint alliance cooperation.
Estonia relations
The Gulf of Finland connects ports, passenger traffic, commerce, energy infrastructure and security concerns.
Nordic Council
Participates in Nordic parliamentary cooperation involving Denmark, Iceland, Norway, Sweden and autonomous areas.
Arctic Council
Participates as an Arctic state; environmental, scientific and Indigenous issues form part of circumpolar cooperation.
Council of Europe
Member of the human-rights organization and subject to applicable European Convention on Human Rights obligations.
OSCE
Helsinki hosted the signing of the 1975 Helsinki Final Act, a landmark in European diplomatic security cooperation.
OECD
Member since 1969, taking part in comparative economic, education and public-sector policy research.
World Trade Organization
Participates in WTO rules through its own membership and EU common commercial policy arrangements.
Baltic Sea environment
Eutrophication, marine pollution and biodiversity management require cooperation with other Baltic coastal states.
Climate diplomacy
EU climate policy, Arctic impacts, energy technology and forest carbon accounting are prominent international issues.
Development cooperation
Finnish bilateral and multilateral aid operates through national budgets, EU structures and international financial institutions.
Humanitarian action
Cooperation includes refugee assistance, global emergency aid and support for civilian protection under applicable frameworks.
Ukraine policy
Finland has supported Ukraine through European and allied diplomatic, humanitarian and defense channels; assistance totals require dated accounting.
Transboundary waters
Shared rivers and Baltic marine ecosystems require bilateral and multilateral arrangements on environmental management.
UNESCO World Heritage
UNESCO's Finland State Party listing contains eight properties in 2026, including the Aalto Works serial cultural inscription.
Suomenlinna
Helsinki sea fortress reflects Swedish, Russian and Finnish eras and is one of Finland's World Heritage cultural properties.
Old Rauma
Historic wooden town demonstrates urban built heritage, conservation and living commercial activity.
Kvarken Archipelago
Natural World Heritage component documents postglacial land uplift and unusual coastal landscape development.
Struve Geodetic Arc
Transnational scientific-heritage inscription includes Finnish survey stations and stretches across several European states.
Aalto Works
2026 serial inscription recognizes selected works connected to Alvar Aalto's architecture, not every structure by the architect.
UNESCO counting
Serial components form a single World Heritage property; tentative nominations do not count as inscribed properties.
Time zone
Standard EET UTC+2 and summer EEST UTC+3 on the mainland; international scheduling requires seasonal adjustment.
International calling
Country code +358; .fi internet domain, with separate .ax domain used for Åland.
Maritime agreements
Baltic shipping rules, environmental conventions and winter navigation create a specialized regional regulatory environment.
Economic diplomacy
EU commercial policy governs many external trade agreements, while national promotion supports Finnish businesses abroad.
Cultural diplomacy
Universities, design, literature, music, education and Nordic languages contribute to the country's external cultural profile.

Interpretation: UNESCO counts World Heritage properties, not individual buildings or serial components. Northern security institutions, EU trade policy and Sámi cross-border cooperation are different institutional relationships. [16] [17] [7]

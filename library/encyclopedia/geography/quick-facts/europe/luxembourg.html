Country Overview

Luxembourg is a landlocked Western European grand duchy bordering Belgium, France and Germany. Established as a grand duchy at the Congress of Vienna in 1815 and territorially defined by the 1839 Treaty of London, it developed into a sovereign parliamentary constitutional monarchy and founding participant in postwar European integration. Luxembourg City is its capital and a major European financial and institutional center. Grand Duke Guillaume succeeded Henri on 3 October 2025. [10]

Luxembourg is a small multilingual parliamentary grand duchy at the crossroads of Belgium, France and Germany. Its institutions emerged gradually from a medieval county, the 1815 creation of a grand duchy, the territorial settlement of 1839 and later constitutional developments. Today Luxembourg participates in the European Union, euro area, NATO, Benelux and Schengen, and hosts important European institutions. Modern Luxembourg City is simultaneously a national capital, an international financial center and a major cross-border employment destination. The state is particularly unusual in statistical comparison: a large proportion of its workforce resides in neighboring countries and commutes into Luxembourg, contributing to domestic output without being counted in the resident population. The resulting GDP-per-resident measure should not be confused with average household income.

Identity and State Formation

Luxembourg’s political identity developed from the fortress and medieval county founded around 963 through ducal and grand-ducal arrangements under successive dynasties. The Congress of Vienna established the Grand Duchy in 1815 with a personal union to the Dutch crown; the division of 1839 assigned western territories to Belgium. The 1867 Treaty of London reinforced international arrangements for Luxembourg and provided for dismantling the fortress. A separate succession in 1890 ended the Dutch personal union. German occupation during both world wars contributed to national solidarity and eventual participation in Benelux, NATO and European integration. Constitutional reform has been incremental, including the major revised text effective 2023. In October 2025 Guillaume became grand duke after Henri abdicated; this dynastic transition did not alter the parliamentary nature of government. [10]

Geography and Population

The country occupies the northern Oesling, connected with the Ardennes, and the more densely settled Gutland region farther south. The valleys of the Alzette, Sûre and Moselle connect its landscapes to the greater Rhine river system; rivers and hills shape settlement patterns and cross-border transportation. Luxembourg is landlocked but close to some of Europe’s largest metropolitan and freight networks. Its population has grown substantially through international migration, with foreign nationals accounting for a remarkably large share of residents. National population counts measure residents, not the hundreds of thousands who cross into the country for employment. Geographic-area sources can differ slightly in treatment of surface and administrative boundaries, and cantons, electoral constituencies and communes serve different purposes. [19]

Economy and International Trade

Luxembourg’s economy evolved from a major steelmaking center into an international base for investment funds, banking, insurance, professional services, logistics and technology. Steel production remains relevant, but financial services and associated activities have an outsized role in measured cross-border transactions. In 2025 the balance-of-payments tables recorded financial-services export credits of about €81.964 billion, general services export credits of €147.497 billion and goods credits of €29.202 billion. These are not mutually comparable measures of domestic production, and merchanting of goods never entering Luxembourg complicates ordinary customs-trade comparisons. A large share of economic activity depends on workers living in Belgium, France and Germany; GDP divided by a resident-only denominator is therefore a weak proxy for typical earnings. Currency is the euro, and economic regulation combines domestic institutions with EU and euro-area frameworks. [16]

People and Society

Luxembourgish, French and German are central to the country’s identity and public administration, but their legal roles are not identical. Luxembourgish is the national language and is used widely in speech; French is important in legislation, while administrative and judicial practice draws on the established multilingual regime. Portuguese, English and many other languages reflect immigration and cross-border employment. Foreign nationals constitute a large fraction of residents, but nationality must not be confused with language proficiency or immigration status. Catholic traditions shaped public life historically, while modern society includes Protestant, Orthodox, Muslim, Jewish, secular and other communities. Population surveys, church registries and administrative figures measure different concepts. Housing, education, healthcare, commuting and regional labor markets are significant dimensions of a small but globally connected society. [9]

Government and Institutions

The Grand Duchy is a constitutional monarchy under the revised 2023 constitution. The grand duke performs formal head-of-state duties, while the cabinet directs executive policy and remains responsible to the elected Chamber of Deputies. The unicameral Chamber has sixty members, elected in four constituencies under proportional representation. The Council of State assists in legislative review, but is not a second elected parliamentary chamber. Domestic law, EU law and judicial oversight interact through separate institutional channels. Luxembourg is divided into cantons and communes, whose number can change with mergers; care is required when comparing older municipal counts with new administrative lists. Guillaume took the throne on 3 October 2025, with Luc Frieden serving as prime minister at the editorial cutoff. [20]

International Profile

Luxembourg was a founding member of the postwar European Communities and remains a center of EU administration. It hosts institutions of the European Court of Justice, the European Investment Bank and parts of the European Commission and Parliament; institutional seats and offices have distinct legal definitions. The town of Schengen supplied the name for the border agreement, and Luxembourg is a member of the border-free zone and euro area. Membership in NATO, the United Nations and the Council of Europe contributes to defense, diplomatic and human-rights cooperation. Luxembourg maintains extensive cross-border transport, environmental and labor arrangements with neighboring states and participates in Benelux. Its UNESCO heritage inscription includes Luxembourg City’s historic quarters and fortifications; wider architectural heritage should not be mislabeled as separate World Heritage inscriptions. [6]

Luxembourg uses ISO codes LU and LUX, international dialing prefix +352 and country internet domain .lu.
Security and rights
Defense cooperation, human-rights courts and European judicial institutions have different legal mandates.
Multilateral taxation
International tax coordination affects a services economy and follows negotiated rules rather than unilateral global sovereignty.
EU institutional geography
Luxembourg shares principal EU institutional locations with Brussels, Strasbourg and Frankfurt; not every EU office is headquartered in Luxembourg.
Luxembourg legal terminology
A grand duchy is a sovereign state in this context; participation in Benelux or EU institutions does not eliminate that sovereignty.

UNESCO inscription count: 1 property on the State Party page; transnational serial properties are counted once for each participating state. [4]

MGU editorial distinction: Different treaties have different parties, dates and scope. Participating in European or multilateral institutions is not the same as holding identical membership across all organizations.

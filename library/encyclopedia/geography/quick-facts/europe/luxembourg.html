Country Overview

Luxembourg is a landlocked Western European grand duchy bordering Belgium, France and Germany. Established as a grand duchy at the Congress of Vienna in 1815 and territorially defined by the 1839 Treaty of London, it developed into a sovereign parliamentary constitutional monarchy and founding participant in postwar European integration. Luxembourg City is its capital and a major European financial and institutional center. Grand Duke Guillaume succeeded Henri on 3 October 2025. [10]

Historical context: The medieval County and later Duchy of Luxembourg passed through Burgundian, Habsburg, Spanish, Austrian and French rule. The Congress of Vienna created the Grand Duchy in 1815; the 1839 Treaty of London separated its western territory, now in Belgium, and the 1867 Treaty of London reinforced its international position. German occupation in both world wars strongly affected national identity. Luxembourg helped found Benelux and the European Communities. The revised constitution took effect in 2023; Guillaume became Grand Duke following Henri’s abdication in October 2025. [10]

People and Society

Luxembourgish (Lëtzebuergesch), recognized in 1984 and enshrined in the 2023 Constitution
Administrative and judicial language regime
French, German and Luxembourgish are used under the 1984 language law; French is generally the language of legislation
Other languages
Portuguese, English and languages of the country’s large multinational resident and commuting populations
Religious traditions
Historically Roman Catholic, with Protestant, Orthodox, Jewish, Muslim, other faith and nonreligious communities
Religious measurement
Survey estimates and voluntary responses differ from administrative registers; do not present historical affiliation as a verified 2025 total
Population structure
Large foreign-national share and substantial cross-border workforce; highly multilingual society

Demographic interpretation: The Luxembourgish national-language designation is not equivalent to saying that French and German are unrecognized. Language use is regulated by statute and multilingual administration. Religion is presented qualitatively, without asserting a 2025 census share. [9]

Country Overview

Switzerland is a landlocked, multilingual federal republic in Central Europe with the Alps, the Swiss Plateau and the Jura Mountains. Bern serves as the federal city and seat of federal institutions; Swiss constitutional arrangements do not formally designate a capital. Swiss governance is organized around 26 cantons, direct democracy and the collegial seven-member Federal Council. The country is not a member of the European Union or NATO, though it participates in Schengen and extensive European economic agreements. [9]

Historical context: The Old Swiss Confederacy emerged from alliances among Alpine communities in the late medieval period. Its cantons expanded and gradually developed distinct institutions amid conflicts with Habsburg powers and the Reformation. A French-backed Helvetic Republic was imposed in 1798, followed by renewed confederal arrangements. In 1848, after the Sonderbund conflict, a new federal constitution created the modern Swiss federal state. The country maintained armed neutrality during the world wars and developed a high-income economy with major finance, manufacturing, scientific and pharmaceutical sectors. Switzerland joined the United Nations in 2002 but is not an EU member. [12]

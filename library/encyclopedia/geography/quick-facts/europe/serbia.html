Country Overview

Serbia is a landlocked republic in southeastern Europe, centered on Belgrade and the Morava and Danube river systems. Its history includes medieval Serbian polities, centuries under Ottoman and Habsburg influence, the kingdoms of Serbia and Yugoslavia, and the socialist Yugoslav federation. After the breakup of the state union with Montenegro in 2006, Serbia continued as an independent republic. Kosovo declared independence in 2008, which Serbia does not recognize; the region has its own institutions and its status remains contested internationally. Serbia is an EU candidate, not an EU member. [12]

Historical context: Medieval Serbian principalities and the Nemanjić dynasty created significant political and religious institutions before Ottoman expansion. During the nineteenth century, Serbian autonomy grew from uprisings beginning in 1804 and 1815, and international recognition of independence followed in 1878. The country helped form Yugoslavia after World War I; in World War II its territory experienced occupation, resistance and atrocity. Socialist Yugoslavia emerged in 1945 and later dissolved amid wars and political upheaval in the 1990s. Serbia and Montenegro remained in a joint state until 2006. Kosovo’s 2008 declaration of independence is recognized by many states but not by Serbia or all UN members; UN Security Council resolution 1244 and later diplomacy form part of the complex international framework.

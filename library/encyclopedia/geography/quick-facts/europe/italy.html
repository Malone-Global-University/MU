Country Overview

Italy is a Southern European parliamentary republic occupying the Italian Peninsula, major Mediterranean islands and Alpine territory. The Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed in 1861 during unification, Rome became the capital in 1871, and Italians voted to establish a republic in 1946. The republican constitution entered into force in 1948. Italy is a founding member of European integration, belongs to the euro area and NATO, and maintains a diversified manufacturing and service economy. [6]

Historical context: The Italian Peninsula contained ancient city-states, Etruscan and Roman polities, and later diverse medieval kingdoms, communes and republics. The nineteenth-century Risorgimento culminated in the Kingdom of Italy in 1861; Rome was incorporated in 1870. Fascist dictatorship arose in the 1920s, and Italy participated in the Second World War. The 1946 referendum chose a republic, followed by the democratic Constitution of 1948. Italy became a founder of the European Economic Community in 1957–1958. [8]

People and Society

Official national language
Italian; the Republic protects historical linguistic minorities under its Constitution
Protected and regional languages
German and French in relevant autonomous regions; Slovene, Ladin, Sardinian, Friulian and other protected minority languages
Religious traditions
Roman Catholic Christianity has historic cultural prominence; Protestant, Orthodox, Jewish, Muslim, Buddhist, Hindu and nonreligious communities also exist
Religious measurement caution
Italy does not collect a comprehensive religion question in its ordinary population census; no unverified 2025 religious percentages are asserted
Demographic structure
Aging population and low birth rates; immigration and regional differences affect demographic trends
Culture and cities
Long traditions in literature, architecture, music, design and regional culinary heritage

Demographic interpretation: Religious tradition is described qualitatively because nominal Catholic affiliation, church records, and survey belief or attendance are not equivalent census measures. [8]

Government and Institutions

System
Unitary parliamentary republic with constitutional regional autonomy
Head of state
President of the Republic, elected by Parliament in joint session with regional delegates for a seven-year term
Head of government
President of the Council of Ministers (prime minister) leads cabinet and must have confidence of both parliamentary chambers
Legislature
Bicameral Parliament: Chamber of Deputies (400 elected seats) and Senate (200 elected seats, plus eligible senators for life)
Territorial organization
20 regions, 5 with special autonomous statutes; further provinces, metropolitan cities and municipalities
Constitution
Republican Constitution, effective 1 January 1948, as amended
Judicial institutions
Constitutional Court, Court of Cassation, Council of State and autonomous judicial institutions

Institutional descriptions refer to the legal and constitutional design; incumbents and cabinet composition may change between updates. [8]

Country Overview

North Macedonia is a landlocked Balkan republic in Southeastern Europe, bordering Kosovo, Serbia, Bulgaria, Greece and Albania. The country became independent from Yugoslavia in 1991 and adopted its present constitutional name in 2019 following the Prespa Agreement with Greece. Its population includes Macedonian, Albanian and other communities, and Skopje is its capital. It is a NATO member and an EU candidate. [7]

Historical context: The territory formed part of ancient and later Byzantine, Bulgarian, Serbian and Ottoman spheres. It became part of Serbia after the Balkan Wars and subsequently the Yugoslav state. As the Socialist Republic of Macedonia within Yugoslavia after 1944, it acquired republican institutions. Voters endorsed sovereign statehood in September 1991. The 2001 armed conflict was followed by the Ohrid Framework Agreement, which expanded protections for ethnic communities. The Prespa Agreement resolved the name dispute with Greece and enabled membership in NATO in 2020. [7]

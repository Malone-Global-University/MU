Country Overview

North Macedonia is a landlocked Balkan republic in Southeastern Europe, bordering Kosovo, Serbia, Bulgaria, Greece and Albania. The country became independent from Yugoslavia in 1991 and adopted its present constitutional name in 2019 following the Prespa Agreement with Greece. Its population includes Macedonian, Albanian and other communities, and Skopje is its capital. It is a NATO member and an EU candidate. [7]

The Republic of North Macedonia is a landlocked Balkan parliamentary republic at the meeting point of the Vardar valley, mountains and historical transport corridors. Its population is multilingual and multiethnic, including Macedonian, Albanian, Turkish, Roma and other communities. The modern state emerged from socialist Yugoslavia in 1991, while the name Republic of North Macedonia came into effect in 2019 following the Prespa Agreement with Greece. The Ohrid Framework Agreement in 2001 shaped institutional and linguistic rights following armed conflict. President Gordana Siljanovska-Davkova and Prime Minister Hristijan Mickoski headed the state and government as of November 6, 2026. North Macedonia joined NATO in 2020 but is not a European Union member. The European Commission’s November 4, 2026 report recorded continued reform efforts and outstanding constitutional changes required to advance accession negotiations.

Identity and State Formation

North Macedonia is not coextensive with all the historical region known as Macedonia, which extends across present-day state borders. Its territory lay within successive ancient Balkan, Roman, Byzantine, medieval Bulgarian and Serbian, and Ottoman political structures, but a direct uninterrupted national-state lineage would be misleading. After the Balkan Wars, most of the present state’s territory was incorporated into Serbia and later Yugoslavia. The post-1944 Socialist Republic of Macedonia became a constituent republic of federal Yugoslavia with modern Macedonian-language public institutions. Independence was endorsed in the 8 September 1991 referendum; peaceful separation did not prevent later internal conflict. The 2001 Ohrid Framework Agreement altered communal representation and language-rights structures. The Prespa Agreement with Greece was signed in 2018 and implemented through the name change in 2019, removing a major obstacle to joining NATO. None of these milestones changed the historical boundaries of ancient Macedon or eliminated all contemporary regional disagreements. [7]

Geography and Population

The Vardar river runs through the principal north–south corridor and connects Skopje to the Aegean drainage basin. Mountain ranges create distinct western, eastern and southern landscapes, while Lake Ohrid, Lake Prespa and Lake Dojran connect environmental policy with neighboring Albania and Greece. The Šar Mountains, Pelister and Mavrovo contain important forests, alpine habitats and mountain settlements. The country has no coastline, so road and rail access through Greece and other neighbors is strategically important for imported fuel, manufactured inputs and outward trade. Skopje dominates administrative and employment geography, but Tetovo, Gostivar, Bitola, Prilep, Ohrid, Kumanovo, Štip and other towns have distinct cultural and economic functions. Statistical agencies distinguish the 2021 usually resident census from citizens living abroad, and the country’s population decline is linked to migration as well as fertility and mortality. [21]

Economy and International Trade

North Macedonia’s economy combines foreign-invested industrial zones, automotive components, supported industrial catalysts, metal processing, garments, food production and a substantial domestic service sector. The country benefits from regional transportation routes and proximity to European markets, but relies on imported energy, machinery, materials and high-value industrial inputs. The 2025 preliminary external-trade release placed merchandise exports at €8.0082 billion and imports at €11.5454 billion, giving a goods deficit; this does not by itself equal the current-account balance. Supported precious-metal catalysts, wiring sets and seat parts rank among prominent product groups. Such exports can contain high-value imported materials, so their gross export value must not be equated with the domestic value-added created in Macedonia. EU member states accounted for 58.9% of combined two-way external trade in the release, which is not the same as the percentage of exports going to EU markets. Remittances, tourism, software and other services need separately sourced accounting. [19]

People and Society

The constitutional settlement incorporates multiple ethnolinguistic communities rather than a single homogeneous population. The 2021 resident census classified 58.44% as Macedonian and 24.30% as Albanian, as well as Turkish, Roma, Serbian, Bosniak, Vlach and other categories; a sizable number of residents was represented through administrative data. Macedonian is official throughout the country; Albanian also has substantial official usage, while municipal thresholds and legal provisions affect other languages. Orthodox Christianity and Islam are prominent, alongside Roman Catholic and other faiths. Ethnicity, declared mother tongue, language used in government, religious affiliation and citizenship are not synonymous measures. Urbanization, emigration and labor-market change affect household structures, public education and healthcare. Lake Ohrid’s heritage, Ottoman and Orthodox architecture, vernacular traditions and contemporary cultural production illustrate the historical diversity of the region. [20]

Government and Institutions

The 1991 constitution established a parliamentary republic with a directly elected president, government dependent on the unicameral Assembly, an independent judiciary and institutions for constitutional review. President Gordana Siljanovska-Davkova began her term in 2024, while Hristijan Mickoski became prime minister in June 2024. Their offices have separate mandates and are not interchangeable; the president does not personally head the cabinet. The 2001 Ohrid settlement influenced decentralization, equitable representation and use of languages. The Assembly generally has 120 members, and local governance is exercised through 80 municipalities plus the institutional framework of the City of Skopje. Courts, anti-corruption agencies, regulators and parliamentary commissions have different statutory jurisdictions. Reform debates involve judicial accountability, administrative capacity and minority protections as well as ordinary economic policy, and official EU assessments must be dated rather than simplified into a single accession grade. International Profile

North Macedonia joined the United Nations in 1993 and NATO in March 2020. Relations with Greece are shaped in part by the Prespa Agreement, while relations with Bulgaria include the 2017 friendship treaty and contentious historical and constitutional issues. The EU conferred candidate status in 2005 and began an accession process, but candidate status is not membership and screening is not equivalent to opening and closing negotiating chapters. The European Commission’s 2026 report identified remaining constitutional amendments as a central prerequisite for advancing the fundamentals cluster. Regional trade arrangements, NATO security commitments, mobility rules and UNESCO World Heritage inscription each involve different legal instruments. The transboundary Lake Ohrid area demonstrates that cultural and ecological preservation can depend upon both Albanian and Macedonian institutions. Diaspora networks and Western Balkan infrastructure corridors also connect the republic to a broader European economic space. [17]

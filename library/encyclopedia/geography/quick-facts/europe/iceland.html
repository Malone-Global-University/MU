Country Overview

Iceland is an island republic in the North Atlantic at the intersection of tectonic plates, with a volcanic landscape, extensive glaciers, and a population concentrated around Reykjavík. The Alþingi traces its origins to a national assembly founded around 930. The country became a sovereign kingdom in personal union with Denmark in 1918, then an independent republic on 17 June 1944. Iceland is a NATO and European Economic Area member but is not a European Union or euro-area member. [11]

Historical context: Icelandic settlement commenced in the late ninth century, and the Alþingi assembly began around 930. The Icelandic Commonwealth ended with submission to Norwegian royal authority in the thirteenth century; later Danish rule followed. A home-rule process preceded the 1918 sovereignty settlement. In 1944, Iceland established the republic while Denmark was occupied during the Second World War. [11]

Unitary parliamentary republic with a directly elected president

Unicameral Alþingi, 63 members
Local government
Municipalities constitute the main elected local-government level; administrative boundaries can be revised
Constitution
1944 Constitution, with subsequent amendments

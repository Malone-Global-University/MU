Country Overview

Portugal is an Atlantic-facing parliamentary democracy with a semi-presidential constitutional system, occupying the western Iberian Peninsula and the autonomous Atlantic archipelagos of the Azores and Madeira. Lisbon is the capital. Portugal developed from a medieval kingdom into an extensive maritime empire; the 1974 Carnation Revolution ended authoritarian rule and led to the democratic constitution of 1976. The country belongs to the European Union, euro area, NATO and Schengen area. [6]

Historical context: Portugal emerged as a kingdom during the medieval Christian reconquests of Iberia and established a durable frontier with Castile. During the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries, Portuguese oceanic voyages and imperial expansion connected Atlantic, African, Asian and South American societies through commerce, forced labour and colonial rule. A republic replaced the monarchy in 1910, but the Estado Novo dictatorship dominated much of the twentieth century. The 1974 Carnation Revolution initiated democratization and accelerated African decolonization. Portugal entered the European Economic Community in 1986 and adopted euro accounting in 1999 and cash in 2002. [6]

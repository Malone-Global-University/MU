Country Overview

Denmark is a Nordic parliamentary constitutional monarchy in Northern Europe. Denmark proper consists of the Jutland peninsula and numerous islands, while the Kingdom of Denmark also comprises the self-governing Faroe Islands and Greenland. The country is a founding member of NATO, joined the European Communities in 1973, and retains the Danish krone through an opt-out from adopting the euro. The area, population and World Bank indicators below refer to Denmark proper unless expressly noted. [6]

Identity and State Formation

Official and common names
Kingdom of Denmark (Kongeriget Danmark); Denmark (Danmark)
Capital
Copenhagen (København)
UN M49 classification
Europe / Northern Europe [3]
Historic formation
Continuous Danish monarchy and medieval state traditions; not a modern independence state with a single founding date
Constitutional milestone
5 June 1849: first democratic constitutional framework replaced absolute monarchy
Current constitutional framework
Constitution of 5 June 1953, providing the basis of the modern parliamentary monarchy
Constitution Day
5 June (Grundlovsdag); widely treated as a national commemoration, although Denmark has no separately designated official national day
Territorial distinction
Kingdom includes Denmark proper, Greenland, and the Faroe Islands; the two North Atlantic territories exercise extensive self-government
ISO 3166-1 identifiers
DK (alpha-2), DNK (alpha-3), 208 (numeric)
Country-code top-level domain
.dk

Historical context: Danish kingship developed during the Viking Age and medieval period. Denmark entered a long union with Norway and other Nordic territories, while the constitutional transition of 1849 ended absolutist monarchy. The modern constitutional order dates to 1953. Greenland and the Faroe Islands are part of the kingdom with distinct constitutional and administrative arrangements; Greenland and the Faroes are outside the EU. [9]

0.5% (2025, World Bank) [1]
Geographic setting
Jutland borders Germany; Zealand, Funen and other islands occupy the Danish straits between the North and Baltic seas
Autonomous territories
Greenland and the Faroe Islands are self-governing parts of the wider kingdom but are excluded from the headline Denmark-proper area and population figures
Physical geography
Low relief, agricultural plains, sandy coasts, islands, fjords and shallow marine passages
Major urban centers
Copenhagen, Aarhus, Odense, Aalborg, and Esbjerg

Data interpretation: A distinction is essential: Denmark proper covers the mainland-and-island European country, whereas the Kingdom of Denmark also encompasses Greenland and the Faroe Islands. World Bank and Eurostat Denmark summary figures should not be read as the entire kingdom’s landmass or population. [1]

People and Society

Principal official language
Danish (in Denmark proper); Faroese and Greenlandic have distinct legal and official roles in the self-governing territories
Other languages
German minority language in southern Jutland; English is widely used; immigrant and heritage languages are also spoken
Established church
Evangelical Lutheran Church of Denmark (Folkekirken), historically the state-supported national church
Church membership, third quarter 2026
4,210,480 registered members of the national church (Statistics Denmark; administrative membership, not a survey of belief or attendance)
Religious communities
Besides Lutheran Christianity, other Christian traditions, Islam, Judaism, Hinduism, Buddhism and nonreligious populations are present
Demographic context
Highly urbanized population, universal public welfare services, and Nordic cultural institutions

Demographic interpretation: Administrative Folkekirken membership represents registered members among Denmark residents, not all faith or non-faith identifications. Religion in Greenland and the Faroe Islands should not be inferred from Denmark-proper figures. [8]

Government and Institutions

System
Unitary parliamentary constitutional monarchy with autonomous North Atlantic territories
Head of state
King Frederik X (reigned since 14 January 2024); constitutional and ceremonial role
Head of government
Prime minister, leading a cabinet that must retain parliamentary confidence
Legislature
Unicameral Folketing (179 seats): 175 elected in Denmark proper and two each from Greenland and the Faroe Islands
Denmark proper administration
Municipal and regional local-government structure; arrangements for Greenland and the Faroe Islands are separate
Constitution
Constitution of the Kingdom of Denmark, 5 June 1953
Legal system
Independent judiciary; Supreme Court at the top of the ordinary court structure

Institutional descriptions refer to the legal and constitutional design; incumbents and cabinet composition may change between updates. [10]

Country Overview

Moldova is a landlocked Eastern European republic between Romania and Ukraine. Its modern state emerged from the dissolution of the Soviet Union and declared independence on 27 August 1991. The Republic of Moldova claims the territory east of the Dniester that is controlled by the unrecognized Transnistrian administration; statistical coverage and effective political control must be distinguished. Chișinău is the capital. [7]

The Republic of Moldova stands between the Romanian and Ukrainian historical and economic spaces. It is an independent Eastern European state with recognized borders that include the Transnistrian region, even though the central government does not exercise effective authority over most of that territory. The difference between sovereignty, effective control and census coverage is fundamental to the country's statistical profile. Moldova's history includes the medieval Principality of Moldavia, Russian imperial Bessarabia, twentieth-century Romanian and Soviet periods, and independence in 1991. Contemporary Moldova's economy combines agriculture and food processing, electrical-component manufacturing, information services, diaspora connections and European commerce. President Maia Sandu and a government led by Vasile Tofan pursued European Union accession reforms in 2026; this candidate and negotiating status does not mean that Moldova is already an EU member.

Identity and State Formation

Moldova's contemporary borders do not reproduce those of the medieval Principality of Moldavia. The eastern lands between the Prut and Dniester were annexed to the Russian Empire in 1812; the territory later known as Bessarabia followed an uneven trajectory through the First World War, association with Romania, and the Soviet territorial reorganization of 1940. The Moldavian Soviet Socialist Republic combined different geographic areas with earlier histories. Independence in 1991 followed the Soviet dissolution, but an armed conflict in 1992 left a long-term de facto territorial division. The 1994 Constitution, the legal recognition of Romanian as the state language, and the EU-integration process are different constitutional and political developments. Cultural and linguistic identity should not be conflated with citizenship, electoral choice or external recognition. [11]

Historical context: The territory of the Republic of Moldova has medieval Moldavian, Ottoman, Russian imperial, Romanian and Soviet histories. The Moldavian Soviet Socialist Republic emerged in 1940 under Soviet authority. Following a declaration of sovereignty in 1990, Moldova declared independence on 27 August 1991. The 1992 Transnistria conflict left the government without effective control over most territory east of the Dniester. A 1994 constitution governs the contemporary republic. Moldova became an EU candidate in 2022 and formal accession negotiations began in 2024. [11]

Geography and Population

Moldova's interior landscape consists largely of low hills, river valleys, fertile soils and cultivated land, including the Codri forest belt and agricultural areas oriented toward the Prut and Dniester. The state's internationally recognized land boundaries adjoin Romania and Ukraine; it has no sovereign seacoast. The Dniester/Nistru has been a transportation and economic corridor as well as a line near which political control differs. The administrative geography of autonomous Gagauzia is distinct from the Transnistrian de facto administration, and Bender/Tighina has its own practical control issues. Moldova's population estimates depend heavily on emigration, births, deaths and definitions of usual residence. A person registered locally but living abroad is not always counted in the same way across census, domestic registry and World Bank series. [7]

Economy and International Trade

Moldova's open economy is dependent on farming conditions, European markets, energy imports, remittances and cross-border transport. Fruit, grains, oilseeds, vegetable oils and wine coexist with automotive wiring, apparel, furniture and information services. National merchandise exports increased in 2025, but domestic-origin exports and re-exports were separate components of the US$3.7827 billion total; imported goods forwarded abroad are not evidence of an equal value of Moldovan factory production. Romania and other European markets are major destinations. Moldova Invest separately reported service exports of roughly US$3.216 billion in 2025; this is not an item within the merchandise-export total. Infrastructure investment and electricity interconnection to Romania are economically consequential in the context of energy-security risks and disrupted eastern routes. [18]

People and Society

Moldova has a multilingual and historically diverse population. The official language is Romanian, while people also identify with Moldovan, Romanian, Ukrainian, Russian, Gagauz, Bulgarian, Roma and other communities. Respondents to the 2024 census could choose their ethnic identity and language descriptions; these voluntary declarations should not be equated with the government's recognition of a state language. Orthodox Christianity is widely identified in the census, but religious self-description does not measure attendance. The population has been affected by decades of outward migration, remittances and family networks linking residents with Romania, other EU states and additional destinations. Rural ageing, urban concentration in Chișinău, schooling, health services and differences in minority-language provision are important social-policy questions. [19]

Government and Institutions

The Constitution provides a directly elected president, a unicameral Parliament and a government answerable through constitutional parliamentary procedures. Maia Sandu was president at the November 2026 editorial cutoff. Prime Minister Vasile Tofan's cabinet took office in July 2026, after the departure of Alexandru Munteanu and a short interim under Eugeniu Osmochescu; government identity is therefore specifically dated. The president represents the state and exercises defined constitutional functions, while the cabinet implements policy and manages departments. Gagauzia is an autonomous territorial unit under Moldova's constitutional order. No assertion about the formal legal system should be mistaken for proof that the Chișinău authorities administer all of Transnistria. Courts, audit institutions, elections and independent regulatory agencies have distinct duties. [15]

International Profile

Moldova joined the United Nations in 1992 and maintains constitutional neutrality, which does not preclude security cooperation or foreign-policy debate. Its EU application resulted in candidate status in 2022 and formal accession negotiations in 2024. The October 2026 accession conference advanced negotiations on additional clusters, but the completion of negotiations and formal EU accession were still future contingencies. Relations with Romania are unusually significant for electricity infrastructure, language, transport, and individual mobility; relations with Ukraine involve border commerce, displaced populations and regional security. The OSCE has been engaged on the Transnistrian conflict, whereas a final political settlement remains unresolved. World Heritage status, trade agreements, border controls and diplomatic recognition have distinct legal meanings. [17]

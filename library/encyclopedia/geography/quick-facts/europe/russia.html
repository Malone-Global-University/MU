Country Overview

The Russian Federation is a transcontinental state extending across Eastern Europe and northern Asia, with Moscow as its capital and most of its population living west of the Urals. It emerged as the internationally recognized continuation of the Soviet Union’s UN membership following the USSR’s dissolution in December 1991. Russia is governed as a federal republic under a highly centralized political system. Its military invasion of Ukraine began on a large scale in February 2022, following the unrecognized annexation of Crimea in 2014; Moscow’s further territorial annexation claims are not recognized by the United Nations General Assembly. [12]

The Russian Federation is the world's largest country by conventional geographical area, stretching from European Russia across Siberia and the Far East to the Pacific. Moscow is its capital and the center of a highly concentrated presidential political system. The federation succeeded the Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic after the dissolution of the USSR in 1991 and continued the Soviet Union's seat in the United Nations, including the Security Council. Russia contains diverse peoples, languages and religious traditions, substantial natural resources and widely varying regional settlement patterns. Its full-scale military invasion of Ukraine began on February 24, 2022 after the occupation and attempted annexation of Crimea in 2014. Russia claims additional Ukrainian territory, but those annexations are not recognized by the UN General Assembly; this profile uses an internationally recognized territorial baseline and distinguishes domestic Russian enumeration from international statistics. In November 2026 Vladimir Putin remained president and Mikhail Mishustin prime minister. Energy production, sanctions, wartime fiscal priorities, altered trade routes and restrictions on civil society all materially affect the contemporary country profile.

Identity and State Formation

Russia's historical trajectory involves East Slavic Rus' polities, shifting centers of power, the rise of Moscow, the Tsardom proclaimed by Ivan IV, an imperial state from 1721, revolution in 1917 and Soviet rule from 1922. Medieval Rus' history belongs to the broader histories of today's Ukraine, Belarus and Russia, not to an exclusive modern national lineage. Imperial expansion and settlement brought various Siberian, Caucasian and Central Asian peoples within a state governed with unequal legal structures. The October Revolution and civil war preceded nationalization, collectivization, famine, mass repression and industrial transformation. During the Second World War the Soviet Union sustained catastrophic losses and helped defeat Nazi Germany; after 1945 it played a leading part in the bipolar international order. Soviet dissolution in 1991 brought new states, a Russian constitutional crisis in 1993, market reforms and later growing executive concentration. The 2014 seizure of Crimea and the 2022 invasion of Ukraine triggered extensive international sanctions and disputes. Political history must keep the continuity of institutions separate from claims of exclusive sovereignty over regions that international law recognizes as Ukrainian. [16]

Historical context: The political history of lands now in Russia encompasses Rus’ polities, regional principalities, the Muscovite state, the Russian Tsardom and the imperial state that grew under the Romanov dynasty. The revolutions of 1917 ended the monarchy and led to Bolshevik rule and the creation of the USSR in 1922. The Soviet period included Stalinist repression, industrialization and victory over Nazi Germany at enormous human cost; the USSR emerged as a superpower. After the USSR collapsed in 1991, Russia underwent difficult market reforms and constitutional crisis, followed by increasing presidential centralization under Vladimir Putin. Russia seized Crimea in 2014 and began its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, violating Ukraine’s sovereignty and leading to widespread sanctions and international condemnation. [9]

Geography and Population

Russia spans the East European Plain, Urals, Western Siberian Lowland, Central Siberian Plateau, mountains of southern Siberia, Kamchatka and the Pacific coast. The Volga and its reservoir system connect industrial cities to southern inland waterways; the Ob, Yenisei and Lena drain vast river basins into the Arctic. Lake Baikal, the Caucasus, boreal forests and tundra illustrate extraordinary ecological variety. Northern settlements face permafrost thaw, high energy and transport costs and major seasonal differences in access. Economic and demographic concentration is strongest west of the Urals, while Novosibirsk, Krasnoyarsk, Yekaterinburg, Vladivostok and other large cities organize regional networks. The sea frontages extend across Arctic, Pacific, Baltic, Black and Caspian settings; not all of these represent the same oceanographic or legal category. Russia's conventional total area is approximately 17.1 million km², but national accounting can include disputed Ukrainian territory. Climate warming, wildfires, forest management and permafrost degradation are material environmental concerns. Time zones, federal subjects and transport corridors require geographic context instead of a single average-statistic description. [1]

Economy and International Trade

The Russian economy contains major oil and natural-gas industries, metalworking, minerals, chemicals, machinery, grain production, transport and service sectors. Hydrocarbons form an important source of export revenue and public income, but their relative weight changes with prices, restrictions and measurement basis. The Federal Customs Service reported 2025 goods exports of US$418.3 billion, imports of US$279.0 billion and a goods surplus of US$139.3 billion. Asian countries accounted for approximately 73.4% of the service's total foreign merchandise trade turnover, reflecting a marked reorientation after 2022. Sanctions, export controls, payment restrictions, alternative transport routes and missing detail complicate independent evaluation; a customs figure should not be presented as independently audited coverage of all trade. The World Bank's US$2.56 trillion 2025 nominal GDP is in current dollars and affected by exchange-rate conversion, whereas real GDP growth measures volume change. Defense procurement and mobilization have reshaped production and budget priorities. The ruble and domestic inflation influence purchasing power, but national GDP per person is not a median salary. Agricultural exports, including grain and fertilizers, must be distinguished from military and strategic-industrial activities. [15]

People and Society

Russia's multiethnic society includes a Russian-speaking majority alongside numerous peoples of the Volga, North Caucasus, Siberia and Far East. Several republics have protected state languages in addition to Russian, though the vitality and everyday use of minority languages differ widely. Religious life includes Russian Orthodoxy, Sunni and other Muslim communities, Buddhism, Judaism, Protestant groups, new religious movements and nonreligion. Independent survey reports are not an official census of faith; the frequently cited Levada responses from 2023 measure self-identification and differ from institutional church or mosque membership. Universities and research centers coexist with evolving state control over curricula, media and public discussion. The country exhibits pronounced geographic disparities in health access, earnings and life expectancy, and some areas are sparsely settled. The effects of the war include casualty uncertainty, military recruitment, migration and restrictions on dissent. Minority identity and political loyalty are separate analytical categories, and the use of official language in administration should not obscure local language histories. Cultural achievement spans literature, music, visual art, cinema, intellectual traditions and cultural production by non-Russian communities. [19]

Government and Institutions

Russia's 1993 Constitution, extensively amended including in 2020, formally specifies a president, government, bicameral legislature and court system within a federation. In political practice the presidency exerts highly centralized control over security, appointments, the executive and many other institutions. Vladimir Putin won the 2024 presidential election under conditions criticized by external observers and remained president in November 2026; Mikhail Mishustin served as chairman of government. The 450-seat State Duma and Federation Council form the Federal Assembly, but formal institutional descriptions alone do not establish the extent of effective opposition or judicial independence. Laws regulating media, political organizations and public expression constrain civil society and independent journalism. The legal administrative classification of federal subjects includes internationally disputed Ukrainian regions, which this encyclopedia describes as Russian claims rather than recognized annexations. Federal republics, krais, oblasts and autonomous jurisdictions have different legal histories and competencies. Regional governments manage public services under significant central fiscal and political oversight. Foreign assessments of human rights and election conditions are sourced and attributed; they are not replaced by unqualified labels or assumptions about individual residents. [20]

International Profile

The Russian Federation has held the USSR's permanent UN Security Council seat since 1991 and possesses extensive military and nuclear capabilities. It is a member of international organizations such as the WTO and OSCE, while participation in other European institutions has changed after 2022. Russia's 2014 occupation and claimed annexation of Crimea was rejected by UN General Assembly resolution 68/262; the Assembly also condemned the attempted 2022 annexation of four Ukrainian regions in ES-11/4. These resolutions and international legal positions must be distinguished from Russia's internal laws and statements. Sanctions and economic countermeasures have affected financial institutions, technology transfer, transport, energy and international collaboration. Relations with China, India, Türkiye and other markets have increased in relative importance for merchandise turnover, but trade partner relationships are not uniform security alliances. Arctic navigation, environmental management, cross-border rivers and the conservation of UNESCO sites remain international issues. Russia has 33 World Heritage properties on UNESCO's current state-party page, among them cultural and natural sites of different dates and regions. Contemporary foreign relations should not be described as a settled postwar order while the invasion of Ukraine continues. [17]

Country Overview

The Russian Federation is a transcontinental state extending across Eastern Europe and northern Asia, with Moscow as its capital and most of its population living west of the Urals. It emerged as the internationally recognized continuation of the Soviet Union’s UN membership following the USSR’s dissolution in December 1991. Russia is governed as a federal republic under a highly centralized political system. Its military invasion of Ukraine began on a large scale in February 2022, following the unrecognized annexation of Crimea in 2014; Moscow’s further territorial annexation claims are not recognized by the United Nations General Assembly. Historical context: The political history of lands now in Russia encompasses Rus’ polities, regional principalities, the Muscovite state, the Russian Tsardom and the imperial state that grew under the Romanov dynasty. The revolutions of 1917 ended the monarchy and led to Bolshevik rule and the creation of the USSR in 1922. The Soviet period included Stalinist repression, industrialization and victory over Nazi Germany at enormous human cost; the USSR emerged as a superpower. After the USSR collapsed in 1991, Russia underwent difficult market reforms and constitutional crisis, followed by increasing presidential centralization under Vladimir Putin. Russia seized Crimea in 2014 and began its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, violating Ukraine’s sovereignty and leading to widespread sanctions and international condemnation. [9]

People and Society

Federal state language
Russian; additional co-official regional languages in some constituent republics
Other languages
Tatar, Bashkir, Chechen, Chuvash, Yakut (Sakha), Buryat, Avar, regional minority languages and languages of migration
Religious landscape
Russian Orthodox Christianity is the largest self-identified tradition; Islam is prominent in North Caucasus and Volga regions; Buddhism, Judaism and other religions are present
2023 identification survey
An independent Levada Center poll cited by the U.S. State Department reported about 72% Orthodox Christian, 7% Muslim and 18% with no religious faith
Religious methodology
Survey categories are not mutually identical to institutional affiliation, church attendance or legally registered religious communities
Population characteristics
More densely settled European part, extensive sparsely settled Siberian and Arctic regions; regional and ethnic diversity
Demographic challenges
Low fertility, aging and migration alongside deaths, displacement and administrative disruptions associated with the war

Demographic interpretation: Russia’s population census is not a comprehensive census of religion; the religious percentages are from a 2023 opinion poll quoted in the U.S. State Department report. They are indicative survey measures, not audited census shares, and national statistics are affected by regional coverage questions. [8]

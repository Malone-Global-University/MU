Country Overview

Malta is an island republic in the central Mediterranean Sea, south of Sicily. It comprises the inhabited islands of Malta, Gozo and Comino and smaller islets. After long periods of Mediterranean and European rule, Malta gained independence from the United Kingdom in 1964 and became a republic in 1974. The capital, Valletta, is a historic fortified port. Malta is an EU member and uses the euro. [6]

Malta is an exceptionally densely settled island republic in the central Mediterranean, between southern Europe and North Africa. The islands’ natural harbors and location have shaped long histories of maritime commerce, military strategy, migration and cultural exchange. Prehistoric temple complexes precede Phoenician, Roman, medieval and early-modern political periods. British colonial government gave way to independence in 1964, republican status in 1974, and a subsequent European integration process culminating in EU membership in 2004 and the adoption of the euro in 2008. The contemporary economy includes travel services, financial and professional activities, manufacturing, maritime logistics and digital services. High immigration, demand for water and energy, and development pressure create distinctive public-policy challenges for a small archipelago.

Identity and State Formation

Malta’s historical chronology connects an island archipelago to several Mediterranean civilizations without implying an unbroken state administration. Megalithic temples attest to an advanced prehistoric building tradition; Phoenician and Roman periods brought different commercial and political systems. Arab-era influence profoundly affected the Maltese language and toponyms. The Knights of St John governed the islands from 1530 until the French invasion in 1798, and later British rule shaped defense and administration. Malta attained independence in 1964 while retaining a constitutional monarch as head of state, and it became a republic in 1974. The 1979 withdrawal of remaining British military forces is commemorated as Freedom Day. European Union accession in 2004 and euro adoption in 2008 are subsequent international institutional changes. Geography and Population

The archipelago is situated south of Sicily and includes the main islands of Malta, Gozo and Comino and smaller islands and islets. Limestone geology creates karst landscapes, cliffs, harbors and relatively limited permanent surface freshwater. Most settlements, workplaces and population are concentrated on the principal island, while Gozo retains a distinct regional character. The extensive coastline creates maritime opportunities alongside high exposure to coastal development pressures. Climate is Mediterranean, with hot dry summers and milder wetter winters. Population growth has been driven largely by net migration, and official annual resident counts are not the same as tourism arrivals or short-term workers. High density makes land use, transport, water supply, waste, marine protection and heat management especially consequential. [18]

Economy and International Trade

Malta is a small open economy whose prominent service exports include international travel, digital services and business activities. Goods exports reflect a different mix: mineral fuels, chemicals, machinery, transport equipment and specialized manufacturing; some trade involves re-exports and high-value goods passing through internationally connected hubs. The National Statistics Office reported €4.886 billion in provisional merchandise exports and €9.207 billion in imports for 2025, leaving a €4.321 billion goods deficit. Excluding fuels and specified aircraft and vessel chapters produces a distinct adjusted series, not an additional set of exports. Tourist spending, freight services, software and other digital activities are largely recorded in services or other national-account categories. The country’s currency is the euro, and public finance, construction and housing are shaped by EU membership and rapid population growth. [19]

People and Society

Maltese is a Semitic language written in the Latin alphabet, with strong historical contact with Sicilian and Italian, while English is also an official language. Italian is widely understood, and recent migration has increased the range of languages heard in workplaces and neighborhoods. Malta’s Catholic heritage is evident in churches, festivals and public culture, but religious census identification does not measure attendance. In the 2021 census, 82.6% of residents aged fifteen and over identified as Roman Catholic; the age denominator and survey date must be preserved. Migration-led growth has increased the noncitizen share and changed the labor force. The country faces challenges involving housing affordability, health, education, transport, inclusion and environmental protection across very densely populated urban areas. [10]

Government and Institutions

Malta is a parliamentary republic with a president as head of state, a prime minister leading the cabinet and a unicameral elected House of Representatives. Members are chosen through the single transferable vote in multi-member constituencies; constitutional provisions can add seats to correct disproportional results and gender representation. Thus the number of members actually sitting after an election can exceed the statutory base of sixty-five. The 30 May 2026 election resulted in a Labour victory and a 79-member chamber after adjustments, with 42 Labour and 37 Nationalist members. Robert Abela took his new oath on 1 June 2026; Myriam Spiteri Debono remained president. A judiciary, independent oversight bodies and 68 local councils operate under constitutional and statutory rules. European Union law applies in relevant areas. [14]

International Profile

Malta’s external relationships combine EU membership, a euro-area monetary regime, the Schengen border system and a constitutional policy of neutrality. The country is not a NATO member, although it participates in selected security and diplomatic forums and Mediterranean cooperation. Its position between Europe and North Africa informs migration, maritime search-and-rescue policy, trade and energy relationships. Malta is a member of the United Nations, Commonwealth, Council of Europe and OSCE, and participates in EU common institutions while retaining national responsibilities. Its maritime jurisdiction is much larger than its landmass, but exclusive economic zones, territorial waters and dry-land area are different measures. Valletta, the Ħal Saflieni Hypogeum and the Megalithic Temples are distinct UNESCO World Heritage inscriptions. [6]

Methodology: Population is the World Bank 2025 country series; GDP and GDP per capita are in current U.S. dollars and real GDP growth is a volume change, not an exchange-rate change.

Country Overview

Poland is a republic in Central Europe, classified in Eastern Europe for UN M49 statistical purposes. It lies between Germany, Czechia, Slovakia, Ukraine, Belarus, Lithuania and the Baltic coast, with Warsaw as its capital. Reconstituted as an independent state in 1918, it underwent Nazi German and Soviet occupation during the Second World War and communist rule before the democratic transition of 1989. It belongs to the EU and NATO while retaining the złoty as its currency. [7]

Historical context: The Piast dynasty established an early Polish polity, and the Polish–Lithuanian union became a major early modern commonwealth. Three late-eighteenth-century partitions removed the state from Europe’s political map. Poland regained sovereignty in 1918, fought Soviet Russia in 1919–1921 and was invaded by Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union in September 1939. Its postwar borders shifted westward; a Soviet-aligned communist government ruled until political negotiation and elections accelerated the transformation in 1989. Poland joined NATO in 1999 and the European Union in 2004. [11]

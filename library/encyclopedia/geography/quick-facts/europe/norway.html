Country Overview

Norway is a Nordic constitutional monarchy extending along the northern and western part of the Scandinavian Peninsula, with a long Atlantic coastline, deep fjords and Arctic territories. Oslo is its capital. The country’s modern independence from Sweden dates to 1905; its economy combines hydrocarbon exports and maritime industries with hydropower and an extensive welfare state. Norway is a NATO member but not an EU member; it participates in the European Economic Area. [7]

Norway is a Nordic constitutional monarchy with a highly varied mainland, island and Arctic geography. Oslo is the capital, while the Atlantic fjords, offshore continental shelf, northern communities and Svalbard require separate geographic and legal descriptions. The modern constitutional system dates to Eidsvoll in 1814, and full independence from Sweden followed in 1905; the country also has older medieval state traditions. Norway combines offshore petroleum and gas exports, maritime and seafood industries, hydropower, sophisticated public services, and a major sovereign wealth fund. King Harald V is head of state, and Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre leads a Labour government, which continued after the September 2025 election. Norway is a founding NATO member and participates in the European Economic Area and Schengen, although it has voted against full European Communities and European Union membership.

Identity and State Formation

Norway’s political development stretches from local chieftaincies and the consolidation of kingship during the Viking Age through Christianization, medieval monarchy, dynastic unions and constitutional reform. The Black Death and later union arrangements changed the kingdom’s demographic and political institutions. Denmark’s long dominance ended amid the Napoleonic settlement, and the Norwegian constitution adopted at Eidsvoll on 17 May 1814 retained significance even through the subsequent union with Sweden. The union dissolved in 1905, followed by a plebiscite and a new monarchy under Haakon VII. During the Second World War Germany occupied Norway, while the king and government operated in exile and resistance movements persisted at home. After 1945 the welfare state expanded, Norway joined NATO and the North Sea petroleum economy transformed public finance. Constitutional continuity does not mean Norwegian sovereignty was unchanged across every historical century. [12]

Geography and Population

Norway’s western and northern coast is characterized by deeply cut fjords, islands, high precipitation and exposure to the Atlantic, whereas eastern inland valleys, boreal forests and mountain plateaus support different patterns of farming and settlement. The Scandinavian Mountains divide major drainage basins and complicate transport across much of the country. Northern Norway includes Sámi cultural landscapes, and the Barents Sea connects Arctic resource questions with fisheries, shipping and security. Statistics Norway’s 2026 area of the Kingdom, 384,483 square kilometers, includes mainland Norway, Svalbard and Jan Mayen. Mainland Norway alone was 323,807 square kilometers, with Svalbard adding 60,299 and Jan Mayen 377. The Svalbard Treaty creates a specific sovereign and access regime, and the archipelago has separate administrative arrangements. Population statistics, domestic GDP, territorial climate risks and international border regulations therefore require explicit scope statements. [6]

Economy and International Trade

Norway is a high-income open economy whose hydrocarbon production gives it an unusually large commodity-export sector and significant exposure to global oil and gas prices. It also supports major aquaculture, wild fisheries, aluminum smelting, shipping, engineering and renewable-electricity industries. Crude oil and natural gas represented approximately 57% of the NOK 1,775 billion of 2025 merchandise exports, even though their export volumes and values fluctuate for different reasons. The Government Pension Fund Global saves and invests petroleum-related state proceeds abroad under a fiscal framework rather than treating the fund’s entire market value as annual budget revenue. It ended 2025 at NOK 21,268 billion; the yearly market return is not equivalent to domestic GDP growth. Statistics Norway publishes separate total and mainland national-account measures, which help distinguish offshore production from mainland activity. Services such as maritime freight, insurance and information technology are not included in the goods-export table. [18]

People and Society

Norwegian language policy recognizes two written standards, Bokmål and Nynorsk, while Sámi peoples are Indigenous to northern parts of Norway and neighboring states. The Sámi Parliament participates in representation of Sámi languages and interests but is not a territorial national legislature with the Storting’s constitutional powers. The cultural landscape also includes Kven, Romani, Romanes, immigrant languages and longstanding coastal and inland communities. Statistics Norway recorded Church of Norway membership at 60.9% of residents in 2025; this is a registration measure, not an all-religion census or an estimate of weekly attendance. Access to education, health services and transport differs between major cities and rural or Arctic settlements. Norway’s welfare institutions coexist with demographic ageing, immigration, housing pressures and debates about resource extraction, Indigenous consent and environmental management. Urban cultural life and dispersed northern settlement reflect distinct service-delivery conditions. [20]

Government and Institutions

Norway operates under the constitution adopted in 1814 and a parliamentary convention established in the nineteenth century. The king remains head of state, while ministers exercise executive policy authority and must be able to govern without losing the Storting’s confidence. The unicameral Storting has 169 elected members and is responsible for legislation, budget appropriations and scrutiny of the executive. Jonas Gahr Støre’s Labour government was appointed in 2021, became a Labour-only cabinet after the Centre Party left in February 2025, and continued following the September 2025 election. Its cabinet composition changed again in September 2026 without changing the identity of the prime minister. Norway has 15 counties following administrative reorganization and numerous municipalities; their powers differ from those of the national government. Svalbard’s Governor and the Sámi Parliament operate under specific legal frameworks, while courts remain institutionally independent. [14]

International Profile

Norway is a founding member of NATO and the United Nations and participates in Nordic, Arctic and multilateral diplomacy. Its relationship with the European Union is substantial but qualified: the European Economic Area provides internal-market participation through EFTA, while Schengen concerns border and travel arrangements. Norway is not an EU member and does not participate in the euro area; its currency is the krone. The 1972 and 1994 referendums rejected membership under the terms then proposed. Arctic diplomacy, relations with Russia, NATO commitments, energy exports to Europe and maritime law create different international responsibilities. The Svalbard Treaty recognizes Norwegian sovereignty over the archipelago while establishing specified equal-treatment provisions; it is not a declaration of international ownership. UNESCO heritage listings, scientific cooperation and international fisheries management illustrate additional sector-specific agreements. Norway’s international development assistance is separate from the sovereign wealth fund’s investment mandate. [22]

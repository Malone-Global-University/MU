Holy See (Vatican City)

Quick Facts — Contemporary Country Profile • Europe

Country Overview

The Holy See is the supreme governing authority of the Catholic Church and an internationally recognized sovereign legal person; Vatican City State is the separate territorial state created by the 1929 Lateran Treaty to guarantee the Holy See's independence. The Pope leads both, but they are not interchangeable geographic, statistical or diplomatic entities. This MGU entry covers the Holy See as the internationally recognized entity while identifying territorial facts expressly as Vatican City State data. [1]

Identity and State Formation

International subject
The Holy See: juridical and diplomatic personality of the central authority of the Catholic Church [1]
Territorial sovereign state
Vatican City State (Stato della Città del Vaticano), founded by Lateran Treaty in 1929 [1]
Seat of the Holy See
Vatican City, within Rome, Italy [3]
Historical sovereignty
The Holy See retained international diplomatic personality after the Papal States lost Rome in 1870, prior to Vatican City State’s creation [1]
Lateran Treaty
Signed 11 February 1929; ratified 7 June 1929, establishing Vatican City State [1]
Contemporary papacy
Pope Leo XIV, elected on 8 May 2025 [6]
State identifiers
VA (ISO alpha-2), VAT (ISO alpha-3), numeric 336; Holy See (Vatican City State) as UN statistical designation [9]
Country-code top-level domain
.va, used by Vatican institutions; distinct from the Holy See’s international legal personality

Historical context: Papal territorial authority in central Italy was reduced during Italian unification and lost Rome in 1870, but the Holy See continued diplomatic activity. The 1929 settlement established a small independent territory, not the first-ever creation of the Holy See. [1]

Geography and Population

UN M49 subregion
Europe / Southern Europe (Holy See; Vatican City State), under UN geographic classification [9]
Vatican City territorial area
0.44 km² (44 hectares); entirely encircled by Italy [3]
Vatican City residents
882 as of 31 December 2024, including citizens and non-citizens [2]
Citizenship versus residence
Vatican citizenship is function-based and differs from actual residence; 673 citizens is separately displayed on the Governorate page without a matching dated 2025 annual series [2]
Territorial setting
Vatican Hill west of the Tiber; St Peter’s Square, basilica, museums, gardens and administrative sites [3]
Extraterritorial buildings
Certain Holy See buildings and major basilicas within Rome enjoy protections under treaty arrangements but are not ordinary Vatican City territory [3]
Conventional national population series
Not available in the 2025 World Bank country tables; do not substitute a church-wide population of Catholics
Major urban areas
Single Vatican territorial enclave; no system of cities, provinces or domestic metropolitan areas [3]

Data interpretation: The 882 figure is a dated Vatican City State resident count, not a count of every Vatican citizen or a population estimate for the worldwide Catholic Church. The Holy See as a juridical person has no analogous resident-population boundary. [2]

People and Society

Religious and institutional identity
The Holy See governs the Roman Catholic Church; Vatican City supports the independent exercise of that ministry [1]
Administrative working language
Italian is extensively used in Vatican City administration; Latin has a distinctive role in Holy See ecclesiastical law and official usage [1]
Other working and diplomatic languages
Multiple languages are used in liturgy, diplomacy and international communications; no general resident language survey is inferred
Religious affiliation percentages
No representative census-based breakdown of Vatican City residents by faith or denomination is claimed
Population characteristics
Residents include clergy, members of religious communities and lay personnel, with nationality and residence governed by specialized rules [2]
Citizenship qualification
Often tied to office, service and residence arrangements, rather than normal jus soli or multigenerational citizenship rules [2]

Population caveat: Vatican City is not a statistically typical sovereign population; worldwide Catholic membership counts do not measure Vatican City residents. [2]

Government and Institutions

Supreme governing authority
The Pope (Roman Pontiff), presently Leo XIV; sovereign of Vatican City State and supreme pastor of the Catholic Church [6]
Form of governance
Elective ecclesiastical monarchy with sovereign powers vested in the Pope; not a parliamentary republic [1]
Holy See government
Roman Curia assists the Pope in governing the Catholic Church; Secretariat of State coordinates diplomatic and central functions [10]
Vatican City State legislature
Pontifical Commission for Vatican City State under the Pope; a 2025 amendment, consolidated in the 2026 Fundamental Law, allows a broader range of appointees than cardinals alone [7]
Vatican City executive administration
Governorate of Vatican City State responsible for civil administrative functions [7]
Judicial system
Vatican City State tribunals exercising judicial authority under its own legal system [7]
Constitutional/legal framework
Lateran Treaty of 1929; current Fundamental Law of Vatican City State promulgated 31 July 2026, replacing the law of 13 May 2023 [7]
Administrative subdivisions
No provinces or municipalities equivalent to those of large territorial states [3]

The Holy See’s central ecclesiastical institutions and Vatican City’s civil institutions are related but not identical. The status of a particular organ depends on its legal mandate. [1]

International Profile

United Nations
Holy See: permanent non-member observer state, with observer presence since 6 April 1964; not a UN Member State [4]
Diplomatic representation
Holy See has treaty-making capacity and exchanges diplomatic missions under its own international personality [1]
European Union
Neither Holy See nor Vatican City State is an EU member
Euro area
Not an EU euro-area member state, despite Vatican City’s use of the euro under monetary arrangements [8]
Schengen area
Not a Schengen member state; practical access occurs through Italy
NATO
Not a member
UNESCO World Heritage
Two inscriptions on the Holy See State Party list: Vatican City (1984) and the Historic Centre of Rome property including protected Holy See sites (1980, expanded 1990) [5]
International dialing
Uses Italian international telephone numbering (+39), with Vatican-specific local arrangements
Time zone
Central European Time (UTC+1); Central European Summer Time (UTC+2)

The Vatican City World Heritage property covers the full 44-hectare state territory. The second Holy See-related inscribed property spans part of Rome and properties with special legal protections. [5]

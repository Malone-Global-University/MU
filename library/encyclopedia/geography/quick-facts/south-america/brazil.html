Country Overview

Brazil, officially the Federative Republic of Brazil, is the largest state in South America by area and population, occupying extensive Atlantic-facing, Amazonian, savanna, wetland and highland territories. Its history encompasses diverse Indigenous civilizations, Portuguese colonization, African enslavement, independence in 1822, an imperial constitutional monarchy, the establishment of a republic in 1889 and the contemporary democratic order of the 1988 Constitution. Brasília is the federal capital, while São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro are the best-known metropolitan concentrations of finance, industry and culture. The economy spans services, manufacturing, agriculture, mining, energy and international trade. Brazil is a federal presidential republic with 26 states and a Federal District, and Portuguese is the official language. The presidential election of October 25, 2026 returned incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva to office for a further term beginning in 2027; he remains the incumbent during this November 2026 reference edition. National and World Bank population series have different figures and update cycles, and Brazil’s customs merchandise exports must not be equated with its broader goods-and-services trade or current account.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Federative Republic of Brazil (República Federativa do Brasil), a federal presidential constitutional republic [7]
Common name and demonym
Brazil (Brasil in Portuguese); citizens commonly described as Brazilians
National capital
Brasília, federal capital inaugurated April 21, 1960, located in the Federal District [2]
UN geographic classification
South America in the UN M49 international regional standard [15]
Precolonial Indigenous societies
Numerous Indigenous peoples occupied and governed varied Amazonian, coastal, savanna and interior regions long before European arrival
Portuguese expansion
Portuguese imperial claim began in 1500; settlement, trade, colonization and Indigenous resistance varied substantially by region
Colonial economy
Sugar cultivation, mineral extraction, plantation labor and later coffee production shaped colonial and imperial political economy
Atlantic slavery
Brazil received a vast number of enslaved Africans through transatlantic trafficking and developed an enduring African-descended society and culture
Transfer of Portuguese royal court
The Portuguese court moved to Rio de Janeiro in 1808, transforming imperial administration and trade institutions
Independence declaration
September 7, 1822, associated with Dom Pedro and the formation of an independent Brazilian empire [16]
Imperial constitutional order
Constitution of 1824 established a monarchical state; imperial Brazil was not yet a republic
Abolition of slavery
May 13, 1888, Lei Áurea legally abolished slavery, without resolving subsequent structural inequality
Proclamation of republic
November 15, 1889, the monarchy was replaced by a republican government; a federal constitutional order was subsequently adopted
Twentieth-century political eras
Old Republic, Vargas period, postwar democratic governments and military rule shaped changing party and economic institutions
Military dictatorship
Military governments ruled from 1964 to 1985, with repression, censorship and serious human-rights violations
Democratic transition
Civilian government returned in 1985 and the 1988 Constitution became the foundation of contemporary democratic institutions [7]
1988 Constitution
Establishes rights, separation of powers, a federal system and direct election of national executive and legislative offices [7]
National Day
Independence Day, September 7, is distinct from the republic's November 15 commemorative holiday
National flag
Green field with yellow diamond, blue celestial globe and motto Ordem e Progresso, reflecting republican state symbolism
Portuguese language
Portuguese is the federal official language; Indigenous and other languages retain distinct legal and cultural protections [7]
National symbols
National anthem, coat of arms and other emblems coexist with regional and Indigenous symbols of identity
2026 presidential election
Lula was declared reelected in the October 25, 2026 runoff with 51.79% of valid votes according to the Superior Electoral Court [5]
ISO codes and domain
BR (alpha-2), BRA (alpha-3), 076 (numeric); .br country-code domain [15]
United Nations membership
Brazil is an original member of the United Nations, contributing to multilateral institutions and diplomacy [17]

State formation: Brazil declared independence in 1822, retained an empire until 1889 and adopted its current democratic Constitution in 1988. Lula’s reelection in October 2026 is a decision about the next presidential term, not the date the incumbent 2023–2026 term ends.

Geography and Population

Territorial extent
Eastern and central South America, with long Atlantic coastline and borders to nearly every mainland South American country except Chile and Ecuador
IBGE official area
8,509,360.850 km² according to the national geographic data for 2025; changes to official territorial measures reflect mapping updates [2]
Largest regional state
Largest sovereign state in South America by land area and population; much of the Amazon Basin lies within Brazil
Neighboring countries and territory
Argentina, Bolivia, Colombia, Guyana, Paraguay, Peru, Suriname, Uruguay, Venezuela and French Guiana (overseas territory of France)
Amazon Basin
Vast river and rainforest system extends across northern Brazil and neighboring countries, with Indigenous territories, biodiversity and major climate functions
Amazon River
Major continental river system with large tributaries including Madeira, Negro, Tapajós, Xingu and Purus; transport and flood cycles vary regionally
Cerrado
Tropical savanna biome in central Brazil with varied vegetation, agriculture and substantial watershed and biodiversity importance
Atlantic Forest
Highly diverse coastal and inland forest biome fragmented by centuries of settlement, farming and urban development
Pantanal
Extensive seasonal wetland in the upper Paraguay basin, particularly Mato Grosso and Mato Grosso do Sul; sensitive to rainfall and wildfire
Caatinga
Semi-arid biome in northeast Brazil characterized by drought adaptation and distinctive rural livelihoods
Pampa grasslands
Southern grassland ecosystem supports cattle and agriculture and spans an international ecological region
Coastal environments
Beaches, bays, mangroves, estuaries, coral systems and densely urbanized shorelines face erosion, pollution and storms
Brazilian Highlands
Broad southern and central highland region includes plateaus, escarpments and many urban and agricultural regions
Highest mountain
Pico da Neblina in Amazonas reaches roughly 2,995 metres near the Venezuelan frontier
Principal river basins
Amazon, Tocantins–Araguaia, São Francisco, Paraná and Paraguay–Paraná systems support hydropower, ecology and transportation
Water and hydropower
Large hydropower installations, reservoirs and rivers are central to energy supply, irrigation and social-environmental debates
National biomes
IBGE commonly distinguishes Amazon, Cerrado, Caatinga, Atlantic Forest, Pampa and Pantanal as six major mainland biomes [2]
Climatic variability
Equatorial rainforest, tropical savanna, semi-arid northeast, subtropical south and alpine mountain conditions differ strongly
Environmental hazards
Amazon drought and wildfire, southern floods, heat waves, landslides, urban flooding and coastal extremes affect specific regions
2022 census population
203,080,756 people in final national 2022 census reporting on IBGE's country data page [2]
2025 IBGE estimate
213,421,037 residents as of July 1, 2025 in the national population-estimates series [2]
2026 IBGE estimate
214,211,951 residents in the updated 2026 IBGE estimate; a different reference date from the World Bank 2025 series [2]
2025 World Bank estimate
212,812,405 people in the World Bank 2025 series, based on its own annual revision conventions [1]
Population density
Roughly 25 residents per km² when national population of about 213 million is divided by the 8.51-million-km² area; an approximate national average
Federal components
26 states and one Federal District, with extensive municipal structures and constitutionally apportioned powers [7]
Major cities
São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, Brasília, Fortaleza, Salvador, Belo Horizonte, Manaus, Curitiba and Recife are important metropolitan centers
Regional macrodivisions
North, Northeast, Central-West, Southeast and South are the five standard IBGE macroregions [2]
Life expectancy
Approximately 76 years in the World Bank 2024 life-expectancy series, which should not be presented as a finalized 2026 outcome [1]
Conservation pressures
Deforestation, biodiversity protection, illegal mining, water management and agricultural land conversion are major regional policy concerns

Population methodology: IBGE reported 203,080,756 in the 2022 census, 213,421,037 in the 2025 national estimate and 214,211,951 in its 2026 estimate; the World Bank 2025 series gives 212,812,405. These figures represent distinct series, reference dates and estimation vintages, not errors to be silently reconciled.

Economy and International Trade

Economic scale
Diverse large economy with services, finance, manufacturing, farming, mining, transport and energy sectors
Nominal GDP
About US$2.2799 trillion for 2025 in the World Bank current-dollar national accounts series [1]
GDP per capita
US$10,713 in 2025 World Bank series; average output per resident is not median wage or household disposable income [1]
Real GDP growth
2.3% in the World Bank 2025 annual series, indicating positive aggregate output growth [1]
Unemployment comparator
6.0% in the World Bank's 2025 modeled ILO series, potentially different from IBGE monthly or quarterly labor surveys [1]
National currency
Brazilian real (BRL), generally expressed R$, issued within national monetary institutions [10]
Central bank
Banco Central do Brasil oversees monetary and banking-system instruments with statutory mandates [10]
Service sectors
Wholesale trade, professional services, banking, retail, health, education, information, transport and administration contribute extensively to GDP
Industrial production
Motor vehicles, aircraft, steel, chemicals, machinery, food processing, consumer products and pharmaceuticals create varied industrial capacity
Agricultural specialization
Soybeans, corn, coffee, sugarcane, cotton, cattle, poultry, oranges and other goods form major supply chains
Mining exports
Iron ore, gold and other minerals are important commodities linked to rail, ports and overseas demand
Oil and fuels
Offshore petroleum, refined fuels, biofuels and other energy products contribute to domestic supply and export receipts
Renewable electricity
Hydropower is a major source of generation alongside rapidly growing wind and solar supply; environmental impacts and reliability vary
Biofuel economy
Ethanol from sugarcane and biodiesel fuel chains link agriculture, manufacturing and transport policy
Logistics
Santos, Paranaguá, Rio de Janeiro and other ports together with roads, railways and river corridors move bulk and manufactured goods
Official 2025 consolidated goods exports
US$348.2785 billion in the MDIC consolidated foreign-trade series, revised after preliminary release [3]
Official 2025 consolidated goods imports
US$280.2084 billion in the same consolidated MDIC merchandise-trade series [3]
Official 2025 goods trade balance
US$68.0700 billion surplus in the paired 2025 MDIC consolidated merchandise series [3]
Revision note for exports
Earlier January 2026 preliminary communications put exports near US$348.7 billion; later consolidated MDIC data show US$348.2785 billion [3]
Key merchandise destinations
China is the leading export market, followed by the European Union, United States and other countries; rankings depend on year and statistical scope [4]
Main foreign suppliers
China, the European Union, United States, Argentina and other economies supply manufactured goods, machinery, chemicals and technology inputs [4]
Agri-food export products
Soybeans, meat, sugar, coffee and related goods are major foreign-exchange sources with environmental and trade-policy implications
Industrial exports
Passenger and commercial vehicles, aircraft, machinery and chemicals move through diversified international supply chains
Oil and ore exports
Crude oil and iron ore values vary by commodity prices, production and overseas market demand
International services
Transport, tourism, professional services, software, finance and other services are excluded from customs merchandise-export values
Goods and services current account
Central bank balance-of-payments exports and imports include valuation adjustments and services; figures differ from MDIC customs trade totals [10]
Trade integration
MERCOSUR commitments and numerous bilateral arrangements influence tariff schedules, industrial trade and cross-border markets [18]
Economic-policy issues
Public finances, inflation, interest rates, investment, productivity, energy transition and regional inequality are ongoing policy debates
Regional disparities
Industrialized Southeast and South, agribusiness Central-West, mineral-rich North and varied Northeast economies have distinct development conditions
Forest-linked economy
Amazon livelihoods, forest products, illegal deforestation, timber rules and ecosystem-service debates connect environment with economic planning

People and Society

Official national language
Portuguese, spoken by the large majority, with regional accents and varieties; not interchangeable with neighboring Spanish-language systems [7]
Indigenous languages
Hundreds of Indigenous linguistic varieties have historical or continuing use, with local recognition and preservation initiatives
Languages of migration
Germanic, Italian, Japanese and other immigrant language communities contribute to regional cultural histories
Indigenous population
2022 census showed around 1.69 million Indigenous residents using census-specific identification questions; territories and communities are highly diverse [12]
Afro-Brazilian heritage
African-descended populations and cultural institutions are central to Brazilian history, religion, foodways, music and public policy
Census race-color categories
IBGE self-identification categories include branco, preto, pardo, amarelo and indígena; classifications must be interpreted in their national context [2]
Quilombola communities
Afro-descended quilombola communities maintain distinct territorial and historical identities; 2022 census counted 1,330,186 residents using specific identification questions [13]
2022 census religion, Catholic
56.7% of surveyed residents aged 10 or older identified as Roman Catholic in the sample-based 2022 religion results [11]
2022 census religion, Evangelical
26.9% of residents aged 10+ reported Evangelical or Protestant affiliation in the 2022 religion sample [11]
2022 census religion, no religion
9.3% of residents aged 10+ reported no religion; this does not necessarily denote atheism [11]
Other religious traditions
Spiritist, Afro-Brazilian religions such as Umbanda and Candomblé, Jewish, Muslim, Buddhist and other communities are present
Religion denominators
IBGE's 2022 religion survey percentages are for people aged 10+ and cannot be applied directly to the whole 2025 population [11]
Education responsibilities
Federal, state and municipal authorities share functions in schooling and higher education under constitutional and legislative frameworks
Higher education
Federal and state universities, research institutes, technical schools and private institutions produce education and scientific research
Public healthcare
Sistema Único de Saúde (SUS) provides a universal public-system framework, alongside private and supplemental provision
Cultural regions
Northeast, Amazonia, Central-West, Southeast and South support diverse culinary, linguistic, musical and historical traditions
Music and performance
Samba, choro, forró, maracatu, bossa nova, funk, sertanejo and other genres reflect regional and transnational influences
Carnival
Multiple cities maintain varied Carnival traditions; Rio de Janeiro is prominent but is not representative of every region
Literature
Machado de Assis, Clarice Lispector, Jorge Amado and many other writers form an extensive literary history
Sport
Football is a major national pastime alongside volleyball, basketball, motorsport, martial arts and region-specific activities
Urban housing
Metropolitan segregation, housing shortages and infrastructure access differ by region and socioeconomic position
Social development challenges
Racial and regional inequality, education quality, public safety, employment and sanitation provision remain major policy questions
Climate and society
Floods, severe drought and wildfire impose unequal burdens on low-income, rural and Indigenous communities

Religion denominator: The 56.7% Catholic, 26.9% Evangelical and 9.3% no-religion values were from the sample-based 2022 census results for residents aged 10 and older, rather than percentages of the full population or a 2026 religion census.

Government and Institutions

Constitutional form
Federal democratic presidential republic under Constitution of October 5, 1988 [7]
Incumbent president
Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, president since January 1, 2023, and incumbent as of November 6, 2026 [8]
Vice president
Geraldo Alckmin, serving alongside Lula in the 2023–2026 term [8]
2026 presidential election
Superior Electoral Court declared Lula reelected after October 25 runoff with 64,256,232 valid votes (51.79%) against Flávio Bolsonaro with 59,826,041 (48.21%) [5]
Next presidential term
Lula's newly won term follows the scheduled 2026 term; the Senate reports an inauguration date of January 5, 2027, after this profile's cutoff [5] [6]
National executive
President appoints ministers and directs federal executive administration, subject to legislative and judicial limits [7]
Federal Congress
National Congress consists of Chamber of Deputies and Federal Senate in a bicameral legislature [9]
Chamber of Deputies
513 lower-house seats allocated across states and Federal District under electoral laws [9]
Federal Senate
81 senators, three for each of the 26 states and the Federal District [9]
States and Federal District
26 states plus the Federal District; municipalities have constitutionally protected local-government functions [7]
Federalism
Union, states, municipalities and Federal District possess distinct taxing, service and governing responsibilities [7]
Presidential elections
Popular presidential election uses a two-round system when no candidate obtains the required first-round majority [5]
Legislative elections
Federal deputies are elected by proportional system, with senators chosen under majority-based rules and staggered renewal [5]
Electoral authority
Tribunal Superior Eleitoral and regional electoral courts oversee elections and associated dispute procedures [5]
Supreme Federal Court
Supremo Tribunal Federal exercises apex constitutional jurisdiction; separate higher courts administer designated other appeals [7]
Rights framework
1988 Constitution protects civil, social, political and other rights; rights enforcement varies across institutions and contexts [7]
Democratic transition
The 1988 order followed the 1964–1985 military government and later civilian transition
2026 election outcome interpretation
Reelection result concerns a future presidential term; it does not mean a new administration formally began in October 2026 [5] [6]
Budget and fiscal institutions
Congress, executive fiscal bodies, Court of Accounts and subnational authorities oversee different dimensions of public finance [9]
Security institutions
Federal police, state military and civil police, armed forces and municipal guards have differentiated legal functions
Environmental governance
Federal environment authorities, state institutions and Indigenous affairs agencies address forests, mining, land use and conservation
Public-policy disputes
Tax reform, health financing, social programs, land rights and environmental protection are debated through elected and judicial institutions

Election timing: The October 25, 2026 runoff returned Lula to the presidency for the next term, but he is described here as the incumbent president in November 2026. The Brazilian Senate gives January 5, 2027 as the next inauguration date.

International Profile

United Nations
Original member of the United Nations with significant involvement in development, peace and climate diplomacy [17]
South American location
Border relationships span the Amazon, Plata Basin, Atlantic coast and Guyana Shield, linking Brazil to multiple regional economies
MERCOSUR
Founding member of MERCOSUR with Argentina, Paraguay and Uruguay, coordinating important aspects of trade policy [18]
G20
Member of the G20 and major participant in global macroeconomic coordination
BRICS
Founding participant in BRICS group diplomacy, subject to changing membership and agendas
World Trade Organization
WTO member engaged in international trade rules and disputes [4]
Amazon cooperation
Treaty and regional institutions address Amazonian ecology, development and cross-border governance [19]
China trade
China is Brazil's largest merchandise export market; commodity sales and industrial imports are particularly important [4]
United States trade
The United States is a major market and investor across manufacturing, aviation, energy and services [4]
European Union relations
EU trade and investment ties are significant, including discussions related to MERCOSUR economic arrangements [4]
Regional diplomacy
Relations with Argentina, Bolivia, Paraguay, Uruguay, Venezuela, Colombia and neighbors affect border trade and transport
Climate negotiations
Amazon deforestation, biodiversity, energy, agriculture and adaptation make Brazil central to global climate discussions
UNESCO World Heritage
Twenty-six properties are on UNESCO’s current Brazil State Party list, including Amazonia Theaters (2026) [14]
World Heritage examples
Brasília, Iguazu National Park, the Atlantic Forest South-East Reserves and Amazon-related properties represent different cultural and natural types [14]
World Heritage category caution
Number of inscriptions includes mixed and serial sites; counting component areas separately would overstate national UNESCO properties [14]
Science and research partnerships
Universities, public laboratories, agricultural research and aerospace institutions participate in international research networks
Regional transport
Inland waterways, international highway routes, bridges, ports and Amazon river systems shape commerce and border administration
Brazilian diaspora
Emigrant communities and migration into Brazil create extensive social, cultural and economic connections with other countries
Telephone code
+55; internet country-code domain .br
Time-zone diversity
Four main standard time zones range approximately UTC−2 to UTC−5; local rules and island territories differ from Brasília time
International currency notation
Brazilian real is BRL, distinct from U.S. dollars used for World Bank international GDP comparison

Heritage-list methodology: UNESCO’s current Brazil State Party page lists 26 World Heritage properties, including a 2026 addition. A single serial or mixed property is counted as one inscription, regardless of the number of component locations.

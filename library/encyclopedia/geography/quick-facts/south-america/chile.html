Country Overview

Chile is a long, narrow republic on the Pacific margin of South America, stretching from the Atacama Desert across central valleys and the southern forests, fjords and glaciers of Patagonia. Its history encompasses diverse Indigenous peoples, Spanish colonization, the independence movement of 1810–1818, twentieth-century constitutional change, the military dictatorship of 1973–1990 and the restoration of electoral democracy. Santiago is the capital, while the National Congress meets in Valparaíso. Copper mining is the largest merchandise-export sector; lithium chemicals, fisheries, fruit, forestry products, manufacturing and services are also important. Chile administers Pacific islands including Rapa Nui and makes an Antarctic claim that is not counted as undisputed national territory. Its 2024 population census and the 2025 World Bank population series use different periods and methods. A presidential transfer of power occurred on March 11, 2026.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Chile (República de Chile), a unitary presidential republic [9]
Capital city
Santiago, seat of the president, central executive administration and Supreme Court; the National Congress operates in Valparaíso [9]
Continent / UN region
South America in United Nations M49 geographic classification [13]
Indigenous foundations
Mapuche, Aymara, Diaguita, Rapa Nui, Quechua, Atacameño/Lickanantay, Kawésqar, Yagán and other peoples have distinct precolonial and continuing histories
Spanish colonial period
Imperial jurisdictions centered on Santiago and connected to the Viceroyalty of Peru shaped institutions, landholding and trade after the sixteenth century
First National Government Junta
September 18, 1810: the junta in Santiago marked the start of the independence process but initially professed loyalty to the captive Spanish monarch [8]
Independence proclamation
February 12, 1818: formal declaration and public affirmation of separation from Spain; not identical to September 18 commemorations [8]
Independence wars
Campaigns associated with Bernardo O'Higgins, José de San Martín, patriots and royalists included Chacabuco in 1817 and Maipú in 1818 [8]
Republican state-building
Nineteenth-century constitutions and political conflicts helped consolidate institutions and expand the central state's territorial reach
War of the Pacific
Conflict with Peru and Bolivia from 1879 into the 1880s redrew regional boundaries and shaped nitrate and mineral-producing territories
Mapuche territorial history
Military occupation of Araucanía and settler expansion in the nineteenth century transformed Indigenous landholding and generated enduring disputes
1925 constitutional order
A new constitutional settlement altered church–state relations and the structure of presidential government
1970–1973 political crisis
Salvador Allende's elected socialist government faced polarization, economic conflict and destabilization before the September 1973 coup
Military dictatorship
Augusto Pinochet's 1973–1990 regime curtailed liberties and committed serious human-rights violations; remembrance and accountability remain subjects of national debate
Return to civilian rule
Elected government resumed in March 1990 after the 1988 plebiscite and the 1989 elections
Constitutional framework
Constitution enacted in 1980 and extensively amended, particularly in 2005; the proposed replacement texts failed referendums in 2022 and 2023 [9]
Contemporary presidential transition
José Antonio Kast Rist succeeded Gabriel Boric on March 11, 2026 for the 2026–2030 term [7]
National celebrations
September 18 commemorates the 1810 junta, with September 19 observing the armed forces; formal independence was proclaimed in February 1818 [8]
National symbols
White, blue and red flag with a white star; coat of arms with huemul and condor; national anthem commonly titled Himno Nacional de Chile
Official institutional language
Spanish predominates in government; Indigenous languages have distinct cultural and local recognition rather than equal nationwide federal status
United Nations membership
An original UN member, with the Charter taking effect in 1945 [18]
ISO and internet identifiers
CL (alpha-2), CHL (alpha-3), 152 (numeric); .cl top-level domain [13]
Antarctic claim
Chile asserts a sectoral Antarctic claim; the Antarctic Treaty system regulates activities, and the claim is excluded from standard comparable mainland area totals [15]

Chronology: September 18, 1810 marks the first national governing junta, while the formal declaration of independence dates to February 12, 1818. The 1973 military coup and the 1990 return to elected civilian rule are distinct institutional milestones. The current constitution remains the much-amended 1980 text after two rejected replacement proposals.

Geography and Population

Geographic setting
Pacific coast of western South America; continental Chile borders Peru to the north and Bolivia and Argentina to the east
Internationally comparable area
Approximately 756,102 km² in common UN territorial reference, excluding claimed Antarctic territory [2]
Antarctic-sector caveat
Domestic cartography can show the Chilean Antarctic Territory; this claim is not treated as universally recognized territory under international reference conventions [15]
North-to-south span
The mainland extends more than 4,000 km north–south, creating exceptional latitudinal variation but a comparatively narrow east–west width
Atacama Desert
Arid northern landscapes include mineral basins, high-elevation salars and some of the world's driest nonpolar environments
Andes Mountains
The eastern high mountain corridor shapes drainage, climate, mining, transport and seismic hazards
Central Valley
Densely settled agricultural and urban region includes Santiago and important wine, fruit and industrial districts
Coastal Range
Parallel mountains and associated basins separate some Pacific coastal plains from the interior valleys
Southern lake district
Volcanic mountains, forests, glaciers and lakes characterize regions south of central Chile, with tourism and forestry economies
Patagonia
Southern mainland and archipelagos include fjords, the Southern Patagonian Ice Field, Magallanes and Torres del Paine landscapes
Rapa Nui (Easter Island)
Remote Polynesian island administered by Chile with distinctive Rapa Nui cultural heritage and World Heritage status [14]
Juan Fernández islands
Pacific archipelago has unique biodiversity, human settlements and oceanic conservation issues
Tierra del Fuego
Chile administers western portions of the archipelago, with the Strait of Magellan separating Tierra del Fuego from the mainland
Cape Horn
Southern island landscape and maritime passage have historical navigation significance
Major cities
Santiago, Valparaíso–Viña del Mar, Concepción, Antofagasta, Temuco, Iquique, La Serena and Puerto Montt
Important rivers
Loa, Maipo, Maule, Biobío, Toltén and Baker basins have different hydrological and economic roles
Climatic range
Hyperarid north, Mediterranean-type central valleys, temperate wet south, subpolar Patagonia and alpine conditions at elevation
Earthquake exposure
Subduction of the Nazca Plate beneath South America generates severe earthquakes and Pacific tsunami risks
Volcanic activity
Active southern Andean volcanoes create ash, lahars and other hazards; monitoring is coordinated through national geological institutions
Climate change
Glacier retreat, drought, wildfire, coastal changes and mountain water stress affect ecosystems and communities
2024 population census
18,480,432 usual residents were counted in the 2024 census; this was a resident-based census, unlike earlier presence-based enumerations [3]
2025 World Bank estimate
19,859,921 persons in the Bank's annual demographic series, not a direct 2025 census [1]
Population-method distinction
The 2024 census enumeration and 2025 World Bank modeled population are different statistical products; their difference is not a direct migration estimate [3] [1]
Population density
Approximately 26.3 residents per km² using the 2025 World Bank series and about 756,102 km² total area; a calculated national average
National administrative units
Sixteen regions subdivided into provinces and communes; decentralized service and elected regional arrangements coexist
Urban settlement
Most population is concentrated in metropolitan Santiago and other central and coastal urban corridors
Life expectancy
World Bank's latest pre-2026 life-expectancy series places average life expectancy near 81 years; date should not be mistaken for 2026 measurement [1]
Natural-resource geography
Copper, lithium-bearing brines, molybdenum, forests, fisheries, agricultural valleys and renewable-energy potential differ greatly by region

Population and territory: The 18,480,432 residents enumerated in the 2024 census are not the same statistical measure as the World Bank’s 19,859,921 estimate for 2025. The standard area excludes Chile’s Antarctic claim; some domestic area statements add that claim and should not be substituted into international comparisons.

Economy and International Trade

Development classification
High-income market economy with internationally traded mineral resources, a large service sector and significant private investment
Nominal GDP
US$357.37 billion in 2025, World Bank current-dollar series [1]
GDP per capita
About US$17,995 in 2025; national output divided by population, not typical disposable household income [1]
Annual real GDP growth
2.5% during 2025 in the World Bank series [1]
Annual consumer-price inflation
4.2% for calendar 2025 in the World Bank consumer-price indicator [1]
Modeled unemployment
About 9.0% in the World Bank's 2025 ILO-modeled unemployment series; Chilean household labor surveys can differ [1]
National currency
Chilean peso (CLP); the Bank of Chile conducts monetary policy within its statutory framework [12]
Monetary authority
Banco Central de Chile oversees policy rate and inflation-related functions and publishes balance-of-payments statistics [12]
Mining backbone
Copper concentrates, refined copper and other mining commodities dominate merchandise export receipts
Copper-bearing regions
Mining operations in northern and central Chile depend on water, energy, rail, road and port logistics
Lithium industries
Lithium carbonate and other compounds are produced from salt-flat brines, with environmental and water-governance debates
Molybdenum and related minerals
Secondary mineral exports connect Chile to metals, chemicals and manufacturing markets
Salmon aquaculture
Southern farmed salmon and other seafood support substantial exports, employment and environmental regulation
Fruit exporters
Cherries, grapes, apples, blueberries, avocados and nuts reach export markets via refrigerated agricultural logistics
Forestry and pulp
Southern plantations, pulp mills and timber activities supply export products, with land-use and fire-related concerns
Wine and beverages
Wine is a prominent agricultural manufacture supported by viticulture in multiple central valleys
National 2025 merchandise exports
US$107.004 billion in SUBREI's 2025 customs-based summary, published January 7, 2026 [5]
WTO/UN Comtrade comparison
WTO profile lists US$106.996 billion for 2025 goods exports, a near-identical but separately compiled and rounded customs/Comtrade series [6]
2025 trade turnover
SUBREI reported US$199.667 billion in combined exports and imports in the 2025 foreign-trade summary [5]
2025 mining exports
US$63.253 billion of mining exports in the SUBREI 2025 statistical release [5]
2025 copper concentrates
Copper concentrates generated roughly US$36.278 billion in the same 2025 release [5]
2025 nontraditional exports
SUBREI identified US$47.044 billion in nontraditional-goods exports under its national reporting classification [5]
Main destination markets
China, United States, European Union, Japan and South Korea were leading 2025 merchandise markets; source coverage influences grouping [6]
China concentration
WTO/UN Comtrade reported China as destination for about 36.4% of 2025 merchandise export value [6]
Merchandise versus services
SUBREI's customs-based US$107.0-billion goods value is not equivalent to balance-of-payments exports of goods and services
Selected cross-border services
A SUBREI administrative register reported US$3.190 billion in 2025 services provided abroad in the covered categories, not necessarily exhaustive BOP services [5]
Transport and ports
Antofagasta, San Antonio, Valparaíso and other terminals connect mines, agriculture and manufacturers with Pacific shipping
Energy transition
Solar generation in the northern desert, wind projects and electricity transmission investment are central to energy planning
Tax and public finance
Copper-sector royalties, taxation, fiscal rules and social expenditure are subjects of legislative and economic policy
External risks
Dependence on copper prices, Chinese demand, climatic shocks, water constraints and global trade conditions affects the business cycle
Measurement note
GDP uses current U.S. dollars and national accounts; trade totals use separate customs or balance-of-payments definitions and should not be divided casually

People and Society

Principal language
Spanish is predominant in administration, schooling, broadcasting and commerce, with regional variation
Mapudungun
Mapuche language remains important in southern and urban communities; revitalization and bilingual education vary by locality
Northern Indigenous languages
Aymara and Quechua are used in Andean communities, while Lickanantay heritage and other languages are subjects of cultural preservation
Rapa Nui language
Polynesian Rapa Nui is used on Easter Island alongside Spanish under distinct local sociolinguistic circumstances
Migration and society
International migration from regional Latin American neighbors and other countries contributes to changing urban and social patterns
2024 census age structure
14.0% of enumerated residents were aged 65 or older, reflecting increasing population aging [3]
2024 census children
17.7% of enumerated residents were aged 14 or younger, smaller than in the early 1990s [3]
2024 religion, Catholic
54.0% of persons aged 15 or older reported Catholic affiliation, according to the national census [4]
2024 religion, Evangelical
16.3% of residents aged 15 or older reported evangelical or Protestant identification [4]
2024 religion, none
25.8% of residents aged 15 or older reported no religion or creed [4]
Religion methodology
Religious percentages use the 2024 census population aged 15+; they are not percentages of all residents or measures of weekly worship [4]
Indigenous identity
Mapuche and other recognized Indigenous peoples have distinct political, linguistic and territorial histories; census identification uses its own definitions
Education
Public and private schools, universities and technical institutions operate under national frameworks with ongoing equity and funding debates
Higher education institutions
University of Chile, Pontifical Catholic University of Chile and regional universities are prominent research and training institutions
Health system
Public FONASA financing and private ISAPRE coverage coexist with public hospitals, clinics and service-access disparities
Culture and literature
Poetry, fiction, theater and film include works by Gabriela Mistral, Pablo Neruda and later generations, alongside Indigenous cultural production
Music and dance
Cueca, nueva canción, Andean forms and contemporary popular music have distinct historical and regional origins
Cuisine
Seafood, empanadas, pastel de choclo, legumes and wine reflect geographic and Indigenous, colonial and immigrant histories
Social challenges
Regional inequality, housing affordability, Indigenous rights, water security, aging and migration policy are continuing national debates
UNESCO heritage
Chile has seven World Heritage properties under the current UNESCO State Party register, including Rapa Nui National Park and historic Valparaíso [14]

Religion denominator: The 54.0% Catholic, 16.3% evangelical/Protestant and 25.8% no-religion shares refer to persons aged 15 and over in Chile’s 2024 census, not to all ages or to rates of religious observance. The national census also measures language, migration and Indigenous identification in separate question sets.

Government and Institutions

Form of state
Unitary presidential republic with regional, provincial and municipal territorial administration [9]
Constitutional law
1980 constitution as subsequently amended, rather than a new charter adopted in either of the unsuccessful 2022 and 2023 referendums [9]
Head of state and government
President of the Republic combines both roles under the presidential system [9]
Incumbent president
José Antonio Kast Rist, inaugurated March 11, 2026, for the 2026–2030 constitutional term [7]
Former presidency
Gabriel Boric served from March 11, 2022 to March 11, 2026, and is not the current incumbent [7]
Presidential election
Direct nationwide elections normally choose the president for a four-year term with a possible runoff when needed [9]
Immediate reelection
Chilean constitutional rules prohibit immediately consecutive presidential terms; nonconsecutive candidacy is a different matter [9]
National legislature
Bicameral National Congress, comprising Senate and Chamber of Deputies, meets principally in Valparaíso [10]
Senate
Fifty elected senators under the current institutional composition, with staggered electoral terms [10]
Chamber of Deputies
One hundred fifty-five elected deputies representing multiple electoral districts [10]
Executive administration
Ministries and central agencies implement national policy, subject to budgetary, legislative and judicial constraints
Regional government
Sixteen regions have regional governors and councils, while national government representatives perform separate state functions
Provincial and communal government
Provinces and 346 communes structure intermediate and municipal governance, with local electoral institutions
Judiciary
Supreme Court, courts of appeal, trial courts and specialized tribunals exercise distinct competences
Constitutional Tribunal
Conducts constitutionally defined legal review separate from ordinary courts [9]
Independent electoral bodies
SERVEL administers elections under electoral law, with electoral tribunals responsible for related adjudication [11]
Electoral participation
Voting is compulsory for most eligible voters in relevant national elections under current law, with defined exceptions and enforcement provisions [11]
Rights and public accountability
Constitutional rights, human-rights treaty commitments and post-dictatorship accountability institutions shape policy debate
Central bank autonomy
Banco Central de Chile has an independent legal mandate for monetary stability and normal payment-system functioning [12]
Indigenous institutional issues
Consultation, land rights and recognition of Indigenous peoples remain important, contested questions of constitutional and public policy
Civilian-military relations
Armed services are constitutionally organized within a civilian-elected state, contrasting with the military regime before 1990
Information cutoff
Current officeholders and the legal order are identified as of November 6, 2026; subsequent elections and amendments require updates

2026 officeholder check: The Chilean Senate and presidency identify José Antonio Kast’s March 11, 2026 inauguration. The National Congress is bicameral and primarily located in Valparaíso although Santiago is the national capital. Later changes in parliamentary seat occupancy should not be confused with the constitutional composition of the chambers.

International Profile

United Nations
Chile is an original member of the UN and participates in specialized agencies and multilateral diplomacy [18]
Regional institutions
Member of the Organization of American States, CELAC and regional economic-dialogue forums
Pacific Alliance
Founding member with Colombia, Mexico and Peru; promotes Pacific-facing economic cooperation [16]
OECD membership
Member of the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development since 2010 [17]
WTO membership
Chile participates in the multilateral trading system and maintains extensive commercial agreements [6]
APEC
Member of Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation, linking trade, standards and regional engagement
Major commercial partner
China is the largest single merchandise export destination in the 2025 WTO partner data [6]
Relations with Argentina
Mountain crossings, energy arrangements, trade and the shared Patagonian environment connect the neighboring states
Relations with Peru and Bolivia
Boundaries and nineteenth-century war legacies are important to regional history; contemporary trade and migration also connect these states
Pacific maritime orientation
Long coast and port system support exports to Asia, North America and other Pacific-facing markets
Antarctic Treaty
Chile participates in the Antarctic Treaty system; claimed Antarctic sector is subject to the treaty's legal arrangements [15]
Maritime and fisheries policy
Exclusive economic zone and fisheries regulation affect Pacific conservation and trade
UNESCO properties
Seven World Heritage properties as listed by UNESCO's Chile State Party page, including cultural and natural sites [14]
Time-zone conventions
Continental Chile generally follows UTC−4 standard time with seasonal changes; regions and Rapa Nui have different schedules
International dialing code
+56; .cl internet country code
Diplomatic issues
Trade diversification, climate cooperation, human rights, migration, maritime security and Antarctic science are recurring international priorities

UNESCO list: UNESCO identifies seven currently inscribed Chilean World Heritage properties. The number of properties counts each inscribed property, including serial sites, as one, and does not equal the number of national parks, protected areas or cultural monuments.

Country Overview

Paraguay is a landlocked South American republic bordered by Bolivia, Brazil and Argentina. The Paraguay River divides a populous eastern region from the sparsely settled Gran Chaco, while the Paraguay and Paraná basins connect the country economically with its neighbors. Indigenous peoples, above all Guaraní-speaking communities, shaped society before Spanish colonization, and Guaraní remains a nationwide official language alongside Spanish. The country became independent in 1811, endured the devastating War of the Triple Alliance and later the Chaco War, and adopted its present democratic Constitution in 1992 after decades of military dictatorship. Soybeans, beef, electricity and manufactured goods are central to commerce, but the national goods-export series also includes re-exports. On November 6, 2026, President Santiago Peña was removed following impeachment and Vice President Pedro Alliana took the constitutional oath of office; this edition reflects that same-day political transition.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Paraguay (República del Paraguay; Tetã Paraguái), a sovereign presidential republic
Capital
Asunción, a national political center on the eastern bank of the Paraguay River [2]
UN geographic classification
South America in the United Nations M49 grouping; the state is landlocked [14]
Indigenous foundations
Guaraní-speaking peoples and other Indigenous societies occupied distinct eastern and western territories before European conquest
Guaraní historical influence
Language, settlement and cultural exchange made Guaraní an enduring element of national identity, although Indigenous peoples have separate contemporary rights
Spanish colonial beginnings
Asunción was founded in 1537, becoming a regional colonial center linked to Spanish administration in the Río de la Plata area
Jesuit missions
Jesuit reductions shaped economic production, built heritage and Indigenous–colonial relations in parts of Paraguay during the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries
Independence movement
Paraguay established independence from Spanish rule during May 14–15, 1811, with distinct early governing juntas [9]
Nineteenth-century state-building
The regimes of José Gaspar Rodríguez de Francia and the López family established powerful centralized institutions while limiting pluralist competition
War of the Triple Alliance
The 1864–1870 conflict against Brazil, Argentina and Uruguay devastated Paraguay's population, economy and territorial position
Postwar reconstruction
Rebuilding institutions, infrastructure and trade involved foreign economic influence and prolonged social change after 1870
Chaco War
Paraguay and Bolivia fought over the Gran Chaco during 1932–1935; the resulting settlement altered borders and remains significant in national remembrance
Military and political instability
Coups and factional conflict culminated in Alfredo Stroessner's long dictatorship beginning in 1954
End of dictatorship
A 1989 military coup removed Stroessner, opening an uneven period of democratization and reform
Constitution of 1992
Current constitutional charter established democratic guarantees, revised executive constraints and civic institutions [8]
Modern elections
The presidency and Congress are elected under a multiparty system, with historically strong influence of the Colorado Party
2023 presidential mandate
Santiago Peña took office on August 15, 2023, following the April election; that term was originally scheduled to end in August 2028 [9]
November 2026 impeachment
The Senate voted on November 6, 2026 to remove Santiago Peña following a banking and public-oversight crisis; removal is a matter of official parliamentary record [6]
Constitutional succession
Pedro Alliana, previously vice president, was sworn in as president on November 6, 2026; this same-day officeholder update supersedes prior presidency lists [7]
National Day
May 14–15 commemorations recall the 1811 independence movement, rather than a single date of twentieth-century constitutional change
Official languages
Spanish and Guaraní share constitutional national official status; both are employed in distinct public and social contexts [8]
National symbols
Red, white and blue horizontal flag with different central emblems on each side; national coat of arms and anthem accompany civic observances
UN membership
Original member of the United Nations, with membership dating to 1945 [15]
ISO coding
PY (alpha-2), PRY (alpha-3), 600 (numeric); country-code domain .py [14]

Constitutional history: Independence was achieved in 1811, dictatorship ended in 1989, and the present Constitution dates from 1992. A November 6, 2026 impeachment removed Santiago Peña; Pedro Alliana was sworn in under the succession process on the same date.

Geography and Population

Area
Approximately 406,752 km² in official and United Nations reference material; direct ocean access is absent [2]
Landlocked location
Borders Bolivia, Brazil and Argentina; international transport relies on river systems, road routes and cross-border corridors
Paraguay River
Principal north–south division between western Chaco and eastern Paraguay; supports inland shipping toward the Paraná and Atlantic estuary
Paraná River
Major eastern and southern river system supporting ports, hydroelectric installations and regional commerce
Eastern region
Relatively well-watered, more densely settled zone with agriculture, cities, forests and rolling hills
Western region / Chaco
Extensive subtropical dry forests, wetlands, floodplains and ranching areas with comparatively low population density
Humid Chaco
Flood-prone eastern Chaco ecosystems differ sharply from the drier western landscapes, requiring locally adapted agriculture
Dry Chaco
Thorn forests and seasonal drought pose conservation and livestock-water-management challenges
Pantanal and Upper Paraguay Basin
Northern wetlands and river systems connect Paraguay ecologically with Brazil and Bolivia
Atlantic Forest remnants
Eastern tropical and subtropical forest ecosystems have undergone agricultural conversion and habitat fragmentation
Itaipú reservoir
Hydroelectric reservoir on the Paraná forms part of Paraguay–Brazil electricity production, distinct from a natural lake
Yacyretá dam
Binational electricity project with Argentina on the Paraná; reservoir and relocation impacts form part of its development history
Capital metro area
Greater Asunción extends beyond the municipality into neighboring Central Department districts
Major cities
Asunción, Ciudad del Este, San Lorenzo, Luque, Capiatá, Encarnación, Pedro Juan Caballero and others
Administrative geography
Seventeen departments plus the separately governed capital district of Asunción
2022 final census count
6,109,903 persons in the INE definitive 2022 population and housing census, released in 2024 [3]
2025 international population
7,013,078 in the World Bank annual population series; different vintage and modeling than the definitive 2022 census [1]
Population-data caution
The difference between the 2022 census and 2025 World Bank estimate is not a measured three-year rise or an official migration total [3] [1]
Population concentration
Central Department, Asunción and the Alto Paraná corridor contain much of the settled population and service employment [3]
Rural settlement
Agricultural communities and dispersed Chaco settlements differ in road access, sanitation, schooling and health facilities
Population density
Approximately 17.2 persons per km² using the 2025 World Bank population divided by 406,752 km²; derived average
Natural-resource setting
Hydroelectric generation, agricultural land, forest resources, river transport and biodiversity are economically important
Climate
Subtropical conditions, hot summers and pronounced seasonal drought and rainfall variation, with strong east–west differences
Disaster exposure
Flooding along major rivers, heat, drought, forest fire and water stress affect livelihoods and infrastructure
Environmental policy
Deforestation pressure in the Chaco and forest conservation in the east create distinct regional land-use questions
Transboundary catchments
Upstream and downstream water use and river navigation link national policy with neighboring governments

Census comparability: INE definitive 2022 enumeration gives 6,109,903 persons. The World Bank 2025 international estimate is 7,013,078; the figures use different reference years and estimation regimes and should not be subtracted as a direct measured demographic change.

Market economy built on agribusiness, livestock, hydroelectric power, trade and services, with growing manufacturing under export-processing regimes
2025 nominal GDP
US$49.28 billion in World Bank current-dollar data [1]
2025 GDP per capita
US$7,026.6; output divided by residents is not average wages or a median household-income indicator [1]
2025 real GDP growth
6.6% in the World Bank national accounts series, not a forecast for 2026 [1]
2025 inflation
4.0% in the World Bank consumer-price annual series [1]
2025 modeled unemployment
4.8% in the ILO-modeled World Bank comparable series, not a count of all informal workers [1]
Currency
Paraguayan guaraní (PYG); monetary policy managed by the central bank [5]
Central bank
Banco Central del Paraguay is responsible for monetary policy, currency and financial stability under national law [5]
Soybean production
Extensive mechanized farming supports soybeans, soybean oil and by-products, with export values sensitive to prices, weather and river logistics
Beef industry
Cattle raising, meat processing and veterinary standards support exports to regional and global markets
Hydroelectricity
Paraguay shares output of the Itaipú and Yacyretá binational projects; electricity exports form part of external accounts
2025 total merchandise exports
US$16,720.3 million in the BCP annual external-commerce release, inclusive of registered exports, re-exports and other export items [4]
2025 registered exports
US$11,082.1 million, or 66.3% of 2025 BCP total exports; a narrower series than total exports [4]
2025 re-exports
US$4,584.7 million, or 27.4% of the BCP total, reflecting trade in goods not necessarily produced in Paraguay [4]
2025 other exports
US$1,053.5 million, or 6.3%, under BCP's additional categories covering additional external-commerce and accounting categories [4]
2025 imports
US$18,111.1 million in the same BCP 2025 release, with different components and valuations from gross exports [4]
Goods-trade balance
Approximately US$1,390.7 million deficit in 2025 according to the paired BCP external-commerce release [4]
Maquila exports
US$1,236.7 million during 2025 in BCP reporting; this regime is a production category and should not be added a second time to exports [4]
Major export sectors
Beef, soybeans and products, maize, electricity, other food commodities and industrial or maquila production
Trading relationships
Brazil, Argentina, Chile and other regional markets are important, while goods frequently transit neighboring waterways and border corridors
Services sector
Retail, logistics, financial services, transport, professional activity and tourism contribute to output but are not captured in merchandise totals
Investment and infrastructure
Roads, transmission lines, river ports, cold storage and electricity-intensive industry affect competitiveness and territorial development
Agriculture and climate
Severe droughts and river-level changes constrain soybean yields, freight rates and electricity generation in different years
Economic policy priorities
Labor formalization, fiscal sustainability, productive diversification, social provision and financial oversight remain major challenges
2026 banking crisis
The collapse and regulatory intervention of a domestic bank precipitated an impeachment and political succession on November 6; it is a 2026 event, not a 2025 trade statistic [6] [7]

People and Society

Bilingual official status
Spanish and Guaraní are constitutional official languages; proficiency, literacy and domestic use vary between urban and rural settings [8]
Guaraní in daily life
Guaraní is widely used in conversation, media and cultural expression, including by people who do not identify as members of Indigenous communities
Jopará speech
Mixed Spanish–Guaraní language practices are common in informal settings, although the label does not describe a single standardized written variety
Indigenous peoples
Guaraní, Nivaclé, Enxet, Ayoreo and other communities maintain distinct languages, territorial relations and political institutions
Ethnic and linguistic distinction
Speaking Guaraní does not, by itself, identify an individual's ethnicity or establish membership in a particular Indigenous nation
Religion
Roman Catholicism remains prominent alongside evangelical and other Christian communities, Indigenous beliefs and persons with other or no affiliation
Religion data caution
Religious percentages from surveys or older census modules should not be recast as 2025 population shares without a matching source and date
Education
State educational policy includes bilingual and intercultural teaching, while school access and completion rates vary by region and household income
Universities
National University of Asunción and other public and private institutions educate professionals, researchers and teachers
Health services
Public, social-insurance and private care operate in parallel, with important access disparities in sparsely populated regions
Rural agriculture
Farming households, cooperative arrangements and seasonal labor are central to many communities outside the principal urban corridors
Urban culture
Greater Asunción, Ciudad del Este and other cities reflect internal migration, cross-border commerce and evolving youth cultures
Music
Paraguayan harp, polka paraguaya and guarania are prominent musical forms with regional and national cultural significance
Food
Chipa, sopa paraguaya, mbejú and other maize- or manioc-based foods reflect agriculture and historical cultural exchange
Tereré
Cold herbal mate infusion is an important shared social practice and intangible cultural heritage in Paraguayan life
Sport
Football is a major national sport; other athletics, basketball and community sports also attract participation
Women and public life
Gender equality, political representation, rural workloads and access to education and care remain ongoing social issues
Migration
Historical movement to Argentina, Brazil and elsewhere, alongside cross-border employment and remittances, shapes families and labor markets
Land inequality
Concentrated landownership and land disputes have long influenced agrarian policy, Indigenous claims and environmental conflict
Cultural heritage
Jesuit mission ruins, Guaraní-language traditions and regional histories represent distinct layers of national heritage
Census interpretation
INE's 2022 final population enumeration and World Bank 2025 estimate are separate products, not a direct survey of all demographic characteristics in 2025 [3] [1]

Language and identity: Guaraní is an official nationwide language used by people of varying identities; speaking it is not identical to legal Indigenous status. Religious affiliation surveys and Indigenous ethnic counts are separate indicators.

Government and Institutions

State system
Unitary presidential constitutional republic operating under the Constitution of 1992 [8]
President as of November 6, 2026
Pedro Alliana assumed the presidency following Senate removal of Santiago Peña, pursuant to the constitutional succession process [6] [7]
Former president
Santiago Peña served from August 15, 2023 to November 6, 2026; parliamentary impeachment ended his tenure before the scheduled 2028 term completion [6]
Immediate trigger
A banking and public-oversight controversy preceded impeachment; allegations and reported investigations must not be treated as criminal convictions [6] [7]
Senate decision
Paraguayan Senate officially reported the November 6, 2026 decision to separate Santiago Peña from office after a political-trial proceeding [6]
Oath and succession
Pedro Alliana, elected vice president in 2023, was sworn in as president on November 6, 2026 according to same-day reporting [7]
Executive power
President appoints ministers and heads the executive; tenure and dismissal follow constitutional limits and legislative accountability mechanisms
Legislature
Bicameral National Congress consists of the 45-member Senate and 80-member Chamber of Deputies, with elected representation
Legislative terms
Ordinary congressional mandates are five years, subject to the constitutional election and vacancy provisions
Elections
President and vice president are normally directly elected for single five-year terms; the 1992 system restricts presidential reelection [8]
Supreme Court
Supreme Court of Justice is the highest judicial body, alongside lower courts and specialized public institutions
Electoral administration
Superior Electoral Justice oversees electoral organization and voting under constitution and election law
Constitutional rights
Fundamental protections include civil liberties, due process, language rights, electoral participation and property provisions [8]
Departmental governance
Seventeen departments have elected or administrative regional structures; Asunción is a separate capital district
Municipal government
Municipalities hold local administration functions, with elected mayors and councils and responsibilities varying by law
Public finance
National budget and tax administration interact with municipalities, departmental priorities and state enterprises
Hydroelectric governance
Itaipú and Yacyretá involve binational treaties and institutions, not solely provincial or municipal utility ownership
Indigenous rights
Constitutional protections and statutory processes address collective identity and community land, with continuing implementation disputes
Security sector
Police and military perform separate roles; organized crime and border trafficking are ongoing state priorities
Financial regulation
Banking supervision and central-bank decisions are distinct from allegations of political interference, especially during the November 2026 crisis [5] [6]
Constitutional succession caveat
This edition uses a November 6, 2026 cutoff; fast-moving institutional developments must be dated when officeholders are named

Same-day update: Parliamentary removal and the Alliana succession occurred November 6, 2026. The underlying allegations about bank governance are presented as allegations or proceedings, not as judicial findings of criminal guilt.

International Profile

United Nations
Paraguay is a founding UN member under the Charter system; diplomacy operates through permanent missions and multilateral organizations [15]
Mercosur
Founding member of the Southern Common Market, with Argentina, Brazil and Uruguay as the original partners [12]
Landlocked trade
Access to ports relies heavily on the Paraguay–Paraná waterway, overland routes and customs coordination with neighbors
Brazil relations
Energy generation at Itaipú, commerce, migration and border security form major bilateral ties
Argentina relations
Yacyretá electricity, river navigation, labor migration and logistics shape bilateral economic relations
Bolivia relations
Shared Chaco frontier and the legacy of the 1932–1935 war coexist with modern cross-border cooperation
Hydropower institutions
Binational operating arrangements at Itaipú and Yacyretá are international legal frameworks as well as energy infrastructure
WTO membership
Paraguay participates in the multilateral trade system and tariff schedules, separate from Mercosur commitments [11]
Inter-American system
Member of the Organization of American States, with constitutional governance and human-rights obligations [13]
International lending
IMF, World Bank and Inter-American Development Bank maintain distinct fiscal, infrastructure and data relationships
UNESCO World Heritage
One inscription: Jesuit Missions of La Santísima Trinidad de Paraná and Jesús de Tavarangue (inscribed 1993) [10]
Tentative heritage list
Additional Paraguayan proposals are candidates for future recognition and are not separately inscribed properties [10]
Foreign investment
Agricultural processing, maquila production, services and energy-linked industry attract companies and foreign capital
Cross-border environmental affairs
Water-sharing, flood management, Chaco conservation and pollution control connect Paraguay with neighbors
International financial oversight
Regional authorities watch the effects of financial-market failures, but allegations are not established convictions [7]
Time zone
Paraguay follows UTC−3 year-round following its permanent-time reform, rather than its older seasonal switching practice
International dialing
+595 country calling code; .py internet country-code domain
Geographic placement
South America despite Guaraní language links to other parts of the continent and landlocked status [14]

Heritage comparison: UNESCO lists one Paraguay World Heritage property, comprising the named Jesuit mission complexes. Separate tentative nominations and the number of physical components do not enlarge the count of inscribed properties.

Country Overview

Uruguay, officially the Oriental Republic of Uruguay, is a constitutional republic in southeastern South America between Brazil and Argentina, with an Atlantic seaboard and a southern shoreline on the Río de la Plata. Montevideo, its capital and principal port, anchors a country marked by rolling grasslands, cattle-raising and farming, urban service industries, sustained republican institutions and extensive public education. Indigenous histories, Spanish and Portuguese frontier competition, independence-era conflict and nineteenth-century state-building preceded the modern republic.

The contemporary political system is a directly elected presidential republic with a bicameral General Assembly. Yamandú Orsi and Vice President Carolina Cosse took office on March 1, 2025. Uruguay is a founding member of MERCOSUR and a significant agricultural exporter; Uruguay XXI reported US$13.493 billion of merchandise exports in 2025, including free-zone production, led by beef, cellulose and soybeans. The national 2023 census count and World Bank 2025 population model differ by date and methodology. In 2025, 98% of national electricity generation was renewable, a metric that must not be confused with the renewable share of all energy used in transport and industry. [6] [9] [11]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Oriental Republic of Uruguay (República Oriental del Uruguay), democratic constitutional republic
Capital
Montevideo, seat of the presidency, national parliament and principal maritime institutions [2]
UN geographic region
South America in the United Nations M49 classification [14]
Indigenous past
Charrúa, Guaraní-related and other Indigenous histories predate the Spanish and Portuguese colonial frontiers
Indigenous continuity
Contemporary Indigenous-descendant identities and ancestry claims are not erased by nineteenth-century dispossession
Colonial frontier
Spanish and Portuguese authorities contested settlements and commerce along the Río de la Plata and north bank
Colonia del Sacramento
Established by Portuguese colonists in 1680, Colonia became a strategically important Atlantic–Plata frontier settlement
Montevideo foundation
Spanish urban settlement developed in the early eighteenth century as a military and trading center
Banda Oriental
Eastern-bank territory formed part of competing imperial administrative jurisdictions and regional political networks
Artigas movement
José Gervasio Artigas led a federalist political project and mobilized rural and regional supporters in the independence era
1811 revolutionary turning point
The Battle of Las Piedras and later campaigns reflected regional resistance to Spanish authorities
Portuguese-Brazilian occupation
Control from Portuguese and Brazilian forces reshaped sovereignty debates after Artigas's defeat
1825 independence declaration
The Thirty-Three Orientals and allied forces challenged Brazilian rule; the August 25, 1825 declaration is commemorated nationally
1828 independent-state settlement
The Preliminary Peace Convention between Brazil and the United Provinces recognized an independent state in the region
1830 Constitution
A republican constitution established institutions for the new Oriental Republic
Nineteenth-century parties
Blanco/National and Colorado political traditions grew through conflict, negotiation and changing political alliances
Civil wars and consolidation
Repeated armed struggles influenced rural property, regional loyalties and constitutional development
Early twentieth-century reforms
José Batlle y Ordóñez and reformers supported expanding social policy, civil institutions and state enterprises
Modern social legislation
Employment protections, secular education and public health developed unevenly through reform and industrialization
1973 authoritarian rupture
A civil-military dictatorship followed the June 1973 institutional breakdown
1985 democratic restoration
Elected civilian government returned in March 1985 following prolonged political mobilization and negotiation
Contemporary multiparty politics
Competitive coalitions and alternation among parties have structured national politics since restoration
2024 presidential runoff
Yamandú Orsi of the Frente Amplio defeated Álvaro Delgado in the November 24, 2024 presidential runoff
2025 presidential inauguration
Orsi took office March 1, 2025, accompanied by Vice President Carolina Cosse [6]
National holiday
Independence Day is observed August 25, marking the 1825 declaration rather than the 1828 international settlement
Official language
Spanish is the primary official and public language; Portuguese-related varieties are used in northern border communities
National symbols
White and blue striped flag with Sun of May, national coat of arms and anthem
UN membership
Uruguay joined the United Nations on December 18, 1945 [2]
ISO identifiers
UY (alpha-2), URY (alpha-3), 858 (numeric); internet domain .uy [14]

Independence chronology: the 1825 declaration against Brazilian rule, the 1828 international peace settlement and the 1830 constitution are separate steps. The current presidential term began March 1, 2025. [6]

Geography and Population

Total surface area
173,626 km² in the UN statistical country profile; alternative total and land-area conventions differ [2]
Land neighbors
Argentina lies west across much of the Uruguay River; Brazil borders northern and eastern land frontiers
Atlantic coast
Eastern shore includes beaches, dunes, lagoons, wetlands and economically important tourist destinations
Río de la Plata
Broad estuary forms the southern maritime frontage and links Montevideo and Colonia with Argentine ports
Uruguay River
Western river system supports hydropower, navigation, fisheries and cross-border management
Río Negro
Major internal river crosses the country and is impounded by hydroelectric reservoirs
Other waterways
Santa Lucía, Yí, Cuareim/Quaraí and numerous streams shape drainage and watershed planning
Topography
Predominantly rolling plains and low hills with extensive grasslands suited to pasture and mixed farming
Highest point
Cerro Catedral reaches approximately 514 meters, illustrating the country's relatively low relief
Cuchilla Grande
Hill and ridge systems divide catchments in the south and east of the country
Coastal lagoons
Laguna Merín/Mirim and Laguna de Rocha support wetlands, wildlife and agricultural water management
Climate
Temperate to humid subtropical conditions with seasonal variation and precipitation throughout the year
Drought cycles
Water scarcity and severe drought can affect drinking-water supplies, cattle herds, agricultural yields and hydroelectricity
Flooding
River floods and severe storms periodically disrupt roads, urban infrastructure and farming communities
Biodiversity
Pampas grasslands, coastal wetlands, river forests and native woodland shelter distinct ecosystems
Agricultural land use
Large shares of the territory support livestock grazing, crop agriculture, forestry and rural settlement
Environmental pressures
Nutrient runoff, water quality, soil degradation, drought, heat and invasive species require different measures
Administrative units
Nineteen departments with elected departmental and municipal authorities
Major cities
Montevideo, Salto, Paysandú, Las Piedras, Rivera, Maldonado, Tacuarembó and Melo are regional urban centers
Montevideo metropolis
Montevideo and adjacent Canelones form the principal population and economic concentration
2023 census total
INE's final population total was 3,499,451 people at the 2023 census reference date [3]
2023 urban population
INE's 2026 weighted census release identifies 3,324,679 urban and 174,772 rural residents [4]
2023 Montevideo population
The updated census release records approximately 1,302,721 people in the department of Montevideo [4]
2025 World Bank population
The annual international estimate was 3,384,688; it is not a new 2025 door-to-door census [1]
Population-series difference
Census reference-date estimates and international demographic models use differing inputs and revision conventions
Demographic profile
Low fertility, population aging and outward/inward migration patterns affect health, pensions and labor markets
Population density
About 19.5 people/km² using 2025 World Bank population and UN total area, but urban density is much higher
Forestry landscapes
Commercial eucalyptus and pine plantations support pulp and timber sectors while raising ecological land-use questions
Hydropower reservoirs
Rincón del Bonete and Salto Grande are major energy assets, though their generation depends on hydrology
Renewable electricity
The government reports that 98% of electricity generated in 2025 came from renewable sources [11]
Coastal tourism zones
Punta del Este, Piriápolis, Cabo Polonio and the Rocha coast have distinct environmental and development pressures

Population methodology: Uruguay’s 2023 census gives 3,499,451 residents, whereas the World Bank international model gives 3,384,688 for 2025. The numerical discrepancy is not explained by treating two different statistical systems as direct year-to-year counts. [3] [1]

Economy and International Trade

Economic model
High-income economy with agricultural exports, services, manufacturing, technology, logistics and tourism
2025 nominal GDP
US$85.35 billion in World Bank current US dollars, distinct from PPP valuation [1]
2025 GDP per capita
US$25,215.8 in nominal current dollars; this is not median household income [1]
Real GDP growth
1.8% for 2025 in the World Bank annual series [1]
Consumer-price inflation
4.7% in 2025 World Bank annual consumer-price indicator [1]
Modeled unemployment
7.5% in the ILO-modeled World Bank 2025 series; national survey rates can differ [1]
Currency
Uruguayan peso (UYU); a floating exchange rate influences imported inputs and export competitiveness [2]
Monetary authority
Banco Central del Uruguay conducts monetary policy, banking oversight and economic-statistical functions
2025 goods exports
US$13.493 billion in Uruguay XXI's annual series, up approximately 5% from 2024 [9]
Trade coverage
Uruguay XXI's goods total includes exports from free-trade zones and excludes electricity, a methodology requiring disclosure [9]
Beef exports
US$2.68 billion in 2025, the leading exported product category in the agency's reporting [9]
Beef value share
Beef represented approximately 20% of 2025 merchandise exports under Uruguay XXI's product grouping [10]
Cellulose pulp
Pulp exports are a major contribution from domestic forestry and free-zone mills, representing around 17% in 2025 [10]
Soybeans
Soy exports formed another leading product category with roughly 11% of 2025 shipments [10]
Dairy exports
Milk powder, cheeses and other dairy products link local farming to regional and international food markets
Rice exports
Irrigated rice is an important specialized crop with regional and extra-regional markets
Wool and textiles
Wool, yarn and related animal-fiber goods have historic and continuing importance in the export profile
Wood and forestry
Plantation timber supports pulp mills, wood products and associated logistics infrastructure
Services economy
Banking, retail, software, tourism, transportation, business services and public administration supply substantial output
Software and IT
Uruguay has developed internationally traded software, digital services and business-process activities
Tourism
Montevideo, Colonia and Atlantic resorts support inbound travel spending, which is separate from merchandise goods exports
Ports and logistics
Port of Montevideo, Nueva Palmira and road corridors link regional farming to overseas shipping
China as trade partner
China was the largest destination in Uruguay XXI's 2025 goods-export reporting [9]
Brazil and Argentina
Neighbors are important buyers, suppliers, tourist origins and cross-border production partners
2025 renewable generation
Renewable sources supplied 98% of electricity produced; hydro 46%, wind 34%, biomass 14%, solar 4% [11]
Electricity versus total energy
The renewable electricity share must not be described as the renewable share of all fuels used in transport and industry
Hydrological variability
Drought can reduce hydropower output and affect the trade in electricity even when structural renewable capacity is high
Investment framework
State enterprises, free-zone regimes, private exporters and infrastructure projects have different legal and fiscal roles
Social policy
Pensions, health coverage, collective bargaining and tax policy influence distribution and fiscal sustainability
Economic measurement
GDP is value added across all sectors; merchandise exports are gross traded values and should never be treated as the same quantity

People and Society

Primary language
Spanish is the dominant language in national institutions, education, media and business
Border languages
Portuguese and Uruguayan Portuguese contact varieties occur especially in northern border areas
Indigenous ancestry
The 2023 census provides measures of Indigenous ancestry that must not be conflated with a singular ethnic group or language [5]
Afro-Uruguayan communities
Afrodescendant populations have deep histories in colonial-era labor, national politics, music and civic movements
2023 ethnic self-identification
INE reports 10.6% Afrodescendant and 6.4% Indigenous ancestry responses; overlapping categories need methodological care [5]
Population aging
Low fertility, long life expectancy and migration produce an older age structure than in many other regional countries
Urbanization
Approximately 3.325 million residents were urban in the weighted 2023 census release [4]
Household composition
Smaller households and one-person households are important for housing and social-protection planning
Gender and representation
Representation and gender-based violence prevention are central areas of contemporary institutional debate
Religion
Roman Catholic heritage coexists with secularism, other faiths and substantial nonreligious identification
Secular institutions
Long-standing secular legal and educational practices play a major role in civic identity
Public education
Primary and secondary schooling operate through national public systems alongside private and technical providers
University sector
Universidad de la República is a major public university; Universidad Tecnológica and other institutions expand regional training
Digital public services
Digital government, identity and social-service infrastructure are widely developed in the region
Health services
Integrated public and private provision functions through national health reforms and insurance arrangements
Social insurance
Retirement, disability and unemployment systems confront pressures associated with population aging
Candombe
Afro-Uruguayan drum traditions and social practices are a major cultural form, with international recognition
Tango and milonga
Río de la Plata musical histories connect Uruguay with Argentina while maintaining distinctive performers and institutions
Literature
Mario Benedetti, Juan Carlos Onetti, Idea Vilariño and other writers have international literary significance
Visual culture
Painting, theater, film and carnival traditions preserve regional and national histories
Carnival
Montevideo carnival, murga performances and neighborhood participation are significant cultural activities
Cuisine
Asado, chivito, mate and regional dairy and pastry foods reflect pastoral and urban traditions
Football
Uruguay won men's FIFA World Cups in 1930 and 1950 and hosted the inaugural 1930 tournament
Other sports
Basketball, rugby, cycling and athletics have organized participation and national histories
Displacement and memory
Political prisoners, exiles and victims of the 1973–1985 dictatorship remain subjects of historical memory and justice efforts
Social inequality
Urban-peripheral poverty, education outcomes, disability inclusion and rural access remain active policy challenges
Housing
Informal settlements, affordability, tenancy rules and coastal development create distinct local problems
Migration
Regional arrivals and departures affect labor markets, demography and social services
Cultural heritage
Colonia del Sacramento, Fray Bentos and the Church of Atlántida are UNESCO-inscribed cultural sites [13]

2023 demographic percentages use INE self-identification categories and may overlap; they must not be forced to sum to 100%. National household and urban-rural figures refer to the 2023 census and later-weighted release rather than a 2026 enumeration. [5] [4]

Government and Institutions

Constitutional system
Presidential representative democracy under the 1967 Constitution as amended by subsequent reforms
Head of state and government
President is both head of state and chief executive, directly elected under national electoral rules
President
Yamandú Orsi assumed the presidency March 1, 2025, for the 2025–2030 term [6]
Vice president
Carolina Cosse took office on the same date and presides over the Senate under constitutional arrangements [6]
Presidential runoff
The November 2024 runoff produced a Frente Amplio victory after the October first round
Executive term
Constitutional presidential term is five years; immediate consecutive presidential reelection is prohibited
Cabinet
Ministers direct portfolios through an executive system with legislative oversight and administrative controls
General Assembly
Asamblea General is bicameral, consisting of the Senate and House of Representatives [7]
Senate
Thirty elected senators serve; the vice president presides and participates in constitutional chamber operations [7]
House of Representatives
Ninety-nine deputies represent electoral constituencies in the lower chamber [7]
2024 legislative election
Parliamentary elections were held October 27, 2024, on the national electoral calendar [8]
Political competition
Frente Amplio, National Party, Colorado Party and others organize electoral and parliamentary competition
Separation of powers
Courts and oversight institutions exercise roles separate from the elected executive and legislature
Supreme Court
Supreme Court of Justice is a senior judicial body within a multiparty constitutional state
Electoral Court
Corte Electoral coordinates election procedures, registration and authoritative electoral results
Administrative court
Tribunal de lo Contencioso Administrativo has specialist jurisdiction over administrative acts
Audit body
Tribunal de Cuentas provides institutional examination of public accounting and expenditure
Department governments
Nineteen elected departmental administrations manage significant local roads, development and service functions
Municipal governance
Municipalities with elected officials operate below departments under decentralization arrangements
Rule of law
Constitutional civil rights and judicial processes shape accountability for institutions and individuals
1973–1985 accountability
Historical rights violations during dictatorship remain the subject of memorialization and judicial proceedings
Labor relations
Collective bargaining councils and labor regulation structure relations between workers and employers
Public enterprises
State enterprises have major roles in utilities, finance and other strategic services
Social legislation
Policy debates concern pensions, education, security, health and tax distribution
Climate and water policy
Cross-institutional coordination governs climate adaptation, potable water and electricity transition
External policy
Presidency and ministries coordinate MERCOSUR negotiations, multilateral diplomacy and investment

Legislature: Uruguay has 30 elected senators and 99 deputies, with the elected vice president presiding over the Senate. Do not mistake the 30 elected Senate seats for 31 separately elected senators. [7]

International Profile

United Nations
Uruguay became a UN member on December 18, 1945 [2]
MERCOSUR foundation
Uruguay signed the March 26, 1991 Treaty of Asunción with Argentina, Brazil and Paraguay [12]
MERCOSUR Secretariat
The MERCOSUR Secretariat is based in Montevideo, underscoring Uruguay's regional institutional role
Southern Cone diplomacy
Brazil, Argentina and Paraguay are major counterparts in commerce, security and river management
Río de la Plata cooperation
Navigation, ports, fisheries and environmental protection require international agreements with Argentina
Uruguay River institutions
Binational water and hydropower projects connect local communities and regional energy planning
Atlantic gateway
Montevideo and Nueva Palmira link interior South American supply chains to maritime routes
China
China remains a leading export destination, notably for beef and agro-industrial commodities [9]
European Union
European buyers and investors participate in beef, pulp, financial and trade relationships
International trade system
Uruguay participates in WTO commitments and negotiated regional trade arrangements
UNESCO properties
Three World Heritage inscriptions: Historic Quarter of Colonia, Fray Bentos Industrial Landscape and Eladio Dieste's Church of Atlántida [13]
UNESCO classification
All three properties are classified as cultural; tentative sites should not be counted as inscriptions [13]
Ramsar and wetlands
Wetland conservation involves cross-border habitats and international biodiversity frameworks
International peacekeeping
Uruguay has a history of contributing personnel to United Nations peace operations
Democracy and diplomacy
Electoral stability and human-rights institutions underpin foreign-relations priorities
Renewable-energy profile
Uruguay's 2025 national generation mix was 98% renewable, led by hydro and wind [11]
Climate commitments
Agricultural methane, land use, renewable energy and adaptation affect climate mitigation planning
Forest sector
Forestry and pulp trade connect corporate investment to international environmental and labor standards
Migration links
Long-standing connections to Argentina, Spain, Brazil and other countries shape family and business networks
World Bank statistics
World Bank national indicators are suitable for cross-country comparison when dates and revisions are disclosed [1]
2023 census evidence
Detailed national demographic planning uses INE figures, which need not equal international 2025 model results [3]
Tourism diplomacy
Colonia, Montevideo and Atlantic coastal areas support cultural exchange, environmental management and trade in services
Regional energy
Electricity exports and hydropower coordination should be measured separately from domestic electricity consumption
Time zone
Uruguay Standard Time (UYT), UTC−3, with no general contemporary daylight-saving adjustment
International dialing
Country code +598; national internet domain .uy

Energy accounting: the 98% renewable figure refers to electricity generated in calendar 2025; it is not the renewable share of all energy consumed nationally. UNESCO lists three World Heritage cultural properties. [11] [13]

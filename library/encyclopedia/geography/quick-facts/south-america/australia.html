Country Overview

Australia is a continental country and federal parliamentary democracy spanning its large mainland, Tasmania and numerous offshore islands between the Indian and Pacific oceans. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have maintained distinct societies, languages, legal traditions and connections to Country for tens of thousands of years. British colonization from 1788 produced profound dispossession, while six colonies federated as the Commonwealth of Australia in 1901. Today Canberra is the federal capital and Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth and Adelaide are major population and economic centers. Services, resource exports, agriculture, manufacturing, international education and tourism connect a high-income economy to Asian and global markets. Australia operates as a constitutional monarchy with King Charles III as sovereign, Governor-General Sam Mostyn and Prime Minister Anthony Albanese, whose Labor government won reelection in May 2025. The 2025 World Bank economic series, Australia’s March 2026 official resident-population estimate and calendar-year trade publications refer to different periods and measures.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Commonwealth of Australia, a sovereign federal parliamentary democracy and constitutional monarchy [15]
Capital
Canberra, established as the federal capital within the Australian Capital Territory [3]
UN geographic region
Oceania; the Australian landmass spans an entire continent and nearby islands [16]
First Peoples
Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander nations sustain long-standing cultures, relationships to Country and diverse political traditions
Ancient habitation
Archaeological evidence places human habitation of the continent at least tens of thousands of years before European colonization
Language diversity before colonization
Hundreds of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander languages and varieties existed, many with strong continuing revival programs
Dutch exploration
Seventeenth-century Dutch voyagers mapped sections of the northern and western coasts
British eastern-coast voyages
James Cook charted sections of the eastern coastline in 1770, without representing first discovery by human beings
First Fleet and 1788 colonization
British ships established a penal colony at Sydney Cove in 1788 on lands inhabited by First Peoples
Colonial expansion
Pastoral settlement, extraction, frontier violence and introduced diseases caused dispossession and severe demographic disruption
Nineteenth-century colonies
Separate British colonies formed political institutions and increasingly responsible government before federation
Gold rushes
Mid-nineteenth-century discoveries accelerated migration, urbanization and investment while intensifying contest over land
Federation date
Six colonies formed the Commonwealth of Australia on January 1, 1901 under the federal Constitution [15]
Immigration Restriction Act
Legislation in 1901 established a central pillar of the racially exclusionary White Australia policy, later dismantled
First World War
Australian forces served alongside other Allied troops, including at Gallipoli and on the Western Front
Second World War
Pacific warfare, U.S. strategic cooperation and regional defense transformed Australian security policy
Statute of Westminster
Australia adopted the statute in 1942, retroactive to 1939, clarifying legislative independence from Britain
Postwar immigration
Large-scale migration from Europe and later other world regions transformed cities, industry and cultural life
1967 constitutional referendum
Voters approved amendments allowing Commonwealth legislation for Aboriginal people and their inclusion in national population counts
Mabo decision
The 1992 High Court judgment rejected terra nullius as a foundation for denying native title in the common law
Native Title Act
The 1993 statute established a legal framework for native-title claims and related procedures
Australia Acts
Legislation taking effect in 1986 ended remaining major UK legislative and judicial links
2008 national apology
The Australian Parliament issued a formal apology to the Stolen Generations
2023 Voice referendum
A national referendum proposing an Indigenous Voice to Parliament was defeated; the vote did not erase existing First Nations rights
2025 federal election
Labor secured a new governing mandate under Prime Minister Anthony Albanese in May 2025 [10]
National day
Australia Day is observed on January 26, a date also contested as Invasion Day and associated with colonization
Official language status
English is the predominant national language in practice; Australia has no single constitutionally designated national official language
National identifiers
AU (ISO alpha-2), AUS (alpha-3), 036 (numeric) and .au internet country-code domain [16]

State-formation chronology: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander presence long predates the 1788 British settlement, the 1901 federal union and later legislative milestones. Federation did not confer equal rights or immediately terminate British constitutional links.

Country Overview

Fiji is an island republic in the South Pacific whose inhabited archipelago is centered on Viti Levu and Vanua Levu, with Suva as the capital. Long-standing Indigenous iTaukei communities developed complex maritime, chiefly and kinship networks before intensifying European contact and British colonial rule from 1874. Colonial plantation policies brought indentured workers from British India, helping create Fiji’s present multilingual and multireligious society. Independence followed in 1970, while a series of coups and constitutional changes shaped modern politics. The 2013 Constitution and 2022 parliamentary election provided the framework for a coalition government led by Prime Minister Sitiveni Rabuka, with President Ratu Naiqama Lalabalavu serving as head of state. A referendum was announced for December 9, 2026, which is still in the future at this profile’s cutoff. Tourism, agriculture, fisheries, remittances, services and industrial exports support a comparatively diversified Pacific economy. The 2017 national census, World Bank 2025 population series and Fiji Bureau of Statistics merchandise records have different definitions and reporting dates.

Country Overview

Ecuador is a republic on the equator in northwestern South America, bordering Colombia, Peru and the Pacific Ocean. Its four commonly described geographic regions are the coastal lowlands, Andean highlands, Amazonian east and the Galápagos Islands. Indigenous civilizations, the Inca expansion, Spanish colonial administration, eighteenth- and nineteenth-century independence campaigns and later constitutional reforms have shaped the nation. Quito is the capital, while Guayaquil is a major economic center and seaport. Ecuador adopted the United States dollar in 2000 after a severe financial crisis and now exports shrimp, bananas, cocoa, gold, flowers, oil and other products. The current Constitution dates from 2008 and recognizes Ecuador as an intercultural and plurinational state. Daniel Noboa began the current elected presidential term in May 2025. The 2022 resident census and 2025 World Bank demographic series are separate reference measures.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Ecuador (República del Ecuador), a unitary presidential republic with a constitution describing a plurinational and intercultural state [8]
Capital
Quito (San Francisco de Quito), a high-Andean capital with nationally important administrative and heritage institutions
Major commercial city
Guayaquil is a principal coastal seaport and commercial center, with a distinctive history in the independence movement
Geographic classification
United Nations M49 South America; the country is named for the equatorial line crossing its territory [17]
Precolonial societies
Valdivia, Jama-Coaque, La Tolita, Cañari, Quitu and other cultural formations occupied different territories before Spanish conquest
Inca expansion
The Tawantinsuyu incorporated extensive Andean territories in what is now Ecuador, while other peoples retained distinct regional identities
Spanish conquest
Sixteenth-century campaigns transformed Indigenous political systems and incorporated the territory into Spanish imperial institutions
Colonial Quito
The Real Audiencia of Quito was an important administrative jurisdiction, with economies based on agriculture, textile work and regional trade
First Quito independence movement
August 10, 1809, commemorates Quito's revolutionary junta and an early call for autonomy, rather than immediate establishment of the modern republic [12]
Guayaquil independence
October 9, 1820: Guayaquil's proclamation advanced regional emancipation before the decisive Andean campaigns [12]
Battle of Pichincha
May 24, 1822: Antonio José de Sucre's victory near Quito helped secure independence from Spanish rule in the highlands [12]
Gran Colombia membership
Ecuadorian provinces became part of the wider Republic of Colombia associated with Simón Bolívar during the 1820s
Independent Ecuadorian republic
Ecuador separated from Gran Colombia and established its own republican political order in 1830 [12]
Nineteenth-century state formation
Conflicts among highland and coastal elites, church–state relationships and landholding systems marked republican consolidation
Liberal Revolution
The 1895 liberal transformation associated with Eloy Alfaro advanced secular public institutions and economic modernization
Twentieth-century politics
Social movements, changes of government, Amazonian resource development and border disputes shaped state and society
Peace with Peru
The 1998 Brasília Act addressed the long-running border conflict between Ecuador and Peru
Dollarization
Ecuador adopted the U.S. dollar as the monetary unit in 2000 after severe banking and exchange-rate instability [11]
2008 constitutional framework
Montecristi Constitution affirmed rights of nature, social guarantees, and plurinational and intercultural principles [8]
Presidential transition 2023
Daniel Noboa first assumed office in November 2023 for the remainder of an abbreviated term
2025 elected presidential term
Daniel Noboa and Vice President María José Pinto were sworn in May 24, 2025 for the 2025–2029 term [7]
National celebrations
August 10 commemorates Quito's 1809 proclamation; May 24 marks the Battle of Pichincha, and October 9 honors Guayaquil's independence
National flag
Yellow, blue and red horizontal stripes with the coat of arms on the national/state flag, distinct from neighboring tricolors
Constitutional language status
Spanish is the official language, while Kichwa and Shuar are official languages of intercultural relations; other ancestral languages have official use in Indigenous areas [8]
UN membership
Ecuador became an original United Nations member in 1945 [18]
ISO identifiers
EC (alpha-2), ECU (alpha-3), 218 (numeric); .ec internet country-code domain [17]

State formation: The Quito junta in 1809, Guayaquil proclamation in 1820, Battle of Pichincha in 1822 and establishment of a separate Ecuadorian republic in 1830 were different milestones. Ecuador’s present Constitution dates from 2008, and Daniel Noboa’s current elected term began in May 2025, after his earlier shortened 2023–2025 presidency.

Geography and Population

Physical location
Northwestern South America with Pacific coast, Andean ranges, Amazonian watersheds and oceanic Galápagos Islands
International area reference
257,217 km² of surface area in UNData geographic reporting; specialized land-only measures may differ [2]
Mainland borders
Colombia lies to the north and Peru borders Ecuador to the east and south; the Pacific bounds its west
Coastal region
La Costa comprises coastal lowlands, mangroves, Guayaquil's estuary and agriculturally important river plains
Highland region
La Sierra encompasses inter-Andean basins and volcanic highlands containing Quito, Cuenca, Riobamba and other cities
Amazon region
El Oriente includes tropical forests, Indigenous territories and energy-producing districts linked to the Amazon Basin
Insular region
Galápagos Islands lie far west of mainland Ecuador, with exceptional endemic species and strict conservation programs [13]
Andean volcanoes
Cotopaxi, Tungurahua, Sangay and other volcanoes are important geophysical features with distinct eruption histories
Highest mountain
Chimborazo reaches about 6,263 metres above sea level; its equatorial location makes its summit exceptionally distant from Earth's center
Cotopaxi
Prominent stratovolcano near Quito with substantial monitoring, conservation and tourism significance
Guayas River basin
Major agricultural and urban drainage system supporting Guayaquil and coastal production
Amazon watersheds
Napo, Pastaza and other river systems flow east toward the Amazon, connecting rainforest communities and ecosystems
Pacific waters
Coastal waters support fisheries, shipping and the productive marine environments associated with ocean currents
Equatorial position
Crosses the equator, yet altitude and ocean circulation create strong variation in local climate
Climate zones
Tropical Pacific lowlands, cool highland valleys, páramo, wet Amazon rainforest and seasonally dry Galápagos conditions
Seismic risk
Nazca Plate subduction creates damaging earthquakes and tsunami exposure along the Pacific margin
Volcanic hazards
Ashfall, lahars and volcanic activity require specialized civil protection and geological monitoring
Rainfall variability
El Niño events can alter coastal rainfall, flooding, fisheries and agricultural conditions
Biodiversity
Cloud forests, mangroves, Amazon rainforest, Andean páramo and Galápagos habitats have high conservation importance
Protected areas
Galápagos National Park and Marine Reserve, Yasuni, Sangay and other reserves support environmental governance [13]
2022 resident census
INEC counted 16,938,986 usual residents in the 2022 census; national tabulations provide sex, age and self-identification breakdowns [3]
2010 census comparison
Ecuador had 14,459,077 usual residents in the corresponding 2010 reference count, illustrating population growth across censuses [3]
2025 World Bank estimate
18,289,896 residents in the international annual estimate, not a new direct census [1]
Population density
Approximately 71.1 persons per km² when 2025 World Bank population is divided by the 257,217-km² country reference
Major cities
Quito, Guayaquil, Cuenca, Santo Domingo, Ambato, Manta, Portoviejo, Loja and Machala
Administrative organization
Twenty-four provinces subdivided into cantons and rural and urban parishes; local authorities have constitutionally allocated responsibilities [8]
Coast–highlands distribution
The 2022 census places more than half of residents in coastal provinces and a substantial share in the Andean region [3]
2022 median age
Approximately 29 years in the 2022 census, reflecting a younger population than many high-income countries [3]
Population methods
A 2022 census enumeration and a 2025 annual international estimate have different reference dates and estimation methods [3] [1]

Population comparability: INEC directly counted 16,938,986 usual residents in 2022, while the World Bank records 18,289,896 in its modeled 2025 annual series. The UNData 257,217-km² reference includes insular territory; using land-only denominators or excluding the Galápagos changes density and area comparisons.

Economy and International Trade

Economic structure
Dollarized upper-middle-income economy reliant on services, agriculture, aquaculture, oil, mining and trade
Nominal GDP
US$130.32 billion in the 2025 World Bank current-dollar series [1]
GDP per capita
US$7,125.3 for 2025, national output per resident rather than typical wages or household income [1]
Annual real GDP growth
3.7% during 2025 under the World Bank comparable series [1]
Consumer-price inflation
0.7% in the 2025 World Bank series, in the context of a dollarized currency regime [1]
ILO-modeled unemployment
3.3% in 2025, a modeled indicator that does not capture all underemployment or informality [1]
National legal tender
United States dollar (USD), adopted in 2000; the state does not operate an independent floating national currency [11]
Central bank function
Banco Central del Ecuador has financial-system, reserve, payment and statistics functions under Ecuador's dollarized framework [11]
Economic geography
Guayaquil and coastal provinces host ports, agriculture and aquaculture; Quito and the Sierra host administration, industry and services
Shrimp aquaculture
Farmed shrimp production and processing have become exceptionally important for merchandise exports and coastal employment
Banana and plantain exports
One of the country's major long-standing agricultural sectors with global value chains and labor and disease-management questions
Cocoa industry
Cocoa beans and processed cocoa products link small producers and exporters to international chocolate manufacturing
Oil extraction
Amazonian crude production and pipelines connect oil-producing regions to the coast, with environmental and Indigenous-rights challenges
Mineral industries
Gold and copper concentrates are increasingly important merchandise categories under regulatory and environmental scrutiny
Flower exports
Cut roses and other flowers from Andean districts travel in refrigerated logistics chains to overseas consumers
Tuna and seafood processing
Tuna, fish and processed seafood accompany shrimp as important marine-related exports
2025 total merchandise exports
US$37.152 billion FOB in the Central Bank of Ecuador's calendar-year 2025 merchandise-trade report [4]
2025 petroleum exports
US$7.7501 billion for crude oil plus petroleum derivatives according to BCE annual classification [4]
2025 nonpetroleum exports
Approximately US$29.4019 billion by subtraction of BCE's petroleum total from total exports; rounding and reporting scope retained [4]
2025 shrimp export value
US$8.4013 billion, exceeding the combined annual petroleum export value under BCE's 2025 classification [4]
2025 shrimp share
Roughly 22.6% of Ecuador's total 2025 merchandise exports, as reported by the central bank [4]
2025 oil export changes
Annual petroleum-export earnings fell about 19% versus 2024, influenced by prices, volume and infrastructure interruptions [4]
Nonpetroleum export mix
Shrimp, cocoa, bananas, mining outputs, flowers, tuna and processed products support export diversification
Main export destinations
United States, EU markets, China and other buyers are major partners; detailed ranking varies by year and national account [5]
Tourism
Galápagos, Quito, Cuenca, Amazon lodges and coastal tourism generate international services earnings that are excluded from customs goods values
Other international services
Transportation, telecommunications, professional activities and travel receipts are separately reported in balance-of-payments accounts
Import structure
Fuels, industrial machinery, vehicles, pharmaceuticals, chemicals and intermediate inputs are major purchase categories
Trade and ports
Guayaquil–Posorja logistics, Pacific ports, transport corridors and customs infrastructure connect production to foreign markets
Andean Community
Ecuador participates in the Andean trading system with Bolivia, Colombia and Peru [15]
Dollarization implications
Currency stability limits independent exchange-rate adjustment; fiscal policy, productivity and external liquidity are important economic constraints
Employment challenges
Informality, rural poverty, crime-related economic effects and youth employment remain prominent policy concerns
Energy and climate
Hydropower, oil revenues, drought-sensitive electricity, renewable investment and resilience feature in energy planning
Measurement distinctions
GDP per capita in current US dollars, annual customs exports in US dollars and national balance-of-payments services are different data systems

People and Society

Spanish-language majority
Spanish is the principal language of national administration, commerce and schooling
Kichwa
An Indigenous language family used across Andean regions and in certain Amazon communities, with constitutional official status for intercultural relations [8]
Shuar
Indigenous language of Amazonian communities, likewise constitutionally recognized for intercultural relations [8]
Other ancestral languages
Waorani, Achuar, Siona, Secoya, Tsáfiki and other languages belong to distinct Indigenous peoples and regions
Plurinational identity
The 2008 Constitution recognizes Ecuador as intercultural and plurinational, without making its many Indigenous nations a single identity [8]
2022 self-identified mestizo
77.5% of residents reported mestizo identity under the 2022 census self-identification categories [3]
2022 self-identified Indigenous
7.7% of residents identified as Indigenous in the same census question [3]
2022 self-identified Montubio
7.7% identified as Montubio, a distinct regional ethnocultural identity associated with coastal rural histories [3]
2022 self-identified Afro-Ecuadorian
4.8% reported Afro-Ecuadorian identification in the 2022 census; census category definitions matter [3]
Other self-identifications
White and other categories are also reported; the questionnaire's classifications should not be interpreted as genetic ancestry [3]
Regional identities
Coastal, Andean, Amazonian and Galápagos communities have different cultural, linguistic and economic patterns
Religion
Roman Catholic and evangelical Christian communities are influential alongside Indigenous spiritual traditions and other faiths
Religious freedom
The constitution protects freedom of conscience and worship and recognizes cultural plurality [8]
Religion-survey caveat
No unsupported 2026 official national religion shares are inferred from historical religion surveys or ethnic census categories
Education
National and local education institutions provide Spanish-language schooling and selected intercultural bilingual initiatives
Universities
Central University of Ecuador, University of Cuenca, Escuela Politécnica Nacional and other institutions support higher education and research
Public healthcare
Ministry of Public Health services coexist with social-security and private healthcare providers, with regional disparities
Migration
Ecuadorians abroad, remittances and migration from nearby countries shape the economy and social services; the balance changes by year
2022 urban share
INEC characterized about 63.1% of residents as urban in the 2022 census, under its urban–rural settlement definitions [3]
2022 household and age change
INEC noted a median age of about 29 and fertility decline compared with earlier censuses [3]
Music and literature
Pasillo, sanjuanito, marimba and Indigenous musical traditions coexist with varied contemporary literary and artistic production
Cultural heritage
Quito, Cuenca, Galápagos and the trans-Andean road system represent different internationally inscribed cultural and natural assets [13]
Cuisine
Plantains, ceviche, maize, potatoes, cacao, regional soups and highland and coastal dishes illustrate geographic influences
Environmental communities
Indigenous forest territories, protected-area governance and Galápagos communities are central to debates about conservation and resource development
Citizen security
Organized crime, prison security, public trust and protection of rights have become major national policy concerns

Census interpretation: The 2022 percentages for mestizo, Indigenous, Montubio and Afro-Ecuadorian are self-identified groups from INEC’s census. They do not establish ancestry or religious affiliation, and no current religious percentage is invented where a comparable official religious census is unavailable.

Government and Institutions

Form of state
Unitary presidential constitutional republic defined as democratic, intercultural and plurinational under the 2008 charter [8]
Constitutional order
2008 Constitution, subsequently amended, guarantees fundamental rights and rights of nature alongside specified branches of state authority [8]
Head of state and government
President of the Republic combines both executive roles under the presidential system
Current president
Daniel Noboa Azín began the current 2025–2029 term on May 24, 2025 after reelection [7]
Current vice president
María José Pinto González-Artigas was sworn in on May 24, 2025 for the same term [7]
Earlier Noboa term
Noboa first became president in November 2023 for the abbreviated remainder of the preceding term
National Assembly
Unicameral Asamblea Nacional is the national legislature, with elected members apportioned by electoral law [9]
Assembly and presidency
Legislature enacts laws and exercises oversight; it is institutionally separate from president and cabinet [8]
Executive ministries
Cabinet ministers oversee portfolios such as economy, health, education, interior, defense and foreign relations
Electoral institutions
National Electoral Council administers national elections; the Electoral Disputes Tribunal resolves specified electoral legal matters [10]
Judicial system
National Court of Justice and lower courts, with constitutionally specified councils and judicial responsibilities
Constitutional Court
Exercises constitutional interpretation and constitutional review under the 2008 charter [8]
Rights of nature
Constitutional provisions recognize nature as a bearer of certain rights, distinct from ordinary national park management [8]
Citizen participation
Participatory rights, constitutional remedies and local consultation mechanisms appear in the 2008 framework
Territorial administration
Twenty-four provinces, cantons and parishes, with autonomous decentralized governments exercising local competences
Provincial leadership
Provincial prefects and councils administer devolved functions under constitution and organic legislation
Municipal leadership
Mayors and municipal councils administer urban services, planning and community responsibilities
Indigenous justice
The Constitution provides scope for Indigenous authorities' justice under constitutionally defined conditions and safeguards [8]
Galápagos special regime
The islands have a special territorial-government and environmental management framework distinct from an ordinary mainland province [14]
Monetary governance
Dollarization limits standard national currency issuance; the central bank retains statutory financial functions [11]
Public-security governance
Police, defense forces, prosecutors and civilian authorities have distinct roles and legal responsibilities
Political accountability
Elections, legislative oversight, courts and rights remedies provide formal checks subject to practical implementation
2017 and 2025 constitutional consultations
Referendums and popular consultations have been used to debate institutional reforms; legal status must be dated to enacted measures, not proposals
Period of government
The incumbent presidential term runs from May 2025 to May 2029 unless altered by constitutionally valid processes [7]
Officeholder verification
National Assembly and Presidency records identify the current executive officials as of the November 2026 editorial cutoff

Constitutional distinction: Ecuador is legally described as a plurinational, intercultural state, with five constitutionally recognized functions of state; this differs from assuming an ethnically homogeneous unitary state. The Galápagos has a special administrative regime and the president and vice president took office for the 2025–2029 period on May 24, 2025.

International Profile

United Nations
Ecuador was an original member of the United Nations in 1945 [18]
Andean Community
Member alongside Bolivia, Colombia and Peru, with shared trade and mobility frameworks [15]
Organization of American States
Participates in hemispheric diplomacy and political cooperation
World Trade Organization
Member of the multilateral trade framework governing international merchandise and services trade [6]
Pacific relationships
Coastal trade links Ecuador with the United States, Asia and other Pacific-basin markets
Relations with Colombia
Border trade, migration, forest conservation and security issues connect the northern Andean neighbors
Relations with Peru
Bilateral ties include the 1998 border settlement, joint river systems, Andean commerce and transport
Amazon cooperation
Participates in regional forest and river-basin cooperation with neighboring Amazon states [16]
Galápagos international interest
Island biodiversity has worldwide scientific and conservation significance and is protected through domestic and international measures [13]
UNESCO World Heritage
Five inscribed properties, including Quito, Cuenca, Galápagos, Sangay and the transnational Qhapaq Ñan [13]
Galápagos UNESCO count
The Galápagos Islands are one listed property, despite multiple components and changes to protected-area boundaries [13]
International trade
Main exports are shrimp, petroleum, cocoa, bananas and other products under BCE's 2025 merchandise accounts [4]
Currency diplomacy
Ecuador has no separate floating currency; external relations and import costs are denominated substantially in U.S. dollars
Climate and environment
Mountain, Amazonian and marine biodiversity place Ecuador in international conservation and climate negotiations
Time zones
Mainland Ecuador follows UTC−5; Galápagos uses UTC−6, with local scheduling and travel differences
Telephone and internet
+593 international dialing code and .ec top-level domain
Regional development issues
Pacific fisheries, Amazon forest protection, migration, security, disaster resilience and trade diversification are important in diplomacy

UNESCO list: UNESCO counts five Ecuadorian inscribed properties, not five plus every component of the Galápagos or Qhapaq Ñan. New Tentative List nominations in 2026 are proposals for possible future inscription and do not increase the currently inscribed total.

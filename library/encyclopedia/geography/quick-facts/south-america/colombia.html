Country Overview

Colombia is a presidential republic in northwestern South America and the only South American country with coastlines on both the Caribbean Sea and the Pacific Ocean. Its territory spans three Andean cordilleras, Caribbean coastal plains, Pacific rainforests, the Llanos and the Amazon Basin. Indigenous civilizations, Spanish colonial rule, independence struggles, the dissolution of Gran Colombia and long-running debates over land, state authority and social inclusion shaped the modern nation. Bogotá is the capital and largest metropolitan center. Petroleum, coal, coffee, gold, cut flowers, agriculture, manufacturing and services contribute to the economy. The 1991 constitution established a rights-based social state of law and recognized cultural diversity. Colombia has undergone a new presidential administration since August 7, 2026. Population figures from the 2018 census and the 2025 World Bank annual estimate are intentionally distinguished.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Colombia (República de Colombia), a unitary constitutional republic [8]
National capital
Bogotá, Distrito Capital, seat of the national executive, Congress and high courts [8]
Continent and UN region
South America; country reaches both the Caribbean and Pacific coasts and is part of UN M49 South America [17]
Precolonial political worlds
Muisca societies in the Andean highlands, Tairona polities of the Sierra Nevada, and numerous other Indigenous communities preceded colonization
Indigenous continuity
Wayuu, Nasa, Embera, Kogi, Arhuaco, Tikuna and many other communities maintain distinct territorial, cultural and linguistic histories
Spanish conquest and New Granada
The colonial New Kingdom of Granada and later Viceroyalty of New Granada linked Andean cities, Atlantic ports and wider imperial commerce
Colonial slavery and resistance
African enslavement and the creation of Afro-descendant communities, including palenques, profoundly affected the Caribbean and Pacific regions
Bogotá uprising
July 20, 1810 commemorates the uprising and revolutionary junta in Santafé, an initial independence milestone rather than completion of liberation [11]
Military independence
Bolívar's victory at Boyacá on August 7, 1819 secured a decisive military result in New Granada's independence campaign [11]
Gran Colombia era
The Republic of Colombia created in 1819 encompassed territories of several present-day states and later fragmented in the early 1830s
New Granada and later state names
Political reorganization in the nineteenth century involved several constitutional republics and successive territorial arrangements
1886 Constitution
A long-lived centralized constitutional order continued, with major change finally introduced in 1991
Panama separation
Panama separated from Colombia in 1903 amid international strategic and commercial competition
La Violencia
Mid-twentieth-century partisan violence caused major human suffering and displacement, shaping rural and national political structures
National Front period
Conservative and Liberal arrangements beginning in 1958 sought to end partisan war but also restricted political competition
Armed conflict
Guerrilla, paramilitary and state security operations shaped decades of conflict, civilian victimization and regional displacement
1991 Constitution
Replaced the 1886 charter; established Colombia as a social state of law, recognized pluralism and expanded rights enforcement [8]
2016 peace agreement
Government and FARC-EP reached an accord that initiated disarmament and institutional commitments, though other armed violence and implementation issues persist [12]
2022–2026 presidency
Gustavo Petro served the 2022–2026 presidential term; he is not the incumbent as of November 2026 [6]
2026 transfer of power
Abelardo Gabriel de la Espriella Otero was sworn in August 7, 2026 for a term scheduled through August 7, 2030 [6]
National holidays
July 20 marks the 1810 independence process; August 7 commemorates the Battle of Boyacá
Flag and symbols
Yellow, blue and red national tricolor; coat of arms features a condor and other republican imagery
Language status
Spanish is the official national language; Indigenous and other ethnic languages have official status within their territories under constitutional provisions [8]
UN membership
One of the 1945 founding members of the United Nations [18]
ISO and country domain
CO (alpha-2), COL (alpha-3), 170 (numeric); .co top-level domain [17]

Historical interpretation: July 20, 1810, August 7, 1819 and the emergence of Gran Colombia correspond to different steps in emancipation and state formation. The August 7, 2026 presidency is the current administration, not a continuation of the 2022–2026 term. The 2016 peace accord did not end all armed violence.

Geography and Population

Location
Northwestern South America, bordered by Panama, Venezuela, Brazil, Peru and Ecuador, with Caribbean and Pacific maritime frontiers
Land/surface reference
Approximately 1,141,748 km² of terrestrial national territory under commonly cited international geographic measures [2]
Maritime-zone caveat
Colombian sovereign rights in maritime zones are governed by different legal rules; adding ocean jurisdictions to land area is not comparable
Andean cordilleras
Western, Central and Eastern cordilleras divide western Colombia into high mountains, valleys and upland plateaus
Magdalena River valley
Major transport and settlement corridor between Andean ranges that drains north into the Caribbean Sea
Cauca River
Important western interior river basin associated with agriculture, hydropower, settlement and regional ecological pressures
Eastern plains
Llanos Orientales in Orinoquía include seasonally flooded grasslands, cattle production and parts of oil-producing regions
Amazon region
Southern forests and river systems connect Colombia to Peru and Brazil and support high biological and cultural diversity
Pacific lowlands
Chocó and adjoining coastal forest regions have exceptionally high rainfall and major Afro-Colombian and Indigenous communities
Caribbean mainland
Northern coastal plain includes cities, ports, wetlands and landscapes of the Magdalena delta and Guajira Peninsula
Insular territory
San Andrés, Providencia and Santa Catalina are Caribbean islands with a distinctive Raizal society and legal-cultural history
Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta
Isolated high mountain system near the Caribbean with strong Indigenous cultural significance
Highest mountain regions
Snow-covered peaks of the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta reach about 5,700 m; precise highest-peak measurements and rankings vary
Volcanoes
Nevado del Ruiz, Galeras and other Andean volcanoes pose eruption and lahar risks in populated regions
Seismic risk
Subduction, faults and mountainous terrain produce earthquake, landslide and volcanic hazards
Floods and storms
Rainfall variability, El Niño/La Niña and Caribbean storms affect coastal and mountain settlements differently
Main ecosystems
Tropical rainforest, páramo, wetlands, Andean forest, savanna, dry Caribbean habitats and coastal mangroves
Paramos and water
High-altitude páramo ecosystems regulate water supplies for urban areas and biodiversity networks
Natural resources
Petroleum, coal, gold, nickel, emeralds, hydropower potential, agricultural land and forests are spatially concentrated
2018 directly enumerated census
DANE reports 44,164,417 persons effectively enumerated in the 2018 census [3]
2018 adjusted census population
DANE's preliminary 2018 census population including estimated omissions was 48,258,494; not the raw enumeration [3]
2025 World Bank estimate
53,425,635 people in the annual international population series [1]
2018–2025 data comparison
Difference between adjusted census count and later modeled population incorporates timing and methodology and should not be read as direct net migration [3] [1]
Calculated population density
About 46.8 residents per km² using 2025 World Bank population and the 1.14-million-km² terrestrial reference
Major cities
Bogotá, Medellín, Cali, Barranquilla, Cartagena, Cúcuta, Bucaramanga and Pereira are important metropolitan and regional nodes
Subnational administration
Thirty-two departments plus Bogotá Capital District; municipalities, districts and Indigenous territorial arrangements have different competences [8]
Climate zones
Humid tropical coasts, mountain temperate zones, high cold páramo, wet Amazon forest and seasonally dry savanna
Conservation pressures
Deforestation, illegal mining, water contamination, habitat loss and climate change intersect with uneven territorial governance
UNESCO landscapes
Chiribiquete, Cartagena, Malpelo, Colombian Coffee Cultural Landscape and other properties illustrate varied natural and cultural heritage [13]

Census methodology: DANE directly counted 44,164,417 people in 2018 and published a preliminary adjusted estimate of 48,258,494 after estimating omissions. World Bank 2025 population is 53,425,635. These numbers have different meanings and dates, while land area must not be combined with maritime jurisdiction areas.

Economy and International Trade

Economic classification
Upper-middle-income, diversified market economy with energy, agriculture, finance, manufacturing, commerce and extensive services
Nominal GDP
US$457.41 billion for 2025 in World Bank current-U.S.-dollar accounts [1]
GDP per capita
US$8,561.6 in 2025 World Bank data; measures GDP per resident rather than median income [1]
Real GDP growth
2.6% in the World Bank annual 2025 series [1]
Consumer-price inflation
5.1% in 2025 in the World Bank CPI annual-change series [1]
Modeled unemployment
8.3% in the 2025 World Bank ILO-modeled series, not directly comparable with every DANE monthly employment estimate [1]
National currency
Colombian peso (COP); peso-denominated figures must not be confused with U.S.-dollar trade values [10]
Central bank
Banco de la República has monetary, currency and payment-system functions under the constitutional framework [10]
Oil and derivatives
Crude oil and refined fuels are important merchandise exports, but commodity values vary with volume, oil prices and infrastructure
Coal production
Thermal coal extraction and exports are major in northern districts, with environmental and transition-policy challenges
Coffee industry
Arabica coffee grown in Andean regions is an internationally recognized export and major source of rural livelihoods
Cut flowers
Roses, carnations and other floriculture production are shipped by air to consumer markets
Gold and mineral trade
Gold, emeralds, nickel and other mineral commodities add to exports; formal and illicit mining need distinct treatment
Bananas and agroindustry
Bananas, plantains, sugar, palm products and processed foods contribute to employment and traded goods
Manufactured goods
Chemicals, pharmaceuticals, automotive components, textiles and household manufactures are produced in multiple city regions
Services contribution
Finance, telecommunications, logistics, retail, health, government services and professional activities form a large economic share
Tourism and travel receipts
Visitors support accommodation, cultural sites, ecotourism and transport; tourism is primarily a services export, not merchandise
Customs goods exports in 2025
Approximately US$50.20 billion FOB in the full-year DANE reported series, released in 2026 [4]
Annual growth in exports
About 1.3% growth compared with 2024, according to the annual DANE-based 2025 trade series [4]
Exports excluding petroleum
DNP's 2025 analysis found approximately US$37.73 billion of exports excluding petroleum and refined products [4]
2025 petroleum and refined exports
Approximately US$12.47 billion under the same DNP classification; classification and rounding matter [4]
Main export market
United States remains the largest individual market in WTO product and destination tables [5]
Other export markets
EU markets, Panama, India, China, Mexico, Brazil and Ecuador are relevant destinations in recent WTO data; rankings vary annually [5]
Main imports
Industrial equipment, machinery, fuels, vehicles, electronics, medicines and agricultural inputs are major goods categories
Export valuation
FOB export values exclude international freight and insurance and are not comparable directly with all CIF import figures
Trade and exchange rates
COP exchange fluctuations and global oil/coal prices affect nominal merchandise valuation and external balances
Foreign investment
Energy, services, infrastructure and other sectors attract investment subject to regulatory, financial and political conditions
Infrastructure corridors
Ports at Cartagena, Barranquilla and Buenaventura, roads and airports connect otherwise fragmented topography
Energy systems
Hydropower plays a major role in electricity while oil, coal and gas sustain significant production and export activity
Informal employment
Informal livelihoods, income inequality, regional disparities and productivity are continuing challenges of economic policy
Development and conflict
Violence, displacement, land tenure and security costs affect rural investment and economic opportunity unevenly
Pacific Alliance participation
Colombia participates in regional Pacific-facing trade initiatives alongside Chile, Mexico and Peru [16]
Goods versus services
DANE customs exports exclude internationally traded professional, transportation and tourism services; balance-of-payments statistics use a wider framework

People and Society

Official national language
Spanish is the national official language, while languages of ethnic groups have official standing in their respective territories [8]
Indigenous languages
Wayuunaiki, Nasa Yuwe, Embera varieties, Kogi, Arhuaco, Tikuna and others represent distinct language communities
Creole and island languages
San Andrés–Providencia Creole and other English-based Caribbean speech varieties have local cultural and institutional significance
Palenquero and Romani
Palenquero, a Spanish-based creole, and varieties of Romani are part of Colombia's linguistic diversity
Indigenous identity
Indigenous communities maintain collective territorial, linguistic and governance institutions; census categories require careful interpretation
Afro-Colombian society
Black, Afro-Colombian, Raizal and Palenquero identities have related but not identical historical and census definitions
2018 census self-identification
DANE's 2018 ethnicity reporting and later estimation differ in method and response characteristics; shares should not be treated as a uniform timeless series [3]
Migrants and refugees
Migration from Venezuela and other neighboring states has affected urban services, border regions and social integration
Internal displacement
Long-running armed conflict and other violence have generated extensive internal displacement, with data tracked in separate government and humanitarian systems [12]
Religious freedom
The Constitution protects freedom of belief and worship; the state is not officially Roman Catholic under the 1991 charter [8]
Christian religious traditions
Catholic and numerous evangelical/Protestant churches are widespread; religious affiliation levels depend on survey year and sample
Other religious communities
Jewish, Muslim, Afro-descendant spiritual, Indigenous and other religious traditions contribute to national pluralism
Religion-data limitation
No new, universally comparable 2025–2026 official religion census is substituted for older survey percentages
Education governance
National ministry and territorial authorities manage public education policy alongside universities and private institutions
Higher education
National University of Colombia, University of Antioquia, University of the Andes and other institutions support research and professional training
Health services
A mixed public and private insurance/provider arrangement operates under regulatory oversight, with rural and urban coverage inequalities
Regional culture
Caribbean, Pacific, Andean, Orinoquía and Amazonian regions have distinct food, music, social customs and historical trajectories
Music and dance
Cumbia, vallenato, currulao, bambuco, salsa and other traditions have regional and transnational histories
Literary heritage
Gabriel García Márquez and many other authors connect Colombian literature with broader Latin American and world traditions
Foodways
Arepas, sancocho, regional maize dishes, coffee and coastal cuisine illustrate regional differentiation
Human rights and memory
Recognition of victims, transitional justice, truth-telling and territorial security are ongoing societal issues [12]
Social inequality
Poverty, land ownership, quality of public services and labor-market opportunity vary significantly between regions
2025 modeled population
World Bank's 53.43-million estimate is not the count of respondents in a 2025 census [1]
Cultural heritage
UNESCO lists nine Colombian World Heritage properties, comprising cultural, natural and mixed classifications [13]

Population and belief measures: 2018 ethnic census responses, a 2025 international population estimate and older religious surveys have different base populations and procedures. No invented 2026 religious census percentages have been substituted. Indigenous, Raizal, Palenquero and Afro-Colombian categories should not be collapsed.

Government and Institutions

State form
Unitary constitutional presidential republic organized as a social state of law under the 1991 Constitution [8]
Constitutional framework
Political Constitution of 1991 with subsequent legal amendments and judicial interpretation [8]
Head of state and government
President holds both roles; national executive operates through ministries and administrative agencies
President in November 2026
Abelardo Gabriel de la Espriella Otero, sworn in August 7, 2026, for the 2026–2030 constitutional period [6]
Presidential inauguration
The Senate reported the August 7, 2026 ceremony and a term expected to conclude August 7, 2030 [6]
Preceding president
Gustavo Petro served the preceding 2022–2026 term and should not be listed as current in November 2026 [6]
Presidential election cycle
The 2026 first round was May 31, with a June 21 second round organized by national electoral institutions [7]
Presidential tenure
The presidency is ordinarily a single four-year term without immediate presidential reelection under current constitutional rules [8]
Legislative branch
Bicameral Congress of the Republic: Senate and House of Representatives [9]
Senate representation
Nationally elected Senate seats and constitutionally defined special representation are governed by electoral law
House of Representatives
Territorial electoral districts and special representation provide the lower chamber's institutional basis
Electoral bodies
Registraduría Nacional administers electoral logistics and civil identification; the National Electoral Council has oversight and adjudicative functions [7]
Judiciary
Supreme Court of Justice, Council of State, Constitutional Court and other high judicial organs have separate jurisdictions
Constitutional Court
Constitutional review, rights jurisprudence and tutela-related functions are central to constitutional governance [8]
Attorney General's office
Fiscalía General de la Nación exercises criminal investigation and prosecution functions with legally defined autonomy
Ombudsman institutions
Defensoría del Pueblo and other control bodies have rights oversight responsibilities
Territorial administration
Thirty-two departments and Bogotá Capital District, alongside municipalities and legally recognized territories [8]
Departmental governance
Elected governors and departmental assemblies administer local competences under national constitutional law
Municipal governance
Mayors and elected councils exercise powers over local public services and planning
Indigenous authorities
Resguardos and Indigenous territorial institutions involve collective rights and distinct local governance arrangements
Peace implementation
Agencies and courts administer distinct obligations under the 2016 agreement, including victims' rights and transitional justice [12]
Security governance
National Police, armed forces and civilian authorities operate under constitutional mandates; security policy is debated in relation to rights and public order
Central-bank independence
Banco de la República's board regulates monetary policy independently within its constitutional and statutory mandate [10]
Public accountability
Constitutional judicial remedies, administrative controls and elected institutions provide checks subject to practical implementation limits
Data cutoff
Officeholder status is verified to November 6, 2026, and must be updated after later executive appointments or constitutional changes

Officeholder verification: Colombia’s Senate recorded Abelardo de la Espriella’s August 7, 2026 swearing-in for the 2026–2030 term. The presidential office, Congress, courts, electoral authorities and department/municipal administrations are distinct institutions with separate mandates.

International Profile

United Nations
Colombia was an original member in 1945 and engages in UN security, humanitarian and development bodies [18]
Andean Community
Member of the Andean Community with Bolivia, Ecuador and Peru, supporting regional trade and mobility arrangements [15]
Pacific Alliance
Member alongside Chile, Mexico and Peru in a Pacific-facing trade integration framework [16]
Organization of American States
Participates in hemispheric diplomacy, electoral and human-rights arrangements
OECD membership
Colombia joined the OECD in 2020, expanding economic and regulatory policy comparisons [14]
World Trade Organization
Colombia participates in WTO goods and services trade commitments [5]
Relationship with United States
Significant trade, migration, counternarcotics and security interactions; policies differ across administrations
Neighboring Venezuela
Border migration, transportation, energy and security issues connect the two states
Ecuador and Peru
Andean and Amazonian borders connect Indigenous communities, ecosystems, transport and markets
Brazil and Amazon
Amazon-region cooperation includes forest governance and the prevention of transboundary ecological harm
Panama and Darién
The Darién border region is ecologically sensitive and associated with migrant movement and security challenges
Caribbean and Pacific security
Maritime routes, fisheries, tourism and coastal trade create overlapping development and enforcement responsibilities
Humanitarian diplomacy
Displacement, victim assistance and peace implementation involve international participation, but domestic institutions retain legal authority
UNESCO World Heritage
Nine properties, including Cartagena, the Coffee Cultural Landscape, Chiribiquete, Malpelo and archaeological parks [13]
Time zone
Colombia uses UTC−5 nationwide as standard legal time without regular seasonal clock changes
Telephone and internet
+57 international calling code and .co top-level domain
Major diplomatic challenges
Regional migration, environmental protection, trade diversification, illegal trafficking, human rights and peaceful conflict resolution

UNESCO classification: Colombia’s nine World Heritage properties include six cultural, two natural and one mixed property. A serial property is still one listed property, and the state-party total should not be confused with national park or museum counts.

Country Overview

Argentina is a federal presidential republic occupying much of southern South America. Its territory extends from the subtropical northeast and the Pampas across the Andes and Patagonia to Tierra del Fuego. The country developed from diverse Indigenous societies, Spanish colonial jurisdictions, nineteenth-century independence and federal state-building, and successive waves of immigration. Buenos Aires is its national capital and principal urban and commercial hub. Agriculture, food processing, manufacturing, services, energy and minerals underpin the economy. The republic declared independence in 1816 and adopted its enduring federal constitutional framework in 1853, later amended in 1994. Contemporary economic statistics require careful treatment because inflation, exchange-rate movements and periodic statistical revisions can make current-dollar GDP and nominal trade figures difficult to compare over time. Argentina also makes claims in the South Atlantic and Antarctica that should be distinguished from internationally recognized and administered territory.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Argentine Republic (República Argentina); a federal presidential constitutional republic [6]
Common name and demonym
Argentina; nationals are Argentines (argentinos and argentinas in Spanish)
National capital
Autonomous City of Buenos Aires, a federally autonomous jurisdiction distinct from Buenos Aires Province [6]
Continental region
South America, United Nations M49 South America grouping; ISO country code AR [9]
Precolonial societies
Guaraní, Quechua-speaking, Diaguita, Mapuche, Wichí, Qom and numerous other Indigenous communities maintained distinct histories and social systems before colonization
Spanish imperial period
Much of the territory fell under Spanish colonial administration; Buenos Aires became the capital of the Viceroyalty of Río de la Plata in 1776
May Revolution
May 25, 1810 marked the revolutionary displacement of Spanish viceregal authority in Buenos Aires; it is not the formal independence declaration
Independence declaration
July 9, 1816 at the Congress of Tucumán; the United Provinces declared independence from Spain and its monarchs [13]
War of independence
Military campaigns associated with José de San Martín and other leaders connected Argentine independence to continental struggles in Chile and Peru
Early political conflicts
Disputes over provincial autonomy, central power and the organization of the republic continued for decades after independence
Federal Constitution
Constitution of 1853 established the principal federal republican framework; Buenos Aires joined the constitutional order following later political settlements [6]
Constitutional reforms
Amendments culminated in the wide-ranging 1994 reform, which altered presidential terms, institutions and the legal status of human-rights instruments [6]
Immigration era
Late nineteenth- and early twentieth-century migration from Europe and surrounding American countries reshaped population, industry, urban society and labor politics
Twentieth-century transformations
Labor mobilization, Peronism, military interventions and democratic movements profoundly influenced national political and social history
Military dictatorship
The 1976–1983 military regime perpetrated systematic state terror and enforced disappearances; human-rights accountability remains central to public memory
Return to elected government
Democratic rule resumed in December 1983; the post-1983 order has included peaceful alternation and episodes of major economic crisis
National Day
Independence Day is July 9; May 25 is commemorated separately as Revolution Day
National flag
Light blue–white–light blue horizontal stripes with the Sun of May at the center of the state flag; iconography reflects independence-era history
Official working language
Spanish is the predominant language of federal institutions; some provinces recognize additional Indigenous languages under their own laws
ISO country identifiers
AR (alpha-2), ARG (alpha-3), 032 (numeric); .ar internet top-level domain [9]
United Nations membership
Argentina was an original member of the United Nations in 1945 [17]
Territorial claim distinction
Claims to the Falkland Islands (Islas Malvinas), South Georgia and South Sandwich Islands, and Antarctic sectors are not equivalent to undisputed mainland administration [8]

Founding sequence: The 1810 May Revolution, the July 9, 1816 independence declaration, the 1853 Constitution and the 1994 constitutional reforms describe distinct events. Argentina’s territorial claims should not be presented as internationally uncontested jurisdiction.

Geography and Population

Continental extent
Southern South America between the Andes and Atlantic, bordering Bolivia, Brazil, Chile, Paraguay and Uruguay [8]
Internationally comparable area
Approximately 2.78 million km² in common international land and surface reporting, excluding Antarctica and disputed South Atlantic islands [8]
Argentine national-area methodology
Government publications include Antarctic and South Atlantic island claims in larger national totals; official domestic and internationally comparable definitions differ [8]
North-to-south extent
Humid subtropical northern environments transition to temperate Pampas, arid western districts and cool Patagonian and subpolar landscapes
Andes Mountains
Western boundary includes high ranges, puna plateaus, passes, valleys and glaciated mountains with distinct local ecosystems
Highest mountain
Aconcagua in Mendoza Province reaches about 6,961 metres, the highest mountain outside Asia
The Pampas
Broad fertile plains support grain cultivation, livestock, major cities and concentrated transport networks
Patagonia
Southern region encompasses steppe, mountains, glaciers, coastal areas and sparsely settled rural districts
Mesopotamia region
Territory between the Paraná and Uruguay rivers includes Corrientes, Entre Ríos and Misiones, with wetlands, forests and major inland waterways
Gran Chaco
Northern lowlands with dry forests, wetlands, farming and Indigenous territories; clearing and ecological fragmentation are concerns
Tierra del Fuego
Southern archipelago includes the Argentine part of Isla Grande and the city of Ushuaia, alongside internationally disputed maritime boundaries
Principal rivers
Paraná, Paraguay, Uruguay, Colorado and Negro systems support hydropower, agriculture, shipping and settlements
Río de la Plata
Broad estuary shared with Uruguay connects inland river commerce with Atlantic maritime trade and metropolitan Buenos Aires
Iguazú Falls
Major waterfalls on the border with Brazil lie within an ecosystem protected by national parks in both states [12]
Major glaciers
Los Glaciares National Park includes the Perito Moreno Glacier and associated Patagonian ice landscapes [12]
Climatic patterns
Subtropical, semi-arid, temperate, alpine and subpolar climates vary greatly by latitude, altitude and distance from the ocean
Natural hazards
Flooding, periodic drought, wildfires, heat waves, Andean earthquakes, volcano-related hazards and glacial change affect regions differently
Final 2022 census count
45,892,285 persons enumerated in the definitive 2022 census; do not confuse this with an adjusted midyear population estimate [3]
2022 adjusted demographic estimate
46,234,830 was the separate government estimate for July 1, 2022; an estimate is not the number directly counted in the census [3]
2025 international population
45,851,378 in the World Bank annual series, an estimate from a different data program and reference year [1]
Population density
Roughly 16.5 residents per km² when 2025 World Bank population is divided by the roughly 2.78-million-km² continental reference; a derived approximation
Major metropolitan regions
Greater Buenos Aires is by far the largest; Córdoba, Rosario, Mendoza, Tucumán and La Plata are major urban centres
Subnational jurisdictions
23 provinces and the Autonomous City of Buenos Aires, with constitutionally divided national and provincial powers [6]
Land resource distribution
Fertile Pampas, hydrocarbon basins, Andean minerals, forest regions and freshwater resources create strongly differentiated regional economies
Vaca Muerta formation
Neuquén Basin shale oil and gas resources support large-scale investment but require transport, water management and environmental safeguards
Conservation areas
National parks and provincial reserves protect subtropical forest, temperate grassland, high Andes, Patagonia and coastal biodiversity [20]
Life expectancy
Around 78 years in the World Bank 2024 life-expectancy series; the value is not a newly measured 2026 outcome [1]

Area and population comparability: An internationally comparable roughly 2.78-million-km² figure excludes Argentine Antarctic and disputed South Atlantic island claims. The 2022 census directly counted 45,892,285 persons; the adjusted 2022 estimate and the World Bank 2025 figure are different statistical series.

Economy and International Trade

Development profile
Diversified upper-middle-income economy with important agriculture, manufacturing, business services, energy and mining sectors
Nominal GDP
US$683.1 billion in 2025, World Bank current-price U.S.-dollar series [1]
GDP per capita
US$14,898 per resident for 2025 in the same World Bank series; not median wages or household income [1]
Real GDP growth
Approximately 4.4% in 2025 using the World Bank annual volume-growth series; distinct from nominal peso changes [1]
Inflation reference
Consumer price inflation is volatile; the World Bank comparison page shows 219.9% for 2024, which must not be labeled a 2025 or 2026 reading [1]
National currency
Argentine peso (ARS), with monetary and foreign-exchange arrangements set through domestic institutions [10]
Monetary authority
Central Bank of the Argentine Republic (BCRA), charged with central-bank functions under national law [10]
Exchange-rate comparability
U.S.-dollar GDP levels respond to peso-dollar valuation and statistical revisions as well as real production; nominal GDP should not be read as purchasing power
Main farming products
Soybeans, corn, wheat, sunflower, beef, dairy products and fruit support domestic consumption and international exports
Soybean value chain
Soybeans, soybean meal and soybean oil link agricultural production to crushing, transport, ports and global feed and food markets
Manufacturing industries
Motor vehicles, food and beverages, pharmaceuticals, chemicals, steel and machinery contribute to industrial employment and exports
Energy production
Conventional oil, natural gas, Vaca Muerta shale resources, electricity and renewable capacity are important economic sectors
Mining profile
Lithium, gold, silver and copper development are significant in Andean provinces, subject to local environmental and community considerations
Services and finance
Wholesale and retail commerce, banking, professional services, education, tourism, communications and technology contribute to GDP
2025 merchandise exports
US$87.111 billion in INDEC's revised 2025 annual reporting; earlier January 2026 preliminary release listed US$87.077 billion [4]
2025 merchandise imports
US$75.791 billion in the same revised 2025 goods-trade series [4]
2025 merchandise trade balance
US$11.320 billion surplus in the revised 2025 goods series, with discrepancies from earlier vintages reflecting revisions [4]
Export composition
Primary products, manufactured agricultural products, industrial manufactures, and fuels and energy are INDEC's four major goods-export groupings [4]
2025 export destinations
Brazil, China, United States, Chile and India were among principal export markets in preliminary 2025 INDEC statistics [4]
Principal importing partners
Brazil, China and the United States were leading origins of merchandise imports in the 2025 national report [4]
Agribusiness logistics
Paraná–Río de la Plata ports, road and rail freight, grain terminals and inland processing sites are important supply-chain assets
Energy trade
Hydrocarbon export and import balances change with prices, local demand, production and pipeline or seaport capacity
Services exports
Knowledge-intensive business services, software, tourism, transportation and other services generate receipts not counted in customs merchandise exports
Industrial-policy debate
Trade rules, fiscal adjustment, infrastructure, labor regulation, capital access and provincial resource revenues shape economic policy debates
Employment comparability
The World Bank modeled-ILO unemployment rate was 7.1% in 2025; it is not necessarily the same as an INDEC quarterly urban labor-force rate [1]
External financing
Sovereign financing, international capital markets and IMF arrangements have been major issues across economic cycles; dated debt data require an identified definition
Trading blocks
MERCOSUR membership links Argentina to a South American customs and market-integration framework with country-specific exceptions [18]

People and Society

Principal language
Spanish (Castellano) predominates in national government, media and education; varieties differ across regions
Indigenous languages
Quechua, Guaraní, Qom, Wichí and Mapudungun are among languages used by communities; provincial protection and vitality vary
Language rights
Provincial laws, Indigenous education programs and national human-rights commitments address linguistic and cultural diversity
Population formation
Communities include Indigenous peoples, descendants of colonial societies, European and neighboring South American immigrants, and later migrations from across the world
Indigenous peoples
Indigenous nations possess distinct identities, territorial relationships and representative organizations; they should not be collapsed into a single ancestry category
Urbanization
Argentina is highly urbanized, with concentration in Greater Buenos Aires and other provincial capital and industrial regions
Major religious tradition
Catholicism remains an important historical and social institution, while Protestant, Jewish, Muslim, other faiths and nonreligious identities are present
2019 religious survey
A 2019 CONICET-linked national survey found 62.9% Catholic, 15.3% evangelical and 18.9% religiously unaffiliated; not a 2026 census [11]
Religious methodology
Self-reported religious affiliation measures identification, not attendance, belief intensity or contemporary 2026 shares [11]
Education structure
National and provincial authorities share responsibilities; compulsory schooling, universities and technical institutes form a large public and private system
Public universities
University of Buenos Aires, National University of Córdoba, National University of La Plata and other institutions have extensive research and teaching roles
Health system
Public-sector facilities, social-security-linked coverage and private care operate alongside important disparities by province and income
Literary traditions
Jorge Luis Borges, Julio Cortázar and numerous other authors represent internationally influential literary movements
Music and dance
Tango, folklore, chamamé, rock nacional and diverse regional traditions reflect historical and contemporary cultural life
Sport and identity
Football is a major national sport, alongside rugby, basketball, field hockey, motor racing and traditional regional sports
Cultural geography
Buenos Aires, the Pampas, Northwest Andes, Mesopotamia, Cuyo and Patagonia maintain distinct historical and cultural patterns
National memory
Remembrance and legal accountability for the 1976–1983 dictatorship are significant in education and public institutions
Migration debates
Historical immigration, present regional mobility, emigration and return migration shape urban demography and employment
Environmental and social issues
Housing affordability, income inequality, rural land disputes, protected habitats and natural-resource development prompt continuing national debate

Religious data: The cited 2019 national survey is neither a census nor a 2026 enumeration. Its Catholic, evangelical and nonaffiliated percentages describe survey respondents and should not be extrapolated as exact current religious membership or practice.

Government and Institutions

Form of state
Federal presidential representative republic under the 1853 Constitution as amended, including in 1994 [6]
Head of state and government
President Javier Gerardo Milei, serving since December 10, 2023, as of the November 6, 2026 editorial cutoff [7]
Vice president
Victoria Villarruel, elected on the 2023 presidential ticket; the vice president also presides over the Senate [15]
Federal executive
President appoints ministers and directs the national executive within legal and constitutional limits [6]
Presidential election cycle
President and vice president elected together for four years, with constitutional rules for consecutive reelection and runoff procedures [6]
2023 presidential transition
Javier Milei won the 2023 runoff and succeeded Alberto Fernández, with the presidential term commencing December 10, 2023 [7]
Federal legislature
National Congress consists of a Chamber of Deputies and a Senate; legislative competence is divided from provincial powers [14]
Chamber of Deputies
257-member lower house, elected through provincial and Buenos Aires constituencies on proportional party lists [14]
Senate
72 senators: three for each of 23 provinces and three for the Autonomous City of Buenos Aires [14]
Legislative election cycles
Chamber terms ordinarily four years with partial biennial renewal; Senate terms six years with one-third of seats renewed every two years [14]
2025 midterm election
Legislative elections in October 2025 altered the political balance of Congress; they did not constitute a presidential election
Provincial governance
23 provinces have their own constitutions, governors and legislatures within the federal constitutional division of powers [6]
Buenos Aires autonomy
The autonomous capital city has elected governing institutions and is distinct from the large surrounding Province of Buenos Aires [6]
Supreme Court
Supreme Court of Justice of the Nation heads the national judiciary in constitutionally assigned matters [16]
Rights and treaties
Constitution and incorporated human-rights treaties shape judicial review, due process and individual liberties [6]
Federal fiscal relations
Revenue sharing, budget transfers, provincial borrowing and public services are recurring federalism questions
Electoral administration
National Electoral Chamber and relevant judicial and executive electoral bodies oversee different aspects of elections [16]
State security
Armed forces, federal policing and provincial law-enforcement bodies have distinct legal mandates
Public transparency
Legislative oversight, auditing bodies, courts, press scrutiny and public-information rules form part of governance mechanisms
Political polarization
Economic stabilization, social policy, unions, protest rights and public spending remain contested policy issues rather than settled national consensus

Constitutional division: The federal president serves as both head of state and head of government; the vice president presides over the Senate. Buenos Aires City and Buenos Aires Province are separate constitutional jurisdictions.

International Profile

United Nations
Original UN member since 1945, with participation in multilateral diplomacy, peacekeeping and international law [17]
MERCOSUR
Founding participant in Southern Common Market integration with Brazil, Paraguay and Uruguay [18]
G20
Member of major-economy forum, with participation in financial and sustainable-development coordination
Organization of American States
OAS member participating in hemispheric diplomatic and political processes
World Trade Organization
WTO member under multilateral trading disciplines and country-specific arrangements [19]
Financial institutions
Member of IMF, World Bank and Inter-American Development Bank, which publish independent economic datasets
Antarctic Treaty System
Participates in Antarctic science and governance; territorial claims are held in abeyance under the treaty's legal arrangements
South Atlantic sovereignty dispute
Argentina claims the Falkland Islands (Islas Malvinas), administered by the United Kingdom; the dispute remains unresolved under international law [8]
Regional border relations
Land boundaries with Chile, Bolivia, Paraguay, Brazil and Uruguay link transboundary rivers, transport corridors and communities
Brazil relationship
Largest MERCOSUR partner and a major buyer and supplier of vehicles, machinery and other merchandise [4]
China relationship
Major buyer of agricultural commodities and supplier of manufactured goods; investment and financing relations vary over time [4]
United States relationship
Trade, finance, investment, technology and diplomatic relations with the United States are economically significant [4]
Other regional markets
Chile, India, Uruguay and neighbors are important to agricultural, manufactured and energy exports [4]
UNESCO World Heritage
Twelve inscribed properties in UNESCO's current State Party list; includes Iguazú, Los Glaciares and the ESMA memorial site [12]
Historical memory in international forums
The ESMA Museum and Site of Memory, inscribed in 2023, recognizes a place associated with state terror and human-rights remembrance [12]
Climate and biodiversity diplomacy
Andes glaciers, wetlands, forest ecosystems, fossil-fuel development and agricultural emissions shape environmental positions
Communications
International calling code +54; ISO top-level domain .ar
Time zones
Argentina Standard Time UTC−3 nationwide in ordinary current practice, without a routine annual daylight-saving schedule
Cultural diplomacy
Literature, film, football, universities and arts are prominent channels of international exchange

Territorial wording: The Falkland Islands (Islas Malvinas) and other South Atlantic claims are disputed. Argentine descriptions of sovereign claims are reported as claims, while the administering authorities and Antarctic treaty constraints are stated distinctly.

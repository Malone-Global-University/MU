Country Overview

Guyana, officially the Co-operative Republic of Guyana, occupies the northern Atlantic coast of South America, between Venezuela, Brazil and Suriname. It is the only sovereign country on the South American mainland whose official language is English. Its history encompasses diverse Indigenous societies, Dutch and British colonialism, the Atlantic slave trade, Indian indenture and independence in 1966. An elected republic since 1970, Guyana includes a heavily populated low coastal plain and a vast rainforest and savanna interior. Offshore oil production began in 2019 and has rapidly transformed national output and exports, although employment, public services and household living standards cannot be inferred from GDP alone. The Essequibo territorial dispute with Venezuela requires careful differentiation between Guyanese administration, international legal proceedings and competing national claims.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Co-operative Republic of Guyana, constitutional republic with an elected executive president [10]
Common name
Guyana; the name is related to the Guiana geographic region and differs from neighboring French Guiana, an overseas region of France
Capital
Georgetown, the chief port and center of national political and administrative institutions [2]
Continental / UN M49 region
South America, within the United Nations Americas geographic grouping [17]
Indigenous foundations
Arawak/Lokono, Carib/Karinya, Wapichan, Makushi, Patamona, Akawaio and other Indigenous peoples inhabited and continue to govern communities in the region
Dutch colonial period
Dutch trading and plantation settlements developed in Essequibo, Demerara and Berbice from the seventeenth century, alongside Indigenous exchange networks
British colonial consolidation
Britain acquired the separate Dutch colonies in the Napoleonic-era settlement and unified them as British Guiana in 1831
Atlantic slavery
Enslaved Africans provided much of the labor for plantation sugar production; emancipation in the 1830s was followed by conflicts over wages and land access
Indentured labor
Indian laborers and smaller numbers of Portuguese, Chinese and others came under contractual migration systems, transforming the country's population and cultural landscape
Labor and constitutional politics
Twentieth-century unions, anticolonial campaigns and parties mobilized around labor conditions, voting rights and self-government
Independence
May 26, 1966: British Guiana became the independent state of Guyana after negotiations with the United Kingdom [16]
Republican status
February 23, 1970: the country became a republic, ending the British monarch's position as head of state; the date is commemorated as Republic Day
1980 constitutional framework
The Constitution of the Co-operative Republic of Guyana established the executive presidency and an institutional system later modified by reform [10]
Democratic political transitions
Political competition, electoral administration and constitutional reform have been central concerns since the restoration of more competitive elections in 1992
Current governing mandate
President Mohamed Irfaan Ali began a second five-year term after the September 1, 2025 general election; he was sworn in on September 7 [8]
National day
Independence Day on May 26; Republic Day on February 23 and Emancipation Day on August 1 are distinct commemorations
National symbols
The Golden Arrowhead flag uses green, gold, black, white and red; the national motto emphasizes one people, one nation and one destiny
Official language
English is used in Parliament, national legislation, administration and formal schooling, though Guyanese Creole and Indigenous languages are widely important
United Nations membership
Admitted September 20, 1966, following independence [2]
ISO country codes
GY (alpha-2), GUY (alpha-3), 328 (numeric) [17]
Country-code domain
.gy; international telephone country code +592
Essequibo historical dispute
A boundary dispute with Venezuela concerns territory west of the Essequibo River, administered by Guyana, with competing diplomatic and judicial claims [12]
International-law status
The International Court of Justice is hearing the controversy concerning the 1899 arbitral award; political maps and unilateral claims must not be presented as a settled new border [12]

Historical and territorial distinction: Independence (1966) and republican status (1970) are separate dates. The Essequibo dispute involves conflicting claims and active international proceedings; Guyana administers the territory, while the ICJ litigation should not be characterized as a final settlement.

Geography and Population

Total surface area
214,969 km² in United Nations country statistics, comprising coastal lowlands, interior forest, mountain terrain and savannas [2]
Atlantic frontage
The coastal plain faces the Atlantic Ocean and contains Georgetown, ports, embanked agricultural estates and much of the nation's population
International neighbors
Venezuela to the west, Brazil to the south and southwest, and Suriname to the east; maritime boundaries involve separate legal arrangements
Coastal drainage
Sea defenses, drainage canals and pumping systems are important because settled sections of the coastal plain are low-lying and vulnerable to flooding
Interior rainforest
Large forest areas support biodiversity, Indigenous customary lands, conservation and economic activities including timber and gold mining
Guiana Shield
Ancient geological formation underlies mineral-rich uplands and forested terrain across parts of northern South America
Pakaraima Mountains
Western mountains include sandstone uplands and the Mount Roraima border area, an ecological and cultural landmark
Kaieteur Falls
Significant waterfall on the Potaro River within Kaieteur National Park, known for its exceptional single-drop scale and biodiversity
Rupununi savannas
Southern grasslands and wetlands extend near Brazil, with ranching, community livelihoods, conservation and seasonal flooding
Essequibo River
The country's largest river system drains extensive interior basins toward the Atlantic coast and is essential for transport and hydrography
Demerara and Berbice rivers
Major waterways historically structured plantations, settlements, ports and transport connections in the coastal economy
Major urban localities
Georgetown, Linden, New Amsterdam, Anna Regina and other regional towns serve different administrative and commercial functions
Ten administrative regions
Barima-Waini; Pomeroon-Supenaam; Essequibo Islands-West Demerara; Demerara-Mahaica; Mahaica-Berbice; East Berbice-Corentyne; Cuyuni-Mazaruni; Potaro-Siparuni; Upper Takutu-Upper Essequibo; Upper Demerara-Berbice
2022 census population
878,674 persons counted at the September 15, 2022 census reference date according to preliminary results published in January 2026 [3]
2012 census comparison
746,955 were recorded in 2012; reported change of about 17.6% to the 2022 census is an intercensal result, not an annual birth rate [3]
2024 national estimate
The Bureau of Statistics estimated 956,044 residents by December 31, 2024 using postcensal vital and migration records [3]
2025 international estimate
World Bank annual population series lists 835,986, using methods and vintage different from Guyana's new census-based national estimates [1]
Population-series caveat
The international and national series differ materially; they must not be spliced into a trend or treated as interchangeable resident counts [3] [1]
Coastal settlement distribution
Preliminary 2022 census found roughly 87% of residents in coastal regions and 13% in the hinterland, with notable regional variation [3]
Population density
Around 3.9 residents per km² when World Bank 2025 population is divided by 214,969 km²; not a census-derived density
Forest and climate
Equatorial tropical climate with alternating rainfall seasons and extensive humid forests, mangroves and wetland ecosystems
Environmental hazards
River and coastal floods, erosion, deforestation, mining contamination and changing precipitation patterns affect development and health
Indigenous territorial importance
Interior communities often depend on river transport, forest resources, customary land relations and local government for services
Freshwater and coastal ecosystems
The Essequibo watershed, coastal mangroves, wetlands and estuarine habitats support fisheries, biodiversity and agricultural land
Offshore petroleum geography
Producing fields of the Stabroek Block lie offshore in the Atlantic and should be distinguished from inland mineral deposits

Population comparability: Guyana’s preliminary 2022 census count of 878,674 and its end-2024 postcensal estimate of 956,044 do not match the World Bank’s 2025 annual estimate of 835,986. Different statistical bases and revisions make simple interpolation inappropriate.

Economy and International Trade

Economic structure
Rapidly growing oil-producing economy alongside agriculture, forestry, mining, construction, retail, transportation and public services; growth is highly uneven across activities
2025 nominal GDP
Approximately US$27.1 billion in the World Bank 2025 current-dollar series; reported at market exchange rates [1]
2025 GDP per capita
US$32,413.8 in the World Bank series; petroleum output is included and does not represent annual household earnings [1]
Real GDP growth
19.3% in 2025 in the World Bank annual series, substantially influenced by expansion of offshore production [1]
2025 inflation
3.3% in the World Bank consumer-price series, rather than an inferred 2026 inflation rate [1]
Modeled unemployment
12.0% for 2025 in the World Bank's ILO-modelled labor series; estimates do not reveal within-country distribution [1]
National currency
Guyanese dollar (GYD), separate from U.S. dollar values used in internationally comparable GDP and goods-trade tables
Monetary authority
Bank of Guyana operates national monetary, currency and financial-sector functions under domestic law [11]
Oil production start
Commercial offshore production began in the Stabroek Block in December 2019, radically changing the relative importance of export sectors
Offshore production system
Production is based on floating production, storage and offloading facilities; platform counts and project output rates change as new developments start [6]
Crude oil exports
US$17.797 billion in the Bureau of Statistics 2025 item table, accounting for 88.4% of its goods-export total [4]
Total goods exports
US$20.138 billion in the Bureau's 2025 detailed item table including re-exports; its trade overview rounds the merchandise series to US$20.09 billion [4] [5]
2025 merchandise imports
Approximately US$10.18 billion, reported on a cost-insurance-freight basis in the Bureau's 2025 trade summary [5]
2025 goods surplus
Roughly US$9.9 billion from the paired Bureau of Statistics export and import summary; export f.o.b. and import c.i.f. values have different valuation conventions [5]
Gold exports
US$1.618 billion (2025), the second largest listed export group after crude oil in the national table [4]
Rice exports
US$237.5 million for rice and paddy combined in the 2025 national item series, supported by irrigated coastal agriculture [4]
Bauxite exports
US$144.1 million in 2025; values respond to international prices and extraction and transport conditions [4]
Other primary exports
Fisheries, manganese, timber, sugar, rum and prepared food products contribute to the smaller non-oil merchandise base [4]
Export market methodology
WTO/Comtrade partner and commodity totals may differ from Guyana Bureau totals because of customs reporting, updates and export valuation [13] [4]
Services trade
Tourism, transport, communications, insurance, construction-related services and financial activity do not appear in the merchandise-export total
Oil revenue governance
Production-sharing contracts, royalties, petroleum taxation and public transfers influence the degree to which production benefits domestic institutions [6]
Natural Resource Fund
Statutory arrangements govern receipts and withdrawals of petroleum income; fund reporting is distinct from the value of crude exports [7]
Agricultural priorities
Rice, sugar, livestock, vegetables and regional food-security plans remain important despite the outsized GDP role of oil
Economic diversification
Skills development, electricity supply, ports, climate resilience, downstream services and local procurement are central questions in long-run growth
Poverty measurement
Older or incomplete household poverty observations do not establish the current share of residents receiving petroleum-related income [1]

People and Society

Language in public administration
English is the national official language used in law, parliamentary business, state administration and formal education
Guyanese Creole
English-lexifier Guyanese Creole is widely used in daily speech, music and community communication; proficiency differs from use of formal standard English
Indigenous linguistic communities
Arawak/Lokono, Wapichan, Makushi, Patamona, Akawaio, Carib/Karinya and other languages retain importance in particular regions
Indigenous peoples
The country's Indigenous communities are culturally and politically distinct; the state commonly uses the administrative term Amerindian in policy settings
Indo-Guyanese communities
Descendants of indentured Indian migrants constitute a major component of the population, with varied linguistic and religious traditions
Afro-Guyanese communities
Descendants of enslaved Africans and other Afro-Caribbean people have shaped political movements, literature, urban society and national culture
Multigroup identities
Persons with mixed ancestry and people of Portuguese, Chinese and other backgrounds form additional parts of the population
Religious landscape
Christianity, Hinduism and Islam have substantial followings, alongside Indigenous spiritual traditions and persons without religious affiliation
Religious methodology
Frequently cited detailed religious proportions derive from older census data; no new 2025 religious census is assumed in this edition
Education system
Public schools and tertiary institutions operate under national policy; teacher access, secondary completion and remote schools vary across regions
University of Guyana
Principal national university, with teaching and research relevant to social development, engineering, health and the economy
Health care
Public health institutions serve urban and rural populations, with differences in access to specialist care and inland transport
Transport access
Coastal roads, ferries, domestic airfields and riverboats connect settlements across markedly different population densities
Gender and employment
Participation in formal and informal work differs across cities, agricultural districts, mining sites and Indigenous communities
2022 household count
Census preliminary report recorded 271,946 households and an average household size of 3.23 persons, compared with 3.65 in 2012 [3]
Foreign-born population
The 2022 preliminary census recorded 3.07% foreign-born residents, compared with 1.10% in 2012; immigration has risen with economic expansion [3]
Cultural forms
Mashramani, Phagwah/Holi, Diwali, Eid, Christian holidays, calypso, chutney and literature reflect multiple historical traditions
Cuisine
Pepperpot, cook-up rice, roti, curry, metemgee and other dishes illustrate African, South Asian, Indigenous and regional Caribbean influences
Sport
Cricket, football, athletics and community recreation link Guyana with Caribbean and international sporting networks
Social inequality
Urban-rural service gaps, coastal flood exposure, labor conditions and unequal participation in high-value sectors are significant policy issues
Heritage protection
Indigenous cultural landscapes, historical timber buildings and biodiversity sites require distinct approaches to conservation and community rights

Population categories: Historical census descriptions of ethnic, language or religious groups should not be converted into 2025 shares without an equivalent current source. Individuals may have mixed identities; nationality is distinct from language and religion.

Government and Institutions

Constitutional form
Unitary sovereign republic with an executive presidency and an elected National Assembly, operating under the 1980 Constitution as amended [10]
President
Mohamed Irfaan Ali, sworn for a second elected term on September 7, 2025 following the September 1 general and regional elections [8]
Prime Minister
Mark Phillips, reappointed in September 2025 under the continuing Ali administration [9]
Vice-presidential roles
More than one vice-presidential title may be held; Bharrat Jagdeo was again appointed vice president after the 2025 election [9]
Executive organization
President and cabinet lead national executive policy, with ministerial portfolios responsible for central administration and sector programs
National Assembly
Unicameral elected legislature, commonly described as having 65 seats; its composition is shaped by proportional electoral rules [10]
Electoral system
National and regional electoral lists allocate representation; geographic constituency arrangements coexist with nationwide proportional voting
Elections
General elections are ordinarily held within five years; political competition, election administration and observer assessments attract public scrutiny
Judicial institutions
Supreme Court of Judicature includes High Court and Court of Appeal; the Caribbean Court of Justice serves as the final appellate tribunal
Legal tradition
English-derived common law and statutory law operate within the republican Constitution and rights guarantees
Regional administration
Ten administrative regions with regional democratic councils; local authorities operate at municipal and neighborhood levels
Indigenous community governance
Village councils and titled lands under Indigenous-community legislation coexist with broader regional and state institutions
Local government
Municipalities, neighborhood democratic councils and regional councils administer specified services and local planning functions
Fiscal governance
Parliament authorizes national budgets and oversees public expenditure; petroleum receipts involve additional fiscal disclosure requirements [6]
Natural Resource Fund oversight
Petroleum-related accounts and withdrawal rules are regulated by legislation and public reporting, a different measure from the annual oil-export value [7]
Independent constitutional bodies
Elections commission, public-service commissions and oversight agencies hold defined roles within the system
Constitutional reform
Debate concerns division of powers, elections, ethnic polarization, local representation and transparency of resource governance
Security institutions
Defense and police organizations have distinct mandates; border tensions and cross-border crime affect resource requirements
Rights and Indigenous land
Environmental regulation, Indigenous land titles and participation in resource decisions remain substantial governance questions
2025 transition date
President's renewed mandate and appointment of principal ministers occurred in September 2025, not in 2026 [8] [9]

Institutional distinction: The president leads the executive; the prime minister and vice presidents do not replace the president’s constitutional role. Offices were renewed after the September 2025 election, and the National Assembly is unicameral.

International Profile

UN membership
Admitted September 20, 1966, months after independence from the United Kingdom [2]
CARICOM membership
Guyana is a member of the Caribbean Community and hosts its headquarters in Georgetown [15]
Commonwealth
Member of the Commonwealth of Nations, maintaining multilateral ties distinct from monarchical status since becoming a republic
South American relations
Cooperation with Brazil, Suriname and other continental states connects infrastructure, border commerce and environmental protection
Essequibo controversy
Guyana administers the disputed territory; Venezuela asserts a competing claim, with the matter before the International Court of Justice [12]
Judicial process
The ICJ's proceedings concern the validity and legal effect of the 1899 arbitral award; interim orders should not be mistaken for final merits judgment [12]
Energy diplomacy
Offshore petroleum creates relationships involving foreign operators, purchasers, international shipping and maritime environmental regulation
WTO membership
Member of the World Trade Organization with merchandise trade commitments and sector-specific schedules [13]
Global finance
World Bank, IMF and Caribbean Development Bank support data, policy engagement and financing in distinct capacities
Climate diplomacy
Forest conservation, carbon accounting, coastal adaptation and climate finance connect domestic ecology with international negotiations
Amazon regional environment
Guiana Shield forest ecosystems intersect wider Amazon and northern South American conservation priorities
UNESCO heritage status
No Guyanese property is inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List as of this edition; tentative proposals are not inscriptions [14]
Tentative heritage nominations
Listed proposals include Georgetown sites, Shell Beach and historical fortifications, without automatic protected World Heritage status [14]
Diaspora relations
Guyanese communities in the United States, Canada, United Kingdom and Caribbean remain important in remittances and civic connections
Major trade markets
United States, European partners and other petroleum buyers have prominent roles, but sector and classification affect market shares [13]
Time zone
Guyana Time, UTC−4 throughout the year; seasonal daylight-saving changes are not generally observed
Telephone and internet
International dialing +592; .gy country-code domain
UN regional classification
Guyana is classified in South America, even while belonging institutionally to several Caribbean organizations [17]

Territorial and heritage classification: Guyana belongs geographically to South America and politically to CARICOM. UNESCO lists zero inscribed World Heritage properties; a tentative listing is only a possible future nomination.

Country Overview

Peru is a western South American republic extending from the Pacific coastal desert across the high Andes into the Amazon Basin. Its territory contains ancient Andean civilizations, including Caral and the Inca Empire, Spanish colonial institutions, diverse Indigenous nations and the modern state whose independence was proclaimed in 1821 and secured through subsequent campaigns. Lima is the capital and the largest urban center, while Cusco, Arequipa and numerous coastal, mountain and Amazonian communities preserve distinctive political and cultural histories. Mining, fishing, agriculture, manufacturing and services sustain a diverse economy whose 2025 merchandise exports reached a new national record. Following the 2026 elections, Keiko Fujimori assumed the presidency on July 28, and Congress returned to a bicameral structure. Economic estimates, census totals, Indigenous-language measures and UNESCO heritage counts use different sources and reporting conventions.

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Republic of Peru (República del Perú), sovereign presidential constitutional republic
Capital
Lima; the metropolitan area includes nationally important executive, judicial, legislative and commercial institutions [2]
UN geographic region
South America in the United Nations M49 system; the state has Pacific coast, Andean and Amazonian territory [18]
Early Andean cultures
Caral-Supe, Chavín, Moche, Nazca, Wari and other societies developed complex settlements, rituals and technologies before the Inca period
Inca state
Tawantinsuyu centered in Cusco integrated major Andean territories through roads, administration, agriculture, political alliances and conquest
Spanish conquest
The 1530s conquest associated with Francisco Pizarro involved Indigenous allies, epidemic disease, coercion and political fragmentation of the Inca state
Viceroyalty of Peru
Lima became an important center of Spanish colonial government and commerce in western South America, while Indigenous communities retained varied political and cultural institutions
Colonial labor and hierarchy
Mining, agriculture, forced labor institutions and racial hierarchies shaped colonial economic and social relations
Independence declaration
José de San Martín proclaimed Peruvian independence in Lima on July 28, 1821; the act did not immediately end all royalist resistance [17]
Wars for independence
Battles and campaigns culminating in Junín and Ayacucho in 1824 helped secure the republican break from Spain [17]
Nineteenth-century republican formation
Regional military leaders, constitutional experiments, guano revenues and conflicts shaped the early state
War of the Pacific
The 1879–1883 war against Chile, fought with Bolivia as an ally, deeply affected territory, finance and national politics
Twentieth-century reform
Industrialization, rural migration, civil conflict, land-reform disputes and expanding franchise altered society and state capacity
1968–1980 military government
Military rule under Juan Velasco Alvarado and successors implemented land reform and other major changes before a return to civilian elections
Internal conflict
Conflict involving Shining Path, the MRTA and state security forces in the 1980s and 1990s caused grave human-rights abuses and extensive loss of life
1993 Constitution
Current constitutional framework was enacted after the 1992 political rupture; it remains amended and contested in political debate [10]
Political instability after 2016
Presidential vacancies, impeachment proceedings, protests and changes in executive leadership intensified disputes about representation and accountability
2026 election
June 7 presidential runoff produced a narrow victory for Keiko Fujimori, officially proclaimed by the national electoral tribunal on July 3 [7]
2026 presidential inauguration
Keiko Sofía Fujimori Higuchi assumed the 2026–2031 presidency on July 28, 2026, following the electoral proclamation [8]
2026 legislature
Congressional bicameralism resumed in July 2026, replacing the former single-chamber 130-member model [9]
National holiday
July 28–29 Fiestas Patrias commemorate independence and the foundation of the republican state
Official languages
Spanish is the main official language; Quechua, Aymara and other Indigenous languages have official standing in areas where they predominate under the Constitution [10]
National symbols
Red-white-red flag, national coat of arms featuring vicuña, cinchona and cornucopia, and national anthem
United Nations
Founding member of the United Nations in 1945 [19]
ISO identifiers
PE (alpha-2), PER (alpha-3), 604 (numeric); internet country-code domain .pe [18]

Chronology: Indigenous and Inca histories precede Spanish colonial rule. July 28, 1821 is the independence proclamation; final military consolidation followed, notably in 1824. The 2026 presidency and bicameral Congress are recent institutional changes, not founding-era milestones.

Geography and Population

Total area
Approximately 1,285,216 km² in commonly used international geographic references, including Pacific, Andean and Amazonian regions [2]
Pacific coast
Western coastal region has extensive desert plains, irrigated agricultural valleys, fisheries, ports and major metropolitan settlements
Costa / Sierra / Selva
Traditional division describes coastal, Andean mountain and Amazonian rainforest regions, but each contains considerable environmental variation
Andean Cordillera
Mountain ranges and high plateaus create steep altitude gradients, distinct ecological zones, transport barriers and glacier systems
Highest mountain
Huascarán in the Cordillera Blanca rises to about 6,768 meters, with glaciers important to regional hydrology
Altiplano
Southern high plateau supports pastoralism, mining, cities and culturally significant Indigenous communities
Lake Titicaca
High-elevation lake shared with Bolivia, with extensive cultural history and basin-specific conservation challenges
Amazon Basin
Eastern forested lowlands, rivers and wetlands host high biodiversity and numerous Indigenous peoples
Major river systems
Amazon, Ucayali, Marañón, Huallaga and Madre de Dios basins connect remote settlements and international watersheds
Headwaters and navigation
River networks are central to transport in the east, while seasonal variability and ecological protections constrain development
Coastal current
The Humboldt/Peru Current supports productive marine ecosystems and large fisheries subject to oceanographic variability
El Niño and climate
El Niño-Southern Oscillation can intensify coastal rain, fisheries change and extreme flood events; effects vary by cycle
Earthquake exposure
Active tectonic subduction generates severe earthquakes and Pacific tsunami risks, particularly along the coastal margin
Volcanoes
Southern Peru includes active volcanic zones monitored for ash, lahars and other hazards
Mountain glaciers
Accelerating glacier loss affects water availability, hydropower planning, disaster risks and regional ecosystem function
Administrative divisions
24 departments, the Constitutional Province of Callao and special regional arrangements; government is decentralized under national law
Major cities
Lima-Callao, Arequipa, Trujillo, Chiclayo, Piura, Cusco, Huancayo, Iquitos, Pucallpa and other regional centers
2017 census total population
31,237,385 when directly enumerated people and estimated census omissions are combined in INEI final figures [3]
Direct 2017 enumeration
29,381,884 people were actually counted on census day; the remaining estimated omission was calculated by postenumeration assessment [3]
2017 omissions adjustment
INEI included 1,855,501 estimated omitted persons in its 2017 total, about 5.94% under the final methodology [3]
2025 World Bank population
34,576,665 in the Bank's modeled annual international series, not an independently conducted 2025 national census [1]
Population growth
World Bank reports approximately 1.0% annual population growth in 2025, a derived statistical indicator rather than census count [1]
Population density
About 26.9 residents per km² using 2025 World Bank population and international total area; the actual settlement density varies greatly
Urban concentration
Lima and coastal urban corridors house a high proportion of residents, while Amazonian and highland areas have very different settlement patterns
Natural resources
Copper, gold, zinc, silver, natural gas, fisheries, forest products, agriculture and renewable-energy resources underpin different sectors
Protected landscapes
Huascarán, Manu, Machu Picchu and other protected areas contain biodiversity, archaeological and cultural assets with different legal designations
Forest conservation
Illegal logging, mining contamination, road expansion and wildfire pressure affect Amazonian ecosystems and Indigenous community lands
Environmental exposure
Coastal flooding, highland landslides, glacier retreat, air pollution and water scarcity require regionally differentiated policies

Population methodology: INEI’s 2017 final total of 31,237,385 includes 29,381,884 enumerated people plus 1,855,501 estimated omissions. The World Bank 2025 figure of 34,576,665 uses a different annual estimation method and date.

Economy and International Trade

Economic system
Upper-middle-income market economy with mining, agriculture, fishing, manufacturing, services, construction and significant informal labor
Nominal GDP
US$334.85 billion (2025 World Bank current US dollars), not adjusted for purchasing power parity [1]
GDP per capita
US$9,684.4 (2025 World Bank current dollars), which is not a median wage or purchasing-power measure [1]
Real GDP growth
3.4% in the World Bank 2025 annual series; a retrospective result, not a 2026 forecast [1]
Inflation
Approximately 1.5% in 2025 in the World Bank annual consumer-price series [1]
Modeled unemployment
About 5.1% in the ILO-modeled World Bank 2025 labor series, which cannot describe all informal employment [1]
Currency
Peruvian sol (PEN), written S/; managed within Peru's national monetary framework
Central bank
Banco Central de Reserva del Perú implements monetary policy and maintains economic and financial statistics [6]
Major exports
Copper, gold, zinc, fishmeal and fish oil, refined minerals, agricultural products, textiles and other industrial goods are internationally traded
2025 goods exports
US$90.082 billion according to the Ministry of Foreign Trade and Tourism; a new calendar-year merchandise-export high [4]
Growth in exports
Approximately 21% increase in the ministry's 2025 merchandise series over 2024, influenced by prices and output [4]
Primary destination markets
China, the European Union and United States were cited among leading merchandise-export markets in the ministry's 2025 release [4]
Copper production
Mines in highland and southern regions contribute substantially to exports, public revenue and infrastructure demands
Gold mining
Industrial and artisanal activities supply international markets; unlawful extraction and environmental damage create major regulatory concerns
Zinc and other metals
Zinc, lead, silver, tin and molybdenum are important alongside copper and gold in the broader mineral sector
Agricultural exports
Blueberries, grapes, avocados, coffee, cocoa, asparagus and other products are supported by diverse agroclimatic conditions
Fishing exports
Anchovy fishmeal and fish oil are sensitive to quotas, ocean temperatures and El Niño-related changes in marine stocks
Manufactured exports
The 2025 ministry review reported US$17.965 billion of manufactured-product exports, an industrial subset that should not be added twice to total goods exports [5]
Tourism receipts
International tourism, hotel services and domestic cultural travel contribute to services rather than the goods-export totals
Mining revenue distinction
Export receipts, production values, taxable profits and state royalty collections are different economic measures
Informal employment
Large informal labor and small-enterprise sectors complicate measurement of productivity, wages and social-security coverage
Trade agreements
Peru participates in trade frameworks with regional, Asian, North American and European partners, with product-specific tariff provisions
Port infrastructure
Callao, Paita, Matarani and other Pacific logistics nodes support mineral, agricultural and industrial trade
New port investment
Chancay port development has raised expectations about Asia-Pacific logistics and debates over local environmental and distributional effects
Infrastructure priorities
Mountain transport, water supply, power, schools and healthcare continue to shape regional productivity and inequality
Fiscal and monetary issues
Resource revenues, regional transfers, inflation management and financial stability require distinct institutional policy tools
Trade measurement
Merchandise exports are not total exports of goods and services; USD values are nominal and depend partly on world commodity prices

People and Society

Official language regime
Spanish is official throughout Peru; Quechua, Aymara and other Indigenous languages are also official where predominant under the constitutional framework [10]
Quechua languages
Several Quechuan varieties are spoken in Andean and migratory urban communities, with important differences across local regions
Aymara
Aymara-speaking communities are particularly prominent in the southern highlands and the Lake Titicaca basin
Amazonian languages
Asháninka, Shipibo-Konibo, Awajún, Wampis and numerous additional languages form a highly diverse Indigenous linguistic landscape
Indigenous identity
Self-identification, ancestral communities, mother tongue and official language recognition are separate statistical and legal concepts
Afro-Peruvian communities
Afro-descendant populations have deep histories in coastal agriculture, cities, music, political life and national culture
Migration and cities
Internal migration connects highlands and Amazonian areas with Lima and coastal industry; overseas emigration and immigration also shape communities
Religious landscape
Roman Catholicism remains historically influential alongside evangelical Protestant, other Christian and nonreligious populations
Religious census caution
Published religious percentages from the 2017 census describe respondents at that time, not a direct 2026 measurement of belief or practice [3]
Education system
Primary, secondary and tertiary provision combines public and private institutions, with bilingual/intercultural programs important in Indigenous regions
Major universities
Universidad Nacional Mayor de San Marcos, Pontificia Universidad Católica del Perú and regional universities hold important educational and research roles
Health systems
Public Ministry of Health providers, social security health insurance and private institutions coexist, with substantial geographic inequalities
Altitude and health
Mountain elevations, remote transport and regional water conditions affect public-health logistics and service availability
Cultural heritage
Precolonial architecture, Inca road networks, Spanish-era urban centers and living Indigenous ceremonies coexist with modern arts
Andean music
Huayno and diverse regional traditional genres use instruments and performance forms tied to local cultural histories
Afro-Peruvian music
Festejo, landó and related coastal artistic traditions are important elements of national cultural production
Food heritage
Ceviche, anticuchos, ají-based dishes, potatoes, quinoa and many regional preparations reflect ecological and historical diversity
Urban cultural production
Literature, cinema, television, popular music and journalism are significant in Lima and regional cultural centers
Sports
Football, volleyball, athletics and surfing are prominent, with participation varying by community and economic resources
Social inequality
Rural–urban gaps, informal labor, Indigenous exclusion, gender inequality and access to sanitation are major public policy questions
Political polarization
Protest, electoral competition and mistrust of political institutions have shaped public life during recent presidencies
Environmental justice
Mining, water access, land rights and Amazonian territorial protection generate complex interactions among communities, firms and regulators
Census interpretation
The 2017 INEI total includes adjusted omissions; 2025 World Bank population is a separate modeled annual estimate [3] [1]

Languages and identities: Quechua, Aymara and Amazonian Indigenous languages differ by region and are not interchangeable ethnic identities. Current affiliations, living speakers and census mother-tongue measures need different sources.

Government and Institutions

Constitutional framework
Republic governed under the Constitution of 1993 as amended, with separation of executive, legislative and judicial powers [10]
President
Keiko Sofía Fujimori Higuchi began the 2026–2031 constitutional presidential term on July 28, 2026 [8]
2026 presidential election
National Electoral Jury proclaimed Fujimori on July 3, 2026 following the June 7 runoff, after resolution of election challenges [7]
Presidential vote detail
Official final runoff counts gave Fujimori's ticket 9,223,396 votes to Juntos por el Perú's 9,173,755, a narrow win [7]
Vice presidents
Luis Galarreta and Miguel Torres were officially proclaimed first and second vice presidents on the elected 2026 ticket [7]
Executive authority
President heads the state and executive branch, appoints ministers and operates within constitutional constraints and legislative checks
Council of Ministers
Ministers are responsible for major policy portfolios and participate in cabinet coordination and confidence procedures
Bicameral restoration
Congress resumed two chambers from July 28, 2026 under the 2024 constitutional reform implemented in the 2026 elections [9]
Senate
Sixty senators serve in the upper chamber, selected through nationwide and constituency electoral methods [9]
Chamber of Deputies
One hundred thirty deputies serve in the lower chamber, apportioned to electoral constituencies under national voting rules [9]
Previous institutional arrangement
The former single-chamber Congress had 130 members; that count is now the deputies' chamber, not the whole 2026 bicameral legislature [9]
Electoral calendar
The 2026–2031 congressional and presidential cycle lasts five years, subject to applicable constitutional rules
Judicial institutions
Supreme Court and lower judicial organs operate under the judicial branch, alongside a Constitutional Tribunal with distinct constitutional functions
Electoral bodies
National Electoral Jury, election process office ONPE and civil registration authority RENIEC carry separate election administration functions
Regional government
Departments and constitutional Callao arrangements involve elected regional authorities with functions assigned by national law
Local government
Provinces and districts have municipalities with elected leaders and different urban and rural resources
Constitutional rights
The Constitution provides citizenship, political rights, legal due process and qualified recognition of Indigenous languages and communities [10]
Security institutions
National police and armed forces hold distinct constitutional functions, with security policy subject to political and rights debate
Oversight bodies
Ombudsman, comptroller, prosecutors and audit institutions have different statutory and constitutional powers
Indigenous consultation
Consultation requirements and community rights affect certain natural-resource and infrastructure projects, with contested implementation
Recent protest history
The deaths and injuries during demonstrations in 2022–2023 remain the subject of public debate, investigation and demands for accountability
2026 transition distinction
Current president is Keiko Fujimori, not interim pre-election officeholders; the bicameral Congress has operated since July 2026 [8] [9]

Institutional update: Keiko Fujimori took office July 28, 2026. The country simultaneously returned to a bicameral legislature with 60 senators and 130 deputies; earlier country profiles showing 130 total legislators are outdated.

International Profile

United Nations
Peru is an original United Nations member, participating in regional and global treaties and agencies [19]
Andean Community
Member of the Andean Community together with Bolivia, Colombia and Ecuador, supporting regional commercial and policy coordination [13]
Pacific Alliance
Participates with Chile, Colombia and Mexico in Pacific-oriented economic cooperation and trade initiatives [14]
Asia-Pacific economy
Peru belongs to APEC, with mining, fisheries, services and logistics connecting it to Pacific Rim markets [15]
WTO membership
Participates in WTO trade rules and tariff schedules, distinct from preferential trade agreements [12]
Bilateral investment
China, the United States, European partners and neighboring South American economies are prominent trade and investment counterparts
Amazon cooperation
Peru participates in regional Amazonian conservation and cooperation over river basins, forests and Indigenous rights [16]
Ecuador border
The 1998 Brazil Act and other settlements helped resolve a long-running Peru–Ecuador boundary dispute, though local border cooperation continues
Bolivia relations
Lake Titicaca, Aymara communities, highland trade, transport and shared water issues connect the two countries
Chile relations
Historical effects of the War of the Pacific coexist with present-day maritime commerce and cross-border social relationships
Pacific maritime jurisdiction
Coastal fisheries, conservation and shipping policy operate under distinct maritime and international legal regimes
UNESCO inscriptions
Thirteen properties appear on UNESCO's 2026 Peru World Heritage State Party list, spanning cultural, natural and mixed categories [11]
Cultural UNESCO examples
Machu Picchu, City of Cusco, Caral-Supe, Nasca lines, Chan Chan, Chankillo and segments of the Qhapaq Ñan network are inscribed [11]
Natural UNESCO examples
Huascarán and Manu national parks plus the mixed Río Abiseo property contribute to the thirteen-property count [11]
Tentative heritage proposals
Proposals under review are not automatically inscribed properties; multiple archaeological components may belong to one UNESCO listing [11]
Tourism diplomacy
Cultural sites, protected landscapes and international travel generate economic and conservation relationships
Humanitarian migration
Cross-border movement of Peruvians and foreign residents requires separate accounting from tourism and commercial services
Climate diplomacy
Glacier changes, El Niño risks, Amazon conservation and ocean fisheries are central to international environmental work
International finance
IMF, World Bank, Inter-American Development Bank and development institutions support distinct economic-data and funding activities
Time zone
Peru Time (PET), UTC−5 year-round, without general daylight-saving adjustment
International dialing
+51; .pe country-code top-level domain

UNESCO interpretation: Peru has thirteen inscribed World Heritage properties, divided among cultural, natural and mixed categories. Tentative sites and constituent archaeological features are not separate additional inscriptions.

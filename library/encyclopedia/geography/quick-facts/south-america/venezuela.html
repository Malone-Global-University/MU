Country Overview

Venezuela, officially the Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela, occupies the northern edge of South America, extending from the Caribbean coast through the Andes, Llanos plains, Orinoco basin and Guiana Shield. Caracas is the capital. Indigenous peoples, Spanish colonial institutions, independence struggles led by Simón Bolívar and others, the nineteenth-century republic and the rise of twentieth-century petroleum have decisively shaped the country’s history. Rich ecosystems and very large proven oil reserves coexist with severe infrastructure, macroeconomic and environmental challenges.

Venezuela underwent a major political rupture in 2026. Following the contested July 2024 presidential election and Nicolás Maduro’s inauguration in January 2025, United States forces captured Maduro in Caracas on January 3, 2026. Delcy Rodríguez, previously vice president, was installed as interim president on January 5. Political legitimacy, electoral reform and the eventual conduct of a broadly accepted national vote remained central unresolved issues in reporting through September 2026. Demographic displacement has been extensive: UNHCR estimated nearly 7.9 million Venezuelan refugees and migrants worldwide as of December 2025. World Bank 2025 economic figures and alternative UN national-account estimates use different reporting series and should not be silently equated. [3] [4] [7]

Identity and State Formation

Official name
Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela (República Bolivariana de Venezuela), republic under the 1999 constitutional framework
Capital
Caracas, seat of major national executive, legislative and judicial institutions [2]
UN region
South America according to the UN M49 classification, with a northern Caribbean maritime frontage [15]
Indigenous societies
Arawakan, Cariban and other Indigenous populations occupied coastal, plains, river and highland zones before colonization
Indigenous continuity
Warao, Wayuu, Pemón, Yanomami and numerous other peoples maintain distinct histories, languages and territorial claims
Early Spanish contact
European expeditions reached the coastline at the end of the fifteenth century; colonization expanded unevenly inland
Colonial economy
Agriculture, cacao, livestock, regional commerce and coerced labor shaped diverse colonial communities
Colonial administration
Provinces became linked to the Captaincy General of Venezuela and late-colonial regional institutions
1810 political movement
Caracas authorities challenged Spanish colonial rule in April 1810 during the wider imperial crisis
1811 independence declaration
A Venezuelan congress declared independence on July 5, 1811, beginning a prolonged process of war and state formation
Wars for independence
Conflicts involved Spanish royalists, revolutionary forces, free and enslaved people, and regional political factions
Battle of Carabobo
The June 24, 1821 battle contributed decisively to the defeat of royalist forces in central Venezuela
Gran Colombia
Venezuelan territory formed part of the Republic of Colombia (Gran Colombia) during the independence-era federation
1830 separate republic
The Venezuelan republic emerged from Gran Colombia in 1830 after political division and separation
Nineteenth-century conflicts
Civil wars, federalist struggles and regional caudillo power influenced state consolidation
Twentieth-century oil transformation
Petroleum development altered exports, urbanization, external investment and the public treasury
1958 democratic transition
The fall of Marcos Pérez Jiménez opened a period of competitive party politics and institutional bargaining
Punto Fijo period
Dominance of AD and COPEI and growing social demands marked the second half of the twentieth century
1989 Caracazo
Widespread unrest and deadly state repression challenged confidence in political and economic institutions
1992 coup attempts
Military uprisings, including one associated with Hugo Chávez, preceded later constitutional change
1998 Chávez election
Hugo Chávez won the presidency and initiated a far-reaching reform and power-concentration program
1999 Constitution
New constitution renamed the state and reorganized republican institutions, with subsequent amendments and disputes
2013 Maduro succession
Nicolás Maduro succeeded Chávez after his death and won the contested 2013 election
Economic and humanitarian crisis
Severe inflation, shortages, reduced oil output and mass emigration deepened during the 2010s and 2020s
2024 presidential election
Electoral authorities announced Maduro's victory, while opposition tally records and independent assessments challenged the official result [4]
January 2025 inauguration
Maduro began a claimed new presidential term amid continued disputes over the July 2024 electoral process
January 2026 removal
U.S. forces captured Nicolás Maduro and Cilia Flores on January 3, 2026, producing a sudden political transition [3]
January 2026 interim presidency
Former vice president Delcy Rodríguez was sworn in as interim president on January 5, 2026 [3]
National holiday
Independence Day is July 5, commemorating the 1811 declaration rather than the final end of royalist military resistance
Official language
Spanish is the principal official language; Indigenous languages have constitutional recognition for Indigenous peoples
National symbols
Yellow-blue-red tricolor, coat of arms and national anthem Gloria al Bravo Pueblo
UN membership
Founding-era member of the United Nations, admitted November 15, 1945 [2]
Country identifiers
VE (alpha-2), VEN (alpha-3), 862 (numeric); internet domain .ve [15]

Key chronology: the 1811 declaration and 1821 Carabobo campaign mark different independence stages. The July 2024 election remained disputed; Maduro was captured January 3, 2026, and Delcy Rodríguez took office as interim president January 5. [3] [4]

Geography and Population

Surface area
UN statistical profile reports approximately 929,690 km²; other references quote different totals due to measurement conventions [2]
Land neighbors
Colombia to the west, Brazil to the south and Guyana to the east; the eastern boundary is linked to an unresolved territorial dispute
Caribbean frontage
Northern coastal regions contain ports, bays, islands and densely populated metropolitan corridors
Gulf of Venezuela
Maritime waters and coastal zones are important for transport, fisheries, petroleum and boundary management
Andes
Northwestern mountain ranges include the Cordillera de Mérida, alpine páramo and steep valleys
Highest summit
Pico Bolívar in the Venezuelan Andes reaches about 4,978 meters, depending on measurement convention
Llanos
Broad central plains support cattle-raising, savanna ecosystems, farming and seasonal wetlands
Orinoco River
One of the continent's major river systems drains much of Venezuela and connects plains, industrial corridors and delta settlements
Orinoco Delta
Extensive delta wetlands support mangroves and Warao communities and face changing industrial and climate pressures
Guiana Shield
Southern uplands host ancient geology, high endemism, mineral resources and protected rainforests
Tepuis
Tabletop mountains including those in the Guiana Highlands contain distinctive evolutionary and ecological communities
Angel Falls
Salto Ángel/Kerepakupai Merú is among the world's tallest uninterrupted waterfalls, in the Canaima landscape
Lake Maracaibo
Large western lake-basin system is central to historical petroleum development and urban industry
Lake Valencia
Central inland lake basin faces water-quality, urban and industrial management concerns
Major cities
Caracas, Maracaibo, Valencia, Barquisimeto, Maracay, Ciudad Guayana, Barcelona and Maturín
Climate zones
Tropical lowlands, seasonally dry plains, montane cloud forests and alpine environments create major regional contrasts
Rain and river seasonality
Wet and dry seasons vary geographically, influencing ranching, hydropower and transport
Hydropower
Guri dam and the Caroní system supply important electricity, subject to drought and infrastructure reliability
Earthquake risk
Northern Venezuela lies near active tectonic boundaries; June 2026 earthquakes caused extensive damage and casualties [4]
Flood and landslide risk
Tropical rainfall, mountain slopes and unplanned urban settlement create repeated severe flood and landslide exposure
Coastal environmental hazards
Refining, oil spills, sewage, port expansion and wetland loss affect different parts of the littoral
Deforestation and mining
Mining and unauthorized extraction threaten forests, biodiversity and Indigenous territories in southern regions
Canaima National Park
UNESCO natural World Heritage property covers tepui landscapes and important ecosystems [13]
Administrative structure
Twenty-three states plus the Capital District and federally administered dependencies under national constitutional arrangements
Capital metropolitan area
Caracas lies in a valley near the coast, with high density, informal settlements and complex transport infrastructure
2011 census baseline
The most recent national census widely used for historic comparison dates to 2011; newer models are not direct re-enumerations
2025 World Bank population
28,516,896 in the Bank's annual international population model [1]
Population density
About 30.7 persons per km² using 2025 World Bank population and UN total area; regional concentrations vary sharply
Migration and population
Large emigration changes age profiles, families, labor markets and the interpretation of household denominators [7]
Essequibo territorial issue
Venezuela claims much of the Essequibo region, administered by Guyana; the dispute is not resolved by Venezuelan domestic legislation [4]
Protected landscapes
Canaima, Henri Pittier, Sierra Nevada and other protected areas have distinct management and ecosystem priorities

Area methodology: UN data reports 929,690 km², while other country references publish lower totals using alternative surface conventions. Claims to the Guyanese-administered Essequibo region are disputed and must not be added to sovereign Venezuelan territory as a settled fact. [2] [4]

Economy and International Trade

Economic system
Oil-centered economy with state enterprises, private business, agriculture, industry and widespread informal and dollar-denominated transactions
2025 nominal GDP
US$99.66 billion in World Bank current-dollar series; alternative international sources report materially different estimates [1] [2]
2025 GDP per capita
US$3,494.8 in the World Bank current-dollar series; not a household income figure [1]
2025 real GDP growth
1.6% in the World Bank annual series; other institution and national estimates can differ substantially [1]
Inflation data caution
World Bank country dashboard displays 2016 as its last CPI inflation observation, not a 2025 CPI rate [1]
2026 price pressures
International Monetary Fund estimates cited by the UK Parliament described triple-digit inflation in early 2026 [3]
Modeled unemployment
5.3% in the 2025 World Bank ILO-modeled series; it is not a comprehensive measure of job quality or informality [1]
Currency
The bolívar is the official monetary unit; dollar pricing and transactions coexist with national-currency payments
Currency redenomination
The 2021 monetary reconversion removed zeros from nominal units; legacy values must not be compared without adjustment
Central bank
Banco Central de Venezuela is a key monetary institution; data availability and political independence remain issues of public debate
National oil company
Petróleos de Venezuela, S.A. (PDVSA) operates with subsidiaries and joint ventures in petroleum extraction and refining
Proven oil reserves
EIA described roughly 303 billion barrels in proved crude-oil reserves in its country analysis, largely extra-heavy Orinoco resources [8]
Reserve versus production
Proved underground reserves do not equal annual output, export volume, refinery capacity or commercially available cash flow
Orinoco Belt
Extra-heavy crude requires specialized processing, diluent, transport and technology; operational expenses vary strongly
Maracaibo basin
Historic western oilfields and service infrastructure remain important even as production patterns have changed
Oil export sector
Crude and petroleum products dominate the strategic export base, though sanctions and reporting shifts complicate time-series comparisons
2025 output context
Reuters reported roughly 1.1 million barrels per day of average Venezuelan oil production in 2025, not 303 billion barrels annually [12]
2026 energy agreements
The interim government pursued oil agreements and discussed returning non-essential state assets to private ownership in October 2026 [9]
Sanctions and trade
U.S. and other restrictions changed banking, logistics and investment; sanctions coverage varies by entity and date
Refining infrastructure
Aging units, maintenance needs and electricity outages constrain some domestic and external petroleum flows
Non-oil industries
Food processing, chemicals, metals, construction and consumer goods have varied by region and economic conditions
Mining
Gold, iron ore, bauxite and other minerals have economic relevance, with illicit extraction and labor-rights hazards
Agriculture
Maize, rice, sugarcane, coffee, cacao, livestock and horticulture supply local and export-oriented markets
Cacao traditions
Distinctive Venezuelan cacao varieties and estates contribute to specialty export markets and historical agricultural identity
Hydropower and reliability
Large dams support national electricity, while transmission, drought and maintenance affect stability
Public finance
Government oil receipts, debt service, monetary financing and sanctions complicate national fiscal management
External debt
International debt restructuring, creditor claims and legal enforcement remain prominent transition questions [3]
Remittances
Household support from diaspora members is economically significant but must not be equated with formal goods exports
Humanitarian impact
Loss of purchasing power, health-service constraints and migration create challenges not captured by GDP alone [7]
Tourism and services
Caribbean sites, mountain landscapes and cultural destinations retain potential subject to security and infrastructure conditions

People and Society

Predominant language
Spanish is the main nationwide language of administration, education, broadcast media and commerce
Indigenous languages
Wayuunaiki, Warao, Pemón, Yanomami and other languages maintain official recognition within Indigenous rights frameworks
Indigenous populations
Wayuu, Warao, Pemón, Ye'kwana, Yanomami and many other peoples have distinct territorial and social histories
Afro-Venezuelan communities
African-descendant histories span coastal villages, cacao agriculture, cities, culture and emancipatory struggles
Mixed ancestries
Venezuelan identities reflect long Indigenous, European, African, Caribbean and more recent migration histories
Regional variation
Andean, Llanos, Orinoco, Caribbean and Guiana regions retain distinctive accents, foodways and settlement patterns
Religion
Roman Catholic heritage remains influential alongside Protestant, evangelical, other religious and nonreligious traditions
Education institutions
Public and private schools and universities operate amid resource constraints, migration and unequal regional service access
Universities
Universidad Central de Venezuela, Universidad Simón Bolívar, Universidad de Los Andes and other institutions remain major intellectual centers
Health services
Public hospitals, social programs, clinics and private care face different funding and workforce shortages
Medical migration
Loss of trained health professionals has affected capacity in some regions during the broader emigration crisis
2025 diaspora estimate
UNHCR reported nearly 7.9 million Venezuelan refugees and migrants worldwide in its December 2025 data [7]
Regional host countries
Colombia, Peru, Brazil, Ecuador and others host substantial Venezuelan communities in need of integration and public services
Migration measure caution
UNHCR's worldwide total refers to refugees and migrants abroad, not a subtraction mechanically performed from the 2025 resident population [7]
Family remittances
Cross-border household transfers affect consumption, schooling and coping strategies, but data quality varies
Poverty measurement
National and international poverty series differ widely in coverage and method; outdated survey figures cannot stand for 2026
Food security
Food access varies by household income, local supply, imports and social assistance
Water and infrastructure
Urban water delivery, power outages, drainage and waste management differ sharply by locality
Music
Joropo, gaita, salsa, merengue and local folk and popular traditions illustrate regional cultural pluralism
Visual arts and literature
Arturo Michelena, Jesús Soto, Carlos Cruz-Diez, Rómulo Gallegos and others helped shape national creative history
Cuisine
Arepas, pabellón criollo, cachapas, hallacas and regional fish and cassava dishes are widely recognized
Baseball
Baseball occupies a prominent place in professional sport and diaspora engagement
Football and other sport
Football, basketball, boxing and athletics have substantial participation and international representation
Cultural sites
UNESCO-listed Coro, Ciudad Universitaria de Caracas and Canaima represent different cultural and natural histories [13]
Political polarization
The disputed 2024 election, repression claims, exile and competing accounts of legitimacy remain central civic questions [4]
Civil society
Community networks, independent journalists, humanitarian groups and faith organizations operate in a contested political environment
November 2026 opposition gathering
María Corina Machado publicly demanded releases of remaining political prisoners after returning from exile, according to November 6 reporting [11]
Human rights
Detention practices, due process, protest restrictions and protection for opposition activity remain major international concerns [4]
2026 disaster response
June 2026 earthquakes strained homes, hospitals and transportation networks, underscoring uneven resilience [4]
Indigenous environmental justice
Mining, conservation policy and cross-border security create serious issues for Indigenous territories in the south
Diasporic cultural ties
Cross-border music, businesses, food and professional networks keep social connections active across the Americas

Migration accounting: the UNHCR figure of nearly 7.9 million refugees and migrants worldwide is dated December 2025 and draws on host-government reporting. It is not a direct headcount of people departing in 2025, nor an alternative population census. [7]

Government and Institutions

Constitutional text
The 1999 Constitution, with subsequent amendments, defines the Bolivarian republic and formal separation of public powers
Political system
Formal presidential republic with a unicameral National Assembly and extensive controversy over judicial and electoral independence
Interim president
Delcy Rodríguez became interim president on January 5, 2026 following Maduro's removal on January 3 [3]
Predecessor
Nicolás Maduro held the presidency from 2013 until his January 2026 capture by U.S. forces [4]
Officeholder status
Rodríguez remains interim president in late-October 2026 reporting; she did not take office through a newly certified national presidential election [9]
2024 election dispute
Electoral authorities declared a Maduro victory, while opposition tally evidence and external observers questioned that outcome [4]
Opposition leadership
Edmundo González Urrutia and María Corina Machado remain prominent opposition figures; Machado had returned from exile by early November 2026 [4] [11]
Political transition
Debates focus on electoral credibility, civil liberties, foreign influence, political prisoner releases and legal continuity [3]
Election timetable
In October 2026 Rodríguez ruled out an immediate election and called for institutional and electoral reforms before setting a voting date [10]
Political-detainee estimates
Rights organization Foro Penal estimated more than 300 people remained detained for political reasons in early November 2026 [11]
Executive powers
Presidency directs the cabinet and state administration within formal constitutional structures whose practical independence is contested
National Assembly
Unicameral legislature with 285 members following the 2025 electoral process [5]
Legislative election
Election was held May 25, 2025, with major opposition coalition boycott and disputed representativeness [6]
2025 governing coalition
Electoral authorities reported a large majority for the government-aligned Grand Patriotic Pole alliance [6]
Assembly composition caution
Seats won by an alliance and the number of members physically seated are different measures under parliamentary reporting
Legislative speaker
The president of the National Assembly presides over plenary procedures and may play a role in constitutional succession
Judiciary
Supreme Tribunal of Justice and ordinary courts exercise formal judicial functions, amid sustained criticism of independence
Electoral administration
Consejo Nacional Electoral governs national electoral procedures; credibility and verification have been heavily disputed
Constitutional oversight
Citizen-power institutions including prosecutors and ombuds bodies hold formal accountability roles
Federal structure
States have governors and legislatures; the Capital District has a distinct institutional framework
Local government
Municipal mayors and councils perform local services with unequal resources and central-state coordination
Indigenous representation
Constitutional and statutory provisions recognize Indigenous peoples and particular electoral representation arrangements
Security agencies
Armed forces, police and intelligence institutions exercise significant roles and are subject to rights and accountability debates
Fiscal governance
Oil contracts, public debt and enterprise supervision raise transparency issues during the 2026 transition
International legal questions
U.S. capture of a sitting Venezuelan head of state raised contentious sovereignty, legal and diplomatic issues [4]
National reconstruction
Recovery of electricity, hospitals, transport, economic confidence and public institutions presents long-horizon challenges
Electoral safeguards
Transparent tabulation, independent observers and equal political participation are repeatedly identified as necessary for credible elections [4]

Officeholder and legitimacy distinction: Delcy Rodríguez serves as interim president after January 2026 and in October ruled out immediate presidential elections while proposing electoral changes. The 2024 election dispute remains unresolved. The National Assembly has 285 members in IPU reporting. [10] [5]

International Profile

United Nations membership
Venezuela joined the United Nations on November 15, 1945 [2]
Caribbean geographic identity
Caribbean coastline, island dependencies and maritime transport link the country to Caribbean affairs
Colombia relationship
Border commerce, migration, river systems, security and shared family networks shape bilateral issues
Brazil relationship
Amazonian forests, Indigenous communities, commerce and migration connect southern frontier areas
Guyana territorial dispute
Venezuela claims the Essequibo territory, which Guyana administers; the international legal dispute is ongoing [4]
International Court of Justice
The Guyana–Venezuela 1899 arbitral-award case involves competing legal positions, provisional measures and unresolved merits questions
2023 referendum
Venezuelan government claims based on a domestic referendum did not constitute an internationally accepted transfer of Guyanese-administered territory [6]
UNHCR humanitarian scope
Nearly 7.9 million Venezuelan refugees and migrants were counted worldwide in the UNHCR December 2025 update [7]
Latin American host states
Colombia, Peru, Brazil, Ecuador and others coordinate migration, regularization and humanitarian services
U.S.–Venezuela 2026 relations
January 2026 intervention and subsequent energy negotiations changed diplomatic and commercial relations [3]
Oil diplomacy
Petroleum production and infrastructure attract external investors, lenders and governments with differing sanctions regimes
OPEC
Venezuela is a founding member of the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries and holds major recorded oil reserves [8]
MERCOSUR status
Venezuela's membership was suspended; do not present it as a fully active participant in ordinary voting arrangements [14]
Andean integration
Venezuela previously belonged to the Andean Community; withdrawal should not be confused with current membership
UNESCO inscriptions
Three World Heritage properties: Coro and its Port, Canaima National Park, and Ciudad Universitaria de Caracas [13]
Heritage categories
Two cultural properties and one natural property are inscribed, not three cultural sites [13]
Climate concerns
Deforestation, hydropower variability, coastal pollution and extreme rainfall affect international environmental cooperation
Orinoco ecosystem
Wetlands, fisheries, forest habitats and river navigation connect conservation to livelihoods
Transnational mining
Informal mining, gold flows and environmental crime create regional regulatory and border-enforcement problems
Sanctions and finance
Restrictions, debt servicing and oil receipts are affected by changing legal authorizations and bank compliance standards
International statistical gaps
Economic time series differ substantially by institution; 2025 World Bank GDP should not be replaced by the UN digest without explanation [1] [2]
2026 earthquake assistance
Large June earthquakes led to humanitarian and recovery needs, according to U.S. Congressional research [4]
Time zone
Venezuela Time (VET), UTC−4, without general daylight-saving observance
Telephone and domain
International dialing code +58; country-code internet domain .ve
Diaspora connections
Education, entrepreneurship, family remittances and cultural networks continue across Latin America, North America and Europe

Territorial distinction: Venezuela asserts claims in Essequibo, but Guyana administers the area and the dispute is the subject of international legal proceedings. UNESCO counts three inscribed sites; tentative properties are not additional inscriptions. [4] [13]

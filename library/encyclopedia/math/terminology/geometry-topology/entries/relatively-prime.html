Relatively Prime

Subfield: Foundations & Logic

Etymology & Roots

Two integers are relatively prime when their greatest common divisor is $1$.

$$ \boxed{ \gcd(a,b)=1 }. $$

The synonymous term coprime is common in number theory. The word relatively is important: the integers need not themselves be prime numbers. Their primality is being judged relative to one another through their common factors.

For example:

$$ \boxed{ \gcd(8,15)=1 }, $$

so $8$ and $15$ are relatively prime even though both are composite.

Operational Nuance

Common-Factor Definition

Two integers are relatively prime precisely when their only positive common divisor is $1$.

$$ \boxed{ \{d>0:d\mid a\text{ and }d\mid b\} = \{1\} }. $$

Prime-Factor Interpretation

Equivalently, $a$ and $b$ are relatively prime when they share no prime factor.

For example:

$$ 18=2\cdot3^2, \qquad 35=5\cdot7, $$

so:

$$ \boxed{ \gcd(18,35)=1 }. $$

Euclidean Algorithm Test

Coprimality can be tested efficiently with the Euclidean algorithm. For example:

$$ 35=1(24)+11, $$
$$ 24=2(11)+2, $$
$$ 11=5(2)+1. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \gcd(35,24)=1 }. $$

Bézout Characterization

Two integers $a$ and $b$ are relatively prime exactly when there exist integers $x$ and $y$ such that:

$$ \boxed{ ax+by=1 }. $$

This is a fundamental bridge between greatest common divisors and linear Diophantine equations.

Reduced Fractions

An integer fraction:

$$ \frac{a}{b}, \qquad b\neq0, $$

is in lowest terms when:

$$ \boxed{ \gcd(|a|,|b|)=1 }. $$

Consecutive Integers

Consecutive integers are always relatively prime:

$$ \boxed{ \gcd(n,n+1)=1 }. $$

Distinct Prime Numbers

Any two distinct prime numbers are relatively prime.

$$ \boxed{ p\neq q,\quad p,q\text{ prime} \Longrightarrow \gcd(p,q)=1 }. $$

A Prime and a Nonmultiple

If $p$ is prime and $p\nmid a$, then:

$$ \boxed{ \gcd(p,a)=1 }. $$

Product Property

If $a$ is relatively prime to both $b$ and $c$, then:

$$ \boxed{ \gcd(a,bc)=1 }. $$

Euclid's Lemma

If:

$$ \gcd(a,b)=1 \quad\text{and}\quad a\mid bc, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ a\mid c }. $$

Least Common Multiple

For positive relatively prime integers:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{lcm}(a,b)=ab }. $$

More generally:

$$ \gcd(a,b)\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)=|ab|. $$

Modular Arithmetic

An integer $a$ has a multiplicative inverse modulo $n$ exactly when:

$$ \boxed{ \gcd(a,n)=1 }. $$

Thus relatively prime residue classes are precisely the invertible classes modulo $n$.

Euler's Totient Function

Euler's totient function counts positive integers up to $n$ that are relatively prime to $n$:

$$ \boxed{ \varphi(n) = \#\{ 1\leq k\leq n: \gcd(k,n)=1 \} }. $$

Pairwise Relatively Prime

A collection is pairwise relatively prime when every pair of distinct members is relatively prime:

$$ \boxed{ \gcd(a_i,a_j)=1 \quad \text{for }i\neq j }. $$

Collectively Coprime Is Weaker

Having:

$$ \gcd(a,b,c)=1 $$

does not necessarily mean every pair is relatively prime. For example:

$$ \boxed{ \gcd(6,10,15)=1 } $$

even though no pair among $6$, $10$, and $15$ is relatively prime.

Zero and Coprimality

Since:

$$ \gcd(0,a)=|a|, $$

zero is relatively prime only to $1$ and $-1$.

One

The integers $1$ and $-1$ are relatively prime to every integer because their greatest common divisor in absolute value is $1$.

Common Misconception

Relatively prime does not mean that both numbers are prime. It means only that they have no common prime factor:

$$ \boxed{ 8\text{ and }15 \text{ are relatively prime} }. $$

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\gcd(a,b)=1$ "A and b are relatively prime," or "a and b are coprime."
$\gcd(8,15)=1$ "Eight and fifteen are relatively prime."
$ax+by=1$ "A x plus b y equals one," the Bézout condition for coprimality.
$\gcd(a,n)=1$ "A is relatively prime to n."
$a\perp b$ "Use with caution: this notation may mean coprime in some number-theory contexts but usually means perpendicular elsewhere."
$\gcd(a_i,a_j)=1$ "The integers are pairwise relatively prime."

The terms relatively prime and coprime are mathematically equivalent in standard integer number theory.

Defense Notation Advice: The clearest notation is usually $\gcd(a,b)=1$. Avoid inferring that either integer must itself be prime. For several integers, distinguish carefully between a total gcd of $1$ and the stronger condition of pairwise coprimality.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Definition: Two integers are relatively prime when their greatest common divisor is $1$.
  • Synonym: Coprime.
  • Common-Factor View: Their only positive common divisor is $1$.
  • Prime-Factor View: They share no prime factor.
  • Euclidean Algorithm: Provides an efficient test for coprimality.
  • Bézout Identity: $\gcd(a,b)=1$ exactly when $ax+by=1$ has integer solutions.
  • Fractions: Numerator and denominator of a reduced integer fraction are relatively prime.
  • Consecutive Integers: Always relatively prime.
  • Distinct Primes: Always relatively prime.
  • LCM: For positive coprime integers, $\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)=ab$.
  • Modular Arithmetic: $a$ is invertible modulo $n$ exactly when $\gcd(a,n)=1$.
  • Euler Totient: $\varphi(n)$ counts residue representatives relatively prime to $n$.
  • Euclid's Lemma: Coprimality strengthens divisibility conclusions.
  • Pairwise Coprime: Every distinct pair has gcd $1$.
  • Collective GCD: A set may have overall gcd $1$ without being pairwise coprime.
  • Zero: Relatively prime only to $\pm1$.
  • Units: $1$ and $-1$ are relatively prime to every integer.
  • Number Theory: Coprimality is central to divisibility, congruences, Diophantine equations, and factorization.
  • Cryptography: Modular inverses and many public-key constructions depend on coprimality conditions.
  • Common Error: Assuming relatively prime numbers must both be prime.
  • Common Error: Confusing $\gcd(a,b)=1$ with $a-b=1$.
  • Common Error: Assuming an overall gcd of $1$ implies pairwise coprimality.
  • Common Error: Assuming numbers with different parity are automatically coprime.
  • Core Interpretation: Relative primality is a relationship between integers, not a claim that either integer is itself prime: $$ \boxed{ a\text{ and }b\text{ are relatively prime} \Longleftrightarrow \gcd(a,b)=1 }. $$ Equivalently, they share no prime divisor. This simple condition underlies reduced fractions, Bézout identities, modular inverses, Euler's totient function, and major divisibility results throughout elementary number theory.

Sum/Difference Identities

Subfield: Geometry & Topology

Etymology & Roots

Sum/difference identities are trigonometric identities for finding the sine, cosine, or tangent of an angle expressed as the sum or difference of two angles.

$$ \boxed{ A\pm B \longrightarrow \sin(A\pm B),\ \cos(A\pm B),\ \tan(A\pm B) }. $$

They are also called angle-sum and angle-difference identities. Their structure reflects the geometry of combining rotations on the unit circle.

Operational Nuance

Sine Sum and Difference

$$ \boxed{ \sin(A+B) = \sin A\cos B+\cos A\sin B } $$
$$ \boxed{ \sin(A-B) = \sin A\cos B-\cos A\sin B }. $$

For sine, the sign inside the identity matches the sign between the original angles.

Cosine Sum and Difference

$$ \boxed{ \cos(A+B) = \cos A\cos B-\sin A\sin B } $$
$$ \boxed{ \cos(A-B) = \cos A\cos B+\sin A\sin B }. $$

For cosine, the internal sign is opposite the sign joining the original angles.

Tangent Sum and Difference

$$ \boxed{ \tan(A+B) = \frac{\tan A+\tan B}{1-\tan A\tan B} } $$
$$ \boxed{ \tan(A-B) = \frac{\tan A-\tan B}{1+\tan A\tan B} }. $$

These formulas apply where all displayed tangent expressions are defined and the denominator on the right is nonzero.

Exact-Angle Evaluation

An unfamiliar angle can often be decomposed into familiar angles. For example:

$$ 75^\circ=45^\circ+30^\circ. $$

Therefore:

$$ \sin75^\circ = \sin45^\circ\cos30^\circ + \cos45^\circ\sin30^\circ. $$

Using standard exact values:

$$ \boxed{ \sin75^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{6}+\sqrt{2}}{4} }. $$

Difference Example

$$ 15^\circ=45^\circ-30^\circ, $$

so:

$$ \boxed{ \cos15^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{6}+\sqrt{2}}{4} }. $$

Derivation from Rotation

The identities can be derived geometrically from rotation matrices. A rotation by $\theta$ is represented by:

$$ R(\theta) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta\\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix}. $$

Since consecutive rotations satisfy:

$$ \boxed{ R(A)R(B)=R(A+B) }, $$

comparing matrix entries yields the sine and cosine sum identities.

Deriving Tangent Identities

The tangent formulas follow from:

$$ \tan(A+B) = \frac{\sin(A+B)}{\cos(A+B)} $$

together with the sine and cosine sum identities, followed by division by $\cos A\cos B$ where valid.

Connection to Other Identities

Double-angle, half-angle, product-to-sum, and sum-to-product formulas can all be derived from or connected back to the angle-sum and angle-difference identities.

Common Misconception

Trigonometric functions do not distribute over addition:

$$ \boxed{ \sin(A+B) \neq \sin A+\sin B } $$

in general. The sum/difference identities provide the correct replacement.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\sin(A+B)$ "Sine of the sum of A and B."
$\sin(A-B)$ "Sine of the difference of A and B."
$\cos(A+B)$ "Cosine of A plus B."
$\cos(A-B)$ "Cosine of A minus B."
$\tan(A+B)$ "Tangent of the sum of A and B."
$\tan(A-B)$ "Tangent of the difference of A and B."

A useful verbal memory pattern is: sine keeps the sign; cosine switches the sign. For tangent, the numerator keeps the sign while the denominator uses the opposite sign.

Defense Notation Advice: Keep the entire angle $A\pm B$ grouped inside the trigonometric function. Do not distribute sine, cosine, or tangent over addition. For tangent identities, also verify that every tangent expression and denominator used in the formula is defined.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Purpose: Evaluate or transform trigonometric functions of summed or subtracted angles.
  • Sine: The sign between the two product terms matches the angle sign.
  • Cosine: The sign between the two product terms is opposite the angle sign.
  • Tangent: The numerator keeps the angle sign and the denominator uses the opposite sign.
  • Exact Values: Useful for angles such as $15^\circ$, $75^\circ$, $105^\circ$, and related radian measures.
  • Unit Circle: The identities express how trigonometric coordinates behave under combined rotations.
  • Rotation Matrices: Matrix multiplication provides a structural derivation of the sine and cosine formulas.
  • Identity Proofs: Frequently used to expand or simplify trigonometric expressions.
  • Double-Angle Formulas: Setting $B=A$ produces important special cases.
  • Difference Formulas: Setting $B$ to a convenient reference angle can simplify unfamiliar angles.
  • Periodic Motion: Useful when combining or shifting phases in oscillatory models.
  • Complex Numbers: Euler's formula provides another route to deriving the same identities.
  • Common Error: Writing $\sin(A+B)=\sin A+\sin B$.
  • Common Error: Forgetting the sign reversal in the cosine formulas.
  • Common Error: Using the wrong sign in the tangent denominator.
  • Common Error: Ignoring tangent-domain restrictions.
  • Core Interpretation: Sum/difference identities describe trigonometric behavior under angle composition: $$ \boxed{ \sin(A\pm B) = \sin A\cos B \pm \cos A\sin B } $$ and: $$ \boxed{ \cos(A\pm B) = \cos A\cos B \mp \sin A\sin B }. $$ They form a central bridge between unit-circle geometry, rotations, exact-angle computation, trigonometric equations, and later identity families.

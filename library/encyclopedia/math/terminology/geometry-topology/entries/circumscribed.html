Circumscribed

Subfield: Geometry, Spatial Analysis & Topology

Etymology & Roots

In geometry, circumscribed describes a figure drawn around another figure while satisfying a specified contact condition.

$$ \boxed{ \text{outer figure} + \text{required contact} \longrightarrow \text{circumscribed relationship} }. $$

The word is relational, so the phrase should normally identify both figures.

Two especially common usages are:

  • a polygon circumscribed about a circle,
  • a circle circumscribed about a polygon.

These describe opposite containment directions and should not be confused.

Operational Nuance

Polygon Circumscribed about a Circle

A polygon is circumscribed about a circle when every side of the polygon is tangent to that circle.

$$ \boxed{ \text{each side tangent to one common circle} }. $$

The circle is then an incircle of the polygon.

Tangential Polygon

A polygon circumscribed about a circle is also called a tangential polygon.

$$ \boxed{ \text{circumscribed about a circle} \iff \text{tangential} }. $$

Tangency Condition

If a circle centered at $I$ touches a side at point $T$, then the radius to the tangency point is perpendicular to that side:

$$ \boxed{ IT\perp\text{side} }. $$

Triangle Circumscribed about a Circle

Every nondegenerate triangle can be circumscribed about a circle because every triangle has an incircle.

The center of that incircle is the incenter.

$$ \boxed{ d(I,AB)=d(I,BC)=d(I,CA) }. $$

Square Circumscribed about a Circle

A square can be circumscribed about a circle centered at the square's center. Each side is tangent to the circle.

If the square has side length $s$, then the inradius is:

$$ \boxed{ r=\frac{s}{2} }. $$

Circle Circumscribed about a Polygon

A circle is circumscribed about a polygon when the polygon's vertices all lie on that circle.

$$ \boxed{ \text{all polygon vertices lie on the circle} }. $$

The polygon is then inscribed in the circle.

Circumcircle

A circle circumscribed about a triangle is the triangle's circumcircle.

$$ \boxed{ A,B,C \in \text{circumcircle} }. $$

Circumcenter

The center of a triangle's circumscribed circle is the circumcenter:

$$ \boxed{ OA=OB=OC }. $$

Circumradius

The radius of the circumscribed circle is:

$$ \boxed{ R=OA=OB=OC }. $$

Every Triangle Has a Circumscribed Circle

Any three noncollinear points determine one unique circle. Therefore every nondegenerate triangle has exactly one circumcircle.

Not Every Polygon Has a Circumscribed Circle

Polygons with more than three vertices are not automatically cyclic. Their vertices must all lie on one common circle.

Cyclic Polygon

A polygon that can be inscribed in a circle—or equivalently has a circle circumscribed about it—is called cyclic.

$$ \boxed{ \text{cyclic polygon} \iff \text{admits a circumcircle} }. $$

Cyclic Quadrilateral

A quadrilateral is cyclic precisely when its opposite angles are supplementary:

$$ \boxed{ A+C=180^\circ }. $$

Circumscribed versus Inscribed

The terms are complementary.

$$ \boxed{ \text{circle circumscribed about polygon} \iff \text{polygon inscribed in circle} }. $$

Likewise:

$$ \boxed{ \text{polygon circumscribed about circle} \iff \text{circle inscribed in polygon} }. $$

Direction Matters

The phrases:

$$ \text{polygon circumscribed about a circle} $$

and:

$$ \text{circle circumscribed about a polygon} $$

describe different geometric relationships.

Circumscribed Polygon

In many elementary geometry texts, the phrase circumscribed polygon commonly means a polygon whose sides are tangent to an inner circle.

Because terminology can vary, the phrase circumscribed about a circle is more precise.

Circumscribed Circle

A circumscribed circle around a polygon is a circle passing through all of the polygon's vertices.

For a triangle, this is simply the circumcircle.

Regular Polygon

Every regular polygon has both:

  • an incircle about which the polygon is circumscribed,
  • a circumcircle circumscribed about the polygon.

Regular Polygon Inradius and Circumradius

For a regular $n$-gon of side length $s$:

$$ \boxed{ R= \frac{s}{2\sin(\pi/n)} } $$

and:

$$ \boxed{ r= \frac{s}{2\tan(\pi/n)} }. $$

Relation between Inradius and Circumradius

For a regular $n$-gon:

$$ \boxed{ r= R\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right) }. $$

Bicentric Figures

A polygon possessing both an incircle and a circumcircle is called bicentric.

Every nondegenerate triangle is bicentric.

Area of a Polygon Circumscribed about a Circle

If a tangential polygon has perimeter $P$ and inradius $r$, then:

$$ \boxed{ K= \frac12Pr }. $$

With semiperimeter $s=P/2$:

$$ \boxed{ K=rs }. $$

Tangent-Length Principle

Tangent segments drawn from the same external point to a circle have equal length.

$$ \boxed{ PA=PB } $$

when $PA$ and $PB$ are tangent segments from $P$.

Circumscribed Quadrilateral

For a tangential quadrilateral with consecutive side lengths $a,b,c,d$:

$$ \boxed{ a+c=b+d }. $$

This equality follows from the tangent-length principle.

Circle Circumscribed about a Right Triangle

For a right triangle, the hypotenuse is a diameter of the circumscribed circle.

$$ \boxed{ R=\frac{c}{2} }. $$

Circle Circumscribed about an Equilateral Triangle

If the equilateral triangle has side length $a$, then:

$$ \boxed{ R=\frac{a}{\sqrt3} }. $$

Circumscription in Three Dimensions

The terminology extends beyond planar figures. For example, a sphere may be circumscribed about a polyhedron when all vertices of the polyhedron lie on the sphere.

$$ \boxed{ \text{polyhedron vertices} \in \text{circumsphere} }. $$

Circumsphere

A sphere passing through all vertices of a polyhedron, when such a sphere exists, is called a circumsphere.

Insphere

Conversely, a polyhedron may be circumscribed about a sphere when its faces are tangent to that sphere. The inner sphere is then an insphere.

Common Misconception: Circumscribed Means Merely Bigger

Circumscription requires a specific incidence or tangency relationship. Merely enclosing another figure does not make a figure circumscribed about it.

Common Misconception: Circumscribed Always Means Tangent

Not in every usage. A polygon circumscribed about a circle uses tangency, whereas a circle circumscribed about a polygon passes through the polygon's vertices.

Common Misconception: Circumscribed and Inscribed Describe Unrelated Ideas

They describe the same geometric configuration from opposite perspectives.

Common Misconception: Every Polygon Has Both an Incircle and Circumcircle

Every triangle does, but arbitrary polygons generally do not.

Common Misconception: Every Quadrilateral Can Be Circumscribed about a Circle

Only tangential quadrilaterals admit an incircle tangent to all four sides.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$IT\perp AB$ "The radius to the tangency point is perpendicular to the side."
$OA=OB=OC=R$ "The circle circumscribed about the triangle has circumradius R."
$a+c=b+d$ "The sums of opposite sides of a tangential quadrilateral are equal."
$K=rs$ "The area of the tangential polygon equals the inradius times the semiperimeter."
$A+C=180^\circ$ "Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary."

The adjective circumscribed should usually be followed by a relational phrase such as circumscribed about a circle or circle circumscribed about a triangle. This prevents ambiguity about which figure is outside and what contact condition is intended.

Defense Notation Advice: Use tangential when you mean a polygon with an incircle, and cyclic when you mean a polygon with a circumcircle. These terms often communicate the intended geometry more precisely than the adjective circumscribed by itself.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Meaning: Circumscribed means drawn around another figure with a required geometric contact condition.
  • Polygon about Circle: Every polygon side is tangent to the inner circle.
  • Circle about Polygon: Every polygon vertex lies on the outer circle.
  • Tangential Polygon: A polygon circumscribed about a circle.
  • Incircle: The circle tangent to all sides of a tangential polygon.
  • Cyclic Polygon: A polygon having a circle circumscribed about it.
  • Circumcircle: The circle through all vertices of a cyclic polygon.
  • Triangle: Every nondegenerate triangle has both an incircle and a circumcircle.
  • Regular Polygon: Every regular polygon has both an incircle and a circumcircle.
  • Tangential Polygon Area: $$ \boxed{ K=rs=\frac12Pr }. $$
  • Tangential Quadrilateral: $$ \boxed{ a+c=b+d }. $$
  • Cyclic Quadrilateral: $$ \boxed{ A+C=180^\circ }. $$
  • Right-Triangle Circumcircle: The hypotenuse is a diameter.
  • Three-Dimensional Extension: Spheres may be circumscribed about polyhedra, producing circumspheres.
  • Complementary Language: A circle circumscribed about a polygon means the polygon is inscribed in the circle.
  • Common Error: Do not treat simple enclosure as sufficient for circumscription.
  • Common Error: Do not assume circumscribed always means tangent; the contact relation depends on which object is outside.
  • Common Error: Do not confuse a tangential polygon with a cyclic polygon.
  • Common Error: Do not assume every polygon admits both an incircle and a circumcircle.
  • Terminological Contrast: Circumscribed describes an outer figure related to an inner figure by tangency or incidence. Inscribed describes the same configuration from the inner figure's perspective. Tangential polygon has an incircle tangent to every side. Cyclic polygon has every vertex on one circumcircle. Incircle lies inside and touches sides. Circumcircle lies around and passes through vertices. Circumsphere passes through the vertices of a polyhedron. Insphere is tangent to the faces of a polyhedron.

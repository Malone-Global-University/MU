Area of a Sector of a Circle

Subfield: Geometry, Spatial Analysis & Topology

Etymology & Roots

A sector of a circle is the region bounded by two radii and the circular arc joining their endpoints. It resembles a wedge or slice cut from a circular disk.

Its area is determined by the fraction of the full rotation represented by its central angle.

If the central angle $\theta$ is measured in degrees, then:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2 }. $$

If $\theta$ is measured in radians, the formula becomes:

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac12r^2\theta }. $$

These are not different geometric rules. They are equivalent formulas written for two different systems of angle measurement.

Operational Nuance

Whole-Circle Reference

The area of a full circle is:

$$ \boxed{ A_{\text{circle}}=\pi r^2 }. $$

A sector occupies only a fraction of this total area.

Degree Formula

A full turn contains:

$$ 360^\circ. $$

Therefore a sector with central angle $\theta$ occupies the fraction:

$$ \frac{\theta}{360^\circ} $$

of the circle.

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2 }. $$

Radian Formula

A full turn contains:

$$ 2\pi $$

radians. Therefore:

$$ A = \frac{\theta}{2\pi}\pi r^2. $$

Simplifying:

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac12r^2\theta }. $$

This compact form requires $\theta$ to be measured in radians.

Why Radians Simplify the Formula

In radians, the full angle $2\pi$ is already expressed using the same constant appearing in the circle-area formula. The factors of $\pi$ cancel:

$$ \frac{\theta}{2\pi}\pi r^2 = \frac12r^2\theta. $$

Example in Degrees

Suppose:

$$ r=6, \qquad \theta=60^\circ. $$

Then:

$$ A= \frac{60^\circ}{360^\circ} \pi(6)^2. $$

Therefore:

$$ A= \frac16(36\pi) = \boxed{ 6\pi }. $$

Example in Radians

The same angle is:

$$ 60^\circ=\frac{\pi}{3}. $$

Using the radian formula:

$$ A = \frac12(6)^2 \left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right) = \boxed{ 6\pi }. $$

Quarter Circle

For:

$$ \theta=90^\circ=\frac{\pi}{2}, $$

the sector occupies one-fourth of the disk:

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac14\pi r^2 }. $$

Semicircle

For:

$$ \theta=180^\circ=\pi, $$

the sector occupies half of the disk:

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac12\pi r^2 }. $$

Three-Quarter Sector

For:

$$ \theta=270^\circ=\frac{3\pi}{2}, $$

the area is:

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac34\pi r^2 }. $$

Minor and Major Sectors

A minor sector usually corresponds to the smaller central angle:

$$ 0^\circ<\theta<180^\circ. $$

The corresponding major sector uses the remaining angle:

$$ \boxed{ 360^\circ-\theta }. $$

In radians:

$$ \boxed{ 2\pi-\theta }. $$

Major-Sector Area

If $\theta$ is the minor central angle in radians:

$$ A_{\text{major}} = \frac12r^2(2\pi-\theta). $$

Equivalently:

$$ \boxed{ A_{\text{major}} = \pi r^2-A_{\text{minor}} }. $$

Sector Area and Arc Length

If $\theta$ is measured in radians, arc length is:

$$ \boxed{ s=r\theta }. $$

Since:

$$ \theta=\frac{s}{r}, $$

substitute into:

$$ A=\frac12r^2\theta. $$

Then:

$$ A = \frac12r^2 \left(\frac{s}{r}\right) = \boxed{ \frac12rs }. $$

Thus a sector may be found from radius and arc length:

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac12r(\text{arc length}) }. $$

Triangle Analogy

The formula:

$$ A=\frac12rs $$

resembles the triangle formula:

$$ A_{\triangle}=\frac12bh. $$

Here the radius plays a role analogous to perpendicular height and the arc length replaces the straight base, though a sector is not literally a triangle.

Solving for Radius from Radian Data

From:

$$ A=\frac12r^2\theta, $$

we obtain:

$$ r^2=\frac{2A}{\theta}. $$

Since radius is nonnegative:

$$ \boxed{ r=\sqrt{\frac{2A}{\theta}} }. $$

Solving for the Central Angle

In radians:

$$ \boxed{ \theta=\frac{2A}{r^2} }. $$

In degrees:

$$ \boxed{ \theta= \frac{360^\circ A}{\pi r^2} }. $$

Solving from Arc Length and Area

From:

$$ A=\frac12rs, $$

solve for radius:

$$ \boxed{ r=\frac{2A}{s} }. $$

Or solve for arc length:

$$ \boxed{ s=\frac{2A}{r} }. $$

Sector Perimeter

The perimeter of a sector includes two radii and the arc:

$$ \boxed{ P_{\text{sector}}=2r+s }. $$

With radians:

$$ \boxed{ P_{\text{sector}} = 2r+r\theta = r(2+\theta) }. $$

Sector perimeter and sector area are different measurements and should not be confused.

Sector versus Circular Segment

A sector is bounded by two radii and an arc.

A circular segment is bounded by a chord and an arc.

For a minor sector with $0<\theta<\pi$, the corresponding isosceles triangle has area:

$$ A_{\triangle} = \frac12r^2\sin\theta. $$

Therefore the corresponding minor circular-segment area is:

$$ \boxed{ A_{\text{segment}} = \frac12r^2(\theta-\sin\theta) } $$

when $\theta$ is in radians.

Annular Sector

An annular sector lies between two concentric circles of outer radius $R$ and inner radius $r$.

For angle $\theta$ in radians:

$$ A = \frac12R^2\theta - \frac12r^2\theta. $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac12 (R^2-r^2)\theta }. $$

Annular Sector in Degrees

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac{\theta}{360^\circ} \pi(R^2-r^2) }. $$

Scaling

For a fixed central angle, if:

$$ r\mapsto kr, $$

then:

$$ A' = \frac12(kr)^2\theta = k^2A. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ A\mapsto k^2A }. $$

Doubling the Radius

$$ \boxed{ r\mapsto2r \Rightarrow A\mapsto4A }. $$

Doubling the Angle

For fixed radius:

$$ \boxed{ \theta\mapsto2\theta \Rightarrow A\mapsto2A } $$

as long as the intended sector is interpreted consistently.

Area as a Function of Angle

For fixed radius:

$$ A(\theta)=\frac12r^2\theta. $$

Thus sector area is a linear function of the central angle when the angle is expressed in radians.

Derivative with Respect to Angle

$$ \boxed{ \frac{dA}{d\theta} = \frac12r^2 }. $$

For a fixed circle, equal increases in central angle produce equal increases in sector area.

Derivative with Respect to Radius

For fixed $\theta$:

$$ A(r)=\frac12r^2\theta. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{dA}{dr}=r\theta }. $$

Since:

$$ s=r\theta, $$

this gives:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{dA}{dr}=s }. $$

Thus the rate at which a fixed-angle sector grows with radius equals the length of its outer arc.

Polar-Coordinate Interpretation

A sector with:

$$ 0\le \rho\le r, \qquad \alpha\le\phi\le\beta $$

has angular width:

$$ \theta=\beta-\alpha. $$

Using the polar area element:

$$ dA=\rho\,d\rho\,d\phi, $$

its area is:

$$ A= \int_\alpha^\beta \int_0^r \rho\,d\rho\,d\phi. $$

Evaluating:

$$ A = \frac12r^2(\beta-\alpha) = \boxed{ \frac12r^2\theta }. $$

Signed Angles versus Geometric Area

Directed-angle conventions may allow negative $\theta$, but ordinary geometric area is nonnegative.

When a signed angle is used in an oriented calculation, the sign can encode orientation. For ordinary sector area, use the positive angular measure of the intended region.

Angles Beyond One Full Turn

The expression:

$$ \frac12r^2\theta $$

can algebraically accommodate $\theta>2\pi$, but such an angle sweeps over parts of the disk more than once. It therefore represents swept area with multiplicity rather than the area of an ordinary single sector of a disk.

For an ordinary sector, one typically uses:

$$ \boxed{ 0\le\theta\le2\pi }. $$

Units

The radius contributes squared length units. Radians are dimensionless in the standard mathematical treatment, so:

$$ \boxed{ A\text{ is measured in square units} }. $$

Common Misconception: The formula $A=\frac12r^2\theta$ cannot be used with a degree measure inserted directly for $\theta$. Convert degrees to radians first, or use the degree formula.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$A=\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2$ "Sector area equals theta over three hundred sixty degrees times pi r squared."
$A=\frac12r^2\theta$ "Sector area equals one-half r squared theta," with theta in radians.
$s=r\theta$ "Arc length equals radius times theta," with theta in radians.
$A=\frac12rs$ "Sector area equals one-half radius times arc length."
$\theta=\frac{2A}{r^2}$ "Theta equals two A divided by r squared."
$A_{\text{major}}=\pi r^2-A_{\text{minor}}$ "The major-sector area equals the circle area minus the minor-sector area."

The symbol $\theta$ must be interpreted together with its angle unit. The formulas:

$$ \frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2 $$

and:

$$ \frac12r^2\theta $$

are equivalent only when the first $\theta$ is a degree measure and the second is the corresponding radian measure.

Defense Notation Advice: Write the degree symbol when using the degree formula. In calculus, trigonometry, polar coordinates, and arc-length work, assume radians unless another unit is explicitly stated.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Meaning: A sector is the region bounded by two radii and their intercepted circular arc.
  • Degree Formula: $$ \boxed{ A= \frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2 }. $$
  • Radian Formula: $$ \boxed{ A=\frac12r^2\theta }. $$
  • Full Circle: $$ \boxed{ \theta=2\pi \Rightarrow A=\pi r^2 }. $$
  • Semicircle: $$ \boxed{ \theta=\pi \Rightarrow A=\frac12\pi r^2 }. $$
  • Quarter Circle: $$ \boxed{ \theta=\frac{\pi}{2} \Rightarrow A=\frac14\pi r^2 }. $$
  • Arc Length: $$ \boxed{ s=r\theta } $$ when $\theta$ is measured in radians.
  • Area from Arc Length: $$ \boxed{ A=\frac12rs }. $$
  • Radius from Area and Angle: $$ \boxed{ r=\sqrt{\frac{2A}{\theta}} } $$ for $\theta$ in radians.
  • Angle from Area: $$ \boxed{ \theta=\frac{2A}{r^2} } $$ in radians.
  • Degree Angle from Area: $$ \boxed{ \theta= \frac{360^\circ A}{\pi r^2} }. $$
  • Minor Sector: Usually refers to the sector determined by the smaller central angle.
  • Major Sector: $$ \boxed{ A_{\text{major}} = \pi r^2-A_{\text{minor}} }. $$
  • Complementary Central Angle: $$ \boxed{ 2\pi-\theta } $$ radians, or: $$ \boxed{ 360^\circ-\theta } $$ degrees.
  • Sector Perimeter: $$ \boxed{ P=2r+s }. $$
  • Sector Perimeter in Radians: $$ \boxed{ P=r(2+\theta) }. $$
  • Annular Sector: $$ \boxed{ A= \frac12(R^2-r^2)\theta } $$ for $\theta$ in radians.
  • Annular Sector in Degrees: $$ \boxed{ A= \frac{\theta}{360^\circ} \pi(R^2-r^2) }. $$
  • Circular-Segment Contrast: A sector uses two radii and an arc; a circular segment uses a chord and an arc.
  • Minor Circular-Segment Area: $$ \boxed{ A_{\text{segment}} = \frac12r^2(\theta-\sin\theta) } $$ for $0<\theta<\pi$ in radians.
  • Scaling: For fixed angle: $$ \boxed{ r\mapsto kr \Rightarrow A\mapsto k^2A }. $$
  • Angle Scaling: For fixed radius, sector area is directly proportional to $\theta$.
  • Area as a Function of Angle: $$ \boxed{ A(\theta)=\frac12r^2\theta }. $$
  • Rate with Respect to Angle: $$ \boxed{ \frac{dA}{d\theta} = \frac12r^2 }. $$
  • Rate with Respect to Radius: $$ \boxed{ \frac{dA}{dr} = r\theta=s }. $$
  • Polar Integral: $$ \boxed{ A= \int_\alpha^\beta \int_0^r \rho\,d\rho\,d\phi = \frac12r^2(\beta-\alpha) }. $$
  • Ordinary Sector Range: $$ \boxed{ 0\le\theta\le2\pi }. $$
  • Angle-Unit Caution: $$ \boxed{ A=\frac12r^2\theta } $$ assumes radians.
  • Square Units: If $r$ is measured in centimeters, sector area is measured in square centimeters.
  • Common Error: Do not divide by $360$ when $\theta$ is already expressed in radians.
  • Common Error: Do not insert a degree number directly into $\frac12r^2\theta$ without first converting it to radians.
  • Terminological Contrast: Radius extends from center to circle. Arc is the curved boundary. Central angle determines the fraction of the full circle. Sector is the entire wedge-shaped region bounded by the two radii and the arc.

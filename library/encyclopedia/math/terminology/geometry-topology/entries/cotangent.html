Cotangent

Subfield: Geometry & Topology

Etymology & Roots

Cotangent is one of the six principal trigonometric functions. It is conventionally abbreviated:

$$ \boxed{\cot\theta}. $$

Cotangent is the reciprocal of tangent:

$$ \boxed{ \cot\theta=\frac{1}{\tan\theta} }. $$

Because:

$$ \tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}, $$

cotangent can also be written:

$$ \boxed{ \cot\theta=\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} }. $$

The prefix co- reflects the historical relationship between trigonometric functions of complementary angles. Cotangent is the tangent of the complementary angle:

$$ \boxed{ \cot\theta = \tan\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta\right) }. $$

In degree measure:

$$ \cot\theta=\tan(90^\circ-\theta). $$

Cotangent therefore belongs to the complementary-function pairs: sine and cosine, tangent and cotangent, and secant and cosecant.

Operational Nuance

Right-Triangle Definition

For an acute angle $\theta$ in a right triangle, tangent is:

$$ \tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}. $$

Taking the reciprocal gives:

$$ \boxed{ \cot\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{opposite}} }. $$

Example: Right Triangle

Suppose the side adjacent to $\theta$ has length $4$ and the side opposite $\theta$ has length $3$. Then:

$$ \tan\theta=\frac34, $$

so:

$$ \boxed{ \cot\theta=\frac43 }. $$

Ratio of Cosine to Sine

On the unit circle:

$$ (\cos\theta,\sin\theta)=(x,y). $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \cot\theta = \frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} = \frac{x}{y} } $$

whenever $y\neq0$.

Reciprocal Relationship

Tangent and cotangent are reciprocals wherever both functions are defined:

$$ \boxed{ \cot x=\frac1{\tan x} }. $$

Equivalently:

$$ \boxed{ \tan x=\frac1{\cot x} }. $$

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ \tan x\cot x=1 } $$

wherever both expressions are defined.

Domain

Since:

$$ \cot x=\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}, $$

cotangent is undefined wherever:

$$ \sin x=0. $$

This occurs at integer multiples of $\pi$:

$$ x=k\pi,\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Dom}(\cot) = \mathbb{R}\setminus \{k\pi:k\in\mathbb{Z}\} }. $$

Range

Cotangent can take every real value:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(\cot)=\mathbb{R} }. $$

Period

Cotangent has fundamental period $\pi$:

$$ \boxed{ \cot(x+\pi)=\cot x }. $$

In degrees:

$$ \cot(\theta+180^\circ)=\cot\theta. $$

This period is half the period of sine and cosine because both the numerator and denominator change sign after an angle increase of $\pi$:

$$ \frac{-\cos x}{-\sin x} = \frac{\cos x}{\sin x}. $$

Odd Symmetry

Cotangent is an odd function:

$$ \boxed{ \cot(-x)=-\cot x }. $$

Its graph is therefore symmetric about the origin.

Zeros

Cotangent equals zero when its numerator is zero while its denominator remains nonzero:

$$ \cos x=0. $$

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ \cot x=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=\frac{\pi}{2}+k\pi, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$

Vertical Asymptotes

Cotangent is undefined where $\sin x=0$, so its graph has vertical asymptotes at:

$$ \boxed{ x=k\pi,\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$

Graph Behavior

Between consecutive vertical asymptotes, the cotangent function decreases continuously from positive infinity to negative infinity.

For example, on:

$$ 0<x<\pi, $$

$\cot x$ decreases from:

$$ +\infty $$

through:

$$ \cot\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=0 $$

and toward:

$$ -\infty. $$

Sign by Quadrant

Because:

$$ \cot\theta=\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}, $$

cotangent is positive when sine and cosine have the same sign and negative when they have opposite signs.

  • Quadrant I: $\cot\theta>0$
  • Quadrant II: $\cot\theta<0$
  • Quadrant III: $\cot\theta>0$
  • Quadrant IV: $\cot\theta<0$

Pythagorean Identity

Begin with:

$$ \sin^2x+\cos^2x=1. $$

Dividing by $\sin^2x$ gives:

$$ 1+\frac{\cos^2x}{\sin^2x} = \frac1{\sin^2x}. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ 1+\cot^2x=\csc^2x }. $$

Cofunction Identity

Tangent and cotangent form a complementary pair:

$$ \boxed{ \cot\theta = \tan\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta\right) }. $$

Likewise:

$$ \boxed{ \tan\theta = \cot\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta\right) }. $$

Common Misconception: Cotangent is the reciprocal of tangent; it is not the inverse tangent function:

$$ \boxed{ \cot x=\frac1{\tan x} } $$

whereas:

$$ \tan^{-1}x=\arctan x $$

conventionally denotes inverse tangent.

The standard abbreviation for cotangent is:

$$ \boxed{\cot}. $$

In mathematical typesetting, cot is treated as a named function and printed upright rather than as the product of three variables.

In LaTeX and MathJax:

$$ \verb|\cot x| $$

renders as:

$$ \cot x. $$

Parentheses are optional for simple arguments:

$$ \cot x $$

and:

$$ \cot(x) $$

ordinarily mean the same thing.

Parentheses improve clarity for compound arguments:

$$ \cot(2x+\phi). $$

Defense Notation Advice: Distinguish carefully among:

$$ \cot x,\qquad \cot^2x,\qquad \operatorname{arccot}x. $$

They mean respectively the cotangent of $x$, the square of the cotangent value, and an inverse cotangent function under an adopted branch convention.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Reciprocal Definition: $$ \boxed{ \cot x=\frac1{\tan x} } $$ wherever the reciprocal expression is defined.
  • Sine-Cosine Definition: $$ \boxed{ \cot x=\frac{\cos x}{\sin x} }. $$
  • Right-Triangle Form: For an acute angle: $$ \boxed{ \cot\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{opposite}} }. $$
  • Unit-Circle Interpretation: If: $$ (\cos\theta,\sin\theta)=(x,y), $$ then: $$ \boxed{ \cot\theta=\frac{x}{y} } $$ whenever $y\neq0$.
  • Domain: $$ \boxed{ x\neq k\pi,\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$
  • Range: $$ \boxed{\mathbb{R}}. $$
  • Period: $$ \boxed{ \cot(x+\pi)=\cot x }. $$
  • Odd Function: $$ \boxed{ \cot(-x)=-\cot x }. $$
  • Zeros: $$ \boxed{ x=\frac{\pi}{2}+k\pi, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$
  • Vertical Asymptotes: $$ \boxed{ x=k\pi,\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$
  • Pythagorean Identity: $$ \boxed{ 1+\cot^2x=\csc^2x }. $$
  • Rearranged Pythagorean Identity: $$ \boxed{ \csc^2x-\cot^2x=1 }. $$
  • Cofunction Identity: $$ \boxed{ \cot\theta = \tan\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta\right) }. $$
  • Reciprocal Identity: $$ \boxed{ \tan x\cot x=1 } $$ wherever both sides are defined.
  • Solving Trigonometric Equations: An equation such as: $$ \cot x=1 $$ can be rewritten: $$ \tan x=1, $$ giving: $$ x=\frac{\pi}{4}+k\pi, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}. $$
  • Graphing: Each branch of $y=\cot x$ lies between consecutive vertical asymptotes and decreases continuously from $+\infty$ to $-\infty$.
  • Relationship to Tangent: Tangent has vertical asymptotes where cotangent has zeros, while cotangent has vertical asymptotes where tangent has zeros.
  • Slope Interpretation: For a line making angle $\theta$ with the positive $x$-axis: $$ m=\tan\theta. $$ Consequently: $$ \cot\theta=\frac1m $$ when $m\neq0$ and the relevant functions are defined.
  • Coordinate Ratios: For a nonzero point $(x,y)$ on a ray from the origin: $$ \tan\theta=\frac{y}{x}, $$ while: $$ \boxed{ \cot\theta=\frac{x}{y} } $$ wherever the denominators are nonzero.
  • Calculus — Derivative: $$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}\cot x=-\csc^2x }. $$
  • Calculus — Antiderivative: $$ \boxed{ \int\cot x\,dx = \ln|\sin x|+C }. $$
  • Differentiating a Composite: By the chain rule: $$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}\cot(u(x)) = -\csc^2(u(x))u'(x) }. $$
  • Spherical Trigonometry: Cotangent appears in identities and computational formulas involving spherical triangles, navigation, astronomy, and geodesy.
  • Polar Geometry: Cotangent may arise when angular relationships are expressed through coordinate ratios or when polar equations are converted or simplified.
  • Complex Analysis: Cotangent extends to complex numbers through: $$ \boxed{ \cot z=\frac{\cos z}{\sin z} }. $$ It is a meromorphic function.
  • Complex Poles: The complex cotangent function has simple poles at: $$ \boxed{ z=k\pi,\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$
  • Complex Zeros: Its zeros occur at: $$ \boxed{ z=\frac{\pi}{2}+k\pi, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$
  • Inverse Cotangent: An inverse cotangent function may be written: $$ \operatorname{arccot}x $$ or sometimes: $$ \cot^{-1}x. $$ Unlike $\arcsin$, $\arccos$, and $\arctan$, conventions for the principal range of inverse cotangent are not completely uniform, so the adopted convention should be stated when ambiguity matters.
  • Reciprocal Trigonometric Pairs: The six standard trigonometric functions form three reciprocal pairs: $$ \boxed{ \sin x\leftrightarrow\csc x }, $$ $$ \boxed{ \cos x\leftrightarrow\sec x }, $$ $$ \boxed{ \tan x\leftrightarrow\cot x }. $$
  • Terminological Contrast: Tangent compares opposite to adjacent: $$ \tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}, $$ while cotangent reverses that ratio: $$ \boxed{ \cot\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{opposite}} }. $$
  • Core Interpretation: Cotangent connects several equivalent viewpoints: $$ \boxed{ \begin{array}{c} \text{reciprocal of tangent}\\ \updownarrow\\ \text{cosine divided by sine}\\ \updownarrow\\ \text{adjacent/opposite triangle ratio}\\ \updownarrow\\ \text{unit-circle coordinate ratio }x/y \end{array} }. $$

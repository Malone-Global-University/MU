Speed

Subfield: Geometry & Topology

Etymology & Roots

Speed measures how rapidly distance is traveled with respect to time. In its simplest constant-rate form:

$$ \boxed{ \text{speed} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}} }. $$

If distance $d$ is traveled in time $t>0$, the average speed is:

$$ \boxed{ s_{\text{avg}} = \frac{d}{t} }. $$

The ordinary word speed describes quickness of motion. Mathematics makes that idea precise as a nonnegative rate relating distance traveled to elapsed time.

Operational Nuance

Distance-Rate-Time Relationship

For constant speed:

$$ \boxed{ d=st }. $$

Equivalent rearrangements are:

$$ \boxed{ s=\frac{d}{t}, \qquad t=\frac{d}{s} } $$

where the denominators are nonzero.

Example

If a vehicle travels $180$ miles in $3$ hours:

$$ s = \frac{180}{3} = \boxed{60\text{ miles per hour}}. $$

Average Speed

Average speed uses total distance traveled divided by total elapsed time:

$$ \boxed{ s_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}} }. $$

It is generally not the simple arithmetic mean of several speeds unless the time spent at each speed is appropriately accounted for.

Instantaneous Speed

Instantaneous speed describes motion at a particular moment. If $\mathbf{r}(t)$ is a position vector, then:

$$ \boxed{ s(t) = \|\mathbf{r}'(t)\| }. $$

In one-dimensional motion with position $x(t)$:

$$ \boxed{ s(t)=|x'(t)| }. $$

Speed vs. Velocity

Velocity contains directional information, while speed is the magnitude of velocity:

$$ \boxed{ \text{speed} = \|\text{velocity}\| }. $$

In one dimension, velocity may be negative, but speed is nonnegative.

Distance vs. Displacement

Average speed uses total distance traveled. Average velocity instead uses net displacement:

$$ \boxed{ \mathbf{v}_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\Delta\mathbf{r}}{\Delta t} }. $$

A traveler who returns to the starting point may have positive average speed while having zero average velocity.

Units

Speed has units of distance divided by time, such as:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}, \qquad \frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}, \qquad \frac{\text{mi}}{\text{h}} }. $$

Unit Conversion

Converting speed requires converting both distance and time units consistently. For example:

$$ 1\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} = 3.6\frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}. $$

Relative Speed

In one-dimensional constant-velocity motion, relative speed is the magnitude of the difference of velocities:

$$ \boxed{ s_{\text{rel}} = |v_1-v_2| }. $$

For objects moving in opposite directions, this may equal the sum of their speed magnitudes.

Circular Motion

A point moving in a circle of radius $r$ with angular speed $\omega$ has tangential speed:

$$ \boxed{ s=r|\omega| }. $$

Graph Interpretation

On a position-versus-time graph, the magnitude of the slope represents speed in one-dimensional motion:

$$ \boxed{ s=|x'(t)| }. $$

On a distance-traveled-versus-time graph, the slope itself is nonnegative speed when the distance function is differentiable.

Common Misconception

Speed is not generally displacement divided by time. That quantity is associated with average velocity. Average speed requires total distance traveled.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$s=d/t$ "Speed equals distance divided by time."
$d=st$ "Distance equals speed times time."
$s_{\text{avg}}=d_{\text{total}}/t_{\text{total}}$ "Average speed equals total distance divided by total time."
$s(t)=|x'(t)|$ "Instantaneous speed is the absolute value of velocity."
$s(t)=\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\|$ "Speed is the magnitude of the velocity vector."
$s=r|\omega|$ "Tangential speed equals radius times angular speed."

The word speed ordinarily denotes a scalar magnitude, while velocity denotes a signed or vector quantity that also carries direction.

Defense Notation Advice: State units with every applied speed, distinguish total distance from displacement, and use absolute value or vector magnitude when converting velocity into speed. In multi-leg trips, compute average speed from total distance and total time rather than automatically averaging the listed speeds.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Meaning: Rate at which distance is traveled with respect to time.
  • Constant-Speed Formula: $s=d/t$.
  • Distance Formula: $d=st$ for constant speed.
  • Average Speed: Total distance divided by total elapsed time.
  • Instantaneous Speed: Magnitude of instantaneous velocity.
  • One-Dimensional Motion: $s(t)=|x'(t)|$.
  • Vector Motion: $s(t)=\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\|$.
  • Velocity Contrast: Velocity contains direction; speed does not.
  • Distance Contrast: Speed calculations use distance traveled rather than net displacement.
  • Units: Distance per unit time.
  • Relative Motion: Relative speed measures how rapidly separation changes in appropriate motion settings.
  • Circular Motion: Tangential speed satisfies $s=r|\omega|$.
  • Kinematics: Speed is a fundamental measure of motion derived from position and velocity.
  • Graphs: Speed is related to the magnitude of slope on position-time graphs.
  • Modeling: Constant-speed models are linear in elapsed time.
  • Common Error: Confusing speed with velocity.
  • Common Error: Using displacement instead of total distance for average speed.
  • Common Error: Averaging several speeds arithmetically without considering the corresponding times.
  • Common Error: Mixing incompatible distance and time units.
  • Common Error: Reporting a negative value as ordinary scalar speed.
  • Core Interpretation: Speed measures the magnitude of motion: $$ \boxed{ s_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}} } $$ and, instantaneously: $$ \boxed{ s(t)=\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\| }. $$ It connects elementary rate problems with geometry of motion, derivatives, vectors, circular motion, and physical modeling.

Altitude of a Prism

Subfield: Geometry, Spatial Analysis & Topology

Etymology & Roots

The altitude of a prism is the perpendicular distance between the two parallel planes containing the prism's congruent bases.

If the base planes are $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$, then:

$$ \boxed{ h=d(\Pi_1,\Pi_2) }. $$

In elementary geometry, altitude and height are commonly used interchangeably for this perpendicular distance.

The word altitude emphasizes perpendicular separation. It does not mean simply the length of a lateral edge or the visually vertical dimension of a drawing.

$$ \boxed{ \text{altitude} = \text{perpendicular distance between the base planes} }. $$

This distinction becomes especially important for oblique prisms, whose lateral edges are slanted relative to the bases.

Operational Nuance

Prism Structure

A prism has two congruent, parallel polygonal bases connected by lateral faces. Corresponding vertices of the two bases are joined by parallel lateral edges.

The altitude measures the perpendicular separation of the planes containing the bases.

Right Prism

In a right prism, the lateral edges are perpendicular to the base planes.

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \text{lateral edge length}=h } $$

for a right prism.

Oblique Prism

In an oblique prism, the lateral edges are not perpendicular to the base planes.

The lateral edge length is therefore generally greater than the altitude:

$$ \boxed{ \text{lateral edge length}\ne h } $$

in general.

Perpendicular Distance

Because the two base planes are parallel, every perpendicular segment joining them has the same length.

$$ \boxed{ h=\text{constant perpendicular separation of the bases} }. $$

Volume

The volume of any prism is:

$$ \boxed{ V=Bh }, $$

where $B$ is the area of one base and $h$ is the perpendicular altitude.

Altitude from Volume

Rearranging:

$$ V=Bh $$

gives:

$$ \boxed{ h=\frac{V}{B} }. $$

Example

Suppose a prism has base area:

$$ B=24 $$

and volume:

$$ V=216. $$

Then:

$$ h = \frac{216}{24} = \boxed{9}. $$

Right Rectangular Prism

In a rectangular prism with dimensions $l$, $w$, and $h$, choosing the $l\times w$ face as the base gives:

$$ \boxed{ V=lwh }. $$

Here $h$ is the altitude perpendicular to that chosen base.

Changing the Base

In a rectangular prism, different pairs of opposite faces may be selected as bases. The corresponding altitude changes with that choice.

For example:

$$ V=(lw)h=(lh)w=(wh)l. $$

The volume remains the same because each base is paired with its corresponding perpendicular altitude.

Triangular Prism

For a triangular prism:

$$ \boxed{ V=B_{\triangle}h }. $$

If the triangular base has base $b$ and triangle altitude $k$, then:

$$ B_{\triangle}=\frac12bk, $$

so:

$$ \boxed{ V=\frac12bkh }. $$

Here $k$ is the altitude of the triangular base, while $h$ is the altitude of the prism. These are different geometric quantities.

Cavalieri's Principle

A right prism and an oblique prism with equal base areas and equal perpendicular altitudes have equal volumes.

$$ \boxed{ \text{same }B + \text{same }h \Longrightarrow \text{same volume} }. $$

This reflects the fact that prism volume depends on base area and perpendicular separation, not on how far the upper base is shifted sideways.

Shearing Interpretation

A right prism can be sheared into an oblique prism while preserving the base area and perpendicular altitude.

Because:

$$ V=Bh, $$

such a shear preserves volume.

Lateral Edge and Altitude Relation

Suppose an oblique lateral edge has length $s$ and makes angle $\theta$ with the base plane. Its perpendicular component gives the altitude:

$$ \boxed{ h=s\sin\theta }. $$

If instead $\phi$ is the angle between the lateral edge and a normal to the base plane, then:

$$ \boxed{ h=s\cos\phi }. $$

Common Misconception: The altitude of an oblique prism is not the slanted lateral edge. The altitude is the perpendicular distance between the two base planes.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$h$ "The height," or "the altitude of the prism."
$h=d(\Pi_1,\Pi_2)$ "The altitude is the distance between the two base planes."
$V=Bh$ "Volume equals base area times height."
$h=\frac{V}{B}$ "Height equals volume divided by base area."
$h=s\sin\theta$ "The altitude equals the lateral edge length times sine theta."
$V=lwh$ "Volume equals length times width times height."

In most elementary prism problems, height and altitude mean the same perpendicular measurement.

The term lateral edge, however, should be kept separate:

$$ \boxed{ \text{right prism: lateral edge}=h } $$

but:

$$ \boxed{ \text{oblique prism: lateral edge}\ne h } $$

in general.

Defense Notation Advice: Before using $V=Bh$, identify the planes containing the two bases and measure $h$ perpendicular to those planes. Do not use a slanted lateral edge unless the prism is right.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Definition: The altitude of a prism is the perpendicular distance between the planes containing its congruent parallel bases.
  • Common Symbol: $$ \boxed{h}. $$
  • Base Planes: If the bases lie in parallel planes $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$: $$ \boxed{ h=d(\Pi_1,\Pi_2) }. $$
  • Right Prism: The lateral edges are perpendicular to the base planes.
  • Right-Prism Lateral Edge: $$ \boxed{ \text{lateral edge length}=h }. $$
  • Oblique Prism: The lateral edges are slanted relative to the base planes.
  • Oblique-Prism Caution: The lateral edge length is not generally the altitude.
  • Shortest Distance: The altitude is the shortest distance between the two parallel base planes.
  • Constant Separation: Every perpendicular segment joining the two parallel base planes has the same length.
  • Volume Formula: $$ \boxed{ V=Bh }. $$
  • Altitude from Volume: $$ \boxed{ h=\frac{V}{B} }. $$
  • Base Area from Volume: $$ \boxed{ B=\frac{V}{h} }. $$
  • Triangular Prism: $$ V=B_{\triangle}h. $$
  • Rectangular Prism: $$ \boxed{ V=lwh }. $$
  • Polygonal Base: The formula $V=Bh$ applies regardless of the polygon used as the prism's base.
  • Base Choice: In some prisms, especially rectangular prisms, different pairs of opposite faces may be chosen as bases.
  • Corresponding Altitude: Changing the chosen base changes which perpendicular dimension serves as $h$.
  • Triangle-Altitude Contrast: A triangular prism can have both an altitude inside its triangular base and a separate altitude between the prism's base planes.
  • Two Altitudes in One Formula: If a triangular base has triangle base $b$, triangle altitude $k$, and prism altitude $h$: $$ \boxed{ V=\frac12bkh }. $$
  • Cavalieri's Principle: Prisms with equal base areas and equal perpendicular altitudes have equal volumes.
  • Shearing: Sliding one base parallel to itself changes a right prism into an oblique prism without changing the perpendicular altitude.
  • Volume Preservation: If $B$ and $h$ remain fixed: $$ \boxed{ V=Bh } $$ remains fixed.
  • Lateral Edge Projection: If an oblique lateral edge of length $s$ makes angle $\theta$ with the base plane: $$ \boxed{ h=s\sin\theta }. $$
  • Normal-Angle Form: If the same edge makes angle $\phi$ with a normal to the base: $$ \boxed{ h=s\cos\phi }. $$
  • Coordinate Geometry: If the base planes are: $$ z=0 $$ and: $$ z=h, $$ then their altitude separation is: $$ \boxed{|h|}. $$
  • Parallel Plane Equations: If the base planes are: $$ Ax+By+Cz+D_1=0 $$ and: $$ Ax+By+Cz+D_2=0, $$ then: $$ \boxed{ h= \frac{|D_2-D_1|} {\sqrt{A^2+B^2+C^2}} }. $$
  • Point-to-Plane Method: If one base lies in: $$ Ax+By+Cz+D=0 $$ and a point $(x_0,y_0,z_0)$ lies in the opposite base plane, then: $$ \boxed{ h= \frac{|Ax_0+By_0+Cz_0+D|} {\sqrt{A^2+B^2+C^2}} }. $$
  • Vector Geometry: The altitude direction is parallel to a normal vector of the base planes.
  • Parallelepiped: For edge vectors $\mathbf{a}$, $\mathbf{b}$, and $\mathbf{c}$, the volume is: $$ \boxed{ V= |\mathbf{a}\cdot(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c})| }. $$
  • Vector Altitude: If the base is spanned by $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$: $$ B=\|\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}\|, $$ so: $$ \boxed{ h= \frac{ |\mathbf{c}\cdot(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b})| }{ \|\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}\| } }. $$
  • Scaling: Under dilation by factor $k$: $$ h\mapsto kh. $$
  • Base-Area Scaling: $$ B\mapsto k^2B. $$
  • Volume Scaling: $$ \boxed{ V\mapsto k^3V }. $$
  • Similar Prisms: Corresponding altitudes scale by the same linear factor as corresponding edge lengths.
  • Volume Ratio: If similar prisms have linear scale factor $k$: $$ \boxed{ \frac{V_2}{V_1}=k^3 }. $$
  • Units: Altitude is measured in linear units such as centimeters, meters, or feet.
  • Orientation Independence: A prism need not stand upright. Its altitude is determined by perpendicular distance between its bases, not by page orientation.
  • Diagram Caution: A visible vertical edge in a perspective drawing is not necessarily the altitude unless it is perpendicular to the base planes.
  • Terminological Contrast: The altitude is the perpendicular distance between the base planes. A lateral edge joins corresponding base vertices. In a right prism, lateral edges are perpendicular to the bases and equal the altitude; in an oblique prism, they are slanted and generally longer than the altitude.

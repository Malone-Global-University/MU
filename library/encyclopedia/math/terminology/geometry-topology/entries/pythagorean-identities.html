Pythagorean Identities

Subfield: Geometry & Topology

Etymology & Roots

The Pythagorean identities are fundamental trigonometric identities derived from the geometric relationship underlying the Pythagorean theorem and the unit circle.

The central identity is:

$$ \boxed{ \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1 }. $$

Dividing this identity by $\cos^2\theta$ or $\sin^2\theta$, where those quantities are nonzero, produces the other two standard Pythagorean identities:

$$ \boxed{ 1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta } $$
$$ \boxed{ 1+\cot^2\theta=\csc^2\theta }. $$

The adjective Pythagorean connects these identities with the Pythagorean theorem for right triangles:

$$ \boxed{ a^2+b^2=c^2 }. $$

If a right triangle has hypotenuse $c$ and acute angle $\theta$, then:

$$ \sin\theta=\frac{a}{c}, \qquad \cos\theta=\frac{b}{c}. $$

Dividing:

$$ a^2+b^2=c^2 $$

by $c^2$ yields:

$$ \frac{a^2}{c^2} + \frac{b^2}{c^2} = 1, $$

hence:

$$ \boxed{ \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1 }. $$

The identity is therefore not an arbitrary symbolic rule. It is the unit-scaled form of right-triangle geometry.

In modern trigonometry, the unit-circle interpretation extends the identity beyond acute angles to every real angle:

$$ \boxed{ (\cos\theta)^2+(\sin\theta)^2=1 }. $$

This follows because the point:

$$ \boxed{ (\cos\theta,\sin\theta) } $$

lies on the unit circle:

$$ \boxed{ x^2+y^2=1 }. $$

Thus the Pythagorean identities link right-triangle geometry, coordinate geometry, the unit circle, and symbolic trigonometry.

Operational Nuance

Primary Pythagorean Identity

$$ \boxed{ \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1 }. $$

Notation Meaning

The notation:

$$ \sin^2\theta $$

means:

$$ \boxed{ (\sin\theta)^2 }, $$

not:

$$ \sin(\theta^2). $$

Likewise for Cosine

$$ \boxed{ \cos^2\theta = (\cos\theta)^2 }. $$

Rearranged Sine Form

$$ \boxed{ \sin^2\theta = 1-\cos^2\theta }. $$

Rearranged Cosine Form

$$ \boxed{ \cos^2\theta = 1-\sin^2\theta }. $$

Factored Difference-of-Squares Forms

$$ \boxed{ 1-\sin^2\theta = \cos^2\theta } $$
$$ \boxed{ 1-\cos^2\theta = \sin^2\theta }. $$

Square-Root Consequence

From:

$$ \sin^2\theta = 1-\cos^2\theta, $$

one obtains:

$$ \boxed{ |\sin\theta| = \sqrt{ 1-\cos^2\theta } }. $$

Sign Must Be Restored

When solving for $\sin\theta$ itself:

$$ \boxed{ \sin\theta = \pm \sqrt{ 1-\cos^2\theta } } $$

with the correct sign selected from the quadrant or other contextual information.

Cosine Square-Root Consequence

$$ \boxed{ \cos\theta = \pm \sqrt{ 1-\sin^2\theta } }. $$

Quadrant Dependence

The squared identity determines magnitude but does not determine the sign of sine or cosine.

Example

If:

$$ \cos\theta=\frac{3}{5}, $$

then:

$$ \sin^2\theta = 1-\frac{9}{25} = \frac{16}{25}. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \sin\theta = \pm\frac{4}{5} }. $$

Additional quadrant information is required to choose the sign.

Quadrant I

If $\theta$ lies in Quadrant I:

$$ \boxed{ \sin\theta>0, \qquad \cos\theta>0 }. $$

Quadrant II

$$ \boxed{ \sin\theta>0, \qquad \cos\theta<0 }. $$

Quadrant III

$$ \boxed{ \sin\theta<0, \qquad \cos\theta<0 }. $$

Quadrant IV

$$ \boxed{ \sin\theta<0, \qquad \cos\theta>0 }. $$

Deriving the Tangent-Secant Identity

Begin with:

$$ \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1. $$

Divide every term by $\cos^2\theta$, provided $\cos\theta\neq0$:

$$ \frac{\sin^2\theta}{\cos^2\theta} + \frac{\cos^2\theta}{\cos^2\theta} = \frac{1}{\cos^2\theta}. $$

Using:

$$ \tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} $$

and:

$$ \sec\theta = \frac{1}{\cos\theta}, $$

gives:

$$ \boxed{ \tan^2\theta+1 = \sec^2\theta }. $$

Standard Tangent-Secant Ordering

This is commonly written:

$$ \boxed{ 1+\tan^2\theta = \sec^2\theta }. $$

Domain of Tangent-Secant Identity

Tangent and secant require:

$$ \boxed{ \cos\theta\neq0 }. $$

Therefore the identity is understood wherever both sides are defined.

Undefined Angles

For real $\theta$:

$$ \cos\theta=0 $$

at:

$$ \boxed{ \theta = \frac{\pi}{2}+k\pi, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$

Rearranged Tangent Form

$$ \boxed{ \tan^2\theta = \sec^2\theta-1 }. $$

Rearranged Secant Form

$$ \boxed{ \sec^2\theta-\tan^2\theta = 1 }. $$

Difference-of-Squares Factorization

$$ \sec^2\theta-\tan^2\theta = (\sec\theta-\tan\theta) (\sec\theta+\tan\theta). $$

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ (\sec\theta-\tan\theta) (\sec\theta+\tan\theta) = 1 }. $$

Reciprocal Consequence

Wherever defined:

$$ \boxed{ \sec\theta-\tan\theta = \frac{1}{ \sec\theta+\tan\theta } }. $$

Deriving the Cotangent-Cosecant Identity

Begin again with:

$$ \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1. $$

Divide by $\sin^2\theta$, provided $\sin\theta\neq0$:

$$ \frac{\sin^2\theta}{\sin^2\theta} + \frac{\cos^2\theta}{\sin^2\theta} = \frac{1}{\sin^2\theta}. $$

Using:

$$ \cot\theta = \frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} $$

and:

$$ \csc\theta = \frac{1}{\sin\theta}, $$

yields:

$$ \boxed{ 1+\cot^2\theta = \csc^2\theta }. $$

Domain of Cotangent-Cosecant Identity

Cotangent and cosecant require:

$$ \boxed{ \sin\theta\neq0 }. $$

Undefined Angles

For real $\theta$:

$$ \sin\theta=0 $$

when:

$$ \boxed{ \theta=k\pi, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$

Rearranged Cotangent Form

$$ \boxed{ \cot^2\theta = \csc^2\theta-1 }. $$

Rearranged Cosecant Form

$$ \boxed{ \csc^2\theta-\cot^2\theta = 1 }. $$

Difference-of-Squares Form

$$ \boxed{ (\csc\theta-\cot\theta) (\csc\theta+\cot\theta) = 1 }. $$

Reciprocal Consequence

$$ \boxed{ \csc\theta-\cot\theta = \frac{1}{ \csc\theta+\cot\theta } } $$

wherever defined.

The Three Standard Identities Together

$$ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta &=1,\\ 1+\tan^2\theta &=\sec^2\theta,\\ 1+\cot^2\theta &=\csc^2\theta. \end{aligned} } $$

Memory Pattern

The basic pair:

$$ \boxed{ \sin,\cos } $$

sums to $1$ when squared.

The tangent family follows:

$$ \boxed{ 1+\tan^2 = \sec^2 }. $$

The cotangent family follows:

$$ \boxed{ 1+\cot^2 = \csc^2 }. $$

Reciprocal Pairing

The identities align with the reciprocal relationships:

$$ \boxed{ \sec\theta = \frac{1}{\cos\theta}, \qquad \csc\theta = \frac{1}{\sin\theta} }. $$

Quotient Pairing

$$ \boxed{ \tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}, \qquad \cot\theta = \frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} }. $$

Unit-Circle Derivation

A point on the unit circle has coordinates:

$$ \boxed{ (x,y) = (\cos\theta,\sin\theta) }. $$

Since:

$$ x^2+y^2=1, $$

substitution gives:

$$ \boxed{ \cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1 }. $$

Why This Extends Beyond Right-Triangle Acute Angles

Unit-circle coordinates define sine and cosine for arbitrary real angles, including negative angles and angles greater than a full revolution.

Periodic Validity

Since:

$$ \sin(\theta+2\pi k) = \sin\theta $$

and:

$$ \cos(\theta+2\pi k) = \cos\theta, $$

the primary Pythagorean identity repeats across all coterminal angles.

Identity vs. Equation

An identity is true for every value in the common domain of the expressions.

$$ \boxed{ \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1 } $$

is therefore not an equation to solve for selected $\theta$ values. It is a universal trigonometric relationship.

Domain Qualification

The primary identity is defined for every real $\theta$, while the tangent-secant and cotangent-cosecant identities have restricted common domains.

Using Pythagorean Identities to Rewrite Expressions

Whenever:

$$ 1-\sin^2\theta $$

appears, it may be replaced by:

$$ \boxed{ \cos^2\theta }. $$

Likewise

$$ \boxed{ 1-\cos^2\theta = \sin^2\theta } $$
$$ \boxed{ \sec^2\theta-1 = \tan^2\theta } $$
$$ \boxed{ \csc^2\theta-1 = \cot^2\theta }. $$

Example Simplification

Simplify:

$$ \frac{1-\cos^2\theta}{\sin\theta}. $$

Replace:

$$ 1-\cos^2\theta = \sin^2\theta. $$

Then:

$$ \frac{\sin^2\theta}{\sin\theta} = \boxed{ \sin\theta } $$

wherever the cancellation is valid, namely $\sin\theta\neq0$ for the original quotient.

Original-Domain Defense

Algebraic simplification does not automatically restore angles excluded by the original denominator.

Example with Tangent and Secant

Simplify:

$$ \sec^2\theta-\tan^2\theta. $$

The Pythagorean identity gives directly:

$$ \boxed{ 1 }. $$

Example with Cosecant and Cotangent

$$ \boxed{ \csc^2\theta-\cot^2\theta=1 }. $$

Solving for Another Trigonometric Function

If:

$$ \tan\theta=\frac{3}{4}, $$

then:

$$ 1+\frac{9}{16} = \sec^2\theta. $$

Hence:

$$ \sec^2\theta = \frac{25}{16}. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \sec\theta = \pm\frac{5}{4} }. $$

Again, quadrant information determines the sign.

From Tangent to Cosine

Since:

$$ \sec\theta = \frac{1}{\cos\theta}, $$

if $\sec\theta=\pm5/4$, then:

$$ \boxed{ \cos\theta = \pm\frac{4}{5} }. $$

Right-Triangle Reconstruction

A tangent ratio:

$$ \tan\theta=\frac{3}{4} $$

corresponds to legs proportional to $3$ and $4$. The Pythagorean theorem gives a hypotenuse proportional to $5$, recovering the familiar $3$-$4$-$5$ triangle.

Connection with Power-Reduction Identities

Combining the Pythagorean identity with:

$$ \cos(2\theta) = \cos^2\theta-\sin^2\theta $$

gives formulas for squared trigonometric functions.

Cosine-Squared Power Reduction

$$ \boxed{ \cos^2\theta = \frac{ 1+\cos(2\theta) }{2} }. $$

Sine-Squared Power Reduction

$$ \boxed{ \sin^2\theta = \frac{ 1-\cos(2\theta) }{2} }. $$

Deriving the Cosine-Squared Formula

Add:

$$ \cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1 $$

to:

$$ \cos^2\theta-\sin^2\theta = \cos(2\theta). $$

Then:

$$ 2\cos^2\theta = 1+\cos(2\theta). $$

Integration

Pythagorean and power-reduction identities are central to integrals involving powers of sine, cosine, tangent, secant, cotangent, and cosecant.

Example

To integrate:

$$ \int\sin^2x\,dx, $$

use:

$$ \sin^2x = \frac{1-\cos2x}{2}. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \int\sin^2x\,dx = \frac{x}{2} - \frac{\sin2x}{4} + C }. $$

Tangent-Secant Integration

The relation:

$$ \boxed{ \tan^2x = \sec^2x-1 } $$

converts powers of tangent into expressions involving secant.

Example

$$ \int\tan^2x\,dx = \int(\sec^2x-1)\,dx. $$

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ \int\tan^2x\,dx = \tan x-x+C }. $$

Cotangent-Cosecant Integration

Likewise:

$$ \boxed{ \cot^2x = \csc^2x-1 }. $$

Trigonometric Substitution

Pythagorean identities explain why certain trigonometric substitutions simplify radicals.

Radical of $a^2-x^2$

Let:

$$ x=a\sin\theta. $$

Then:

$$ a^2-x^2 = a^2(1-\sin^2\theta) = a^2\cos^2\theta. $$

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ \sqrt{a^2-x^2} = |a\cos\theta| } $$

with the sign simplified further when the substitution interval guarantees $\cos\theta\geq0$.

Radical of $a^2+x^2$

Let:

$$ x=a\tan\theta. $$

Then:

$$ a^2+x^2 = a^2(1+\tan^2\theta) = a^2\sec^2\theta. $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ \sqrt{a^2+x^2} = |a\sec\theta| }. $$

Radical of $x^2-a^2$

Let:

$$ x=a\sec\theta. $$

Then:

$$ x^2-a^2 = a^2(\sec^2\theta-1) = a^2\tan^2\theta. $$

Circle Parametrization

The unit circle is parameterized by:

$$ \boxed{ x=\cos\theta, \qquad y=\sin\theta }. $$

The Pythagorean identity verifies automatically that:

$$ x^2+y^2=1. $$

Circle of Radius $r$

More generally:

$$ \boxed{ x=r\cos\theta, \qquad y=r\sin\theta } $$

gives:

$$ x^2+y^2 = r^2 \left( \cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta \right) = \boxed{ r^2 }. $$

Parametric Motion

Uniform circular motion may be written:

$$ x(t)=r\cos(\omega t+\phi), $$
$$ y(t)=r\sin(\omega t+\phi). $$

Then:

$$ \boxed{ x(t)^2+y(t)^2=r^2 } $$

for all $t$.

Vector Interpretation

The unit vector:

$$ \mathbf{u} = \langle \cos\theta, \sin\theta \rangle $$

has norm:

$$ \|\mathbf{u}\| = \sqrt{ \cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta } = \boxed{ 1 }. $$

Rotation Matrix Connection

The planar rotation matrix:

$$ R(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta\\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix} $$

preserves lengths partly because:

$$ \boxed{ \cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1 }. $$

Orthogonality of Rotation Columns

Each column has unit length:

$$ \cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1. $$

Their dot product is:

$$ -\cos\theta\sin\theta + \sin\theta\cos\theta = 0. $$

Matrix Identity

$$ \boxed{ R(\theta)^TR(\theta) = I }. $$

Complex Number Connection

Euler's formula:

$$ e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta+i\sin\theta $$

has modulus:

$$ |e^{i\theta}| = \sqrt{ \cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta } = \boxed{ 1 }. $$

Complex Unit Circle

The Pythagorean identity is therefore equivalent to the statement that $e^{i\theta}$ lies on the complex unit circle.

Oscillation and Energy

In ideal sinusoidal models, combinations of squared sine and cosine terms often produce constant quantities because:

$$ \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1. $$

Simple Harmonic Oscillator Example

If:

$$ x(t)=A\cos(\omega t), $$

then velocity is:

$$ v(t) = -A\omega\sin(\omega t). $$

Consequently:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{x(t)^2}{A^2} + \frac{v(t)^2}{A^2\omega^2} = 1 }. $$

Phase-Space Geometry

The normalized oscillator state traces a circle in a suitably scaled phase plane, again reflecting the Pythagorean identity.

Identity Verification Strategy

When proving a trigonometric identity, rewrite one side until it matches the other, using known identities rather than treating the statement as an equation to solve.

Example Verification

Verify:

$$ \frac{1-\sin^2\theta}{\cos\theta} = \cos\theta. $$

From the Pythagorean identity:

$$ 1-\sin^2\theta = \cos^2\theta. $$

Therefore:

$$ \frac{\cos^2\theta}{\cos\theta} = \cos\theta $$

wherever the original expression is defined, namely $\cos\theta\neq0$.

Convert Everything to Sine and Cosine

A reliable identity strategy is to use:

$$ \tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}, \qquad \sec\theta = \frac{1}{\cos\theta}, $$

then simplify using:

$$ \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1. $$

Example

Verify:

$$ \sec^2\theta-\tan^2\theta=1. $$

Rewrite:

$$ \frac{1}{\cos^2\theta} - \frac{\sin^2\theta}{\cos^2\theta} = \frac{ 1-\sin^2\theta }{ \cos^2\theta }. $$

Since:

$$ 1-\sin^2\theta = \cos^2\theta, $$

the expression equals:

$$ \boxed{ 1 }. $$

Common Error: Confusing the Pythagorean Identity with the Pythagorean Theorem

The theorem concerns side lengths of a right triangle:

$$ a^2+b^2=c^2. $$

The trigonometric identity is:

$$ \boxed{ \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1 }. $$

The second is derived from the geometric structure expressed by the first.

Common Error: Writing $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=\theta$

The correct right side is always:

$$ \boxed{ 1 }. $$

Common Error: Squaring the Angle Instead of the Function Value

$$ \boxed{ \sin^2\theta = (\sin\theta)^2 } $$

and not:

$$ \sin(\theta^2). $$

Common Error: Forgetting the Squares

In general:

$$ \sin\theta+\cos\theta \neq 1. $$

Counterexample

At $\theta=\pi/4$:

$$ \sin\theta+\cos\theta = \sqrt{2}, $$

not $1$.

Common Error: Using $\sin^2\theta-\cos^2\theta=1$

The primary Pythagorean identity uses addition:

$$ \boxed{ \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1 }. $$

Common Error: Writing $1+\sin^2\theta=\cos^2\theta$

The valid rearrangement is:

$$ \boxed{ 1-\sin^2\theta=\cos^2\theta }. $$

Common Error: Mixing Reciprocal Families

The correct identities are:

$$ 1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta $$

and:

$$ 1+\cot^2\theta=\csc^2\theta. $$

Tangent pairs with secant; cotangent pairs with cosecant.

Common Error: Writing $1+\tan^2\theta=\csc^2\theta$

This incorrectly crosses the reciprocal-function families.

Common Error: Forgetting the Domain when Dividing the Primary Identity

Deriving the tangent-secant identity by division requires $\cos\theta\neq0$, while deriving the cotangent-cosecant identity requires $\sin\theta\neq0$.

Common Error: Taking a Square Root without Absolute Value

From:

$$ \sin^2\theta = 1-\cos^2\theta, $$

the principal radical satisfies:

$$ \boxed{ |\sin\theta| = \sqrt{ 1-\cos^2\theta } }. $$

Common Error: Selecting the Positive Sign Automatically

Sine and cosine may be negative depending on the quadrant.

Common Error: Assuming Squared Trigonometric Values Determine the Angle

Squaring loses sign information and many angles can share the same squared sine or cosine.

Common Error: Treating an Identity as an Equation with a Few Solutions

An identity is valid throughout its common domain.

Common Error: Canceling across Addition

From:

$$ \frac{ \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta }{ \sin\theta }, $$

one cannot cancel a single $\sin\theta$ from only one term in the numerator.

Common Error: Ignoring Original Restrictions after Simplification

If an original expression contains a denominator, values making that denominator zero remain excluded even when algebraic simplification removes the visible factor.

Common Error: Assuming $1-\sin^2\theta=\cos\theta$

The correct result is:

$$ \boxed{ 1-\sin^2\theta = \cos^2\theta }. $$

Common Error: Assuming $\sqrt{\cos^2\theta}=\cos\theta$ Always

The principal square root gives:

$$ \boxed{ \sqrt{\cos^2\theta} = |\cos\theta| }. $$

Common Error: Forgetting that Secant and Cosecant Are Reciprocals

$$ \boxed{ \sec\theta=\frac{1}{\cos\theta}, \qquad \csc\theta=\frac{1}{\sin\theta} }. $$

Common Error: Memorizing Without Seeing the Division Structure

Both secondary identities follow directly from the primary identity by division. Remembering that derivation makes the formulas easier to reconstruct and less prone to sign or function-pairing errors.

Common Error: Assuming Pythagorean Identities Apply Only to Acute Angles

The unit-circle definition makes the primary identity valid for all real angles, with the secondary identities valid throughout their respective domains.

Common Error: Assuming the Identity Depends on Degrees Rather than Radians

The identity is independent of angular unit. It holds whether an angle is expressed in degrees or radians, provided the trigonometric functions interpret the angle consistently.

Common Error: Treating $x^2+y^2=1$ and $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$ as Unrelated

The trigonometric identity is exactly the unit-circle equation under:

$$ x=\cos\theta, \qquad y=\sin\theta. $$

Common Misconception: The Pythagorean identities are not three unrelated formulas to memorize. The central identity:

$$ \boxed{ \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1 } $$

is the unit-circle form of the Pythagorean theorem. Dividing it by $\cos^2\theta$ produces $1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta$, and dividing it by $\sin^2\theta$ produces $1+\cot^2\theta=\csc^2\theta$. Understanding this common source makes the entire family reconstructible rather than purely memorized.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$ "Sine squared theta plus cosine squared theta equals one."
$1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta$ "One plus tangent squared theta equals secant squared theta."
$1+\cot^2\theta=\csc^2\theta$ "One plus cotangent squared theta equals cosecant squared theta."
$\sin^2\theta$ "Sine squared theta," meaning the square of sine theta.
$1-\sin^2\theta=\cos^2\theta$ "One minus sine squared theta equals cosine squared theta."
$\sec^2\theta-\tan^2\theta=1$ "Secant squared theta minus tangent squared theta equals one."
$\csc^2\theta-\cot^2\theta=1$ "Cosecant squared theta minus cotangent squared theta equals one."

The phrase:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"Pythagorean identity"} } $$

most commonly refers first to:

$$ \boxed{ \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1 }, $$

although the plural Pythagorean identities usually includes the tangent-secant and cotangent-cosecant forms.

The spoken phrase:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"sine squared theta"} } $$

means:

$$ \boxed{ (\sin\theta)^2 }, $$

not sine of theta squared.

The phrase:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"use a Pythagorean identity"} } $$

usually means replace a squared trigonometric expression or combination with an equivalent expression from this identity family.

Defense Notation Advice: Read $\sin^2\theta$ as $(\sin\theta)^2$, not $\sin(\theta^2)$. Keep tangent paired with secant and cotangent paired with cosecant. When extracting an unsquared trigonometric value, restore the appropriate sign using quadrant or domain information. During identity proofs, preserve the original common domain and do not cancel factors that may be zero without recording the resulting restriction.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Primary Identity: $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$.
  • Tangent-Secant Identity: $1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta$.
  • Cotangent-Cosecant Identity: $1+\cot^2\theta=\csc^2\theta$.
  • Origin: Derived from Pythagorean geometry.
  • Right-Triangle Form: Divide $a^2+b^2=c^2$ by $c^2$.
  • Unit-Circle Form: Substitute $x=\cos\theta$ and $y=\sin\theta$ into $x^2+y^2=1$.
  • Coordinate Meaning: $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ is always a unit vector.
  • Sine Rearrangement: $\sin^2\theta=1-\cos^2\theta$.
  • Cosine Rearrangement: $\cos^2\theta=1-\sin^2\theta$.
  • Tangent Rearrangement: $\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta-1$.
  • Cotangent Rearrangement: $\cot^2\theta=\csc^2\theta-1$.
  • Secant Difference Form: $\sec^2\theta-\tan^2\theta=1$.
  • Cosecant Difference Form: $\csc^2\theta-\cot^2\theta=1$.
  • Square Notation: $\sin^2\theta=(\sin\theta)^2$.
  • Square-Root Caveat: $\sqrt{\sin^2\theta}=|\sin\theta|$.
  • Sign Recovery: Unsquared sine and cosine values may require $\pm$.
  • Quadrant Information: Determines the appropriate sign.
  • Primary Domain: Valid for every real angle.
  • Tangent-Secant Domain: Requires $\cos\theta\neq0$.
  • Cotangent-Cosecant Domain: Requires $\sin\theta\neq0$.
  • Derivation of Tangent-Secant Form: Divide the primary identity by $\cos^2\theta$.
  • Derivation of Cotangent-Cosecant Form: Divide the primary identity by $\sin^2\theta$.
  • Reciprocal Identity Connection: Uses secant and cosecant as reciprocals of cosine and sine.
  • Quotient Identity Connection: Uses tangent and cotangent as sine-cosine quotients.
  • Identity Proof: Rewrites one expression into an equivalent form throughout the common domain.
  • Identity vs. Conditional Equation: Pythagorean identities are not restricted to isolated angle solutions.
  • Simplification: Replaces $1-\sin^2\theta$ by $\cos^2\theta$ and analogous forms.
  • Solving for Missing Trigonometric Values: Relates sine and cosine magnitudes or tangent and secant magnitudes.
  • Power Reduction: Combines with double-angle identities to rewrite squared trigonometric functions.
  • Sine Power Reduction: $\sin^2\theta=(1-\cos2\theta)/2$.
  • Cosine Power Reduction: $\cos^2\theta=(1+\cos2\theta)/2$.
  • Integration: Reduces trigonometric powers to integrable forms.
  • Tangent Integration: $\tan^2x=\sec^2x-1$.
  • Cotangent Integration: $\cot^2x=\csc^2x-1$.
  • Trigonometric Substitution: Simplifies radicals involving quadratic sums and differences.
  • $a^2-x^2$ Substitution: $x=a\sin\theta$ uses $1-\sin^2\theta=\cos^2\theta$.
  • $a^2+x^2$ Substitution: $x=a\tan\theta$ uses $1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta$.
  • $x^2-a^2$ Substitution: $x=a\sec\theta$ uses $\sec^2\theta-1=\tan^2\theta$.
  • Circle Parametrization: $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$ guarantees $x^2+y^2=r^2$.
  • Circular Motion: Maintains constant radius through the primary identity.
  • Vector Geometry: $\langle\cos\theta,\sin\theta\rangle$ has norm $1$.
  • Rotation Matrices: Pythagorean structure contributes to length preservation and orthogonality.
  • Complex Numbers: $|\cos\theta+i\sin\theta|=1$.
  • Euler Formula: The complex exponential $e^{i\theta}$ lies on the unit circle.
  • Oscillations: Squared sine-cosine combinations can represent conserved normalized quantities.
  • Simple Harmonic Motion: Position and scaled velocity satisfy a Pythagorean phase-space relation.
  • Degrees and Radians: Identity is independent of angular unit when interpreted consistently.
  • Periodic Extension: Unit-circle definitions extend the identity to all real angles.
  • Common Error: Confusing Pythagorean identities with the Pythagorean theorem itself.

  • Common Error: Forgetting that both sine and cosine are squared.
  • Common Error: Reading $\sin^2\theta$ as $\sin(\theta^2)$.
  • Common Error: Writing a minus sign instead of plus in the primary identity.
  • Common Error: Replacing $1-\sin^2\theta$ by $\cos\theta$ rather than $\cos^2\theta$.
  • Common Error: Replacing $1-\cos^2\theta$ by $\sin\theta$ rather than $\sin^2\theta$.
  • Common Error: Pairing tangent with cosecant.
  • Common Error: Pairing cotangent with secant.
  • Common Error: Forgetting domain restrictions in the tangent-secant identity.
  • Common Error: Forgetting domain restrictions in the cotangent-cosecant identity.
  • Common Error: Automatically choosing the positive square root when recovering sine or cosine.
  • Common Error: Ignoring the absolute value in $\sqrt{\sin^2\theta}$ or $\sqrt{\cos^2\theta}$.
  • Common Error: Treating squared values as sufficient to determine a unique angle.
  • Common Error: Treating an identity as though it were an equation with only a few solutions.
  • Common Error: Canceling across addition.
  • Common Error: Restoring values excluded by an original denominator after simplification.

  • Common Error: Memorizing all three formulas without recognizing that the latter two are derived from the first.
  • Core Interpretation: The Pythagorean identities are the trigonometric expression of unit-circle geometry. Their foundation is: $$ \boxed{ \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1 }. $$ Dividing by $\cos^2\theta$ gives: $$ \boxed{ 1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta }, $$ while dividing by $\sin^2\theta$ gives: $$ \boxed{ 1+\cot^2\theta=\csc^2\theta }. $$ These identities connect the Pythagorean theorem, the unit circle, reciprocal and quotient identities, trigonometric simplification, equation solving, power reduction, integration, trigonometric substitution, circular motion, vectors, rotation matrices, and complex-number geometry.

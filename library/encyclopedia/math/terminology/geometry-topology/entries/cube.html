Cube

Subfield: Geometry & Topology

Etymology & Roots

A cube is a three-dimensional solid bounded by six congruent square faces. Every edge has the same length, and three mutually perpendicular edges meet at each vertex.

$$ \boxed{ \text{Cube} = \text{regular solid with six congruent square faces} }. $$

The word cube comes through Latin cubus from the Greek kybos, referring to a cube or die. The geometric term later became connected with the arithmetic operation of raising a quantity to the third power because the volume of a cube with side length $s$ is:

$$ \boxed{V=s^3}. $$

Thus the geometric object and the algebraic idea of "cubing" a number are historically and conceptually connected.

A cube is a special case of several broader classes of solids. It is:

  • a rectangular prism,
  • a square prism,
  • a parallelepiped,
  • a convex polyhedron,
  • a hexahedron, and
  • one of the five Platonic solids.

In the language of regular polyhedra, the cube is also called the regular hexahedron.

Operational Nuance

Basic Structure

A cube has:

$$ \boxed{ 6\text{ faces},\qquad 12\text{ edges},\qquad 8\text{ vertices} }. $$

Each face is a square, and all twelve edges have the same length.

Faces

A cube contains six square faces arranged in three pairs of opposite faces.

Each face is congruent to every other face:

$$ \boxed{ F_1\cong F_2\cong\cdots\cong F_6 }. $$

Edges

If the side length of the cube is $s$, then every edge has length:

$$ \boxed{s}. $$

Since there are twelve edges, the total edge length is:

$$ \boxed{12s}. $$

Vertices

Three edges meet at each vertex, and those three edges are mutually perpendicular.

$$ \boxed{ 3\text{ edges meet at each vertex} }. $$

Face Area

Since each face is a square of side length $s$, the area of one face is:

$$ \boxed{s^2}. $$

Surface Area

A cube has six congruent square faces, so its total surface area is:

$$ \boxed{ SA=6s^2 }. $$

Volume

The volume of a cube is the product of its length, width, and height. Since all three dimensions equal $s$:

$$ V=s\cdot s\cdot s. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ V=s^3 }. $$

Recovering Side Length from Volume

If the volume $V$ is known:

$$ V=s^3, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ s=\sqrt[3]{V} }. $$

Face Diagonal

A face of the cube is a square with side length $s$. By the Pythagorean theorem, its diagonal $d_f$ satisfies:

$$ d_f^2=s^2+s^2=2s^2. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ d_f=s\sqrt2 }. $$

Space Diagonal

A space diagonal connects two opposite vertices and passes through the interior of the cube.

Using the face diagonal:

$$ d_s^2=(s\sqrt2)^2+s^2. $$

Thus:

$$ d_s^2=3s^2, $$

and:

$$ \boxed{ d_s=s\sqrt3 }. $$

Number of Face Diagonals

Each square face has two diagonals. Since the six faces are distinct:

$$ \boxed{ 6\cdot2=12 } $$

face diagonals occur on the cube.

Number of Space Diagonals

Each vertex has one opposite vertex, giving four distinct pairs of opposite vertices. Therefore a cube has:

$$ \boxed{4} $$

space diagonals.

Euler's Polyhedron Formula

A cube satisfies Euler's formula:

$$ V-E+F=2, $$

where here $V$ denotes the number of vertices, $E$ the number of edges, and $F$ the number of faces.

For the cube:

$$ 8-12+6=2. $$

Dihedral Angle

Adjacent faces of a cube meet at right angles. Therefore the interior dihedral angle is:

$$ \boxed{90^\circ=\frac{\pi}{2}}. $$

Vertex Figure

Three square faces meet at each vertex. The local combinatorial pattern around a vertex is therefore represented by:

$$ \boxed{4.4.4}. $$

This notation indicates that three squares meet at every vertex.

Schläfli Symbol

As a regular polyhedron, the cube has Schläfli symbol:

$$ \boxed{\{4,3\}}. $$

The $4$ indicates square faces, and the $3$ indicates that three faces meet at each vertex.

Cube as a Platonic Solid

The cube is one of the five Platonic solids because:

  • all faces are congruent regular polygons,
  • the same number of faces meet at every vertex, and
  • the solid has full regular symmetry.

Coordinate Representation

A cube of side length $s$ aligned with the coordinate axes may have vertices:

$$ \boxed{ (0,0,0),\; (s,0,0),\; (0,s,0),\; (0,0,s),\; (s,s,0),\; (s,0,s),\; (0,s,s),\; (s,s,s) }. $$

Centered Coordinate Representation

A cube centered at the origin with side length $2a$ may be represented by all sign combinations:

$$ \boxed{ (\pm a,\pm a,\pm a) }. $$

Space Diagonal from Coordinates

The distance between:

$$ (0,0,0) \quad\text{and}\quad (s,s,s) $$

is:

$$ \sqrt{s^2+s^2+s^2} = \boxed{s\sqrt3}. $$

Inscribed Sphere

A sphere tangent to all six faces of a cube is its insphere. Its radius is half the side length:

$$ \boxed{ r_{\text{in}}=\frac{s}{2} }. $$

Circumscribed Sphere

A sphere passing through all eight vertices has radius equal to half the space diagonal:

$$ \boxed{ r_{\text{circ}} = \frac{s\sqrt3}{2} }. $$

Sphere Through Edge Midpoints

A sphere passing through the midpoints of the twelve edges has radius:

$$ \boxed{ r_{\text{mid}} = \frac{s}{\sqrt2} = \frac{s\sqrt2}{2} }. $$

Cube and Square Relationship

A square is two-dimensional, while a cube is three-dimensional. A cube can be viewed as extending a square through a distance equal to its side length.

$$ \boxed{ \text{square}\longrightarrow\text{cube} } $$

parallels the dimensional progression:

$$ s^2\longrightarrow s^3. $$

Cube vs. Rectangular Prism

Every cube is a rectangular prism, but not every rectangular prism is a cube. A rectangular prism may have three different edge lengths:

$$ \ell,\quad w,\quad h, $$

whereas a cube requires:

$$ \boxed{ \ell=w=h=s }. $$

Common Misconception: A cube is not merely any six-faced solid. A cube requires six congruent square faces and equal edge lengths. A general rectangular prism has six rectangular faces but need not be a cube.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$V=s^3$ "The volume equals s cubed."
$SA=6s^2$ "The surface area equals six s squared."
$d_f=s\sqrt2$ "The face diagonal equals s square root of two."
$d_s=s\sqrt3$ "The space diagonal equals s square root of three."
$r=\frac{s}{2}$ "The inradius equals one-half the side length."
$\{4,3\}$ "The Schläfli symbol four comma three."
$(\pm a,\pm a,\pm a)$ "All coordinate triples with each coordinate equal to plus or minus a."

The word cube can refer either to the geometric solid or, in arithmetic, to the third power of a number. Context determines the intended meaning.

Geometrically:

$$ \boxed{\text{cube}=\text{three-dimensional regular hexahedron}}. $$

Algebraically:

$$ \boxed{ x^3=\text{"x cubed"} }. $$

The phrase unit cube means a cube whose side length is one unit:

$$ \boxed{s=1}. $$

Consequently:

$$ V=1, \qquad SA=6. $$

Defense Notation Advice: Distinguish between $V$, used for geometric volume, and $V$ used in Euler's formula to denote the number of vertices. When both appear in the same context, write the quantities explicitly or choose different symbols to avoid ambiguity.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Definition: A cube is a regular convex polyhedron consisting of six congruent square faces.
  • Faces: $$ \boxed{F=6}. $$
  • Edges: $$ \boxed{E=12}. $$
  • Vertices: $$ \boxed{V=8}. $$
  • Faces per Vertex: Exactly three faces meet at every vertex.
  • Edges per Vertex: Exactly three edges meet at every vertex.
  • Face Shape: Every face is a square.
  • Edge Equality: All twelve edges have equal length.
  • Face Area: $$ \boxed{ A_{\text{face}}=s^2 }. $$
  • Surface Area: $$ \boxed{ SA=6s^2 }. $$
  • Volume: $$ \boxed{ V=s^3 }. $$
  • Total Edge Length: $$ \boxed{ L=12s }. $$
  • Face Diagonal: $$ \boxed{ d_f=s\sqrt2 }. $$
  • Space Diagonal: $$ \boxed{ d_s=s\sqrt3 }. $$
  • Face Diagonals: A cube has: $$ \boxed{12} $$ face diagonals.
  • Space Diagonals: A cube has: $$ \boxed{4} $$ space diagonals.
  • Center: The four space diagonals intersect at the center of the cube.
  • Distance from Center to Face: $$ \boxed{ \frac{s}{2} }. $$
  • Distance from Center to Vertex: $$ \boxed{ \frac{s\sqrt3}{2} }. $$
  • Distance from Center to Edge Midpoint: $$ \boxed{ \frac{s\sqrt2}{2} }. $$
  • Insphere Radius: $$ \boxed{ r=\frac{s}{2} }. $$
  • Circumsphere Radius: $$ \boxed{ R=\frac{s\sqrt3}{2} }. $$
  • Euler Formula: $$ \boxed{ 8-12+6=2 }. $$
  • Schläfli Symbol: $$ \boxed{\{4,3\}}. $$
  • Platonic Solid: The cube is one of exactly five convex regular polyhedra.
  • Regular Hexahedron: The formal polyhedral name for a cube is: $$ \boxed{\text{regular hexahedron}}. $$
  • Rectangular Prism: Every cube is a rectangular prism because all square faces are also rectangles.
  • Square Prism: A cube can be regarded as a right square prism whose height equals the side length of its square base.
  • Parallelepiped: A cube is a special parallelepiped with equal edge lengths and right angles.
  • Convexity: A cube is convex: every line segment joining two points of the cube lies entirely within the cube.
  • Dihedral Angle: Adjacent faces meet at: $$ \boxed{90^\circ}. $$
  • Opposite Faces: Opposite faces are parallel and congruent.
  • Adjacent Faces: Adjacent faces lie in perpendicular planes.
  • Coordinate Model: An axis-aligned cube of side $s$ can be represented by: $$ \boxed{ 0\leq x\leq s,\qquad 0\leq y\leq s,\qquad 0\leq z\leq s }. $$
  • Centered Coordinate Model: A cube centered at the origin with side $2a$ is: $$ \boxed{ |x|\leq a,\qquad |y|\leq a,\qquad |z|\leq a }. $$
  • Vertices in Centered Form: $$ \boxed{ (\pm a,\pm a,\pm a) }. $$
  • Unit Cube: The standard unit cube is: $$ \boxed{ [0,1]^3 }. $$
  • Unit Cube Volume: $$ \boxed{1}. $$
  • Unit Cube Surface Area: $$ \boxed{6}. $$
  • Scaling: If every edge is multiplied by a scale factor $k$, surface area scales by: $$ k^2 $$ and volume scales by: $$ k^3. $$
  • Similarity: All cubes are similar because their corresponding linear dimensions have a common scale factor.
  • Congruence: Two cubes are congruent exactly when their side lengths are equal.
  • Net: A cube can be unfolded into a planar arrangement of six connected squares called a net.
  • Number of Cube Nets: Up to rotations and reflections in the plane, there are: $$ \boxed{11} $$ distinct nets of a cube.
  • Cross Section Parallel to a Face: A plane parallel to a face produces a square cross section.
  • Other Cross Sections: Depending on plane orientation and location, cross sections of a cube can be triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, or hexagons.
  • Maximum-Sided Planar Cross Section: A plane can intersect all six faces of a cube, producing a hexagonal cross section.
  • Dual Polyhedron: The dual of the cube is the regular octahedron.
  • Face-Vertex Duality: The cube has: $$ 6\text{ faces and }8\text{ vertices}, $$ while the octahedron has: $$ 8\text{ faces and }6\text{ vertices}. $$
  • Symmetry: The cube possesses extensive rotational and reflectional symmetry, making it one of the most symmetric three-dimensional solids.
  • Rotational Symmetry Group: The orientation-preserving symmetry group of a cube has: $$ \boxed{24} $$ rotational symmetries.
  • Full Symmetry Group: Including orientation-reversing symmetries, the cube has: $$ \boxed{48} $$ Euclidean symmetries.
  • Tessellation of Space: Congruent cubes can fill three-dimensional Euclidean space without gaps or overlaps.
  • Cubic Honeycomb: The regular tiling of space by cubes is called the cubic honeycomb.
  • Measurement: Cubic units such as: $$ \text{cm}^3,\qquad \text{m}^3,\qquad \text{ft}^3 $$ arise from measuring three-dimensional volume.
  • Algebraic Connection: The third power: $$ x^3 $$ is read "x cubed" because it corresponds geometrically to the volume of a cube with side length $x$.
  • Cube Root Connection: If: $$ x^3=V, $$ then: $$ \boxed{x=\sqrt[3]{V}}. $$
  • Higher-Dimensional Analogue: The $n$-dimensional generalization of the cube is the hypercube. In four dimensions it is called a tesseract.
  • Terminological Contrast: A cube is the geometric solid. A cube number or perfect cube is a number of the form: $$ n^3. $$ To cube a quantity means to raise it to the third power.
  • Core Interpretation: A cube is the maximally regular three-dimensional analogue of a square: $$ \boxed{ 6\text{ square faces},\; 12\text{ equal edges},\; 8\text{ vertices},\; V=s^3 }. $$

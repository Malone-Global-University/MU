Sum to Product Identities

Subfield: Geometry & Topology

Etymology & Roots

Sum to product identities are trigonometric identities that rewrite a sum or difference of sine or cosine expressions as a product of trigonometric factors.

$$ \boxed{ \text{sum or difference} \longrightarrow \text{product} }. $$

The name describes the direction of the transformation. These identities are closely related to the angle-sum and angle-difference formulas and are algebraic rearrangements of the same trigonometric structure.

Operational Nuance

Sine Plus Sine

$$ \boxed{ \sin A+\sin B = 2\sin\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right) \cos\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right) }. $$

Sine Minus Sine

$$ \boxed{ \sin A-\sin B = 2\cos\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right) \sin\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right) }. $$

Cosine Plus Cosine

$$ \boxed{ \cos A+\cos B = 2\cos\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right) \cos\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right) }. $$

Cosine Minus Cosine

$$ \boxed{ \cos A-\cos B = -2\sin\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right) \sin\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right) }. $$

Average and Half-Difference Angles

Each identity uses:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{A+B}{2} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \frac{A-B}{2} }. $$

The first is the average of the two angles, while the second is half their difference.

Derivation

Let:

$$ u=\frac{A+B}{2}, \qquad v=\frac{A-B}{2}. $$

Then $A=u+v$ and $B=u-v$. Applying the ordinary sine and cosine sum-and-difference identities produces the sum-to-product formulas.

Example

$$ \sin(5x)+\sin(x) = 2\sin(3x)\cos(2x). $$

Here:

$$ \frac{5x+x}{2}=3x, \qquad \frac{5x-x}{2}=2x. $$

Solving Equations

Sum-to-product identities can convert an additive trigonometric equation into a factored form:

$$ \sin A+\sin B=0 $$

becomes:

$$ 2\sin\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right) \cos\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right)=0. $$

The zero-product property can then be applied.

Connection to Product to Sum Identities

Product-to-sum identities perform the reverse style of transformation:

$$ \boxed{ \text{product} \longrightarrow \text{sum or difference} }. $$

The two families are algebraically related, although they should not be confused as identical formulas.

Wave Interpretation

The identity:

$$ \sin A+\sin B = 2\sin\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right) \cos\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right) $$

reveals how adding two oscillations can be viewed as a product involving an average phase or frequency and a difference-related modulation factor. This structure is useful in wave and beat phenomena.

Common Misconception

The half-sum and half-difference must be computed from the complete angles or arguments. They are not formed by separately halving only selected terms.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\sin A+\sin B$ "The sum of sine A and sine B."
$\frac{A+B}{2}$ "Half the sum of A and B," or "the average of A and B."
$\frac{A-B}{2}$ "Half the difference of A and B."
$2\sin\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right)$ "Two times sine of the half-sum times cosine of the half-difference."
$\cos A-\cos B$ "Cosine A minus cosine B."
$-2\sin\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right)$ "Negative two times sine of the half-sum times sine of the half-difference."

Defense Notation Advice: Keep the average-angle factor and half-difference factor visually grouped with parentheses. The cosine-difference identity carries a leading negative sign, which is a frequent source of errors. Reversing the order of $A-B$ can also change a sine factor's sign.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Purpose: Rewrite sums or differences of trigonometric functions as products.
  • Sine Sum: $\sin A+\sin B=2\sin((A+B)/2)\cos((A-B)/2)$.
  • Sine Difference: $\sin A-\sin B=2\cos((A+B)/2)\sin((A-B)/2)$.
  • Cosine Sum: $\cos A+\cos B=2\cos((A+B)/2)\cos((A-B)/2)$.
  • Cosine Difference: $\cos A-\cos B=-2\sin((A+B)/2)\sin((A-B)/2)$.
  • Equation Solving: Product form allows use of the zero-product property.
  • Simplification: Additive trigonometric expressions may become easier to compare or factor.
  • Wave Analysis: The formulas reveal average and difference frequency structure.
  • Beat Phenomena: Sums of nearby frequencies can be expressed as rapidly oscillating and slowly varying factors.
  • Trigonometric Proofs: Useful for transforming one side of an identity into another.
  • Product-to-Sum Contrast: Product-to-sum identities transform in the opposite direction.
  • Common Error: Forgetting to divide both the sum and difference of the angles by $2$.
  • Common Error: Losing the negative sign in the cosine-difference identity.
  • Common Error: Interchanging sine and cosine factors among the four formulas.
  • Common Error: Reversing $A-B$ without accounting for the odd symmetry of sine.
  • Core Interpretation: Sum-to-product identities reorganize two trigonometric terms around their average and half-difference: $$ \boxed{ \text{two-term trigonometric sum or difference} \longrightarrow \text{product involving } \frac{A+B}{2} \text{ and } \frac{A-B}{2} }. $$ They connect angle-addition formulas with factorization, equation solving, oscillation, and wave behavior.

Acute Triangle

Subfield: Geometry, Spatial Analysis & Topology

Etymology & Roots

An acute triangle is a triangle in which all three interior angles are acute. If the interior angles are $A$, $B$, and $C$, then:

$$ \boxed{ 0^\circ

The adjective acute comes from the same word family used for an acute angle, referring to an angle that is narrower than a right angle. The classification therefore describes the triangle through the measures of its three interior angles.

This is an angle-based classification. It is independent of the separate side-based classifications scalene, isosceles, and equilateral. A triangle can therefore be both acute and scalene, both acute and isosceles, or both acute and equilateral.

Every equilateral triangle is acute because each of its three angles measures:

$$ \boxed{60^\circ}. $$

The reverse is not true: most acute triangles are not equilateral.

Operational Nuance

Angle Definition

A triangle $\triangle ABC$ is acute precisely when:

$$ \boxed{ A<90^\circ,\qquad B<90^\circ,\qquad C<90^\circ }. $$

Since every Euclidean triangle satisfies:

$$ A+B+C=180^\circ, $$

all three angles can simultaneously be acute.

Why One Acute Angle Is Not Enough

A right triangle has two acute angles and one right angle. An obtuse triangle also has two acute angles and one obtuse angle. Therefore, the existence of acute angles alone does not make a triangle acute.

The condition is:

$$ \boxed{ \text{all three angles must be acute} }. $$

Largest-Angle Test

Because only the largest angle can potentially reach or exceed $90^\circ$, it is enough to determine whether the largest angle is acute.

If:

$$ C=\max\{A,B,C\}, $$

then the triangle is acute exactly when:

$$ \boxed{C<90^\circ}. $$

Side-Length Test

Suppose the side lengths are $a$, $b$, and $c$, with $c$ the longest side:

$$ c\ge a,\qquad c\ge b. $$

Then the triangle is acute precisely when:

$$ \boxed{ c^2

This gives an efficient classification test when the angles are not known directly.

Example from Side Lengths

Consider a triangle with side lengths:

$$ 5,\qquad6,\qquad7. $$

The longest side is $7$. Compare:

$$ 7^2=49 $$

with:

$$ 5^2+6^2=25+36=61. $$

Since:

$$ 49<61, $$

the triangle is acute.

Law of Cosines Explanation

The side-length test follows directly from the Law of Cosines. If $C$ is the largest angle and $c$ is its opposite side:

$$ c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C. $$

If $C$ is acute, then:

$$ \cos C>0. $$

Therefore:

$$ -2ab\cos C<0, $$

which gives:

$$ \boxed{ c^2

Comparison with Right and Obtuse Triangles

If $c$ is the longest side, the three angle classifications can be compared through:

$$ \boxed{ \begin{array}{rcl} c^2a^2+b^2 &\Longrightarrow& \text{obtuse triangle}. \end{array} } $$

Location of the Orthocenter

In an acute triangle, all three altitudes intersect at a point inside the triangle. This point is the orthocenter.

$$ \boxed{ \text{acute triangle} \Longrightarrow \text{orthocenter lies inside} }. $$

Location of the Circumcenter

The perpendicular bisectors of the sides also meet inside an acute triangle. Their intersection is the circumcenter.

$$ \boxed{ \text{acute triangle} \Longrightarrow \text{circumcenter lies inside} }. $$

Common Misconception: A triangle is not acute merely because it contains acute angles. Every right and obtuse triangle also contains two acute angles. All three interior angles must be acute.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$A,B,C<90^\circ$ "Angles A, B, and C are each less than ninety degrees."
$A+B+C=180^\circ$ "The three interior angles sum to one hundred eighty degrees."
$c^2<a^2+b^2$ "C squared is less than a squared plus b squared."
$\cos C>0$ "The cosine of angle C is positive."
$\triangle ABC$ "Triangle A B C."
$m\angle A=65^\circ$ "The measure of angle A is sixty-five degrees."

The phrase acute triangle refers to the classification of the triangle as a whole. By contrast, acute angle refers to one individual angle.

If:

$$ m\angle A=50^\circ,\qquad m\angle B=60^\circ,\qquad m\angle C=70^\circ, $$

one may say:

$$ \boxed{ \triangle ABC\text{ is acute} }. $$

$$ c^2

The symbol $c$ in this test must represent the longest side. Applying the inequality to an arbitrary side does not establish the classification.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Definition: A triangle is acute when: $$ \boxed{ A<90^\circ,\quad B<90^\circ,\quad C<90^\circ }. $$
  • Triangle Angle Sum: Every Euclidean triangle satisfies: $$ \boxed{ A+B+C=180^\circ }. $$
  • At Least Two Acute Angles: Every nondegenerate Euclidean triangle has at least two acute angles.
  • All Three Required: A triangle is classified as acute only if all three angles are acute.
  • Largest-Angle Test: If the largest angle is less than $90^\circ$, then all three angles are acute.
  • Side-Length Test: If $c$ is the longest side: $$ \boxed{ c^2
  • Right-Triangle Boundary: If: $$ c^2=a^2+b^2, $$ the triangle is right rather than acute.
  • Obtuse-Triangle Comparison: If: $$ c^2>a^2+b^2, $$ the triangle is obtuse.
  • Law of Cosines: For the largest angle $C$: $$ c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C. $$ The triangle is acute when: $$ \boxed{\cos C>0}. $$
  • Equilateral Triangle: Every equilateral triangle is acute because: $$ A=B=C=60^\circ. $$
  • Isosceles Acute Triangle: An isosceles triangle may be acute if all three of its angles remain below $90^\circ$.
  • Scalene Acute Triangle: A scalene triangle may also be acute. Angle classification and side classification are independent systems.
  • Side Classification: Equilateral, isosceles, and scalene describe side relationships.
  • Angle Classification: Acute, right, and obtuse describe the largest interior angle and therefore the triangle's angle type.
  • Altitudes: In an acute triangle, each altitude segment from a vertex to the opposite side meets the opposite side itself rather than an extension of that side.
  • Orthocenter: The three altitudes of an acute triangle intersect inside the triangle.
  • Circumcenter: The perpendicular bisectors of an acute triangle intersect inside the triangle.
  • Incenter: The incenter lies inside every nondegenerate triangle, including every acute triangle.
  • Centroid: The centroid also lies inside every nondegenerate triangle.
  • Circumcircle: Every triangle has a circumcircle. In an acute triangle, its center lies inside the triangle.
  • Diameter Criterion: If a triangle is inscribed in a circle, an angle subtending a diameter is right. Therefore an acute triangle cannot have one of its sides serving as a diameter of its circumcircle.
  • Area: Acute classification does not determine area by itself. An acute triangle's area may still be calculated by: $$ \boxed{ A=\frac12bh }. $$
  • Trigonometric Area Formula: If sides $a$ and $b$ include angle $C$: $$ \boxed{ A=\frac12ab\sin C }. $$
  • Positive Cosines: Because every angle of an acute triangle lies between $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$: $$ \boxed{ \cos A>0,\qquad \cos B>0,\qquad \cos C>0 }. $$
  • Positive Sines: Likewise: $$ \sin A>0,\qquad \sin B>0,\qquad \sin C>0. $$
  • Angle-Side Ordering: The largest side lies opposite the largest angle, and the smallest side lies opposite the smallest angle.
  • Coordinate Geometry: A triangle defined by three coordinate points can be classified as acute by computing squared side lengths and comparing the square of the longest side with the sum of the other two squares.
  • Dot-Product Test: At each vertex, the dot product of the two side vectors forming the interior angle is positive when that angle is acute.
  • Vector Characterization: For vectors $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ forming an interior angle: $$ \mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}>0 $$ precisely when that angle is acute.
  • Rigid Motions: Translation, rotation, and reflection preserve angle measures, so they preserve whether a triangle is acute.
  • Dilation: A dilation changes side lengths but preserves angles. Therefore an acute triangle remains acute under dilation.
  • Similarity: Every triangle similar to an acute triangle is also acute because corresponding angles are congruent.
  • Diagram Caution: A triangle that appears acute in a drawing is not necessarily mathematically acute. Classification should follow from measurements, given information, or valid geometric reasoning.
  • Terminological Contrast: An acute triangle has three acute angles. A right triangle has one right angle and two acute angles. An obtuse triangle has one obtuse angle and two acute angles.

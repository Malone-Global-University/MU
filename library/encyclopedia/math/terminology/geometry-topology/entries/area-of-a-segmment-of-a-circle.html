Area of a Segment of a Circle

Subfield: Geometry, Spatial Analysis & Topology

Etymology & Roots

A segment of a circle, more precisely a circular segment, is the region bounded by a chord of a circle and the arc subtended by that chord.

It should be distinguished from a sector, which is bounded by two radii and an arc.

For a minor circular segment with radius $r$ and central angle $\theta$ measured in radians:

$$ \boxed{ A_{\text{segment}} = \frac12r^2(\theta-\sin\theta) }. $$

This formula comes from subtracting the area of the central triangle from the area of the corresponding sector.

Operational Nuance

Sector Minus Triangle

A minor circular segment may be decomposed conceptually as:

$$ \boxed{ A_{\text{segment}} = A_{\text{sector}} - A_{\triangle} }. $$

Sector Area

For $\theta$ in radians:

$$ A_{\text{sector}} = \frac12r^2\theta. $$

Triangle Area

The two radii and the chord form an isosceles triangle with two sides of length $r$ and included angle $\theta$.

Its area is:

$$ A_{\triangle} = \frac12r^2\sin\theta. $$

Minor-Segment Formula in Radians

Subtract:

$$ A_{\text{segment}} = \frac12r^2\theta - \frac12r^2\sin\theta. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ A_{\text{segment}} = \frac12r^2(\theta-\sin\theta) }. $$

For the usual minor-segment interpretation:

$$ 0\le\theta\le\pi. $$

Degree Formula

If $\theta$ is measured in degrees, then the sector area is:

$$ \frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2. $$

The triangle area is:

$$ \frac12r^2\sin\theta, $$

with the sine interpreted in degree mode.

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ A_{\text{segment}} = \frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2 - \frac12r^2\sin\theta }. $$

Example

Suppose:

$$ r=6, \qquad \theta=\frac{\pi}{3}. $$

Then:

$$ A = \frac12(6)^2 \left( \frac{\pi}{3} - \sin\frac{\pi}{3} \right). $$

Since:

$$ \sin\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{\sqrt3}{2}, $$

we obtain:

$$ A = 18 \left( \frac{\pi}{3} - \frac{\sqrt3}{2} \right). $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ A= 6\pi-9\sqrt3 }. $$

Chord Length

For radius $r$ and central angle $\theta$:

$$ \boxed{ c= 2r\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) }. $$

Here $c$ is the chord bounding the segment.

Sagitta

The sagitta $h$ is the maximum perpendicular distance from the chord to the corresponding arc.

For a minor segment:

$$ \boxed{ h= r-r\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) }. $$

Equivalently:

$$ \boxed{ h= r\left( 1-\cos\frac{\theta}{2} \right) }. $$

Chord from Radius and Sagitta

Half the chord, the distance $r-h$, and the radius form a right triangle:

$$ \left(\frac c2\right)^2 +(r-h)^2 = r^2. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ c= 2\sqrt{2rh-h^2} }. $$

Radius from Chord and Sagitta

Rearranging the same relation gives:

$$ \boxed{ r= \frac{c^2}{8h} + \frac h2 }. $$

Central Angle from Chord

From:

$$ c= 2r\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right), $$

we obtain:

$$ \boxed{ \theta= 2\arcsin\left(\frac{c}{2r}\right) }. $$

Central Angle from Sagitta

Since:

$$ r-h= r\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right), $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ \theta= 2\arccos\left(\frac{r-h}{r}\right) }. $$

Area from Radius and Sagitta

A common form uses $r$ and sagitta $h$ directly:

$$ \boxed{ A= r^2 \arccos\left(\frac{r-h}{r}\right) - (r-h)\sqrt{2rh-h^2} }. $$

The inverse cosine is interpreted in radians in this formula.

Area from Radius and Chord

If $c$ is the chord length, then:

$$ \theta= 2\arcsin\left(\frac{c}{2r}\right). $$

Substitution into the standard segment formula yields:

$$ \boxed{ A= r^2 \arcsin\left(\frac{c}{2r}\right) - \frac c4 \sqrt{4r^2-c^2} }. $$

Minor and Major Segments

A chord divides a disk into two circular segments.

The smaller one is the minor segment; the larger one is the major segment.

Major-Segment Area

If $A_{\text{minor}}$ is the smaller segment area, then:

$$ \boxed{ A_{\text{major}} = \pi r^2-A_{\text{minor}} }. $$

Semicircle Case

If the chord is a diameter:

$$ \theta=\pi. $$

Then:

$$ A = \frac12r^2 (\pi-\sin\pi) = \boxed{ \frac12\pi r^2 }. $$

The two segments are then equal semicircles.

Sector versus Segment

A sector includes the central triangle and the circular segment:

$$ \boxed{ A_{\text{sector}} = A_{\triangle} + A_{\text{segment}} }. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ A_{\text{segment}} = A_{\text{sector}} - A_{\triangle} }. $$

Circular Segment versus Parabolic Segment

Both are regions bounded partly by a chord, but the curved boundary differs:

  • a circular segment uses a circular arc,
  • a parabolic segment uses a parabolic arc.

Their area formulas are therefore different.

Scaling

For fixed central angle, if:

$$ r\mapsto kr, $$

then:

$$ A' = \frac12(kr)^2 (\theta-\sin\theta). $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ A\mapsto k^2A }. $$

Doubling Radius

$$ \boxed{ r\mapsto2r \Rightarrow A\mapsto4A }. $$

Small-Angle Behavior

For small positive $\theta$:

$$ \sin\theta = \theta-\frac{\theta^3}{6} +O(\theta^5). $$

Therefore:

$$ \theta-\sin\theta = \frac{\theta^3}{6} +O(\theta^5). $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ A \approx \frac{r^2\theta^3}{12} } $$

for a very small minor segment.

Coordinate Interpretation

For the circle:

$$ x^2+y^2=r^2, $$

a horizontal chord:

$$ y=k $$

cuts off a segment. Its endpoints occur at:

$$ x= \pm\sqrt{r^2-k^2}. $$

The area of the upper segment is:

$$ \boxed{ A= \int_{-\sqrt{r^2-k^2}}^{\sqrt{r^2-k^2}} \left( \sqrt{r^2-x^2}-k \right)\,dx }. $$

Integral and Geometric Formulas Agree

Evaluating that integral reproduces the equivalent radius-sagitta or sector-minus-triangle formulas.

This connects elementary circle geometry with calculus area-between-curves methods.

Segment Height Is Not Chord Length

The chord $c$ measures straight-line width across the segment, while the sagitta $h$ measures the maximum perpendicular depth of the segment.

$$ \boxed{ c\ne h } $$

except in accidental numerical cases.

Arc Length Is Not Chord Length

The arc follows the circle, while the chord is straight.

For a nondegenerate minor arc:

$$ \boxed{ \text{arc length}>c }. $$

Units

Because the formula contains $r^2$, area is measured in square units:

$$ \boxed{ \text{units}^2 }. $$

Common Misconception: The segment area is not the same as the sector area. The central triangle must be removed for a minor circular segment.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$A=\frac12r^2(\theta-\sin\theta)$ "Segment area equals one-half r squared times theta minus sine theta," with theta in radians.
$A_{\text{segment}}=A_{\text{sector}}-A_{\triangle}$ "The circular-segment area equals the sector area minus the triangle area."
$c=2r\sin(\theta/2)$ "Chord length equals two r sine theta over two."
$h=r(1-\cos(\theta/2))$ "Sagitta equals r times one minus cosine theta over two."
$A_{\text{major}}=\pi r^2-A_{\text{minor}}$ "Major-segment area equals the circle area minus the minor-segment area."
$r=\frac{c^2}{8h}+\frac h2$ "Radius equals c squared over eight h plus h over two."

In the standard formula:

$$ A=\frac12r^2(\theta-\sin\theta), $$

$\theta$ and $\sin\theta$ must use the same angular convention, and the compact form assumes radians.

Defense Notation Advice: Identify whether the requested region is the minor or major segment. Do not substitute a degree measure directly into the radian formula, and distinguish the chord, arc, and sagitta before selecting a formula.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Meaning: A circular segment is the region bounded by a chord and its corresponding arc.
  • Minor-Segment Formula: $$ \boxed{ A= \frac12r^2(\theta-\sin\theta) } $$ for $\theta$ in radians.
  • Sector Formula: $$ \boxed{ A_{\text{sector}} = \frac12r^2\theta }. $$
  • Central-Triangle Formula: $$ \boxed{ A_{\triangle} = \frac12r^2\sin\theta }. $$
  • Decomposition: $$ \boxed{ A_{\text{segment}} = A_{\text{sector}} - A_{\triangle} }. $$
  • Degree Form: $$ \boxed{ A= \frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2 - \frac12r^2\sin\theta }. $$
  • Chord Length: $$ \boxed{ c= 2r\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) }. $$
  • Sagitta: $$ \boxed{ h= r\left( 1-\cos\frac{\theta}{2} \right) }. $$
  • Chord from Sagitta: $$ \boxed{ c= 2\sqrt{2rh-h^2} }. $$
  • Radius from Chord and Sagitta: $$ \boxed{ r= \frac{c^2}{8h} + \frac h2 }. $$
  • Angle from Chord: $$ \boxed{ \theta= 2\arcsin\left(\frac{c}{2r}\right) }. $$
  • Angle from Sagitta: $$ \boxed{ \theta= 2\arccos\left(\frac{r-h}{r}\right) }. $$
  • Area from Radius and Sagitta: $$ \boxed{ A= r^2 \arccos\left(\frac{r-h}{r}\right) - (r-h)\sqrt{2rh-h^2} }. $$
  • Area from Radius and Chord: $$ \boxed{ A= r^2 \arcsin\left(\frac{c}{2r}\right) - \frac c4 \sqrt{4r^2-c^2} }. $$
  • Minor Segment: The smaller region cut off by a chord.
  • Major Segment: The larger region cut off by the same chord.
  • Major-Segment Area: $$ \boxed{ A_{\text{major}} = \pi r^2-A_{\text{minor}} }. $$
  • Diameter Chord: If the chord is a diameter: $$ \boxed{ A=\frac12\pi r^2 }. $$
  • Sector Contrast: A sector is bounded by two radii and an arc.
  • Segment Contrast: A circular segment is bounded by a chord and an arc.
  • Parabolic-Segment Contrast: A parabolic segment uses a parabolic arc rather than a circular arc.
  • Scaling: For fixed $\theta$: $$ \boxed{ r\mapsto kr \Rightarrow A\mapsto k^2A }. $$
  • Small-Angle Approximation: $$ \boxed{ A\approx \frac{r^2\theta^3}{12} } $$ for small positive $\theta$ in radians.
  • Coordinate Integral: For a horizontal chord $y=k$ in $x^2+y^2=r^2$: $$ \boxed{ A= \int_{-\sqrt{r^2-k^2}}^{\sqrt{r^2-k^2}} \left( \sqrt{r^2-x^2}-k \right)\,dx }. $$
  • Arc-Length Caution: Arc length and chord length are different measurements.
  • Sagitta Caution: Sagitta measures perpendicular segment depth, not boundary length.
  • Angle-Unit Caution: $$ \boxed{ \frac12r^2(\theta-\sin\theta) } $$ assumes radians.
  • Square Units: Circular-segment area is measured in square units.
  • Common Error: Using sector area alone gives too much area for a minor circular segment because it includes the central triangle.
  • Terminological Contrast: Chord is the straight boundary. Arc is the curved boundary. Sagitta is the maximum perpendicular distance from chord to arc. Sector includes the center. Circular segment is the region enclosed by the chord and arc.

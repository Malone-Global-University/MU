SOHCAHTOA

Subfield: Geometry, Spatial Analysis & Topology

Etymology & Roots

SOHCAHTOA is a mnemonic formed from the initial letters of the three basic right-triangle trigonometric ratios: sine, cosine, and tangent. Unlike terms such as sine or tangent, SOHCAHTOA is not derived from an ancient mathematical language. It is an educational memory device constructed directly from English terminology.

$$ \text{SOH}: \quad \text{Sine} = \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Hypotenuse}} $$ $$ \text{CAH}: \quad \text{Cosine} = \frac{\text{Adjacent}}{\text{Hypotenuse}} $$ $$ \text{TOA}: \quad \text{Tangent} = \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Adjacent}} $$

The mnemonic is commonly written as SOHCAHTOA, SOH CAH TOA, or SohCahToa. Printed examples are documented by at least the mid-twentieth century, although the precise individual responsible for inventing the mnemonic is not securely established. Its longevity comes from compressing three related formulas into a single pronounceable sequence.

Operational Nuance

SOHCAHTOA is used with a right triangle and a selected acute reference angle $\theta$. The three side names are assigned relative to that angle: the hypotenuse is the side opposite the right angle, the opposite side lies across from $\theta$, and the adjacent side is the non-hypotenuse side touching $\theta$.

$$ \sin\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} $$ $$ \cos\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} $$ $$ \tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} $$

The hypotenuse remains the same regardless of which acute angle is selected because it is always opposite the $90^\circ$ angle. The labels opposite and adjacent, however, depend on the reference angle. If the other acute angle is selected, the two legs exchange roles.

For example, suppose a right triangle has sides of lengths $3$, $4$, and $5$, with angle $\theta$ opposite the side of length $3$. Relative to $\theta$:

$$ \text{opposite}=3, \qquad \text{adjacent}=4, \qquad \text{hypotenuse}=5 $$

SOHCAHTOA then gives:

$$ \sin\theta=\frac{3}{5} $$ $$ \cos\theta=\frac{4}{5} $$ $$ \tan\theta=\frac{3}{4} $$

The mnemonic is also useful when solving for unknown side lengths. If the opposite side and angle are known but the hypotenuse is unknown, SOH identifies sine as the appropriate relationship. If the adjacent and hypotenuse are involved, CAH points to cosine. If only the two legs are involved, TOA points to tangent.

When the side lengths are known and the angle itself is unknown, inverse trigonometric functions can be used:

$$ \theta = \sin^{-1}\left( \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \right) $$ $$ \theta = \cos^{-1}\left( \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \right) $$ $$ \theta = \tan^{-1}\left( \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} \right) $$

Common Misconception: SOHCAHTOA does not mean that one side of a triangle is permanently "opposite" and another is permanently "adjacent." Those labels are relative to the chosen acute angle. Only the hypotenuse has an absolute role within a right triangle. A side that is opposite one acute angle will be adjacent to the other acute angle.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
SOH "Sine is opposite over hypotenuse."
CAH "Cosine is adjacent over hypotenuse."
TOA "Tangent is opposite over adjacent."
$\sin\theta=\frac{O}{H}$ "Sine theta equals opposite over hypotenuse."
$\cos\theta=\frac{A}{H}$ "Cosine theta equals adjacent over hypotenuse."
$\tan\theta=\frac{O}{A}$ "Tangent theta equals opposite over adjacent."

SOHCAHTOA is commonly pronounced approximately "soh-kuh-TOH-uh," although pronunciation varies because the expression was created as a mnemonic rather than as an ordinary lexical word. In instruction, it is often broken into three units: "SOH, CAH, TOA."

The abbreviations $O$, $A$, and $H$ are useful pedagogically, but formal mathematical work generally labels triangle sides with variables such as $a$, $b$, and $c$. SOHCAHTOA should therefore be understood as a memory framework for identifying the correct ratio rather than as a formal notation system of its own.

Defense Notation Advice: Before applying SOHCAHTOA, explicitly identify the reference angle and label the three relevant sides. A strong explanation is: "Relative to $\theta$, this side is opposite, this leg is adjacent, and this side is the hypotenuse. Since the known and unknown quantities involve opposite and adjacent, I use tangent." This demonstrates understanding of the geometry rather than merely reciting the mnemonic.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Right-Triangle Trigonometry: SOHCAHTOA is primarily a memory aid for the definitions of sine, cosine, and tangent in a right triangle: $$ \sin\theta=\frac{O}{H}, \qquad \cos\theta=\frac{A}{H}, \qquad \tan\theta=\frac{O}{A}. $$ It is especially useful when solving for missing sides and acute angles.
  • Angles of Elevation & Depression: Many applied trigonometry problems construct a right triangle from a horizontal distance, vertical height, and line of sight. Once the angle of elevation or depression has been identified correctly, SOHCAHTOA helps determine which trigonometric ratio connects the known and unknown quantities.
  • Indirect Measurement: Surveying-style classroom problems use SOHCAHTOA to estimate heights and distances that cannot conveniently be measured directly. For example, if a building is viewed from horizontal distance $d$ at angle of elevation $\theta$, then: $$ \tan\theta=\frac{h}{d} $$ and therefore: $$ h=d\tan\theta. $$
  • Complementary Angles: The mnemonic also helps reveal why sine and cosine are cofunctions. In a right triangle, the side opposite one acute angle is adjacent to the other. Since the two acute angles are complementary: $$ \sin\theta=\cos(90^\circ-\theta). $$ The relationship is therefore built directly into the geometry of the triangle.
  • Beyond Right Triangles: SOHCAHTOA is not a universal procedure for every trigonometric problem. For non-right triangles, methods such as the Law of Sines and Law of Cosines are generally required. The mnemonic specifically encodes right-triangle definitions of sine, cosine, and tangent.
  • Unit Circle & Higher Trigonometry: In more advanced mathematics, sine and cosine are defined beyond acute angles through the unit circle, and later through functions, infinite series, and complex analysis. SOHCAHTOA remains consistent with these broader definitions when restricted to acute angles in right triangles, but it is not itself the foundational definition used for all real or complex arguments.
  • Reciprocal Trigonometric Functions: SOHCAHTOA directly encodes only sine, cosine, and tangent. The remaining three classical trigonometric functions are their reciprocals: $$ \csc\theta=\frac{1}{\sin\theta} =\frac{H}{O}, $$ $$ \sec\theta=\frac{1}{\cos\theta} =\frac{H}{A}, $$ $$ \cot\theta=\frac{1}{\tan\theta} =\frac{A}{O}. $$
  • Terminological Contrast: SOHCAHTOA is a mnemonic, not a theorem, identity, or independent mathematical law. It packages three right-triangle definitions into an easily remembered sequence. Understanding which side is opposite, adjacent, or the hypotenuse remains necessary before the mnemonic can be applied correctly.

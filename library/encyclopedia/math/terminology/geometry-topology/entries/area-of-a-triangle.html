Area of a Triangle

Subfield: Geometry, Spatial Analysis & Topology

Etymology & Roots

The area of a triangle is the measure of the two-dimensional region enclosed by its three sides.

The fundamental area formula is:

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac12bh }. $$

Here $b$ is any chosen side used as the base, and $h$ is the perpendicular distance from the opposite vertex to the line containing that base.

The factor $\frac12$ reflects a basic geometric relationship: a triangle with base $b$ and height $h$ has exactly half the area of a parallelogram having the same base and perpendicular height.

$$ \boxed{ A_{\triangle} = \frac12A_{\text{parallelogram}} }. $$

Operational Nuance

Standard Base-Height Formula

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac12bh }. $$

The height must be perpendicular to the chosen base or to the line containing that base.

Any Side Can Be the Base

If the sides are $a$, $b$, and $c$, with corresponding altitudes $h_a$, $h_b$, and $h_c$, then:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac12ah_a = \frac12bh_b = \frac12ch_c }. $$

Changing the base changes the corresponding height, but not the area.

Altitude Placement

The altitude's location depends on the triangle.

  • In an acute triangle, all three altitude feet lie on the opposite sides.
  • In a right triangle, two sides themselves serve as altitudes.
  • In an obtuse triangle, some altitude feet lie on extensions of opposite sides.

Therefore the height is not required to lie entirely inside the triangle.

Why the Formula Works

Two congruent copies of any triangle can be arranged to form a parallelogram with the same base $b$ and height $h$.

The parallelogram has area:

$$ bh. $$

Since it consists of two congruent triangles:

$$ \boxed{ A_{\triangle}=\frac12bh }. $$

Example

Suppose:

$$ b=10, \qquad h=7. $$

Then:

$$ A= \frac12(10)(7) = \boxed{ 35 }. $$

Solving for Height

Starting with:

$$ A=\frac12bh, $$

multiply by $2$:

$$ 2A=bh. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ h=\frac{2A}{b} }. $$

Solving for Base

$$ \boxed{ b=\frac{2A}{h} }. $$

Right Triangle

In a right triangle, the two legs are perpendicular. Therefore, if their lengths are $a$ and $b$:

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac12ab }. $$

One leg serves as the base while the other serves as the height.

Area from Two Sides and Included Angle

If sides $a$ and $b$ enclose angle $C$, the altitude relative to side $a$ is:

$$ h=b\sin C. $$

Therefore:

$$ A = \frac12a(b\sin C) = \boxed{ \frac12ab\sin C }. $$

Equivalent Trigonometric Forms

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac12bc\sin A = \frac12ca\sin B = \frac12ab\sin C }. $$

Example with Two Sides and an Angle

Suppose:

$$ a=8, \qquad b=5, \qquad C=30^\circ. $$

Then:

$$ A= \frac12(8)(5)\sin30^\circ. $$

Since:

$$ \sin30^\circ=\frac12, $$

we obtain:

$$ \boxed{ A=10 }. $$

Heron's Formula

If all three side lengths $a$, $b$, and $c$ are known, first compute the semiperimeter:

$$ \boxed{ s=\frac{a+b+c}{2} }. $$

Then:

$$ \boxed{ A= \sqrt{ s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c) } }. $$

Heron's formula is especially useful when no altitude or angle is given directly.

Example with Heron's Formula

For a triangle with sides:

$$ 3,\quad4,\quad5, $$

the semiperimeter is:

$$ s= \frac{3+4+5}{2} = 6. $$

Therefore:

$$ A= \sqrt{ 6(6-3)(6-4)(6-5) }. $$

Thus:

$$ A= \sqrt{36} = \boxed{ 6 }. $$

Equilateral Triangle

If every side has length $s$, the altitude is:

$$ h=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}s. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac{\sqrt3}{4}s^2 }. $$

Area from Inradius

Let $r$ be the inradius and $s$ the semiperimeter.

Connecting the incenter to the vertices divides the triangle into three smaller triangles, each having height $r$.

Therefore:

$$ A = \frac12ar+ \frac12br+ \frac12cr. $$

Factor:

$$ A= \frac12(a+b+c)r. $$

Since:

$$ s=\frac{a+b+c}{2}, $$

we obtain:

$$ \boxed{ A=rs }. $$

Inradius from Area

$$ \boxed{ r=\frac{A}{s} }. $$

Area from Circumradius

Let $R$ be the circumradius. Using the extended Law of Sines:

$$ c=2R\sin C. $$

Since:

$$ A=\frac12ab\sin C, $$

substitute:

$$ \sin C=\frac{c}{2R}. $$

Then:

$$ A= \frac12ab \left( \frac{c}{2R} \right). $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac{abc}{4R} }. $$

Circumradius from Area

$$ \boxed{ R=\frac{abc}{4A} }. $$

Coordinate Formula

If the vertices are:

$$ (x_1,y_1), \qquad (x_2,y_2), \qquad (x_3,y_3), $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac12 \left| x_1(y_2-y_3) + x_2(y_3-y_1) + x_3(y_1-y_2) \right| }. $$

Determinant Form

The coordinate formula can be written:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac12 \left| \det \begin{pmatrix} x_1 & y_1 & 1\\ x_2 & y_2 & 1\\ x_3 & y_3 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \right| }. $$

Vector Form in the Plane

Let:

$$ \mathbf{u} = \overrightarrow{AB}, \qquad \mathbf{v} = \overrightarrow{AC}. $$

Then:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac12 |\det(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})| }. $$

If:

$$ \mathbf{u}= \langle u_1,u_2\rangle, \qquad \mathbf{v}= \langle v_1,v_2\rangle, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac12 |u_1v_2-u_2v_1| }. $$

Cross-Product Formula

In three-dimensional vector notation:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac12 \| \mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v} \| }. $$

Since:

$$ \|\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}\| = \|\mathbf{u}\| \|\mathbf{v}\| \sin\theta, $$

this is equivalent to the trigonometric formula.

Signed Area

Without absolute value:

$$ \frac12 \det(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}) $$

is an oriented or signed area. Reversing the vertex order reverses the sign.

Ordinary geometric area is:

$$ \boxed{ A\ge0 }. $$

Collinearity Test

Three points are collinear exactly when the corresponding triangle has zero area:

$$ \boxed{ A=0 }. $$

Equivalently, their determinant is zero.

Median and Area

A median joins a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side.

Since the two smaller triangles have equal base lengths and the same altitude:

$$ \boxed{ A_1=A_2=\frac12A }. $$

The Three Medians

The three medians meet at the centroid. Together they divide the triangle into six smaller triangles of equal area:

$$ \boxed{ A_i=\frac{A}{6} }. $$

Similarity and Area Ratios

If two similar triangles have corresponding side ratio:

$$ k, $$

then their corresponding heights also have ratio $k$. Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{A_2}{A_1}=k^2 }. $$

Scaling

If every length is multiplied by $k$:

$$ b\mapsto kb, \qquad h\mapsto kh, $$

then:

$$ A' = \frac12(kb)(kh) = k^2A. $$

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ A\mapsto k^2A }. $$

Doubling Every Side

$$ \boxed{ k=2 \Rightarrow A\mapsto4A }. $$

Fixed Base

For fixed base $b$:

$$ A=\frac b2h. $$

Therefore area is directly proportional to height.

Fixed Height

For fixed height $h$:

$$ A=\frac h2b. $$

Therefore area is directly proportional to base length.

Triangles between the Same Parallels

Triangles sharing the same base and having their opposite vertices on a line parallel to that base have the same altitude.

Consequently:

$$ \boxed{ \text{same base + same height} \Rightarrow \text{same area} }. $$

Maximum Area from Two Fixed Sides

If two side lengths $a$ and $b$ are fixed:

$$ A=\frac12ab\sin C. $$

Since:

$$ \sin C\le1, $$

the greatest area occurs when:

$$ C=90^\circ. $$

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ A_{\max}=\frac12ab }. $$

Maximum Area for Fixed Perimeter

Among all triangles with a fixed perimeter, the equilateral triangle has the greatest area.

If the perimeter is $P$, each side of that maximizing triangle is:

$$ \frac P3. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ A_{\max} = \frac{\sqrt3}{36}P^2 }. $$

Area and Degenerate Triangles

If the three vertices become collinear, the perpendicular height becomes zero:

$$ h=0. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ A=0 }. $$

Such a configuration is called a degenerate triangle rather than an ordinary nondegenerate triangle.

Units

Since area multiplies two length measurements:

$$ \frac12bh, $$

the result is measured in square units:

$$ \boxed{ \text{units}^2 }. $$

If $b$ and $h$ are measured in centimeters, the area is measured in square centimeters.

Common Misconception

A side adjacent to the chosen base is not automatically the height. Unless it is perpendicular to the base, it cannot be substituted directly for $h$.

$$ \boxed{ h=\text{perpendicular height} }. $$

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$A=\frac12bh$ "Area equals one-half base times height."
$A=\frac12ab\sin C$ "Area equals one-half a b sine C."
$s=\frac{a+b+c}{2}$ "The semiperimeter equals a plus b plus c, divided by two."
$A=\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}$ "Area equals the square root of s times s minus a times s minus b times s minus c."
$A=rs$ "Area equals the inradius times the semiperimeter."
$A=\frac{abc}{4R}$ "Area equals a b c divided by four R."
$A=\frac12|\det(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})|$ "Area equals one-half the absolute value of the determinant of u and v."

The phrase base and height describes a paired relationship. A height belongs to a particular chosen base, so changing the base generally changes the numerical height.

Defense Notation Advice: Distinguish the semiperimeter $s$ in Heron's formula from a side length that might also be labeled $s$ in another context. Whenever possible, state explicitly which quantity each symbol represents.

  • Square Units: Triangle area is measured in square units.
  • Common Error: The height is not necessarily a side of the triangle.
  • Common Error: In an obtuse triangle, an altitude may meet an extension of the opposite side; this does not invalidate the base-height formula.
  • Terminological Contrast: Base is the side chosen for reference. Altitude is the perpendicular segment from the opposite vertex to the base line. Height is the length of that altitude. Area measures the enclosed two-dimensional region and is one-half the product of a base and its corresponding perpendicular height.

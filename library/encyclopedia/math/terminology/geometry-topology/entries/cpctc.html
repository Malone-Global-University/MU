CPCTC

Subfield: Geometry & Topology

Etymology & Roots

CPCTC is an initialism for:

$$ \boxed{ \text{Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent} }. $$

The principle states that once two triangles have been established as congruent, every side and angle of one triangle is congruent to the corresponding side or angle of the other triangle.

The expression combines two fundamental ideas in geometry: congruence and correspondence. Congruence means that two triangles have the same size and shape, while correspondence identifies which vertices, sides, and angles occupy matching positions in the two triangles.

If:

$$ \triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF, $$

then the order of the letters establishes:

$$ A\leftrightarrow D,\qquad B\leftrightarrow E,\qquad C\leftrightarrow F. $$

CPCTC then allows the corresponding side and angle relationships to be concluded from the established triangle congruence.

CPCTC is a triangle-specific form of the broader geometric principle sometimes expressed as CPCFC:

$$ \boxed{ \text{Corresponding Parts of Congruent Figures are Congruent} }. $$

Operational Nuance

Basic Principle

Suppose:

$$ \boxed{ \triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF }. $$

The correspondence is:

$$ A\leftrightarrow D,\qquad B\leftrightarrow E,\qquad C\leftrightarrow F. $$

CPCTC therefore gives the corresponding side congruences:

$$ \overline{AB}\cong\overline{DE}, $$ $$ \overline{BC}\cong\overline{EF}, $$ $$ \overline{AC}\cong\overline{DF}. $$

It also gives the corresponding angle congruences:

$$ \angle A\cong\angle D, $$ $$ \angle B\cong\angle E, $$ $$ \angle C\cong\angle F. $$

Correspondence Is Determined by Order

The order of the vertices in a congruence statement is mathematically significant.

From:

$$ \triangle ABC\cong\triangle PQR, $$

one reads:

$$ A\leftrightarrow P,\qquad B\leftrightarrow Q,\qquad C\leftrightarrow R. $$

Therefore:

$$ AB=PQ,\qquad BC=QR,\qquad AC=PR. $$

Writing the vertices in an incorrect order can create an incorrect correspondence even when the same two triangles are being discussed.

CPCTC Comes After Triangle Congruence

CPCTC is generally used only after the triangles have already been proved congruent.

$$ \boxed{ \text{prove triangle congruence} \longrightarrow \text{apply CPCTC} \longrightarrow \text{conclude corresponding parts congruent} }. $$

A typical proof might first establish:

$$ \triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF $$

using a congruence criterion such as SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, or HL, and then conclude:

$$ \overline{BC}\cong\overline{EF} $$

by CPCTC.

CPCTC Is Not a Congruence Criterion

CPCTC should not be confused with triangle congruence criteria. SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, and HL provide sufficient information to establish that two triangles are congruent.

CPCTC instead provides consequences once congruence is known.

$$ \boxed{ \text{SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, HL} \Rightarrow \text{triangle congruence} \Rightarrow \text{CPCTC} }. $$

Congruent Segments and Equal Lengths

When referring to geometric segments themselves, congruence notation is used:

$$ \overline{AB}\cong\overline{DE}. $$

When referring to their numerical lengths, equality notation is used:

$$ AB=DE. $$

Thus:

$$ \overline{AB}\cong\overline{DE} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad AB=DE. $$

Congruent Angles and Equal Angle Measures

Likewise:

$$ \angle A\cong\angle D $$

means:

$$ m\angle A=m\angle D. $$

Example in a Proof

Suppose a proof establishes:

$$ \triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF. $$

If the goal is to prove:

$$ \angle C\cong\angle F, $$

then no additional triangle calculation is required. Since $C$ corresponds to $F$, the conclusion follows directly by CPCTC.

Rigid-Motion Interpretation

In transformation geometry, congruent triangles can be mapped onto one another through rigid motions such as translations, rotations, reflections, or compositions of these transformations.

Rigid motions preserve distance and angle measure:

$$ \boxed{ \text{rigid motion} \Longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{length preserved},\\ \text{angle measure preserved} \end{cases} }. $$

CPCTC can therefore be understood as a consequence of the fact that every corresponding part retains its measurement under the rigid motion carrying one triangle onto the other.

Using CPCTC to Solve for Unknowns

Suppose:

$$ \triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF $$

and corresponding sides have lengths:

$$ AB=3x+2,\qquad DE=17. $$

CPCTC gives:

$$ 3x+2=17. $$

Solving:

$$ 3x=15, $$ $$ \boxed{x=5}. $$

Reverse Reasoning

If two parts that would have to correspond are not congruent, then the triangles cannot be congruent under that proposed correspondence.

For example, if:

$$ AB=5 \qquad\text{and}\qquad DE=8, $$

then a proposed correspondence:

$$ AB\leftrightarrow DE $$

is incompatible with:

$$ \triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF. $$

CPCTC and Similarity

CPCTC applies to congruent triangles, not merely similar triangles.

If:

$$ \triangle ABC\sim\triangle DEF, $$

corresponding angles are congruent, but corresponding side lengths need only be proportional:

$$ \frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{BC}{EF} = \frac{AC}{DF}. $$

Only when the similarity scale factor is $1$ are the triangles congruent.

Common Misconception: CPCTC cannot normally be used to prove the triangles congruent in the first place. It is a consequence of established congruence, not a substitute for a valid congruence argument.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
CPCTC "Corresponding parts of congruent triangles are congruent."
$\triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF$ "Triangle A B C is congruent to triangle D E F."
$A\leftrightarrow D$ "Vertex A corresponds to vertex D."
$\overline{AB}\cong\overline{DE}$ "Segment A B is congruent to segment D E."

$\angle B\cong\angle E$ "Angle B is congruent to angle E."
$m\angle B=m\angle E$ "The measure of angle B equals the measure of angle E."

CPCTC frequently appears in two-column proofs as a reason:

Statement Reason
$\triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF$ SAS
$\overline{AC}\cong\overline{DF}$ CPCTC

The acronym may be spoken letter by letter:

$$ \text{"C-P-C-T-C"} $$

or expanded fully as:

$$ \text{"Corresponding parts of congruent triangles are congruent."} $$

Defense Notation Advice: Always verify the correspondence encoded by the order of the triangle names before applying CPCTC. From:

$$ \triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF, $$

the valid correspondence is:

$$ A\leftrightarrow D,\qquad B\leftrightarrow E,\qquad C\leftrightarrow F. $$

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Expanded Form: $$ \boxed{ \text{CPCTC} = \text{Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent} }. $$
  • Core Implication: $$ \boxed{ \triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF \Longrightarrow \text{all corresponding parts are congruent} }. $$
  • Vertex Correspondence: From: $$ \triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF, $$ one obtains: $$ A\leftrightarrow D,\quad B\leftrightarrow E,\quad C\leftrightarrow F. $$
  • First Pair of Corresponding Sides: $$ \boxed{ \overline{AB}\cong\overline{DE} }. $$
  • Second Pair of Corresponding Sides: $$ \boxed{ \overline{BC}\cong\overline{EF} }. $$
  • Third Pair of Corresponding Sides: $$ \boxed{ \overline{AC}\cong\overline{DF} }. $$
  • Corresponding Angles: $$ \boxed{ \angle A\cong\angle D,\qquad \angle B\cong\angle E,\qquad \angle C\cong\angle F }. $$
  • SSS: If three pairs of corresponding sides are congruent, triangle congruence may be established by Side-Side-Side. CPCTC may then justify additional angle relationships.
  • SAS: Side-Angle-Side can establish triangle congruence when two corresponding sides and their included angles are congruent.
  • ASA: Angle-Side-Angle can establish triangle congruence from two corresponding angles and their included side.
  • AAS: Angle-Angle-Side can establish triangle congruence from two corresponding angles and a nonincluded corresponding side.
  • HL: For right triangles, Hypotenuse-Leg can establish congruence when the corresponding hypotenuses and one corresponding pair of legs are congruent.
  • Proof Sequence: A common proof structure is: $$ \boxed{ \text{given information} \rightarrow \text{congruence criterion} \rightarrow \triangle_1\cong\triangle_2 \rightarrow \text{CPCTC} }. $$
  • Two-Column Proofs: CPCTC commonly appears in the reason column after triangle congruence has already been established.
  • Flow Proofs: A flow proof may show: $$ \triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF \xrightarrow{\text{CPCTC}} \angle C\cong\angle F. $$
  • Solving Segment Equations: If corresponding sides satisfy: $$ AB=2x+7,\qquad DE=19, $$ CPCTC gives: $$ 2x+7=19, $$ so: $$ x=6. $$
  • Solving Angle Equations: If: $$ m\angle A=4x+10 $$ and: $$ m\angle D=70^\circ, $$ CPCTC gives: $$ 4x+10=70, $$ so: $$ x=15. $$
  • Isosceles Constructions: Triangle congruence and CPCTC are frequently used to prove equal sides or equal angles in geometric constructions involving bisectors, medians, altitudes, and symmetry.
  • Angle Bisectors: Dividing a figure into two congruent triangles can allow CPCTC to prove that newly created angles are congruent.
  • Segment Bisectors: Congruent triangles can be used with CPCTC to establish equality of corresponding segment lengths.
  • Perpendicular Bisectors: CPCTC often appears in proofs involving points equidistant from endpoints of a segment.
  • Parallelogram Proofs: Drawing a diagonal through a parallelogram creates two triangles that can be proved congruent. CPCTC can then establish relationships among opposite sides and angles.
  • Kites: Triangle congruence followed by CPCTC can establish angle or diagonal properties of kites.
  • Rigid Motions: If one triangle maps exactly onto another through a rigid motion, the triangles are congruent and corresponding sides and angles retain their measures.
  • Translations: Translating a triangle preserves all corresponding side lengths and angle measures.
  • Rotations: Rotating a triangle preserves all corresponding lengths and angles.
  • Reflections: Reflecting a triangle reverses orientation but preserves distances and angle measures, producing a congruent triangle.
  • Coordinate Geometry: Distance, slope, and transformation methods may establish triangle congruence. CPCTC can then transfer that result to particular corresponding components.
  • Similarity Contrast: If: $$ \triangle ABC\sim\triangle DEF, $$ corresponding angles are congruent, but side lengths are generally only proportional. CPCTC requires congruence, not similarity alone.
  • Scale Factor: Congruent triangles are similar triangles with scale factor: $$ \boxed{1}. $$
  • Correspondence Error: From: $$ \triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF, $$ it is incorrect to infer: $$ \overline{AB}\cong\overline{EF} $$ merely by CPCTC, because $\overline{AB}$ corresponds to $\overline{DE}$, not $\overline{EF}$.
  • Circular Reasoning: One should not assume all corresponding parts are congruent by CPCTC and then use those same assumptions to establish the triangle congruence on which CPCTC depends.
  • CPCFC Relationship: CPCTC is the triangle-specific version of the broader idea: $$ \boxed{ \text{CPCFC} = \text{Corresponding Parts of Congruent Figures are Congruent} }. $$
  • Terminological Contrast: Triangle congruence criteria establish that two triangles are congruent. CPCTC uses that established congruence to infer facts about matching sides and angles.
  • Core Interpretation: CPCTC transfers congruence of the complete triangles to each matched part: $$ \boxed{ \text{triangles congruent} \Longrightarrow \text{corresponding sides and angles congruent} }. $$

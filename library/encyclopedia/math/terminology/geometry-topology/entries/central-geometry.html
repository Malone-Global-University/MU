Central Angle

Subfield: Geometry, Spatial Analysis & Topology

Etymology & Roots

A central angle is an angle whose vertex is located at the center of a circle and whose two sides are radii of that circle.

If $O$ is the center of a circle and $A$ and $B$ lie on the circle, then:

$$ \boxed{ \angle AOB } $$

is a central angle.

$$ \boxed{ OA=OB=r } $$

because both sides of the angle are radii.

The adjective central refers specifically to the location of the angle's vertex at the center of the circle.

$$ \boxed{ \text{central angle} = \text{angle with vertex at circle center} }. $$

Operational Nuance

Defining Structure

For a circle centered at $O$, a central angle:

$$ \angle AOB $$

requires:

  • $A$ and $B$ to lie on the circle,
  • $O$ to be the center,
  • $OA$ and $OB$ to be radii.

Intercepted Arc

A central angle determines an arc of the circle between its endpoints.

The arc from $A$ to $B$ is called the intercepted arc of $\angle AOB$.

$$ \boxed{ \angle AOB \longleftrightarrow \widehat{AB} }. $$

Central Angle and Arc Measure

When angle measure and arc measure are both expressed in degrees:

$$ \boxed{ m\angle AOB = m\widehat{AB} }. $$

Thus a $60^\circ$ central angle intercepts a $60^\circ$ arc.

Degree Measure

One complete revolution around the center is:

$$ \boxed{ 360^\circ }. $$

Therefore a central angle of measure $\theta^\circ$ occupies the fraction:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\theta}{360} } $$

of the full circle.

Radian Measure

In radians, a full revolution is:

$$ \boxed{ 2\pi }. $$

A central angle of $\theta$ radians occupies the fraction:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\theta}{2\pi} } $$

of a full circle.

Definition of a Radian

One radian is the central angle that intercepts an arc whose length equals the circle's radius.

$$ \boxed{ s=r \Rightarrow \theta=1\text{ radian} }. $$

Arc Length Formula

If $\theta$ is measured in radians, the arc length intercepted by a central angle is:

$$ \boxed{ s=r\theta }. $$

Here:

  • $s$ is arc length,
  • $r$ is radius,
  • $\theta$ is central angle measure in radians.

Why Radians Are Natural

Radian measure is built directly from the ratio:

$$ \boxed{ \theta=\frac{s}{r} }. $$

This makes radian measure especially natural in calculus and rotational mathematics.

Arc Length in Degrees

If the central angle is $\theta^\circ$, then:

$$ \boxed{ s= \frac{\theta}{360} (2\pi r) }. $$

This is equivalent to $s=r\theta$ after converting degrees to radians.

Sector

Two radii and the intercepted arc enclose a region called a sector.

$$ \boxed{ \text{sector} = \text{region bounded by two radii and an arc} }. $$

Sector Area in Degrees

For a central angle of $\theta^\circ$:

$$ \boxed{ A_{\text{sector}} = \frac{\theta}{360} \pi r^2 }. $$

Sector Area in Radians

If $\theta$ is measured in radians:

$$ \boxed{ A_{\text{sector}} = \frac12r^2\theta }. $$

Fraction-of-a-Circle Interpretation

Central angles connect angular measure directly with circumference and area:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\theta}{360^\circ} = \frac{s}{2\pi r} = \frac{A_{\text{sector}}}{\pi r^2} } $$

when $\theta$ is expressed in degrees.

Semicircle

A central angle of:

$$ \boxed{ 180^\circ=\pi } $$

radians intercepts a semicircle.

Quarter Circle

A central angle of:

$$ \boxed{ 90^\circ=\frac{\pi}{2} } $$

radians intercepts one quarter of a circle.

Full Circle

A complete turn about the center has angular measure:

$$ \boxed{ 360^\circ=2\pi } $$

radians.

Minor Central Angle

A central angle less than $180^\circ$ intercepts a minor arc.

$$ \boxed{ 0^\circ<\theta<180^\circ } $$

Straight Central Angle

A central angle of $180^\circ$ has opposite radii as its sides and intercepts a semicircle.

Reflex Central Angle

If the directed or reflex angle between two radii is greater than $180^\circ$ but less than $360^\circ$, it intercepts the corresponding major arc.

$$ \boxed{ 180^\circ<\theta<360^\circ }. $$

Minor and Major Arcs

Two distinct points $A$ and $B$ on a circle generally determine:

  • a minor arc,
  • a major arc.

Their degree measures add to:

$$ \boxed{ 360^\circ }. $$

Complementary Central Arcs

If the minor central angle has measure $\theta$, then the reflex angle subtending the major arc has measure:

$$ \boxed{ 360^\circ-\theta }. $$

Central Angle versus Inscribed Angle

A central angle has its vertex at the center of the circle.

An inscribed angle has its vertex on the circle.

$$ \boxed{ \text{central angle} \rightarrow \text{vertex at center} } $$
$$ \boxed{ \text{inscribed angle} \rightarrow \text{vertex on circle} }. $$

Central Angle–Inscribed Angle Theorem

If a central angle and an inscribed angle intercept the same arc, then:

$$ \boxed{ m\angle_{\text{central}} = 2m\angle_{\text{inscribed}} }. $$

Equivalently:

$$ \boxed{ m\angle_{\text{inscribed}} = \frac12 m\widehat{AB} }. $$

Example

If a central angle intercepting arc $\widehat{AB}$ measures $100^\circ$, then any inscribed angle intercepting that same arc from the appropriate side has measure:

$$ \boxed{ 50^\circ }. $$

Central Angle and Chord Length

If a chord subtends a central angle $\theta$ in a circle of radius $r$, then:

$$ \boxed{ c= 2r\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) }. $$

The formula follows by splitting the isosceles triangle formed by the two radii and the chord into two right triangles.

Isosceles Triangle Formed by Two Radii

Since:

$$ OA=OB, $$

triangle $AOB$ is isosceles.

If:

$$ m\angle AOB=\theta, $$

then each base angle equals:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{180^\circ-\theta}{2} }. $$

Equal Central Angles Intercept Equal Arcs

In the same circle or in congruent circles:

$$ \boxed{ \text{equal central angles} \Rightarrow \text{equal arc measures} }. $$

Equal Central Angles Intercept Equal Chords

In the same circle or congruent circles:

$$ \boxed{ \text{equal central angles} \Rightarrow \text{equal chord lengths} }. $$

Converse Relationship

Equal chords in the same circle subtend equal central angles:

$$ \boxed{ AB=CD \Rightarrow m\angle AOB=m\angle COD }. $$

Regular Polygons

A regular $n$-gon inscribed in a circle divides the circle into $n$ equal central angles.

$$ \boxed{ \theta_{\text{central}} = \frac{360^\circ}{n} }. $$

In radians:

$$ \boxed{ \theta_{\text{central}} = \frac{2\pi}{n} }. $$

Example: Regular Hexagon

For $n=6$:

$$ \theta = \frac{360^\circ}{6} = \boxed{60^\circ}. $$

Example: Regular Pentagon

$$ \theta = \frac{360^\circ}{5} = \boxed{72^\circ}. $$

Rotation Interpretation

A central angle can also be viewed as the amount of rotation needed to turn one radius onto another about the center.

$$ \boxed{ OA \xrightarrow{\theta} OB }. $$

This connects circle geometry with rotational transformations and the unit circle.

Unit Circle

On the unit circle:

$$ r=1. $$

Therefore the arc-length formula becomes:

$$ \boxed{ s=\theta } $$

when $\theta$ is measured in radians.

This is one reason radian measure is fundamental in trigonometry.

Trigonometric Coordinates

Rotating a unit radius through central angle $\theta$ from the positive $x$-axis reaches:

$$ \boxed{ (\cos\theta,\sin\theta) }. $$

Thus central-angle measure is directly connected to the coordinate definitions of sine and cosine.

Central Angle and Angular Velocity

If an object rotates through angle $\theta$ over time $t$, average angular velocity is:

$$ \boxed{ \omega_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\Delta\theta}{\Delta t} }. $$

Central-angle measure therefore extends naturally into rotational motion.

Linear and Angular Motion

Since:

$$ s=r\theta, $$

differentiating with respect to time gives:

$$ \boxed{ v=r\omega } $$

when $r$ is constant and $\omega=d\theta/dt$.

Central Angle versus Sector

The central angle is the angular object. The sector is the two-dimensional region determined by that angle and the corresponding arc.

Central Angle versus Arc

The central angle is measured as an angle, while the arc can be described either by angular measure or physical arc length.

$$ \boxed{ \text{arc measure} \ne \text{arc length} }. $$

Arc Measure versus Arc Length

Two circles can contain arcs with the same angular measure but different lengths if their radii differ.

For the same central angle $\theta$:

$$ \boxed{ s=r\theta }. $$

Larger radius means longer arc.

Common Misconception: Every Angle inside a Circle Is Central

The vertex must be exactly at the circle's center.

Common Misconception: Central Angle Equals Arc Length

In degrees, central-angle measure equals arc measure in degrees, not physical arc length.

In radians:

$$ \theta=\frac{s}{r}. $$

Only when $r=1$ does the numerical arc length equal the radian measure.

Common Misconception: The Inscribed Angle Has the Same Measure

An inscribed angle intercepting the same arc has half the central angle's measure.

Common Misconception: Arc AB Is Always the Minor Arc

Two endpoints generally determine both a minor and a major arc. Context or notation must indicate which arc is intended when ambiguity matters.

Common Misconception: Degree Formulas Can Use Radians without Conversion

Formulas such as:

$$ \frac{\theta}{360}\pi r^2 $$

assume $\theta$ is measured in degrees. The radian form is instead:

$$ \boxed{ \frac12r^2\theta }. $$

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\angle AOB$ "Angle A O B," with O understood as the circle's center.
$m\angle AOB=60^\circ$ "The measure of central angle A O B is sixty degrees."
$m\widehat{AB}=60^\circ$ "Arc A B has measure sixty degrees."
$s=r\theta$ "Arc length equals radius times the central angle in radians."
$A=\frac12r^2\theta$ "Sector area equals one-half r squared theta, with theta in radians."
$\theta=\frac{2\pi}{n}$ "The central angle of a regular n-gon is two pi over n radians."

In geometric language, a central angle is often named by three letters with the center as the middle letter, such as $\angle AOB$. This helps distinguish it from an inscribed angle such as $\angle ACB$.

Defense Notation Advice: Keep separate the three related quantities: central-angle measure, arc measure, and arc length. Central-angle measure equals arc measure when both are expressed angularly, while physical arc length requires the radius through $s=r\theta$ with $\theta$ in radians.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Definition: A central angle has its vertex at the center of a circle.
  • Sides: The sides of a central angle are radii.
  • Intercepted Arc: A central angle determines the arc joining the endpoints of its radii.
  • Degree Relationship: $$ \boxed{ m\angle AOB = m\widehat{AB} }. $$
  • Full Turn: $$ \boxed{ 360^\circ=2\pi\text{ radians} }. $$
  • Radian Definition: $$ \boxed{ \theta=\frac{s}{r} }. $$
  • Arc Length: $$ \boxed{ s=r\theta } $$ for $\theta$ in radians.
  • Sector Area in Radians: $$ \boxed{ A=\frac12r^2\theta }. $$
  • Sector Area in Degrees: $$ \boxed{ A= \frac{\theta}{360} \pi r^2 }. $$
  • Semicircle: $180^\circ=\pi$ radians.
  • Quarter Circle: $90^\circ=\pi/2$ radians.
  • Inscribed-Angle Relation: A central angle is twice an inscribed angle intercepting the same arc.
  • Chord Formula: $$ \boxed{ c= 2r\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) }. $$
  • Equal Central Angles: Intercept equal arcs and equal chords in the same or congruent circles.
  • Regular n-Gon: $$ \boxed{ \theta= \frac{360^\circ}{n} = \frac{2\pi}{n} }. $$
  • Unit Circle: With $r=1$, numerical arc length equals central-angle measure in radians.
  • Trigonometry: A central angle $\theta$ on the unit circle determines the point $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$.
  • Rotation: Central-angle measure can represent the rotation from one radius to another.
  • Angular Motion: Central angles form the basis of angular displacement and angular velocity.
  • Common Error: Do not call an angle central unless its vertex is at the circle center.
  • Common Error: Do not confuse arc measure with arc length.
  • Common Error: Do not give an inscribed angle the same measure as the central angle intercepting the same arc.
  • Common Error: Do not use degree-based sector or arc formulas with a radian input without conversion.
  • Terminological Contrast: Central angle has its vertex at the center of a circle. Inscribed angle has its vertex on the circle. Intercepted arc is the portion of the circle determined by the angle. Arc measure is angular and matches the corresponding central-angle measure. Arc length is linear and depends on both radius and angle. Sector is the region bounded by two radii and their intercepted arc.

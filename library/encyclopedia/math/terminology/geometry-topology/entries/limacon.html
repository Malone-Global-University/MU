Limaçon

Subfield: Geometry, Spatial Analysis & Topology

Etymology & Roots

A limaçon is a family of plane curves commonly represented in polar coordinates by equations of the form:

$$ \boxed{ r=a+b\cos\theta } $$

or:

$$ \boxed{ r=a+b\sin\theta }. $$

The term limaçon comes through French and refers to a snail-like form. Its linguistic root is associated with Latin limax, meaning "snail."

The curve is also known as the limaçon of Pascal. The Pascal in this name is Étienne Pascal, the mathematician and father of Blaise Pascal—not Blaise Pascal himself.

Étienne Pascal studied the curve during the seventeenth century, and Gilles-Personne de Roberval gave it the name limaçon. However, an earlier construction of the same family appears in Albrecht Dürer's 1525 work on geometric construction, so the curve's history predates the Pascal name.

In modern mathematics, limaçon refers not to a single fixed shape but to a family whose appearance changes dramatically according to the parameters $a$ and $b$.

Operational Nuance

Consider the standard cosine form:

$$ r=a+b\cos\theta. $$

Here:

  • $r$ is the directed radial coordinate;
  • $\theta$ is the polar angle;
  • $a$ controls the baseline radial displacement;
  • $b$ controls the strength of the angular variation.

Because:

$$ -1\le\cos\theta\le1, $$

the radial coordinate ranges between:

$$ a-b $$

and:

$$ a+b. $$

This simple observation explains much of the curve's geometry.

Cosine vs. Sine Forms

The cosine form:

$$ r=a+b\cos\theta $$

is symmetric about the polar axis, corresponding to the Cartesian $x$-axis.

The sine form:

$$ r=a+b\sin\theta $$

is symmetric about the vertical line:

$$ \theta=\frac{\pi}{2}. $$

The two equations therefore generate essentially the same family rotated relative to the coordinate axes.

Common Misconception: A limaçon does not always contain an inner loop. Whether a loop appears depends on the relationship between $a$ and $b$.

The French spelling contains a cedilla:

$$ \text{limaçon}. $$

English mathematical sources may occasionally omit the diacritic and write:

$$ \text{limacon}. $$

Both spellings refer to the same family of curves, though limaçon preserves the traditional spelling.

Defense Notation Advice: The letters used for the two parameters are not standardized. One source may write:

$$ r=a+b\cos\theta, $$

while another may reverse the roles:

$$ r=b+a\cos\theta. $$

Shape classifications must therefore be interpreted according to the actual equation, not memorized solely from the parameter letters.

Chord

Subfield: Geometry, Spatial Analysis & Topology

Etymology & Roots

A chord is a line segment whose endpoints lie on a curve. In elementary geometry, the term most often refers to a segment whose endpoints lie on a circle.

If $A$ and $B$ are points on a circle, then:

$$ \boxed{ \overline{AB} \text{ is a chord} }. $$

The interior points of the segment ordinarily lie inside the circle, while the endpoints lie on the circumference.

$$ \boxed{ A,B\in\text{circle} \Rightarrow \overline{AB} \text{ is a chord} }. $$

The term extends beyond circles to other curves, but circle geometry provides the standard setting in which chord theorems are introduced.

Operational Nuance

Chord of a Circle

Let a circle have center $O$, and let $A$ and $B$ lie on the circle. Then $\overline{AB}$ is a chord.

Diameter as a Special Chord

If the chord passes through the center of the circle, it is a diameter.

$$ \boxed{ O\in\overline{AB} \Rightarrow \overline{AB} \text{ is a diameter} }. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \text{every diameter is a chord} } $$

but:

$$ \boxed{ \text{not every chord is a diameter} }. $$

Longest Chord

The diameter is the longest possible chord of a circle.

$$ \boxed{ c_{\max}=2r }. $$

Any chord not passing through the center has length less than $2r$.

Chord and Central Angle

A chord $\overline{AB}$ determines a central angle:

$$ \angle AOB. $$

The size of the chord is controlled by the size of the corresponding central angle.

Chord Length Formula

If the circle has radius $r$ and chord $\overline{AB}$ subtends a central angle $\theta$, then:

$$ \boxed{ AB= 2r\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) }. $$

This follows by splitting isosceles triangle $AOB$ into two congruent right triangles.

Derivation of the Chord Formula

Let $M$ be the midpoint of $\overline{AB}$. Then $OM$ is perpendicular to the chord, and:

$$ AM=\frac{AB}{2}. $$

Also:

$$ \angle AOM=\frac{\theta}{2}. $$

Therefore:

$$ \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) = \frac{AB/2}{r}. $$

Solving for $AB$ gives:

$$ \boxed{ AB= 2r\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) }. $$

Chord Length from Distance to the Center

Suppose a chord lies a perpendicular distance $d$ from the center of a circle of radius $r$.

Half the chord, the distance $d$, and the radius form a right triangle:

$$ \left(\frac{c}{2}\right)^2+d^2=r^2. $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ c= 2\sqrt{r^2-d^2} }. $$

Distance from Center from Chord Length

Solving the same relationship for $d$ gives:

$$ \boxed{ d= \sqrt{ r^2-\left(\frac{c}{2}\right)^2 } }. $$

Perpendicular from Center to Chord

A perpendicular drawn from the center of a circle to a chord bisects that chord.

$$ \boxed{ OM\perp AB \Rightarrow AM=MB }. $$

Converse

A line from the center to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to the chord.

$$ \boxed{ AM=MB \Rightarrow OM\perp AB }. $$

Perpendicular Bisector Property

The perpendicular bisector of any non-diameter chord passes through the center of the circle.

$$ \boxed{ O \in \operatorname{PB}(\overline{AB}) }. $$

Finding the Center of a Circle

Because the perpendicular bisector of every chord passes through the center, the center of an unknown circle can be found by constructing perpendicular bisectors of two nonparallel chords.

$$ \boxed{ O= \operatorname{PB}(AB) \cap \operatorname{PB}(CD) }. $$

Equal Chords

In the same circle, or in congruent circles, equal chords subtend equal central angles.

$$ \boxed{ AB=CD \Rightarrow \angle AOB=\angle COD }. $$

Equal Central Angles

The converse is also true:

$$ \boxed{ \angle AOB=\angle COD \Rightarrow AB=CD }. $$

Equal Chords and Equal Arcs

In the same circle or congruent circles:

$$ \boxed{ \text{equal chords} \iff \text{equal intercepted arcs} }. $$

Equal Chords and Distance from Center

Equal chords in the same circle are equidistant from the center.

$$ \boxed{ AB=CD \Rightarrow d(O,AB)=d(O,CD) }. $$

Conversely, chords equidistant from the center have equal length.

Chord Size and Center Distance

Within one circle, the closer a chord lies to the center, the longer it is.

$$ \boxed{ d_1c_2 }. $$

The diameter is the limiting case $d=0$.

Chord and Arc

A chord connects the endpoints of an arc but is not itself part of the circumference.

$$ \boxed{ \text{chord} \ne \text{arc} }. $$

A chord is straight; an arc follows the curve of the circle.

Chord versus Radius

A radius has one endpoint at the center and one on the circle.

A chord has both endpoints on the circle.

$$ \boxed{ \text{radius}: O\to A } $$
$$ \boxed{ \text{chord}: A\to B }. $$

Chord versus Secant

A secant line intersects a circle at two points and continues beyond them.

The portion between those intersection points is a chord.

$$ \boxed{ \text{secant line} \supset \text{chord segment} }. $$

Chord versus Tangent

A tangent meets a circle at one point in the standard Euclidean setting, whereas a chord has two endpoints on the circle.

Intersecting Chords Theorem

Suppose chords $AB$ and $CD$ intersect at an interior point $P$.

Then:

$$ \boxed{ PA\cdot PB = PC\cdot PD }. $$

Power-of-a-Point Connection

The intersecting-chords relation is one form of the broader power of a point principle.

Example of Intersecting Chords

If:

$$ PA=3,\qquad PB=8,\qquad PC=4, $$

then:

$$ 3(8)=4(PD). $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ PD=6 }. $$

Angle Formed by Intersecting Chords

If two chords intersect inside a circle, the measure of an angle formed is half the sum of the measures of the arcs intercepted by that angle and its vertical angle.

$$ \boxed{ m\angle = \frac12 \left( m\widehat{AB} + m\widehat{CD} \right) }. $$

Chord and Inscribed Angle

Two chords meeting at a point on the circle form an inscribed angle.

If $\angle ACB$ intercepts arc $\widehat{AB}$, then:

$$ \boxed{ m\angle ACB = \frac12 m\widehat{AB} }. $$

Thales’ Theorem

If the chord $\overline{AB}$ is a diameter and $C$ is another point on the circle, then:

$$ \boxed{ \angle ACB=90^\circ }. $$

Thus a diameter, as a special chord, subtends a right angle from every point on the corresponding semicircle.

Chord of a General Curve

The term chord is not limited to circles. If $A$ and $B$ are two points on a curve, the segment $\overline{AB}$ may be called a chord of that curve.

Chord of a Function Graph

For a graph $y=f(x)$, choosing:

$$ P=(a,f(a)), \qquad Q=(b,f(b)) $$

produces a chord joining two points on the graph.

Secant Slope

The slope of that chord is:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a} }. $$

In calculus this is the average rate of change of $f$ over $[a,b]$.

Chord-to-Tangent Limit

As the two points on a smooth curve approach one another, the secant line through the chord approaches the tangent line, under appropriate differentiability conditions.

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{b\to a} \frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a} = f'(a) }. $$

Chord of an Ellipse

A segment joining two points on an ellipse is also called a chord of the ellipse. Diameter terminology for conics is more specialized, but the basic chord idea remains the same.

Degenerate Chord

In some advanced contexts, limiting or degenerate cases may allow the two endpoints to approach one another, connecting chord geometry to tangent geometry. In ordinary elementary geometry, however, a chord is understood to have two distinct endpoints.

Common Misconception: A Chord Must Pass through the Center

Only a diameter must pass through the center.

Common Misconception: Every Secant Is a Chord

A secant is generally a full line. The chord is only the segment between the two points of intersection with the curve.

Common Misconception: Chord and Arc Are the Same

The chord is straight, whereas the arc follows the circumference.

Common Misconception: Equal Arcs Can Have Different Chords in the Same Circle

In the same circle, equal arcs correspond to equal chords.

Common Misconception: Larger Central Angle Always Means Larger Chord without Qualification

For minor central angles from $0$ to $\pi$, chord length increases with the angle. For general directed angles, one must account for the fact that angles $\theta$ and $2\pi-\theta$ determine the same chord endpoints.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\overline{AB}$ "Chord A B," when A and B lie on the circle or curve.
$AB=2r\sin(\theta/2)$ "The chord length equals two r sine theta over two."
$c=2\sqrt{r^2-d^2}$ "The chord length is twice the square root of r squared minus d squared."
$OM\perp AB$ "The perpendicular from the center to chord A B bisects the chord."
$PA\cdot PB=PC\cdot PD$ "The products of the two intersecting chord segments are equal."

In circle geometry, the context usually makes it clear that $\overline{AB}$ is being regarded as a chord rather than merely as an arbitrary segment. In calculus, chord may refer to the segment joining two points of a graph, while the corresponding full line is usually called a secant line.

Defense Notation Advice: Distinguish carefully among chord, diameter, radius, arc, secant, and tangent. When using a chord-length formula, specify whether the given quantity is a central angle or the perpendicular distance from the center.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Definition: A chord is a segment joining two points on a curve.
  • Circle Geometry: A chord has both endpoints on the circle.
  • Diameter: A chord passing through the center.
  • Longest Chord: $$ \boxed{ 2r }. $$
  • Central-Angle Formula: $$ \boxed{ c= 2r\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) }. $$
  • Center-Distance Formula: $$ \boxed{ c= 2\sqrt{r^2-d^2} }. $$
  • Perpendicular-Bisector Property: A perpendicular from the center to a chord bisects the chord.
  • Center Construction: Perpendicular bisectors of two nonparallel chords intersect at the circle's center.
  • Equal Chords: Equal chords subtend equal central angles and equal arcs in the same or congruent circles.
  • Distance from Center: Equal chords are equidistant from the center.
  • Intersecting Chords: $$ \boxed{ PA\cdot PB = PC\cdot PD }. $$
  • Inscribed Angles: Two chords meeting on the circle form an inscribed angle.
  • Diameter Chord: A diameter subtends a right inscribed angle.
  • Secant Contrast: A secant is a line through two points of the circle; its internal segment is a chord.
  • Arc Contrast: A chord is straight, while an arc follows the circle.
  • General Curve: A chord may join any two points on a curve.
  • Calculus: The chord between $(a,f(a))$ and $(b,f(b))$ has secant slope $$ \boxed{ \frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a} }. $$
  • Tangent Connection: A secant through a shrinking chord approaches a tangent under appropriate smoothness conditions.
  • Common Error: Do not assume every chord is a diameter.
  • Common Error: Do not confuse a chord with a secant line.
  • Common Error: Do not confuse chord length with arc length.
  • Common Error: Do not assume a chord must pass through the center.
  • Terminological Contrast: Chord is a segment joining two points on a curve. Diameter is a chord through the center of a circle. Radius joins the center to a point on the circle. Arc is the curved portion of the circumference between two points. Secant is a line intersecting a curve at two points. Tangent meets a circle at one point in the standard Euclidean setting. Intercepted arc is the arc associated with a chord or angle in circle geometry.

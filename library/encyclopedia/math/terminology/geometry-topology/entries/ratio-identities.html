Ratio Identities

Subfield: Geometry & Topology

Etymology & Roots

Ratio identities are trigonometric identities that express one trigonometric function as a quotient or reciprocal relationship involving other trigonometric functions. The most fundamental quotient identities are:

$$ \boxed{ \tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} } $$
$$ \boxed{ \cot\theta = \frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} }. $$

Closely associated reciprocal identities are:

$$ \boxed{ \sec\theta = \frac{1}{\cos\theta}, \qquad \csc\theta = \frac{1}{\sin\theta} } $$
$$ \boxed{ \cot\theta = \frac{1}{\tan\theta}, \qquad \tan\theta = \frac{1}{\cot\theta} } $$

The word ratio emphasizes that trigonometric functions arise from comparisons of lengths or coordinates. The word identity means that the equality holds for every angle for which both sides are defined.

Many textbooks use the more specific term quotient identities for the tangent and cotangent formulas. Ratio identities is a broader pedagogical label that may include quotient and reciprocal relationships among the six standard trigonometric functions.

Operational Nuance

Primary Quotient Identities

$$ \boxed{ \tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} } $$
$$ \boxed{ \cot\theta = \frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} }. $$

Why Tangent Is a Ratio

On the unit circle, a point corresponding to angle $\theta$ has coordinates:

$$ \boxed{ (\cos\theta,\sin\theta) }. $$

Therefore, whenever $\cos\theta\neq0$:

$$ \tan\theta = \frac{y}{x} = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}. $$

Right-Triangle Interpretation

For an acute angle in a right triangle:

$$ \sin\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}, \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}. $$

Taking their quotient:

$$ \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} = \frac{ \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} }{ \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} } = \boxed{ \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} } = \tan\theta. $$

Reciprocal Identities

$$ \boxed{ \sec\theta = \frac{1}{\cos\theta} } $$
$$ \boxed{ \csc\theta = \frac{1}{\sin\theta} } $$
$$ \boxed{ \cot\theta = \frac{1}{\tan\theta} } $$

Equivalent Rearrangements

From:

$$ \tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}, $$

one may write, where the expressions involved are defined:

$$ \boxed{ \sin\theta = \tan\theta\cos\theta }. $$

Likewise:

$$ \boxed{ \cos\theta = \cot\theta\sin\theta }. $$

Domain Restriction for Tangent

Since tangent divides by cosine:

$$ \boxed{ \tan\theta \text{ is defined only when } \cos\theta\neq0 }. $$

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ \theta \neq \frac{\pi}{2}+k\pi, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$

Domain Restriction for Cotangent

Since cotangent divides by sine:

$$ \boxed{ \cot\theta \text{ is defined only when } \sin\theta\neq0 }. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \theta\neq k\pi, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$

Domain Restriction for Secant

$$ \boxed{ \sec\theta \text{ requires } \cos\theta\neq0 }. $$

Domain Restriction for Cosecant

$$ \boxed{ \csc\theta \text{ requires } \sin\theta\neq0 }. $$

Identity Means Common Domain

A ratio identity is understood on the set of angles where both sides are defined. For example:

$$ \tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} $$

is an identity on the domain where $\cos\theta\neq0$.

Conversion to Sine and Cosine

Ratio identities are especially useful for rewriting complicated trigonometric expressions entirely in terms of sine and cosine.

For example:

$$ \tan\theta\cos\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} \cos\theta. $$

On the original common domain:

$$ \boxed{ \tan\theta\cos\theta = \sin\theta }. $$

Simplification Example

$$ \frac{\tan\theta}{\sec\theta} = \frac{ \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} }{ \frac{1}{\cos\theta} } = \boxed{ \sin\theta } $$

wherever the original expression is defined.

Another Simplification

$$ \frac{\cot\theta}{\csc\theta} = \frac{ \frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} }{ \frac{1}{\sin\theta} } = \boxed{ \cos\theta }. $$

Preserving Original Restrictions

Algebraic cancellation may produce a simpler expression defined at more points than the original expression. Those newly available points are not automatically added to the original domain.

For example:

$$ \tan\theta\cos\theta = \sin\theta $$

is obtained from an expression containing $\tan\theta$, so the original expression still excludes:

$$ \boxed{ \cos\theta=0 }. $$

Deriving Pythagorean Identities

Ratio identities connect naturally with Pythagorean identities. Dividing:

$$ \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1 $$

by $\cos^2\theta$, where $\cos\theta\neq0$, gives:

$$ \frac{\sin^2\theta}{\cos^2\theta}+1 = \frac{1}{\cos^2\theta}. $$

Using ratio and reciprocal identities:

$$ \boxed{ \tan^2\theta+1 = \sec^2\theta }. $$

Cotangent Pythagorean Identity

Dividing the primary Pythagorean identity by $\sin^2\theta$, where $\sin\theta\neq0$, yields:

$$ \boxed{ 1+\cot^2\theta = \csc^2\theta }. $$

Identity Verification

When verifying a trigonometric identity, converting tangent, cotangent, secant, and cosecant into sine and cosine often reveals common factors or Pythagorean relationships.

Recommended Strategy

  1. Choose the more complicated side.
  2. Convert quotient and reciprocal functions to sine and cosine when useful.
  3. Factor or combine fractions.
  4. Apply Pythagorean identities if needed.
  5. Preserve domain restrictions throughout.

Example Verification

Verify:

$$ \frac{\sin\theta}{\tan\theta} = \cos\theta. $$

Substitute:

$$ \tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}. $$

Then:

$$ \frac{\sin\theta}{ \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} } = \boxed{ \cos\theta } $$

on the domain of the original left-hand side, which requires both $\tan\theta$ and the quotient by $\tan\theta$ to be defined and nonzero.

Unit Circle and Slope

Since:

$$ (\cos\theta,\sin\theta) $$

lies on the unit circle, the ratio:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} } $$

is the slope of the ray from the origin to that point whenever $\cos\theta\neq0$. This gives tangent a direct geometric interpretation.

Coordinate Generalization

For a point $(x,y)$ on a circle of radius $r$ centered at the origin:

$$ \cos\theta=\frac{x}{r}, \qquad \sin\theta=\frac{y}{r}. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \tan\theta = \frac{y}{x} } $$

whenever $x\neq0$.

Reciprocal Coordinate Forms

$$ \boxed{ \sec\theta = \frac{r}{x}, \qquad \csc\theta = \frac{r}{y}, \qquad \cot\theta = \frac{x}{y} } $$

where the corresponding denominators are nonzero.

Common Error: Reversing Tangent

The correct quotient is:

$$ \boxed{ \tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} }, $$

not $\cos\theta/\sin\theta$.

Common Error: Reversing Cotangent

$$ \boxed{ \cot\theta = \frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} }. $$

Common Error: Confusing Quotient and Reciprocal Identities

Tangent and cotangent are quotient relationships between sine and cosine, while secant and cosecant are reciprocals of cosine and sine.

Common Error: Treating $\sin/\cos$ as $\sin-\cos$

The fraction bar means division, not subtraction.

Common Error: Ignoring Undefined Angles

The identities do not make tangent or secant defined where cosine is zero, nor cotangent or cosecant defined where sine is zero.

Common Error: Restoring Excluded Points after Cancellation

Simplification preserves equality on the original domain; it does not erase denominator restrictions from the original expression.

Common Error: Assuming an Identity Is an Equation to Solve

An identity states equality throughout a common domain. A conditional trigonometric equation asks which particular angles satisfy an equality.

Common Error: Applying Triangle-Only Language to Every Angle

Right-triangle definitions directly cover acute angles. Unit-circle definitions extend the trigonometric functions and their ratio identities to broader angle domains.

Common Misconception: Ratio identities are not merely memorized fraction formulas. They arise naturally from right-triangle side ratios and unit circle coordinates. Their algebraic usefulness comes from converting among the six trigonometric functions, but every conversion must respect the denominator restrictions built into the original functions.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$ "Tangent theta equals sine theta divided by cosine theta."
$\cot\theta=\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}$ "Cotangent theta equals cosine theta divided by sine theta."
$\sec\theta=\frac{1}{\cos\theta}$ "Secant theta is the reciprocal of cosine theta."
$\csc\theta=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}$ "Cosecant theta is the reciprocal of sine theta."
$\cot\theta=\frac{1}{\tan\theta}$ "Cotangent theta is the reciprocal of tangent theta," where both sides are defined.
$\tan\theta=y/x$ "Tangent theta equals y over x," in coordinate form when x is nonzero.

The phrase:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"quotient identity"} } $$

most commonly refers specifically to:

$$ \boxed{ \tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}, \qquad \cot\theta = \frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} }. $$

The broader phrase:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"ratio identities"} } $$

may include these quotient identities together with closely related reciprocal identities, depending on textbook terminology.

Defense Notation Advice: Write full numerators and denominators, preserve the order $\sin/\cos$ for tangent and $\cos/\sin$ for cotangent, and state denominator restrictions whenever domain matters. When converting an expression to sine and cosine, remember that algebraic cancellation does not restore angles excluded from the original expression.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Quotient Identity: $\tan\theta=\sin\theta/\cos\theta$.
  • Second Quotient Identity: $\cot\theta=\cos\theta/\sin\theta$.
  • Secant Reciprocal: $\sec\theta=1/\cos\theta$.
  • Cosecant Reciprocal: $\csc\theta=1/\sin\theta$.
  • Tangent-Cotangent Reciprocity: $\tan\theta=1/\cot\theta$ and $\cot\theta=1/\tan\theta$ on their common domains.
  • Right-Triangle Interpretation: Ratios arise from opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse side lengths.
  • Unit-Circle Interpretation: $\tan\theta$ is the ratio of the $y$-coordinate to the $x$-coordinate.
  • Coordinate Form: $\tan\theta=y/x$ and $\cot\theta=x/y$ where defined.
  • Tangent Domain: Requires $\cos\theta\neq0$.
  • Cotangent Domain: Requires $\sin\theta\neq0$.
  • Secant Domain: Requires $\cos\theta\neq0$.
  • Cosecant Domain: Requires $\sin\theta\neq0$.
  • Identity Domain: Equality is understood where both sides are defined.
  • Simplification: Quotient and reciprocal identities can reduce complex trigonometric expressions.
  • Sine-Cosine Conversion: Often useful when verifying identities.
  • Pythagorean Connection: Ratio identities help derive $1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta$.
  • Cotangent Pythagorean Connection: Help derive $1+\cot^2\theta=\csc^2\theta$.
  • Domain Preservation: Cancellation does not remove original exclusions.
  • Graphical Meaning: Tangent corresponds to slope where the relevant coordinate quotient is defined.
  • Terminology Variation: Many textbooks call tangent and cotangent formulas quotient identities rather than ratio identities.
  • Broader Pedagogical Usage: Ratio identities may group quotient and reciprocal identities together.
  • Common Error: Reversing sine and cosine in the tangent identity.
  • Common Error: Reversing sine and cosine in the cotangent identity.
  • Common Error: Confusing quotient identities with reciprocal identities.
  • Common Error: Ignoring denominator-zero restrictions.
  • Common Error: Restoring excluded angles after algebraic cancellation.
  • Common Error: Treating an identity as a conditional equation to solve.
  • Common Error: Restricting the identities only to acute right-triangle angles.
  • Core Interpretation: Ratio identities express trigonometric functions through quotient relationships: $$ \boxed{ \tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}, \qquad \cot\theta = \frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} }. $$ Closely related reciprocal identities give: $$ \boxed{ \sec\theta = \frac{1}{\cos\theta}, \qquad \csc\theta = \frac{1}{\sin\theta} }. $$ These identities arise from geometric side and coordinate ratios and are central tools for trigonometric simplification, verification, conversion, and derivation, provided all original domain restrictions are preserved.

Arc

Subfield: Geometry, Spatial Analysis & Topology

Etymology & Roots

The mathematical term arc comes through Old French from the Latin arcus, meaning "bow," "arch," or "curve." The connection is visual: a portion of a circle resembles the curved shape of a drawn bow. The same root survives in ordinary words such as arch and in technical expressions describing curved geometric paths.

In elementary geometry, arc most commonly refers to a portion of a circle's circumference. In more advanced mathematics, however, the word can be generalized to describe certain continuous curves. The surrounding field therefore determines whether an arc means specifically a circular arc or a more general curve segment.

Operational Nuance

In circle geometry, an arc is a connected portion of the circumference of a circle between two points. If $A$ and $B$ lie on a circle, the notation $\widehat{AB}$ ordinarily refers to the shorter arc connecting them when no additional information indicates otherwise.

$$ \widehat{AB} $$

Two distinct points on a circle generally determine two arcs: a minor arc and a major arc. A minor arc measures less than $180^\circ$, while a major arc measures more than $180^\circ$. If the endpoints are opposite ends of a diameter, each resulting arc is a semicircle and measures exactly $180^\circ$.

$$ 0^\circ < m\widehat{AB} < 180^\circ \qquad \text{minor arc} $$ $$ 180^\circ < m\widehat{ACB} < 360^\circ \qquad \text{major arc} $$

The measure of an arc and the length of an arc are different quantities. The measure of a circular arc is an angular quantity and equals the measure of its corresponding central angle. If central angle $\angle AOB$ intercepts arc $\widehat{AB}$, then:

$$ m\widehat{AB} = m\angle AOB $$

Arc length, by contrast, is an actual distance measured along the circumference. If $\theta$ is expressed in radians and the circle has radius $r$, then the arc length $s$ is:

$$ s = r\theta $$

If the central angle is expressed in degrees, the same relationship can be written:

$$ s = \frac{\theta}{360^\circ}(2\pi r) $$

Common Misconception: Arc measure and arc length are not the same thing. Two circles can contain arcs with the same angular measure but different arc lengths because their radii are different. For example, a $60^\circ$ arc on a large circle is physically longer than a $60^\circ$ arc on a smaller circle even though the two arcs have identical angular measure.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\widehat{AB}$ "Arc A-B."
$m\widehat{AB} = 80^\circ$ "The measure of arc A-B is eighty degrees."
$\widehat{ACB}$ "Major arc A-C-B," when the three-letter name identifies the intended arc.
$s = r\theta$ "Arc length equals the radius times the central angle in radians."
$m\widehat{AB} = m\angle AOB$ "The measure of arc A-B equals the measure of central angle A-O-B."

A minor arc is commonly named using its two endpoints, such as $\widehat{AB}$. A major arc is usually named with three letters, such as $\widehat{ACB}$, so that the intermediate point identifies which of the two possible arcs is intended. Semicircles may also require three-letter notation when the diagram contains ambiguity.

Defense Notation Advice: Distinguish verbally between the measure of an arc and its length. Saying "arc A-B is sixty degrees" ordinarily refers to arc measure, while saying "arc A-B has length five centimeters" refers to distance along the circle. When using $s=r\theta$, explicitly state that $\theta$ is measured in radians.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Circle Geometry: An arc is a portion of a circle's circumference determined by endpoints. Minor arcs, major arcs, and semicircles are distinguished according to their angular measure and position on the circle.
  • Central Angles: The measure of a minor arc equals the measure of the central angle that intercepts it: $$ m\widehat{AB} = m\angle AOB. $$ This relationship provides one of the principal links between angular geometry and circular geometry.
  • Inscribed Angles: An inscribed angle has its vertex on the circle and intercepts an arc. Its measure equals one-half the measure of the intercepted arc: $$ m\angle ACB = \frac{1}{2}m\widehat{AB}. $$ Equivalently, the intercepted arc has twice the measure of the inscribed angle.
  • Arc Length: In trigonometry and calculus, the physical length of a circular arc is expressed naturally using radians: $$ s = r\theta. $$ This relationship is one of the principal reasons radian measure is preferred in higher mathematics.
  • Calculus & Parametric Curves: The idea of arc length extends beyond circles to general curves. If a smooth plane curve is represented parametrically by $x=x(t)$ and $y=y(t)$ for $a \le t \le b$, its length is: $$ L = \int_a^b \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2+ \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2}\,dt. $$ Here, arc length refers to distance measured along the curve rather than specifically along a circle.
  • Topology: In topology, an arc has a more general meaning. An arc is a space homeomorphic to the closed interval $[0,1]$. Informally, it behaves like a simple continuous curve with two distinct endpoints and no self-intersections. A circular arc is therefore one geometric example of the broader topological concept.
  • Terminological Contrast: An arc lies along a curved boundary, whereas a chord is the straight line segment joining two points on a circle. A sector is the region enclosed by two radii and their intercepted arc, while a segment of a circle is the region enclosed by a chord and its corresponding arc.

Secant Line

Subfield: Geometry, Spatial Analysis & Topology

Etymology & Roots

The term secant comes from the Latin secare, meaning "to cut." A secant line is therefore literally a "cutting line": rather than merely touching a curve, it passes through it at selected points. The same Latin root appears in English words such as section, dissect, and intersection.

The geometric meaning predates calculus. In classical circle geometry, a secant is a line that passes through a circle and intersects its circumference at two points. Calculus later adopted the same concept for general curves, where a secant line through two points on a graph became fundamental to the development of average rates of change and derivatives.

Operational Nuance

A secant line is a line determined by two distinct points on a curve. If a function $f$ has points:

$$ P=(x_1,f(x_1)) $$ $$ Q=(x_2,f(x_2)) $$

with $x_1\neq x_2$, then the secant line through $P$ and $Q$ has slope:

$$ m_{\text{sec}} = \frac{f(x_2)-f(x_1)} {x_2-x_1}. $$

This slope is the average rate of change of the function between $x_1$ and $x_2$. It compares the total change in the function's output with the total change in its input across an interval.

For example, if:

$$ f(x)=x^2 $$

and the chosen points correspond to $x=1$ and $x=3$, then:

$$ P=(1,1) $$ $$ Q=(3,9) $$

and the secant slope is:

$$ m_{\text{sec}} = \frac{9-1}{3-1} = \frac{8}{2} = 4. $$

The equation of the secant line can then be found using point-slope form:

$$ y-y_1 = m_{\text{sec}}(x-x_1). $$

In this example:

$$ y-1=4(x-1) $$ $$ y=4x-3. $$

The relationship between secant and tangent lines is fundamental to calculus. Fix a point:

$$ P=(a,f(a)) $$

and choose a nearby point:

$$ Q=(a+h,f(a+h)). $$

The slope of the corresponding secant line is:

$$ m_{\text{sec}} = \frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}, \qquad h\neq0. $$

As $h$ approaches zero, the second point approaches the first. If the secant slopes approach a finite limit, that limit is the derivative:

$$ f'(a) = \lim_{h\to0} \frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}. $$

Geometrically, the tangent line can therefore be understood as the limiting position of secant lines whose two selected points move together.

Common Misconception: A secant line does not have to intersect an entire curve at exactly two points. It is determined by two selected points on the curve, but the extended line may happen to intersect the same curve at additional points elsewhere. The essential feature is that two distinct points on the curve are being used to determine the line.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$P=(x_1,f(x_1))$ "P is the point x-one, f of x-one."
$Q=(x_2,f(x_2))$ "Q is the point x-two, f of x-two."
$m_{\text{sec}}=\frac{f(x_2)-f(x_1)}{x_2-x_1}$ "The secant slope equals the change in f over the change in x."
$\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$ "Delta y over delta x," or "change in y over change in x."
$\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$ "The difference quotient f of a plus h minus f of a, divided by h."
$f'(a)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$ "F prime of a is the limit of the secant slopes as h approaches zero."

There is no universal special symbol for a secant line. It may simply be named using two points, such as line $PQ$, while its slope is often written $m_{\text{sec}}$ when comparison with a tangent slope is important.

The expression:

$$ \frac{f(x_2)-f(x_1)}{x_2-x_1} $$

is commonly called a difference quotient. It represents the slope of the secant line and the average rate of change of the function over the specified interval.

Defense Notation Advice: Distinguish clearly between the secant line, its slope, and the difference quotient used to calculate that slope. Saying "the secant is the difference quotient" is imprecise. The secant is a geometric line; the difference quotient is a numerical or algebraic expression giving its slope.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Circle Geometry: A secant line intersects a circle at two distinct points. If line $\ell$ passes through circle points $A$ and $B$, then $\ell$ is a secant of the circle. The portion $\overline{AB}$ lying inside the circle is a chord, while the complete line extends indefinitely beyond both points.
  • Secant-Secant Theorem: If two secants are drawn from the same external point $P$, intersecting a circle at $A,B$ and $C,D$, respectively, then: $$ PA\cdot PB=PC\cdot PD. $$ This relationship is part of the broader power-of-a-point framework in circle geometry.
  • Average Rate of Change: For a function $f$, the secant slope: $$ \frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a} $$ represents the average rate of change of $f$ over the interval $[a,b]$. This concept appears throughout algebra, precalculus, statistics, economics, physics, and other quantitative fields.
  • Calculus: Secant lines provide the geometric foundation for the derivative. As one endpoint approaches the other: $$ \frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h} \longrightarrow f'(a), $$ provided the limit exists. The derivative is therefore obtained by passing from an average rate of change to an instantaneous rate of change.
  • Tangent Line: A tangent line describes local behavior at a point, while a secant line describes behavior between two selected points. In calculus: $$ m_{\text{sec}} = \frac{f(x)-f(a)}{x-a} $$ approaches: $$ m_{\text{tan}} = f'(a) $$ as $x\to a$, when the derivative exists.
  • Numerical Analysis: Secant slopes are used computationally to approximate derivatives when exact differentiation is inconvenient or impossible. Related ideas also appear in the secant method for approximating roots of equations, where successive secant lines are used instead of tangent lines.
  • Physics: A secant slope on a position-versus-time graph gives average velocity: $$ v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{x(t_2)-x(t_1)} {t_2-t_1}. $$ Allowing the time interval to shrink leads conceptually to instantaneous velocity, represented by the tangent slope.
  • Terminological Contrast: A secant line is determined by two distinct points on a curve. A tangent line represents the limiting local direction of the curve at a point. In circle geometry, a chord is only the finite segment joining two points on the circle, whereas a secant is the entire line extending through those points.

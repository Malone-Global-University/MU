Centroid Formula

Subfield: Geometry, Spatial Analysis & Topology

Etymology & Roots

A centroid formula is a formula for locating the geometric average position of a figure, region, solid, or collection of points.

$$ \boxed{ \text{centroid} = \text{geometric average position} }. $$

For a triangle with vertices:

$$ A=(x_1,y_1),\qquad B=(x_2,y_2),\qquad C=(x_3,y_3), $$

the standard centroid formula is:

$$ \boxed{ G= \left( \frac{x_1+x_2+x_3}{3}, \frac{y_1+y_2+y_3}{3} \right) }. $$

This is the coordinate form of the fact that the triangle centroid is the equal average of its three vertex positions.

For continuous geometric regions, the same averaging idea is expressed through integration rather than finite sums.

Operational Nuance

Triangle Centroid Formula

For:

$$ A=(x_1,y_1),\quad B=(x_2,y_2),\quad C=(x_3,y_3), $$

the centroid is:

$$ \boxed{ \bar{x} = \frac{x_1+x_2+x_3}{3} } $$
$$ \boxed{ \bar{y} = \frac{y_1+y_2+y_3}{3} }. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ G=(\bar{x},\bar{y}) }. $$

Example

If:

$$ A=(1,2),\qquad B=(7,2),\qquad C=(4,8), $$

then:

$$ \bar{x} = \frac{1+7+4}{3} = 4 $$

and:

$$ \bar{y} = \frac{2+2+8}{3} = 4. $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ G=(4,4) }. $$

Vector Formula for a Triangle

If the position vectors of the vertices are $\mathbf a,\mathbf b,\mathbf c$, then:

$$ \boxed{ \mathbf g = \frac{\mathbf a+\mathbf b+\mathbf c}{3} }. $$

Median Interpretation

The same point is the intersection of the three medians.

If $M$ is the midpoint of the side opposite $A$, then:

$$ \boxed{ AG=\frac23AM }. $$

Thus the coordinate-average formula and the median-concurrency definition identify the same geometric point.

Finite Point Set

For $n$ equally weighted points:

$$ P_i=(x_i,y_i), \qquad i=1,\ldots,n, $$

the centroid is:

$$ \boxed{ \bar{x} = \frac1n \sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i } $$
$$ \boxed{ \bar{y} = \frac1n \sum_{i=1}^{n}y_i }. $$

Vector Average of Points

$$ \boxed{ \mathbf{\bar p} = \frac1n \sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf p_i }. $$

Area Centroid of a Planar Region

Let $R$ be a planar region of area:

$$ \boxed{ A= \iint_R dA }. $$

The centroid coordinates are:

$$ \boxed{ \bar{x} = \frac1A \iint_R x\,dA } $$
$$ \boxed{ \bar{y} = \frac1A \iint_R y\,dA }. $$

Moment Interpretation

Define the first area moments:

$$ \boxed{ Q_y= \iint_R x\,dA } $$
$$ \boxed{ Q_x= \iint_R y\,dA }. $$

Then:

$$ \boxed{ \bar{x}=\frac{Q_y}{A}, \qquad \bar{y}=\frac{Q_x}{A} }. $$

Why the Subscripts Appear Reversed

The moment about the $y$-axis uses horizontal distance $x$, while the moment about the $x$-axis uses vertical distance $y$.

Region between Two Functions

Suppose:

$$ R= \{(x,y): a\le x\le b,\, g(x)\le y\le f(x)\}. $$

Its area is:

$$ \boxed{ A= \int_a^b [f(x)-g(x)]\,dx }. $$

x-Coordinate for a Region between Curves

$$ \boxed{ \bar{x} = \frac{ \int_a^b x[f(x)-g(x)]\,dx }{ A } }. $$

y-Coordinate for a Region between Curves

$$ \boxed{ \bar{y} = \frac{ \frac12 \int_a^b [f(x)^2-g(x)^2]\,dx }{ A } }. $$

Why the y Formula Contains Squares

A thin vertical strip has centroid halfway between $g(x)$ and $f(x)$:

$$ y_{\text{strip}} = \frac{f(x)+g(x)}2. $$

Multiplying by strip area:

$$ [f(x)-g(x)]\,dx $$

gives:

$$ \frac12 [f(x)^2-g(x)^2]\,dx. $$

Region Described Horizontally

If:

$$ R= \{(x,y): c\le y\le d,\, g(y)\le x\le f(y)\}, $$

then:

$$ A= \int_c^d [f(y)-g(y)]\,dy. $$

The centroid formulas become:

$$ \boxed{ \bar{x} = \frac{ \frac12 \int_c^d [f(y)^2-g(y)^2]\,dy }{ A } } $$
$$ \boxed{ \bar{y} = \frac{ \int_c^d y[f(y)-g(y)]\,dy }{ A } }. $$

Centroid of a Composite Region

If a region is divided into pieces with areas $A_i$ and known centroids $(x_i,y_i)$, then:

$$ \boxed{ \bar{x} = \frac{ \sum A_i x_i }{ \sum A_i } } $$
$$ \boxed{ \bar{y} = \frac{ \sum A_i y_i }{ \sum A_i } }. $$

Weighted-Average Structure

Composite centroid formulas are area-weighted averages:

$$ \boxed{ \text{centroid coordinate} = \frac{ \text{sum of area}\times\text{coordinate} }{ \text{total area} } }. $$

Holes and Removed Regions

A hole may be represented with negative area:

$$ \boxed{ A_{\text{hole}}<0 }. $$

The same composite formulas can then be used without changing their algebraic structure.

Polygon Area Centroid

For a simple polygon with cyclic vertices $(x_i,y_i)$ and $(x_{n+1},y_{n+1})=(x_1,y_1)$, define signed area:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac12 \sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i y_{i+1}-x_{i+1}y_i) }. $$

Then:

$$ \boxed{ \bar{x} = \frac1{6A} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i+x_{i+1}) (x_i y_{i+1}-x_{i+1}y_i) } $$
$$ \boxed{ \bar{y} = \frac1{6A} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i+y_{i+1}) (x_i y_{i+1}-x_{i+1}y_i) }. $$

Vertex Average versus Polygon Area Centroid

For an arbitrary polygon, simply averaging the vertex coordinates does not generally produce the centroid of the polygon's area.

$$ \boxed{ \text{vertex average} \ne \text{area centroid in general} }. $$

A triangle is an important special case where they agree.

Centroid of a Uniform Solid

For a solid $V$, let:

$$ \mathcal V= \iiint_V dV. $$

Then:

$$ \boxed{ \bar{x} = \frac1{\mathcal V} \iiint_V x\,dV } $$
$$ \boxed{ \bar{y} = \frac1{\mathcal V} \iiint_V y\,dV } $$
$$ \boxed{ \bar{z} = \frac1{\mathcal V} \iiint_V z\,dV }. $$

Three-Dimensional Vector Form

$$ \boxed{ \mathbf r_G = \frac{ \iiint_V \mathbf r\,dV }{ \iiint_V dV } }. $$

Symmetry Shortcut

Before applying a formula, symmetry should be checked.

If a uniform region is symmetric about the $y$-axis:

$$ \boxed{ \bar{x}=0 }. $$

If it is symmetric about both coordinate axes:

$$ \boxed{ (\bar{x},\bar{y})=(0,0) }. $$

Rectangle

For a rectangle:

$$ [a,b]\times[c,d], $$

symmetry gives:

$$ \boxed{ G= \left( \frac{a+b}{2}, \frac{c+d}{2} \right) }. $$

Circle

The centroid of a uniform disk is its geometric center.

Semicircular Area

For a semicircular region of radius $R$ centered symmetrically above a diameter on the $x$-axis:

$$ \boxed{ \bar{x}=0, \qquad \bar{y}= \frac{4R}{3\pi} }. $$

Relation to Center of Mass

A centroid formula treats geometric measure uniformly.

A center-of-mass formula includes density:

$$ \boxed{ \bar{x} = \frac{ \int x\,dm }{ \int dm } }. $$

If density is constant, it cancels and the center-of-mass formula reduces to the geometric centroid formula.

$$ \boxed{ \text{uniform density} \Rightarrow \text{center of mass} = \text{centroid} }. $$

Centroid as an Average

All centroid formulas share the same pattern:

$$ \boxed{ \text{centroid} = \frac{ \text{weighted position total} }{ \text{total geometric measure} } }. $$

For points, the measure is counting measure. For a lamina, it is area. For a solid, it is volume.

Common Misconception: There Is Only One Centroid Formula

The correct formula depends on whether the object is a triangle, finite point set, planar region, composite area, polygon, or solid.

Common Misconception: Average All Polygon Vertices

The simple vertex-average formula is not the general area-centroid formula for polygons.

Common Misconception: Divide by 3 for Every Shape

Division by $3$ occurs in the triangle vertex formula because there are three equally weighted vertices.

It is not a universal centroid rule.

Common Misconception: Centroid and Center of Mass Always Coincide

They coincide for uniform density, but a nonuniform density distribution generally shifts the center of mass away from the geometric centroid.

Common Misconception: A Centroid Must Lie inside the Material

A triangle centroid lies inside the triangle, but the centroid of some nonconvex regions may lie outside the material region itself.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$G=\left(\frac{x_1+x_2+x_3}{3},\frac{y_1+y_2+y_3}{3}\right)$ "The triangle centroid is found by averaging the three x-coordinates and the three y-coordinates."
$\bar{x}=\frac1A\iint_R x\,dA$ "x bar is the area-average x-coordinate."
$\bar{y}=\frac1A\iint_R y\,dA$ "y bar is the area-average y-coordinate."
$\bar{x}=\frac{\sum A_i x_i}{\sum A_i}$ "The composite x-coordinate is the area-weighted average of the component centroids."
$AG=\frac23AM$ "The triangle centroid lies two-thirds of the way from the vertex along the median."
$\mathbf g=\frac{\mathbf a+\mathbf b+\mathbf c}{3}$ "The centroid position vector is the average of the three vertex position vectors."

The phrase centroid formula is context-sensitive. In elementary coordinate geometry it usually refers to the triangle formula. In calculus or engineering it may refer to integral or composite-area formulas.

Defense Notation Advice: Identify what is being averaged before choosing a formula. Use the triangle coordinate average only for triangle vertices, area integrals for continuous regions, and area-weighted formulas for composite figures. If density varies, use a center-of-mass formula instead of a purely geometric centroid formula.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Idea: A centroid formula computes geometric average position.
  • Triangle Formula: $$ \boxed{ G= \left( \frac{x_1+x_2+x_3}{3}, \frac{y_1+y_2+y_3}{3} \right) }. $$
  • Triangle Vector Formula: $$ \boxed{ \mathbf g= \frac{\mathbf a+\mathbf b+\mathbf c}{3} }. $$
  • Median Relation: $$ \boxed{ AG=\frac23AM }. $$
  • Finite Point Set: $$ \boxed{ \mathbf{\bar p} = \frac1n \sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf p_i }. $$
  • Planar Area: $$ \boxed{ \bar{x} = \frac1A\iint_R x\,dA, \qquad \bar{y} = \frac1A\iint_R y\,dA }. $$
  • Region between Curves: $$ \boxed{ \bar{x} = \frac{ \int_a^b x[f(x)-g(x)]\,dx }{ A } }. $$
  • Vertical Moment Formula: $$ \boxed{ \bar{y} = \frac{ \frac12 \int_a^b [f(x)^2-g(x)^2]\,dx }{ A } }. $$
  • Composite Region: $$ \boxed{ \bar{x} = \frac{\sum A_i x_i}{\sum A_i}, \qquad \bar{y} = \frac{\sum A_i y_i}{\sum A_i} }. $$
  • Hole Convention: Removed regions may be assigned negative area.
  • Uniform Solid: Centroid coordinates are volume averages obtained by triple integration.
  • Symmetry: Use symmetry before integrating whenever possible.
  • Triangle Special Case: Averaging the vertices gives the area centroid.
  • Polygon Warning: Averaging the vertices of an arbitrary polygon does not generally give its area centroid.
  • Center-of-Mass Connection: Uniform density makes the center of mass coincide with the centroid.
  • Common Error: Do not divide by $3$ unless the formula actually involves three equal weights.
  • Common Error: Do not use the triangle formula for arbitrary polygons.
  • Common Error: Do not ignore holes in composite regions; treat them as removed area.
  • Common Error: Do not substitute geometric area weights for mass weights when density is nonuniform.
  • Terminological Contrast: Centroid formula computes geometric average position. Triangle centroid formula averages the three vertex coordinates. Area centroid averages position over a planar region. Composite centroid uses area-weighted averages of component centroids. Center of mass formula uses mass or density rather than purely geometric measure. First moment is the weighted position quantity divided by total area or mass to obtain a centroid or center of mass.

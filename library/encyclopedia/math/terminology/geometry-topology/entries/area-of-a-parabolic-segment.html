Area of a Parabolic Segment

Subfield: Geometry, Spatial Analysis & Topology

Etymology & Roots

A parabolic segment is a region bounded by an arc of a parabola and a chord joining two points on that parabola.

The word segment here describes a portion cut from the larger parabolic region by a straight line. It should not be confused with a line segment itself.

If the chord is treated as a base of length $b$, and $h$ is the perpendicular distance from the chord to the point of the parabolic arc whose tangent is parallel to the chord, then the area of the parabolic segment is:

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac{2}{3}bh }. $$

Equivalently, if $T$ is the area of the triangle having the same base $b$ and height $h$, then:

$$ T=\frac12bh, $$

so:

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac43T }. $$

Operational Nuance

Standard Base-Height Formula

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac23bh }. $$

Here:

  • $b$ is the length of the chord bounding the segment,
  • $h$ is the perpendicular height of the parabolic segment relative to that chord.

The Height Is Perpendicular

The height is not simply any distance from the chord to the parabola. It is measured perpendicular to the chord.

$$ \boxed{ h=\text{perpendicular distance from the chord to the opposite point of the arc} }. $$

Triangle Relationship

Construct the triangle whose base is the chord and whose opposite vertex is the point on the parabola where the tangent is parallel to the chord.

That triangle has area:

$$ T=\frac12bh. $$

The parabolic segment has area:

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac43T }. $$

Recovering the Base-Height Formula

Substitute:

$$ T=\frac12bh $$

into:

$$ A=\frac43T. $$

Then:

$$ A = \frac43 \left( \frac12bh \right) = \boxed{ \frac23bh }. $$

A Convenient Coordinate Model

A parabolic segment of base $b$ and height $h$ can be represented by:

$$ \boxed{ y= h\left( 1-\frac{4x^2}{b^2} \right) } $$

over:

$$ -\frac{b}{2} \le x\le \frac{b}{2}. $$

The bounding chord is the $x$-axis between:

$$ x=-\frac b2 \qquad\text{and}\qquad x=\frac b2. $$

Integral Derivation

The area between the parabola and the chord is:

$$ A= \int_{-b/2}^{b/2} h\left( 1-\frac{4x^2}{b^2} \right)\,dx. $$

Factor out $h$:

$$ A= h \int_{-b/2}^{b/2} \left( 1-\frac{4x^2}{b^2} \right)\,dx. $$

An antiderivative is:

$$ x-\frac{4x^3}{3b^2}. $$

Evaluating gives:

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac23bh }. $$

Symmetry Derivation

Since the coordinate model is symmetric about the $y$-axis:

$$ A= 2 \int_0^{b/2} h\left( 1-\frac{4x^2}{b^2} \right)\,dx. $$

This again evaluates to:

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac23bh }. $$

Example

Suppose a parabolic segment has chord length:

$$ b=12 $$

and height:

$$ h=5. $$

Then:

$$ A= \frac23(12)(5). $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ A=40 }. $$

Triangle Check

The corresponding triangle has area:

$$ T= \frac12(12)(5) = 30. $$

Then:

$$ \frac43T = \frac43(30) = \boxed{40}. $$

Solving for Height

Starting from:

$$ A=\frac23bh, $$

solve for $h$:

$$ \boxed{ h=\frac{3A}{2b} }. $$

Solving for the Chord Length

Similarly:

$$ \boxed{ b=\frac{3A}{2h} }. $$

Area Ratio to the Bounding Rectangle

A rectangle with the same base $b$ and height $h$ has area:

$$ A_{\text{rectangle}}=bh. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{A_{\text{segment}}} {A_{\text{rectangle}}} = \frac23 }. $$

So the parabolic segment occupies two-thirds of this corresponding base-height rectangle.

Area Ratio to the Inscribed Triangle

Since:

$$ T=\frac12bh $$

and:

$$ A=\frac23bh, $$

their ratio is:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{A}{T} = \frac43 }. $$

Difference between the Segment and Triangle

The area lying inside the parabolic segment but outside the inscribed triangle is:

$$ A-T = \frac23bh-\frac12bh. $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ A-T=\frac16bh }. $$

Relative to the triangle:

$$ \boxed{ A-T=\frac13T }. $$

General Vertical Parabola

Suppose:

$$ y=f(x)=ax^2+bx+c $$

and a chord joins the points corresponding to $x=x_1$ and $x=x_2$.

If the chord is represented by:

$$ y=L(x), $$

then the parabolic-segment area is computed by:

$$ \boxed{ A= \left| \int_{x_1}^{x_2} [f(x)-L(x)]\,dx \right| }. $$

The absolute value accounts for whether the parabola lies above or below its chord in the chosen coordinate orientation.

Difference between a Quadratic and Its Chord

If $L(x)$ is the chord through:

$$ (x_1,f(x_1)) \qquad\text{and}\qquad (x_2,f(x_2)), $$

then for a quadratic $f(x)=ax^2+bx+c$:

$$ \boxed{ f(x)-L(x) = a(x-x_1)(x-x_2) }. $$

This factorization makes the segment-area integral especially simple.

Area from the Quadratic Coefficient and Chord Projection

For a vertical parabola $y=ax^2+bx+c$ with chord endpoints having $x$-coordinates $x_1$ and $x_2$, let:

$$ \Delta x=|x_2-x_1|. $$

Integrating the difference between the quadratic and its chord gives:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac{|a|}{6} (\Delta x)^3 }. $$

This formula uses horizontal coordinate width rather than the actual geometric chord length, so it should not be confused with the invariant base-height formula $\frac23bh$.

Affine Geometry

Parabolas are preserved as parabolas under suitable affine transformations. Areas under a linear transformation $M$ scale by:

$$ \boxed{ |\det M| }. $$

Consequently, the four-thirds triangle relationship is preserved under affine transformations.

Scaling

If all lengths are multiplied by $k$:

$$ b\mapsto kb, \qquad h\mapsto kh, $$

then:

$$ A' = \frac23(kb)(kh) = k^2A. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ A\mapsto k^2A }. $$

Doubling Base and Height

$$ \boxed{ b\mapsto2b,\quad h\mapsto2h \Rightarrow A\mapsto4A }. $$

Doubling Only the Base

If $h$ is held fixed:

$$ \boxed{ b\mapsto2b \Rightarrow A\mapsto2A }. $$

Doubling Only the Height

If $b$ is held fixed:

$$ \boxed{ h\mapsto2h \Rightarrow A\mapsto2A }. $$

Orientation Does Not Matter

The chord need not be horizontal and the parabola need not open upward or downward. The base-height relation is geometric rather than tied to a particular coordinate orientation.

Parabolic Segment versus Parabola

A parabola itself is a curve and therefore does not have a finite planar area. A parabolic segment is a bounded two-dimensional region created when a chord closes off part of that curve.

Parabolic Segment versus Circular Segment

A circular segment is bounded by a circular arc and a chord. A parabolic segment is bounded by a parabolic arc and a chord. Their area formulas are different.

Common Misconception: A parabolic segment is not generally one-half of a base-times-height rectangle. That would be the area of a triangle. The parabolic segment has the larger area:

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac23bh }. $$

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$A=\frac23bh$ "Area equals two-thirds base times height."
$A=\frac43T$ "The parabolic segment has four-thirds the area of the corresponding triangle."
$T=\frac12bh$ "The triangle area equals one-half base times height."
$h=\frac{3A}{2b}$ "Height equals three A divided by two b."
$A=\left|\int_{x_1}^{x_2}[f(x)-L(x)]\,dx\right|$ "Area is the absolute value of the integral of the parabola minus the chord."
$A=\frac{|a|}{6}(x_2-x_1)^3$ "Area equals absolute a over six times the horizontal endpoint separation cubed."

The phrase base of the parabolic segment normally refers to the chord, not the curved parabolic boundary.

The word height refers to perpendicular distance relative to that chord. If coordinates have been rotated or sheared, the geometric height should not be confused with an ordinary vertical coordinate difference.

Defense Notation Advice: When applying $A=\frac23bh$, identify the actual chord length and its corresponding perpendicular height. When working directly from equations, integrating the difference between the parabola and chord avoids ambiguity.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Meaning: A parabolic segment is the bounded region enclosed by a parabolic arc and a chord.
  • Core Area Formula: $$ \boxed{ A=\frac23bh }. $$
  • Base: $b$ is the length of the bounding chord.
  • Height: $h$ is measured perpendicular to the chord.
  • Corresponding Triangle: $$ \boxed{ T=\frac12bh }. $$
  • Four-Thirds Relation: $$ \boxed{ A=\frac43T }. $$
  • Rectangle Ratio: $$ \boxed{ A=\frac23A_{\text{rectangle}} } $$ for the corresponding rectangle of dimensions $b$ and $h$.
  • Area beyond the Triangle: $$ \boxed{ A-T=\frac13T }. $$
  • Equivalent Difference: $$ \boxed{ A-T=\frac16bh }. $$
  • Height from Area: $$ \boxed{ h=\frac{3A}{2b} }. $$
  • Base from Area: $$ \boxed{ b=\frac{3A}{2h} }. $$
  • Symmetric Coordinate Model: $$ \boxed{ y= h\left( 1-\frac{4x^2}{b^2} \right) }. $$
  • Model Domain: $$ \boxed{ -\frac b2\le x\le\frac b2 }. $$
  • Integral Formula: $$ \boxed{ A= \int_{-b/2}^{b/2} h\left( 1-\frac{4x^2}{b^2} \right)\,dx }. $$
  • General Coordinate Method: $$ \boxed{ A= \left| \int_{x_1}^{x_2} [f(x)-L(x)]\,dx \right| }. $$
  • Chord Function: $L(x)$ represents the line passing through the two endpoints of the parabolic segment.
  • Quadratic-Chord Difference: For $f(x)=ax^2+bx+c$: $$ \boxed{ f(x)-L(x) = a(x-x_1)(x-x_2) }. $$
  • Vertical-Parabola Coordinate Formula: $$ \boxed{ A= \frac{|a|}{6} |x_2-x_1|^3 }. $$
  • Coordinate Caution: $|x_2-x_1|$ is horizontal separation, not necessarily the geometric chord length.
  • Scaling: $$ \boxed{ \text{linear scale }k \Rightarrow \text{area scale }k^2 }. $$
  • Affine Area Scaling: Under a linear transformation $M$: $$ \boxed{ A_{\text{new}} = |\det M|A_{\text{old}} }. $$
  • Affine Invariance of Ratio: The ratio: $$ \boxed{ \frac{A_{\text{segment}}} {A_{\text{triangle}}} = \frac43 } $$ is preserved under nonsingular affine transformations.
  • Orientation: The chord may be horizontal, vertical, or oblique.
  • Opening Direction: The parabola may open upward, downward, left, or right after a suitable change of coordinates.
  • Geometric Area: Area is always nonnegative even if a coordinate integral initially produces a negative signed value.
  • Square Units: If $b$ and $h$ are measured in meters, area is measured in square meters.
  • Parabola Contrast: A parabola is a curve; a parabolic segment is a bounded region formed from part of that curve and a chord.
  • Circular-Segment Contrast: Circular and parabolic segments are both bounded by a chord and an arc, but the arcs belong to different conic sections and require different area formulas.
  • Triangle Contrast: $$ \boxed{ A_{\triangle} = \frac12bh } $$ while: $$ \boxed{ A_{\text{parabolic segment}} = \frac23bh }. $$
  • Common Error: Do not use $\frac12bh$ for the whole parabolic segment; that gives only the corresponding triangle area.
  • Common Error: Do not confuse the chord length with its horizontal projection unless the chord is horizontal.
  • Terminological Contrast: Chord is the straight boundary connecting two points on the parabola. Parabolic arc is the curved boundary between those points. Parabolic segment is the entire region enclosed by the chord and arc.

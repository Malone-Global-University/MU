arcsin

Subfield: Algebraic & Analytic Structures

Etymology & Roots

arcsin, read arc sine, is the inverse of the sine function after sine has been restricted to an interval on which it is one-to-one. It answers the question:

$$ \boxed{ \text{What principal angle has sine }x? }. $$

Symbolically:

$$ \boxed{ \theta=\arcsin(x) \iff \sin\theta=x, \qquad -\frac{\pi}{2}\le\theta\le\frac{\pi}{2} }. $$

Another common notation is:

$$ \boxed{ \sin^{-1}(x) }. $$

In this context the superscript $-1$ denotes an inverse function, not a reciprocal.

$$ \boxed{ \arcsin x = \sin^{-1}x \ne \frac1{\sin x} }. $$

The reciprocal of sine is cosecant:

$$ \boxed{ \frac1{\sin x}=\csc x }. $$

Operational Nuance

Why Sine Must Be Restricted

The sine function is periodic:

$$ \boxed{ \sin(\theta+2\pi)=\sin\theta }. $$

Therefore sine is not one-to-one on all real numbers and cannot possess a single-valued inverse over its entire domain.

To define arcsin, sine is restricted to:

$$ \boxed{ -\frac{\pi}{2} \le \theta \le \frac{\pi}{2} }. $$

On this interval sine increases continuously from $-1$ to $1$ and takes each value exactly once.

Domain

Since real sine values satisfy:

$$ -1\le\sin\theta\le1, $$

the real domain of arcsin is:

$$ \boxed{ [-1,1] }. $$

Range

The principal-value range is:

$$ \boxed{ \left[ -\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2} \right] }. $$

In degrees:

$$ \boxed{ [-90^\circ,90^\circ] }. $$

Function Mapping

$$ \boxed{ \arcsin: [-1,1] \to \left[ -\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2} \right] }. $$

Important Values

$$ \boxed{ \arcsin(-1) = -\frac{\pi}{2} }. $$
$$ \boxed{ \arcsin\left(-\frac12\right) = -\frac{\pi}{6} }. $$
$$ \boxed{ \arcsin(0)=0 }. $$
$$ \boxed{ \arcsin\left(\frac12\right) = \frac{\pi}{6} }. $$
$$ \boxed{ \arcsin\left(\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\right) = \frac{\pi}{4} }. $$
$$ \boxed{ \arcsin\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right) = \frac{\pi}{3} }. $$
$$ \boxed{ \arcsin(1) = \frac{\pi}{2} }. $$

Unit-Circle Interpretation

A point on the unit circle corresponding to angle $\theta$ has coordinates:

$$ (\cos\theta,\sin\theta). $$

Therefore $\arcsin(x)$ returns the principal angle in the right half of the unit circle whose vertical coordinate is $x$.

Right-Triangle Interpretation

For an acute angle in a right triangle:

$$ \boxed{ \sin\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}} {\text{hypotenuse}} }. $$

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ \theta = \arcsin \left( \frac{\text{opposite}} {\text{hypotenuse}} \right) }. $$

Inverse Composition

For every $x\in[-1,1]$:

$$ \boxed{ \sin(\arcsin x)=x }. $$

Reverse Composition Requires the Principal Range

The expression:

$$ \arcsin(\sin\theta) $$

does not equal $\theta$ for every real angle.

It equals $\theta$ directly when:

$$ \boxed{ -\frac{\pi}{2} \le \theta \le \frac{\pi}{2} }. $$

Outside this interval, arcsin returns the corresponding principal angle having the same sine value.

Example of Principal-Value Reduction

Since:

$$ \sin\left(\frac{5\pi}{6}\right) = \frac12, $$

we obtain:

$$ \boxed{ \arcsin \left( \sin\frac{5\pi}{6} \right) = \frac{\pi}{6} }. $$

The result is $\pi/6$, not $5\pi/6$, because $\pi/6$ lies in the principal range of arcsin.

Graph

The graph:

$$ y=\arcsin x $$

is the reflection across:

$$ y=x $$

of the restricted sine graph:

$$ y=\sin x, \qquad -\frac{\pi}{2} \le x\le \frac{\pi}{2}. $$

Monotonicity

Because sine is increasing on its restricted interval:

$$ \boxed{ \arcsin x \text{ is strictly increasing on }[-1,1] }. $$

Odd Symmetry

Arcsin is an odd function:

$$ \boxed{ \arcsin(-x) = -\arcsin(x) }. $$

Derivative

For:

$$ -1

the derivative is:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}\arcsin x = \frac1{\sqrt{1-x^2}} }. $$

Derivative by Implicit Differentiation

Let:

$$ y=\arcsin x. $$

Then:

$$ \sin y=x. $$

Differentiate:

$$ \cos y\frac{dy}{dx}=1. $$

Therefore:

$$ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac1{\cos y}. $$

Because $y$ lies in $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$, $\cos y\ge0$. Hence:

$$ \cos y = \sqrt{1-\sin^2y} = \sqrt{1-x^2}. $$

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac1{\sqrt{1-x^2}} }. $$

Chain Rule

For differentiable $u(x)$ with $|u(x)|<1$:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx} \arcsin(u) = \frac{u'} {\sqrt{1-u^2}} }. $$

Antiderivative

$$ \boxed{ \int\arcsin x\,dx = x\arcsin x + \sqrt{1-x^2} + C }. $$

Relationship with arccos

For $x\in[-1,1]$:

$$ \boxed{ \arcsin x + \arccos x = \frac{\pi}{2} }. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \arcsin x = \frac{\pi}{2} - \arccos x }. $$

Solving Sine Equations

Suppose:

$$ \sin\theta=c, \qquad -1\le c\le1. $$

Let:

$$ \alpha=\arcsin c. $$

One convenient general solution form is:

$$ \boxed{ \theta = n\pi+(-1)^n\alpha, \qquad n\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$

Equivalently, solutions can be written as the two families:

$$ \boxed{ \theta=\alpha+2\pi k } $$

or:

$$ \boxed{ \theta=\pi-\alpha+2\pi k, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$

Connection with arccsc

For $|x|\ge1$:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{arccsc}x = \arcsin\left(\frac1x\right) }. $$

Thus arcsin is often used computationally to evaluate inverse cosecant.

Common Misconception: $\arcsin(x)$ returns one principal angle, not every angle having sine $x$. Periodic equation solving requires additional solution families.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\arcsin x$ "Arc sine of x."
$\sin^{-1}x$ "Inverse sine of x."
$\arcsin(0)=0$ "The arc sine of zero is zero."
$\arcsin(1/2)=\pi/6$ "The arc sine of one-half is pi over six."
$\sin(\arcsin x)=x$ "Sine of arc sine x equals x."
$\frac{d}{dx}\arcsin x$ "The derivative of arc sine x."

The notation $\sin^{-1}x$ can be ambiguous when inverse functions and reciprocals appear together.

As inverse-function notation:

$$ \boxed{ \sin^{-1}(x) = \arcsin(x) }. $$

But as a reciprocal:

$$ \boxed{ (\sin x)^{-1} = \frac1{\sin x} = \csc x }. $$

Defense Notation Advice: Prefer $\arcsin$ when there is any chance that $\sin^{-1}$ could be mistaken for a reciprocal. Also distinguish carefully between a principal inverse value and the complete solution set of a trigonometric equation.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Definition: Arcsin returns the principal angle whose sine is the given input.
  • Equivalent Notation: $$ \boxed{ \arcsin x=\sin^{-1}x }. $$
  • Real Domain: $$ \boxed{ [-1,1] }. $$
  • Principal Range: $$ \boxed{ \left[ -\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2} \right] }. $$
  • Range in Degrees: $$ \boxed{ [-90^\circ,90^\circ] }. $$
  • Function Mapping: $$ \boxed{ \arcsin: [-1,1] \to \left[ -\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2} \right] }. $$
  • Restricted Sine: Sine is restricted to $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ so that it becomes one-to-one.
  • Negative Endpoint: $$ \boxed{ \arcsin(-1) = -\frac{\pi}{2} }. $$
  • Zero: $$ \boxed{ \arcsin(0)=0 }. $$
  • Positive Endpoint: $$ \boxed{ \arcsin(1) = \frac{\pi}{2} }. $$
  • Special Value: $$ \boxed{ \arcsin\left(\frac12\right) = \frac{\pi}{6} }. $$
  • Special Value: $$ \boxed{ \arcsin\left(\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\right) = \frac{\pi}{4} }. $$
  • Special Value: $$ \boxed{ \arcsin\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right) = \frac{\pi}{3} }. $$
  • Inverse Composition: $$ \boxed{ \sin(\arcsin x)=x } $$ for $x\in[-1,1]$.
  • Restricted Reverse Composition: $$ \boxed{ \arcsin(\sin\theta)=\theta } $$ for: $$ -\frac{\pi}{2} \le\theta\le \frac{\pi}{2}. $$
  • Principal-Value Caution: Outside the principal range, $\arcsin(\sin\theta)$ may differ from $\theta$.
  • Odd Function: $$ \boxed{ \arcsin(-x) = -\arcsin x }. $$
  • Monotonicity: Arcsin is strictly increasing throughout its domain.
  • Continuity: Arcsin is continuous on: $$ \boxed{ [-1,1] }. $$
  • Differentiability: Arcsin is differentiable on: $$ \boxed{ (-1,1) }. $$
  • Derivative: $$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}\arcsin x = \frac1{\sqrt{1-x^2}} }. $$
  • Chain Rule: $$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}\arcsin(u) = \frac{u'}{\sqrt{1-u^2}} }. $$
  • Antiderivative: $$ \boxed{ \int\arcsin x\,dx = x\arcsin x + \sqrt{1-x^2} + C }. $$
  • arccos Relationship: $$ \boxed{ \arcsin x+\arccos x = \frac{\pi}{2} }. $$
  • Right-Triangle Use: $$ \boxed{ \theta = \arcsin \left( \frac{\text{opposite}} {\text{hypotenuse}} \right) }. $$
  • Unit-Circle Meaning: Arcsin returns the principal angle whose unit-circle $y$-coordinate equals the input.
  • Sine Equation: If: $$ \sin\theta=c, $$ then: $$ \boxed{ \alpha=\arcsin c } $$ gives a principal solution.
  • General Sine Solution: $$ \boxed{ \theta=n\pi+(-1)^n\arcsin(c), \qquad n\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$
  • Equivalent Two-Family Solution: $$ \boxed{ \theta=\arcsin(c)+2\pi k } $$ or: $$ \boxed{ \theta=\pi-\arcsin(c)+2\pi k, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$
  • Calculator Mode: Numerical output depends on whether the calculator is using degrees or radians.
  • Calculus Convention: Standard derivative formulas assume radian measure.
  • Real-Domain Failure: $\arcsin x$ has no real value when: $$ \boxed{ |x|>1 }. $$
  • Inverse Cosecant Connection: For $|x|\ge1$: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{arccsc}x = \arcsin\left(\frac1x\right) }. $$
  • Graphical Relationship: The graph of arcsin is the reflection of the restricted sine graph across $y=x$.
  • Complex Extension: In complex analysis, arcsin extends beyond $[-1,1]$ using complex logarithms and square roots together with branch choices.
  • Inverse vs. Reciprocal: $$ \boxed{ \arcsin x \ne \csc x }. $$
  • Terminological Contrast: Sine maps an angle to a ratio or unit-circle vertical coordinate. Arcsin maps a permissible sine value back to its principal angle. Cosecant is the reciprocal of sine, not its inverse function.

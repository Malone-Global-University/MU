arctan

Subfield: Geometry, Spatial Analysis & Topology

Etymology & Roots

arctan, read arc tangent, is the inverse of the tangent function after tangent has been restricted to an interval on which it is one-to-one. It returns a principal angle from a tangent value.

$$ \boxed{ \theta=\arctan(x) \iff \tan\theta=x, \qquad -\frac{\pi}{2} < \theta < \frac{\pi}{2} }. $$

In geometric language, arctan reverses the process of taking a tangent ratio. If tangent converts an angle into:

$$ \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}, $$

then arctan converts that ratio back into its principal angle.

Another common notation is:

$$ \boxed{ \tan^{-1}(x) }. $$

Here $-1$ denotes an inverse function, not a reciprocal.

$$ \boxed{ \arctan x = \tan^{-1}x \ne \frac1{\tan x} }. $$

The reciprocal of tangent is cotangent:

$$ \boxed{ \frac1{\tan x} = \cot x }. $$

Operational Nuance

Why Tangent Must Be Restricted

Tangent is periodic:

$$ \boxed{ \tan(\theta+\pi)=\tan\theta }. $$

Therefore tangent is not one-to-one over its entire domain and cannot possess a single-valued inverse until a principal interval is selected.

Principal Interval

Tangent is conventionally restricted to:

$$ \boxed{ -\frac{\pi}{2} < \theta < \frac{\pi}{2} }. $$

On this interval tangent is continuous, strictly increasing, and takes every real value exactly once.

Domain

Tangent assumes every real value, so:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Dom}(\arctan)=\mathbb{R} }. $$

Range

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(\arctan) = \left( -\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2} \right) }. $$

In degrees:

$$ \boxed{ (-90^\circ,90^\circ) }. $$

Function Mapping

$$ \boxed{ \arctan: \mathbb{R} \to \left( -\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2} \right) }. $$

Important Values

$$ \boxed{ \arctan(0)=0 }. $$
$$ \boxed{ \arctan\left(\frac1{\sqrt3}\right) = \frac{\pi}{6} }. $$
$$ \boxed{ \arctan(1) = \frac{\pi}{4} }. $$
$$ \boxed{ \arctan(\sqrt3) = \frac{\pi}{3} }. $$
$$ \boxed{ \arctan(-1) = -\frac{\pi}{4} }. $$

Right-Triangle Interpretation

In a right triangle:

$$ \boxed{ \tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}} {\text{adjacent}} }. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \theta = \arctan \left( \frac{\text{opposite}} {\text{adjacent}} \right) }. $$

Example

If the opposite side is $3$ and the adjacent side is $4$:

$$ \tan\theta=\frac34. $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ \theta=\arctan\left(\frac34\right) }. $$

Slope and Angle

One of the most important geometric applications of arctan is converting slope into an angle.

For a nonvertical line with inclination angle $\theta$:

$$ \boxed{ m=\tan\theta }. $$

Therefore a principal direction angle can be recovered by:

$$ \boxed{ \theta=\arctan(m) }. $$

Rise and Run

Since:

$$ m=\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}, $$

the associated principal angle is:

$$ \boxed{ \theta = \arctan \left( \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} \right) }. $$

Quadrant Limitation

The simple expression:

$$ \arctan\left(\frac{y}{x}\right) $$

does not by itself determine the full direction of a point $(x,y)$ because the quotient $y/x$ loses information about the individual signs of $x$ and $y$.

For example:

$$ \frac{1}{1} = \frac{-1}{-1}. $$

Yet $(1,1)$ and $(-1,-1)$ point in opposite directions.

atan2

Computational mathematics often uses a two-argument inverse-tangent function, commonly written:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{atan2}(y,x) }. $$

Unlike ordinary $\arctan(y/x)$, this function examines the signs of both coordinates and can therefore identify the correct quadrant.

Polar Coordinates

When converting Cartesian coordinates to polar coordinates:

$$ x=r\cos\theta, \qquad y=r\sin\theta, $$

division gives:

$$ \tan\theta=\frac{y}{x}. $$

Thus:

$$ \theta = \arctan\left(\frac{y}{x}\right) $$

gives a reference or principal angle, but quadrant information must be restored. In computational work, $\operatorname{atan2}(y,x)$ performs this role directly.

Vector Direction

For a two-dimensional vector:

$$ \mathbf{v}=\langle v_x,v_y\rangle, $$

its direction is often computed from:

$$ \boxed{ \theta=\operatorname{atan2}(v_y,v_x) }. $$

Arctan therefore connects ratios of vector components to angular direction.

Angles of Elevation and Depression

If horizontal distance is $d$ and vertical displacement is $h$, then:

$$ \tan\theta=\frac{h}{d}. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \theta = \arctan\left(\frac{h}{d}\right) }. $$

Inverse Composition

For every real $x$:

$$ \boxed{ \tan(\arctan x)=x }. $$

Reverse Composition

The identity:

$$ \arctan(\tan\theta)=\theta $$

holds directly only when:

$$ \boxed{ -\frac{\pi}{2} < \theta < \frac{\pi}{2} }. $$

Outside this interval, arctan returns the coterminal principal angle in its range.

Example of Principal-Value Reduction

Since:

$$ \tan\left(\frac{5\pi}{4}\right)=1, $$

we have:

$$ \boxed{ \arctan \left( \tan\frac{5\pi}{4} \right) = \frac{\pi}{4} }. $$

Graph

The graph:

$$ y=\arctan x $$

is the reflection across $y=x$ of the restricted tangent graph:

$$ y=\tan x, \qquad -\frac{\pi}{2}

Monotonicity

$$ \boxed{ \arctan x \text{ is strictly increasing on }\mathbb{R} }. $$

Odd Symmetry

$$ \boxed{ \arctan(-x) = -\arctan(x) }. $$

Horizontal Asymptotes

As $x\to\infty$:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to\infty}\arctan x = \frac{\pi}{2} }. $$

As $x\to-\infty$:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to-\infty}\arctan x = -\frac{\pi}{2} }. $$

The values $\pm\pi/2$ are approached but never attained.

Derivative

$$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}\arctan x = \frac1{1+x^2} }. $$

Implicit Derivation

Let:

$$ y=\arctan x. $$

Then:

$$ \tan y=x. $$

Differentiating:

$$ \sec^2y\frac{dy}{dx}=1. $$

Therefore:

$$ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac1{\sec^2y}. $$

Since:

$$ \sec^2y=1+\tan^2y=1+x^2, $$

we obtain:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac1{1+x^2} }. $$

Chain Rule

$$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}\arctan(u) = \frac{u'}{1+u^2} }. $$

Fundamental Antiderivative

$$ \boxed{ \int\frac{1}{1+x^2}\,dx = \arctan x+C }. $$

Integral of arctan

$$ \boxed{ \int\arctan x\,dx = x\arctan x - \frac12\ln(1+x^2) + C }. $$

Solving Tangent Equations

If:

$$ \tan\theta=c, $$

then:

$$ \alpha=\arctan c $$

is the principal solution.

Since tangent has period $\pi$, the complete real solution is:

$$ \boxed{ \theta = \arctan(c) + k\pi, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$

Relationship with arccot

Under the common arccot range $(0,\pi)$:

$$ \boxed{ \arctan x + \operatorname{arccot}x = \frac{\pi}{2} }. $$

Equivalently:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{arccot}x = \frac{\pi}{2}-\arctan x }. $$

Common Misconception: $\arctan(y/x)$ does not automatically give the full geometric direction of the point $(x,y)$. Ordinary arctan returns only a principal angle between $-\pi/2$ and $\pi/2$; quadrant information may need to be supplied separately.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\arctan x$ "Arc tangent of x."
$\tan^{-1}x$ "Inverse tangent of x."
$\arctan(1)=\frac{\pi}{4}$ "The arc tangent of one is pi over four."
$\theta=\arctan(m)$ "Theta equals the arc tangent of the slope."
$\tan(\arctan x)=x$ "Tangent of arc tangent x equals x."
$\operatorname{atan2}(y,x)$ "Atan two of y comma x."

The notation $\tan^{-1}x$ must be distinguished from the reciprocal:

$$ (\tan x)^{-1}. $$

As inverse-function notation:

$$ \boxed{ \tan^{-1}(x) = \arctan(x) }. $$

As reciprocal notation:

$$ \boxed{ (\tan x)^{-1} = \frac1{\tan x} = \cot x }. $$

Defense Notation Advice: Prefer $\arctan$ when inverse and reciprocal notation might be confused. For coordinate direction, distinguish $\arctan(y/x)$ from a quadrant-aware construction such as $\operatorname{atan2}(y,x)$.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Definition: Arctan returns the principal angle whose tangent equals the given real number.
  • Equivalent Notation: $$ \boxed{ \arctan x=\tan^{-1}x }. $$
  • Domain: $$ \boxed{ \mathbb{R} }. $$
  • Principal Range: $$ \boxed{ \left( -\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2} \right) }. $$
  • Range in Degrees: $$ \boxed{ (-90^\circ,90^\circ) }. $$
  • Restricted Tangent: Tangent is restricted to: $$ \boxed{ \left( -\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2} \right) } $$ to make it one-to-one.
  • Zero: $$ \boxed{ \arctan(0)=0 }. $$
  • Special Value: $$ \boxed{ \arctan(1)=\frac{\pi}{4} }. $$
  • Special Negative Value: $$ \boxed{ \arctan(-1)=-\frac{\pi}{4} }. $$
  • Special Value: $$ \boxed{ \arctan(\sqrt3)=\frac{\pi}{3} }. $$
  • Odd Function: $$ \boxed{ \arctan(-x) = -\arctan x }. $$
  • Inverse Composition: $$ \boxed{ \tan(\arctan x)=x }. $$
  • Restricted Reverse Composition: $$ \boxed{ \arctan(\tan\theta)=\theta } $$ for: $$ -\frac{\pi}{2}<\theta<\frac{\pi}{2}. $$
  • Right-Triangle Use: $$ \boxed{ \theta = \arctan \left( \frac{\text{opposite}} {\text{adjacent}} \right) }. $$
  • Slope: If: $$ m=\tan\theta, $$ then a principal angle satisfies: $$ \boxed{ \theta=\arctan m }. $$
  • Coordinate Slope: $$ \boxed{ \theta = \arctan \left( \frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1} \right) } $$ gives a principal angle when the denominator is nonzero.
  • Line Inclination Caution: Standard line inclination is often taken in $[0,\pi)$, while arctan returns values only in $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$. A negative-slope line may therefore require adding $\pi$ to obtain its standard inclination.
  • Polar Coordinates: $$ \tan\theta=\frac{y}{x} $$ connects Cartesian coordinates with angular direction.
  • Quadrant Caution: $$ \arctan(y/x) $$ alone cannot always distinguish opposite quadrants.
  • atan2: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{atan2}(y,x) } $$ incorporates the signs of both coordinates to recover the appropriate direction angle.
  • Vector Direction: For: $$ \mathbf{v}=\langle v_x,v_y\rangle, $$ direction can be represented by: $$ \boxed{ \theta=\operatorname{atan2}(v_y,v_x) }. $$
  • Angle of Elevation: $$ \boxed{ \theta = \arctan \left( \frac{\text{vertical rise}} {\text{horizontal distance}} \right) }. $$
  • Angle of Depression: A tangent ratio can similarly recover the magnitude of a depression angle from vertical and horizontal displacement.
  • Inclination of a Line: The relation: $$ \boxed{ m=\tan\theta } $$ makes arctan a basic bridge between algebraic slope and geometric angle.
  • Monotonicity: Arctan is strictly increasing throughout $\mathbb{R}$.
  • Continuity: Arctan is continuous for every real input.
  • Differentiability: Arctan is differentiable for every real input.
  • Derivative: $$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}\arctan x = \frac1{1+x^2} }. $$
  • Chain Rule: $$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}\arctan(u) = \frac{u'}{1+u^2} }. $$
  • Inverse-Tangent Integral: $$ \boxed{ \int\frac{dx}{1+x^2} = \arctan x+C }. $$
  • Integral of arctan: $$ \boxed{ \int\arctan x\,dx = x\arctan x - \frac12\ln(1+x^2) + C }. $$
  • Horizontal Upper Limit: $$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to\infty}\arctan x = \frac{\pi}{2} }. $$
  • Horizontal Lower Limit: $$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to-\infty}\arctan x = -\frac{\pi}{2} }. $$
  • Tangent Equation: If: $$ \tan\theta=c, $$ the principal solution is: $$ \boxed{ \theta=\arctan c }. $$
  • General Tangent Solution: $$ \boxed{ \theta = \arctan(c)+k\pi, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$
  • Tangent Period: $$ \boxed{\pi}. $$
  • arccot Relationship: Under the common arccot branch: $$ \boxed{ \arctan x+\operatorname{arccot}x = \frac{\pi}{2} }. $$
  • Calculator Mode: Numerical output depends on whether the calculator is set to degrees or radians.
  • Calculus Convention: Standard derivative and integral formulas assume radian measure.
  • Computer Graphics: Inverse tangent and atan2 are routinely used to compute object direction, camera orientation, and angular position from coordinate components.
  • Navigation and Engineering: Arctan converts component ratios into inclination, bearing-related angles, slope angles, and directional information, subject to the coordinate convention being used.
  • Complex Extension: In complex analysis, arctan extends to complex inputs using logarithmic expressions and branch choices.
  • Inverse vs. Reciprocal: $$ \boxed{ \arctan x \ne \cot x }. $$
  • Terminological Contrast: Tangent maps an angle to a ratio or slope. Arctan maps a tangent value or slope back to a principal angle. Cotangent is the reciprocal of tangent, not its inverse function.

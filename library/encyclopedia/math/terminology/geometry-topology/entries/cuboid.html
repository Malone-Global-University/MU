Cuboid

Subfield: Geometry & Topology

Etymology & Roots

A cuboid is, in the most common elementary and analytic geometry usage, a three-dimensional rectangular solid bounded by six rectangular faces. It is also called a rectangular prism or rectangular parallelepiped.

$$ \boxed{ \text{cuboid} = \text{rectangular prism} } $$

Its three mutually perpendicular dimensions are conventionally denoted:

$$ \boxed{ \ell,\qquad w,\qquad h }, $$

representing length, width, and height.

The word cuboid combines cube with the suffix -oid, meaning "resembling" or "having the form of." A cuboid therefore resembles a cube but does not require all edge lengths to be equal.

A cube is a special cuboid satisfying:

$$ \boxed{ \ell=w=h }. $$

Terminology is not entirely uniform across all mathematical literature. In some broader geometric contexts, cuboid may be used for more general cube-like hexahedra. In elementary geometry, however, the standard intended meaning is the rectangular prism treated on this page.

Operational Nuance

Basic Structure

A rectangular cuboid has:

$$ \boxed{ 6\text{ faces},\qquad 12\text{ edges},\qquad 8\text{ vertices} }. $$

Opposite faces are congruent and parallel.

Three Dimensions

A cuboid is determined by three perpendicular edge lengths:

$$ \boxed{ \ell,\quad w,\quad h }. $$

Four edges have length $\ell$, four have length $w$, and four have length $h$.

Faces

The six faces occur in three congruent opposite pairs:

$$ \boxed{ 2(\ell\times w),\qquad 2(\ell\times h),\qquad 2(w\times h) }. $$

Volume

The volume of a cuboid is:

$$ \boxed{ V=\ell wh }. $$

This is the product of three mutually perpendicular dimensions.

Surface Area

The total surface area is the combined area of all six faces:

$$ SA = 2\ell w+2\ell h+2wh. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ SA=2(\ell w+\ell h+wh) }. $$

Lateral Surface Area

If the $\ell\times w$ faces are designated as bases, the lateral faces have total area:

$$ 2\ell h+2wh. $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ LA=2h(\ell+w) }. $$

Total Edge Length

Since four edges occur in each of the three dimensions:

$$ \boxed{ L_{\text{edges}} = 4(\ell+w+h) }. $$

Face Diagonals

The three types of rectangular face diagonals are:

$$ \boxed{ d_{\ell w}=\sqrt{\ell^2+w^2} } $$
$$ \boxed{ d_{\ell h}=\sqrt{\ell^2+h^2} } $$
$$ \boxed{ d_{wh}=\sqrt{w^2+h^2} }. $$

Space Diagonal

A space diagonal joins opposite vertices through the interior of the cuboid. Applying the Pythagorean theorem twice gives:

$$ d^2 = \ell^2+w^2+h^2. $$

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ d=\sqrt{\ell^2+w^2+h^2} }. $$

Number of Space Diagonals

A cuboid has four pairs of opposite vertices, so it has:

$$ \boxed{4} $$

space diagonals.

All four have the same length and intersect at the center of the cuboid.

Coordinate Representation

An axis-aligned cuboid may be represented by:

$$ \boxed{ 0\leq x\leq\ell,\qquad 0\leq y\leq w,\qquad 0\leq z\leq h }. $$

Its vertices are:

$$ (0,0,0),\; (\ell,0,0),\; (0,w,0),\; (0,0,h), $$ $$ (\ell,w,0),\; (\ell,0,h),\; (0,w,h),\; (\ell,w,h). $$

Centered Coordinate Representation

A cuboid centered at the origin with half-dimensions $a$, $b$, and $c$ can be written:

$$ \boxed{ |x|\leq a,\qquad |y|\leq b,\qquad |z|\leq c }. $$

Its eight vertices are all sign combinations:

$$ \boxed{ (\pm a,\pm b,\pm c) }. $$

Center

For the axis-aligned cuboid:

$$ [0,\ell]\times[0,w]\times[0,h], $$

the center is:

$$ \boxed{ \left( \frac{\ell}{2}, \frac{w}{2}, \frac{h}{2} \right) }. $$

Distance from Center to a Vertex

Since a space diagonal passes through the center, the distance from the center to any vertex is half the space diagonal:

$$ \boxed{ R = \frac12 \sqrt{\ell^2+w^2+h^2} }. $$

This is also the radius of the sphere passing through all eight vertices.

Circumsphere

Every rectangular cuboid has a circumscribed sphere centered at its center. Its radius is:

$$ \boxed{ R= \frac{\sqrt{\ell^2+w^2+h^2}}{2} }. $$

Insphere

A general cuboid does not have a sphere tangent to all six faces unless its three dimensions are equal.

A sphere tangent to both faces in each opposite pair would require:

$$ 2r=\ell,\qquad 2r=w,\qquad 2r=h. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \ell=w=h } $$

is necessary, so only the cube among rectangular cuboids has an insphere tangent to all six faces.

Euler's Formula

A cuboid satisfies Euler's formula for convex polyhedra:

$$ V-E+F=2. $$

Indeed:

$$ \boxed{ 8-12+6=2 }. $$

Dihedral Angles

Every pair of adjacent faces meets at a right angle:

$$ \boxed{ 90^\circ=\frac{\pi}{2} }. $$

Cube as a Special Cuboid

If:

$$ \ell=w=h=s, $$

the formulas reduce to those of a cube:

$$ V=s^3, $$
$$ SA=6s^2, $$
$$ d=s\sqrt3. $$

Square Prism as a Special Cuboid

If two dimensions are equal, for example:

$$ \ell=w\neq h, $$

the cuboid is a right square prism.

Scaling

If all dimensions are multiplied by a common scale factor $k$, then:

$$ \boxed{ SA\longrightarrow k^2SA } $$

and:

$$ \boxed{ V\longrightarrow k^3V }. $$

Similarity

Two cuboids are similar when their corresponding dimensions are proportional:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\ell_1}{\ell_2} = \frac{w_1}{w_2} = \frac{h_1}{h_2} }. $$

Congruence

Two rectangular cuboids are congruent if their three corresponding edge dimensions agree, allowing for permutation of the dimensions:

$$ \boxed{ \{\ell_1,w_1,h_1\} = \{\ell_2,w_2,h_2\} } $$

as multisets of edge lengths.

Common Misconception: A cuboid need not be a cube. A cube requires all three dimensions to be equal; a general cuboid may have three different dimensions.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$V=\ell wh$ "Volume equals length times width times height."
$SA=2(\ell w+\ell h+wh)$ "Surface area equals twice the sum of length times width, length times height, and width times height."
$d=\sqrt{\ell^2+w^2+h^2}$ "The space diagonal equals the square root of length squared plus width squared plus height squared."
$0\leq x\leq\ell$ "X lies between zero and length, inclusive."
$(\pm a,\pm b,\pm c)$ "All sign combinations of plus or minus a, plus or minus b, and plus or minus c."
$\ell=w=h$ "Length, width, and height are equal."
$V-E+F=2$ "Vertices minus edges plus faces equals two."

In elementary geometry, the terms:

$$ \boxed{ \text{cuboid} \approx \text{rectangular prism} } $$

are usually used for the same solid.

The term rectangular parallelepiped is another more formal name for this shape:

$$ \boxed{ \text{rectangular parallelepiped} = \text{rectangular cuboid} }. $$

A cube should be described as a special cuboid, not as a contrasting unrelated shape:

$$ \boxed{ \text{cube} \subset \text{cuboids} }. $$

Defense Notation Advice: Because some advanced sources use cuboid more broadly, specify rectangular cuboid or rectangular prism whenever right angles and rectangular faces are essential to an argument.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Elementary Definition: A cuboid is a three-dimensional rectangular prism with six rectangular faces.
  • Alternative Name: $$ \boxed{ \text{cuboid} = \text{rectangular prism} } $$ in standard elementary usage.
  • Formal Alternative: A rectangular cuboid may also be called a rectangular parallelepiped.
  • Faces: $$ \boxed{6}. $$
  • Edges: $$ \boxed{12}. $$
  • Vertices: $$ \boxed{8}. $$
  • Opposite Faces: Opposite faces are parallel and congruent.
  • Adjacent Faces: Adjacent faces lie in perpendicular planes.
  • Edge Classes: A cuboid has four edges of length $\ell$, four of length $w$, and four of length $h$.
  • Volume: $$ \boxed{ V=\ell wh }. $$
  • Surface Area: $$ \boxed{ SA=2(\ell w+\ell h+wh) }. $$
  • Lateral Area: If $\ell\times w$ is the base: $$ \boxed{ LA=2h(\ell+w) }. $$
  • Total Edge Length: $$ \boxed{ 4(\ell+w+h) }. $$
  • Space Diagonal: $$ \boxed{ d=\sqrt{\ell^2+w^2+h^2} }. $$
  • Number of Space Diagonals: $$ \boxed{4}. $$
  • Face Diagonal on an $\ell\times w$ Face: $$ \boxed{ \sqrt{\ell^2+w^2} }. $$
  • Face Diagonal on an $\ell\times h$ Face: $$ \boxed{ \sqrt{\ell^2+h^2} }. $$
  • Face Diagonal on a $w\times h$ Face: $$ \boxed{ \sqrt{w^2+h^2} }. $$
  • Center: All four space diagonals intersect at the center and bisect one another.
  • Circumsphere Radius: $$ \boxed{ R= \frac12\sqrt{\ell^2+w^2+h^2} }. $$
  • Insphere Condition: A sphere tangent to all six faces exists only when: $$ \boxed{ \ell=w=h }, $$ so the rectangular cuboid is a cube.
  • Euler Formula: $$ \boxed{ 8-12+6=2 }. $$
  • Dihedral Angles: Every interior dihedral angle is: $$ \boxed{90^\circ}. $$
  • Axis-Aligned Coordinate Form: $$ \boxed{ [0,\ell]\times[0,w]\times[0,h] }. $$
  • Centered Form: $$ \boxed{ [-a,a]\times[-b,b]\times[-c,c] }. $$
  • Centered Vertices: $$ \boxed{ (\pm a,\pm b,\pm c) }. $$
  • Cube Relationship: $$ \boxed{ \ell=w=h \Longrightarrow \text{cuboid is a cube} }. $$
  • Square Prism Relationship: If exactly two dimensions are equal, the cuboid is also a right square prism.
  • Rectangular Prism Relationship: Every rectangular cuboid is a right prism whose bases are rectangles.
  • Parallelepiped Relationship: Every rectangular cuboid is a parallelepiped, but a general parallelepiped need not have right angles.
  • Convexity: A rectangular cuboid is a convex polyhedron.
  • Cross Section Parallel to a Face: A plane parallel to one face produces a rectangle congruent in shape to that face.
  • Diagonal Cross Sections: Suitable planes passing through opposite edges or vertices can produce rectangular or other polygonal cross sections.
  • Net: A cuboid can be unfolded into a planar net consisting of six rectangles.
  • Measurement: Cuboids model boxes, rooms, containers, bricks, packages, and other rectangular three-dimensional objects.
  • Capacity: Container capacity is often computed from: $$ V=\ell wh. $$
  • Packaging: Surface area calculations determine material required to cover a closed rectangular box.
  • Dimensional Analysis: If $\ell$, $w$, and $h$ are measured in meters, then: $$ V $$ is measured in: $$ \text{m}^3. $$
  • Scaling: Scaling all three dimensions by $k$ multiplies volume by: $$ \boxed{k^3}. $$
  • Surface-Area Scaling: Scaling all three dimensions by $k$ multiplies surface area by: $$ \boxed{k^2}. $$
  • Similar Cuboids: Two cuboids are similar when their three corresponding dimensions have a common scale factor.
  • Congruent Cuboids: Two cuboids are congruent when their corresponding dimensions are equal, up to reorientation.
  • Optimization: Cuboid dimensions frequently appear in optimization problems involving maximum volume, minimum surface area, or material constraints.
  • Fixed Volume: Among rectangular cuboids of a fixed volume, imposing additional geometric constraints can lead to surface-area minimization problems.
  • Cube Optimization: Among rectangular cuboids of fixed volume with freely varying positive dimensions, the cube minimizes total surface area.
  • Three-Dimensional Pythagorean Theorem: The space diagonal formula: $$ d^2=\ell^2+w^2+h^2 $$ is a direct three-dimensional extension of the Pythagorean theorem.
  • Vector Interpretation: The vector: $$ \langle\ell,w,h\rangle $$ from one vertex to the opposite vertex has magnitude: $$ \sqrt{\ell^2+w^2+h^2}. $$
  • Topology: As a solid region, every cuboid is homeomorphic to a closed $3$-ball, despite its flat faces and sharp edges.
  • Higher-Dimensional Analogue: The natural higher-dimensional analogue is an orthotope, sometimes called a hyperrectangle.
  • Three-Dimensional Orthotope: A rectangular cuboid is precisely the three-dimensional case of an orthotope.
  • Terminological Variation: Some sources use cuboid broadly for cube-like polyhedra. When precision is important, rectangular cuboid or rectangular prism avoids ambiguity.
  • Terminological Contrast: A cube is a cuboid with: $$ \ell=w=h. $$ A general cuboid permits unequal dimensions: $$ \ell,\;w,\;h. $$
  • Core Interpretation: A rectangular cuboid is the three-dimensional form determined by three mutually perpendicular lengths: $$ \boxed{ V=\ell wh,\qquad SA=2(\ell w+\ell h+wh),\qquad d=\sqrt{\ell^2+w^2+h^2} }. $$

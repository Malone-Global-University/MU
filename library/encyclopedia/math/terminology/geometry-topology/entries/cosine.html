cos

Subfield: Trigonometry

Etymology & Roots

cos is the standard abbreviation and function notation for cosine, one of the six principal trigonometric functions. It is commonly written:

$$ \boxed{\cos\theta}. $$

The word cosine developed historically from the idea of the sine of the complement. If two acute angles are complementary, then the cosine of one equals the sine of the other:

$$ \boxed{ \cos\theta = \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta\right) }. $$

In degree measure:

$$ \cos\theta=\sin(90^\circ-\theta). $$

The prefix co- in cosine reflects this historical relationship with the complementary angle.

In modern mathematics, cos is treated as the name of a function rather than as the product of three variables $c$, $o$, and $s$. Proper mathematical typesetting therefore renders it as an upright function name:

$$ \cos x $$

rather than:

$$ c\cdot o\cdot s\cdot x. $$

Operational Nuance

Right-Triangle Definition

For an acute angle $\theta$ in a right triangle, cosine is the ratio of the length of the side adjacent to the angle to the length of the hypotenuse:

$$ \boxed{ \cos\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} }. $$

This relationship forms the CAH portion of the familiar mnemonic SOHCAHTOA:

$$ \boxed{ \text{CAH}: \quad \cos\theta = \frac{\text{Adjacent}}{\text{Hypotenuse}} }. $$

Example: Right Triangle

If the side adjacent to $\theta$ has length $4$ and the hypotenuse has length $5$, then:

$$ \cos\theta=\frac45. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{\cos\theta=0.8}. $$

Unit-Circle Definition

The unit-circle definition extends cosine beyond acute angles. If an angle $\theta$ determines the point:

$$ P=(x,y) $$

on the unit circle:

$$ x^2+y^2=1, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{x=\cos\theta}, $$

and:

$$ \boxed{y=\sin\theta}. $$

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ P=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta) }. $$

In the unit-circle interpretation, cosine is the horizontal coordinate of the point associated with an angle.

Domain and Range

The real cosine function is defined for every real number:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Dom}(\cos)=\mathbb{R} }. $$

Its values always lie between $-1$ and $1$:

$$ \boxed{ -1\le\cos x\le1 }. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(\cos)=[-1,1] }. $$

Periodic Behavior

Cosine is periodic with fundamental period $2\pi$:

$$ \boxed{ \cos(x+2\pi)=\cos x }. $$

In degrees:

$$ \cos(\theta+360^\circ)=\cos\theta. $$

Even Function

Cosine is an even function:

$$ \boxed{ \cos(-x)=\cos x }. $$

Consequently, the graph of:

$$ y=\cos x $$

is symmetric about the $y$-axis.

Zeros of Cosine

Cosine equals zero at odd multiples of $\frac{\pi}{2}$:

$$ \boxed{ \cos x=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=\frac{\pi}{2}+k\pi, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$

Maximum and Minimum

The maximum value of cosine is:

$$ 1, $$

attained when:

$$ x=2k\pi. $$

The minimum value is:

$$ -1, $$

attained when:

$$ x=(2k+1)\pi. $$

Cosine and Sine

Cosine and sine are connected through the fundamental Pythagorean identity:

$$ \boxed{ \sin^2x+\cos^2x=1 }. $$

They are also phase-shifted versions of one another:

$$ \boxed{ \cos x = \sin\left(x+\frac{\pi}{2}\right) }. $$

Cosine and Secant

The reciprocal trigonometric function associated with cosine is secant:

$$ \boxed{ \sec x=\frac{1}{\cos x} }. $$

Secant is therefore undefined wherever:

$$ \cos x=0. $$

Cosine and Tangent

Tangent can be defined in terms of sine and cosine:

$$ \boxed{ \tan x=\frac{\sin x}{\cos x} }. $$

Consequently, tangent is undefined when $\cos x=0$.

Function Notation

The expression:

$$ \cos x $$

means that the cosine function is evaluated at $x$. It does not mean multiplication by a variable called cos.

Common Misconception: In:

$$ \cos^2x, $$

the exponent applies to the value of the cosine:

$$ \boxed{ \cos^2x=(\cos x)^2 }. $$

It does not mean:

$$ \cos(\cos x). $$

The preferred mathematical typesetting treats cos as a named function and therefore prints it upright rather than in ordinary italic variable notation.

In LaTeX and MathJax, this is achieved with:

$$ \verb|\cos x| $$

which renders as:

$$ \cos x. $$

Parentheses are optional in many mathematical contexts:

$$ \cos x $$

and:

$$ \cos(x) $$

ordinarily mean the same thing.

Parentheses are especially useful when the argument is complicated:

$$ \cos(2x+\phi). $$

Defense Notation Advice: The notation:

$$ \cos^{-1}x $$

conventionally means the inverse cosine function, not:

$$ \frac{1}{\cos x}. $$

The reciprocal of cosine is:

$$ \boxed{\sec x}. $$

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Right-Triangle Trigonometry: For an acute angle: $$ \boxed{ \cos\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} }. $$
  • Unit Circle: The point determined by $\theta$ is: $$ \boxed{ (\cos\theta,\sin\theta) }. $$ Thus cosine gives the horizontal coordinate.
  • Range: For real $x$: $$ \boxed{-1\le\cos x\le1}. $$
  • Period: Cosine repeats every $2\pi$ radians: $$ \cos(x+2\pi)=\cos x. $$
  • Even Symmetry: $$ \boxed{\cos(-x)=\cos x}. $$
  • Pythagorean Identity: $$ \boxed{\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1}. $$
  • Cofunction Identity: $$ \boxed{ \cos\theta = \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta\right) }. $$
  • Angle Addition Formula: $$ \boxed{ \cos(\alpha+\beta) = \cos\alpha\cos\beta - \sin\alpha\sin\beta }. $$
  • Angle Subtraction Formula: $$ \boxed{ \cos(\alpha-\beta) = \cos\alpha\cos\beta + \sin\alpha\sin\beta }. $$
  • Double-Angle Formula: $$ \boxed{ \cos(2x) = \cos^2x-\sin^2x }. $$ Equivalent forms include: $$ \cos(2x)=2\cos^2x-1 $$ and: $$ \cos(2x)=1-2\sin^2x. $$
  • Half-Angle Formula: $$ \boxed{ \cos^2\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) = \frac{1+\cos x}{2} }. $$
  • Law of Cosines: In a triangle with sides $a$, $b$, and $c$ opposite angles $A$, $B$, and $C$: $$ \boxed{ c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C }. $$
  • Dot Product: For nonzero vectors $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$: $$ \boxed{ \mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = \|\mathbf{u}\| \|\mathbf{v}\| \cos\theta }. $$ Thus cosine measures angular alignment between vectors.
  • Angle Between Vectors: Rearranging the dot-product formula gives: $$ \boxed{ \cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}} {\|\mathbf{u}\|\|\mathbf{v}\|} }. $$
  • Orthogonal Vectors: If: $$ \theta=\frac{\pi}{2}, $$ then: $$ \cos\theta=0, $$ so: $$ \mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=0. $$
  • Projection: The scalar projection of $\mathbf{u}$ onto $\mathbf{v}$ involves: $$ \|\mathbf{u}\|\cos\theta. $$
  • Coordinate Geometry: A point at distance $r$ and angle $\theta$ from the origin has horizontal coordinate: $$ \boxed{x=r\cos\theta}. $$
  • Polar Coordinates: Conversion from polar to Cartesian coordinates uses: $$ x=r\cos\theta,\qquad y=r\sin\theta. $$
  • Parametric Circle: A circle of radius $r$ centered at the origin can be parameterized as: $$ \boxed{ x=r\cos t,\qquad y=r\sin t }. $$
  • Harmonic Motion: Oscillatory motion may be modeled by: $$ x(t)=A\cos(\omega t+\phi), $$ where $A$ is amplitude, $\omega$ is angular frequency, and $\phi$ is phase.
  • Wave Models: Cosine functions model periodic phenomena such as sound, vibration, electromagnetic waves, tides, and alternating electrical signals.
  • Calculus — Derivative: $$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}\cos x=-\sin x }. $$
  • Calculus — Antiderivative: $$ \boxed{ \int\cos x\,dx=\sin x+C }. $$
  • Second Derivative: $$ \frac{d^2}{dx^2}\cos x=-\cos x. $$ Thus cosine satisfies: $$ y''+y=0. $$
  • Power Series: Cosine has the Maclaurin expansion: $$ \boxed{ \cos x = 1-\frac{x^2}{2!} +\frac{x^4}{4!} -\frac{x^6}{6!} +\cdots }. $$
  • Infinite-Series Form: $$ \boxed{ \cos x = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n\frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!} }. $$
  • Complex Exponentials: Euler's formula gives: $$ e^{ix}=\cos x+i\sin x. $$ Therefore: $$ \boxed{ \cos x = \frac{e^{ix}+e^{-ix}}{2} }. $$
  • Fourier Analysis: Cosine functions form fundamental building blocks for representing periodic signals and functions through Fourier series and Fourier transforms.
  • Inverse Cosine: The inverse of cosine after restricting its domain appropriately is: $$ \arccos x $$ or: $$ \cos^{-1}x. $$ Its standard real domain and range are: $$ -1\le x\le1, $$ $$ 0\le\arccos x\le\pi. $$
  • Reciprocal: The reciprocal of cosine is secant: $$ \boxed{ \sec x=\frac1{\cos x} }. $$
  • Terminological Contrast: cos is the standard abbreviation and symbolic function name for cosine. It is not a separate mathematical operation from cosine; the two terms refer to the same trigonometric function.
  • Core Interpretation: Cosine connects several equivalent viewpoints: $$ \boxed{ \begin{array}{c} \text{right-triangle ratio}\\ \updownarrow\\ \text{unit-circle }x\text{-coordinate}\\ \updownarrow\\ \text{periodic function}\\ \updownarrow\\ \text{vector angular relationship} \end{array} }. $$

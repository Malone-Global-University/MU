Area of a Regular Polygon

Subfield: Geometry, Spatial Analysis & Topology

Etymology & Roots

The area of a regular polygon is the measure of the two-dimensional region enclosed by a polygon whose sides are all congruent and whose interior angles are all congruent.

Because a regular polygon has a well-defined center, it can be divided into congruent isosceles triangles by joining the center to each vertex. This symmetry produces the standard area formula:

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac12Pa }. $$

Here:

  • $P$ is the perimeter of the polygon,
  • $a$ is the apothem, the perpendicular distance from the center to a side.

If the regular polygon has $n$ sides of length $s$, then:

$$ \boxed{ P=ns }. $$

Combining these relationships leads to several equivalent area formulas involving side length, apothem, circumradius, or the number of sides.

Operational Nuance

Standard Formula

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac12Pa }. $$

The formula is especially convenient when the perimeter and apothem are known.

Why the Formula Works

A regular $n$-gon can be divided into $n$ congruent isosceles triangles by drawing segments from the center to each vertex.

Each triangle has base $s$ and height $a$, so each has area:

$$ A_{\triangle}=\frac12sa. $$

Since there are $n$ such triangles:

$$ A = n\left(\frac12sa\right). $$

Therefore:

$$ A = \frac12nsa. $$

Since $P=ns$:

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac12Pa }. $$

Central Angle

The full rotation around the center is $2\pi$ radians, so each central triangle has central angle:

$$ \boxed{ \theta=\frac{2\pi}{n} }. $$

In degrees:

$$ \boxed{ \theta=\frac{360^\circ}{n} }. $$

Half-Central Angle

Bisecting one of the central isosceles triangles creates two right triangles with angle:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\pi}{n} }. $$

Apothem from Side Length

In one of these right triangles:

$$ \tan\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right) = \frac{s/2}{a}. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ a= \frac{s} {2\tan(\pi/n)} }. $$

Area from Number of Sides and Side Length

Substitute:

$$ P=ns $$

and:

$$ a= \frac{s} {2\tan(\pi/n)} $$

into:

$$ A=\frac12Pa. $$

This gives:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac{ns^2} {4\tan(\pi/n)} }. $$

Cotangent Form

Since:

$$ \cot x=\frac1{\tan x}, $$

the same formula may be written:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac14ns^2 \cot\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right) }. $$

Example: Regular Hexagon

A regular hexagon has:

$$ n=6. $$

Using:

$$ A= \frac{ns^2} {4\tan(\pi/n)}, $$

we obtain:

$$ A = \frac{6s^2} {4\tan(\pi/6)}. $$

Since:

$$ \tan\frac{\pi}{6} = \frac1{\sqrt3}, $$

this simplifies to:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac{3\sqrt3}{2}s^2 }. $$

Equilateral Triangle as a Special Case

For:

$$ n=3, $$

the regular-polygon formula becomes:

$$ A= \frac{3s^2} {4\tan(\pi/3)}. $$

Since:

$$ \tan\frac{\pi}{3}=\sqrt3, $$

we obtain:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac{\sqrt3}{4}s^2 }. $$

Square as a Special Case

For:

$$ n=4, $$

the formula gives:

$$ A= \frac{4s^2} {4\tan(\pi/4)}. $$

Since:

$$ \tan\frac{\pi}{4}=1, $$

we recover:

$$ \boxed{ A=s^2 }. $$

Area from Apothem Alone

From:

$$ \tan\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right) = \frac{s}{2a}, $$

solve for $s$:

$$ s= 2a\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right). $$

Since $P=ns$:

$$ P= 2na\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right). $$

Then:

$$ A = \frac12Pa = \boxed{ na^2\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right) }. $$

Inradius

For a regular polygon, the apothem is also the radius of the inscribed circle. Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ a=r }. $$

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ A=\frac12Pr }. $$

Area from Circumradius

Let $R$ be the circumradius, the distance from the center to any vertex.

Each central triangle has two sides of length $R$ with included angle:

$$ \frac{2\pi}{n}. $$

Its area is:

$$ \frac12R^2 \sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right). $$

Multiplying by $n$:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac12nR^2 \sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right) }. $$

Side Length from Circumradius

The side length is:

$$ \boxed{ s= 2R\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right) }. $$

Apothem from Circumradius

The apothem satisfies:

$$ \boxed{ a= R\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right) }. $$

Right-Triangle Relationship

The circumradius, apothem, and half-side form a right triangle:

$$ \boxed{ R^2 = a^2+ \left(\frac{s}{2}\right)^2 }. $$

Solving for the Apothem from Area

From:

$$ A=\frac12Pa, $$

we obtain:

$$ \boxed{ a=\frac{2A}{P} }. $$

Solving for Perimeter from Area

$$ \boxed{ P=\frac{2A}{a} }. $$

Triangulation Interpretation

The regular-polygon area formula is fundamentally a triangulation formula:

$$ \boxed{ A= \sum_{i=1}^{n} A_{\triangle_i} }. $$

Regularity makes these triangles congruent, allowing the sum to collapse into one simple expression.

Scaling

If every linear dimension is multiplied by $k$:

$$ s\mapsto ks, \qquad a\mapsto ka, \qquad R\mapsto kR, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ A\mapsto k^2A }. $$

Doubling Side Length

$$ \boxed{ s\mapsto2s \Rightarrow A\mapsto4A }. $$

Area and Perimeter Scale Differently

Under linear scaling by $k$:

$$ P\mapsto kP $$

but:

$$ A\mapsto k^2A. $$

This distinction is fundamental to similarity and dimensional analysis.

Coordinate Geometry

A regular $n$-gon centered at the origin with circumradius $R$ can be represented by vertices:

$$ \boxed{ \left( R\cos\frac{2\pi k}{n}, R\sin\frac{2\pi k}{n} \right), \qquad k=0,1,\ldots,n-1 }. $$

Applying the shoelace formula to these cyclically ordered vertices produces:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac12nR^2 \sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right) }. $$

Increasing the Number of Sides

A regular polygon inscribed in a fixed circle more closely approximates that circle as the number of sides increases.

For circumradius $R$:

$$ A_n= \frac12nR^2 \sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right). $$

As:

$$ n\to\infty, $$

use:

$$ \lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1. $$

Then:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{n\to\infty}A_n = \pi R^2 }. $$

Thus the areas of regular polygons inscribed in a circle approach the area of the circle.

Circle Connection through Perimeter and Apothem

As the number of sides becomes very large:

$$ P\to2\pi R $$

and:

$$ a\to R. $$

Therefore:

$$ \frac12Pa \to \frac12(2\pi R)(R) = \boxed{ \pi R^2 }. $$

Regularity Is Essential

The formula:

$$ A=\frac12Pa $$

assumes a single common apothem and a regular arrangement of sides around a center. It is not a general formula for arbitrary polygons.

Irregular polygons may require triangulation, coordinate methods, or other decomposition techniques.

Number of Sides Alone Does Not Determine Area

Knowing that a figure is a regular pentagon, hexagon, or octagon does not determine its numerical area without at least one length measurement.

$$ \boxed{ n\text{ alone does not determine area} }. $$

Perimeter Alone with Known n

For a regular $n$-gon, perimeter does determine side length:

$$ s=\frac{P}{n}. $$

Substitution into the side-length formula gives:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac{P^2} {4n\tan(\pi/n)} }. $$

Area from Perimeter and n

Equivalently:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac{P^2}{4n} \cot\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right) }. $$

Units

Since area multiplies two length dimensions:

$$ \boxed{ A\text{ is measured in square units} }. $$

If side length and apothem are measured in meters, area is measured in square meters.

Common Misconception: The apothem is not generally the same as the circumradius. The apothem reaches the midpoint of a side perpendicularly, while the circumradius reaches a vertex.

$$ \boxed{ a= R\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right) }. $$

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$A=\frac12Pa$ "Area equals one-half the perimeter times the apothem."
$P=ns$ "Perimeter equals the number of sides times the side length."
$A=\frac{ns^2}{4\tan(\pi/n)}$ "Area equals n s squared divided by four tangent pi over n."
$A=\frac14ns^2\cot(\pi/n)$ "Area equals one-fourth n s squared cotangent pi over n."
$A=\frac12nR^2\sin(2\pi/n)$ "Area equals one-half n R squared sine two pi over n."
$a=R\cos(\pi/n)$ "The apothem equals R cosine pi over n."

The letter $a$ in the formula $A=\frac12Pa$ usually denotes the apothem, not area. Context distinguishes the lowercase $a$ from the uppercase $A$ conventionally used for area.

Defense Notation Advice: Verify that the polygon is regular before using the apothem formula. If only the side length is known, identify the number of sides $n$ so the apothem or equivalent trigonometric factor can be determined.

  • Square Units: Area is measured in units such as $\text{cm}^2$, $\text{m}^2$, and $\text{ft}^2$.
  • Terminological Contrast: Side length measures one edge. Perimeter is the total boundary length. Apothem is the perpendicular center-to-side distance. Circumradius is the center-to-vertex distance. The symmetry of a regular polygon ties these measurements together and makes several exact area formulas possible.

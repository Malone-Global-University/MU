Rectangular Parallelepiped

Subfield: Geometry & Topology

Etymology & Roots

A rectangular parallelepiped is a three-dimensional solid whose six faces are rectangles and whose adjacent edges meet at right angles. It is also commonly called a rectangular prism or cuboid.

If its three mutually perpendicular edge lengths are $a$, $b$, and $c$, the solid may be viewed as the Cartesian product:

$$ \boxed{ [0,a]\times[0,b]\times[0,c] }. $$

The term parallelepiped identifies a solid bounded by six parallelograms. The adjective rectangular imposes the stronger condition that all six faces are rectangles.

$$ \boxed{ \text{rectangular parallelepiped} \subset \text{parallelepipeds} }. $$

A cube is a special rectangular parallelepiped in which all three dimensions are equal.

Operational Nuance

Dimensions

Let the edge lengths be:

$$ \boxed{ a,\ b,\ c>0 }. $$

These are often called length, width, and height, although the labels depend on orientation.

Faces

The six faces occur in three opposite congruent pairs with dimensions:

$$ \boxed{ a\times b, \qquad a\times c, \qquad b\times c }. $$

Edges and Vertices

A rectangular parallelepiped has:

$$ \boxed{ 6\text{ faces}, \quad 12\text{ edges}, \quad 8\text{ vertices} }. $$

Volume

$$ \boxed{ V=abc }. $$

This is the product of the three mutually perpendicular dimensions.

Surface Area

$$ \boxed{ S = 2(ab+ac+bc) }. $$

Each of the three distinct face areas occurs twice.

Space Diagonal

The distance between opposite vertices is:

$$ \boxed{ d = \sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2} }. $$

This follows from applying the Pythagorean theorem in three dimensions.

Face Diagonals

The three possible face-diagonal lengths are:

$$ \boxed{ \sqrt{a^2+b^2}, \qquad \sqrt{a^2+c^2}, \qquad \sqrt{b^2+c^2} }. $$

Coordinate Representation

An axis-aligned rectangular parallelepiped with one vertex at the origin has vertices:

$$ \boxed{ (0,0,0), (a,0,0), (0,b,0), (0,0,c), (a,b,0), (a,0,c), (0,b,c), (a,b,c) }. $$

Center

For the axis-aligned form above, the center is:

$$ \boxed{ \left( \frac{a}{2}, \frac{b}{2}, \frac{c}{2} \right) }. $$

Opposite Vertices and Diagonals

The four space diagonals intersect at the center and bisect one another.

Vector Description

If three mutually perpendicular vectors $\mathbf{u}$, $\mathbf{v}$, and $\mathbf{w}$ determine adjacent edges, then:

$$ \boxed{ \mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{w} = 0 }. $$

The edge lengths are:

$$ \boxed{ a=\|\mathbf{u}\|, \qquad b=\|\mathbf{v}\|, \qquad c=\|\mathbf{w}\| }. $$

Vector Volume

For a general parallelepiped:

$$ V = \left| \mathbf{u}\cdot (\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{w}) \right|. $$

In the rectangular case, perpendicularity reduces this to:

$$ \boxed{ V = \|\mathbf{u}\| \|\mathbf{v}\| \|\mathbf{w}\| = abc }. $$

Cube as a Special Case

If:

$$ a=b=c=s, $$

then the solid is a cube with:

$$ \boxed{ V=s^3, \qquad S=6s^2, \qquad d=s\sqrt{3} }. $$

Euler Characteristic

Its boundary satisfies the polyhedral relation:

$$ \boxed{ V-E+F = 8-12+6 = 2 }. $$

Rectangular Prism Terminology

In elementary geometry, rectangular prism is usually the most common term. Rectangular parallelepiped emphasizes that the solid belongs to the wider parallelepiped family.

Cuboid Terminology

Cuboid is widely used for the same solid, especially in geometry, engineering, and international mathematical usage.

Rectangular Parallelepiped vs. General Parallelepiped

A general parallelepiped may have oblique angles and parallelogram faces. A rectangular parallelepiped requires right angles between adjacent edges and rectangular faces.

Rectangular Parallelepiped vs. Rectangle

A rectangle is two-dimensional. A rectangular parallelepiped is three-dimensional.

Bounding Boxes

In computational geometry and computer graphics, an axis-aligned rectangular parallelepiped is commonly used as a three-dimensional bounding box.

Measurement and Units

Edge lengths use linear units, surface area uses square units, and volume uses cubic units.

$$ \boxed{ [V] = [\text{length}]^3 }. $$

Common Misconception

A rectangular parallelepiped does not need to have all equal edges. Equal edges are required only for the special case of a cube.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$V=abc$ "The volume equals the product of the three edge lengths."
$S=2(ab+ac+bc)$ "The surface area is twice the sum of the three distinct face areas."
$d=\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}$ "The space diagonal is the square root of a squared plus b squared plus c squared."
$[0,a]\times[0,b]\times[0,c]$ "The Cartesian product of three closed intervals."
$\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=0$ "The edge vectors are perpendicular."
$a=b=c$ "All three edge lengths are equal, so the solid is a cube."

The phrase:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"rectangular parallelepiped"} } $$

identifies a parallelepiped whose adjacent edge directions are mutually perpendicular.

The terms:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"rectangular prism"} \quad\text{and}\quad \text{"cuboid"} } $$

commonly refer to the same geometric solid.

Defense Notation Advice: Keep the three dimensions distinct unless the problem explicitly states they are equal. Use $abc$ for volume and $2(ab+ac+bc)$ for total surface area. Distinguish a face diagonal such as $\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ from the space diagonal $\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}$.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Definition: Three-dimensional parallelepiped with six rectangular faces.
  • Common Synonyms: Rectangular prism and cuboid.
  • Dimensions: Three mutually perpendicular edge lengths $a$, $b$, and $c$.
  • Faces: Six rectangles in three congruent opposite pairs.
  • Edges: Twelve.
  • Vertices: Eight.
  • Volume: $V=abc$.
  • Surface Area: $S=2(ab+ac+bc)$.
  • Space Diagonal: $d=\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}$.
  • Face Diagonals: $\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$, $\sqrt{a^2+c^2}$, and $\sqrt{b^2+c^2}$.
  • Cartesian Model: $[0,a]\times[0,b]\times[0,c]$.
  • Center: Midpoint common to all space diagonals.
  • Vector Geometry: Adjacent edge vectors are pairwise orthogonal.
  • Triple Product: General volume formula reduces to $abc$ in the orthogonal case.
  • Cube: Special case with $a=b=c$.
  • Euler Relation: $8-12+6=2$.
  • Coordinate Geometry: Conveniently represented by eight corner coordinates.
  • Computational Geometry: Used as a three-dimensional bounding box.
  • Engineering: Models boxes, rooms, containers, blocks, and components.
  • Measurement: Volume uses cubic units and surface area uses square units.
  • General-Parallelepiped Contrast: General parallelepipeds may be oblique; rectangular ones are orthogonal.
  • Rectangle Contrast: Rectangle is two-dimensional; rectangular parallelepiped is three-dimensional.
  • Common Error: Assuming all three edge lengths must be equal.
  • Common Error: Confusing surface area with volume.
  • Common Error: Using a face-diagonal formula for the space diagonal.
  • Common Error: Omitting one pair of faces in the surface-area calculation.
  • Common Error: Treating every parallelepiped as rectangular.
  • Core Interpretation: A rectangular parallelepiped is the three-dimensional orthogonal box determined by three perpendicular dimensions: $$ \boxed{ a,\ b,\ c }. $$ Its principal measurements are: $$ \boxed{ V=abc, \qquad S=2(ab+ac+bc), \qquad d=\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2} }. $$ It connects elementary solid geometry with Cartesian coordinates, vectors, spatial measurement, computational bounding boxes, and the broader geometry of parallelepipeds.

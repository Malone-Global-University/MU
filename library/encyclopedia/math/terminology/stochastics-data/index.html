Probability & Inference Terminology

An operational dictionary of terms governing random variables, mathematical uncertainty, data modeling transitions, and rigorous inductive proof frameworks.

Subfield Scope

Probability and Statistical Inference provide the mathematical machinery to measure, model, and decode randomness. Probability maps forward, building axiomatic deductive models of systems governed by chance. Statistical inference works in reverse, using empirical data samples to make inductive conclusions about hidden population properties. Precision in this vocabulary prevents flawed experimental claims and ensures algorithmic modeling remains stable across data environments.

Core Stochastic & Data Terminology

  • Stochastic

    From Greek stokhastikos ("aiming at a target or conjectural"). Explore systems, processes, or numerical sequences whose progressions cannot be predicted exactly, modeled instead as a series of random variables over time.

  • Variance

    From Latin variantia ("a shifting or diversity"). Analyze the formal expectation value of the squared deviation of a random variable from its mean, tracking the spread of a probability distribution.

  • Regression

    From Latin regressio ("a turning back"). Investigate the structural estimation of relationships between variables, tracking how a dependent criterion shifts alongside independent predictors.

  • Significance

    From Latin significantia ("force or meaning"). Examine the rigorous threshold metric proving an observed sample effect is highly unlikely to have occurred purely by chance under a null hypothesis.

  • Distribution

    From Latin distributio ("a division or allocation"). Decode the functional mathematical profiles—such as Gaussian, Poisson, or Binomial models—that allocate probabilities across an entire sample space.

  • Expectation

    From Latin expectationem ("an anticipating or awaiting"). Investigate the long-term theoretical average value of a random variable computed over infinite trials, acting as the foundational balance point of a system.

Pedagogical Note

A persistent barrier to accuracy in data modeling is the conflation of descriptive metrics with inductive inference conclusions. Students must maintain clear dividing lines between sample properties (e.g., a sample standard deviation) and true unobserved population parameters to avoid committing deep confirmation biases or misinterpreting hypothesis testing outcomes.

Fixed

Subfield: Foundations & Logic

Etymology & Roots

In mathematics, fixed means held unchanged, specified in advance, or treated as constant while other quantities are allowed to vary. The exact meaning depends on what mathematical object is being described.

For example, in:

$$ f(x)=ax+b, $$

one may say that $a$ and $b$ are fixed while $x$ varies. This means that during the particular discussion, $a$ and $b$ retain assigned values.

The central contrast is:

$$ \boxed{ \text{fixed} \quad\text{vs.}\quad \text{variable} }. $$

A fixed quantity need not be universally constant. It may simply be held constant within a particular problem, proof, calculation, or stage of an argument.

Thus the word is fundamentally contextual: an object may be fixed relative to one operation while remaining variable in a broader family of problems.

Operational Nuance

Fixed Value

A fixed value is a value that is specified and not changed during the current argument.

For example:

$$ \boxed{x=5} $$

fixes $x$ at the value $5$.

Fixed Constant

In an expression such as:

$$ y=3x+7, $$

the numbers $3$ and $7$ are fixed constants while $x$ may vary.

Fixed Parameter

A parameter may vary across an entire family of objects but be fixed while studying one member of the family.

For example:

$$ f_a(x)=ax^2. $$

For a particular function, one may fix $a$ and vary $x$.

Across the family:

$$ \{f_a:a\in\mathbb{R}\}, $$

the parameter $a$ itself varies.

Fixed but Arbitrary

A common phrase in proofs is:

$$ \boxed{ \text{fix an arbitrary }x\in S }. $$

This means choose one unspecified element $x$ of $S$ and hold it fixed while reasoning about it. Because the chosen element was arbitrary, the resulting argument may apply to every element of $S$.

Fixed Does Not Mean Known Numerically

A quantity can be fixed even when its numerical value is not explicitly known.

For example:

$$ \boxed{ \text{Let }a>0\text{ be fixed} } $$

means $a$ remains unchanged during the argument, not that a decimal value for $a$ has been supplied.

Fixed Input

Given a function:

$$ f(x,y), $$

one may hold $y$ fixed and vary $x$.

Symbolically:

$$ \boxed{ y=c } $$

turns the two-variable function into the one-variable function:

$$ g(x)=f(x,c). $$

Fixed Variable

Although the phrase may sound contradictory, a variable can be held fixed temporarily. This means it is treated as constant during a particular operation.

Partial Differentiation

In:

$$ f(x,y), $$

the partial derivative:

$$ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} $$

differentiates with respect to $x$ while treating $y$ as fixed.

Fixed Coefficients

In a polynomial:

$$ P(x)=a_nx^n+\cdots+a_1x+a_0, $$

the coefficients may be regarded as fixed while the input $x$ varies.

Fixed Degree

The phrase polynomials of fixed degree $n$ means that $n$ is held constant while coefficients or other features may vary.

Fixed Base

An exponential function:

$$ f(x)=a^x $$

has a fixed base $a$ and a variable exponent $x$.

Power Function Contrast

A power function:

$$ f(x)=x^p $$

instead has a fixed exponent $p$ and a variable base $x$.

Fixed Point

A fixed point of a function or transformation is an input that is left unchanged by that function:

$$ \boxed{ f(x)=x }. $$

If $c$ satisfies:

$$ f(c)=c, $$

then $c$ is a fixed point of $f$.

Fixed Point Example

Let:

$$ f(x)=x^2. $$

Fixed points satisfy:

$$ x^2=x. $$

Therefore:

$$ x(x-1)=0, $$

and:

$$ \boxed{ x=0,\quad x=1 }. $$

Fixed Point of a Transformation

If a transformation $T$ acts on a set $X$, then $x\in X$ is fixed when:

$$ \boxed{ T(x)=x }. $$

Fixed Point in Geometry

Under a rotation about a center $C$, the center remains unchanged:

$$ \boxed{ T(C)=C }. $$

Thus the center is a fixed point of the rotation.

Fixed Points under Reflection

Every point lying on the axis of reflection is fixed by that reflection.

Fixed Set

For a transformation $T$, the set of all fixed points is:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Fix}(T) = \{x:T(x)=x\} }. $$

Pointwise Fixed

A set $A$ is fixed pointwise by $T$ if:

$$ \boxed{ T(a)=a \quad \text{for every }a\in A }. $$

Setwise Fixed

A set may instead be preserved as a whole:

$$ \boxed{ T(A)=A }. $$

This does not require every point of $A$ to remain in its original position.

Pointwise vs. Setwise Fixing

Pointwise fixing is stronger:

$$ \boxed{ T(a)=a\ \forall a\in A \Longrightarrow T(A)=A }. $$

The converse need not hold.

Fixed Point vs. Invariant Set

A fixed point remains individually unchanged. An invariant set may be mapped into or onto itself even though its individual points move.

Invariant Quantity

A quantity is invariant if its value remains unchanged under a specified transformation.

This is closely related to fixed, but invariant usually emphasizes preservation under an operation rather than a parameter being held constant by convention.

Fixed Distance

Geometry often defines loci using fixed distances.

A circle is the set of points at a fixed distance $r$ from a fixed center $C$:

$$ \boxed{ \{P:d(P,C)=r\} }. $$

Fixed Sum

The phrase fixed sum means that a sum is constrained to equal a prescribed value:

$$ \boxed{ x+y=S } $$

where $S$ is held constant.

Fixed Product

Likewise:

$$ \boxed{ xy=P } $$

describes variables whose product is fixed.

Fixed Ratio

A fixed ratio means:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{y}{x}=k } $$

for a constant $k$, where defined.

Fixed Radius

A circle or sphere has a specified radius $r$ that is fixed for that particular object.

Fixed Angle

In geometry, a construction may require an angle measure to remain fixed while points or lengths vary.

Fixed Endpoint

In optimization, geometry, or calculus, one endpoint may be held fixed while another moves.

Fixed Boundary

Boundary-value problems may prescribe fixed values along a boundary.

Fixed Initial Condition

A differential equation may be studied under a fixed initial condition such as:

$$ \boxed{ y(0)=y_0 }. $$

Fixed Sample Size

In statistics, an experiment may be analyzed for a fixed sample size:

$$ \boxed{n=\text{constant}}. $$

Fixed Probability

In a binomial model, the success probability $p$ may be assumed fixed across repeated trials:

$$ \boxed{ P(X=k) = \binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k} }. $$

Fixed Number of Trials

In a binomial experiment, $n$ is commonly fixed before the experiment begins.

Fixed Effects

In statistics, the specialized phrase fixed effects has a technical meaning distinct from the generic adjective fixed. It contrasts with random effects in statistical modeling.

Fixed Precision

Numerical computation may use a fixed number of decimal places, significant figures, or machine bits.

Fixed-Point Arithmetic

In computing, fixed-point arithmetic represents numbers with a predetermined scale or position of the radix point, contrasting with floating-point representation.

Fixed-Point Iteration

Numerical analysis uses iterations of the form:

$$ \boxed{ x_{n+1}=g(x_n) }. $$

The goal is often to approach a fixed point $r$ satisfying:

$$ \boxed{ g(r)=r }. $$

Fixed Point and Equation Solving

An equation:

$$ f(x)=0 $$

may sometimes be rewritten:

$$ x=g(x), $$

converting root finding into a fixed-point problem.

Fixed Point Theorems

Several major theorems assert conditions under which functions or mappings must possess fixed points.

The generic term fixed in these theorems retains the literal meaning: an object is mapped to itself.

Fixed Point of the Identity

For the identity map:

$$ I(x)=x, $$

every point is fixed:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Fix}(I)=X }. $$

Constant Function Contrast

For:

$$ f(x)=c, $$

the function value is fixed for every input, but the function has a fixed point only at $x=c$ when $c$ belongs to the domain:

$$ f(c)=c. $$

Fixed vs. Constant

Constant usually describes a quantity or function that does not vary over its relevant domain.

Fixed often describes a choice made and then held unchanged during an argument.

Example of the Distinction

In:

$$ f_a(x)=ax, $$

$a$ can vary across the family of functions, yet for one chosen function $a$ is fixed.

Fixed vs. Given

Given means supplied as part of the assumptions or data. Fixed means held unchanged.

A quantity may be both given and fixed, but the terms emphasize different aspects.

Fixed vs. Arbitrary

These words are not opposites in mathematical proof language.

A mathematician may choose an element arbitrarily and then fix it for the duration of the proof.

Fixed vs. Determined

A value may be determined by conditions, whereas a fixed value may simply be prescribed or held unchanged. The mechanisms differ even when both result in a single value.

Fixed vs. Unique

A fixed object need not be unique.

For example, the map:

$$ f(x)=x^2 $$

has two real fixed points:

$$ \boxed{0,\ 1}. $$

Fixed Does Not Imply Universal

A fixed value in one problem may differ from the fixed value chosen in another problem.

Common Misconception: In mathematics, fixed does not necessarily mean permanently or universally constant. It often means that an object has been chosen and is being held unchanged while other objects vary. Likewise, a "fixed point" is not merely a point whose coordinates are known; it is specifically a point mapped to itself by a function or transformation.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)

$f_a(x)=ax$ with fixed $a$ "For a fixed value of a, consider f sub a as a function of x."
$f(c)=c$ "C is a fixed point of f."

A common proof phrase is:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"Fix }x\in X\text{."} } $$

This does not assign a particular numerical value to $x$. It means select one element of $X$ and hold it unchanged while the argument proceeds.

Defense Notation Advice: Specify what is fixed and what is allowed to vary. Statements such as:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"for fixed }a\text{ and variable }x } $$

prevent ambiguity. Also distinguish the ordinary adjective fixed from specialized terms such as fixed point, fixed set, and fixed-point iteration.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • General Meaning: Fixed means held unchanged during the relevant mathematical discussion.
  • Fixed Value: A specified value that is not varied.
  • Fixed Parameter: A parameter held constant while another variable changes.
  • Fixed Constant: A constant treated as unchanged within an expression or model.
  • Fixed but Arbitrary: An arbitrary object may be chosen and then held fixed for a proof.
  • Known vs. Fixed: A fixed value need not be numerically known.
  • Variable Held Fixed: A variable may temporarily be treated as constant.
  • Partial Derivative: Other independent variables are held fixed while differentiating with respect to one variable.
  • Fixed Coefficient: Polynomial coefficients may be held fixed while the input varies.
  • Fixed Degree: Degree remains prescribed while coefficients may vary.
  • Fixed Base: An exponential function has a fixed base and variable exponent.
  • Fixed Exponent: A power function has a variable base and fixed exponent.
  • Fixed Point: $$ \boxed{f(c)=c}. $$
  • Fixed-Point Set: $$ \operatorname{Fix}(f)=\{x:f(x)=x\}. $$
  • Identity Map: Every point is fixed by the identity transformation.
  • Rotation: The center of rotation is fixed.
  • Reflection: Points on the reflection axis are fixed.
  • Pointwise Fixed: Every point of a specified set remains individually unchanged.
  • Setwise Fixed: The set is mapped to itself, though its points may move.
  • Invariant Set: Related notion emphasizing preservation under a map.
  • Fixed Distance: A distance is prescribed and held constant.
  • Circle: Points lie at a fixed radius from a fixed center.
  • Sphere: Points lie at a fixed distance from a center in three-dimensional space.
  • Fixed Sum: $$ x+y=S $$ with constant $S$.
  • Fixed Product: $$ xy=P $$ with constant $P$.
  • Fixed Ratio: $$ y/x=k $$ where defined.
  • Fixed Radius: The radius is prescribed for a particular circle or sphere.
  • Fixed Angle: The angle measure is held unchanged during a construction or argument.
  • Fixed Endpoint: One endpoint remains stationary while another may vary.
  • Fixed Boundary: Boundary conditions prescribe unchanging values or locations.
  • Initial-Value Problem: An initial value such as $y(0)=y_0$ may be fixed.
  • Optimization: Some variables may be fixed while others are optimized.
  • Constraint: A fixed quantity can appear as a constraint parameter.
  • Statistics: Sample size may be fixed in advance.
  • Binomial Model: $n$ and $p$ are treated as fixed parameters while the random count varies.
  • Fixed Effects: A specialized statistical-modeling term distinct from the generic adjective.
  • Probability: A probability parameter may be fixed across repeated trials.
  • Numerical Analysis: Fixed-point iteration seeks a solution of $g(x)=x$.
  • Fixed-Point Iteration: $$ x_{n+1}=g(x_n). $$
  • Convergence: If the iteration converges to $r$ under suitable conditions, typically $g(r)=r$.
  • Equation Solving: Root equations may be rearranged into fixed-point form.
  • Fixed-Point Theorem: A theorem guaranteeing fixed points under specified hypotheses.
  • Topology: Fixed-point theorems study self-maps of topological spaces.
  • Analysis: Contraction mappings lead to important fixed-point results.
  • Dynamical Systems: Fixed points represent states unchanged by the evolution map.
  • Equilibrium: In many dynamical systems, a fixed point represents an equilibrium state.
  • Linear Algebra: A fixed vector under a linear transformation satisfies $Tv=v$.
  • Eigenvalue Connection: A nonzero fixed vector is an eigenvector with eigenvalue $1$.
  • Group Actions: A group element may fix points of the set on which it acts.
  • Stabilizer: The stabilizer of a point consists of group elements that fix that point.
  • Symmetry: Symmetry transformations often possess fixed points, lines, or sets.
  • Computer Arithmetic: Fixed-point representation uses a predetermined numerical scale.
  • Fixed-Point Arithmetic: Contrasts with floating-point arithmetic.
  • Fixed Precision: A calculation may use a prescribed number of digits or bits.
  • Algorithm Analysis: One parameter may be fixed while another tends to infinity.
  • Asymptotics: Phrases such as "for fixed $k$ as $n\to\infty$" specify which parameter remains unchanged.
  • Combinatorics: One may count objects of fixed size $n$ while varying another property.
  • Number Theory: A modulus $m$ may be fixed while integers vary.
  • Modular Arithmetic: In $a\equiv b\pmod m$, the modulus $m$ is often treated as fixed.
  • Sequence Analysis: An index difference $k$ may be fixed while $n$ increases.
  • Parameter Family: A parameter can vary globally but be fixed locally.
  • Fixed vs. Constant: Fixed emphasizes the current choice; constant emphasizes nonvariation.
  • Fixed vs. Given: Given means supplied; fixed means held unchanged.
  • Fixed vs. Arbitrary: An arbitrary element may be fixed after being chosen.
  • Fixed vs. Unique: A problem may contain several fixed points.
  • Fixed vs. Invariant: Invariant emphasizes preservation under an operation; fixed often emphasizes unchanged status or a held parameter.
  • Fixed Point vs. Known Point: A fixed point is defined by $f(x)=x$, not merely by having known coordinates.
  • Context Dependence: A quantity may be fixed in one stage of a problem and variable in another.
  • Proof Language: "Fix $x$" means hold the chosen element constant during the argument.
  • Terminological Core: The word fixed always requires an implicit or explicit question: fixed relative to what variation, operation, or transformation?
  • Core Interpretation: In mathematics, fixed means held unchanged relative to a specified context. A parameter may be fixed while a variable changes, a geometric quantity may be constrained to a fixed value, or an object may be fixed by a transformation: $$ \boxed{ T(x)=x }. $$ The word therefore connects the elementary distinction between constants and variables with deeper concepts such as fixed points, invariant sets, equilibrium states, parameter families, and fixed-point iteration.

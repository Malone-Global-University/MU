Additive Inverse of a Number

Subfield: Foundations & Mathematical Logic

Etymology & Roots

The additive inverse of a number is the unique number that combines with the original number under addition to produce zero.

If the original number is $a$, its additive inverse is:

$$ \boxed{-a}. $$

The defining relationship is:

$$ \boxed{ a+(-a)=0 }. $$

In elementary arithmetic, additive inverses are often called opposites. Thus the opposite of $7$ is $-7$, and the opposite of $-12$ is $12$.

The word inverse indicates that one mathematical action reverses or cancels another. The modifier additive specifies that the relevant operation is addition. This distinguishes an additive inverse from a multiplicative inverse, or reciprocal.

The number zero is special because it is its own additive inverse:

$$ \boxed{-0=0}. $$

Operational Nuance

Basic Definition

For every real number $a$, there exists a unique number $-a$ such that:

$$ \boxed{ a+(-a)=0 }. $$

Because addition is commutative:

$$ (-a)+a=0 $$

as well.

Examples

$$ \boxed{ \begin{array}{ccl} 8 &\longrightarrow& -8,\\[4pt] -3 &\longrightarrow& 3,\\[4pt] \frac{5}{7} &\longrightarrow& -\frac{5}{7},\\[6pt] -\sqrt{2} &\longrightarrow& \sqrt{2},\\[4pt] 0 &\longrightarrow& 0. \end{array} } $$

Number-Line Interpretation

On the real number line, a number and its additive inverse lie the same distance from zero but on opposite sides, unless the number itself is zero.

For example:

$$ |5|=|-5|=5. $$

Thus $5$ and $-5$ have equal absolute value but opposite signs.

Subtraction as Addition of an Additive Inverse

Subtraction can be defined using additive inverses:

$$ \boxed{ a-b=a+(-b) }. $$

For example:

$$ 9-4 = 9+(-4) = 5. $$

Likewise:

$$ 9-(-4) = 9+4 = 13. $$

Double Additive Inverse

Taking the additive inverse twice returns the original number:

$$ \boxed{ -(-a)=a }. $$

This follows because the additive inverse of $-a$ is the number that cancels $-a$, namely $a$.

Negative Sign as an Operation

The symbol:

$$ -a $$

should not always be read as "a negative number." It means the additive inverse of $a$.

If:

$$ a=-6, $$

then:

$$ -a=-(-6)=6, $$

which is positive.

Uniqueness

The additive inverse of a number is unique. Suppose both $b$ and $c$ satisfy:

$$ a+b=0 $$

and:

$$ a+c=0. $$

Then:

$$ b = b+0 = b+(a+c) = (b+a)+c = 0+c = c. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \text{each number has exactly one additive inverse} }. $$

Not the Reciprocal

The additive inverse of $a$ is:

$$ -a, $$

while the multiplicative inverse of a nonzero number is:

$$ \frac{1}{a}. $$

Their defining equations are different:

$$ \boxed{ a+(-a)=0 } $$

versus:

$$ \boxed{ a\left(\frac1a\right)=1 }. $$

Common Misconception: The additive inverse of $5$ is $-5$, not $\frac15$. The first cancels under addition; the second cancels under multiplication.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$-a$ "Negative a," or more precisely, "the additive inverse of a."
$a+(-a)=0$ "A plus its additive inverse equals zero."
$-(-a)=a$ "The additive inverse of the additive inverse of a is a."
$a-b=a+(-b)$ "A minus b equals a plus the additive inverse of b."
$-0=0$ "Zero is its own additive inverse."
$\frac1a$ "The reciprocal of a," or "the multiplicative inverse of a."

The minus sign has several closely related uses. In:

$$ -7, $$

it forms the additive inverse of $7$. In:

$$ -x, $$

it means the additive inverse of whatever value $x$ represents.

In:

$$ a-b, $$

the binary subtraction operation can be interpreted as:

$$ a+(-b). $$

Defense Notation Advice: A leading minus sign does not guarantee that the resulting quantity is negative. The sign denotes an operation:

$$ \boxed{ -a=\text{the additive inverse of }a }. $$

Its actual sign depends on the value of $a$.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Definition: $$ \boxed{ a+(-a)=0 }. $$
  • Opposite Number: Opposite is the common elementary-school term for additive inverse.
  • Positive Number: The additive inverse of a positive number is negative.
  • Negative Number: The additive inverse of a negative number is positive.
  • Zero: $$ \boxed{ -0=0 }. $$ Zero is the only real number equal to its own additive inverse.
  • Integers: Every integer has an integer additive inverse: $$ n\longleftrightarrow -n. $$
  • Rational Numbers: If: $$ q=\frac ab, $$ then: $$ \boxed{ -q=-\frac ab=\frac{-a}{b}=\frac{a}{-b} }. $$
  • Irrational Numbers: Irrational numbers also have additive inverses. For example: $$ \sqrt2\longleftrightarrow-\sqrt2. $$
  • Real Numbers: The real numbers are closed under additive inverses: $$ a\in\mathbb{R} \Longrightarrow -a\in\mathbb{R}. $$
  • Complex Numbers: For: $$ z=a+bi, $$ the additive inverse is: $$ \boxed{ -z=-a-bi }. $$
  • Absolute Value: A number and its additive inverse have the same absolute value: $$ \boxed{ |-a|=|a| }. $$
  • Number-Line Symmetry: $a$ and $-a$ are reflections of one another across zero on the real number line.
  • Sum to Zero: $$ \boxed{ a+(-a)=(-a)+a=0 }. $$
  • Double Inverse: $$ \boxed{ -(-a)=a }. $$
  • Negative of a Sum: $$ \boxed{ -(a+b)=(-a)+(-b) }. $$
  • Negative of a Difference: $$ \boxed{ -(a-b)=b-a }. $$
  • Product with Negative One: $$ \boxed{ -a=(-1)a }. $$
  • Product of Opposites: $$ (-a)(-b)=ab. $$
  • Opposite of a Product: $$ \boxed{ -(ab)=(-a)b=a(-b) }. $$
  • Subtraction: $$ \boxed{ a-b=a+(-b) }. $$ This identity is the structural connection between subtraction and additive inverses.
  • Subtracting a Negative: $$ \boxed{ a-(-b)=a+b }. $$
  • Additive Cancellation: If: $$ a+c=b+c, $$ then adding $-c$ to both sides gives: $$ \boxed{a=b}. $$
  • Solving Equations: For: $$ x+a=b, $$ add $-a$ to both sides: $$ x+a+(-a)=b+(-a), $$ giving: $$ \boxed{ x=b-a }. $$
  • Additive Identity: Zero is the identity element for addition: $$ \boxed{ a+0=a }. $$ Additive inverses are defined relative to this identity.
  • Group Structure: In an additive group, every element $a$ has an inverse $-a$ satisfying: $$ a+(-a)=0. $$ The number systems $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Q}$, $\mathbb{R}$, and $\mathbb{C}$ all have this structure under addition.
  • Vector Generalization: The same idea extends to vectors: $$ \mathbf{v}+(-\mathbf{v})=\mathbf{0}. $$
  • Matrix Generalization: For a matrix $A$: $$ A+(-A)=0. $$ Thus the additive inverse principle applies throughout linear algebra.
  • Polynomial Generalization: The additive inverse of: $$ p(x) $$ is: $$ -p(x), $$ obtained by negating every coefficient.
  • Function Generalization: The additive inverse of a real-valued function $f$ is: $$ (-f)(x)=-f(x). $$
  • Not the Reciprocal: $$ \boxed{ -a\ne\frac1a } $$ in general.
  • Reciprocal Exists Differently: Every real number has an additive inverse, including zero. A multiplicative inverse: $$ \frac1a $$ exists only when: $$ a\ne0. $$
  • Terminological Contrast: The additive inverse of $a$ is $-a$ and produces zero when added to $a$. The multiplicative inverse, or reciprocal, is $1/a$ and produces one when multiplied by $a$, provided $a\ne0$.

Even Number

Subfield: Foundations & Logic

Etymology & Roots

An even number is an integer divisible by $2$ with no remainder.

Equivalently, an integer $n$ is even when there exists an integer $k$ such that:

$$ \boxed{ n=2k, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$

Examples include:

$$ \boxed{ \ldots,-6,-4,-2,0,2,4,6,8,\ldots }. $$

The word even reflects the idea that a collection containing an even number of objects can be divided into two groups of equal whole-number size. For example:

$$ 8=4+4. $$

By contrast, an odd integer leaves a remainder of $1$ when divided by $2$ and can be written:

$$ \boxed{ n=2k+1 } $$

for some integer $k$.

The distinction between even and odd numbers is called parity.

Operational Nuance

Divisibility Definition

An integer $n$ is even exactly when:

$$ \boxed{ 2\mid n }. $$

The notation:

$$ 2\mid n $$

is read "two divides $n$."

Algebraic Form

The standard algebraic representation of an even integer is:

$$ \boxed{ 2k, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$

This form is especially useful in proofs.

Examples

$$ 10=2(5),\qquad -14=2(-7),\qquad 0=2(0). $$

Therefore $10$, $-14$, and $0$ are all even.

Zero Is Even

Since:

$$ \boxed{ 0=2(0) }, $$

zero satisfies the definition of an even integer.

Remainder Characterization

An integer is even exactly when division by $2$ produces remainder $0$.

$$ \boxed{ n=2q+0 }. $$

Modular Characterization

In modular arithmetic:

$$ \boxed{ n\equiv0\pmod2 } $$

exactly when $n$ is even.

Odd Contrast

An odd integer satisfies:

$$ \boxed{ n\equiv1\pmod2 }. $$

Every Integer Has One of Two Parities

Every integer is either even or odd:

$$ \boxed{ n=2k \quad\text{or}\quad n=2k+1 } $$

for some integer $k$.

No integer is simultaneously even and odd.

Last-Digit Test in Base Ten

A base-ten integer is even exactly when its final digit is:

$$ \boxed{ 0,\ 2,\ 4,\ 6,\ \text{or }8 }. $$

This works because every power of $10$ greater than or equal to $10^1$ is divisible by $2$, so only the units digit affects parity.

Proof of the Last-Digit Test

Write an integer:

$$ n=10q+d, $$

where $d$ is the units digit.

Since:

$$ 10q=2(5q), $$

$10q$ is even. Therefore $n$ has the same parity as $d$.

Sum of Two Even Numbers

Let:

$$ a=2m, \qquad b=2n. $$

Then:

$$ a+b = 2m+2n = 2(m+n). $$

Since $m+n$ is an integer:

$$ \boxed{ \text{even}+\text{even} = \text{even} }. $$

Difference of Two Even Numbers

$$ \boxed{ \text{even}-\text{even} = \text{even} }. $$

Sum of Even and Odd

$$ \boxed{ \text{even}+\text{odd} = \text{odd} }. $$

Sum of Two Odd Numbers

If:

$$ a=2m+1, \qquad b=2n+1, $$

then:

$$ a+b = 2m+2n+2 = 2(m+n+1). $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \text{odd}+\text{odd} = \text{even} }. $$

Product Involving an Even Number

If $a=2m$, then for any integer $b$:

$$ ab=2(mb). $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ \text{even}\times\text{integer} = \text{even} }. $$

Even Times Even

$$ \boxed{ \text{even}\times\text{even} = \text{even} }. $$

Even Times Odd

$$ \boxed{ \text{even}\times\text{odd} = \text{even} }. $$

Odd Times Odd

$$ \boxed{ \text{odd}\times\text{odd} = \text{odd} }. $$

Powers of Even Integers

If $n$ is even and $r$ is a positive integer, then:

$$ \boxed{ n^r \text{ is even} }. $$

This follows because $n=2k$, so:

$$ n^r = (2k)^r = 2(2^{r-1}k^r). $$

Square Parity

If $n$ is even, then $n^2$ is even.

Conversely, if $n^2$ is even, then $n$ must be even.

$$ \boxed{ n^2\text{ even} \iff n\text{ even} }. $$

This result is frequently used in proofs concerning irrational numbers and divisibility.

Example: Proof Pattern

Suppose $n$ is even. Then:

$$ n=2k. $$

Squaring:

$$ n^2 = 4k^2 = 2(2k^2). $$

Since $2k^2$ is an integer, $n^2$ is even.

Division Does Not Preserve Evenness

An even integer divided by another integer need not be an even integer.

For example:

$$ \boxed{ \frac62=3 }. $$

Thus "even divided by even is even" is false.

Half of an Even Integer

Every even integer has an integer half:

$$ \boxed{ n\text{ even} \iff \frac n2\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$

The half itself may be even or odd.

Consecutive Even Numbers

Consecutive even integers differ by $2$:

$$ \boxed{ 2k,\quad2k+2,\quad2k+4,\ldots }. $$

Previous and Next Even Number

If $n$ is even, then:

$$ \boxed{ n-2 \quad\text{and}\quad n+2 } $$

are the immediately neighboring even integers.

Parity Alternates Among Consecutive Integers

If $n$ is even, then:

$$ \boxed{ n+1 \text{ is odd} }. $$

If $n$ is odd, then $n+1$ is even.

Two Consecutive Integers

Among any two consecutive integers, exactly one is even:

$$ \boxed{ n,\ n+1 \text{ have opposite parity} }. $$

Product of Consecutive Integers

Because one of $n$ and $n+1$ is even:

$$ \boxed{ n(n+1) \text{ is always even} }. $$

Even Prime Number

The number:

$$ \boxed{2} $$

is the only even prime number.

Every even integer greater than $2$ is divisible by both $1$, $2$, and itself, so it is composite.

Negative Even Integers

Evenness is not restricted to positive integers.

For example:

$$ -8=2(-4), $$

so:

$$ \boxed{-8\text{ is even}}. $$

Fractions Are Not Classified as Even or Odd in the Standard Sense

The usual parity classification applies to integers.

Thus:

$$ \frac12,\qquad 3.7,\qquad \sqrt2 $$

are ordinarily described as neither even nor odd because they are not integers.

Evenness Is About Divisibility, Not Magnitude

A number can be negative, zero, or positive and still be even. Its size is irrelevant; only divisibility by $2$ matters.

Parity Classes Modulo 2

The integers split into exactly two congruence classes modulo $2$:

$$ \boxed{ [0] = \{\text{even integers}\} } $$

and:

$$ \boxed{ [1] = \{\text{odd integers}\} }. $$

Parity in Proof

Evenness is often proved by exhibiting an integer factor:

$$ \boxed{ n=2k }. $$

This is more rigorous than merely checking a few numerical examples.

Parity and Contradiction

Parity arguments are frequently used in proofs by contradiction. If a quantity is forced to be both even and odd, the assumptions leading to that conclusion cannot all be correct.

Parity Invariance

Adding or subtracting an even integer does not change parity:

$$ \boxed{ n \text{ and } n+2k \text{ have the same parity} }. $$

Adding an Odd Integer Changes Parity

Adding an odd integer switches parity:

$$ \boxed{ \text{even}+\text{odd}=\text{odd}, \qquad \text{odd}+\text{odd}=\text{even} }. $$

Binary Representation

In base $2$, an integer is even exactly when its final binary digit is $0$.

$$ \boxed{ \text{even binary integer ends in }0 }. $$

An odd binary integer ends in $1$.

Computer Arithmetic

Because parity corresponds to the least significant binary digit, determining whether an integer is even is especially simple in digital computation.

Even Number vs. Even Function

An even number is an integer divisible by $2$.

An even function satisfies:

$$ f(-x)=f(x). $$

The two uses of even are historically related through even powers but refer to different mathematical properties.

Common Misconception: Zero is not "neither even nor odd." Zero is even because it is an integer multiple of $2$: $0=2\cdot0$. Also, the usual labels even and odd apply to integers, not to arbitrary real numbers.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$n=2k,\ k\in\mathbb{Z}$ "N is twice an integer k."
$2\mid n$ "Two divides n."
$n\equiv0\pmod2$ "N is congruent to zero modulo two."
$n=2k+1$ "N is odd; n equals twice an integer plus one."
$0=2(0)$ "Zero is even because zero is twice zero."
$2k,\ 2k+2,\ 2k+4$ "Three consecutive even integers."
$n(n+1)$ "The product of two consecutive integers, which is always even."

A rigorous way to say that $n$ is even is:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"there exists an integer }k\text{ such that }n=2k" }. $$

The phrase:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"divisible by two"} } $$

means that division by $2$ produces an integer quotient with remainder $0$.

Defense Notation Advice: When proving parity statements, write an even integer explicitly as:

$$ \boxed{2k} $$

with $k\in\mathbb{Z}$, and an odd integer as:

$$ \boxed{2k+1}. $$

This prevents ambiguity and makes divisibility by $2$ visible in the algebra.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Definition: An even number is an integer divisible by $2$.
  • Algebraic Form: $$ \boxed{ n=2k, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$
  • Divisibility Form: $$ \boxed{2\mid n}. $$
  • Modular Form: $$ \boxed{ n\equiv0\pmod2 }. $$
  • Examples: $$ \ldots,-8,-6,-4,-2,0,2,4,6,8,\ldots $$ are even.
  • Zero: $$ \boxed{0\text{ is even}}. $$
  • Reason Zero Is Even: $$ 0=2(0). $$
  • Negative Integers: Negative integers can be even.
  • Example: $$ -12=2(-6). $$
  • Odd Contrast: $$ \boxed{ n=2k+1 } $$ represents an odd integer.
  • Parity: The classification of integers as even or odd is called parity.
  • Exhaustiveness: Every integer is either even or odd.
  • Exclusivity: No integer is both even and odd.
  • Remainder Test: Even integers leave remainder $0$ upon division by $2$.
  • Last-Digit Test: In base ten, an integer ending in $0$, $2$, $4$, $6$, or $8$ is even.
  • Binary Test: In base two, an even integer ends in $0$.
  • Half Test: $$ \boxed{ n\text{ even} \iff n/2\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$
  • Sum of Evens: $$ \boxed{ \text{even}+\text{even} = \text{even} }. $$
  • Difference of Evens: $$ \boxed{ \text{even}-\text{even} = \text{even} }. $$
  • Even Plus Odd: $$ \boxed{ \text{even}+\text{odd} = \text{odd} }. $$
  • Odd Plus Odd: $$ \boxed{ \text{odd}+\text{odd} = \text{even} }. $$
  • Product with Even Factor: Any integer product containing an even factor is even.
  • Even Times Even: $$ \boxed{ \text{even}\cdot\text{even} = \text{even} }. $$
  • Even Times Odd: $$ \boxed{ \text{even}\cdot\text{odd} = \text{even} }. $$
  • Odd Times Odd: $$ \boxed{ \text{odd}\cdot\text{odd} = \text{odd} }. $$
  • Even Powers: Positive integer powers of an even integer remain even.
  • Square Test: $$ \boxed{ n^2\text{ even} \iff n\text{ even} }. $$
  • Division Caution: Division does not obey a simple parity table analogous to addition and multiplication.
  • Counterexample: $$ 6/2=3, $$ so even divided by even need not be even.
  • Consecutive Even Integers: $$ \boxed{ 2k,\ 2k+2,\ 2k+4,\ldots }. $$
  • Spacing: Consecutive even integers differ by $2$.
  • Neighboring Parity: Every integer adjacent to an even integer is odd.
  • Consecutive Integers: Exactly one of $n$ and $n+1$ is even.
  • Consecutive Product: $$ \boxed{ n(n+1) \text{ is even} }. $$
  • Three Consecutive Integers: At least one is even, and in fact either one or two of them are even.
  • Prime Numbers: $$ \boxed{2} $$ is the only even prime.
  • Composite Numbers: Every even integer greater than $2$ is composite.
  • Factor of Two: Every nonzero even integer has $2$ as a factor.
  • Greatest Power of Two: An even integer may be divisible by $2$, $4$, $8$, or higher powers of $2$ depending on its factorization.
  • Example: $$ 24=2^3\cdot3. $$
  • Modulo Two Classes: Even integers form the residue class $[0]$ modulo $2$.
  • Odd Class: Odd integers form the residue class $[1]$ modulo $2$.
  • Additive Closure: Even integers are closed under addition.
  • Subtractive Closure: Even integers are closed under subtraction.
  • Multiplicative Closure: Even integers are closed under multiplication.
  • Division Closure: Even integers are not closed under division.
  • Additive Identity: Zero belongs to the even integers.
  • Additive Inverses: If $n$ is even, then $-n$ is even.
  • Parity Preservation: Adding an even integer does not change parity.
  • Parity Switching: Adding an odd integer switches parity.
  • Subtraction: Subtracting an odd integer also switches parity.
  • Proof Technique: To prove a quantity is even, rewrite it as $2$ times an integer.
  • Direct Proof: If an expression simplifies to: $$ 2(\text{integer}), $$ its evenness follows immediately.
  • Contradiction Proofs: Parity contradictions are useful in number-theoretic arguments.
  • Irrationality Proofs: The relation between the parity of $n$ and $n^2$ appears in classical proofs such as the irrationality of $\sqrt2$.
  • Combinatorics: Parity often determines whether counts or permutations fall into two structural classes.
  • Graph Theory: Even vertex degrees are central to Eulerian-cycle criteria.
  • Handshake Lemma: Every finite graph has an even number of vertices of odd degree.
  • Computer Science: Evenness can be detected from the least significant binary digit.
  • Programming: Integer remainder tests such as division modulo $2$ commonly detect parity.
  • Noninteger Caution: Ordinary fractions, irrational numbers, and noninteger decimals are not normally classified as even or odd.
  • Example: $$ \frac32 $$ is neither even nor odd in the standard integer-parity sense.
  • Magnitude Caution: Evenness is independent of whether an integer is positive, negative, or zero.
  • Even Number vs. Even Function: An even number is divisible by $2$; an even function satisfies $f(-x)=f(x)$.
  • Even Exponent vs. Even Number: An exponent such as $4$ is itself an even integer, while an expression such as $x^4$ need not have an even numerical value for arbitrary noninteger $x$.
  • Terminological Core: Even in elementary number theory means membership in the multiples of $2$.
  • Core Interpretation: An integer is even exactly when it contains a factor of $2$: $$ \boxed{ n=2k, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$ Equivalently: $$ \boxed{ 2\mid n \qquad\text{or}\qquad n\equiv0\pmod2 }. $$ This simple divisibility condition produces the fundamental two-way parity classification of the integers and supports many algebraic, number-theoretic, combinatorial, and computational arguments.

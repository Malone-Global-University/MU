Proper Fraction

Subfield: Foundations & Logic

Etymology & Roots

A proper fraction is, in standard elementary usage, a fraction whose numerator has smaller magnitude than its denominator, so the fraction's magnitude is less than $1$.

$$ \boxed{ \left| \frac{a}{b} \right| < 1 } $$

for integers $a$ and $b$ with $b\neq0$.

In the most common school setting, where both numerator and denominator are taken to be positive, the definition appears as:

$$ \boxed{ 0\leq a<b \Longrightarrow \frac{a}{b} \text{ is proper} }. $$

Examples include:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{1}{2}, \quad \frac{3}{5}, \quad \frac{7}{10} }. $$

The word fraction comes from language associated with breaking, dividing, or forming a part. A fraction such as:

$$ \frac{3}{5} $$

represents three parts when a whole has been partitioned into five equal parts.

The adjective proper historically reflects a classification in which the numerator is smaller than the denominator, distinguishing such fractions from improper fractions, whose numerator is at least as large in magnitude as the denominator under the corresponding convention.

$$ \boxed{ \text{proper fraction} \longleftrightarrow \text{fraction with magnitude below one} }. $$

The terminology describes the form and magnitude of the fraction. It does not mean that improper fractions are mathematically wrong or defective. Both proper and improper fractions are legitimate representations of rational numbers.

Operational Nuance

Standard Positive-Fraction Definition

For positive integers $a$ and $b$:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} \text{ is proper} \iff a<b }. $$

Magnitude-Based Definition

A broader signed convention classifies:

$$ \frac{a}{b} $$

as proper when:

$$ \boxed{ |a|<|b| }. $$

Equivalently:

$$ \boxed{ \left| \frac{a}{b} \right| <1 }. $$

Terminology Convention Caveat

Elementary textbooks often define proper fractions only for positive numerator and denominator values. In that setting, signed examples such as $-3/5$ may not be explicitly classified. When negative fractions are included, the magnitude-based definition is the cleaner generalization.

Numerator

In:

$$ \frac{a}{b}, $$

$a$ is the numerator.

Denominator

The quantity $b$ is the denominator and must satisfy:

$$ \boxed{ b\neq0 }. $$

Positive Proper Fraction

If:

$$ 0<a<b, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ 0<\frac{a}{b}<1 }. $$

Negative Proper Fraction

Under the magnitude convention:

$$ \boxed{ -1 < \frac{a}{b} < 0 } $$

is also proper whenever $|a|<|b|$.

Example

$$ \boxed{ -\frac{3}{7} } $$

has magnitude $3/7<1$ and is therefore proper under a signed magnitude-based convention.

Zero Fraction

For nonzero $b$:

$$ \frac{0}{b}=0. $$

Because:

$$ |0|<|b|, $$

$0/b$ may be included among proper fractions under magnitude-based definitions. Some elementary curricula instead reserve the terminology for positive fractions, so convention should be checked.

Improper Fraction

In the usual positive-integer setting:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} \text{ is improper} \iff a\geq b }. $$

Examples of Improper Fractions

$$ \boxed{ \frac{5}{4}, \quad \frac{8}{3}, \quad \frac{7}{7} }. $$

Fraction Equal to One

If numerator and denominator are equal and nonzero:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{a}=1 }. $$

Under the standard positive convention, this is not a proper fraction because the numerator is not smaller than the denominator.

Proper Fraction and the Unit Interval

Positive proper fractions lie inside:

$$ \boxed{ (0,1) } $$

when zero is excluded.

Number-Line Interpretation

A positive proper fraction represents a point strictly between $0$ and $1$ on the real number line.

Partition Interpretation

For:

$$ \frac{3}{5}, $$

divide one whole into five equal parts and select three of them.

Proper Fraction Does Not Require Simplest Form

The fraction:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{6}{10} } $$

is proper even though it simplifies to:

$$ \frac{3}{5}. $$

Proper and Reduced Are Different Properties

A fraction is proper because of the relative sizes of numerator and denominator. It is reduced or in lowest terms because the numerator and denominator share no common factor greater than $1$.

$$ \boxed{ \text{proper} \neq \text{reduced} }. $$

Example

$$ \frac{8}{12} $$

is proper but not reduced.

Its reduced form is:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{2}{3} }. $$

Equivalent Fractions

Multiplying numerator and denominator by the same nonzero number preserves the fraction's value:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} = \frac{ka}{kb} } $$

for $k\neq0$.

Properness under Equivalent Positive Scaling

If:

$$ 0<a<b $$

and $k>0$, then:

$$ \boxed{ 0<ka<kb }. $$

Thus equivalent positive forms remain proper.

Decimal Form

Every positive proper fraction has a decimal value strictly between $0$ and $1$.

Example

$$ \boxed{ \frac{3}{4} = 0.75 }. $$

Terminating Decimal

A reduced fraction:

$$ \frac{a}{b} $$

has a terminating decimal expansion exactly when the prime factorization of the positive denominator contains no primes other than $2$ and $5$.

$$ \boxed{ b = 2^m5^n } $$

for nonnegative integers $m,n$.

Repeating Decimal

A rational number whose reduced denominator has another prime factor has a nonterminating repeating decimal expansion.

Example

$$ \boxed{ \frac{1}{3} = 0.\overline{3} }. $$

Percent Form

A positive proper fraction corresponds to a percentage strictly between $0\%$ and $100\%$.

Example

$$ \frac{3}{5} = 0.6 = \boxed{ 60\% }. $$

Ratio Interpretation

A fraction may represent a ratio:

$$ \frac{a}{b} = a:b. $$

Properness then reflects that the first positive quantity is smaller than the second, assuming the comparison is dimensionally meaningful.

Division Interpretation

Every fraction denotes division:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} = a\div b }. $$

For a positive proper fraction, the dividend is smaller than the divisor.

Mixed Number Contrast

A mixed number combines an integer part with a proper fractional part:

$$ \boxed{ 2\frac{3}{5} }. $$

Here $3/5$ is the proper-fraction component.

Improper Fraction to Mixed Number

For positive integers $a\geq b>0$, division gives:

$$ a=bq+r, \qquad 0\leq r<b. $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} = q+\frac{r}{b} }. $$

The remainder fraction $r/b$ is proper when $r>0$.

Example

$$ 17 = 5(3)+2. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{17}{5} = 3\frac{2}{5} }. $$

Euclidean Division Connection

The proper-fraction part of a positive mixed-number representation comes from the remainder condition:

$$ \boxed{ 0\leq r<b }. $$

Proper Fraction as a Remainder Fraction

This makes proper fractions natural in division: a remainder is necessarily smaller than the divisor.

Rational Number Connection

Every proper fraction with integer numerator and nonzero integer denominator represents a rational number.

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} \in \mathbb{Q} }. $$

Not Every Rational Number Is Represented by a Proper Fraction without Adjustment

Rational numbers with magnitude at least $1$ require an improper fraction, integer, or mixed-number representation rather than a proper fraction of the same value.

Density

Proper rational fractions are dense between $0$ and $1$: between any two distinct real numbers in that interval there exists a rational number, and therefore a proper fraction representing one.

Example Using the Average

If:

$$ 0<\frac{a}{b}<\frac{c}{d}<1, $$

their arithmetic mean is another rational number strictly between them.

Mediant

For positive denominators, the mediant of:

$$ \frac{a}{b} \quad\text{and}\quad \frac{c}{d} $$

is:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a+c}{b+d} }. $$

If:

$$ \frac{a}{b} < \frac{c}{d}, $$

then under positive-denominator conditions:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} < \frac{a+c}{b+d} < \frac{c}{d} }. $$

Comparing Proper Fractions with Same Denominator

If $b>0$:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} < \frac{c}{b} \iff a<c }. $$

Comparing Unit Fractions

For positive denominators:

$$ \boxed{ a<b \Longrightarrow \frac{1}{a} > \frac{1}{b} }. $$

A larger denominator means smaller equal pieces when the numerator remains $1$.

Cross-Multiplication Comparison

For positive denominators $b$ and $d$:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} < \frac{c}{d} \iff ad<bc }. $$

Benchmark Comparison

Proper fractions are often compared with benchmarks such as:

$$ 0, \quad \frac{1}{2}, \quad 1. $$

Comparing with One-Half

For positive $b$:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} < \frac{1}{2} \iff 2a<b }. $$

Addition of Proper Fractions

The sum of two proper fractions need not be proper.

Example Remaining Proper

$$ \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{3} = \boxed{ \frac{7}{12} }. $$

Example Becoming Improper

$$ \frac{3}{4} + \frac{2}{3} = \frac{9}{12} + \frac{8}{12} = \boxed{ \frac{17}{12} }. $$

Subtraction of Positive Proper Fractions

If the larger proper fraction is first, the difference remains between $0$ and $1$.

Product of Positive Proper Fractions

If:

$$ 0<a<1, \qquad 0<b<1, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ 0<ab<1 }. $$

Thus the product of two positive proper fractions is again a positive proper fraction.

Product Becomes Smaller

For:

$$ 0<a<1, \qquad 0<b<1, $$

we also have:

$$ \boxed{ ab<a \quad\text{and}\quad ab<b }. $$

Division of Proper Fractions

The quotient of two proper fractions need not be proper.

Example

$$ \frac{3}{4} \div \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{4} \cdot2 = \boxed{ \frac{3}{2} }. $$

Reciprocal of a Positive Proper Fraction

If:

$$ 0<\frac{a}{b}<1, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{b}{a}>1 }. $$

Thus the reciprocal of a nonzero positive proper fraction is improper.

Example

$$ \frac{3}{7} \longrightarrow \boxed{ \frac{7}{3} }. $$

Complement to One

For a positive proper fraction:

$$ 0<\frac{a}{b}<1, $$

its complement relative to one is:

$$ \boxed{ 1-\frac{a}{b} = \frac{b-a}{b} }. $$

This is also a positive proper fraction.

Probability

Probabilities strictly between $0$ and $1$ can often be represented as proper fractions in finite equally likely models.

Example

For a fair six-sided die:

$$ P(\text{even}) = \frac{3}{6} = \boxed{ \frac{1}{2} }. $$

Probability Caveat

Not every probability is rational, so not every probability can be represented exactly by an integer proper fraction.

Rates and Proportions

Proper fractions can represent proportions below one, such as:

$$ \frac{18}{25} = 0.72. $$

Scale Factors

A positive proper fraction used as a scale factor produces a contraction in magnitude:

$$ \boxed{ 0<k<1 }. $$

Geometric Scaling

If every length is multiplied by $k$ with $0<k<1$, the resulting figure is smaller but similar to the original.

Area Scaling

Under a length scale factor $k$:

$$ \boxed{ \text{area scale factor} = k^2 }. $$

Volume Scaling

$$ \boxed{ \text{volume scale factor} = k^3 }. $$

Continued Fractions

Proper fractions frequently appear as reciprocal remainders in continued-fraction expansions.

Egyptian Fractions

A positive rational proper fraction can be represented as a finite sum of distinct positive unit fractions:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} = \frac{1}{n_1} + \frac{1}{n_2} + \cdots + \frac{1}{n_k} }. $$

Such representations are called Egyptian fraction representations.

Unit Fraction

A unit fraction has numerator $1$:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{1}{n}, \qquad n>1 }. $$

Every positive unit fraction with denominator greater than $1$ is proper.

Proper Fraction Is Not the Same as Unit Fraction

For example:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{3}{8} } $$

is proper but not a unit fraction.

Lowest-Term Representation

Every nonzero rational number has a representation:

$$ \frac{a}{b} $$

with:

$$ \boxed{ \gcd(|a|,|b|)=1 } $$

and usually $b>0$.

Properness of Reduced Representation

A rational number $q$ with $|q|<1$ has a reduced fraction representation whose numerator has smaller magnitude than its denominator.

Algebraic Rational Expressions

In algebra, the term proper rational expression is analogous but uses polynomial degree rather than integer magnitude.

Proper Rational Expression

For polynomials $P$ and nonzero $Q$:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} \text{ is proper} \iff \deg P<\deg Q }. $$

Analogy

Integer proper fraction:

$$ \boxed{ \text{numerator smaller than denominator} } $$

parallels:

$$ \boxed{ \text{numerator degree smaller than denominator degree} } $$

for rational expressions.

Do Not Transfer the Definition Literally

Polynomial expressions are not ordered by ordinary numerical size, so proper rational expressions use degree rather than comparing $P(x)$ and $Q(x)$ pointwise.

Common Error: Saying a Proper Fraction Must Be Less than One without Considering Sign

For positive fractions this is correct. With signed fractions, the clearest general statement is:

$$ \boxed{ \left| \frac{a}{b} \right| <1 }. $$

Common Error: Calling $\frac{7}{7}$ Proper

Since:

$$ \frac{7}{7}=1, $$

it is not proper under the standard definition.

Common Error: Calling $\frac{9}{4}$ Proper

Since:

$$ \frac{9}{4}>1, $$

it is improper.

Common Error: Thinking Proper Means Reduced

The fraction:

$$ \frac{6}{8} $$

is proper even though it is not in lowest terms.

Common Error: Thinking an Improper Fraction Is Incorrect

Improper is a classification label, not a statement that the fraction is invalid.

Common Error: Comparing Numerator and Denominator without Normalizing Signs

For signed fractions, comparing ordinary signed integers can be misleading. Magnitudes or a standardized positive denominator give a clearer classification.

Example

$$ \frac{3}{-5} = -\frac{3}{5}. $$

Under the magnitude-based convention it is proper because:

$$ \boxed{ 3<5 }. $$

Common Error: Assuming the Sum of Proper Fractions Is Always Proper

$$ \frac{3}{4} + \frac{3}{4} = \boxed{ \frac{3}{2} }. $$

Common Error: Assuming the Quotient of Proper Fractions Is Proper

$$ \frac{3}{4} \div \frac{1}{4} = \boxed{ 3 }. $$

Common Error: Assuming the Reciprocal of a Proper Fraction Is Proper

The reciprocal of a nonzero positive proper fraction is greater than $1$.

Common Error: Adding Numerators and Denominators Separately

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} + \frac{c}{d} \neq \frac{a+c}{b+d} } $$

in general.

Common Error: Treating the Mediant as Ordinary Fraction Addition

The mediant:

$$ \frac{a+c}{b+d} $$

is a separate construction and is not the ordinary sum.

Common Error: Assuming Larger Denominator Means Larger Fraction

For unit fractions with positive denominators, the opposite is true.

Common Error: Comparing Fractions by Numerators Alone

Numerators can be compared directly only when the positive denominators are equal.

Common Error: Comparing Fractions by Denominators Alone

Denominator size alone does not determine order when numerators differ.

Common Error: Confusing a Proper Fraction with a Percentage

A percentage is another representation. For example:

$$ \frac{3}{4}=75\%. $$

Common Error: Assuming Every Number between Zero and One Is a Proper Fraction

Every rational number between $0$ and $1$ can be represented by a proper fraction, but irrational numbers such as:

$$ \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} $$

lie between $0$ and $1$ without being fractions of integers in the rational-number sense.

Common Error: Confusing Proper Fraction with Proper Rational Expression

Integer fractions use numerator-denominator magnitude; rational expressions use polynomial degree.

Common Error: Assuming Mixed Number and Proper Fraction Are Opposites

A mixed number contains a whole-number part and ordinarily a proper-fraction part. The direct contrast to a proper fraction is an improper fraction, not a mixed number.

Common Error: Forgetting Denominator Cannot Be Zero

An expression:

$$ \frac{a}{0} $$

is undefined and therefore cannot be classified as a proper fraction.

Common Misconception: A proper fraction is not merely a fraction written in simplest form. Properness describes its size relative to one: in the standard positive setting, the numerator is smaller than the denominator. Thus $6/10$ is proper even though it reduces to $3/5$, while $7/7$ is not proper even though it is already reducible to the integer $1$.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\frac{3}{5}$ "Three fifths," a positive proper fraction.
$0<\frac{a}{b}<1$ "A over b lies strictly between zero and one."
$|a|<|b|$ "The magnitude of the numerator is less than the magnitude of the denominator."
$\frac{a}{b}$ "A over b" or "a divided by b."
$\frac{17}{5}=3\frac{2}{5}$ "Seventeen fifths equals three and two fifths."
$\frac{a}{b}=\frac{ka}{kb}$ "Equivalent fractions are obtained by multiplying numerator and denominator by the same nonzero factor."
$\deg P<\deg Q$ "The numerator degree is less than the denominator degree," for a proper rational expression.

The phrase:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"proper fraction"} } $$

most commonly means a positive fraction whose numerator is smaller than its denominator.

The phrase:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"fraction less than one"} } $$

is safe for positive fractions but should be refined to magnitude less than one if negative fractions are being included in the classification.

The phrase:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"in lowest terms"} } $$

refers to reduction, not properness.

Defense Notation Advice: State whether fractions are assumed positive before using the shortcut "numerator less than denominator." For signed fractions, compare absolute values or first normalize the denominator to be positive. Distinguish proper from reduced, and distinguish a proper numerical fraction from a proper rational expression, where degree rather than numerical magnitude controls the definition.

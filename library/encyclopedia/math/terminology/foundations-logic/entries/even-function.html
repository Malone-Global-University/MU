Even Function

Subfield: Foundations & Logic

Etymology & Roots

An even function is a function whose output is unchanged when the sign of the input is reversed.

A function $f$ is even when:

$$ \boxed{ f(-x)=f(x) } $$

for every $x$ in its domain for which $-x$ is also in the domain.

In the usual definition, the domain is symmetric about $0$, so:

$$ \boxed{ x\in\operatorname{Dom}(f) \Longrightarrow -x\in\operatorname{Dom}(f) }. $$

The term even comes from the behavior of even powers. For example:

$$ (-x)^2=x^2,\qquad (-x)^4=x^4,\qquad (-x)^6=x^6. $$

Therefore functions built entirely from even powers often satisfy:

$$ f(-x)=f(x). $$

Graphically, an even function is symmetric about the $y$-axis.

$$ \boxed{ (x,f(x)) \Longleftrightarrow (-x,f(x)) }. $$

Operational Nuance

Defining Condition

The standard algebraic test for evenness is:

$$ \boxed{ f(-x)=f(x) }. $$

One substitutes $-x$ for $x$, simplifies, and compares the result with the original function.

Example: $f(x)=x^2$

Let:

$$ f(x)=x^2. $$

Then:

$$ f(-x)=(-x)^2=x^2=f(x). $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ f(x)=x^2 \text{ is even} }. $$

Example: Even Polynomial

Consider:

$$ f(x)=3x^6-2x^4+7x^2+5. $$

Replacing $x$ by $-x$ gives:

$$ f(-x) = 3(-x)^6-2(-x)^4+7(-x)^2+5. $$

Since all powers are even:

$$ f(-x) = 3x^6-2x^4+7x^2+5 = f(x). $$

Hence $f$ is even.

Polynomial Criterion

A real polynomial:

$$ p(x)=a_0+a_1x+a_2x^2+\cdots+a_nx^n $$

is even exactly when every coefficient of an odd power is zero:

$$ \boxed{ a_1=a_3=a_5=\cdots=0 }. $$

Thus an even polynomial has the form:

$$ \boxed{ p(x)=a_0+a_2x^2+a_4x^4+\cdots }. $$

Constant Functions

Every constant function:

$$ f(x)=c $$

on a domain symmetric about $0$ is even because:

$$ f(-x)=c=f(x). $$

Zero Function

The zero function:

$$ \boxed{f(x)=0} $$

is even.

It is also odd because:

$$ 0=-0. $$

Thus the zero function is the standard function that is both even and odd.

Graphical Symmetry

If:

$$ f(-x)=f(x), $$

then whenever:

$$ (x,y) $$

lies on the graph, so does:

$$ \boxed{(-x,y)}. $$

This is reflection symmetry about the $y$-axis.

Even-Function Test from a Graph

Reflect the graph across the $y$-axis. If the reflected graph coincides with the original graph, the function is even.

Domain Symmetry

The equation:

$$ f(-x)=f(x) $$

requires both $x$ and $-x$ to be valid inputs. Thus the standard notion of an even function assumes a domain symmetric about the origin:

$$ \boxed{ x\in D\Rightarrow -x\in D }. $$

Example of a Domain Issue

The rule:

$$ f(x)=x^2 $$

is even on:

$$ \mathbb{R}. $$

But if the function is explicitly defined only on:

$$ [0,\infty), $$

then its domain is not symmetric about $0$, and it is not ordinarily classified as an even function on that restricted domain.

Even vs. Odd

An even function satisfies:

$$ \boxed{ f(-x)=f(x) }, $$

whereas an odd function satisfies:

$$ \boxed{ f(-x)=-f(x) }. $$

Even Symmetry

Even functions have $y$-axis symmetry.

Odd Symmetry

Odd functions have symmetry under a $180^\circ$ rotation about the origin.

Neither Even nor Odd

Many functions satisfy neither condition.

For example:

$$ f(x)=x+1. $$

Then:

$$ f(-x)=-x+1, $$

which is neither $f(x)$ nor $-f(x)$ in general.

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ x+1 \text{ is neither even nor odd} }. $$

Absolute Value

The absolute-value function is even:

$$ |-x|=|x|. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ f(x)=|x| \text{ is even} }. $$

Cosine

Cosine is an even trigonometric function:

$$ \boxed{ \cos(-x)=\cos x }. $$

Sine Contrast

Sine is odd:

$$ \sin(-x)=-\sin x. $$

Secant

Since:

$$ \sec x=\frac1{\cos x}, $$

and cosine is even:

$$ \boxed{ \sec(-x)=\sec x } $$

wherever secant is defined.

Product of Even Functions

If $f$ and $g$ are even, then:

$$ (fg)(-x) = f(-x)g(-x) = f(x)g(x). $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \text{even}\times\text{even} = \text{even} }. $$

Product of Odd Functions

If $f$ and $g$ are odd:

$$ f(-x)=-f(x), \qquad g(-x)=-g(x). $$

Hence:

$$ f(-x)g(-x) = f(x)g(x). $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \text{odd}\times\text{odd} = \text{even} }. $$

Product of Even and Odd Functions

$$ \boxed{ \text{even}\times\text{odd} = \text{odd} }. $$

Sum of Even Functions

If $f$ and $g$ are even:

$$ (f+g)(-x) = f(-x)+g(-x) = f(x)+g(x). $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ \text{even}+\text{even} = \text{even} }. $$

Difference of Even Functions

$$ \boxed{ \text{even}-\text{even} = \text{even} }. $$

Scalar Multiples

If $f$ is even and $c$ is a scalar, then:

$$ \boxed{ cf \text{ is even} }. $$

Quotient of Even Functions

If $f$ and $g$ are even and $g(x)\neq0$, then:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{f}{g} \text{ is even} } $$

on the symmetric domain where the quotient is defined.

Composition with an Even Outer Function

If $f$ is even, then for any function $g$ for which the compositions are defined:

$$ f(g(x))=f(-g(x)). $$

However, this alone does not imply that $f\circ g$ is even; the behavior of $g(-x)$ must also be considered.

Composition with an Odd Inner Function

If $f$ is even and $g$ is odd, then:

$$ (f\circ g)(-x) = f(g(-x)) = f(-g(x)) = f(g(x)). $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ f\text{ even},\ g\text{ odd} \Longrightarrow f\circ g\text{ even} }. $$

Composition of Even Functions

If $g$ is even, then:

$$ g(-x)=g(x). $$

Consequently $f\circ g$ is even for any suitable outer function $f$:

$$ \boxed{ g\text{ even} \Longrightarrow f\circ g\text{ even} } $$

whenever both sides are defined.

Derivative of an Even Function

If $f$ is differentiable and even on a symmetric interval, then:

$$ f(-x)=f(x). $$

Differentiating with respect to $x$ gives:

$$ -f'(-x)=f'(x). $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ f'(-x)=-f'(x) }. $$

Thus the derivative of a differentiable even function is odd.

Derivative at the Origin

If an even function is differentiable at $0$, then its derivative satisfies:

$$ f'(0)=-f'(0). $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{f'(0)=0}. $$

Second Derivative

If $f$ is sufficiently differentiable and even, then $f'$ is odd and $f''$ is even.

More generally, derivatives alternate parity:

$$ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} &f^{(2k)}\text{ is even},\\ &f^{(2k+1)}\text{ is odd}, \end{aligned} } $$

wherever the derivatives exist on a symmetric domain.

Integration over Symmetric Intervals

If $f$ is integrable and even:

$$ \boxed{ \int_{-a}^{a}f(x)\,dx = 2\int_0^a f(x)\,dx }. $$

This follows from the mirror symmetry of the graph.

Contrast with Odd-Function Integration

If $f$ is odd and integrable:

$$ \boxed{ \int_{-a}^{a}f(x)\,dx=0 }. $$

Even Part of a Function

Any function $f$ defined on a domain symmetric about $0$ can be used to form:

$$ \boxed{ f_{\text{even}}(x) = \frac{f(x)+f(-x)}{2} }. $$

This function is even.

Odd Part of a Function

Similarly:

$$ \boxed{ f_{\text{odd}}(x) = \frac{f(x)-f(-x)}{2} }. $$

is odd.

Even–Odd Decomposition

Adding these parts gives:

$$ \boxed{ f(x) = f_{\text{even}}(x) + f_{\text{odd}}(x) }. $$

Thus every suitable function on a symmetric domain decomposes uniquely into an even part and an odd part.

Example of Decomposition

Let:

$$ f(x)=x^3+x^2+2x+5. $$

Then:

$$ f(-x) = -x^3+x^2-2x+5. $$

The even part is:

$$ \frac{f(x)+f(-x)}2 = \boxed{x^2+5}. $$

The odd part is:

$$ \frac{f(x)-f(-x)}2 = \boxed{x^3+2x}. $$

Fourier Series

Evenness has an important consequence in Fourier analysis. An even function on a symmetric interval has a Fourier series involving cosine terms but no sine terms under the standard formulation.

This occurs because cosine is even and sine is odd.

Symmetry Can Simplify Computation

Knowing that a function is even can reduce work. Values on the negative half of the domain are determined by values on the positive half:

$$ \boxed{ f(-a)=f(a) }. $$

Even Does Not Mean Positive

The word even describes symmetry, not the sign of the function.

For example:

$$ f(x)=-x^2 $$

is even even though:

$$ f(x)\leq0 $$

for all real $x$.

Even Does Not Mean Even-Valued

An even function does not have to produce even integers.

For example:

$$ f(x)=x^2+\frac12 $$

is even despite producing many noninteger values.

Even Does Not Mean Even Degree in Every Context

Polynomial degree can help classify polynomial functions, but the term even function is broader.

For example:

$$ f(x)=\cos x $$

is even but is not a polynomial.

Functions Can Have Symmetry Without Being Even

A graph may possess reflection symmetry about a vertical line other than the $y$-axis without satisfying the definition of an even function.

For example:

$$ f(x)=(x-2)^2 $$

is symmetric about:

$$ x=2, $$

but it is not even because:

$$ f(-x)\neq f(x) $$

in general.

Common Misconception: An even function is not a function whose outputs are even numbers. The term refers to the symmetry condition $f(-x)=f(x)$, which means opposite inputs produce the same output and the graph is symmetric about the $y$-axis.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$f(-x)=f(x)$ "F of negative x equals f of x."
$f(-a)=f(a)$ "Opposite inputs a and negative a have the same output."
$\cos(-x)=\cos x$ "Cosine is an even function."
$|-x|=|x|$ "Absolute value is even."
$f'(-x)=-f'(x)$ "The derivative of an even function is odd."
$\int_{-a}^{a}f(x)\,dx=2\int_0^a f(x)\,dx$ "For an even function, the integral over negative a to a is twice the integral from zero to a."
$f_{\text{even}}(x)=\frac{f(x)+f(-x)}2$ "The even part of f is one half of f of x plus f of negative x."

To test whether a function is even, the standard spoken instruction is:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"replace }x\text{ by }-x\text{ and simplify"} }. $$

If the result is exactly the original expression:

$$ \boxed{ f(-x)=f(x) }, $$

the function is even, assuming the domain is appropriately symmetric.

Defense Notation Advice: Do not classify a function by visual appearance alone when an algebraic test is available. Verify both the domain symmetry:

$$ x\in D\Rightarrow -x\in D $$

and the output condition:

$$ \boxed{ f(-x)=f(x) }. $$

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Definition: $$ \boxed{ f(-x)=f(x) } $$ for every relevant $x$.
  • Domain Requirement: The standard definition assumes the domain is symmetric about $0$.
  • Domain Symmetry: $$ \boxed{ x\in D\Rightarrow -x\in D }. $$
  • Graphical Symmetry: An even function is symmetric about the $y$-axis.
  • Point Symmetry Rule: If $(x,y)$ lies on the graph, then $(-x,y)$ also lies on the graph.
  • Basic Example: $$ \boxed{f(x)=x^2}. $$
  • Absolute Value: $$ \boxed{ |-x|=|x| }. $$
  • Cosine: $$ \boxed{ \cos(-x)=\cos x }. $$
  • Secant: $$ \boxed{ \sec(-x)=\sec x } $$ wherever defined.
  • Constant Functions: Constant functions are even on symmetric domains.
  • Zero Function: The zero function is even.
  • Both Even and Odd: The zero function is also odd.
  • Typical Polynomial Form: $$ \boxed{ p(x)=a_0+a_2x^2+a_4x^4+\cdots }. $$
  • Polynomial Criterion: An even polynomial has zero coefficients on all odd powers.
  • Even Degree Caution: A polynomial having even degree does not automatically make the function even.
  • Counterexample: $$ x^2+x $$ has even degree but is not an even function.
  • Odd Function Contrast: $$ \boxed{ f(-x)=-f(x) }. $$
  • Odd Graph Symmetry: Odd functions have origin symmetry.
  • Neither Classification: A function may be neither even nor odd.
  • Example of Neither: $$ \boxed{f(x)=x+1}. $$
  • Sum of Even Functions: $$ \boxed{ \text{even}+\text{even} = \text{even} }. $$
  • Difference of Even Functions: $$ \boxed{ \text{even}-\text{even} = \text{even} }. $$
  • Scalar Multiple: A scalar multiple of an even function is even.
  • Product of Even Functions: $$ \boxed{ \text{even}\cdot\text{even} = \text{even} }. $$
  • Product of Odd Functions: $$ \boxed{ \text{odd}\cdot\text{odd} = \text{even} }. $$
  • Even–Odd Product: $$ \boxed{ \text{even}\cdot\text{odd} = \text{odd} }. $$
  • Quotient of Even Functions: A quotient of even functions is even wherever the denominator is nonzero and the resulting domain remains symmetric.
  • Odd-over-Odd Quotient: A quotient of two odd functions is even wherever defined on a symmetric domain.
  • Even-over-Odd Quotient: A quotient of an even function by an odd function is odd wherever defined on an appropriate symmetric domain.
  • Composition with Even Inner Function: If $g$ is even, then $f\circ g$ is even whenever defined.
  • Composition with Odd Inner Function: If $f$ is even and $g$ is odd, then $f\circ g$ is even.
  • Derivative: The derivative of a differentiable even function is odd.
  • Derivative Formula: $$ \boxed{ f'(-x)=-f'(x) }. $$
  • Derivative at Zero: If an even function is differentiable at $0$: $$ \boxed{ f'(0)=0 }. $$
  • Second Derivative: The second derivative of a sufficiently differentiable even function is even.
  • Derivative Parity Pattern: Even-order derivatives are even and odd-order derivatives are odd when all relevant derivatives exist.
  • Symmetric Integral: $$ \boxed{ \int_{-a}^{a}f(x)\,dx = 2\int_0^a f(x)\,dx }. $$
  • Integral Simplification: Even symmetry can cut a symmetric definite-integral calculation in half.
  • Odd Integral Contrast: For odd $f$: $$ \int_{-a}^{a}f(x)\,dx=0. $$
  • Even Part: $$ \boxed{ f_e(x) = \frac{f(x)+f(-x)}2 }. $$
  • Odd Part: $$ \boxed{ f_o(x) = \frac{f(x)-f(-x)}2 }. $$
  • Decomposition: $$ \boxed{ f=f_e+f_o }. $$
  • Uniqueness: The even–odd decomposition is unique for functions on symmetric domains.
  • Fourier Series: Even functions naturally generate cosine terms in standard Fourier-series expansions.
  • Sine Terms: Standard sine coefficients vanish for even functions on symmetric intervals.
  • Physics: Even functions often describe quantities that are unchanged under reversal of a spatial coordinate.
  • Probability: Symmetric probability density functions centered at $0$ are often even.
  • Normal Density Example: The standard normal density: $$ \frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^2/2} $$ is even.
  • Symmetric Distribution: An even density about $0$ reflects equal behavior at $x$ and $-x$.
  • Power Functions: $$ x^{2n} $$ is even for every nonnegative integer $n$.
  • Reciprocal Even Power: $$ \frac1{x^2} $$ is even on: $$ \mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}. $$
  • Domain Example: $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}$ is symmetric about $0$.
  • Restricted-Domain Caution: A formula that is algebraically even may cease to qualify under the standard definition when restricted to a nonsymmetric domain.
  • Translation Caution: Horizontal translation generally destroys evenness about the $y$-axis.
  • Example: $$ (x-3)^2 $$ is symmetric about $x=3$, not about the $y$-axis.
  • Vertical Translation: Adding a constant to an even function preserves evenness.
  • Vertical Scaling: Multiplying an even function by a constant preserves evenness.
  • Horizontal Reflection: Replacing $x$ by $-x$ leaves an even function unchanged.
  • Algebraic Test: Substitute $-x$, simplify completely, and compare with $f(x)$.
  • Graphical Test: Reflect the graph across the $y$-axis and check whether it coincides with itself.
  • Table Test: A table for an even function satisfies: $$ f(-a)=f(a) $$ for every paired input $a,-a$.
  • Sign Caution: Evenness says nothing about whether $f(x)$ itself is positive or negative.
  • Integer Caution: The term even does not mean that function values are even integers.
  • Degree Caution: An even function need not be a polynomial and therefore need not possess a polynomial degree.
  • Symmetry Caution: Reflection symmetry about a vertical line other than the $y$-axis does not by itself make a function even.
  • Core Interpretation: An even function treats opposite inputs identically: $$ \boxed{ f(-x)=f(x) }. $$ This algebraic condition is equivalent to $y$-axis symmetry when the domain is symmetric about zero, and it leads to useful structural consequences in algebra, graphing, calculus, probability, and Fourier analysis.

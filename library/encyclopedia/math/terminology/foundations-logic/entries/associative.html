Associative

Subfield: Foundations & Mathematical Logic

Etymology & Roots

In mathematics, associative describes an operation for which changing the way operands are grouped does not change the result.

For a binary operation $\ast$, associativity means:

$$ \boxed{ (a\ast b)\ast c = a\ast(b\ast c) } $$

for every admissible $a$, $b$, and $c$.

The word is related to the idea of associating or grouping objects together. In an associative operation, different parenthetical groupings produce the same value.

$$ \boxed{ \text{associative} = \text{grouping does not affect the result} }. $$

Operational Nuance

General Associative Law

Let:

$$ \ast:S\times S\to S $$

be a binary operation on a set $S$. The operation is associative if:

$$ \boxed{ (a\ast b)\ast c = a\ast(b\ast c) } $$

for all:

$$ a,b,c\in S. $$

Associativity of Addition

Ordinary addition of real numbers is associative:

$$ \boxed{ (a+b)+c = a+(b+c) }. $$

For example:

$$ (2+3)+4 = 5+4 = 9 $$

and:

$$ 2+(3+4) = 2+7 = 9. $$

Associativity of Multiplication

Ordinary multiplication is also associative:

$$ \boxed{ (ab)c = a(bc) }. $$

For example:

$$ (2\cdot3)\cdot4 = 6\cdot4 = 24 $$

and:

$$ 2\cdot(3\cdot4) = 2\cdot12 = 24. $$

Associativity Changes Grouping, Not Order

This distinction is crucial.

Associativity compares:

$$ \boxed{ (a\ast b)\ast c \quad\text{and}\quad a\ast(b\ast c) }. $$

The operands remain in the same order:

$$ a,\ b,\ c. $$

Only their grouping changes.

Associativity versus Commutativity

Commutativity changes order:

$$ \boxed{ a\ast b=b\ast a }. $$

Associativity changes grouping:

$$ \boxed{ (a\ast b)\ast c = a\ast(b\ast c) }. $$

These are distinct properties.

An Operation Can Be Associative but Not Commutative

Matrix multiplication provides an important example:

$$ \boxed{ (AB)C=A(BC) } $$

whenever the products are defined.

But generally:

$$ \boxed{ AB\ne BA }. $$

Thus matrix multiplication is associative but not generally commutative.

Subtraction Is Not Associative

In general:

$$ \boxed{ (a-b)-c \ne a-(b-c) }. $$

For example:

$$ (10-4)-2 = 6-2 = 4, $$

while:

$$ 10-(4-2) = 10-2 = 8. $$

Division Is Not Associative

In general:

$$ \boxed{ (a\div b)\div c \ne a\div(b\div c) }. $$

For example:

$$ (24\div6)\div2 = 4\div2 = 2, $$

but:

$$ 24\div(6\div2) = 24\div3 = 8. $$

Exponentiation Is Not Associative

In general:

$$ \boxed{ (a^b)^c \ne a^{(b^c)} }. $$

For example:

$$ (2^3)^2 = 8^2 = 64, $$

while:

$$ 2^{(3^2)} = 2^9 = 512. $$

Parentheses Can Be Omitted for Associative Operations

Because addition is associative:

$$ a+b+c $$

can be interpreted without ambiguity as either:

$$ (a+b)+c $$

or:

$$ a+(b+c). $$

The result is the same.

Longer Sums

Associativity extends to any finite number of terms:

$$ \boxed{ a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_n } $$

has the same value regardless of how parentheses are inserted, provided the order of terms remains unchanged.

Longer Products

Similarly:

$$ \boxed{ a_1a_2\cdots a_n } $$

can be regrouped freely when multiplication is associative.

Associativity and Mental Arithmetic

Associativity allows convenient regrouping.

For example:

$$ 25+17+75 $$

may be regrouped as:

$$ 17+(25+75) = 17+100 = 117. $$

Here commutativity may also be used to reorder terms, while associativity handles regrouping.

Associativity and Repeated Multiplication

Expressions such as:

$$ abcd $$

are manageable because associativity guarantees:

$$ ((ab)c)d = (ab)(cd) = a(b(cd)). $$

Matrix Addition

Matrix addition is associative:

$$ \boxed{ (A+B)+C = A+(B+C) }. $$

Matrix Multiplication

Matrix multiplication is associative:

$$ \boxed{ (AB)C = A(BC) }. $$

This property is essential for products involving several linear transformations.

Function Composition

Function composition is associative:

$$ \boxed{ (f\circ g)\circ h = f\circ(g\circ h) }. $$

Applied to an input $x$:

$$ ((f\circ g)\circ h)(x) = f(g(h(x))). $$

Function Composition Is Usually Not Commutative

Even though composition is associative:

$$ \boxed{ f\circ g \ne g\circ f } $$

in general.

This gives another important example of associativity without commutativity.

Vector Addition

Vector addition is associative:

$$ \boxed{ (\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v})+\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{w}) }. $$

Set Union

Union is associative:

$$ \boxed{ (A\cup B)\cup C = A\cup(B\cup C) }. $$

Set Intersection

Intersection is associative:

$$ \boxed{ (A\cap B)\cap C = A\cap(B\cap C) }. $$

Logical Conjunction

Logical AND is associative:

$$ \boxed{ (P\land Q)\land R \equiv P\land(Q\land R) }. $$

Logical Disjunction

Logical OR is associative:

$$ \boxed{ (P\lor Q)\lor R \equiv P\lor(Q\lor R) }. $$

Maximum Operation

The operation $\max$ is associative:

$$ \boxed{ \max(\max(a,b),c) = \max(a,\max(b,c)) }. $$

Minimum Operation

Likewise:

$$ \boxed{ \min(\min(a,b),c) = \min(a,\min(b,c)) }. $$

Associativity in Algebraic Structures

Associativity is one of the central axioms used to define many algebraic structures.

A semigroup consists of a set equipped with an associative binary operation.

$$ \boxed{ (a\ast b)\ast c = a\ast(b\ast c) }. $$

Monoid

A monoid is an associative structure that also has an identity element.

Thus a monoid requires:

  • closure,
  • associativity,
  • an identity element.

Groups

A group requires associativity together with an identity and inverses.

Importantly, a group need not be commutative.

$$ \boxed{ \text{group} \not\Rightarrow \text{commutative} }. $$

Abelian Groups

A group whose operation is also commutative is called an abelian group.

Thus associativity is required for all groups, while commutativity is an additional property possessed only by some groups.

Rings

In a ring, addition is associative and multiplication is also associative under the standard definition of ring.

Multiplication, however, need not be commutative.

Associative Algebra

In higher algebra, the adjective associative can distinguish algebras whose multiplication satisfies:

$$ \boxed{ (ab)c=a(bc) } $$

from nonassociative structures in which this condition is not required.

Nonassociative Operations

An operation is nonassociative if there exist elements $a,b,c$ for which:

$$ \boxed{ (a\ast b)\ast c \ne a\ast(b\ast c) }. $$

Only one valid counterexample is needed to prove that an operation is not associative.

How to Prove Associativity

To prove an operation associative, one must establish:

$$ (a\ast b)\ast c = a\ast(b\ast c) $$

for arbitrary admissible $a,b,c$.

Checking only a few numerical examples cannot establish the property universally.

How to Disprove Associativity

To disprove associativity, a single counterexample suffices.

For subtraction:

$$ (5-3)-1=1 $$

whereas:

$$ 5-(3-1)=3. $$

Therefore subtraction is not associative.

Associativity versus Distributivity

Distributivity involves two operations:

$$ \boxed{ a(b+c)=ab+ac }. $$

Associativity concerns regrouping within a single repeated operation:

$$ \boxed{ (ab)c=a(bc) }. $$

These are different structural laws.

Associativity and Order of Operations

Associativity explains why parentheses may be omitted in chains of the same associative operation.

It does not mean that arbitrary parentheses can be moved across different operations.

For example:

$$ (a+b)c $$

cannot be changed into:

$$ a+(bc) $$

by associativity because addition and multiplication are different operations.

Numerical Computation and Floating Point

Exact real-number addition is associative mathematically. Finite floating-point computer arithmetic, however, may exhibit rounding effects such that:

$$ (a+b)+c $$

and:

$$ a+(b+c) $$

produce slightly different machine results.

This does not invalidate associativity of real addition; it reflects the finite approximation used by the computer.

Common Misconception

Associativity does not permit changing:

$$ a+b+c $$

into:

$$ c+b+a. $$

That change of order uses commutativity.

Associativity alone permits:

$$ \boxed{ (a+b)+c \longleftrightarrow a+(b+c) }. $$

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$(a+b)+c=a+(b+c)$ "Addition is associative."
$(ab)c=a(bc)$ "Multiplication is associative."
$(a-b)-c\ne a-(b-c)$ "Subtraction is not associative."
$(AB)C=A(BC)$ "Matrix multiplication is associative."
$(f\circ g)\circ h=f\circ(g\circ h)$ "Function composition is associative."
$(a\ast b)\ast c=a\ast(b\ast c)$ "The operation star is associative."

The adjective associative normally modifies an operation: associative addition, associative multiplication, or an associative binary operation. The corresponding noun is associativity.

Defense Notation Advice: When checking associativity, preserve the order of $a$, $b$, and $c$ exactly. Change only the grouping. If the order changes, you are testing commutativity rather than associativity.

Base of a Trapezoid

Subfield: Geometry, Spatial Analysis & Topology

Etymology & Roots

The bases of a trapezoid are its two parallel sides. Unlike a triangle, where one side may be selected as a base for a particular calculation, the word bases in trapezoid terminology refers specifically to the parallel pair.

If trapezoid $ABCD$ has:

$$ AB\parallel CD, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ AB\text{ and }CD = \text{bases} }. $$

The remaining sides:

$$ AD \qquad\text{and}\qquad BC $$

are called the legs.

$$ \boxed{ \text{bases} = \text{parallel sides}, \qquad \text{legs} = \text{nonparallel sides} }. $$

Operational Nuance

Two Bases, Not One

A trapezoid normally has two bases because both parallel sides play the same structural role.

Their lengths are commonly denoted:

$$ \boxed{ b_1 \qquad\text{and}\qquad b_2 }. $$

Longer Base and Shorter Base

When the bases have unequal lengths, authors may refer to the:

  • longer base, and
  • shorter base.

If:

$$ b_1>b_2, $$

then $b_1$ is the longer base.

The Bases Need Not Be Horizontal

A trapezoid may be rotated without changing which sides are its bases. The bases are identified by parallelism, not by their visual position.

$$ \boxed{ \text{base status depends on parallelism, not orientation} }. $$

Height of a Trapezoid

The height, or altitude, of a trapezoid is the perpendicular distance between the lines containing its two bases.

$$ \boxed{ h = \operatorname{dist} (\ell_1,\ell_2) } $$

where $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ are the parallel lines containing the bases.

Height Is Perpendicular to Both Bases

Because the bases are parallel, any segment perpendicular to one base and reaching the other is perpendicular to both.

$$ \boxed{ h\perp b_1, \qquad h\perp b_2 }. $$

Area of a Trapezoid

If the base lengths are $b_1$ and $b_2$ and the perpendicular height is $h$, then:

$$ \boxed{ A= \frac12(b_1+b_2)h }. $$

Average-Base Interpretation

The area formula can be written:

$$ \boxed{ A= \left( \frac{b_1+b_2}{2} \right)h }. $$

Thus the area equals:

$$ \boxed{ \text{average base length} \times \text{height} }. $$

Why Both Bases Matter

A trapezoid generally widens or narrows from one base to the other. Its area is therefore not usually found by multiplying only one base by the height.

Instead, the effective width is the arithmetic mean:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{b_1+b_2}{2} }. $$

Midsegment

The segment joining the midpoints of the trapezoid's legs is called the midsegment or median of the trapezoid.

Its length is:

$$ \boxed{ m= \frac{b_1+b_2}{2} }. $$

Midsegment Is Parallel to the Bases

$$ \boxed{ m\parallel b_1\parallel b_2 }. $$

The trapezoid area can therefore also be expressed:

$$ \boxed{ A=mh }. $$

Solving for a Missing Base

Starting with:

$$ A=\frac12(b_1+b_2)h, $$

multiply by $2$:

$$ 2A=(b_1+b_2)h. $$

Divide by $h$:

$$ \frac{2A}{h}=b_1+b_2. $$

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ b_2= \frac{2A}{h}-b_1 }. $$

Solving for Height

From the same area formula:

$$ \boxed{ h= \frac{2A}{b_1+b_2} }. $$

Example

Suppose a trapezoid has bases:

$$ b_1=12, \qquad b_2=8, $$

and height:

$$ h=5. $$

Then:

$$ A= \frac12(12+8)(5) = \frac12(20)(5) = \boxed{50}. $$

Midsegment in the Example

The midsegment length is:

$$ m= \frac{12+8}{2} = \boxed{10}. $$

Then:

$$ A=mh=10(5)=50. $$

Isosceles Trapezoid

An isosceles trapezoid has congruent legs.

If $AB\parallel CD$ are the bases, then:

$$ \boxed{ AD=BC }. $$

Base Angles of an Isosceles Trapezoid

In an isosceles trapezoid, each pair of angles adjacent to the same base is congruent.

If $AB\parallel CD$, then:

$$ \boxed{ \angle A=\angle B, \qquad \angle D=\angle C }. $$

Diagonals of an Isosceles Trapezoid

The diagonals of an isosceles trapezoid are congruent:

$$ \boxed{ AC=BD }. $$

Symmetry of an Isosceles Trapezoid

A non-parallelogram isosceles trapezoid has one reflectional symmetry axis, perpendicular to both bases and passing through their midpoints.

$$ \boxed{ \text{symmetry axis} \perp \text{bases} }. $$

Right Trapezoid

A right trapezoid has a leg perpendicular to the bases. That leg therefore has length equal to the trapezoid's height.

$$ \boxed{ \text{perpendicular leg}=h }. $$

Base Difference

In many geometric problems, the difference between the base lengths:

$$ \boxed{ |b_1-b_2| } $$

measures the total horizontal offset between the endpoints of the two bases after a convenient coordinate placement.

Isosceles Trapezoid and Base Difference

For an isosceles trapezoid with longer base $B$, shorter base $b$, and height $h$, dropping perpendiculars from the endpoints of the shorter base divides the excess length equally:

$$ \boxed{ x= \frac{B-b}{2} }. $$

Leg Length in an Isosceles Trapezoid

If each leg has length $\ell$, then the right triangles formed at the sides give:

$$ \ell^2 = h^2+ \left( \frac{B-b}{2} \right)^2. $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ \ell= \sqrt{ h^2+ \left( \frac{B-b}{2} \right)^2 } }. $$

Coordinate Representation

A trapezoid can be positioned with horizontal bases such as:

$$ A=(0,0), \qquad B=(B,0), $$
$$ D=(p,h), \qquad C=(p+b,h). $$

Then the base lengths are:

$$ \boxed{ AB=B, \qquad DC=b }. $$

and the height is $h$.

Slope Test for Bases

In coordinate geometry, two nonvertical sides are parallel when they have equal slopes.

Thus candidate base segments $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{CD}$ satisfy:

$$ \boxed{ m_{AB}=m_{CD} }. $$

Vector Test for Parallel Bases

Two sides are parallel if their direction vectors are scalar multiples.

$$ \boxed{ \overrightarrow{CD} = k\overrightarrow{AB} } $$

for some nonzero scalar $k$.

Base versus Leg

A trapezoid's bases are the parallel sides. Its legs are the remaining sides.

$$ \boxed{ \text{bases} \parallel, \qquad \text{legs generally not parallel} }. $$

Base versus Height

A base is a side of the trapezoid. Height is the perpendicular distance between the two base lines.

Height therefore need not coincide with either leg.

Base versus Midsegment

The midsegment is not a base because it lies inside the trapezoid rather than on its boundary.

However, it is parallel to both bases and has length equal to their arithmetic mean.

Base versus Base Length

A base is a geometric segment, while its length is a number.

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ \overline{AB} = \text{base segment}, \qquad AB=b_1 = \text{base length} }. $$

Trapezoid Definition Convention

There are two common conventions for defining a trapezoid:

  • inclusive: a quadrilateral with at least one pair of parallel sides;
  • exclusive: a quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel sides.

Under the exclusive convention, the bases are unambiguous because there is exactly one parallel pair.

Inclusive Definition and Parallelograms

Under the inclusive convention, parallelograms count as trapezoids because they have at least one pair of parallel sides.

A parallelogram has two pairs of parallel sides, so either pair may be selected as the bases relative to the chosen orientation and height.

Common Misconception: Bottom and Top

The bases are often drawn as the bottom and top sides, but this is merely a convenient orientation.

$$ \boxed{ \text{parallelism determines the bases} }. $$

Common Misconception: Only the Longer Side Is the Base

Both parallel sides are bases. The longer one may be called the longer base, but the shorter parallel side is still a base.

Common Misconception: A Leg Can Be Used as b in the Area Formula

In:

$$ A= \frac12(b_1+b_2)h, $$

$b_1$ and $b_2$ must be the lengths of the parallel sides, not the legs.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$AB\parallel CD$ "A B is parallel to C D, so A B and C D are the bases."
$b_1,\ b_2$ "The two base lengths."
$A=\frac12(b_1+b_2)h$ "The area equals one-half the sum of the bases times the height."
$m=\frac{b_1+b_2}{2}$ "The trapezoid midsegment equals the average of the two base lengths."
$A=mh$ "Area equals the midsegment times the height."
$b_2=\frac{2A}{h}-b_1$ "Solve for the missing base by doubling the area, dividing by the height, and subtracting the known base."

In trapezoid problems, the plural bases is often more natural than the singular base because the parallel sides form a distinguished pair. Phrases such as longer base and shorter base identify one member of that pair.

Defense Notation Advice: Identify the parallel sides before using any trapezoid formula. Do not infer the bases merely from which sides appear horizontal in a particular diagram.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Definition: The bases of a trapezoid are its parallel sides.
  • Standard Notation: $$ \boxed{ b_1,\ b_2 } $$ often denote the base lengths.
  • Legs: The nonparallel sides are called the legs.
  • Orientation: Bases need not appear horizontally in a drawing.
  • Height: The perpendicular distance between the two base lines.
  • Area: $$ \boxed{ A= \frac12(b_1+b_2)h }. $$
  • Average-Base Form: $$ \boxed{ A= \left( \frac{b_1+b_2}{2} \right)h }. $$
  • Midsegment: $$ \boxed{ m= \frac{b_1+b_2}{2} }. $$
  • Midsegment Area Form: $$ \boxed{ A=mh }. $$
  • Missing Base: $$ \boxed{ b_2= \frac{2A}{h}-b_1 }. $$
  • Missing Height: $$ \boxed{ h= \frac{2A}{b_1+b_2} }. $$
  • Longer and Shorter Base: Unequal parallel sides may be distinguished by length.
  • Isosceles Trapezoid: The legs are congruent.
  • Isosceles Base Angles: Angles adjacent to each individual base occur in congruent pairs.
  • Isosceles Diagonals: The diagonals are congruent.
  • Isosceles Symmetry: The reflection axis is perpendicular to both bases and passes through their midpoints.
  • Right Trapezoid: A perpendicular leg has length equal to the height.
  • Base Difference: $|b_1-b_2|$ is useful in decompositions into rectangles and right triangles.
  • Isosceles Offset: $$ \boxed{ \frac{B-b}{2} } $$ is the horizontal offset on each side when an isosceles trapezoid is decomposed symmetrically.
  • Coordinate Test: Parallel sides may be identified by equal slopes.
  • Vector Test: Parallel base direction vectors are scalar multiples.
  • Segment-Length Distinction: $\overline{AB}$ is a base segment; $AB$ or $b_1$ is its length.
  • Inclusive Definition: If trapezoid means "at least one pair of parallel sides," parallelograms are included.
  • Exclusive Definition: If trapezoid means "exactly one pair of parallel sides," the bases are the unique parallel pair.
  • Common Error: Do not call only the longer parallel side a base; both parallel sides are bases.
  • Common Error: Do not use a leg in place of $b_1$ or $b_2$ in the trapezoid area formula.
  • Common Error: Do not confuse slanted leg length with perpendicular height.
  • Terminological Contrast: Bases are the trapezoid's parallel sides. Legs are the remaining sides. Height is the perpendicular distance between the bases. Midsegment joins the leg midpoints, is parallel to the bases, and has length equal to the arithmetic mean of their lengths. Base angles are the angles adjacent to a particular base.

Fraction

Subfield: Foundations & Logic

Etymology & Roots

A fraction is a mathematical expression representing one quantity divided by another. In its standard form:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} } $$

the quantity $a$ is the numerator and $b$ is the denominator, with:

$$ \boxed{ b\neq0 }. $$

The word fraction comes from Latin roots associated with breaking or dividing something into parts. This historical language explains the familiar elementary interpretation of a fraction as part of a whole.

For example:

$$ \boxed{ \frac34 } $$

may represent three of four equal parts of a whole.

In modern mathematics, however, a fraction is broader than a part-whole picture. It may represent:

  • a quotient,
  • a ratio,
  • a rate,
  • an operator,
  • a rational number,
  • or an algebraic expression involving division.

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} = a\div b } $$

whenever the division is defined.

Operational Nuance

Numerator

In:

$$ \frac{a}{b}, $$

$a$ is the numerator. In a part-whole interpretation, it tells how many equal parts are being considered.

Denominator

The denominator $b$ identifies the size of the equal parts in a part-whole interpretation and acts as the divisor in the quotient interpretation.

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} = a\div b }. $$

Denominator Cannot Be Zero

Division by zero is undefined. Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{0} \text{ is undefined} }. $$

Part-Whole Interpretation

If a whole is divided into $b$ equal parts and $a$ of those parts are selected, the selected portion is represented by:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} }. $$

Quotient Interpretation

A fraction may represent division:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{12}{5} = 12\div5 }. $$

Ratio Interpretation

A fraction can compare two quantities:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{3}{5} } $$

may represent the ratio of $3$ units of one quantity to $5$ units of another.

Operator Interpretation

A fraction may act as a scaling operator. For example:

$$ \frac34\cdot20 = \boxed{15}. $$

Here $\frac34$ means take three-fourths of $20$.

Measure Interpretation

A fraction may locate a quantity on the number line:

$$ \frac34 $$

is a number between $0$ and $1$.

Fractions Greater Than One

Fractions are not restricted to values between $0$ and $1$.

For example:

$$ \boxed{ \frac74=1.75 }. $$

Proper Fraction

In elementary arithmetic, a proper fraction usually has numerator magnitude smaller than denominator magnitude.

For positive numerator and denominator:

$$ \boxed{ 0<a<b \Longrightarrow 0<\frac ab<1 }. $$

Improper Fraction

An improper fraction has numerator at least as large in magnitude as its denominator under common elementary conventions.

Example:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{11}{4} }. $$

Mixed Number

A mixed number combines an integer and a proper fraction:

$$ \boxed{ 2\frac34 }. $$

This means:

$$ 2+\frac34. $$

Mixed Number to Improper Fraction

$$ 2\frac34 = \frac{2\cdot4+3}{4} = \boxed{\frac{11}{4}}. $$

Improper Fraction to Mixed Number

Divide the numerator by the denominator:

$$ 11=2\cdot4+3. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{11}{4} = 2\frac34 }. $$

Equivalent Fractions

Fractions can have different numerators and denominators while representing the same number:

$$ \boxed{ \frac12 = \frac24 = \frac36 = \frac{50}{100} }. $$

Generating Equivalent Fractions

For nonzero $k$:

$$ \boxed{ \frac ab = \frac{ak}{bk} }. $$

This follows because:

$$ \frac{k}{k}=1 \qquad (k\neq0). $$

Simplifying a Fraction

A fraction is simplified by dividing numerator and denominator by a common nonzero factor.

For example:

$$ \frac{18}{24} = \frac{18\div6}{24\div6} = \boxed{\frac34}. $$

Lowest Terms

An integer fraction:

$$ \frac ab $$

is in lowest terms when:

$$ \boxed{ \gcd(a,b)=1 } $$

after sign conventions have been normalized.

Reducing by the Greatest Common Factor

The most direct reduction uses:

$$ d=\gcd(a,b). $$

Then:

$$ \boxed{ \frac ab = \frac{a/d}{b/d} }. $$

Cancellation Means Dividing Factors

In:

$$ \frac{6\cdot5}{6\cdot7}, $$

the common nonzero factor $6$ may cancel:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{6\cdot5}{6\cdot7} = \frac57 }. $$

Terms Do Not Cancel Across Addition

In:

$$ \frac{x+3}{x}, $$

the $x$ in the denominator is not a factor of the entire numerator, so one cannot cancel it from the $x+3$ expression.

Sign Placement

For nonzero $b$:

$$ \boxed{ -\frac ab = \frac{-a}{b} = \frac{a}{-b} }. $$

But:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{-a}{-b} = \frac ab }. $$

Standard Sign Convention

Fractions are commonly written with a positive denominator:

$$ \frac{3}{-5} = \boxed{-\frac35}. $$

Zero Numerator

If $b\neq0$:

$$ \boxed{ \frac0b=0 }. $$

Zero Denominator

$$ \boxed{ \frac a0 \text{ is undefined} }. $$

Fraction Equal to One

For nonzero $a$:

$$ \boxed{ \frac aa=1 }. $$

Reciprocal

For nonzero:

$$ \frac ab, $$

its reciprocal is:

$$ \boxed{ \frac ba }. $$

Their product is:

$$ \frac ab\cdot\frac ba=1. $$

Multiplying Fractions

Multiply numerators and denominators:

$$ \boxed{ \frac ab\cdot\frac cd = \frac{ac}{bd} } $$

where $b\neq0$ and $d\neq0$.

Multiplication Example

$$ \frac23\cdot\frac57 = \boxed{\frac{10}{21}}. $$

Cross-Canceling Before Multiplication

Common factors may be reduced before multiplication:

$$ \frac68\cdot\frac{10}{15}. $$

Reducing factors first can make the arithmetic simpler.

Dividing Fractions

Division by a nonzero fraction is multiplication by its reciprocal:

$$ \boxed{ \frac ab \div \frac cd = \frac ab \cdot \frac dc } $$

where $b,c,d\neq0$.

Division Example

$$ \frac34\div\frac25 = \frac34\cdot\frac52 = \boxed{\frac{15}{8}}. $$

Adding Fractions with Like Denominators

If denominators are the same:

$$ \boxed{ \frac ab+\frac cb = \frac{a+c}{b} }. $$

The denominator is not added because the size of the parts remains unchanged.

Example

$$ \frac27+\frac37 = \boxed{\frac57}. $$

Subtracting Fractions with Like Denominators

$$ \boxed{ \frac ab-\frac cb = \frac{a-c}{b} }. $$

Unlike Denominators

Fractions with different denominators must first be rewritten using a common denominator.

General Addition Formula

$$ \boxed{ \frac ab+\frac cd = \frac{ad+bc}{bd} } $$

where $b,d\neq0$.

General Subtraction Formula

$$ \boxed{ \frac ab-\frac cd = \frac{ad-bc}{bd} }. $$

Example with Unlike Denominators

$$ \frac12+\frac13 = \frac36+\frac26 = \boxed{\frac56}. $$

Least Common Denominator

The least common denominator is usually the least common multiple of the denominators.

For:

$$ \frac14+\frac16, $$

the least common denominator is:

$$ \operatorname{lcm}(4,6) = \boxed{12}. $$

Cross Multiplication

If:

$$ \frac ab=\frac cd $$

with nonzero denominators, then:

$$ \boxed{ ad=bc }. $$

This follows by multiplying both sides by $bd$.

Cross Multiplication Is Not General Fraction Addition

The relation $ad=bc$ applies to an equation of two fractions. It is not a rule for adding or subtracting fractions.

Comparing Positive Fractions

For positive denominators:

$$ \frac ab<\frac cd $$

exactly when:

$$ \boxed{ ad<bc }. $$

Common Denominator Comparison

If denominators are equal and positive:

$$ \boxed{ \frac ab<\frac cb \iff a<c }. $$

Common Numerator Comparison

For positive values with equal positive numerators:

$$ \frac ab \quad\text{and}\quad \frac ac, $$

the fraction with the larger denominator is smaller:

$$ 0<b<c \Longrightarrow \boxed{ \frac ac<\frac ab }. $$

Fractions as Rational Numbers

A rational number is any number expressible as:

$$ \boxed{ \frac ab, \qquad a,b\in\mathbb{Z}, \quad b\neq0 }. $$

Thus integer fractions are representations of rational numbers.

One Rational Number, Many Fractions

The rational number $\frac12$ has infinitely many fractional representations:

$$ \frac12 = \frac24 = \frac36 = \cdots $$

Therefore the fraction expression and the abstract rational number should not always be treated as identical concepts.

Canonical Reduced Representation

Every nonzero rational number can be written uniquely as:

$$ \boxed{ \frac ab } $$

where:

$$ \gcd(|a|,b)=1, \qquad b>0. $$

Integers as Fractions

Every integer can be represented as a fraction:

$$ \boxed{ n=\frac n1 }. $$

Decimal Representation

A fraction may be converted to a decimal by performing the indicated division.

For example:

$$ \frac34 = \boxed{0.75}. $$

Terminating Decimal

In base ten, a reduced fraction has a terminating decimal exactly when its denominator has no prime factors other than $2$ and $5$.

$$ \boxed{ b=2^m5^n } $$

for nonnegative integers $m,n$.

Repeating Decimal

Every rational number has a decimal representation that either terminates or eventually repeats.

For example:

$$ \frac13 = 0.\overline3. $$

Decimal-to-Fraction Conversion

A terminating decimal can be written over a power of ten:

$$ 0.375 = \frac{375}{1000} = \boxed{\frac38}. $$

Percent Connection

A percent is a fraction with denominator $100$:

$$ \boxed{ 37\% = \frac{37}{100} }. $$

Unit Fraction

A unit fraction has numerator $1$:

$$ \boxed{ \frac1n } $$

for nonzero integer $n$, often with $n>0$ in elementary contexts.

Egyptian Fraction

An Egyptian fraction is a sum of distinct positive unit fractions.

Example:

$$ \frac23 = \frac12+\frac16. $$

Complex Fraction

A fraction whose numerator or denominator contains another fraction is often called a complex fraction.

Example:

$$ \frac{\frac12}{\frac34}. $$

Simplifying:

$$ \frac12\div\frac34 = \frac12\cdot\frac43 = \boxed{\frac23}. $$

Compound Fraction

The term compound fraction is sometimes used as a synonym for complex fraction, though usage varies by source.

Algebraic Fraction

A fraction may contain variables:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{x+1}{x-3} }. $$

Such expressions require domain restrictions.

Rational Expression

A quotient of polynomials:

$$ \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} $$

with $Q(x)\neq0$ is called a rational expression.

Domain Restriction

In:

$$ \frac{x+1}{x-3}, $$

one must require:

$$ \boxed{x\neq3}. $$

Canceling Algebraic Factors

Consider:

$$ \frac{(x-2)(x+5)}{x-2}. $$

For $x\neq2$:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{(x-2)(x+5)}{x-2} = x+5 }. $$

Original Restriction Remains

Although the factor $x-2$ cancels algebraically, the original fraction remains undefined at:

$$ \boxed{x=2}. $$

Fraction Bar as Grouping Symbol

The fraction bar groups the entire numerator and denominator.

$$ \frac{a+b}{c+d} $$

means:

$$ \boxed{ (a+b)\div(c+d) }. $$

Fraction Bar Precedence

Because the numerator and denominator are grouped, one should not interpret:

$$ \frac{a+b}{c} $$

as:

$$ a+\frac bc. $$

The correct distribution, when useful, is:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a+b}{c} = \frac ac+\frac bc } $$

for $c\neq0$.

Denominator Does Not Distribute over Addition

In general:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b+c} \neq \frac ab+\frac ac }. $$

Fractions and Proportions

An equation:

$$ \frac ab=\frac cd $$

expresses a proportion when the fractions are interpreted as ratios.

Fractions and Rates

A rate is often expressed fractionally:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{60\text{ miles}}{2\text{ hours}} = 30\text{ miles/hour} }. $$

Units in Fractions

Units may appear in both numerator and denominator and participate in dimensional simplification.

For example:

$$ \frac{100\text{ km}}{2\text{ h}} = 50\frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}. $$

Dimensional Ratios

A fraction can be dimensionless if numerator and denominator have matching units:

$$ \frac{3\text{ m}}{12\text{ m}} = \boxed{\frac14}. $$

Probability

Elementary probabilities are often represented as fractions:

$$ \boxed{ P(E) = \frac{\text{favorable outcomes}} {\text{equally likely outcomes}} } $$

when a finite equally likely model applies.

Slope

Slope is fundamentally a ratio:

$$ \boxed{ m = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} }. $$

Derivative

Derivative notation:

$$ \frac{dy}{dx} $$

resembles a fraction and often behaves suggestively like one in calculations, but in rigorous analysis it denotes a derivative rather than an ordinary quotient of two independently defined numbers $dy$ and $dx$.

Partial Derivative

Similarly:

$$ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} $$

is derivative notation, not simply a numerical fraction.

Ratios vs. Fractions

The terms overlap substantially but emphasize different ideas.

A fraction emphasizes division or numerical representation, while a ratio emphasizes comparison between quantities.

Fraction vs. Division Expression

The written form:

$$ \frac ab $$

is simultaneously a fraction and a notation for the quotient $a\div b$.

Fraction vs. Rational Number

A rational number is an abstract number. A fraction is one expression that may represent that number.

Thus:

$$ \frac12 \quad\text{and}\quad \frac24 $$

are different fractional expressions representing the same rational number.

Fraction vs. Decimal

Fractions and decimals are alternative representations of numbers.

$$ \boxed{ \frac58=0.625 }. $$

Fraction vs. Percent

Percent notation normalizes a ratio to denominator $100$:

$$ \boxed{ \frac14 = 25\% }. $$

Fraction vs. Mixed Number

A mixed number is an alternative representation of an improper fraction, not a fundamentally different numerical object.

Density of Rational Fractions

Between any two distinct rational numbers there exists another rational number.

For example, if:

$$ a<b, $$

then their average:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a+b}{2} } $$

lies strictly between them.

Mediant

For positive denominators, the mediant of:

$$ \frac ab \quad\text{and}\quad \frac cd $$

is:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a+c}{b+d} }. $$

Under suitable ordering and positivity assumptions, the mediant lies between the two original fractions.

Continued Fractions

More advanced mathematics represents numbers using nested fractional expressions such as:

$$ a_0+ \cfrac{1}{ a_1+ \cfrac{1}{ a_2+\cdots } }. $$

These are called continued fractions.

Fraction Field

In abstract algebra, the term field of fractions generalizes the construction of rational numbers from integers.

Just as:

$$ \mathbb{Q} = \left\{ \frac ab: a,b\in\mathbb{Z},\ b\neq0 \right\}, $$

one may construct fractions from elements of more general integral domains.

Formal Fraction Equivalence

In a formal construction of rational numbers, pairs:

$$ (a,b) \qquad (b\neq0) $$

are treated as equivalent when:

$$ \boxed{ ad=bc }. $$

This formalizes why:

$$ \frac12=\frac24. $$

Fraction Bar and Vinculum

The horizontal line separating numerator and denominator is called a fraction bar and may also be called a vinculum in broader notation terminology.

Slash Notation

Fractions may also be written inline:

$$ \boxed{ 3/4 }. $$

This is convenient in plain text but may require parentheses for clarity in complex expressions.

Ambiguity in Slash Notation

The expression:

$$ a/bc $$

can be visually ambiguous. Parentheses clarify whether one means:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{bc} } $$

or:

$$ \boxed{ \frac ab\,c }. $$

Spoken Fraction Names

Elementary fractions have conventional spoken forms:

$$ \frac12 \quad\text{is "one half,"} $$
$$ \frac13 \quad\text{is "one third,"} $$
$$ \frac34 \quad\text{is "three fourths" or "three quarters."} $$

Cardinal-over-Cardinal Reading

In advanced mathematics, especially for algebraic expressions, fractions are often read using over:

$$ \frac{x+1}{x-2} $$

may be spoken as:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"x plus one over x minus two"} }. $$

Common Error: Adding Numerators and Denominators

In general:

$$ \boxed{ \frac ab+\frac cd \neq \frac{a+c}{b+d} }. $$

For example:

$$ \frac12+\frac12=1, $$

while:

$$ \frac{1+1}{2+2} = \frac12. $$

Common Error: Multiplying Like Addition

Fraction multiplication does not require a common denominator:

$$ \boxed{ \frac ab\cdot\frac cd = \frac{ac}{bd} }. $$

Common Error: Forgetting the Reciprocal in Division

Fraction division requires multiplying by the reciprocal of the divisor:

$$ \boxed{ \frac ab \div \frac cd = \frac ab\cdot\frac dc }. $$

Common Error: Reciprocal of Zero

Zero has no reciprocal because:

$$ \frac10 $$

is undefined.

Common Error: Canceling Additive Terms

Cancellation applies to common factors, not isolated terms separated by addition or subtraction.

Common Error: Thinking Larger Denominator Means Larger Fraction

For unit fractions with positive denominators:

$$ \boxed{ b<c \Longrightarrow \frac1b>\frac1c }. $$

More pieces of the same whole make each individual piece smaller.

Common Misconception: A fraction is not merely "a number less than one." Fractions may equal whole numbers, exceed one, be negative, represent ratios or rates, and contain algebraic expressions. The essential structure is division: $\frac{a}{b}$ represents $a$ divided by nonzero $b$.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\frac12$ "One half."
$\frac34$ "Three fourths," or "three quarters."
$\frac{a}{b}$ "A over b," or "a divided by b."
$\frac{x+1}{x-2}$ "X plus one over x minus two."
$2\frac34$ "Two and three fourths."
$\frac ab=\frac cd$ "A over b equals c over d."
$\frac ab\cdot\frac cd$ "A over b times c over d."

The fraction bar acts as a grouping symbol. Thus:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a+b}{c+d} } $$

should be understood as:

$$ \boxed{ (a+b)\div(c+d) }. $$

Defense Notation Advice: When slash notation is used in plain text, add parentheses whenever the numerator or denominator contains more than one term. Write:

$$ \boxed{ (x+1)/(x-2) } $$

rather than relying on ambiguous visual precedence. Keep denominators nonzero, and when simplifying algebraic fractions, cancel only common multiplicative factors.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Definition: A fraction represents one quantity divided by another.
  • Standard Form: $$ \boxed{\frac ab}. $$
  • Numerator: The quantity written above the fraction bar.
  • Denominator: The quantity written below the fraction bar.
  • Restriction: The denominator must be nonzero.
  • Division Interpretation: $$ \frac ab=a\div b. $$
  • Part-Whole Interpretation: The numerator counts selected equal parts and the denominator determines the partition size.
  • Ratio Interpretation: A fraction can compare two quantities multiplicatively.
  • Operator Interpretation: A fraction can scale another quantity.
  • Measure Interpretation: A fraction locates a number on the number line.
  • Proper Fraction: Commonly has numerator magnitude smaller than denominator magnitude.
  • Improper Fraction: Commonly has numerator magnitude at least as large as denominator magnitude.
  • Mixed Number: Combines a whole-number part with a proper fraction.
  • Mixed-to-Improper Conversion: Multiply the whole number by the denominator and add the numerator.
  • Equivalent Fractions: Different fraction expressions may represent the same value.
  • Scaling Rule: $$ \frac ab=\frac{ak}{bk} $$ for $k\neq0$.
  • Lowest Terms: Numerator and denominator share no common factor greater than $1$.
  • Greatest Common Factor: Useful for reducing integer fractions.
  • Cancellation: Common nonzero multiplicative factors may be divided out.
  • Cancellation Restriction: Terms separated by addition or subtraction do not cancel individually.
  • Zero Numerator: $\frac0b=0$ for $b\neq0$.
  • Zero Denominator: $\frac a0$ is undefined.
  • Unity: $\frac aa=1$ for $a\neq0$.
  • Reciprocal: The reciprocal of $\frac ab$ is $\frac ba$ when $a,b\neq0$.
  • Multiplication: $$ \frac ab\cdot\frac cd=\frac{ac}{bd}. $$
  • Division: $$ \frac ab\div\frac cd=\frac ab\cdot\frac dc. $$
  • Like-Denominator Addition: $$ \frac ab+\frac cb=\frac{a+c}{b}. $$
  • Like-Denominator Subtraction: $$ \frac ab-\frac cb=\frac{a-c}{b}. $$
  • Unlike-Denominator Addition: Convert to equivalent fractions with a common denominator.
  • General Addition: $$ \frac ab+\frac cd=\frac{ad+bc}{bd}. $$
  • General Subtraction: $$ \frac ab-\frac cd=\frac{ad-bc}{bd}. $$
  • Least Common Denominator: Usually based on the least common multiple of the denominators.
  • Proportion: An equality between two ratios or fractions.
  • Cross Multiplication: $$ \frac ab=\frac cd\iff ad=bc $$ when denominators are nonzero.
  • Comparison: Positive-denominator fractions may be compared using cross products.
  • Rational Number: Any number expressible as $\frac ab$ with integers $a,b$ and $b\neq0$.
  • Integer as Fraction: $$ n=\frac n1. $$
  • Multiple Representations: One rational number has infinitely many equivalent fractional forms.
  • Reduced Rational Form: Conventionally uses coprime numerator and denominator with positive denominator.
  • Decimal Conversion: Perform the indicated division.
  • Terminating Decimal: A reduced denominator with only prime factors $2$ and $5$ terminates in base ten.
  • Repeating Decimal: Nonterminating rational decimal expansions eventually repeat.
  • Percent: A ratio normalized to denominator $100$.
  • Unit Fraction: A fraction with numerator $1$.
  • Egyptian Fraction: A sum of distinct positive unit fractions.
  • Complex Fraction: Contains a fraction in its numerator or denominator.
  • Compound Fraction: Sometimes used as a synonym for complex fraction.
  • Algebraic Fraction: May contain variables or algebraic expressions.
  • Rational Expression: A quotient of polynomials.
  • Domain Restriction: Values making a denominator zero must be excluded.
  • Original Domain: Canceling a factor does not restore excluded values.
  • Fraction Bar: Serves as both division notation and a grouping symbol.
  • Vinculum: Another term for a horizontal grouping bar, including the fraction bar.
  • Slash Notation: $a/b$ is a common inline representation.
  • Parentheses: Essential in slash notation when numerators or denominators are compound.
  • Rates: Often written as fractions of unlike units.
  • Unit Rate: A rate with denominator normalized to one unit.
  • Slope: $$ m=\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}. $$
  • Probability: Finite equally likely probabilities are often expressed as favorable outcomes divided by total outcomes.
  • Geometry: Fractions express ratios of lengths, areas, angles, and scale factors.
  • Trigonometry: Trigonometric ratios are quotient relationships such as $\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$ in right triangles.
  • Calculus: Difference quotients and derivative notation use fractional forms.
  • Derivative Caution: $\frac{dy}{dx}$ is not ordinarily interpreted as an independent quotient of two ordinary real numbers.
  • Physics: Rates such as velocity, density, and acceleration commonly use quotient notation.
  • Dimensional Analysis: Units in numerator and denominator can simplify multiplicatively.
  • Dimensionless Ratio: Same-unit numerator and denominator may cancel units.
  • Number Theory: Fractions represent rational numbers and connect to divisibility and greatest common divisors.
  • Continued Fractions: Nested fraction expansions provide approximations and structural information about real numbers.
  • Abstract Algebra: Fields of fractions generalize rational-number construction.
  • Formal Construction: Rational numbers may be represented by equivalence classes of integer pairs.
  • Equivalence Criterion: $(a,b)\sim(c,d)$ when $ad=bc$ with nonzero denominators.
  • Fraction vs. Ratio: Fraction emphasizes quotient representation; ratio emphasizes comparison.
  • Fraction vs. Rational Number: A fraction is a representation; a rational number is the represented value.
  • Fraction vs. Decimal: Alternative representations of the same numerical value.
  • Fraction vs. Percent: Percent is a denominator-$100$ normalization.
  • Fraction vs. Mixed Number: Mixed number is another notation for certain improper fractions.
  • Common Addition Error: Numerators and denominators are not both added.
  • Common Multiplication Error: A common denominator is unnecessary for multiplication.
  • Common Division Error: Division requires multiplying by the reciprocal.
  • Common Cancellation Error: Only common factors may cancel.
  • Common Magnitude Error: A larger positive denominator makes a unit fraction smaller.
  • Core Interpretation: A fraction is fundamentally a quotient: $$ \boxed{ \frac ab = a\div b, \qquad b\neq0 }. $$ It may represent part of a whole, a ratio, a rate, a scaling operation, or a rational number. Equivalent fractions arise by multiplying numerator and denominator by the same nonzero quantity, and all fraction arithmetic follows from the ordinary laws of division, multiplication, addition, equivalence, and nonzero denominators.

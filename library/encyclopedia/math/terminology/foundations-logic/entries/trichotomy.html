Trichotomy

Subfield: Foundations & Logic

Etymology & Roots

Trichotomy is the principle that two elements in a suitable ordered system stand in exactly one of three mutually exclusive relationships.

$$ \boxed{ a<b, \qquad a=b, \qquad a>b } $$

For real numbers, exactly one of these statements is true for any pair $a$ and $b$. The word comes from roots meaning a division into three parts: tri- for three and a root associated with cutting or dividing.

Operational Nuance

Trichotomy Law

For real numbers $a$ and $b$:

$$ \boxed{ \text{exactly one of } a<b,\ a=b,\ a>b \text{ holds} }. $$

Mutual Exclusivity

The three alternatives cannot hold simultaneously. For example, if $a<b$, then:

$$ a\neq b \qquad\text{and}\qquad a\not>b. $$

Exhaustiveness

Trichotomy also says that the three cases cover every possible comparison in the ordered system. No fourth comparison case is needed.

Comparison with Zero

Applying trichotomy to a real number $x$ and zero gives:

$$ \boxed{ x<0, \qquad x=0, \qquad x>0 }. $$

This three-way division is fundamental to sign analysis.

Difference Form

Comparing $a$ and $b$ can equivalently be reduced to the sign of $a-b$:

$$ \boxed{ a<b \Longleftrightarrow a-b<0 } $$
$$ \boxed{ a=b \Longleftrightarrow a-b=0 } $$
$$ \boxed{ a>b \Longleftrightarrow a-b>0 }. $$

Total Order

Trichotomy is closely associated with a total order, where every pair of elements is comparable. In a total order, one may equivalently formulate comparability using $\leq$:

$$ \boxed{ a\leq b \quad\text{or}\quad b\leq a }. $$

Partial Orders

Trichotomy need not hold in a partial order because two elements may be incomparable. For example, under set inclusion, neither:

$$ \{1\}\subseteq\{2\} $$

nor:

$$ \{2\}\subseteq\{1\} $$

is true.

Ordered Number Systems

The usual orders on $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Q}$, and $\mathbb{R}$ satisfy trichotomy.

Complex Numbers

The complex numbers do not carry a standard total order compatible with their field operations in the same way as the real numbers. Thus expressions such as:

$$ i>0 $$

have no meaning under the ordinary complex-number structure.

Use in Proofs

Trichotomy supports proof by cases. A proof involving a real number $x$ may divide into:

$$ x<0, \qquad x=0, \qquad x>0. $$

If each case is established, all possibilities have been covered.

Absolute Value

Trichotomy underlies common piecewise definitions such as:

$$ |x|= \begin{cases} x, & x\geq0,\\ -x, & x<0. \end{cases} $$

More detailed sign arguments often separate the zero case explicitly.

Common Misconception

Trichotomy is not merely the statement that one of three symbols can be written between two numbers. It asserts both exhaustiveness and mutual exclusivity: exactly one comparison is true.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$a<b$ "A is strictly less than b."
$a=b$ "A equals b."
$a>b$ "A is strictly greater than b."
$x<0,\ x=0,\ x>0$ "X is negative, zero, or positive."
$a\leq b$ or $b\leq a$ "Every pair is comparable in the total order."
$a-b<0$ "A minus b is negative, so a is less than b."

The phrase by trichotomy commonly introduces a proof step that splits an ordered comparison into the three cases less than, equal to, and greater than.

Defense Notation Advice: Use trichotomy only where the relevant order makes every pair comparable. Do not transfer ordinary real-number comparison symbols automatically to partially ordered structures or to complex numbers.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Principle: Exactly one of $a<b$, $a=b$, or $a>b$ holds in the standard real order.
  • Mutual Exclusivity: No two trichotomy cases can hold at once.
  • Exhaustiveness: The three cases cover every comparison in a total order.
  • Sign Analysis: Every real number is negative, zero, or positive.
  • Integers: The ordinary integer order satisfies trichotomy.
  • Rational Numbers: The ordinary rational order satisfies trichotomy.
  • Real Numbers: The ordinary real order satisfies trichotomy.
  • Total Orders: Every pair of elements is comparable.
  • Partial Orders: Trichotomy can fail because incomparable elements may exist.
  • Complex Numbers: No standard field-compatible ordering gives the usual real-style trichotomy.
  • Proof by Cases: Trichotomy supplies a natural three-case logical partition.
  • Inequalities: Comparison problems often reduce to determining the sign of a difference.
  • Absolute Value: Piecewise definitions depend on sign-based case distinctions.
  • Common Error: Using trichotomy in a structure where elements may be incomparable.
  • Common Error: Treating $a<b$ and $a\leq b$ as interchangeable.
  • Common Error: Forgetting that equality is its own third case.
  • Common Error: Attempting ordinary greater-than or less-than comparisons of complex numbers without defining a separate ordering convention.
  • Core Interpretation: Trichotomy divides comparison into exactly three exhaustive and mutually exclusive cases: $$ \boxed{ a<b \quad\text{or}\quad a=b \quad\text{or}\quad a>b }. $$ It is a foundational property of ordinary ordered number systems and a central logical tool for inequalities, sign analysis, ordering, and proofs by cases.

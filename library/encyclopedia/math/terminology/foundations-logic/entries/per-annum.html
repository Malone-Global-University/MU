Per Annum

Subfield: Foundations & Logic

Etymology & Roots

Per annum is a Latin-derived phrase meaning per year or for each year. In mathematics, finance, economics, science, and measurement, it indicates that a quantity is being expressed relative to a one-year time interval.

$$ \boxed{ \text{per annum} = \text{per year} } $$

The Latin word per means "through," "by," or "for each," while annum is a form of the Latin word for year. In modern technical language, the phrase functions primarily as a unit qualifier.

For example:

$$ \boxed{ 5\%\text{ per annum} = 5\%\text{ per year} }. $$

The phrase does not by itself specify how a rate is applied. A quoted rate of $5\%$ per annum could refer to simple interest, an effective annual rate, a nominal annual rate compounded periodically, a continuous growth parameter, or some other annualized convention.

$$ \boxed{ \text{annual rate} \neq \text{complete compounding rule} }. $$

Thus per annum identifies the time basis, while additional information may be needed to determine the precise mathematical operation.

Operational Nuance

Unit Meaning

A quantity stated per annum is normalized to one year.

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\text{quantity}}{\text{year}} }. $$

Rate Example

A salary of:

$$ \$60{,}000\text{ per annum} $$

means:

$$ \boxed{ \$60{,}000 \text{ for one year} }. $$

Percentage per Annum

If a rate is:

$$ 6\%\text{ per annum}, $$

the decimal annual rate is:

$$ \boxed{ r=0.06 }. $$

Percentage Conversion

$$ \boxed{ 6\% = \frac{6}{100} = 0.06 }. $$

Simple Annual Rate

Under simple interest:

$$ \boxed{ I=Prt } $$

where:

  • $P$ is principal;
  • $r$ is the annual rate as a decimal;
  • $t$ is time in years;
  • $I$ is interest.

Simple-Interest Amount

$$ \boxed{ A=P(1+rt) }. $$

Example

Suppose:

$$ P=1000, \qquad r=0.05, \qquad t=3. $$

Then:

$$ I = 1000(0.05)(3) = \boxed{ 150 }. $$

Compound Interest

An annual rate can also be used in a compound-interest model:

$$ \boxed{ A = P \left( 1+\frac{r}{n} \right)^{nt} }. $$

Here $r$ is a nominal annual rate and $n$ is the number of compounding periods per year.

Annual Compounding

If compounding occurs once per year:

$$ n=1, $$

so:

$$ \boxed{ A=P(1+r)^t }. $$

Monthly Compounding

If a nominal rate $r$ per annum is compounded monthly:

$$ \boxed{ n=12 }. $$

and the periodic rate is:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{r}{12} }. $$

Quarterly Compounding

$$ \boxed{ n=4, \qquad \text{periodic rate} = \frac{r}{4} }. $$

Daily Compounding

A daily-compounding convention may use:

$$ \boxed{ n=365 } $$

or another specified day-count convention.

Per Annum Does Not Imply Annual Compounding

A rate may be quoted annually while compounding occurs more frequently.

$$ \boxed{ \text{rate quoted per annum} \not\Longrightarrow \text{compounded once per year} }. $$

Effective Annual Rate

If a nominal annual rate $r$ is compounded $n$ times per year, the effective annual rate is:

$$ \boxed{ r_{\mathrm{eff}} = \left( 1+\frac{r}{n} \right)^n -1 }. $$

Example

A nominal annual rate of $12\%$ compounded monthly has:

$$ r=0.12, \qquad n=12. $$

Therefore:

$$ r_{\mathrm{eff}} = \left( 1+\frac{0.12}{12} \right)^{12} -1. $$

This effective annual rate is greater than $12\%$ because of intra-year compounding.

Continuous Compounding

For continuous compounding:

$$ \boxed{ A=Pe^{rt} }. $$

Here $r$ is a continuously compounded annual rate when $t$ is measured in years.

Effective Rate under Continuous Compounding

$$ \boxed{ r_{\mathrm{eff}} = e^r-1 }. $$

Annual Growth Factor

A growth rate $r$ per annum corresponds under annual discrete compounding to a one-year multiplication factor:

$$ \boxed{ 1+r }. $$

Annual Decay Factor

A decay rate $d$ per annum, with $0<d<1$, corresponds to:

$$ \boxed{ 1-d }. $$

Example of Annual Growth

A population growing $4\%$ per annum under annual compounding satisfies:

$$ \boxed{ P(t) = P_0(1.04)^t }. $$

Example of Annual Decay

A quantity decreasing $8\%$ per annum under annual discrete decay satisfies:

$$ \boxed{ Q(t) = Q_0(0.92)^t }. $$

Annual Rate vs. Annual Change

A percentage per annum is usually relative to some base quantity, while an absolute annual change uses units such as:

$$ \boxed{ \$5000/\text{year} } $$

or:

$$ \boxed{ 200\text{ people}/\text{year} }. $$

Dimensional Interpretation

The phrase per annum contributes inverse-time units:

$$ \boxed{ \text{year}^{-1} }. $$

Rate Constant Units

In:

$$ \frac{dP}{dt}=kP, $$

if $t$ is measured in years, then:

$$ \boxed{ [k] = \text{year}^{-1} }. $$

Continuous Relative Growth Rate

For:

$$ P(t)=P_0e^{kt}, $$

the parameter $k$ represents a continuously compounded relative growth rate per unit time.

Discrete vs. Continuous Annual Rate

A discrete annual rate $r$ and a continuous annual rate $k$ that produce the same one-year growth satisfy:

$$ \boxed{ 1+r=e^k }. $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ k=\ln(1+r) }. $$

Reverse Conversion

$$ \boxed{ r=e^k-1 }. $$

Annualizing a Periodic Rate

If a periodic effective rate is $i$ and there are $n$ identical periods per year, the equivalent effective annual rate is:

$$ \boxed{ (1+i)^n-1 }. $$

Monthly Effective Rate to Annual Effective Rate

$$ \boxed{ r_{\mathrm{annual}} = (1+r_{\mathrm{monthly}})^{12}-1 }. $$

Annual Effective Rate to Monthly Effective Rate

$$ \boxed{ r_{\mathrm{monthly}} = (1+r_{\mathrm{annual}})^{1/12}-1 }. $$

Why Dividing by 12 May Be Wrong

Dividing an annual effective rate by $12$ does not generally produce the equivalent monthly effective rate because compounding is multiplicative.

Nominal-Rate Exception

If a quoted nominal annual rate is explicitly convertible monthly, then the contractual periodic rate may be defined as:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{r_{\mathrm{nominal}}}{12} }. $$

This is a convention attached to the nominal rate, not a universal law for all annual rates.

Annualized Rate

An annualized rate expresses a shorter-period performance or rate on an equivalent one-year basis.

Simple Annualization

In some noncompounded contexts, a monthly amount $M$ may be annualized as:

$$ \boxed{ 12M }. $$

Compound Annualization

For growth rates, compound annualization generally uses powers rather than simple multiplication.

Annual Percentage Rate

APR commonly abbreviates annual percentage rate. Its exact legal and financial definition depends on jurisdiction and product.

Annual Percentage Yield

APY commonly refers to an effective annual yield that incorporates compounding according to the applicable convention.

APR and APY Are Not Automatically Equal

$$ \boxed{ \text{nominal annual rate} \neq \text{effective annual yield} } $$

whenever intra-year compounding changes the one-year growth factor.

Per Annum in Loans

Loan interest may be quoted per annum even though interest accrues daily, monthly, or according to another schedule.

Per Annum in Investments

Investment returns may be expressed as annualized percentages to permit comparison across different holding periods.

Per Annum in Rent

Commercial leases may quote rent as:

$$ \boxed{ \$X\text{ per annum} } $$

or as an annual amount per unit area.

Per Square Foot per Annum

A property rate might be written:

$$ \boxed{ \$25 / (\text{ft}^2\cdot\text{year}) }. $$

This indicates an annual charge of $25$ for each square foot.

Annual Rent Calculation

For area $A$ square feet and rent $r$ dollars per square foot per annum:

$$ \boxed{ R_{\mathrm{annual}} = Ar }. $$

Monthly Equivalent from Annual Rent

If the annual amount is evenly divided into twelve equal payments:

$$ \boxed{ R_{\mathrm{monthly}} = \frac{R_{\mathrm{annual}}}{12} }. $$

This is a payment conversion, not necessarily an interest-rate conversion.

Per Annum in Salaries

A salary quoted annually can be divided by the number of pay periods when the annual salary is allocated evenly.

Biweekly Payroll Example

With $26$ equal pay periods:

$$ \boxed{ \text{gross pay per period} = \frac{\text{annual salary}}{26} }. $$

Weekly Equivalent

$$ \boxed{ \text{weekly equivalent} = \frac{\text{annual amount}}{52} } $$

when using a simplified $52$-week conversion.

Daily Equivalent

Annual-to-daily conversion depends on the convention:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\text{annual amount}}{365} } $$

may be appropriate in one setting, while financial contracts may use $360$, actual calendar days, business days, or other bases.

Day-Count Convention

In finance, the effective fraction of a year may depend on conventions such as actual/365, actual/360, 30/360, or other specified methods.

Per Annum Is Not Always Exactly 365 Days Operationally

"Year" is the conceptual time unit, but contractual calculations may define how fractions of that year are measured.

Leap-Year Caveat

A calendar year may contain $365$ or $366$ days, so converting an annual amount to a daily amount requires an explicit convention when exactness matters.

Rate as a Quotient

A rate per annum can be viewed conceptually as:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t} } $$

with time measured in years.

Average Annual Rate

An average rate over a time interval can be:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{Q(t_2)-Q(t_1)} {t_2-t_1} }. $$

Average Annual Growth Percentage

Simple averaging of yearly percentage changes generally differs from a compounded annual growth rate.

Compound Annual Growth Rate

If a quantity changes from $V_0$ to $V_n$ over $n$ years, the compound annual growth rate is:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{CAGR} = \left( \frac{V_n}{V_0} \right)^{1/n} -1 }. $$

Meaning of CAGR

CAGR is the constant annual compound rate that would transform $V_0$ into $V_n$ over the specified number of years.

CAGR Is Not the Arithmetic Mean of Annual Returns

If annual returns vary, the compound-equivalent annual rate is governed by the geometric product of growth factors.

Growth Factors across Years

If annual effective rates are $r_1,\ldots,r_n$, then:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{V_n}{V_0} = \prod_{j=1}^{n} (1+r_j) }. $$

Equivalent Constant Annual Rate

$$ \boxed{ 1+r_{\mathrm{eq}} = \left[ \prod_{j=1}^{n} (1+r_j) \right]^{1/n} }. $$

Inflation per Annum

An inflation rate of $r$ per annum indicates annual proportional change in a price index under the stated measurement convention.

Population Growth per Annum

Population changes may be quoted as persons per annum or as a percentage per annum. These are different dimensional statements.

Absolute Population Change

$$ \boxed{ 10{,}000\text{ people/year} } $$

Relative Population Change

$$ \boxed{ 2\%\text{ per year} }. $$

Scientific Rates

Environmental, demographic, biological, and engineering quantities can all be expressed per annum when a yearly time scale is convenient.

Mortality or Failure Rates

A rate per annum may sometimes represent a hazard or event frequency rather than a simple percentage change. The underlying mathematical interpretation must therefore be read from context.

Rate vs. Probability

A rate with units of $\text{year}^{-1}$ is not automatically the same thing as an annual probability.

$$ \boxed{ \text{rate} \neq \text{probability} } $$

even though one may sometimes be converted into the other under a specified model.

Constant Hazard Example

If a continuous hazard rate is $\lambda$ per year, then under a constant-hazard model the one-year survival probability is:

$$ \boxed{ e^{-\lambda} }. $$

and the one-year event probability is:

$$ \boxed{ 1-e^{-\lambda} }. $$

Thus $\lambda$ itself is not generally the annual event probability.

Per Annum and Dimensional Analysis

Units should be carried explicitly when possible:

$$ \boxed{ 0.08\text{ year}^{-1} } $$

may communicate the mathematical rate structure more clearly than an isolated decimal.

Percentage per Annum and Units

A percentage is dimensionless as a ratio, while "per annum" supplies the temporal interpretation.

Annual Rate of Change

If:

$$ Q=Q(t), $$

with $t$ in years, then:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{dQ}{dt} } $$

has units of quantity per annum.

Relative Rate of Change

$$ \boxed{ \frac{1}{Q} \frac{dQ}{dt} } $$

has inverse-time units and may be expressed as a percentage per annum.

Per Annum vs. Annually

Per annum means "per year." Annually can mean either "once each year" or "on a yearly basis," depending on context.

Important Language Distinction

A rate of $6\%$ per annum compounded monthly is perfectly coherent: the rate is annualized while the compounding operation occurs monthly.

Per Annum vs. Per Calendar Year

Some contracts or datasets may distinguish a generic annual rate from a quantity measured specifically over calendar years. Context determines the time interval used.

Per Annum vs. Year over Year

Year over year commonly compares a value with the corresponding value one year earlier:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{V_t-V_{t-1}}{V_{t-1}} }. $$

This is not automatically synonymous with an annualized rate.

Per Annum vs. Annualized

Per annum identifies a yearly basis. Annualized often means a rate observed over another period has been converted to an equivalent or projected yearly rate.

Per Annum vs. Per Capita

These are different "per" constructions:

$$ \boxed{ \text{per annum} = \text{per year} } $$
$$ \boxed{ \text{per capita} = \text{per person} }. $$

Combined Rates

A quantity may be expressed with both:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\text{units}} {\text{person}\cdot\text{year}} }. $$

This corresponds to a rate per person per annum.

Common Error: Treating Per Annum as a Compounding Instruction

The phrase specifies the annual basis, not the compounding frequency.

Common Error: Assuming $12\%$ Per Annum Means $1\%$ Effective per Month

That conclusion is valid only under a nominal-rate convention that explicitly defines the monthly periodic rate as $12\%/12$.

Common Error: Dividing an Effective Annual Rate by $12$

The equivalent monthly effective rate requires a twelfth root:

$$ \boxed{ (1+r_{\mathrm{annual}})^{1/12}-1 }. $$

Common Error: Multiplying a Monthly Effective Rate by $12$

Compound annualization requires:

$$ \boxed{ (1+r_{\mathrm{monthly}})^{12}-1 }. $$

Common Error: Confusing Nominal and Effective Annual Rates

A nominal annual percentage can differ from the actual one-year percentage change produced by compounding.

Common Error: Treating Percentage as an Absolute Amount

A percentage rate must be applied to an appropriate base quantity.

Common Error: Forgetting to Convert Percent to Decimal

In formulas:

$$ \boxed{ 7\% = 0.07 } $$

rather than $7$.

Common Error: Mixing Months and Years

If $r$ is per annum and the formula expects time in years, then $18$ months should be converted to:

$$ \boxed{ 1.5\text{ years} }. $$

Common Error: Ignoring Units

A rate of $500$ per annum and a rate of $500$ per month are not comparable without conversion.

Common Error: Assuming Every Year Has the Same Number of Days for Contractual Calculations

Day-count conventions can matter in precise financial calculations.

Common Error: Confusing Annual Salary with Annual Hourly Rate

A salary quoted per annum is an annual compensation amount, not a rate per hour unless converted using an assumed work schedule.

Common Error: Confusing Annual Rent with Monthly Payment

Annual rent may be paid monthly, quarterly, or according to another schedule.

Common Error: Assuming an Annualized Return Was Actually Earned for a Full Year

Annualization may extrapolate or convert a shorter-period return for comparison.

Common Error: Confusing CAGR with Average Annual Percentage Change

CAGR is a compound-equivalent geometric rate, not ordinarily the arithmetic mean of annual returns.

Common Error: Treating a Hazard Rate as a Probability

Rates and probabilities have different mathematical structures and units.

Common Error: Assuming Per Annum Means the Same Formula in Every Field

The phrase provides a time basis, but the surrounding mathematical model determines whether the quantity is simple, compound, continuous, average, instantaneous, effective, nominal, or contractual.

Common Misconception: "Per annum" does not mean "compounded annually." It means only "per year." A rate may be quoted per annum while compounding monthly, daily, continuously, or not at all. Correct interpretation therefore requires both the annual rate and the rule describing how that rate is applied.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$5\%\text{ per annum}$ "Five percent per annum," or "five percent per year."
$0.05\text{ year}^{-1}$ "Zero point zero five per year."
$A=P(1+r)^t$ "The amount under annual discrete compounding at annual rate r."
$A=P(1+r/n)^{nt}$ "Compound interest using nominal annual rate r compounded n times per year."
$r_{\mathrm{eff}}=(1+r/n)^n-1$ "The effective annual rate corresponding to the nominal rate."
$A=Pe^{rt}$ "Continuous compounding at annual continuous rate r."
$\operatorname{CAGR}=(V_n/V_0)^{1/n}-1$ "The compound annual growth rate over n years."

The abbreviation:

$$ \boxed{ \text{p.a.} } $$

is commonly used for per annum, especially in financial, contractual, and commercial contexts.

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ 7\%\text{ p.a.} = 7\%\text{ per annum} }. $$

In mathematical writing, the unit form:

$$ \boxed{ \text{year}^{-1} } $$

can be preferable when dimensional structure matters.

Defense Notation Advice: State whether the quoted annual rate is simple, nominal, effective, continuously compounded, or merely annualized. Keep time units consistent with the formula. If a rate is quoted per annum but applied monthly or daily, identify the conversion convention explicitly. Do not infer compounding frequency from the phrase per annum alone.

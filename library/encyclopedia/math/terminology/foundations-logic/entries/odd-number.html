Odd Number

Subfield: Foundations & Logic

Etymology & Roots

An odd number is an integer that is not divisible by $2$. Equivalently, an integer is odd when division by $2$ leaves remainder $1$.

$$ \boxed{ n\text{ is odd} \iff n=2k+1 \text{ for some }k\in\mathbb{Z} } $$

The word odd entered mathematical language through an older sense of something unmatched, left over, or not paired. This fits the elementary grouping interpretation: when an odd number of objects is arranged in pairs, one object remains unpaired.

For example, $7$ can be grouped as:

$$ 7 = 2+2+2+1. $$

The final $1$ is the remainder after forming as many pairs as possible.

This pairing idea becomes the formal arithmetic statement:

$$ \boxed{ n\equiv1\pmod2 }. $$

Thus odd is fundamentally a statement about parity, the classification of integers into exactly two classes: even and odd.

Operational Nuance

Standard Algebraic Form

Every odd integer can be written:

$$ \boxed{ 2k+1, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$

Equivalent Form

Since shifting the integer parameter changes nothing essential, odd integers can also be written:

$$ \boxed{ 2k-1, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$

Examples

$$ \ldots,-7,-5,-3,-1,1,3,5,7,9,\ldots $$

Zero Is Not Odd

Since:

$$ 0=2(0), $$

zero is even.

$$ \boxed{ 0\text{ is even, not odd} }. $$

Negative Odd Numbers

Oddness is not restricted to positive integers.

For example:

$$ -5 = 2(-3)+1. $$

Therefore $-5$ is odd.

Divisibility Criterion

An integer $n$ is odd exactly when:

$$ \boxed{ 2\nmid n }. $$

Remainder Criterion

By the Division Algorithm, every integer $n$ can be written uniquely as:

$$ n=2q+r, \qquad 0\leq r<2. $$

Therefore $r$ is either $0$ or $1$.

  • $r=0$ gives an even integer;
  • $r=1$ gives an odd integer.

Modular Criterion

$$ \boxed{ n\text{ odd} \iff n\equiv1\pmod2 }. $$

Parity

Parity means whether an integer is even or odd.

$$ \boxed{ \mathbb{Z} = \{\text{even integers}\} \cup \{\text{odd integers}\} } $$

with the two classes disjoint.

Every Integer Has Exactly One Parity

$$ \boxed{ \text{every integer is exactly one of even or odd} }. $$

Last-Digit Test in Base Ten

A decimal integer is odd exactly when its final digit is one of:

$$ \boxed{ 1,\ 3,\ 5,\ 7,\ 9 }. $$

Why the Last-Digit Test Works

Any decimal integer can be written:

$$ n=10q+d, $$

where $d$ is the last digit.

Since $10q$ is even, $n$ has the same parity as $d$.

Binary Interpretation

In base two, an integer is odd exactly when its least significant binary digit is $1$.

$$ \boxed{ n\text{ odd} \iff \text{binary representation ends in }1 }. $$

Successive Integers Alternate Parity

If $n$ is odd, then:

$$ n+1 $$

and:

$$ n-1 $$

are even.

Consecutive Odd Integers

Consecutive odd integers differ by $2$.

$$ \boxed{ 2k+1,\quad 2k+3,\quad 2k+5,\quad\ldots }. $$

Difference between Odd Numbers

The difference of any two odd integers is even.

$$ (2a+1)-(2b+1) = 2(a-b). $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \text{odd}-\text{odd} = \text{even} }. $$

Sum of Two Odd Numbers

$$ (2a+1)+(2b+1) = 2(a+b+1). $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ \text{odd}+\text{odd} = \text{even} }. $$

Odd Plus Even

$$ (2a+1)+2b = 2(a+b)+1. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \text{odd}+\text{even} = \text{odd} }. $$

Odd Minus Even

$$ (2a+1)-2b = 2(a-b)+1. $$

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ \text{odd}-\text{even} = \text{odd} }. $$

Even Minus Odd

$$ 2a-(2b+1) = 2(a-b-1)+1. $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ \text{even}-\text{odd} = \text{odd} }. $$

Odd Times Odd

$$ (2a+1)(2b+1) = 4ab+2a+2b+1. $$

Rewriting:

$$ (2a+1)(2b+1) = 2(2ab+a+b)+1. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \text{odd}\cdot\text{odd} = \text{odd} }. $$

Odd Times Even

If one factor is even:

$$ (2a+1)(2b) = 2b(2a+1), $$

so:

$$ \boxed{ \text{odd}\cdot\text{even} = \text{even} }. $$

Product Is Odd Exactly When Every Integer Factor Is Odd

$$ \boxed{ ab\text{ odd} \iff a\text{ odd and }b\text{ odd} } $$

for integers $a$ and $b$.

Odd Powers

Every positive integer power of an odd integer is odd.

$$ \boxed{ n\text{ odd} \Longrightarrow n^k\text{ odd} } $$

for positive integers $k$.

Square of an Odd Integer

Let:

$$ n=2k+1. $$

Then:

$$ n^2 = 4k^2+4k+1 = 4k(k+1)+1. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ n^2\equiv1\pmod8 } $$

for every odd integer $n$.

Odd Squares Modulo $8$

$$ \boxed{ \text{every odd square is congruent to }1\pmod8 }. $$

Odd Squares Modulo $4$

In particular:

$$ \boxed{ n^2\equiv1\pmod4 } $$

for odd $n$.

Parity of a Square

An integer and its square have the same parity:

$$ \boxed{ n^2\text{ odd} \iff n\text{ odd} }. $$

Proof by Contrapositive

If $n$ is even, then:

$$ n=2k, $$

so:

$$ n^2=4k^2=2(2k^2), $$

which is even. Therefore if $n^2$ is odd, $n$ must be odd.

Odd Cube

If $n$ is odd, then $n^3$ is odd.

Sum of an Odd Number of Odd Integers

The sum of an odd number of odd integers is odd.

Sum of an Even Number of Odd Integers

The sum of an even number of odd integers is even.

General Sum Rule

A finite sum of integers is odd exactly when it contains an odd number of odd summands.

$$ \boxed{ \text{sum parity} = \text{parity of number of odd summands} }. $$

Odd Numbers and Modulo $2$

Parity arithmetic can be reduced to arithmetic modulo $2$.

$$ \boxed{ \text{even}\leftrightarrow0, \qquad \text{odd}\leftrightarrow1 }. $$

Parity Addition Table

$$ \begin{aligned} 0+0 &\equiv 0\pmod2,\\ 0+1 &\equiv 1\pmod2,\\ 1+0 &\equiv 1\pmod2,\\ 1+1 &\equiv 0\pmod2. \end{aligned} $$

Parity Multiplication Table

$$ \begin{aligned} 0\cdot0 &\equiv 0\pmod2,\\ 0\cdot1 &\equiv 0\pmod2,\\ 1\cdot0 &\equiv 0\pmod2,\\ 1\cdot1 &\equiv 1\pmod2. \end{aligned} $$

Parity as a Two-Class System

Modulo $2$, every integer belongs to one of exactly two residue classes:

$$ \boxed{ [0]_2 \quad\text{or}\quad [1]_2 }. $$

Odd Numbers Are the Class $[1]_2$

$$ \boxed{ \{\text{odd integers}\} = [1]_2 }. $$

Consecutive Integer Product

Among any two consecutive integers, one is even and one is odd.

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ n(n+1)\text{ is always even} }. $$

Three Consecutive Integers

Among any three consecutive integers, either one or two are odd, depending on the parity of the starting integer.

Consecutive Odd Integer Modeling

Word problems involving consecutive odd integers can be modeled as:

$$ \boxed{ 2k+1,\quad 2k+3,\quad 2k+5 }. $$

Alternative Consecutive-Odd Model

$$ \boxed{ n,\quad n+2,\quad n+4 } $$

provided $n$ is explicitly declared odd.

Odd Prime Numbers

Every prime number greater than $2$ is odd.

$$ \boxed{ p\text{ prime and }p>2 \Longrightarrow p\text{ odd} }. $$

Why $2$ Is Exceptional

The number $2$ is the only even prime.

Odd Does Not Mean Prime

Many odd numbers are composite:

$$ 9=3^2, \qquad 15=3\cdot5. $$

Odd Composite Numbers

An odd composite number has no factor $2$, but it has some other nontrivial factorization.

Odd Divisors

Every divisor of an odd integer is odd.

Proof

If an odd integer had an even divisor, then the integer would be a product containing a factor $2$ and would therefore be even, a contradiction.

Greatest Common Divisor of Odd Numbers

The greatest common divisor of two odd positive integers is odd.

Least Common Multiple of Odd Numbers

The least common multiple of positive odd integers is odd.

Odd Factorial Behavior

For $n\geq2$, $n!$ is even because the factor $2$ appears in the product.

Thus the only positive odd factorial value in the usual sequence beyond the convention $0!=1$ is:

$$ 1!=1. $$

Sum of the First $n$ Odd Positive Integers

A fundamental identity is:

$$ \boxed{ 1+3+5+\cdots+(2n-1)=n^2 }. $$

Example

$$ 1+3+5+7+9 = 25 = 5^2. $$

Geometric Interpretation

Consecutive odd numbers can be viewed as successive L-shaped layers added to a square array, explaining geometrically why their partial sums are perfect squares.

Difference of Consecutive Squares

Consecutive perfect squares differ by an odd number:

$$ (n+1)^2-n^2 = 2n+1. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \text{difference of consecutive squares is odd} }. $$

Every Odd Integer Is a Difference of Consecutive Squares

Since:

$$ (k+1)^2-k^2 = 2k+1, $$

every positive odd integer has this representation.

Odd Number as Difference of Two Squares

More generally, every positive odd integer $n$ can be written:

$$ \boxed{ n = \left( \frac{n+1}{2} \right)^2 - \left( \frac{n-1}{2} \right)^2 }. $$

Polynomial Parity Testing

Expressions built from integers can often be classified as odd or even by reducing coefficients and variables modulo $2$.

Example

For integer $n$:

$$ n^2+n = n(n+1). $$

Since consecutive integers have opposite parity:

$$ \boxed{ n^2+n\text{ is always even} }. $$

Odd-Valued Expression Example

For any integer $n$:

$$ 2n^2+4n+1 = 2(n^2+2n)+1, $$

so the expression is always odd.

Parity Proof Technique

To prove an integer expression is odd, a standard strategy is to rewrite it in the form:

$$ \boxed{ 2k+1 } $$

for some integer $k$.

Direct Proof Example

Suppose $a$ and $b$ are odd. Then:

$$ a=2m+1, \qquad b=2n+1. $$

Their product is:

$$ ab = 2(2mn+m+n)+1, $$

which proves $ab$ is odd.

Proof by Contradiction

Parity arguments are frequently used in contradiction proofs, especially when a hypothesis forces a number to be both even and odd.

Irrationality Proof Connection

Classical proofs involving $\sqrt{2}$ use parity properties such as:

$$ n^2\text{ even} \Longrightarrow n\text{ even}. $$

Odd-number reasoning is the complementary side of the same parity structure.

Odd Functions Are Different

The term odd function is related linguistically but does not mean a function whose outputs are odd integers.

An odd function satisfies:

$$ \boxed{ f(-x)=-f(x) }. $$

Odd Number vs. Odd Function

An odd number is an integer parity classification. An odd function is a symmetry classification.

Odd Degree Is Different

A polynomial of odd degree has an odd integer as its degree, but the polynomial itself is not thereby an "odd number."

Odd Exponent

An odd exponent is simply an exponent whose integer value is odd:

$$ 1,\ 3,\ 5,\ 7,\ldots $$

Parity and Computer Science

Computers commonly test integer parity using the remainder modulo $2$ or the least significant binary bit.

Modulo Test

$$ \boxed{ n\bmod2=1 } $$

is a common mathematical description for nonnegative odd integers, though programming-language remainder conventions for negative integers should be checked.

Bitwise Test

In binary arithmetic, odd integers have a final bit equal to $1$.

Parity Bits

In digital communication and storage, the term parity also appears in parity-bit schemes that count whether a collection contains an even or odd number of $1$ bits.

Odd Cardinality

A finite set can be described as having odd cardinality when its number of elements is an odd integer.

Odd Number of Objects

Any finite collection with odd cardinality leaves one item unmatched when all possible pairs are formed.

Odd Cycles

In graph theory, an odd cycle is a cycle containing an odd number of edges or vertices.

Bipartite-Graph Connection

A graph is bipartite exactly when it contains no odd cycle.

Odd Permutations

In group theory, an odd permutation is a permutation expressible as a product of an odd number of transpositions. This is another parity-based use of the adjective odd.

Common Error: Thinking Odd Means Positive

Negative integers such as $-3$ and $-11$ are odd.

Common Error: Thinking Zero Is Neither Even nor Odd

Zero is even because:

$$ 0=2(0). $$

Common Error: Thinking Odd Means Prime

Numbers such as $9$, $15$, and $21$ are odd but composite.

Common Error: Thinking Every Prime Is Odd

The prime number $2$ is even.

Common Error: Using $2k$ for an Odd Integer

The expression $2k$ always represents an even integer.

Odd integers require:

$$ \boxed{ 2k+1 } $$

or an equivalent form.

Common Error: Forgetting That $k$ Must Be an Integer

The characterization:

$$ n=2k+1 $$

establishes oddness only when:

$$ \boxed{ k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$

Common Error: Assuming Odd Plus Odd Is Odd

Two odd integers sum to an even integer.

Common Error: Assuming Odd Times Odd Is Even

The product of odd integers remains odd.

Common Error: Assuming an Odd Number Cannot Be Divisible by Anything

Odd means only "not divisible by $2$." An odd number may have many other divisors.

Common Error: Testing Parity from the First Digit

In base ten, the final digit determines parity, not the first digit.

Common Error: Calling Nonintegers Odd

In elementary number theory, even and odd are classifications of integers. Numbers such as:

$$ \frac{3}{2}, \qquad \sqrt{2}, \qquad \pi $$

are ordinarily neither even nor odd.

Common Error: Confusing Odd Number with Odd Function

Integer parity and function symmetry are distinct concepts.

Common Error: Assuming Every Odd Square Can Be Any Odd Residue Modulo $8$

In fact:

$$ \boxed{ n^2\equiv1\pmod8 } $$

for every odd integer $n$.

Common Misconception: An odd number is not simply a number with an "odd-looking" decimal representation or a positive number ending in $1$, $3$, $5$, $7$, or $9$. Formally, oddness is an integer divisibility property: $n=2k+1$ for some integer $k$, equivalently $n\equiv1\pmod2$. This definition includes negative integers and supports a complete algebra of parity for sums, differences, products, powers, and proofs.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$n=2k+1$ "N is odd because it is two times an integer plus one."
$n\equiv1\pmod2$ "N is congruent to one modulo two."
$2\nmid n$ "Two does not divide n."
$2k+1,\ 2k+3,\ 2k+5$ "Three consecutive odd integers."
$\text{odd}+\text{odd}=\text{even}$ "The sum of two odd integers is even."
$\text{odd}\cdot\text{odd}=\text{odd}$ "The product of two odd integers is odd."
$n^2\equiv1\pmod8$ "The square of an odd integer is congruent to one modulo eight."

In informal elementary speech, an odd number is often described as "a number that cannot be divided evenly by two." A more precise statement is that an odd integer is not divisible by $2$.

When proving parity algebraically, the standard written form is:

$$ \boxed{ n=2k+1, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$

The modular form:

$$ \boxed{ n\equiv1\pmod2 } $$

is especially compact in number theory and discrete mathematics.

Defense Notation Advice: Reserve even and odd classifications for integers unless a specialized context explicitly extends the terminology. When using $2k+1$, state or imply that $k$ is an integer. For consecutive odd integers, increase by $2$, not $1$. In modular arguments, $1\pmod2$ is the odd residue class, while $0\pmod2$ is the even class. Do not confuse odd-number parity with the unrelated definition of an odd function.

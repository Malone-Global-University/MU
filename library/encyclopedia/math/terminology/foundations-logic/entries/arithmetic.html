Arithmetic

Subfield: Foundations & Mathematical Logic

Etymology & Roots

Arithmetic is the branch of elementary mathematics concerned with numbers, numerical relationships, and computation. Its central operations are addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division, while elementary arithmetic commonly extends to fractions, decimals, ratios, percentages, powers, roots, and related numerical calculations.

The word ultimately derives from the Greek arithmos, meaning number, through Greek, Latin, Anglo-French, and Middle English forms. This origin reflects arithmetic's foundational concern with counting and numerical computation.

At its simplest, arithmetic asks questions such as:

$$ 7+5=? $$
$$ 18-11=? $$
$$ 6\cdot4=? $$
$$ 20\div5=? $$

But arithmetic is more than memorizing calculations. It includes the rules and structural properties that explain why numerical operations behave as they do.

Operational Nuance

Addition

Addition combines quantities into a sum:

$$ \boxed{ a+b=c }. $$

Here $a$ and $b$ are addends and $c$ is their sum.

Subtraction

Subtraction finds a difference:

$$ \boxed{ a-b=c }. $$

Here $a$ is the minuend, $b$ is the subtrahend, and $c$ is the difference.

Subtraction can be interpreted through additive inverses:

$$ \boxed{ a-b=a+(-b) }. $$

Multiplication

Multiplication produces a product:

$$ \boxed{ ab=c }. $$

In elementary settings it can describe repeated equal groups, scaling, array size, rectangular area, or combinations of quantities.

Division

Division produces a quotient:

$$ \boxed{ a\div b = \frac{a}{b} } $$

when:

$$ \boxed{ b\ne0 }. $$

Division may represent partitioning a quantity into equal groups or determining how many groups of a given size fit into another quantity.

Division by Zero

Ordinary arithmetic does not define:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{0} }. $$

There is no real or complex number $q$ satisfying:

$$ 0q=a $$

when $a\ne0$, and $0/0$ does not determine a unique quotient.

Exponentiation

Arithmetic commonly includes numerical powers:

$$ \boxed{ a^n }. $$

For a positive integer exponent:

$$ a^n = \underbrace{ a\cdot a\cdot\ldots\cdot a }_{n\text{ factors}}. $$

For example:

$$ 2^5=32. $$

Roots

Root operations reverse appropriate powers. For example:

$$ \boxed{ \sqrt{25}=5 } $$

because:

$$ 5^2=25. $$

Whole Numbers

Arithmetic often begins with the whole numbers:

$$ \boxed{ 0,1,2,3,\ldots }. $$

Within this set, addition and multiplication always produce whole numbers, but subtraction and division do not.

Integers

Allowing negative numbers gives the integers:

$$ \boxed{ \mathbb Z= \{\ldots,-2,-1,0,1,2,\ldots\} }. $$

Integers are closed under addition, subtraction, and multiplication, but not under ordinary division.

Rational Numbers

Fractions extend arithmetic to numbers of the form:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{p}{q}, \qquad p,q\in\mathbb Z, \quad q\ne0 }. $$

Rational numbers are closed under addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division by nonzero rational numbers.

Real Numbers

Arithmetic extends naturally to the real numbers:

$$ \boxed{ \mathbb R }. $$

These include rational and irrational numbers such as:

$$ \sqrt2, \qquad \pi, \qquad e. $$

Complex Arithmetic

Arithmetic also extends beyond the real numbers. If:

$$ z=a+bi, \qquad w=c+di, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ z+w= (a+c)+(b+d)i }. $$

Multiplication follows distributivity together with:

$$ \boxed{ i^2=-1 }. $$

Fractions

Fraction arithmetic follows the same operations but requires attention to denominators.

For addition:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} + \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ad+bc}{bd} } $$

when $b,d\ne0$.

For multiplication:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ac}{bd} }. $$

Division of Fractions

Dividing by a nonzero fraction is equivalent to multiplying by its reciprocal:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{d}{c} } $$

provided all required denominators are nonzero.

Decimals

Decimal notation is a base-ten representation of numbers. For example:

$$ 34.57 = 3(10) + 4(1) + 5\left(\frac1{10}\right) + 7\left(\frac1{100}\right). $$

Arithmetic with terminating decimals can be understood through place value and equivalent fraction arithmetic.

Percentages

A percentage expresses a number relative to $100$:

$$ \boxed{ p\% = \frac{p}{100} }. $$

For example:

$$ 25\% = \frac{25}{100} = \frac14 = 0.25. $$

Ratios

A ratio compares quantities multiplicatively:

$$ \boxed{ a:b = \frac{a}{b} } $$

when the quotient is defined.

Properties of Addition

Addition is commutative:

$$ \boxed{ a+b=b+a }. $$

It is associative:

$$ \boxed{ (a+b)+c = a+(b+c) }. $$

Its identity element is $0$:

$$ \boxed{ a+0=a }. $$

Additive Inverses

Every real or complex number $a$ has an additive inverse $-a$:

$$ \boxed{ a+(-a)=0 }. $$

Properties of Multiplication

Multiplication of ordinary real or complex numbers is commutative:

$$ \boxed{ ab=ba }. $$

It is associative:

$$ \boxed{ (ab)c=a(bc) }. $$

Its identity is $1$:

$$ \boxed{ a\cdot1=a }. $$

Multiplicative Inverses

Every nonzero real or complex number $a$ has reciprocal:

$$ \boxed{ a^{-1} = \frac1a }. $$

such that:

$$ a\left(\frac1a\right)=1. $$

Distributive Property

Multiplication distributes over addition:

$$ \boxed{ a(b+c) = ab+ac }. $$

This property forms one of the major bridges from arithmetic into algebra.

Subtraction Is Not Commutative

$$ \boxed{ a-b \ne b-a } $$

in general.

For example:

$$ 7-2=5, \qquad 2-7=-5. $$

Division Is Not Commutative

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a}{b} \ne \frac{b}{a} } $$

in general.

Subtraction Is Not Associative

$$ \boxed{ (a-b)-c \ne a-(b-c) } $$

in general.

Division Is Not Associative

$$ \boxed{ (a\div b)\div c \ne a\div(b\div c) } $$

in general.

Order of Operations

A numerical expression can contain several operations. Grouping symbols and precedence conventions specify which operations are performed first.

A common hierarchy is:

$$ \boxed{ \text{grouping} \rightarrow \text{powers and roots} \rightarrow \text{multiplication and division} \rightarrow \text{addition and subtraction} }. $$

Operations sharing the same precedence level are ordinarily processed according to the expression's grouping and standard left-to-right parsing conventions.

Example of Precedence

$$ 2+3\cdot4 = 2+12 = \boxed{14}. $$

But:

$$ (2+3)\cdot4 = 5\cdot4 = \boxed{20}. $$

Parentheses alter the grouping and therefore the result.

Exact Arithmetic

An exact result preserves the mathematical value without approximation:

$$ \boxed{ \frac13+\frac16 = \frac12 }. $$

Approximate Arithmetic

Decimal approximations may be used when an exact value is inconvenient:

$$ \pi \approx 3.14159. $$

The symbol:

$$ \boxed{ \approx } $$

indicates approximation rather than exact equality.

Rounding

Rounding replaces a number with a nearby representable value at a chosen place value or precision.

For example:

$$ 7.386 \approx 7.39 $$

to the nearest hundredth.

Estimation

Arithmetic also includes estimation strategies used to judge whether an exact or calculator-produced answer is reasonable.

For example:

$$ 19.8(5.1) \approx 20(5) = 100. $$

An exact result far from $100$ would deserve rechecking.

Arithmetic Mean

The word arithmetic appears in the arithmetic mean:

$$ \boxed{ \bar{x} = \frac{x_1+x_2+\cdots+x_n}{n} }. $$

The term distinguishes it from means such as the geometric and harmonic means.

Arithmetic Sequence

An arithmetic sequence has constant successive difference:

$$ \boxed{ a_{n+1}-a_n=d }. $$

Here arithmetic refers to additive rather than multiplicative progression.

Arithmetic Series

The sum of the first $n$ terms of an arithmetic sequence is:

$$ \boxed{ S_n= \frac{n}{2}(a_1+a_n) }. $$

Modular Arithmetic

The term modular arithmetic refers to arithmetic performed with congruence classes relative to a modulus.

For example:

$$ \boxed{ 17\equiv5\pmod{12} } $$

because:

$$ 12\mid(17-5). $$

Clock arithmetic is a familiar application of this idea.

Integer Arithmetic

The phrase integer arithmetic emphasizes calculations carried out within $\mathbb Z$, often with exact results and without fractional intermediate values unless division is introduced.

Floating-Point Arithmetic

In computing, floating-point arithmetic uses finite digital representations to approximate real-number calculations.

Consequently, a computer may represent some simple decimal quantities only approximately.

$$ \boxed{ \text{mathematical real arithmetic} \ne \text{finite floating-point arithmetic} }. $$

Mental Arithmetic

Mental arithmetic means performing numerical computation mentally, without relying primarily on written algorithms or computational devices.

Arithmetic versus Calculation

Calculation refers to carrying out a computation. Arithmetic can refer both to such calculation and to the branch of mathematics governing the underlying numerical operations.

Arithmetic versus Algebra

Arithmetic primarily works with particular numbers:

$$ 7+8=15. $$

Algebra generalizes numerical relationships using symbols:

$$ \boxed{ a+b=b+a }. $$

Thus algebra does not replace arithmetic; it abstracts and generalizes arithmetic patterns.

Arithmetic versus Number Theory

Elementary arithmetic emphasizes computation with numbers. Number theory studies deeper structural properties of numbers, especially integers, such as:

  • divisibility,
  • prime numbers,
  • congruences,
  • Diophantine equations.

The areas overlap, but their emphasis differs.

Arithmetic versus Analysis

Arithmetic concerns discrete numerical operations and computation, while mathematical analysis studies limiting processes, continuity, differentiation, integration, infinite series, and related structures.

Analysis still depends continuously on arithmetic operations.

Arithmetic as a Foundation

More advanced mathematics repeatedly reuses arithmetic structures. Algebra, geometry, statistics, calculus, linear algebra, numerical analysis, and computer science all depend on reliable numerical operation.

$$ \boxed{ \text{arithmetic is foundational rather than isolated} }. $$

Common Misconception

Arithmetic is not merely "easy math" or a collection of memorized facts. Its laws, inverses, identities, number systems, representations, and operational conventions form much of the structural foundation upon which later mathematics is built.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$a+b$ "A plus b."
$a-b$ "A minus b."
$ab$ "A times b," or "the product of a and b."
$a/b$ "A divided by b," or "a over b."
$a^n$ "A to the nth power."
$\sqrt[n]{a}$ "The nth root of a."
$a\equiv b\pmod n$ "A is congruent to b modulo n."

The noun arithmetic can refer either to a mathematical subject or to a particular calculation. For example, "check the arithmetic" usually means "check the numerical computation," while "study arithmetic" refers to the broader subject.

Defense Notation Advice: Numerical notation can become ambiguous when grouping is omitted. Parentheses, fraction bars, radicals, and exponent placement should be used deliberately so the intended arithmetic structure is visible rather than inferred.

First Derivative

Subfield: Calculus & Analysis

Etymology & Roots

The first derivative is the derivative obtained from the first application of differentiation to a function. It measures the function's instantaneous rate of change and, for a graph, the slope of its tangent line.

$$ \boxed{ f'(x) = \lim_{h\to0} \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h} } $$

The adjective first distinguishes this derivative from repeated derivatives such as $f''$, $f^{(3)}$, and higher orders.

Operational Nuance

Derivative Function

When $f$ is differentiable throughout a region, its first derivative is itself a function:

$$ \boxed{ x\mapsto f'(x) }. $$

It assigns to each differentiable input the local rate of change of $f$ there.

Derivative at a Point

The notation $f'(a)$ refers to the first derivative evaluated at the specific input $a$. Geometrically:

$$ \boxed{ f'(a)=m_{\text{tangent}} }. $$

Increasing and Decreasing Behavior

The sign of the first derivative provides local information about change:

$$ \boxed{ f'(x)>0 \Rightarrow f\text{ increasing}, \qquad f'(x)<0 \Rightarrow f\text{ decreasing} } $$

on intervals where the corresponding sign condition holds.

Critical Numbers

Interior candidates for local extrema occur among critical numbers, where:

$$ f'(c)=0 $$

or where $f'(c)$ does not exist while $f(c)$ is defined.

First-Derivative Test

A sign change in $f'$ can classify a local extremum:

$$ \boxed{ +\to- \Rightarrow \text{local maximum}, \qquad -\to+ \Rightarrow \text{local minimum} }. $$

Motion

If $s(t)$ is position, its first derivative is velocity:

$$ \boxed{ v(t)=s'(t) }. $$

The second derivative $s''(t)$ then represents acceleration.

Units

The units of a first derivative are output units divided by input units. If distance is measured in meters and time in seconds, $ds/dt$ has units of meters per second.

Common Misconception

A zero first derivative does not automatically mean the function has a maximum or minimum. The derivative's behavior on neighboring points must also be examined.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$f'(x)$ "F prime of x."
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ "The first derivative of y with respect to x."
$\dfrac{d}{dx}f(x)$ "The derivative of f with respect to x."
$f'(a)$ "The derivative of f at a."
$f'(x)=0$ "The first derivative is zero."
$f'(x)>0$ "The first derivative is positive."

In ordinary calculus, derivative often means first derivative unless a higher order is specified. The explicit phrase becomes useful when several derivative orders are being compared.

Defense Notation Advice: Distinguish the derivative function $f'(x)$ from its value $f'(a)$ at one point, and distinguish a zero derivative from an actual maximum or minimum.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Meaning: The first derivative measures instantaneous rate of change.
  • Geometry: $f'(a)$ gives the tangent-line slope at a differentiable point.
  • Increasing Functions: Positive first derivative indicates increasing behavior on an interval.
  • Decreasing Functions: Negative first derivative indicates decreasing behavior on an interval.
  • Critical Numbers: Zeros and certain undefined points of the first derivative provide candidates for extrema.
  • Optimization: First-derivative sign analysis is used to classify local maxima and minima.
  • Kinematics: The first derivative of position is velocity.
  • Economics: First derivatives appear as marginal cost, marginal revenue, and other marginal quantities.
  • Modeling: First derivatives express sensitivity of outputs to small changes in inputs.
  • Higher Derivatives: Differentiating $f'$ produces the second derivative $f''$.
  • Common Error: Treating $f'(x)=0$ as sufficient proof of an extremum.
  • Common Error: Confusing $f'(x)$ with $f(x)$ or with the differential $df$.
  • Common Error: Ignoring the units of the derivative in applied problems.
  • Core Interpretation: The first derivative translates local change into a new function: $$ \boxed{ f(x) \longrightarrow f'(x) \longrightarrow \text{slope, rate, direction of change, and sensitivity} }. $$ It is the primary first-order tool for analyzing how a differentiable function behaves from point to point.

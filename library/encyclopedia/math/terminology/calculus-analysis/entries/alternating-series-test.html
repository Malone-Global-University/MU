Alternating Series Test

Subfield: Calculus & Analysis

Etymology & Roots

The Alternating Series Test is a convergence theorem for series whose terms alternate in sign and whose magnitudes decrease toward zero. It is also commonly called the Leibniz test.

$$ \boxed{ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}b_n } $$

with $b_n\geq0$ is the standard form. The word alternating describes the sign pattern, while test indicates that the theorem supplies sufficient conditions for convergence.

Operational Nuance

The Two Conditions

The test applies when the positive magnitudes $b_n$ satisfy:

$$ \boxed{ b_{n+1}\leq b_n \text{ eventually} } $$

and:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{n\to\infty}b_n=0 }. $$

Under these conditions:

$$ \boxed{ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}b_n \text{ converges} }. $$

Eventual Decrease Is Enough

The magnitudes do not need to decrease from the first term onward. It is sufficient that they become nonincreasing after some finite index.

Why the Limit Condition Matters

If $b_n$ does not tend to zero, then the original series cannot converge because its terms fail the basic necessary condition:

$$ \boxed{ a_n\to0 }. $$

Classic Example

$$ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n} $$

converges because $1/n$ decreases to zero.

Error Estimate

When the test applies, the remainder after the first $N$ terms satisfies:

$$ \boxed{ |R_N| \leq b_{N+1} }. $$

Thus the first omitted magnitude provides a direct upper bound on the approximation error.

Direction of the Error

Under the usual hypotheses, successive partial sums lie on alternating sides of the limiting sum. This explains why the first omitted term controls the error.

Absolute vs. Conditional Convergence

The Alternating Series Test proves ordinary convergence. It does not by itself tell whether the convergence is absolute. To determine that, separately examine:

$$ \boxed{ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_n| }. $$

If the absolute-value series diverges while the alternating series converges, the original series is conditionally convergent.

Failure of the Test

If one of the test's hypotheses fails, the theorem becomes inconclusive. Failure of the Alternating Series Test does not automatically imply that the series diverges.

Common Misconception

Alternating signs alone do not guarantee convergence. The magnitudes must approach zero, and the standard test additionally requires eventual nonincrease.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\sum(-1)^{n-1}b_n$ "An alternating series with magnitudes b sub n."
$b_{n+1}\leq b_n$ "The term magnitudes are eventually nonincreasing."
$b_n\to0$ "The term magnitudes tend to zero."
$|R_N|\leq b_{N+1}$ "The remainder is no larger than the first omitted term in magnitude."
$S_N$ "The Nth partial sum."
$R_N=S-S_N$ "The remainder after N terms."

The expressions Alternating Series Test, Leibniz test, and alternating-series criterion may refer to the same theorem, though textbook terminology varies.

Defense Notation Advice: Rewrite the series so that $b_n$ denotes the nonnegative magnitude. Check eventual decrease and the limit $b_n\to0$ separately, and do not claim divergence merely because the test is inconclusive.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Purpose: Establishes convergence of certain alternating series.
  • Standard Form: $\sum(-1)^{n-1}b_n$ or $\sum(-1)^n b_n$ with $b_n\geq0$.
  • First Condition: $b_n$ is eventually nonincreasing.
  • Second Condition: $b_n\to0$.
  • Conclusion: The alternating series converges.
  • Error Bound: $|R_N|\leq b_{N+1}$.
  • Approximation: The first omitted term determines a convenient guaranteed error bound.
  • Conditional Convergence: May be established by combining this test with divergence of the absolute-value series.
  • Absolute Convergence: Must be checked separately.
  • Eventual Behavior: A finite number of irregular initial terms does not prevent use of the test.
  • Power Series: Alternating behavior often occurs at endpoints of intervals of convergence.
  • Common Error: Assuming every alternating series converges.
  • Common Error: Forgetting to verify $b_n\to0$.
  • Common Error: Requiring strict decrease rather than eventual nonincrease.
  • Common Error: Concluding divergence because the test fails to apply.
  • Core Interpretation: The Alternating Series Test converts controlled sign cancellation into a rigorous convergence criterion: $$ \boxed{ b_{n+1}\leq b_n \text{ eventually}, \qquad b_n\to0 \Longrightarrow \sum(-1)^{n-1}b_n \text{ converges} }. $$ Its accompanying remainder estimate also makes it especially useful for numerical approximation.

Applied Discrete and Stochastic Problem Families

This section brings together practical problem families in which discrete structures, probability, statistics, optimization, algorithms, and stochastic processes are used to analyze real systems and make decisions under uncertainty.

Overview

The lessons in this section move from structured decision tools into scheduling, networks, cryptography, operations, reliability, strategic behavior, biological systems, financial risk, public-health inference, forecasting, and simulation.

Each lesson emphasizes how discrete and stochastic mathematics is actually used to represent uncertainty, compare alternatives, analyze constraints, and support practical decisions.

Lessons

  1. Decision Tables and Decision Trees — Organize conditional decisions, states of nature, payoffs, expected values, rollback, and value-of-information reasoning.
  2. Scheduling, Assignment, and Routing — Model jobs, resources, assignments, shortest paths, routing, optimization, and constrained decision procedures.
  3. Voting and Apportionment — Compare voting rules, preference aggregation, fairness criteria, quotas, divisor methods, and apportionment paradoxes.
  4. Networks and Communication — Study connectivity, routing, capacity, bottlenecks, flow, spanning trees, redundancy, resilience, and communication structure.
  5. Coding, Security, and Cryptography — Explore binary representation, error detection, Hamming distance, modular arithmetic, ciphers, hashing, authentication, and secure systems.
  6. Inventory and Queueing — Analyze stock levels, reorder rules, EOQ, arrival and service rates, utilization, Little's Law, waiting systems, and capacity tradeoffs.
  7. Reliability and Quality Control — Model system reliability, redundancy, failure rates, sampling, defect probabilities, control charts, and process capability.
  8. Games and Strategic Behavior — Examine players, strategies, payoffs, dominance, Nash equilibrium, mixed strategies, repeated interaction, and sequential decisions.
  9. Biological and Population Processes — Model growth, survival, branching, migration, Markov transitions, epidemic-style spread, extinction, and stochastic population change.
  10. Finance, Insurance, and Risk — Apply expected value, loss distributions, premiums, deductibles, diversification, portfolio risk, tail measures, and ruin reasoning.
  11. Medical Testing and Public-Health Inference — Use sensitivity, specificity, predictive values, Bayes' theorem, likelihood ratios, risk measures, sampling, and population inference.
  12. Surveys, Experiments, and Forecasting — Study sampling, bias, randomization, experimental design, confidence intervals, trend models, forecast error, and calibration.
  13. Simulation and Data-Driven Decision-Making — Combine Monte Carlo methods, scenario generation, sensitivity analysis, model validation, optimization, and evidence-based decisions.

How the Section Fits Together

The section begins with discrete decision structures, then moves through networks, algorithms, operations, and strategic systems before extending into stochastic biological, financial, medical, and forecasting problems. The final lesson brings the section together through simulation and data-driven decision-making.

Across the section, the recurring pattern is:

define the state, identify uncertainty, represent the available choices, calculate consequences, compare alternatives, and interpret the result in the context of the original problem.

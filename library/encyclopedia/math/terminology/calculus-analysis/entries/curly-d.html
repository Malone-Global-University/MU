Curly d

Subfield: Calculus & Analysis

Etymology & Roots

Curly d is an informal spoken name for the symbol:

$$ \boxed{\partial} $$

used most prominently in the notation of partial derivatives.

The symbol is formally called the partial derivative symbol or simply partial. It is not an ordinary lowercase letter $d$, although its curved shape causes it to be described informally as "curly d," "rounded d," or "partial d."

The symbol developed historically from stylized differential notation and became standard in multivariable calculus, differential equations, physics, geometry, and related areas.

Its most familiar use is:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} } $$

meaning the partial derivative of $f$ with respect to $x$.

The notation distinguishes differentiation with respect to one variable while other independent variables are held fixed from the ordinary one-variable derivative notation:

$$ \frac{df}{dx}. $$

Operational Nuance

Partial Derivative

Suppose:

$$ f=f(x,y). $$

The partial derivative with respect to $x$ is written:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} }. $$

Operationally, $y$ is treated as constant while differentiation is performed with respect to $x$.

Example

Let:

$$ f(x,y)=x^2y+3y^2. $$

Differentiating with respect to $x$:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 2xy }. $$

Differentiating with respect to $y$:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = x^2+6y }. $$

Why Ordinary $d$ Is Not Used Here

In elementary single-variable calculus:

$$ y=f(x) $$

has only one independent variable, so the derivative may be written:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{dy}{dx} }. $$

If:

$$ z=f(x,y), $$

then $z$ can vary because either $x$ or $y$ varies. The partial derivative notation identifies which independent variable is being varied:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial z}{\partial x}, \qquad \frac{\partial z}{\partial y} }. $$

Holding Other Variables Constant

The expression:

$$ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} $$

means that $x$ changes while the other independent variables are held fixed. This is the central operational meaning of partial differentiation.

Alternative Notation

The same partial derivative may be written:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = f_x = \partial_x f = D_xf }. $$

The preferred notation depends on field and context.

Second Partial Derivatives

Differentiating twice with respect to $x$ gives:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} = f_{xx} }. $$

Similarly:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2} = f_{yy} }. $$

Mixed Partial Derivatives

A mixed partial derivative differentiates with respect to more than one variable:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y\,\partial x} }. $$

Under the common operator-reading convention, the denominator is interpreted from right to left: first differentiate with respect to $x$, then with respect to $y$.

Thus:

$$ \frac{\partial^2f}{\partial y\,\partial x} = \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \left( \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} \right). $$

Equality of Mixed Partials

Under suitable smoothness conditions, especially continuity of the relevant second partial derivatives near a point:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x\,\partial y} = \frac{\partial^2f}{\partial y\,\partial x} }. $$

This result is commonly associated with Clairaut's or Schwarz's theorem.

Partial Derivative Operator

The notation:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} } $$

can be regarded as an operator that differentiates whatever function follows it with respect to $x$.

For example:

$$ \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x^2y) = 2xy. $$

Gradient

Partial derivatives form the components of the gradient:

$$ \boxed{ \nabla f = \left\langle \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial z} \right\rangle }. $$

Thus the curly-d symbol appears repeatedly in multivariable differentiation.

Jacobian Matrix

For a vector-valued function:

$$ \mathbf{F} = (F_1,\ldots,F_m) $$

of variables:

$$ x_1,\ldots,x_n, $$

the Jacobian matrix contains partial derivatives:

$$ \boxed{ J_{\mathbf{F}} = \left[ \frac{\partial F_i}{\partial x_j} \right] }. $$

Hessian Matrix

The Hessian contains second partial derivatives:

$$ \boxed{ H_f = \left[ \frac{\partial^2f} {\partial x_i\partial x_j} \right] }. $$

Partial Differential Equations

The symbol $\partial$ is fundamental in partial differential equations because the unknown function depends on several independent variables.

For example, the one-dimensional heat equation is:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \alpha \frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2} }. $$

Wave Equation

A common form of the wave equation is:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial^2u}{\partial t^2} = c^2 \frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2} }. $$

Laplace Equation

In two dimensions:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2u}{\partial y^2} = 0 }. $$

Total Differential Contrast

If:

$$ z=f(x,y), $$

then the total differential is:

$$ \boxed{ dz = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\,dx + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\,dy }. $$

Here both $d$ and $\partial$ appear, but they serve different notational roles.

Curly d Is Not Delta

The symbol:

$$ \partial $$

should not be confused with the Greek letter delta:

$$ \delta \qquad\text{or}\qquad \Delta. $$

These symbols have different origins and mathematical uses.

Curly d Is Not an Ordinary $d$

Although spoken informally as "curly d," the symbol:

$$ \boxed{\partial} $$

is a distinct mathematical symbol and Unicode character, not simply a stylized font form of:

$$ d. $$

Common Misconception: The curly-d symbol does not mean "differentiate partially" merely because a function contains several letters. It is used when differentiation is taken with respect to one independent variable of a multivariable dependence, or in established notation derived from that setting.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\partial$ "Partial," or informally "curly d."
$\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}$ "The partial derivative of f with respect to x."
$f_x$ "F sub x."
$\partial_xf$ "Partial x of f," or "the x partial of f."
$\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x^2}$ "The second partial derivative of f with respect to x."
$\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial y\,\partial x}$ "The mixed second partial derivative, first with respect to x and then y."
$\nabla f$ "The gradient of f."

The informal name:

$$ \boxed{\text{"curly d"}} $$

refers visually to the shape of:

$$ \boxed{\partial}. $$

In formal mathematical speech, however:

$$ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} $$

is normally read:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"the partial derivative of f with respect to x"} }. $$

It is generally not read literally as "curly d f over curly d x" in professional mathematical speech, though that phrasing may occur informally during instruction.

In LaTeX, the symbol is produced by:

$$ \boxed{\verb|\partial|}. $$

Thus:

$$ \verb|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}| $$

renders:

$$ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}. $$

Defense Notation Advice: Use $\partial$ for established partial-derivative notation, not a handwritten or typographic approximation using $d$. Distinguishing $\partial$, $d$, $\delta$, and $\Delta$ prevents several common notation errors.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Symbol: $$ \boxed{\partial}. $$
  • Formal Name: The symbol is commonly called the partial derivative symbol or simply partial.
  • Informal Name: Curly d refers to the visual resemblance between $\partial$ and a stylized lowercase $d$.
  • First Partial Derivative: $$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} }. $$
  • Subscript Form: $$ \boxed{ f_x = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} }. $$
  • Operator Form: $$ \boxed{ \partial_x = \frac{\partial}{\partial x} }. $$
  • Second Partial: $$ \boxed{ f_{xx} = \frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x^2} }. $$
  • Mixed Partial: $$ \boxed{ f_{xy} = \frac{\partial^2f}{\partial y\,\partial x} } $$ under the convention that the subscript order records successive differentiation first in $x$, then in $y$.
  • Multivariable Calculus: $\partial$ distinguishes differentiation with respect to one variable from the total variation of a multivariable function.
  • Gradient: $$ \boxed{ \nabla f = \left( \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_1}, \ldots, \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_n} \right) }. $$
  • Directional Derivative: Partial derivatives provide coordinate components used to form: $$ D_{\mathbf{u}}f = \nabla f\cdot\mathbf{u}. $$
  • Jacobian: $$ \boxed{ J_{ij} = \frac{\partial F_i}{\partial x_j} }. $$
  • Hessian: $$ \boxed{ H_{ij} = \frac{\partial^2f} {\partial x_i\partial x_j} }. $$
  • Divergence: For: $$ \mathbf{F} = \langle F_1,F_2,F_3\rangle, $$ $$ \boxed{ \nabla\cdot\mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial F_3}{\partial z} }. $$
  • Curl: Curl notation also contains partial derivative operators: $$ \boxed{ \nabla\times\mathbf{F} }. $$
  • Laplacian: For a scalar function in three Cartesian variables: $$ \boxed{ \nabla^2f = \frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2f}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2f}{\partial z^2} }. $$
  • Partial Differential Equations: PDEs contain partial derivatives of an unknown multivariable function.
  • Heat Equation: $$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \alpha\nabla^2u }. $$
  • Wave Equation: $$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial^2u}{\partial t^2} = c^2\nabla^2u }. $$
  • Laplace Equation: $$ \boxed{ \nabla^2u=0 }. $$
  • Poisson Equation: $$ \boxed{ \nabla^2u=f }. $$
  • Thermodynamics: Partial derivatives are used to specify how one state variable changes while selected other variables are held fixed.
  • Physics: The notation: $$ \left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_V $$ explicitly indicates differentiation of pressure with respect to temperature while volume is held constant.
  • Economics: Partial derivatives express marginal effects in functions depending on several inputs.
  • Optimization: Interior critical points of a differentiable function: $$ f(x_1,\ldots,x_n) $$ are commonly found by solving: $$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_1} = \cdots = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_n} = 0 }. $$
  • Total Derivative Contrast: A total derivative accounts for all routes through which dependent variables change, whereas a partial derivative isolates variation with respect to one independent variable.
  • Ordinary Differential Contrast: $$ \boxed{ \frac{dy}{dx} } $$ is ordinary derivative notation, while: $$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} } $$ is partial derivative notation.
  • Total Differential: For: $$ z=f(x,y), $$ $$ \boxed{ dz = f_x\,dx+f_y\,dy }. $$
  • Differential Geometry: The same symbol $\partial$ is also used for other related mathematical operators, including coordinate vector fields such as: $$ \frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}. $$
  • Boundary Operator: In algebraic topology and differential geometry, $\partial$ also commonly denotes a boundary operator: $$ \boxed{ \partial:C_n\to C_{n-1} }. $$ This use is conceptually distinct from partial differentiation.
  • Boundary of a Set: In topology, notation such as: $$ \boxed{\partial A} $$ may denote the boundary of a set $A$.
  • Context Dependence: Therefore $\partial$ does not always mean "partial derivative." Its meaning must be inferred from surrounding notation.
  • Unicode: The character is: $$ \boxed{\partial} $$ and is distinct from ordinary Latin $d$ and Greek delta.
  • LaTeX: The command: $$ \boxed{\verb|\partial|} $$ produces $\partial$.
  • Pronunciation: In an expression such as: $$ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}, $$ professional speech normally says: $$ \boxed{ \text{"partial f with respect to x"} } $$ or: $$ \boxed{ \text{"the partial derivative of f with respect to x"} }. $$
  • Terminological Contrast: Curly d is an informal visual nickname. Partial or partial derivative symbol is the more formal mathematical terminology.
  • Core Interpretation: In calculus, the curly-d symbol: $$ \boxed{\partial} $$ signals differentiation that isolates one variable within a multivariable dependence: $$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} }. $$ Outside calculus it can also denote boundaries and related operators, so context determines its precise role.

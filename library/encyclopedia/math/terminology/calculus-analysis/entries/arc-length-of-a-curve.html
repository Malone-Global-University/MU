Arc Length of a Curve

Subfield: Calculus & Analysis

Etymology & Roots

The arc length of a curve is the distance measured along the curve between two specified points.

$$ \boxed{ L=\int_C ds }. $$

The word arc historically refers to a curved portion of a line or circle, while length measures one-dimensional extent. In calculus, the idea extends from circular arcs to general rectifiable curves.

Operational Nuance

Graph of a Function

For a continuously differentiable curve $y=f(x)$ on $[a,b]$:

$$ \boxed{ L= \int_a^b \sqrt{1+\left(f'(x)\right)^2}\,dx }. $$

Why the Formula Appears

Over a very small horizontal change $dx$, the curve behaves approximately like a line segment with vertical change $dy=f'(x)\,dx$. The Pythagorean relation gives:

$$ ds= \sqrt{dx^2+dy^2} = \sqrt{1+\left(f'(x)\right)^2}\,dx. $$

Parametric Curves

If:

$$ x=x(t), \qquad y=y(t), \qquad \alpha\leq t\leq\beta, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ L= \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \sqrt{ \left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2+ \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2 }\,dt }. $$

Space Curves

For $\mathbf{r}(t)=(x(t),y(t),z(t))$:

$$ \boxed{ L= \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \|\mathbf{r}'(t)\|\,dt }. $$

Polar Curves

For a sufficiently smooth polar curve $r=r(\theta)$:

$$ \boxed{ L= \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \sqrt{ r(\theta)^2+ \left(\frac{dr}{d\theta}\right)^2 }\,d\theta }. $$

Rectifiable Curves

A curve is rectifiable when it has finite arc length. Some curves can be continuous yet have infinite total length.

Parameter Independence

For regular reparameterizations that trace the same curve once without changing orientation improperly, arc length is a geometric property of the curve rather than of the particular parameter used to describe it.

Arc-Length Parameter

One may define accumulated length from a starting parameter $t_0$ by:

$$ \boxed{ s(t)= \int_{t_0}^{t} \|\mathbf{r}'(u)\|\,du }. $$

When a curve is parameterized directly by arc length, its speed satisfies:

$$ \boxed{ \left\| \frac{d\mathbf{r}}{ds} \right\|=1 }. $$

Common Misconception

Arc length is not the straight-line distance between endpoints. Straight-line distance gives the chord length; arc length measures distance along the curve itself.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$L=\int_C ds$ "The length of the curve is the integral of differential arc length."
$ds=\sqrt{dx^2+dy^2}$ "Differential arc length follows the Pythagorean relation."
$\int_a^b\sqrt{1+(f'(x))^2}\,dx$ "The arc length of the graph from a to b."
$\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\|$ "The speed along the parameterized curve."
$s(t)$ "The accumulated arc-length function."
$\|d\mathbf{r}/ds\|=1$ "The curve is parameterized by arc length."

The terms arc length, curve length, and length of a curve are often interchangeable. Arc is especially common for a specified portion of a larger curve.

Defense Notation Advice: Match the formula to the curve's representation. Use $f'(x)$ for a graph, $\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\|$ for a parametric curve, and retain the parameter interval so the intended portion of the curve is clear.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Graph Form: $L=\int_a^b\sqrt{1+(f'(x))^2}\,dx$.
  • Parametric Form: Integrates the speed $\sqrt{(dx/dt)^2+(dy/dt)^2}$.
  • Space Curves: $L=\int\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\|\,dt$.
  • Polar Form: Uses $\sqrt{r^2+(dr/d\theta)^2}$.
  • Geometry: Measures distance along a curved path rather than across it.
  • Vector Calculus: Arc length is the line integral $\int_C ds$.
  • Kinematics: If $\mathbf{r}(t)$ is position, arc length equals total distance traveled when the path is traced once over the stated interval.
  • Rectifiability: Finite length is not guaranteed for every continuous curve.
  • Reparameterization: Suitable reparameterizations preserve the geometric length of the traced curve.
  • Arc-Length Parameter: Provides a natural parameter measuring distance traveled along the curve.
  • Common Error: Using endpoint distance instead of distance along the curve.
  • Common Error: Omitting the square root in the arc-length integrand.
  • Common Error: Using the graph formula for a curve that is more naturally parameterized or polar.
  • Common Error: Ignoring repeated tracing, which can make a parameterized path accumulate length more than once.
  • Core Interpretation: Arc length accumulates infinitesimal distances along a curve: $$ \boxed{ L=\int_C ds }. $$ In coordinate form, the differential $ds$ is built from the local rate of change of position, making arc length a direct connection between geometry, derivatives, integration, and motion.

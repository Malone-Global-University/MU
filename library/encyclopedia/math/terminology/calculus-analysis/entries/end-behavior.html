End Behavior

Subfield: Calculus & Analysis

Etymology & Roots

End behavior describes how a function behaves far to the left and far to the right of its graph, usually as the input becomes arbitrarily large in magnitude.

In limit notation, end behavior concerns expressions such as:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to\infty}f(x) \qquad\text{and}\qquad \lim_{x\to-\infty}f(x) }. $$

The word end does not imply that the real-number line literally has endpoints at $\infty$ or $-\infty$. Rather, it refers informally to the far-right and far-left portions of a graph.

The word behavior emphasizes the qualitative or asymptotic pattern: whether the function rises without bound, falls without bound, approaches a finite value, oscillates, or exhibits some other long-run pattern.

For example, the function:

$$ f(x)=x^2 $$

has:

$$ \boxed{ f(x)\to\infty \text{ as } x\to\infty } $$

and:

$$ \boxed{ f(x)\to\infty \text{ as } x\to-\infty }. $$

Thus both ends of its graph rise.

Operational Nuance

Right-End Behavior

The right-end behavior of $f$ concerns what happens as:

$$ \boxed{x\to\infty}. $$

For example:

$$ f(x)=x^3 $$

satisfies:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to\infty}x^3=\infty }. $$

Left-End Behavior

The left-end behavior concerns what happens as:

$$ \boxed{x\to-\infty}. $$

For:

$$ f(x)=x^3, $$

one has:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to-\infty}x^3=-\infty }. $$

Polynomial End Behavior

For a polynomial:

$$ p(x) = a_nx^n+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_0, \qquad a_n\neq0, $$

the end behavior is determined by the leading term:

$$ \boxed{a_nx^n}. $$

Lower-degree terms become comparatively insignificant as $|x|$ becomes very large.

Leading-Term Approximation

For large $|x|$:

$$ \boxed{ p(x)\sim a_nx^n }. $$

More precisely:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{p(x)}{a_nx^n}\to1 \quad\text{as}\quad x\to\pm\infty }. $$

Even Degree, Positive Leading Coefficient

If $n$ is even and $a_n>0$, then:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to\infty}p(x)=\infty } $$

and:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to-\infty}p(x)=\infty }. $$

Both ends rise.

Even Degree, Negative Leading Coefficient

If $n$ is even and $a_n<0$, then:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to\infty}p(x)=-\infty } $$

and:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to-\infty}p(x)=-\infty }. $$

Both ends fall.

Odd Degree, Positive Leading Coefficient

If $n$ is odd and $a_n>0$, then:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to-\infty}p(x)=-\infty, \qquad \lim_{x\to\infty}p(x)=\infty }. $$

The graph falls on the left and rises on the right.

Odd Degree, Negative Leading Coefficient

If $n$ is odd and $a_n<0$, then:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to-\infty}p(x)=\infty, \qquad \lim_{x\to\infty}p(x)=-\infty }. $$

The graph rises on the left and falls on the right.

Degree Leading Coefficient Left End Right End

Example: Quartic Polynomial

Consider:

$$ f(x)=3x^4-7x^2+x-9. $$

The leading term is:

$$ \boxed{3x^4}. $$

Its degree is even and its leading coefficient is positive, so:

$$ \boxed{ f(x)\to\infty \text{ as } x\to\pm\infty }. $$

Example: Negative Cubic

For:

$$ f(x)=-2x^3+5x^2+100, $$

the leading term is:

$$ -2x^3. $$

Since the degree is odd and the leading coefficient is negative:

$$ \boxed{ f(x)\to\infty \text{ as } x\to-\infty } $$

and:

$$ \boxed{ f(x)\to-\infty \text{ as } x\to\infty }. $$

Rational Functions

For:

$$ R(x)=\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}, $$

end behavior is strongly influenced by the degrees and leading coefficients of $P$ and $Q$.

Numerator Degree Less Than Denominator Degree

If:

$$ \deg P<\deg Q, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ R(x)\to0 \quad\text{as}\quad x\to\pm\infty }. $$

Thus:

$$ \boxed{y=0} $$

is a horizontal asymptote.

Equal Degrees

If:

$$ \deg P=\deg Q, $$

then the end behavior approaches the ratio of leading coefficients.

If:

$$ P(x)=a_nx^n+\cdots, \qquad Q(x)=b_nx^n+\cdots, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to\pm\infty} \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} = \frac{a_n}{b_n} }. $$

Numerator Degree One Greater

If:

$$ \deg P=\deg Q+1, $$

polynomial division often produces a linear quotient:

$$ R(x)=mx+b+\frac{S(x)}{Q(x)}, $$

where:

$$ \frac{S(x)}{Q(x)}\to0. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ y=mx+b } $$

is a slant or oblique asymptote.

Polynomial Asymptotes

More generally, polynomial division may reveal a polynomial $q(x)$ such that:

$$ \boxed{ R(x)-q(x)\to0 } $$

as $x\to\pm\infty$.

Exponential Growth

If:

$$ f(x)=a^x, \qquad a>1, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ a^x\to\infty \text{ as } x\to\infty } $$

and:

$$ \boxed{ a^x\to0 \text{ as } x\to-\infty }. $$

Exponential Decay

If:

$$ 0<a<1, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ a^x\to0 \text{ as } x\to\infty } $$

and:

$$ \boxed{ a^x\to\infty \text{ as } x\to-\infty }. $$

Natural Exponential Function

For:

$$ f(x)=e^x, $$

one has:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to\infty}e^x=\infty, \qquad \lim_{x\to-\infty}e^x=0 }. $$

Logarithmic End Behavior

For:

$$ f(x)=\ln x, $$

only the right-end limit at infinity applies because the real domain is:

$$ (0,\infty). $$

Its right-end behavior is:

$$ \boxed{ \ln x\to\infty \text{ as } x\to\infty }. $$

Domain Matters

End behavior should be discussed only along directions in which the domain extends indefinitely.

A function defined only on:

$$ [0,\infty) $$

has a right-end behavior as $x\to\infty$, but no real left-end behavior as $x\to-\infty$ because negative inputs are not in its domain.

Horizontal Asymptotes

If:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=L } $$

or:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to-\infty}f(x)=L }, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{y=L} $$

is a horizontal asymptote in the corresponding direction.

Different Asymptotes at Opposite Ends

A function can approach different finite values on its two ends.

For example:

$$ f(x)=\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}} $$

satisfies:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=1 } $$

and:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to-\infty}f(x)=-1 }. $$

Infinite Limits at Infinity

Statements such as:

$$ \lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=\infty $$

do not mean that the limit is the real number $\infty$. They mean that $f(x)$ can be made arbitrarily large by taking $x$ sufficiently large.

Oscillatory End Behavior

Not every function approaches a finite number or infinity.

For:

$$ f(x)=\sin x, $$

the function continues oscillating:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to\infty}\sin x \text{ does not exist} }. $$

Nevertheless, the oscillation itself is part of the function's end behavior.

Bounded Oscillation

The function $\sin x$ remains bounded:

$$ \boxed{ -1\leq\sin x\leq1 } $$

even though it has no single limiting value as $x\to\infty$.

Damped Oscillation

A function such as:

$$ f(x)=\frac{\sin x}{x} $$

oscillates with decreasing amplitude and satisfies:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x}=0 }. $$

Growth-Rate Comparisons

End behavior also supports comparisons among function families.

For fixed $n>0$:

$$ \boxed{ \ln x \ll x^n \ll e^x } $$

as $x\to\infty$, in the sense that logarithmic growth is slower than positive power growth, while exponential growth eventually exceeds every fixed power.

Exponential Dominates Polynomial

For every fixed nonnegative integer $n$:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{x^n}{e^x}=0 }. $$

Highest-Degree Term Dominates a Polynomial

For:

$$ p(x)=a_nx^n+\cdots+a_0, $$

one has:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to\pm\infty} \frac{p(x)}{a_nx^n}=1 }. $$

End Behavior vs. Local Behavior

End behavior describes what happens far from the central portion of the graph. It does not by itself describe:

  • zeros,
  • local maxima,
  • local minima,
  • holes,
  • finite discontinuities, or
  • turning points.

Same End Behavior, Different Middle Behavior

Two functions may have identical end behavior while differing greatly near the origin.

For example:

$$ f(x)=x^4 $$

and:

$$ g(x)=x^4-100x^2+7 $$

both satisfy:

$$ \boxed{ f(x),g(x)\to\infty \text{ as } x\to\pm\infty }. $$

Graphing Technology

A graphing window may give a misleading impression of end behavior if the visible range is too small.

Algebraic analysis of leading terms or limits is therefore more reliable than visual inspection alone.

Common Misconception: End behavior does not mean the value of a function at $\infty$ or $-\infty$. These symbols are not ordinary real input values. End behavior describes what happens as $x$ increases or decreases without bound.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$x\to\infty$ "As x approaches positive infinity."
$x\to-\infty$ "As x approaches negative infinity."
$f(x)\to\infty$ "F of x increases without bound."
$f(x)\to-\infty$ "F of x decreases without bound."
$\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=L$ "The limit of f of x as x approaches infinity is L."
$p(x)\sim a_nx^n$ "P of x is asymptotic to its leading term a sub n x to the n."
$y=L$ "Y equals L is a horizontal asymptote."

Common verbal descriptions include:

$$ \boxed{\text{"both ends rise"}} $$
$$ \boxed{\text{"both ends fall"}} $$
$$ \boxed{\text{"left end falls and right end rises"}} $$
$$ \boxed{\text{"left end rises and right end falls"}}. $$

These phrases are convenient graphical summaries, but limit notation is more precise.

For example, "both ends rise" for a polynomial means:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to-\infty}f(x)=\infty \qquad\text{and}\qquad \lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=\infty }. $$

Defense Notation Advice: Avoid writing expressions such as $f(\infty)$ unless a specialized context has explicitly defined such notation. Prefer:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to\infty}f(x) } $$

because $\infty$ is not an ordinary real number at which the function is being evaluated.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Basic Meaning: End behavior describes a function far to the left and right of its graph.
  • Right End: $$ \boxed{x\to\infty}. $$
  • Left End: $$ \boxed{x\to-\infty}. $$
  • Limit Language: End behavior is formally expressed using limits at infinity.
  • Unbounded Rise: $$ \boxed{ f(x)\to\infty }. $$
  • Unbounded Fall: $$ \boxed{ f(x)\to-\infty }. $$
  • Finite Approach: $$ \boxed{ f(x)\to L } $$ indicates approach to a finite value.
  • Horizontal Asymptote: If: $$ \lim_{x\to\pm\infty}f(x)=L, $$ then: $$ \boxed{y=L} $$ is a horizontal asymptote in that direction.
  • Polynomial Rule: Polynomial end behavior is determined by its leading term.
  • Leading Term: $$ \boxed{a_nx^n}. $$
  • Even Degree: Both polynomial ends point in the same vertical direction.
  • Odd Degree: Polynomial ends point in opposite vertical directions.
  • Positive Leading Coefficient: The right end of a nonconstant polynomial rises.
  • Negative Leading Coefficient: The right end of a nonconstant polynomial falls.
  • Even Positive: $$ \boxed{ x\to\pm\infty \Longrightarrow f(x)\to\infty }. $$
  • Even Negative: $$ \boxed{ x\to\pm\infty \Longrightarrow f(x)\to-\infty }. $$
  • Odd Positive: $$ \boxed{ x\to-\infty\Rightarrow f(x)\to-\infty, \qquad x\to\infty\Rightarrow f(x)\to\infty }. $$
  • Odd Negative: $$ \boxed{ x\to-\infty\Rightarrow f(x)\to\infty, \qquad x\to\infty\Rightarrow f(x)\to-\infty }. $$
  • Constant Polynomial: A constant polynomial approaches the same finite constant at both ends.
  • Linear Function: A nonconstant linear function has opposite infinite behavior at its two ends.
  • Quadratic Function: Both ends rise if the leading coefficient is positive and fall if it is negative.
  • Cubic Function: Its two ends move in opposite vertical directions.
  • Rational Function: Compare numerator and denominator degrees to determine many common forms of end behavior.
  • Numerator Degree Lower: $$ \boxed{ \deg P<\deg Q \Rightarrow \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}\to0 }. $$
  • Equal Rational Degrees: The ratio approaches the ratio of leading coefficients.
  • Slant Asymptote: Degree difference $1$ may produce a linear asymptote.
  • Polynomial Asymptote: Larger degree differences may yield higher-degree polynomial asymptotes.
  • Exponential Growth: For $a>1$: $$ \boxed{ a^x\to\infty \text{ as }x\to\infty }. $$
  • Exponential Left End: For $a>1$: $$ \boxed{ a^x\to0 \text{ as }x\to-\infty }. $$
  • Exponential Decay: For $0<a<1$, the corresponding directions are reversed.
  • Natural Exponential: $$ \boxed{ e^x\to\infty \text{ as }x\to\infty }. $$
  • Natural Exponential Left End: $$ \boxed{ e^x\to0 \text{ as }x\to-\infty }. $$
  • Logarithmic Growth: $$ \boxed{ \ln x\to\infty \text{ as }x\to\infty }. $$
  • Logarithmic Domain Caution: Real $\ln x$ has no end behavior as $x\to-\infty$ because its domain does not extend there.
  • Square-Root Function: $\sqrt{x}$ has right-end behavior as $x\to\infty$ but no real left-end behavior as $x\to-\infty$.
  • Absolute Value: $$ |x|\to\infty $$ as $x\to\pm\infty$.
  • Reciprocal Function: $$ \boxed{ \frac1x\to0 \text{ as }x\to\pm\infty }. $$
  • Sine: $\sin x$ oscillates indefinitely and has no finite limit as $x\to\pm\infty$.
  • Cosine: $\cos x$ likewise oscillates indefinitely.
  • Bounded Oscillation: A function can lack a limit at infinity while remaining bounded.
  • Damped Oscillation: An oscillating function may still approach a finite value if its amplitude tends to zero.
  • Example: $$ \boxed{ \frac{\sin x}{x}\to0 \text{ as }x\to\infty }. $$
  • Different Left and Right Limits: The two ends of a function need not behave alike.
  • Different Horizontal Asymptotes: A function can approach one finite value as $x\to-\infty$ and another as $x\to\infty$.
  • Growth Comparison: End behavior is used to compare rates of growth among functions.
  • Logarithmic vs. Polynomial: Positive power functions eventually grow faster than logarithms.
  • Polynomial vs. Exponential: Positive exponential functions eventually grow faster than every fixed polynomial.
  • Leading-Term Dominance: Lower-degree polynomial terms do not determine polynomial end behavior.
  • Coefficient Magnitude: The magnitude of the leading coefficient affects vertical scale but not the basic four-way polynomial end-behavior classification.
  • Leading-Coefficient Sign: Its sign determines whether the right end rises or falls for a nonconstant polynomial.
  • Degree Parity: Even or odd degree determines whether the two polynomial ends agree or oppose one another vertically.
  • Graph Sketching: End behavior provides the large-scale framework within which intercepts and turning points can be placed.
  • Turning Points Contrast: Turning points describe local changes in direction; end behavior describes asymptotic direction.
  • Local Extrema Contrast: Local maxima and minima do not determine what eventually happens as $|x|\to\infty$.
  • Vertical Asymptote Contrast: Vertical asymptotes describe behavior near finite input values, whereas end behavior concerns inputs tending to $\pm\infty$.
  • Horizontal Asymptote Connection: Horizontal asymptotes directly describe finite end behavior.
  • Slant Asymptote Connection: A slant asymptote describes a linear pattern approached at an end.
  • Limit Notation: Limits at infinity provide the rigorous language for end behavior.
  • Infinity Caution: $\infty$ and $-\infty$ are not ordinary real inputs.
  • No $f(\infty)$ Requirement: End behavior ordinarily uses $\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)$ rather than $f(\infty)$.
  • Graphing Window Caution: A small viewing window may conceal the true eventual direction of a graph.
  • Numerical Sampling Caution: Evaluating a few large numbers can suggest end behavior but does not replace algebraic or limit analysis.
  • Asymptotic Analysis: In advanced analysis, end behavior is studied through limits, asymptotic equivalence, growth orders, and comparison methods.
  • Terminological Variant: Long-run behavior, behavior at infinity, and asymptotic behavior may overlap with the idea of end behavior, though the latter terms can have broader technical meanings.
  • Core Interpretation: End behavior describes what a function does as its input moves without bound: $$ \boxed{ x\to-\infty \qquad\text{and}\qquad x\to\infty }. $$ For polynomials, the leading term determines this behavior; for more general functions, limits at infinity provide the rigorous framework for determining whether the graph grows, decays, approaches an asymptote, oscillates, or follows another asymptotic pattern.

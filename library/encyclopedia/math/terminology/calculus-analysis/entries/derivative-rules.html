Derivative Rules

Subfield: Calculus & Analysis

Etymology & Roots

Derivative rules are formulas that allow derivatives of common functions and combinations of functions to be computed without returning each time to the limit definition of the derivative.

$$ \boxed{ f'(x) = \frac{d}{dx}f(x) } $$

The word derivative refers to a quantity derived from local change, while rule identifies a reusable operational law. These rules turn the derivative from a definition based on limits into a practical computational system.

Operational Nuance

Constant Rule

$$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}(c)=0 }. $$

A constant has no change with respect to $x$.

Power Rule

$$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}(x^n) = nx^{n-1} } $$

for values of $n$ and domains where the expression and derivative are defined.

Constant Multiple Rule

$$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}[c f(x)] = c f'(x) }. $$

Sum and Difference Rules

$$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}[f(x)\pm g(x)] = f'(x)\pm g'(x) }. $$

Product Rule

$$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}[f(x)g(x)] = f'(x)g(x)+f(x)g'(x) }. $$

Differentiating a product is not the same as multiplying the separate derivatives.

Quotient Rule

$$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx} \left[ \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} \right] = \frac{ f'(x)g(x)-f(x)g'(x) }{ [g(x)]^2 } } $$

wherever $g(x)\neq0$ and the required derivatives exist.

Chain Rule

$$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}f(g(x)) = f'(g(x))g'(x) }. $$

The chain rule governs composition and is one of the most important rules in differential calculus.

Exponential and Logarithmic Rules

$$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}e^x=e^x, \qquad \frac{d}{dx}a^x=a^x\ln a } $$
$$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}\ln x = \frac{1}{x} } $$

with the usual real-domain restrictions. More generally, $\frac{d}{dx}\ln|u(x)|=\frac{u'(x)}{u(x)}$ wherever $u(x)\neq0$.

Trigonometric Rules

$$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}\sin x=\cos x, \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\cos x=-\sin x, \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\tan x=\sec^2x }. $$

Corresponding rules exist for the remaining trigonometric and inverse trigonometric functions.

Rules Combine

Complicated derivatives are usually found by recognizing nested structure and applying several rules together.

$$ \boxed{ \text{identify structure} \longrightarrow \text{choose rule} \longrightarrow \text{differentiate layer by layer} }. $$

Common Misconception

Derivative rules are not interchangeable templates. A product requires the product rule, a composition requires the chain rule, and a quotient requires either the quotient rule or an equivalent reformulation.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$f'(x)$ "F prime of x."
$\dfrac{d}{dx}f(x)$ "The derivative of f with respect to x."
$(fg)'=f'g+fg'$ "Derivative of the first times the second plus the first times derivative of the second."
$(f\circ g)'=(f'\circ g)g'$ "Differentiate the outer function, keep the inner input, then multiply by the derivative of the inner function."
$(x^n)'=nx^{n-1}$ "Bring down the exponent and reduce the exponent by one."
$(\ln|u|)'=u'/u$ "The derivative of log absolute u is u prime over u."

Authors may distinguish basic derivative formulas from differentiation rules. The former often list derivatives of standard functions, while the latter emphasize structural operations such as product, quotient, and chain rules.

Defense Notation Advice: Parse the expression before differentiating. Parentheses, composition, products, powers, and quotients determine which rule applies and in what order.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Constant Rule: Constants differentiate to zero.
  • Power Rule: Differentiates powers by multiplying by the exponent and lowering it by one.
  • Linearity: Constant multiples, sums, and differences differentiate term by term.
  • Product Rule: Accounts for simultaneous change in two multiplied functions.
  • Quotient Rule: Handles ratios while preserving the restriction that the denominator remain nonzero.
  • Chain Rule: Handles nested functions and composition.
  • Exponential Functions: $e^x$ is its own derivative.
  • Logarithmic Functions: Natural logarithms produce reciprocal-type derivatives.
  • Trigonometric Functions: Their derivatives form recurring relationships among sine, cosine, tangent, and related functions.
  • Implicit Differentiation: Uses the same derivative rules while treating dependent variables as functions of the independent variable.
  • Higher Derivatives: The rules can be applied repeatedly when sufficient differentiability exists.
  • Common Error: Writing $(fg)'=f'g'$.
  • Common Error: Forgetting the derivative of the inner function when applying the chain rule.
  • Common Error: Reversing the numerator terms in the quotient rule.
  • Common Error: Applying a derivative formula without checking the domain on which the original function is defined.
  • Core Interpretation: Derivative rules form a structured calculus for local change: $$ \boxed{ \text{basic derivatives} + \text{linearity} + \text{product} + \text{quotient} + \text{chain} }. $$ Together they convert the limit-based definition of a derivative into an efficient operational system for differentiating complex expressions.

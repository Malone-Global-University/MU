Cardinal Numbers

Subfield: Foundations & Mathematical Logic

Etymology & Roots

A cardinal number describes the number of elements in a collection. It answers the question:

$$ \boxed{ \text{How many?} } $$

In elementary mathematics, cardinal numbers include familiar counting values such as:

$$ \boxed{ 1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ldots }. $$

If a set $A$ contains five elements, its cardinality is five:

$$ \boxed{ |A|=5 }. $$

In set theory, the idea is generalized beyond finite counting. Cardinal numbers describe the size of both finite and infinite sets.

$$ \boxed{ \text{cardinal number} = \text{measure of set size} }. $$

Operational Nuance

Cardinality of a Finite Set

If:

$$ A=\{a,b,c,d\}, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ |A|=4 }. $$

The number $4$ is the cardinal number associated with the set.

Cardinality Notation

The cardinality of a set $A$ is commonly written:

$$ \boxed{ |A| } $$

or in more advanced contexts:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{card}(A) }. $$

Empty Set

The empty set contains no elements:

$$ \varnothing=\{\}. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ |\varnothing|=0 }. $$

Thus $0$ is the cardinal number of the empty set.

Singleton Set

A singleton contains exactly one element:

$$ A=\{x\}. $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ |A|=1 }. $$

Cardinal Number versus Element Value

The values of the elements do not determine the cardinality.

For example:

$$ A=\{2,4,1000\}, \qquad B=\{-8,\pi,\sqrt2\}. $$

Both sets contain three elements:

$$ \boxed{ |A|=|B|=3 }. $$

Order Does Not Affect Cardinality

Since sets do not depend on the order in which their elements are written:

$$ \{1,2,3\} = \{3,2,1\}, $$

both have cardinal number $3$.

Repeated Entries Do Not Increase Cardinality

In ordinary set notation:

$$ \{1,1,2,2,3\} = \{1,2,3\}. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ |\{1,1,2,2,3\}|=3 }. $$

One-to-One Correspondence

Two sets have the same cardinality when their elements can be paired in a one-to-one correspondence.

Formally:

$$ \boxed{ |A|=|B| \iff \text{there exists a bijection }f:A\to B }. $$

Finite Example

Let:

$$ A=\{a,b,c\}, \qquad B=\{7,12,20\}. $$

The pairing:

$$ a\leftrightarrow7, \qquad b\leftrightarrow12, \qquad c\leftrightarrow20 $$

shows:

$$ \boxed{ |A|=|B|=3 }. $$

Cardinality and Counting

Ordinary counting establishes a one-to-one correspondence between the elements of a finite set and an initial segment of the natural numbers.

A set has cardinality $n$ when its members can be paired with:

$$ \boxed{ \{1,2,\ldots,n\} }. $$

Cardinal versus Ordinal Number

Cardinal numbers answer:

$$ \boxed{ \text{How many?} } $$

Ordinal numbers answer:

$$ \boxed{ \text{Which position?} }. $$

Compare:

$$ \boxed{ 5=\text{five objects} } $$

with:

$$ \boxed{ 5\text{th}=\text{fifth position} }. $$

Cardinal Numbers in Everyday Language

Words such as:

$$ \text{one, two, three, four, five} $$

are cardinal-number words because they express quantity.

By contrast:

$$ \text{first, second, third, fourth, fifth} $$

are ordinal-number words because they express order or rank.

Infinite Cardinality

Set theory extends cardinal numbers beyond finite quantities.

The set of natural numbers:

$$ \mathbb N $$

has an infinite cardinality denoted:

$$ \boxed{ |\mathbb N|=\aleph_0 }. $$

The symbol $\aleph_0$, read "aleph-null" or "aleph-zero," denotes the cardinality of a countably infinite set.

Countably Infinite Sets

A set is countably infinite if it can be placed in one-to-one correspondence with $\mathbb N$.

$$ \boxed{ |A|=\aleph_0 \iff A\text{ is countably infinite} }. $$

Natural Numbers and Even Numbers

Consider:

$$ \mathbb N = \{1,2,3,\ldots\} $$

and the positive even integers:

$$ E= \{2,4,6,\ldots\}. $$

The map:

$$ \boxed{ f(n)=2n } $$

is a bijection from $\mathbb N$ to $E$.

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ |\mathbb N| = |E| = \aleph_0 }. $$

Proper Subset with the Same Infinite Cardinality

The even positive integers form a proper subset of the natural numbers:

$$ E\subsetneq\mathbb N, $$

yet:

$$ |E|=|\mathbb N|. $$

This behavior cannot occur for finite sets and is one of the important distinctions between finite and infinite cardinality.

Integers

The integers are also countably infinite:

$$ \boxed{ |\mathbb Z|=\aleph_0 }. $$

Although $\mathbb Z$ extends indefinitely in both positive and negative directions, its elements can still be systematically listed.

Rational Numbers

The rational numbers are also countably infinite:

$$ \boxed{ |\mathbb Q|=\aleph_0 }. $$

Thus:

$$ |\mathbb N| = |\mathbb Z| = |\mathbb Q|. $$

Uncountable Sets

Not every infinite set has cardinality $\aleph_0$.

The real numbers form an uncountable set:

$$ \boxed{ |\mathbb R|>\aleph_0 }. $$

Thus infinite sets can have different cardinal sizes.

Continuum Cardinality

The cardinality of the real numbers is commonly called the cardinality of the continuum and may be denoted:

$$ \boxed{ \mathfrak c = |\mathbb R| }. $$

Intervals of Real Numbers

A nondegenerate interval of real numbers has the same cardinality as the entire real line.

For example:

$$ \boxed{ |(0,1)| = |\mathbb R| = \mathfrak c }. $$

This is another phenomenon that differs sharply from finite intuition.

Power Set

The power set $\mathcal P(A)$ is the set of all subsets of $A$.

For a finite set with $n$ elements:

$$ \boxed{ |\mathcal P(A)|=2^n }. $$

Example

If:

$$ A=\{a,b,c\}, $$

then:

$$ |A|=3 $$

and:

$$ \boxed{ |\mathcal P(A)|=2^3=8 }. $$

Cantor's Theorem

For any set $A$, its power set has strictly greater cardinality:

$$ \boxed{ |A| < |\mathcal P(A)| }. $$

This holds for finite and infinite sets alike.

Consequently, there is no largest cardinal number: from any set size, forming the power set produces a strictly larger one.

Cardinal Comparison

For sets $A$ and $B$, one writes:

$$ |A|\le|B| $$

when there exists an injection:

$$ \boxed{ f:A\to B }. $$

Intuitively, the members of $A$ can be placed into distinct positions within $B$.

Equal Cardinality

Equality of cardinal numbers is established by a bijection:

$$ \boxed{ |A|=|B| \iff A\leftrightarrow B \text{ bijectively} }. $$

Cardinal Addition for Finite Disjoint Sets

If $A$ and $B$ are finite and disjoint:

$$ A\cap B=\varnothing, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ |A\cup B| = |A|+|B| }. $$

Inclusion-Exclusion for Two Finite Sets

If the sets overlap:

$$ \boxed{ |A\cup B| = |A|+|B|-|A\cap B| }. $$

The intersection is subtracted because its elements were counted twice.

Cardinal Multiplication for Finite Sets

The Cartesian product satisfies:

$$ \boxed{ |A\times B| = |A||B| } $$

for finite sets.

Example

If:

$$ |A|=3, \qquad |B|=4, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ |A\times B|=12 }. $$

Finite Cardinal Exponentiation

If $|A|=m$ and $|B|=n$, then the number of functions from $B$ to $A$ is:

$$ \boxed{ m^n }. $$

This gives a set-theoretic interpretation of exponentiation.

Cardinal Arithmetic for Infinite Sets

Infinite cardinal arithmetic can behave differently from ordinary finite arithmetic.

For example:

$$ \boxed{ \aleph_0+\aleph_0 = \aleph_0 }. $$

Combining two countably infinite disjoint sets still produces a countably infinite set.

Infinite Cardinal Multiplication

Likewise:

$$ \boxed{ \aleph_0\cdot\aleph_0 = \aleph_0 }. $$

This corresponds, for example, to the countability of ordered pairs of natural numbers.

Cardinality and Functions

Functions provide the standard language for comparing cardinalities:

  • injection: one set is no larger than another,
  • surjection: one set is large enough to cover another,
  • bijection: two sets have equal cardinality.

Finite Cardinality and Natural Numbers

Every finite cardinal number can be represented by a natural number:

$$ \boxed{ 0,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3,\ldots }. $$

Whether $0$ is included in the notation $\mathbb N$ depends on convention, but $0$ is unambiguously the cardinality of the empty set.

Cardinal Numbers and Numerals

A number is an abstract mathematical object, while a numeral is a written representation of that number.

Thus:

$$ 5,\qquad V,\qquad 101_2 $$

can all represent the same finite cardinal number in different numeral systems or notational conventions.

Cardinal Number versus Cardinality

The terms are closely related but grammatically different.

The cardinality of a set is its size, while the cardinal number is the number representing that size.

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{cardinality}(A)=|A| }. $$

Cardinal Number versus Count

For finite sets, the cardinal number agrees with the result of counting.

For infinite sets, cardinality is compared through bijections rather than by finishing a counting process.

Cardinality and Subsets

If $A\subseteq B$ and both sets are finite, then:

$$ \boxed{ |A|\le|B| }. $$

For infinite sets, a proper subset can have the same cardinality as the containing set.

Finite Proper Subsets

If $A$ is finite and:

$$ B\subsetneq A, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ |B|<|A| }. $$

Infinite Proper Subsets

For infinite sets, the analogous conclusion need not hold:

$$ 2\mathbb N\subsetneq\mathbb N, $$

yet:

$$ |2\mathbb N| = |\mathbb N|. $$

Common Misconception: Cardinal Means Position

Cardinal numbers measure quantity, while ordinal numbers describe position.

$$ \boxed{ \text{cardinal: how many} \qquad \text{ordinal: which one} }. $$

Common Misconception: Larger Elements Mean Larger Cardinality

The numerical magnitude of elements has nothing to do with the number of elements in the set.

Thus:

$$ |\{1,2\}| = |\{1000000,2000000\}| = 2. $$

Common Misconception: Every Infinite Set Has the Same Size

Infinite sets can have different cardinalities. In particular:

$$ \boxed{ |\mathbb N| < |\mathbb R| }. $$

Common Misconception: A Proper Subset Must Always Be Smaller

This is true for finite sets but not necessarily for infinite sets.

Common Misconception: Cardinality Counts Written Entries

In a set, repeated representations of the same element do not create additional elements.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$|A|=5$ "A has cardinality five."
$|A|=|B|$ "A and B have the same cardinality."
$|\varnothing|=0$ "The empty set has cardinality zero."
$|\mathbb N|=\aleph_0$ "The natural numbers have cardinality aleph-null."
$|\mathbb R|=\mathfrak c$ "The real numbers have the cardinality of the continuum."
$|A|<|B|$ "A has strictly smaller cardinality than B."

In elementary speech, cardinal number often means an ordinary counting number such as "three" or "twenty." In set theory, the term is broader and refers to abstract measures of set size, including infinite cardinalities.

Defense Notation Advice: Keep cardinality separate from numerical magnitude, order, and position. For finite sets, counting is sufficient. For infinite sets, compare size through injections and bijections rather than informal visual intuition.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Meaning: Cardinal numbers answer "how many?"
  • Finite Cardinality: A finite set with $n$ elements has cardinal number $n$.
  • Cardinality Notation: $$ \boxed{ |A| } $$ denotes the number of elements of $A$.
  • Empty Set: $$ \boxed{ |\varnothing|=0 }. $$
  • Singleton: A one-element set has cardinality $1$.
  • Equality of Cardinality: Two sets have equal cardinality exactly when there is a bijection between them.
  • Finite Counting: Counting pairs the elements of a finite set with an initial segment of the natural numbers.
  • Ordinal Contrast: Cardinal numbers express quantity; ordinal numbers express rank or position.
  • Countably Infinite: $$ \boxed{ |\mathbb N|=\aleph_0 }. $$
  • Integers: $$ \boxed{ |\mathbb Z|=\aleph_0 }. $$
  • Rationals: $$ \boxed{ |\mathbb Q|=\aleph_0 }. $$
  • Real Numbers: $\mathbb R$ is uncountable and has cardinality greater than $\aleph_0$.
  • Continuum: $$ \boxed{ \mathfrak c=|\mathbb R| }. $$
  • Infinite Proper Subsets: A proper subset of an infinite set may have the same cardinality as the whole.
  • Power Set: For finite $A$: $$ \boxed{ |\mathcal P(A)|=2^{|A|} }. $$
  • Cantor's Theorem: $$ \boxed{ |A|<|\mathcal P(A)| } $$ for every set $A$.
  • Finite Union: For disjoint finite sets: $$ \boxed{ |A\cup B|=|A|+|B| }. $$
  • Inclusion-Exclusion: $$ \boxed{ |A\cup B| = |A|+|B|-|A\cap B| }. $$
  • Cartesian Product: For finite sets: $$ \boxed{ |A\times B|=|A||B| }. $$
  • Function Counting: If $|A|=m$ and $|B|=n$, there are $m^n$ functions from $B$ to $A$.
  • Infinite Cardinal Arithmetic: $$ \boxed{ \aleph_0+\aleph_0=\aleph_0 } $$ and: $$ \boxed{ \aleph_0\cdot\aleph_0=\aleph_0 }. $$
  • Injection: Used to express that one cardinality is no greater than another.
  • Bijection: Establishes equal cardinality.
  • Numeral Contrast: A cardinal number is an abstract quantity; a numeral is a written representation of that quantity.
  • Common Error: Do not confuse cardinal numbers with ordinal numbers.
  • Common Error: Do not infer cardinality from the numerical size of the elements.
  • Common Error: Do not count repeated entries in ordinary set notation as distinct elements.
  • Common Error: Do not assume every infinite set has cardinality $\aleph_0$.
  • Common Error: Do not assume every proper subset has smaller cardinality; this fails for many infinite sets.
  • Terminological Contrast: Cardinal number represents quantity or set size. Cardinality is the size assigned to a set. Ordinal number represents order or position. Finite cardinal is represented by an ordinary nonnegative integer. Countably infinite means cardinality $\aleph_0$. Uncountable means an infinite cardinality strictly larger than $\aleph_0$. Continuum cardinality refers to the size of $\mathbb R$.

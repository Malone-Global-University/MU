Divergent Series

Subfield: Calculus & Analysis

Etymology & Roots

A divergent series is an infinite series whose sequence of partial sums does not converge to a finite limit.

$$ \boxed{ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n \text{ diverges} \iff \left( \sum_{n=1}^{N}a_n \right)_{N=1}^{\infty} \text{ does not converge} } $$

The word divergent means failing to approach the limiting behavior required for convergence. For a series, the relevant objects are the partial sums rather than the terms by themselves.

Operational Nuance

Partial-Sum Definition

For the series:

$$ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n, $$

define the $N$th partial sum by:

$$ \boxed{ S_N=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_n }. $$

The series diverges when $(S_N)$ has no finite limit.

Term Test for Divergence

If the terms of a series fail to approach zero, the series must diverge:

$$ \boxed{ a_n\nrightarrow0 \Longrightarrow \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n \text{ diverges} }. $$

The converse is false. Terms approaching zero do not guarantee convergence.

Harmonic Series

The classical example:

$$ \boxed{ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n} } $$

diverges even though $\frac{1}{n}\to0$.

Unbounded Partial Sums

A series may diverge because its partial sums grow without bound. For example:

$$ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}1 $$

has partial sums $S_N=N$, so $S_N\to+\infty$.

Oscillatory Divergence

Divergence can also occur through persistent oscillation. The series:

$$ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^n $$

has partial sums alternating between $1$ and $0$, so the partial sums do not approach a single limit.

Geometric Series

For:

$$ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}ar^n, $$

convergence occurs when $|r|<1$. When $a\neq0$ and $|r|\geq1$, the series diverges.

Divergence Tests

Comparison, limit comparison, ratio, root, integral, and other tests can establish divergence when their hypotheses and divergence conditions are satisfied.

Series vs. Sequence

The sequence $(a_n)$ may converge while the series $\sum a_n$ diverges. The harmonic sequence $a_n=1/n$ converges to zero, but its associated series diverges.

Common Misconception

Saying that the terms $a_n$ approach zero is not enough to conclude that the series converges. Series convergence depends on the cumulative behavior of the partial sums.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n$ diverges "The infinite series diverges."
$S_N=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_n$ "S sub N is the Nth partial sum."
$S_N\nrightarrow S$ "The partial sums do not converge to a finite sum."
$a_n\nrightarrow0$ "The terms fail the necessary condition for series convergence."
$S_N\to+\infty$ "The partial sums grow without bound."
$\sum 1/n$ diverges "The harmonic series diverges."

The verb is diverge, the adjective is divergent, and the noun is divergence. In rigorous usage, a series diverges because its sequence of partial sums fails to converge.

Defense Notation Advice: Distinguish the term sequence $(a_n)$ from the partial-sum sequence $(S_N)$. The latter determines whether the infinite series itself converges or diverges.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Definition: A series diverges when its partial sums fail to approach a finite limit.
  • Term-Test Case: If $a_n\nrightarrow0$, divergence is immediate.
  • Harmonic Case: Demonstrates that $a_n\to0$ does not guarantee convergence.
  • Unbounded Case: Partial sums may grow toward $+\infty$ or $-\infty$.
  • Oscillatory Case: Partial sums may remain bounded yet fail to settle to one value.
  • Geometric Series: Nonzero geometric series diverge when $|r|\geq1$.
  • Comparison Methods: Divergence can be inherited from suitable comparison with a known divergent positive-term series.
  • Improper-Integral Connection: The integral test can transfer convergence or divergence information between certain positive decreasing series and improper integrals.
  • Power Series: A power series diverges outside its radius of convergence, while boundary points require separate analysis.
  • Common Error: Assuming a series converges because its terms become small.
  • Common Error: Confusing divergence of $(a_n)$ with divergence of $\sum a_n$.
  • Common Error: Assuming every divergent series has partial sums tending to infinity.
  • Common Error: Treating an inconclusive convergence test as proof of divergence.
  • Core Interpretation: A divergent series is an infinite accumulation whose partial sums fail to stabilize: $$ \boxed{ a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_N = S_N, \qquad S_N \text{ has no finite limit} }. $$ Divergence can result from unbounded accumulation, persistent oscillation, or another failure of the partial sums to converge.

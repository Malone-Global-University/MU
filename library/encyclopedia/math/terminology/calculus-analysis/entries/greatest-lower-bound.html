Greatest Lower Bound

Subfield: Calculus & Analysis

Etymology & Roots

The greatest lower bound of a set is the largest value that is less than or equal to every element of that set. The standard modern synonym is infimum.

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{GLB}(S)=\inf S } $$

The phrase is descriptive: first identify all values that lie below the set, then choose the greatest among those lower bounds.

Operational Nuance

Lower Bound

A number $L$ is a lower bound of $S$ if:

$$ \boxed{ L\leq x \quad\text{for every }x\in S }. $$

Greatest Among the Lower Bounds

A number $g$ is the greatest lower bound when it is itself a lower bound and every other lower bound lies at or below it:

$$ \boxed{ g\leq x \text{ for every }x\in S, \qquad L\leq g \text{ for every lower bound }L }. $$

Infimum Notation

$$ \boxed{ g=\inf S }. $$

The notation $\inf S$ is more common than writing an explicit GLB operator in advanced mathematics.

Greatest Lower Bound vs. Minimum

A minimum must be an element of the set. A greatest lower bound does not have to be. For example:

$$ S=(0,1) \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \inf S=0, $$

but $S$ has no minimum because $0\notin S$.

When the GLB Is Attained

If the infimum belongs to the set, then it is the minimum:

$$ \boxed{ \inf S\in S \Longrightarrow \min S=\inf S }. $$

Approximation from Above

If $g=\inf S$ is finite, then values of $S$ occur arbitrarily close above $g$. For every $\varepsilon>0$, there exists $x\in S$ such that:

$$ \boxed{ g\leq x<g+\varepsilon }. $$

Completeness of the Real Numbers

Every nonempty subset of $\mathbb{R}$ that is bounded below has a greatest lower bound in $\mathbb{R}$. This is one of the standard formulations of completeness.

Common Misconception

The greatest lower bound is not necessarily the smallest member of the set. It is the sharpest possible lower boundary, whether or not that boundary is attained.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\inf S$ "The infimum of S."
$g=\inf S$ "G is the greatest lower bound of S."
$L\leq x$ for all $x\in S$ "L is a lower bound of S."
$\inf S\in S$ "The infimum is attained."
$\min S=\inf S$ "The minimum equals the greatest lower bound."
$\inf S\notin S$ "The greatest lower bound is not an element of the set."

Greatest lower bound, GLB, and infimum describe the same core concept. The word minimum should be reserved for an attained least element.

Defense Notation Advice: Distinguish carefully among lower bound, greatest lower bound, and minimum. A set can have many lower bounds, one greatest lower bound, and no minimum.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Meaning: The greatest lower bound is the largest value lying below every element of the set.
  • Synonym: Greatest lower bound and infimum are equivalent terms.
  • Minimum Distinction: A minimum must belong to the set; an infimum need not.
  • Open Interval: $(a,b)$ has greatest lower bound $a$ but no minimum.
  • Closed Interval: $[a,b]$ has greatest lower bound $a$, which is also its minimum.
  • Functions: The infimum of the range may exist even if the function never attains it.
  • Bounded Functions: Lower boundedness provides the setting in which a finite infimum may be studied.
  • Real Analysis: The greatest-lower-bound property is fundamental to completeness of $\mathbb{R}$.
  • Optimization: An infimum may describe the best achievable lower limit even when no actual minimizer exists.
  • Order Theory: Infimum extends naturally to partially ordered sets, where it need not be a numerical quantity.
  • Dual Concept: The corresponding upper-bound notion is the least upper bound, or supremum.
  • Common Error: Choosing merely a lower bound instead of the greatest lower bound.
  • Common Error: Assuming the infimum must belong to the set.
  • Common Error: Treating $\inf S$ and $\min S$ as interchangeable without checking attainment.
  • Core Interpretation: The greatest lower bound is the sharp lower edge of an ordered set: $$ \boxed{ g\leq x \text{ for every }x\in S, \qquad \text{and no larger lower bound exists} }. $$ It captures the best possible lower boundary even when the set never actually reaches that boundary.

Extremum

Subfield: Calculus & Analysis

Etymology & Roots

An extremum is a maximum or minimum value of a function, sequence, or related mathematical object. The plural is extrema.

$$ \boxed{ \text{extremum} = \text{maximum or minimum} } $$

The word comes from Latin extremus, meaning outermost or farthest. In analysis and optimization, it refers to values that lie at the upper or lower extreme of the relevant comparison set.

Operational Nuance

Maximum and Minimum

If $f(c)$ is greater than or equal to all comparison values, it is a maximum. If it is less than or equal to all comparison values, it is a minimum.

$$ \boxed{ f(c)\geq f(x) \quad\text{or}\quad f(c)\leq f(x) } $$

Absolute Extremum

An absolute or global extremum compares the function value with every value on the stated domain.

$$ f(c)\leq f(x) \text{ for all }x\in D $$

defines an absolute minimum on $D$.

Local Extremum

A local or relative extremum compares values only within some neighborhood of the point.

$$ \boxed{ f(c)\leq f(x) \text{ for all }x \text{ sufficiently near }c } $$

Critical Numbers

For a differentiable interior point, a local extremum commonly occurs where:

$$ \boxed{ f'(c)=0 }. $$

More generally, critical numbers include points where $f'(c)=0$ or where $f'(c)$ does not exist while $f(c)$ is defined. Critical numbers are candidates for extrema, not guarantees.

Endpoint Extrema

An absolute extremum on a closed interval can occur at an endpoint even though an endpoint need not satisfy the usual interior critical-point condition.

Extreme Point vs. Extreme Value

The input $c$ is a location where an extremum occurs, while $f(c)$ is the extremal value itself. The graph point is $(c,f(c))$.

Strict vs. Non-Strict Extrema

A strict local maximum satisfies $f(c)>f(x)$ for nearby $x\neq c$, while a non-strict maximum permits equality at nearby points.

Common Misconception

A point where $f'(c)=0$ is not automatically an extremum. For example, $f(x)=x^3$ has $f'(0)=0$, but $x=0$ is neither a local maximum nor a local minimum.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$f(c)=\max_{x\in D}f(x)$ "F of c is the absolute maximum value on D."
$f(c)=\min_{x\in D}f(x)$ "F of c is the absolute minimum value on D."
$f'(c)=0$ "C is a stationary critical number."
$(c,f(c))$ "The point where the extremum occurs."
$f(c)>f(x)$ nearby "F has a strict local maximum at c."
$f(c)<f(x)$ nearby "F has a strict local minimum at c."

Extremum is singular and extrema is plural. The terms local and relative are often used interchangeably, as are absolute and global.

Defense Notation Advice: State whether the extremum is local or absolute, identify the domain of comparison, and distinguish the input location from the resulting extreme value.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Maximum: The upper extremum of the relevant comparison set.
  • Minimum: The lower extremum of the relevant comparison set.
  • Local Extremum: Comparison is restricted to a neighborhood of the point.
  • Absolute Extremum: Comparison is made over the entire stated domain.
  • Critical Numbers: Interior extrema commonly occur among critical numbers.
  • Endpoints: Absolute extrema on restricted intervals may occur at boundary points.
  • Optimization: Finding extrema is a central goal in maximizing profit, minimizing cost, optimizing dimensions, and selecting best feasible outcomes.
  • Extreme Value Theorem: A continuous real-valued function on a compact interval attains both an absolute maximum and an absolute minimum.
  • Several Variables: Extrema generalize to functions on higher-dimensional domains, where gradients and boundary constraints become important.
  • Strict Extremum: The extremal value is strictly greater or less than neighboring competing values.
  • Common Error: Calling every critical number an extremum.
  • Common Error: Confusing a local extremum with an absolute extremum.
  • Common Error: Confusing the extremizing input $c$ with the extremal value $f(c)$.
  • Core Interpretation: An extremum identifies an upper or lower extreme under a specified comparison: $$ \boxed{ \text{domain} + \text{comparison rule} \longrightarrow \text{maximum or minimum} }. $$ The meaning depends on whether the comparison is local or global and on the domain over which the function is being considered.

Area between Curves

Subfield: Calculus & Analysis

Etymology & Roots

Area between curves is the geometric area of a region bounded by two or more curves. In elementary calculus, it is commonly found by integrating the distance between an upper and lower function over an interval.

$$ \boxed{ A=\int_a^b \left[f(x)-g(x)\right]\,dx } $$

when $f(x)\geq g(x)$ throughout $[a,b]$. The phrase emphasizes geometric separation: each thin slice has height equal to the difference between the bounding curves.

Operational Nuance

Upper Minus Lower

For vertical slices, the basic rule is:

$$ \boxed{ A= \int_a^b (\text{upper}-\text{lower})\,dx }. $$

The difference gives the vertical height of each infinitesimal strip.

Finding the Bounds

When the region is bounded by intersections of $y=f(x)$ and $y=g(x)$, solve:

$$ \boxed{ f(x)=g(x) } $$

to locate possible integration limits.

Example

Consider:

$$ y=2x \qquad\text{and}\qquad y=x^2. $$

Their intersections satisfy:

$$ x^2=2x \Longrightarrow x=0,\ 2. $$

Since $2x\geq x^2$ on $[0,2]$:

$$ A= \int_0^2(2x-x^2)\,dx = \boxed{\frac{4}{3}}. $$

Curves That Switch Order

If the upper and lower functions exchange positions, split the interval at their intersections:

$$ \boxed{ A= \int_a^c [f(x)-g(x)]\,dx + \int_c^b [g(x)-f(x)]\,dx }. $$

Absolute-Difference Form

More compactly, when appropriate:

$$ \boxed{ A= \int_a^b |f(x)-g(x)|\,dx }. $$

Horizontal Slices

Some regions are easier to describe using $y$ as the variable. If $x=R(y)$ is the right boundary and $x=L(y)$ the left boundary:

$$ \boxed{ A= \int_c^d [R(y)-L(y)]\,dy }. $$

Right Minus Left

Thus the horizontal-slice analogue of upper minus lower is:

$$ \boxed{ \text{right}-\text{left} }. $$

Area Must Be Nonnegative

Geometric area cannot be negative. A negative integral usually indicates that the bounding functions were subtracted in the wrong order for the chosen slice.

Several Boundary Pieces

A single geometric region may require multiple integrals if its upper, lower, left, or right boundary changes formula across the region.

Common Misconception

The area between curves is not automatically the integral of one function minus the other in the order they were listed. The correct order is determined geometrically by which curve is above or below, or right or left, on each part of the region.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\int_a^b[f(x)-g(x)]\,dx$ "Integrate the upper function minus the lower function from a to b."
$f(x)=g(x)$ "Find the intersection points of the curves."
$|f(x)-g(x)|$ "The vertical distance between the two graphs."
$\int_c^d[R(y)-L(y)]\,dy$ "Integrate right minus left with respect to y."
$A\geq0$ "Geometric area is nonnegative."
$dx$ "Use vertical slices."

In classroom language, the mnemonic top minus bottom is common for $dx$ integration, while right minus left is common for $dy$ integration. More formally, the integrand represents the thickness-direction distance between the boundaries.

Defense Notation Advice: Sketch or analyze the region before integrating. Identify intersections, determine which boundary is upper or right, and split the integral whenever that ordering changes.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Vertical Slices: Use $\int(\text{upper}-\text{lower})\,dx$.
  • Horizontal Slices: Use $\int(\text{right}-\text{left})\,dy$.
  • Intersection Points: Often determine the integration bounds.
  • Absolute Difference: $\int|f-g|$ represents total vertical separation over an interval.
  • Changing Order: Split the region where the curves cross.
  • Piecewise Boundaries: Multiple integrals may be needed when boundary formulas change.
  • Geometric Area: Must be nonnegative.
  • Area below a Curve: Is the special case in which the lower boundary is often the $x$-axis.
  • Integral Interpretation: The integrand gives a local separation and integration accumulates these thin strips.
  • Modeling: The same structure can measure differences between cumulative profiles, cross sections, or competing quantities.
  • Common Error: Subtracting lower minus upper.
  • Common Error: Using incorrect intersection points as limits.
  • Common Error: Failing to split an interval when the curves exchange order.
  • Common Error: Choosing $dx$ when a $dy$ setup would require fewer pieces, or vice versa.
  • Core Interpretation: Area between curves accumulates the distance between boundaries: $$ \boxed{ A= \int (\text{outer boundary}-\text{inner boundary}) \times (\text{slice thickness}) }. $$ For vertical slices this becomes upper minus lower; for horizontal slices it becomes right minus left.

First Order Differential Equation

Subfield: Calculus & Analysis

Etymology & Roots

A first order differential equation is a differential equation in which the highest derivative of the unknown function is a first derivative.

$$ \boxed{ F\left(x,y,y'\right)=0 } $$

The word order refers to the highest derivative present. Thus an equation involving $y'$ but no $y''$, $y^{(3)}$, or higher derivative is first order.

Operational Nuance

Explicit First-Order Form

Many first-order equations can be written as:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{dy}{dx}=f(x,y) }. $$

This form specifies the slope of a solution curve at each point where $f$ is defined.

Solution

A solution is a differentiable function $y=\phi(x)$ that satisfies the equation throughout the interval under consideration.

$$ \phi'(x)=f\left(x,\phi(x)\right). $$

Initial Value Problem

A first-order equation is often paired with one initial condition:

$$ \boxed{ y'=f(x,y), \qquad y(x_0)=y_0 }. $$

Under suitable conditions, the initial value selects a unique solution from a family of possible solutions.

Separable Equations

A separable first-order equation can be rearranged so that the variables occur on opposite sides:

$$ \frac{dy}{dx}=g(x)h(y). $$

Where the required divisions are valid, it may be rewritten and integrated as:

$$ \frac{1}{h(y)}\,dy=g(x)\,dx. $$

First-Order Linear Equation

The standard linear form is:

$$ \boxed{ y'+P(x)y=Q(x) }. $$

Such equations are commonly solved using an integrating factor.

Autonomous Equation

If the independent variable does not appear explicitly, the equation is autonomous:

$$ \boxed{ y'=f(y) }. $$

Equilibrium solutions occur at values $y=y_*$ satisfying $f(y_*)=0$.

Slope Fields

The equation $y'=f(x,y)$ assigns a slope at each suitable point $(x,y)$. A slope field visualizes these local directions even when an explicit solution formula is unavailable.

Numerical Solutions

First-order equations can be approximated numerically. Euler's method, for example, advances according to:

$$ \boxed{ y_{n+1} = y_n+h\,f(x_n,y_n) }. $$

Common Misconception

First order does not mean the equation has degree one or is necessarily linear. An equation such as $y'=y^2+x$ is first order but nonlinear.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$y'=f(x,y)$ "A first-order ordinary differential equation."
$y'+P(x)y=Q(x)$ "A first-order linear differential equation."
$y'=g(x)h(y)$ "A separable first-order differential equation."
$y'=f(y)$ "An autonomous first-order differential equation."
$y(x_0)=y_0$ "The initial condition specifies the solution value at x zero."
$f(y_*)=0$ "Y star is an equilibrium value."

The forms first-order differential equation and differential equation of first order have the same mathematical meaning. The hyphenated form is customary when first-order directly modifies differential equation.

Defense Notation Advice: Determine order from the highest derivative, not from powers appearing elsewhere in the equation. Then separately classify the equation as linear, nonlinear, separable, autonomous, exact, or another applicable type.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Definition: The highest derivative present is a first derivative.
  • Explicit Form: $y'=f(x,y)$ directly specifies the local slope of a solution.
  • Implicit Form: A first-order equation may instead appear as $F(x,y,y')=0$.
  • Linear Case: $y'+P(x)y=Q(x)$ is the standard first-order linear form.
  • Nonlinear Case: First-order equations may contain nonlinear dependence on $y$ or $y'$.
  • Separable Case: Variables can sometimes be separated and integrated independently.
  • Autonomous Case: $y'=f(y)$ supports equilibrium and phase-line analysis.
  • Initial Value Problems: One initial condition commonly accompanies a first-order ODE.
  • Slope Fields: Graphical direction information can reveal qualitative solution behavior.
  • Growth and Decay: Models such as $y'=ky$ are first-order differential equations.
  • Logistic Models: Equations such as $y'=ry(1-y/K)$ are first-order but nonlinear.
  • Numerical Analysis: Euler and Runge-Kutta methods approximate solutions when analytic formulas are unavailable.
  • Common Error: Calling an equation second order because a variable is squared.
  • Common Error: Assuming every first-order equation is linear.
  • Common Error: Dividing by a function of $y$ during separation without checking whether equilibrium solutions are lost.
  • Common Error: Treating a general solution family as the completed solution of an initial value problem before applying the initial condition.
  • Core Interpretation: A first-order differential equation specifies how the current state determines its immediate rate of change: $$ \boxed{ \text{current }(x,y) \longrightarrow y' \longrightarrow \text{local evolution of the solution} }. $$ It is the basic differential-equation framework for continuous growth, decay, flow, adjustment, and many other evolving systems.

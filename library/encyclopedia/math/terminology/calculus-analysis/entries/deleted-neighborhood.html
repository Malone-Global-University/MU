Deleted Neighborhood

Subfield: Geometry & Topology

Etymology & Roots

A deleted neighborhood of a point is a neighborhood surrounding that point with the point itself removed. It is also commonly called a punctured neighborhood.

$$ \boxed{ 0<|x-a|<\delta } $$

The word deleted refers to deleting the central point $a$ while retaining nearby points. This distinction is fundamental in limit theory, where behavior near a point can matter even when the value at the point does not.

Operational Nuance

Real-Line Form

A standard $\delta$-neighborhood of $a$ is:

$$ (a-\delta,a+\delta). $$

Deleting the center gives:

$$ \boxed{ (a-\delta,a)\cup(a,a+\delta) }. $$

Absolute-Value Form

The same deleted neighborhood can be written compactly as:

$$ \boxed{ \{x:0<|x-a|<\delta\} }. $$

The condition $|x-a|<\delta$ means that $x$ is close to $a$, while $0<|x-a|$ excludes $x=a$.

Connection to Limits

The definition of a function limit deliberately uses a deleted neighborhood:

$$ \boxed{ 0<|x-a|<\delta \Longrightarrow |f(x)-L|<\varepsilon }. $$

This allows $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)$ to exist even when $f(a)$ is undefined or differs from the limiting value.

Example

Consider:

$$ f(x)=\frac{x^2-1}{x-1}. $$

The formula is undefined at $x=1$, but for every $x\neq1$:

$$ f(x)=x+1. $$

Therefore behavior within deleted neighborhoods of $1$ gives:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to1}f(x)=2 }. $$

Metric-Space Form

In a metric space with distance function $d$, a deleted ball around $a$ may be written:

$$ \boxed{ \{x:0<d(x,a)<r\} }. $$

Topological Form

More generally, if $N$ is a neighborhood of $a$, then:

$$ \boxed{ N\setminus\{a\} } $$

is the corresponding deleted neighborhood. After removal of $a$, the resulting set need not itself be a neighborhood of $a$ in the technical topological sense.

Accumulation Points

A point $a$ is an accumulation point of a set $S$ when every neighborhood of $a$ contains a point of $S$ other than $a$ itself. Equivalently, every deleted neighborhood of $a$ meets $S$.

Common Misconception

A deleted neighborhood does not mean deleting an entire interval around the point. Only the center point is removed; arbitrarily nearby points remain.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$|x-a|<\delta$ "X lies within delta of a."
$0<|x-a|<\delta$ "X lies within a deleted delta-neighborhood of a."
$(a-\delta,a)\cup(a,a+\delta)$ "The interval around a with the point a removed."
$N\setminus\{a\}$ "The neighborhood N with a deleted."
$0<d(x,a)<r$ "X lies within the punctured ball of radius r around a."
$x\to a$ "X approaches a without being required to equal a."

Deleted neighborhood and punctured neighborhood are standard synonyms. In metric-space language, punctured ball is also common.

Defense Notation Advice: In limit statements, retain the strict condition $0<|x-a|$. Omitting the lower inequality includes the center point and changes the logical condition being stated.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Meaning: A neighborhood with its center point removed.
  • Real Analysis: Commonly represented by $0<|x-a|<\delta$.
  • Limit Theory: Allows limits to depend on nearby behavior rather than the value at the point itself.
  • Removable Discontinuities: Deleted neighborhoods make it possible to analyze a function around a missing point.
  • Metric Spaces: A punctured open ball excludes its center while retaining points within the specified radius.
  • Topology: A deleted neighborhood may be written $N\setminus\{a\}$ for a neighborhood $N$ of $a$.
  • Accumulation Points: Every deleted neighborhood of an accumulation point intersects the relevant set.
  • Isolation: A point is isolated in a set when some deleted neighborhood contains no other points of that set.
  • Continuity Contrast: Continuity requires both the limit near the point and the actual function value at the point to fit together.
  • Common Error: Including the center point when a deleted neighborhood is required.
  • Common Error: Assuming $f(a)$ must exist in order to discuss $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)$.
  • Common Error: Interpreting "deleted" as removing a whole region rather than one point.
  • Terminology Variation: "Punctured neighborhood" is frequently preferred in topology and analysis.
  • Core Interpretation: A deleted neighborhood captures the mathematical idea of being arbitrarily close to a point without being at that point: $$ \boxed{ 0<\text{distance from }x\text{ to }a<\text{chosen radius} }. $$ This distinction is central to limits, accumulation points, discontinuities, and local analysis.

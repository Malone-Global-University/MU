Discontinuous Function

Subfield: Calculus & Analysis

Etymology & Roots

A discontinuous function is a function that fails to be continuous at one or more points of its domain or over the region being considered.

$$ \boxed{ f\text{ discontinuous at }a \iff \lim_{x\to a}f(x)\neq f(a) \text{ or the required limit/value fails to exist} } $$

The prefix dis- indicates a break or failure of continuity. The phrase describes a function whose nearby behavior does not fit the continuity condition everywhere under consideration.

Operational Nuance

Continuity Condition

A function is continuous at $a$ when:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to a}f(x)=f(a) }. $$

A discontinuous function violates this condition somewhere.

Removable Discontinuity

The limit may exist while the function value is missing or incorrectly assigned:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to a}f(x)=L, \qquad f(a)\neq L \text{ or }f(a)\text{ undefined} }. $$

Jump Discontinuity

A finite jump occurs when the one-sided limits exist but disagree:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to a^-}f(x) \neq \lim_{x\to a^+}f(x) }. $$

Infinite Discontinuity

A function may become unbounded near a point:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to a^\pm}f(x)=\pm\infty }. $$

Vertical asymptotes commonly produce this behavior.

Oscillatory Discontinuity

A function can also fail to approach a limit because it oscillates indefinitely, as with:

$$ f(x)=\sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) $$

near $x=0$.

Piecewise Functions

Piecewise-defined functions are often discontinuous at points where their formulas change unless the neighboring pieces match appropriately.

Discontinuous Does Not Mean Undefined Everywhere

A function can be well defined at almost every point and still be classified as discontinuous because continuity fails at even one relevant point.

Common Misconception

A discontinuous function need not have a dramatic visible break everywhere. A single removable hole, isolated mismatch, jump, or asymptotic failure is enough to destroy continuity on an interval containing that point.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$f$ discontinuous at $a$ "F is discontinuous at a."
$\lim_{x\to a}f(x)\neq f(a)$ "The limiting value does not agree with the function value."
$\lim_{x\to a^-}f(x)\neq\lim_{x\to a^+}f(x)$ "The one-sided limits disagree."
$f(a)$ undefined "The function has no value assigned at a."
$\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=\infty$ "The function becomes unbounded near a."
$f\notin C([a,b])$ "F is not continuous on the interval from a to b."

The phrase discontinuous function is global or interval-based unless a specific point is named. More precisely, mathematicians often say that a function is discontinuous at $a$.

Defense Notation Advice: Identify the actual failure: undefined value, mismatched limit, unequal one-sided limits, unbounded behavior, or oscillation. This is more informative than merely labeling a function discontinuous.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Meaning: A discontinuous function fails the continuity condition at one or more relevant points.
  • Removable Case: The nearby limit exists but the point value is missing or mismatched.
  • Jump Case: Left- and right-hand limits exist but are unequal.
  • Infinite Case: The function becomes unbounded near a point.
  • Oscillatory Case: The function fails to settle toward a single limiting value.
  • Piecewise Functions: Boundary points between formulas are natural places to test continuity.
  • Rational Functions: Denominator zeros may create removable or infinite discontinuities.
  • Step Functions: Commonly contain jump discontinuities.
  • Differentiability: A discontinuous function cannot be differentiable at the same point.
  • Integration: Some discontinuous functions remain integrable despite isolated or controlled discontinuities.
  • Modeling: Discontinuities can represent switches, impacts, thresholds, shocks, or idealized abrupt changes.
  • Common Error: Assuming every undefined point is removable.
  • Common Error: Confusing discontinuity with nondifferentiability at a corner or cusp.
  • Common Error: Declaring an entire function meaningless because it is discontinuous at one point.
  • Core Interpretation: A discontinuous function has at least one location where local values fail to fit together continuously: $$ \boxed{ \text{nearby behavior} \not\longrightarrow \text{compatible point behavior} }. $$ The particular failure may be removable, jumping, unbounded, oscillatory, or another form of discontinuity depending on the function and domain.

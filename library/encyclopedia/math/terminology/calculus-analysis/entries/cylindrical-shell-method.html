Cylindrical Shell Method

Subfield: Calculus & Analysis

Etymology & Roots

The cylindrical shell method is a technique for finding the volume of a solid of revolution by decomposing it into many thin cylindrical shells.

$$ \boxed{ dV = 2\pi(\text{radius})(\text{height})(\text{thickness}) } $$

The word shell emphasizes that each element is a thin hollow cylindrical layer rather than a solid disk or washer. Integrating the shell volumes accumulates the entire solid.

Operational Nuance

Basic Shell Formula

A thin cylindrical shell of radius $r$, height $h$, and thickness $dr$ has approximate volume:

$$ dV \approx (2\pi r)h\,dr. $$

Passing to the integral gives:

$$ \boxed{ V= 2\pi \int (\text{radius})(\text{height})\,d(\text{thickness variable}) }. $$

Rotation about the $y$-Axis

If a region under $y=f(x)$ for $a\leq x\leq b$ is revolved about the $y$-axis and $x\geq0$, vertical strips produce shells with radius $x$ and height $f(x)$:

$$ \boxed{ V= 2\pi \int_a^b x f(x)\,dx }. $$

Region between Two Curves

If the upper boundary is $y=f(x)$ and the lower boundary is $y=g(x)$, then shell height is:

$$ \boxed{ h(x)=f(x)-g(x) }. $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ V= 2\pi \int_a^b (\text{radius}) [f(x)-g(x)] \,dx }. $$

Shifted Axis of Rotation

If the axis is the vertical line $x=c$, the shell radius is the distance from the strip to that line:

$$ \boxed{ r(x)=|x-c| }. $$

On an interval lying entirely on one side of the axis, the absolute value is usually simplified according to the geometry.

Horizontal Shells

Rotation about a horizontal axis can be handled with horizontal strips. For example, about the $x$-axis:

$$ \boxed{ V= 2\pi \int (\text{vertical radius}) (\text{horizontal shell height}) \,dy }. $$

Shells vs. Washers

Shells use slices parallel to the axis of rotation. Disk and washer methods use slices perpendicular to the axis of rotation.

$$ \boxed{ \text{shells: parallel slices} \qquad \text{washers: perpendicular slices} }. $$

Example

Revolve the region under $y=x$ from $x=0$ to $x=1$ about the $y$-axis:

$$ V= 2\pi \int_0^1 x(x)\,dx = 2\pi \left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^1 = \boxed{\frac{2\pi}{3}}. $$

Common Misconception

The factor $2\pi r$ is a circumference, not an area. A shell volume comes from circumference times shell height times shell thickness.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$2\pi r$ "The circumference of the cylindrical shell."
$h(x)$ "The height of the shell."
$dx$ "The shell thickness for vertical strips."
$V=2\pi\int rh\,dx$ "Integrate two pi times radius times shell height."
$r(x)=|x-c|$ "The shell radius is the distance from x to the axis x equals c."
$h(x)=f(x)-g(x)$ "The shell height is upper function minus lower function."

The expressions shell method, method of cylindrical shells, and cylindrical shell method usually refer to the same volume technique.

Defense Notation Advice: Identify the axis of rotation before assigning the radius. Radius is always a distance to that axis, while shell height is the length of the strip parallel to it.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Formula: $V=2\pi\int(\text{radius})(\text{height})(\text{thickness})$.
  • Vertical Axis: Vertical strips usually generate cylindrical shells.
  • Horizontal Axis: Horizontal strips usually generate cylindrical shells.
  • Radius: The perpendicular distance from a representative strip to the axis of rotation.
  • Height: The length of the representative strip parallel to the axis.
  • Thickness: Usually $dx$ for vertical shells and $dy$ for horizontal shells.
  • Between Curves: Shell height is commonly upper minus lower or right minus left, depending on orientation.
  • Shifted Axes: Radius must be adjusted to measure distance from the actual axis of rotation.
  • Disk/Washer Contrast: Shell slices are parallel to the axis; washer slices are perpendicular.
  • Efficiency: Shells can avoid solving equations for the opposite variable when a washer setup would be cumbersome.
  • Common Error: Using the coordinate itself as the radius when the axis of rotation is shifted.
  • Common Error: Forgetting the factor $2\pi$.
  • Common Error: Confusing shell height with shell radius.
  • Common Error: Choosing slices perpendicular to the axis and then trying to use the shell formula.
  • Core Interpretation: The shell method builds volume from thin cylindrical layers: $$ \boxed{ dV = (\text{circumference}) (\text{height}) (\text{thickness}) = 2\pi r h\,dr }. $$ It is especially useful when slices parallel to the axis of rotation produce a simpler integral than disks or washers.

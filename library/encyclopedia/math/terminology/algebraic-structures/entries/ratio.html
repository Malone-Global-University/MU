Ratio

Subfield: Algebraic & Analytic Structures

Etymology & Roots

The mathematical word ratio comes from Latin ratio, which carried meanings such as "reckoning," "calculation," "account," and "reasoning." It is related to the Latin verb reri, meaning "to reckon" or "to calculate." The mathematical sense of a quantitative relationship between magnitudes became established in English during the seventeenth century.

The history of the word reflects the central idea behind a ratio: one quantity is understood by reckoning it in relation to another. Rather than describing a quantity in isolation, a ratio expresses how two quantities compare.

Operational Nuance

A ratio is a comparison of two quantities by division. If quantities $a$ and $b$ are compared, with $b\neq0$, their ratio may be written:

$$ a:b $$ $$ a\text{ to }b $$ $$ \frac{a}{b}. $$

These three forms communicate the same basic comparison. For example, if a class has $12$ students studying French and $8$ studying German, the ratio of French students to German students is:

$$ 12:8=\frac{12}{8}=\frac{3}{2}. $$

This may be read as "twelve to eight," "three to two," or as saying that there are $\frac{3}{2}$ as many French students as German students.

Ratios can often be simplified in the same way as fractions. Thus:

$$ 18:24=3:4. $$

Simplifying does not change the comparison because both quantities are divided by the same nonzero factor.

The order of a ratio matters. In general:

$$ a:b\neq b:a. $$

For example:

$$ 2:5=\frac{2}{5}, \qquad 5:2=\frac{5}{2}. $$

The two comparisons are reciprocals, not identical statements.

Ratios may compare a part to another part, a part to the whole, or the whole to a part. Suppose a basket contains $3$ red apples and $7$ green apples, for a total of $10$:

$$ \text{red : green}=3:7 $$ $$ \text{red : total}=3:10 $$ $$ \text{total : red}=10:3. $$

These are all legitimate ratios, but they answer different questions.

A ratio may be dimensionless when quantities with the same units are compared. For example:

$$ \frac{6\text{ cm}}{3\text{ cm}}=2. $$

The centimeters cancel. When quantities with different units are compared, however, the resulting comparison is commonly called a rate:

$$ \frac{120\text{ miles}}{2\text{ hours}} = 60\text{ miles per hour}. $$

Thus, a rate is a specialized kind of ratio involving quantities measured in different units.

Common Misconception: A ratio and a proportion are not the same thing. A ratio is a single comparison:

$$ \frac{a}{b}. $$

A proportion is an equation stating that two ratios are equal:

$$ \frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}. $$

The distinction is fundamental: ratio names the comparison; proportion names an equality between comparisons.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$3:5$ "Three to five."
$\frac{3}{5}$ "Three to five," or "the ratio of three to five," depending on context.
$a:b$ "The ratio of a to b."
$a:b=c:d$ "A is to b as c is to d."
$\frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}$ "The ratio a to b equals the ratio c to d."
$3:4:5$ "Three to four to five."

The colon notation makes the comparative structure especially visible:

$$ a:b. $$

Fraction notation emphasizes division:

$$ \frac{a}{b}. $$

Which notation is preferable depends on context. Geometry, recipes, scale drawings, and verbal comparisons often use colon notation, while algebraic calculations commonly use fraction notation because division operations are easier to manipulate symbolically.

Ratios can also contain more than two terms:

$$ 2:3:5. $$

Such a ratio compares three quantities simultaneously. If a mixture contains ingredients in the ratio $2:3:5$, then the whole mixture consists of:

$$ 2+3+5=10 $$

equal ratio-parts.

Defense Notation Advice: Always preserve the order named in the question. If a problem asks for the ratio of boys to girls, the number of boys must appear first. Also distinguish carefully between a ratio such as $2:3$ and a statement such as "two out of three." The latter usually represents the part-to-whole ratio $2:3$, whereas a situation with two objects of one type and three of another has a total of five and therefore a part-to-whole ratio of $2:5$.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Arithmetic: Ratios provide a basic way to compare quantities: $$ a:b=\frac{a}{b}. $$ Equivalent ratios are obtained by multiplying or dividing both terms by the same nonzero number: $$ 2:3=4:6=10:15. $$
  • Proportions: Two equal ratios form a proportion: $$ \frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}. $$ When $b$ and $d$ are nonzero, this equality implies: $$ ad=bc. $$ The cross-product relationship follows from ordinary multiplication of both sides by $bd$.
  • Rates: A ratio comparing quantities with different units is commonly called a rate. For example: $$ \frac{300\text{ km}}{5\text{ h}} = 60\text{ km/h}. $$ When the denominator is reduced to one unit, the result is a unit rate.
  • Geometry: Ratios compare lengths, areas, angles, and other geometric quantities. Similar figures have equal ratios of corresponding side lengths: $$ \frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{BC}{EF}. $$ Scale factors are therefore ratios.
  • Trigonometry: The elementary trigonometric functions sine, cosine, and tangent are defined as ratios of side lengths in right triangles: $$ \sin\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}, $$ $$ \cos\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}, $$ $$ \tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}. $$
  • Slope: Slope is a ratio of vertical change to horizontal change: $$ m = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}. $$ Thus, slope is fundamentally a comparison between two changes.
  • Probability: Odds are expressed using ratios. If an event has $3$ favorable outcomes and $2$ unfavorable outcomes, the odds in favor are: $$ 3:2. $$ This differs from probability, which compares favorable outcomes with the total: $$ \frac{3}{5}. $$
  • Statistics: Ratios appear in quantities such as odds ratios, risk ratios, likelihood ratios, and demographic comparisons. Their interpretation depends heavily on what appears in the numerator and denominator.
  • Finance & Economics: Financial analysis uses ratios such as debt-to-income, price-to-earnings, current ratio, and debt-to-equity. The mathematical operation is division, but the economic meaning comes from the specific quantities being compared.
  • Science: Ratios occur in concentration, density comparisons, mechanical advantage, stoichiometric relationships, and many dimensionless quantities. The ratio between quantities often reveals a relationship that absolute values alone do not show.
  • Scaling & Similarity: If every length in a figure is multiplied by a common factor $k$, then $k$ is the scale ratio or scale factor. A scale of: $$ 1:100 $$ means that one unit on the representation corresponds to one hundred of the same units in the represented object.
  • Golden Ratio: The term ratio also appears in named mathematical constants. The golden ratio $\varphi$ satisfies: $$ \frac{a+b}{a} = \frac{a}{b} = \varphi $$ for appropriately related positive lengths $a>b$.
  • Terminological Contrast: A ratio compares two quantities by division. A rate is generally a ratio between quantities with different units. A unit rate has a denominator corresponding to one unit. A proportion states that two ratios are equal. These concepts are closely related but should not be used as interchangeable terms.

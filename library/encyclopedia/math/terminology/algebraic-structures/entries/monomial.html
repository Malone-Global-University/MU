Monomial

Subfield: Algebraic Structures

Etymology & Roots

A monomial is a single algebraic term formed from a coefficient multiplied by variables raised to nonnegative integer powers.

A typical monomial in variables $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ has the form:

$$ \boxed{ c x_1^{a_1}x_2^{a_2}\cdots x_n^{a_n} } $$

where $c$ is a coefficient and:

$$ \boxed{ a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq0} }. $$

The word monomial combines the prefix mono-, meaning "one," with a root associated with mathematical terms or expressions. Linguistically, the name emphasizes that a monomial consists of one term, in contrast with binomials, trinomials, and more general polynomials.

$$ \boxed{ \text{mono} \longrightarrow \text{one term} }. $$

Examples include:

$$ \boxed{ 7,\quad 3x,\quad -5x^2,\quad 4x^3y^2,\quad \frac{2}{3}a^2b }. $$

Operational Nuance

Single-Term Structure

A monomial is one term rather than a sum or difference of distinct terms.

$$ \boxed{ 5x^3y^2 } $$

is a monomial, while:

$$ 5x^3y^2+2x $$

is not one monomial because it contains two terms.

Coefficient

The numerical factor multiplying the variable part is the coefficient.

In:

$$ -7x^4y, $$

the coefficient is:

$$ \boxed{ -7 }. $$

Variable Part

The variable portion of:

$$ -7x^4y $$

is:

$$ \boxed{ x^4y }. $$

Implicit Coefficient

A monomial such as:

$$ x^3 $$

has implicit coefficient:

$$ \boxed{ 1 }. $$

Negative Implicit Coefficient

The monomial:

$$ -x^3 $$

has coefficient:

$$ \boxed{ -1 }. $$

Constant Monomial

A nonzero constant is a monomial:

$$ \boxed{ 7 = 7x^0 }. $$

The Zero Monomial

The expression $0$ is also commonly called the zero monomial or simply the zero polynomial when considered in a polynomial context.

Its degree requires special care: unlike a nonzero constant, it does not have an ordinary finite degree under the standard convention.

Exponent Restriction

In ordinary polynomial algebra, variable exponents in a monomial must be nonnegative integers.

$$ \boxed{ x^0,\ x^1,\ x^2,\ldots } $$

Valid Example

$$ \boxed{ 4x^3y^2 } $$

Negative Exponent Is Not a Polynomial Monomial

The expression:

$$ x^{-2} $$

is not a monomial in the ordinary polynomial sense because the exponent is negative.

Fractional Exponent Is Not a Polynomial Monomial

Likewise:

$$ x^{1/2} $$

is not a polynomial monomial.

Variable in the Denominator

The expression:

$$ \frac{3}{x} = 3x^{-1} $$

is therefore not a polynomial monomial.

Numerical Fractions Are Allowed as Coefficients

The coefficient itself may be fractional:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{3}{5}x^4 } $$

is a monomial.

Irrational Coefficients

Depending on the coefficient field, a coefficient such as $\sqrt{2}$ is also permitted:

$$ \boxed{ \sqrt{2}x^3 }. $$

Complex Coefficients

Over the complex numbers:

$$ \boxed{ (2+i)x^4 } $$

is a monomial.

Coefficient Domain Matters

Whether a coefficient is allowed depends on the polynomial ring or coefficient system being used.

One-Variable Monomial

In one variable:

$$ \boxed{ ax^n } $$

where $n$ is a nonnegative integer.

Multivariable Monomial

In several variables:

$$ \boxed{ cx^ay^bz^d }. $$

Every variable exponent must be a nonnegative integer.

Degree of a One-Variable Monomial

For nonzero:

$$ ax^n, $$

its degree is:

$$ \boxed{ n }. $$

Degree of a Multivariable Monomial

The total degree of:

$$ cx^ay^bz^d $$

is:

$$ \boxed{ a+b+d }. $$

Example of Total Degree

$$ 5x^3y^2z^4 $$

has total degree:

$$ \boxed{ 3+2+4=9 }. $$

Degree with Respect to a Variable

For:

$$ 5x^3y^2z^4, $$

the degree with respect to $x$ is $3$, with respect to $y$ is $2$, and with respect to $z$ is $4$.

Degree of a Nonzero Constant

$$ \boxed{ \deg(c)=0 } $$

for nonzero constant $c$.

Degree of Zero

The zero polynomial has no ordinary finite degree. Some advanced conventions assign it degree $-\infty$ for technical convenience.

Like Monomials

Two monomials are like terms when they have the same variable factors raised to the same powers.

For example:

$$ \boxed{ 3x^2y \quad\text{and}\quad -5x^2y } $$

are like terms.

Unlike Monomials

$$ x^2y \qquad\text{and}\qquad xy^2 $$

are not like terms because their exponent patterns differ.

Exponent Vector

A monomial:

$$ x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_n^{a_n} $$

can be encoded by its exponent vector:

$$ \boxed{ (a_1,\ldots,a_n) }. $$

Multi-Index Notation

If:

$$ \alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n), $$

one often writes:

$$ \boxed{ x^\alpha = x_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots x_n^{\alpha_n} }. $$

Total Degree in Multi-Index Form

$$ \boxed{ |\alpha| = \alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_n }. $$

Multiplication of Monomials

Multiply coefficients and add exponents of matching bases:

$$ \boxed{ (ax^m)(bx^n) = abx^{m+n} }. $$

Example

$$ (3x^2)(-4x^5) = -12x^7. $$

Multivariable Multiplication

$$ (2x^3y)(5x^2y^4) = \boxed{ 10x^5y^5 }. $$

Product of Monomials Is a Monomial

Polynomial monomials are closed under multiplication.

$$ \boxed{ \text{monomial}\cdot\text{monomial} = \text{monomial} }. $$

Degree of a Product

For nonzero monomials $M$ and $N$:

$$ \boxed{ \deg(MN) = \deg(M)+\deg(N) }. $$

Division of Monomials

Dividing monomials subtracts exponents:

$$ \frac{ax^m}{bx^n} = \frac{a}{b}x^{m-n} $$

when $b\neq0$.

Division May Leave Polynomial Monomials

If $m\geq n$, the exponent $m-n$ remains nonnegative.

$$ \frac{x^5}{x^2} = \boxed{x^3}. $$

Division May Leave the Polynomial Class

If $m<n$:

$$ \frac{x^2}{x^5} = x^{-3} = \frac{1}{x^3}, $$

which is not a polynomial monomial.

Monomial Divisibility

A monomial:

$$ x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_n^{a_n} $$

divides:

$$ x_1^{b_1}\cdots x_n^{b_n} $$

precisely when:

$$ \boxed{ a_i\leq b_i \text{ for every }i }. $$

Example of Monomial Divisibility

The monomial:

$$ x^2y $$

divides:

$$ x^5y^3 $$

because:

$$ 2\leq5 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 1\leq3. $$

Greatest Common Monomial Factor

For variable parts, the greatest common monomial factor uses the minimum exponent of each shared variable.

For:

$$ x^5y^2 \qquad\text{and}\qquad x^3y^7, $$

the common variable factor is:

$$ \boxed{ x^3y^2 }. $$

Least Common Multiple of Monomial Variable Parts

Use the maximum exponent of each variable:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{lcm} (x^5y^2,x^3y^7) = x^5y^7 }. $$

Addition of Like Monomials

Like monomials combine by adding coefficients:

$$ \boxed{ ax^m+bx^m = (a+b)x^m }. $$

Example

$$ 3x^4-7x^4 = \boxed{ -4x^4 }. $$

Addition of Unlike Monomials

Unlike monomials do not combine into a single monomial by ordinary collection of like terms.

$$ x^2+x^3 $$

remains a two-term polynomial.

Monomial vs. Term

In a polynomial, each term is itself a monomial.

For:

$$ 3x^2-5x+7, $$

the monomial terms are:

$$ \boxed{ 3x^2,\quad -5x,\quad 7 }. $$

Monomial vs. Polynomial

A monomial is a polynomial with exactly one nonzero term.

$$ \boxed{ \text{monomial} \subset \text{polynomial} }. $$

Binomial

A polynomial with two nonzero terms is a binomial:

$$ \boxed{ x+3 }. $$

Trinomial

A polynomial with three nonzero terms is a trinomial:

$$ \boxed{ x^2+3x+2 }. $$

Polynomial

A polynomial is a finite sum of monomials:

$$ \boxed{ P(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{n}a_kx^k }. $$

Multivariable Polynomial

More generally:

$$ \boxed{ P(x_1,\ldots,x_n) = \sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}x^\alpha } $$

with only finitely many nonzero coefficients $c_\alpha$.

Monomial as Building Block

Monomials are the basic multiplicative building blocks from which polynomial expressions are assembled.

Standard Form

A monomial is usually written with:

  1. the numerical coefficient first;
  2. variables following;
  3. powers combined by base;
  4. variables often arranged alphabetically.

Example of Standardization

The expression:

$$ 3yx^2x $$

simplifies to:

$$ \boxed{ 3x^3y }. $$

Implicit Exponent

A variable written without a visible exponent has exponent $1$:

$$ \boxed{ x=x^1 }. $$

Missing Variable as Exponent Zero

In a multivariable context:

$$ 3x^2 = 3x^2y^0. $$

This convention makes exponent-vector notation consistent.

Evaluation of a Monomial

Substitute values for the variables and evaluate using ordinary arithmetic.

If:

$$ M(x,y)=3x^2y $$

and:

$$ x=2, \qquad y=-1, $$

then:

$$ M(2,-1) = 3(2)^2(-1) = \boxed{-12}. $$

Sign Behavior

The sign of a monomial depends on its coefficient and on the parity of variable exponents when negative inputs are substituted.

Even Powers

$$ (-x)^{2k}=x^{2k}. $$

Odd Powers

$$ (-x)^{2k+1}=-x^{2k+1}. $$

One-Variable Monomial Function

A function of the form:

$$ \boxed{ f(x)=ax^n } $$

is often called a monomial function.

Scaling Property

For:

$$ f(x)=ax^n, $$

scaling the input gives:

$$ \boxed{ f(tx)=t^nf(x) }. $$

Homogeneous Monomial

Every monomial in several variables is homogeneous of its total degree.

If:

$$ M(x_1,\ldots,x_n) $$

has total degree $d$, then:

$$ \boxed{ M(tx_1,\ldots,tx_n) = t^dM(x_1,\ldots,x_n) }. $$

Example of Homogeneity

For:

$$ M(x,y)=3x^2y^3, $$

the total degree is $5$, and:

$$ M(tx,ty) = 3(tx)^2(ty)^3 = t^5M(x,y). $$

Monomial Ordering

In multivariable polynomial algebra, monomials can be arranged according to a monomial order, such as lexicographic or graded lexicographic order.

Leading Monomial

Once a monomial order is chosen, a nonzero polynomial has a leading monomial.

Example

In:

$$ P(x,y) = 4x^3y^2+2x^2y^5-x, $$

which monomial is considered leading can depend on the chosen monomial ordering.

Monomial Ideals

In abstract algebra, a monomial ideal is an ideal generated by monomials.

For example:

$$ \boxed{ (x^2,xy^3,y^5) } $$

is a monomial ideal in a polynomial ring such as $k[x,y]$.

Laurent Monomial

In a Laurent polynomial ring, negative integer exponents are allowed.

$$ \boxed{ x^{-2}y^3 } $$

can therefore be called a Laurent monomial, even though it is not a monomial in the ordinary polynomial ring $k[x,y]$.

Generalized Uses

In more advanced mathematics, the word monomial may be extended beyond the elementary polynomial definition, so the ambient algebraic structure should be identified.

Common Error: Calling Any Single Algebraic Expression a Monomial

Being written as one visual piece is not sufficient. Variable exponents must obey the requirements of the polynomial context.

Common Error: Allowing Negative Variable Exponents

In ordinary polynomial algebra:

$$ x^{-1} $$

is not a monomial.

Common Error: Allowing Fractional Variable Exponents

The expression:

$$ x^{2/3} $$

is not an ordinary polynomial monomial.

Common Error: Rejecting Fractional Coefficients

A fractional coefficient is acceptable:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{2}{5}x^3 }. $$

Common Error: Confusing Coefficient and Degree

In:

$$ 7x^4, $$

the coefficient is $7$, while the degree is $4$.

Common Error: Adding Exponents When Adding Monomials

Incorrect:

$$ x^2+x^3=x^5. $$

Exponents are added during multiplication of like bases, not during addition.

Common Error: Multiplying Coefficients but Forgetting Exponent Addition

Correct:

$$ \boxed{ (2x^3)(4x^5) = 8x^8 }. $$

Common Error: Combining Unlike Terms

The expression:

$$ 3x^2+4x^3 $$

cannot be combined into a single monomial by adding coefficients.

Common Error: Forgetting Hidden Exponents

In:

$$ 4xy^3, $$

the exponent of $x$ is $1$.

Common Error: Forgetting Hidden Coefficients

In $x^4$, the coefficient is $1$; in $-x^4$, it is $-1$.

Common Error: Assigning the Zero Monomial Degree Zero

A nonzero constant has degree $0$, but the zero polynomial does not have ordinary degree $0$ under the standard convention.

Common Error: Confusing Total Degree with a Single Variable's Exponent

For:

$$ x^2y^5, $$

the total degree is:

$$ \boxed{ 2+5=7 }, $$

not $5$.

Common Error: Treating Division of Monomials as Always Producing a Monomial

The quotient can introduce negative powers and leave the ordinary polynomial class.

Common Error: Confusing Monomial with Polynomial

Every monomial is a polynomial, but a polynomial may contain several monomial terms.

Common Error: Ignoring the Coefficient Domain

Whether coefficients such as rational, real, or complex numbers are permitted depends on the polynomial ring being used.

Common Misconception: A monomial is not merely an expression with no visible plus sign. In ordinary polynomial algebra, it has the precise form of a coefficient times variables raised to nonnegative integer powers. Thus $3x^2y^4$ is a monomial, but $3/x$, $\sqrt{x}$, and $x^{-2}$ are not ordinary polynomial monomials.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$5x^3$ "Five x cubed," a monomial of degree three.
$-2x^4y^3$ "Negative two x to the fourth y cubed."
$cx_1^{a_1}\cdots x_n^{a_n}$ "A coefficient c times a product of variables raised to nonnegative integer powers."
$\deg(x^3y^2)=5$ "The total degree of x cubed y squared is five."
$x^\alpha$ "X to the multi-index alpha."
$(ax^m)(bx^n)=abx^{m+n}$ "Multiply the coefficients and add the exponents of like bases."
$3x^2y$ and $-7x^2y$ "Like monomials," because their variable parts are identical.

The term monomial describes structure rather than merely visual length. A complicated-looking expression can still be one monomial if it simplifies to a coefficient times nonnegative integer powers of variables.

For example:

$$ 2x^3\cdot5x^2y $$

simplifies to:

$$ \boxed{ 10x^5y }, $$

which is a monomial.

Defense Notation Advice: Put the coefficient first, combine powers of identical variables, and make hidden coefficients or exponents explicit when analyzing structure. In ordinary polynomial contexts, reject negative or fractional variable exponents. When discussing multivariable monomials, distinguish total degree from degree in an individual variable, and specify the coefficient domain when it matters.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • General Form: $cx_1^{a_1}\cdots x_n^{a_n}$.
  • Coefficient: Numerical or scalar factor.
  • Variable Part: Product of powers of variables.
  • Implicit Coefficient: $x^n$ has coefficient $1$.
  • Negative Implicit Coefficient: $-x^n$ has coefficient $-1$.
  • Constant Monomial: Every nonzero constant is a degree-zero monomial.
  • Zero Monomial: Zero requires a special degree convention.
  • Exponent Restriction: Ordinary polynomial monomials use nonnegative integer exponents.
  • Negative Exponent: Not allowed in ordinary polynomial monomials.
  • Fractional Exponent: Not allowed in ordinary polynomial monomials.
  • Variable Denominator: Produces a negative exponent and leaves the polynomial monomial class.
  • Fractional Coefficient: Allowed when the coefficient domain permits it.
  • Real Coefficient: Allowed over real polynomial rings.
  • Complex Coefficient: Allowed over complex polynomial rings.
  • One Variable: Typical form $ax^n$.
  • Several Variables: Typical form $cx^ay^bz^d$.
  • One-Variable Degree: Exponent of the variable.
  • Total Degree: Sum of exponents in a multivariable monomial.
  • Degree in a Variable: Exponent attached to that variable.
  • Nonzero Constant Degree: Zero.
  • Zero Polynomial Degree: Not ordinarily assigned a finite nonnegative degree.
  • Like Monomials: Same variable part and exponent pattern.
  • Unlike Monomials: Different variable or exponent patterns.
  • Exponent Vector: $(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$ records the variable powers.
  • Multi-Index: $x^\alpha$ compactly represents a multivariable monomial.
  • Multiplication: Multiply coefficients and add exponents of matching bases.
  • Closure under Multiplication: Product of polynomial monomials is a polynomial monomial.
  • Degree of Product: Degrees add for nonzero monomials.
  • Division: Subtracts exponents.
  • Division Caveat: Negative resulting exponents leave the polynomial monomial class.
  • Monomial Divisibility: Exponentwise comparison determines divisibility of variable parts.
  • Greatest Common Monomial Factor: Uses minimum exponents.
  • Least Common Multiple: Uses maximum exponents of variable parts.
  • Addition: Like monomials combine through their coefficients.
  • Unlike-Term Addition: Does not collapse to one monomial.
  • Polynomial Term: Every nonzero term of a polynomial is a monomial.
  • Monomial vs. Polynomial: A monomial is a one-term polynomial.
  • Binomial: Two-term polynomial.
  • Trinomial: Three-term polynomial.
  • Polynomial: Finite sum of monomials.
  • Standard Form: Coefficient first, combined variable powers afterward.
  • Implicit Exponent: $x=x^1$.
  • Missing Variable: May be interpreted as exponent zero in exponent-vector notation.
  • Evaluation: Substitute values into the monomial and calculate.
  • Sign Behavior: Depends on coefficient and parity of exponents.
  • Monomial Function: Function of form $f(x)=ax^n$.
  • Scaling: $f(tx)=t^nf(x)$ for a one-variable monomial of degree $n$.
  • Homogeneity: Every monomial is homogeneous of its total degree.
  • Monomial Order: Ordering used in multivariable polynomial algebra.
  • Leading Monomial: Largest monomial under a chosen monomial order.
  • Monomial Ideal: Ideal generated by monomials.
  • Laurent Monomial: Generalized monomial allowing negative integer exponents in a Laurent polynomial ring.
  • Abstract Algebra: Monomials serve as natural multiplicative basis elements of polynomial rings.

  • Core Interpretation: A monomial is a single polynomial building block: $$ \boxed{ c x_1^{a_1}x_2^{a_2}\cdots x_n^{a_n}, \qquad a_i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq0} }. $$ Its total degree is: $$ \boxed{ a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_n }, $$ and monomial multiplication adds exponent vectors: $$ \boxed{ x^\alpha x^\beta = x^{\alpha+\beta} }. $$ Finite sums of such terms form polynomials, making monomials the basic multiplicative units of polynomial algebra.

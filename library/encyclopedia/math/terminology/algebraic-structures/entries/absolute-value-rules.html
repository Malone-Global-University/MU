Absolute Value Rules

Subfield: Algebraic & Analytic Structures

Etymology & Roots

Absolute Value Rules refers to the standard algebraic identities, equation rules, and inequality rules that govern expressions involving absolute value.

These rules grow from the basic definition:

$$ \boxed{ |x| = \text{the nonnegative distance from }x\text{ to }0 }. $$

Algebraically:

$$ \boxed{ |x|= \begin{cases} x, & x\ge0,\\[4pt] -x, & x<0. \end{cases} } $$

Many familiar absolute-value rules can be derived directly from this definition. Others follow from the interpretation of absolute value as distance.

The word rules is pedagogical rather than the name of a single theorem. Depending on context, a textbook may group product properties, quotient properties, equations, inequalities, and triangle inequalities under the heading absolute value rules.

Operational Nuance

Rule 1: Nonnegativity

Absolute value can never be negative:

$$ \boxed{|x|\ge0}. $$

Furthermore:

$$ \boxed{ |x|=0 \iff x=0 }. $$

Rule 2: Opposites Have Equal Absolute Value

$$ \boxed{ |-x|=|x| }. $$

This reflects the fact that $x$ and $-x$ are equally far from zero.

Rule 3: Product Rule

$$ \boxed{ |ab|=|a||b| }. $$

For example:

$$ |(-3)(5)| = |-15| = 15 $$ $$ = 3\cdot5 = |-3||5|. $$

Rule 4: Quotient Rule

If:

$$ b\ne0, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ \left|\frac ab\right| = \frac{|a|}{|b|} }. $$

Rule 5: Powers

For integer powers:

$$ \boxed{ |x^n|=|x|^n } $$

whenever the expressions are defined.

In particular:

$$ |x|^2=x^2. $$

Rule 6: Square Root of a Square

For real $x$:

$$ \boxed{ \sqrt{x^2}=|x| }. $$

This rule is important because the principal square root is always nonnegative.

Therefore:

$$ \sqrt{x^2}=x $$

is only valid when:

$$ x\ge0. $$

Rule 7: Absolute Value Equation

If:

$$ |x|=a, $$

then the possible cases depend on $a$.

If:

$$ a>0, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ x=a \quad\text{or}\quad x=-a }. $$

If:

$$ a=0, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{x=0}. $$

If:

$$ a<0, $$

there is no real solution.

For:

$$ a>0, $$

Likewise:

$$ \boxed{ |x|\le a \iff -a\le x\le a }. $$

Rule 9: Greater-Than Inequalities

For:

$$ a>0, $$

the inequality:

$$ |x|>a $$

is equivalent to:

$$ \boxed{ x<-a \quad\text{or}\quad x>a }. $$

Likewise:

$$ \boxed{ |x|\ge a \iff x\le-a \text{ or } x\ge a }. $$

$$ \boxed{ |x-c| = \text{distance between }x\text{ and }c }. $$

Rule 11: Triangle Inequality

$$ \boxed{ |a+b| \le |a|+|b| }. $$

The magnitude of a sum cannot exceed the sum of the individual magnitudes.

Rule 12: Reverse Triangle Inequality

$$ \boxed{ \big||a|-|b|\big| \le |a-b| }. $$

This compares the difference of magnitudes with the magnitude of the difference.

Common Misconception: Absolute value does not distribute over addition. In general:

$$ \boxed{ |a+b| \ne |a|+|b| }. $$

For example:

$$ |3+(-3)|=0 $$

while:

$$ |3|+|-3|=6. $$

Absolute-value equations are often verbalized using distance language. For example:

$$ |x-5|=3 $$

may be read conceptually as:

$$ \text{"x is exactly three units from five."} $$

This immediately gives:

$$ x=2 \quad\text{or}\quad x=8. $$

Defense Notation Advice: Do not confuse:

$$ |a-b| $$

with:

$$ |a|-|b|. $$

They are generally not equal.

  • No Subtraction Distribution: In general: $$ \boxed{ |a-b| \ne |a|-|b| }. $$

  • Absolute Value of a Product of Many Factors: $$ \boxed{ |a_1a_2\cdots a_n| = |a_1||a_2|\cdots|a_n| }. $$
  • Even Powers: For integer $n$: $$ x^{2n}\ge0, $$ so: $$ |x^{2n}|=x^{2n}. $$
  • Odd Powers: $$ \boxed{ |x^{2n+1}| = |x|^{2n+1} }. $$
  • Factoring Inside Absolute Value: $$ |k(x-a)| = |k||x-a|. $$ This is often useful when solving equations or inequalities.
  • Example: $$ |2x-6| = |2(x-3)| = 2|x-3|. $$
  • Absolute Value Function: $$ f(x)=|x| $$ is defined piecewise by: $$ \boxed{ f(x)= \begin{cases} x, & x\ge0,\\ -x, & x<0. \end{cases} } $$
  • Graph Symmetry: Since: $$ |-x|=|x|, $$ the parent absolute-value function is even and symmetric about the $y$-axis.
  • Transformation Rule: For: $$ y=a|x-h|+k, $$ the vertex is: $$ \boxed{(h,k)}. $$
  • Calculus: Absolute value is continuous everywhere but: $$ f(x)=|x| $$ is not differentiable at: $$ x=0. $$
  • Analysis: Absolute value is used to express error and distance: $$ |f(x)-L|<\varepsilon. $$
  • Complex Numbers: The product and quotient rules extend to complex modulus: $$ |zw|=|z||w| $$ and: $$ \left|\frac zw\right| = \frac{|z|}{|w|}. $$
  • Absolute Convergence: For a series: $$ \sum a_n, $$ absolute convergence means: $$ \boxed{ \sum|a_n| \text{ converges} }. $$
  • Most Important Structural Distinction: Absolute value distributes cleanly over multiplication and division: $$ |ab|=|a||b|, $$ $$ \left|\frac ab\right| = \frac{|a|}{|b|}, $$ but not over addition and subtraction.
  • Terminological Contrast: Absolute value rules collectively describe the algebraic behavior of absolute value. Absolute value equations concern equality conditions, while absolute value inequalities translate distance restrictions into intervals or unions of intervals.

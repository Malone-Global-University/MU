FOIL Method

Subfield: Algebraic & Analytic Structures

Etymology & Roots

The FOIL Method is a mnemonic for multiplying two binomials. The letters in FOIL stand for:

  • F — First
  • O — Outer
  • I — Inner
  • L — Last

These labels describe the four pairwise products formed when multiplying two binomials such as:

$$ (a+b)(c+d). $$

The FOIL Method is not an independent algebraic law. It is a specialized way of organizing repeated use of the distributive property.

Thus the underlying mathematics is:

$$ (a+b)(c+d) = a(c+d)+b(c+d), $$

followed by:

$$ ac+ad+bc+bd. $$

FOIL simply gives memorable names to these four products.

Operational Nuance

Consider:

$$ (a+b)(c+d). $$

Applying FOIL gives:

First:

$$ a\cdot c=ac. $$

Outer:

$$ a\cdot d=ad. $$

Inner:

$$ b\cdot c=bc. $$

Last:

$$ b\cdot d=bd. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ (a+b)(c+d)=ac+ad+bc+bd }. $$

Example

Multiply:

$$ (x+3)(x+5). $$

First:

$$ x\cdot x=x^2. $$

Outer:

$$ x\cdot5=5x. $$

Inner:

$$ 3\cdot x=3x. $$

Last:

$$ 3\cdot5=15. $$

Combine like terms:

$$ x^2+5x+3x+15 $$ $$ =x^2+8x+15. $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ (x+3)(x+5)=x^2+8x+15 }. $$

Signs Matter

Consider:

$$ (x-4)(x+2). $$

FOIL gives:

$$ x^2+2x-4x-8. $$

Combining like terms:

$$ \boxed{ x^2-2x-8 }. $$

The negative sign belongs to the term $-4$, so every multiplication involving that term must preserve its sign.

Common Misconception: FOIL does not mean "multiply only adjacent terms." Every term in the first binomial must multiply every term in the second. The labels merely organize those four products.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$(a+b)(c+d)$ "The quantity a plus b times the quantity c plus d."
$ac$ "The product of the first terms."
$ad$ "The product of the outer terms."
$bc$ "The product of the inner terms."
$bd$ "The product of the last terms."
$ac+ad+bc+bd$ "The sum of the four distributive products."

The words outer and inner depend on how the binomials are written visually. In:

$$ (a+b)(c+d), $$

the outer terms are $a$ and $d$, while the inner terms are $b$ and $c$.

Because these labels depend on layout rather than algebraic structure, they are best understood as a memory aid rather than as formal terminology.

Defense Notation Advice: Parentheses are essential. Writing:

$$ (x+2)(x-7) $$

makes clear that two entire binomials are being multiplied. Omitting parentheses can completely change the expression.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Binomial Multiplication: FOIL is designed specifically for: $$ (\text{two terms})(\text{two terms}). $$ It produces exactly four initial products before like terms are combined.
  • Distributive Property: The real algebraic principle is: $$ a(b+c)=ab+ac. $$ FOIL is repeated distribution applied to two binomials.
  • General Form: $$ \boxed{ (a+b)(c+d)=ac+ad+bc+bd }. $$
  • Like Terms: FOIL may produce terms that must be combined. For example: $$ (x+4)(x+7) = x^2+7x+4x+28 $$ becomes: $$ x^2+11x+28. $$
  • Difference of Squares: Applying FOIL to: $$ (a+b)(a-b) $$ gives: $$ a^2-ab+ab-b^2. $$ The middle terms cancel, producing: $$ \boxed{ a^2-b^2 }. $$
  • Square of a Binomial: Applying FOIL to: $$ (a+b)^2=(a+b)(a+b) $$ gives: $$ \boxed{ a^2+2ab+b^2 }. $$
  • Square of a Difference: $$ (a-b)^2 = (a-b)(a-b) $$ gives: $$ \boxed{ a^2-2ab+b^2 }. $$
  • Not a General Polynomial Rule: FOIL is poorly suited to expressions such as: $$ (x+2)(x^2+3x+4) $$ because the second factor has three terms. Ordinary distribution is the more general method.
  • General Distribution: For: $$ (a+b)(c+d+e), $$ distribute both first-factor terms across all three second-factor terms: $$ ac+ad+ae+bc+bd+be. $$ There is no useful FOIL label for six products.
  • Box Method: Polynomial multiplication may also be organized using a rectangular grid or box. For two binomials, the four cells correspond exactly to the four FOIL products.
  • Area Model: If a rectangle has side lengths: $$ a+b $$ and: $$ c+d, $$ subdividing it produces four smaller rectangles with areas: $$ ac,\ ad,\ bc,\ bd. $$ Thus: $$ (a+b)(c+d)=ac+ad+bc+bd. $$
  • Factoring as Reverse FOIL: Factoring a quadratic such as: $$ x^2+8x+15 $$ asks for binomials whose FOIL expansion reproduces the polynomial: $$ \boxed{ x^2+8x+15=(x+3)(x+5) }. $$
  • Leading Coefficients: FOIL also works when neither leading coefficient is $1$: $$ (2x+3)(4x-5). $$ Expanding: $$ 8x^2-10x+12x-15 $$ gives: $$ \boxed{ 8x^2+2x-15 }. $$
  • Variables in Both Terms: FOIL is not restricted to expressions involving $x$ and constants. For example: $$ (2a+3b)(a-4b) $$ expands to: $$ 2a^2-8ab+3ab-12b^2 $$ and therefore: $$ \boxed{ 2a^2-5ab-12b^2 }. $$
  • Noncommutative Caution: In ordinary elementary algebra, multiplication of numbers and polynomial coefficients is commutative. In more advanced algebraic systems where multiplication may not commute, the order of factors such as $ad$ and $da$ can matter. The distributive law remains the deeper principle.
  • Operator Precedence: Parenthetical factors are treated as complete quantities. Multiplication is distributed across the sums or differences inside each factor before like terms are collected.
  • Efficiency: FOIL is useful for beginners because it provides a checklist ensuring that all four products are included. With experience, many algebraists simply distribute each term without explicitly naming the four positions.
  • Pedagogical Limitation: Overreliance on FOIL can obscure the broader distributive structure of polynomial multiplication. Students should understand that: $$ (\text{sum})(\text{sum}) $$ is handled by multiplying every term in one factor by every term in the other.
  • Terminological Contrast: The FOIL Method is a mnemonic for multiplying two binomials. The distributive property is the general algebraic law that justifies the method. The box method and area model are alternative organizational approaches to the same multiplication.

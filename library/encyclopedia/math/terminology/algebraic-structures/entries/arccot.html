arccot

Subfield: Algebraic & Analytic Structures

Etymology & Roots

arccot, read arc cotangent, is the inverse function of cotangent after cotangent has been restricted to an interval on which it is one-to-one.

In functional language, arccot answers:

$$ \boxed{ \text{What principal angle has cotangent }x? }. $$

Under one widely used convention:

$$ \boxed{ \theta=\operatorname{arccot}(x) \iff \cot\theta=x, \qquad 0<\theta<\pi }. $$

Another notation is:

$$ \boxed{ \cot^{-1}(x) }. $$

Here the exponent $-1$ means inverse function, not reciprocal. This distinction matters because:

$$ \boxed{ (\cot x)^{-1} = \frac{1}{\cot x} = \tan x }. $$

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{arccot}x \ne \tan x }. $$

Operational Nuance

Cotangent

Cotangent is defined by:

$$ \boxed{ \cot\theta = \frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} }. $$

In a right triangle:

$$ \boxed{ \cot\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}} {\text{opposite}} }. $$

Periodicity

Cotangent repeats every $\pi$ radians:

$$ \boxed{ \cot(\theta+\pi)=\cot\theta }. $$

Because of this periodicity, cotangent cannot have a single-valued inverse on its entire domain without first restricting the angle.

Common Principal Branch

A common restriction is:

$$ \boxed{ 0<\theta<\pi }. $$

On this interval, cotangent decreases continuously from $+\infty$ to $-\infty$ and takes every real value exactly once.

Domain

Under this convention:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Dom}(\operatorname{arccot}) = \mathbb{R} }. $$

Range

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(\operatorname{arccot}) = (0,\pi) }. $$

In degrees:

$$ \boxed{ (0^\circ,180^\circ) }. $$

Branch Convention Is Not Universal

The principal range of arccot is less standardized than the principal ranges of arcsin, arccos, and arctan. Some authors use different intervals, particularly conventions related to:

$$ \left(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right). $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \text{always check the stated branch of arccot} }. $$

Common Values Under the (0,π) Convention

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{arccot}(\sqrt3) = \frac{\pi}{6} }. $$
$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{arccot}(1) = \frac{\pi}{4} }. $$
$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{arccot}(0) = \frac{\pi}{2} }. $$
$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{arccot}(-1) = \frac{3\pi}{4} }. $$
$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{arccot}(-\sqrt3) = \frac{5\pi}{6} }. $$

Relationship with arctan

Under the common convention:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{arccot}x = \frac{\pi}{2} - \arctan x }. $$

This identity is especially useful because arctan is more commonly available on calculators and software.

Relationship with arctan(1/x)

Because:

$$ \cot\theta=\frac1{\tan\theta}, $$

arccot can also be related to $\arctan(1/x)$. However, the correct formula depends on the sign of $x$ because of principal-value restrictions.

$$ \operatorname{arccot}x= \begin{cases} \arctan(1/x), & x>0,\\[4pt] \frac{\pi}{2}, & x=0,\\[4pt] \pi+\arctan(1/x), & x<0. \end{cases} $$

Right-Triangle Interpretation

For an acute angle $\theta$ in a right triangle:

$$ \cot\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}} {\text{opposite}}. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \theta = \operatorname{arccot} \left( \frac{\text{adjacent}} {\text{opposite}} \right) }. $$

Inverse Composition

For every real $x$:

$$ \boxed{ \cot(\operatorname{arccot}x)=x }. $$

Reverse Composition

The identity:

$$ \operatorname{arccot}(\cot\theta)=\theta $$

is valid directly only when $\theta$ lies in the selected principal range.

Monotonicity

Under the $(0,\pi)$ branch:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{arccot}x \text{ is strictly decreasing} }. $$

Larger cotangent values correspond to smaller principal angles.

Limits

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to\infty} \operatorname{arccot}x = 0 }. $$
$$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to-\infty} \operatorname{arccot}x = \pi }. $$

Derivative

For the standard decreasing branch:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx} \operatorname{arccot}x = -\frac1{1+x^2} }. $$

Derivation

Set:

$$ y=\operatorname{arccot}x. $$

Then:

$$ \cot y=x. $$

Differentiate implicitly:

$$ -\csc^2y\frac{dy}{dx}=1. $$

Hence:

$$ \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac1{\csc^2y}. $$

Using:

$$ \csc^2y=1+\cot^2y=1+x^2, $$

gives:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac1{1+x^2} }. $$

Chain Rule

For differentiable $u(x)$:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx} \operatorname{arccot}(u(x)) = -\frac{u'(x)} {1+u(x)^2} }. $$

Antiderivative

$$ \boxed{ \int \operatorname{arccot}x\,dx = x\operatorname{arccot}x + \frac12\ln(1+x^2) + C }. $$

Solving Cotangent Equations

If:

$$ \cot\theta=c, $$

define the principal solution:

$$ \alpha=\operatorname{arccot}c. $$

Since cotangent has period $\pi$, the complete solution is:

$$ \boxed{ \theta = \operatorname{arccot}c+k\pi, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$

Graph

Under the $(0,\pi)$ convention, the graph of:

$$ y=\operatorname{arccot}x $$

is continuous and strictly decreasing on all real numbers. It passes through:

$$ \boxed{ \left(0,\frac{\pi}{2}\right) }. $$

Its horizontal limiting values are $0$ and $\pi$, neither of which is attained.

Common Misconception: Two sources can give different numerical values for arccot while both are internally consistent if they use different principal-value branches. Branch choice is part of the definition.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\operatorname{arccot}x$ "Arc cotangent of x."
$\cot^{-1}x$ "Inverse cotangent of x."
$\operatorname{arccot}(1)=\frac{\pi}{4}$ "The arc cotangent of one is pi over four."
$\operatorname{arccot}(0)=\frac{\pi}{2}$ "The arc cotangent of zero is pi over two," under the common zero-to-pi branch.
$\cot(\operatorname{arccot}x)=x$ "Cotangent of arc cotangent x equals x."
$\frac{d}{dx}\operatorname{arccot}x$ "The derivative of arc cotangent x."

The notation $\cot^{-1}x$ should be distinguished from:

$$ (\cot x)^{-1}. $$

As an inverse-function notation:

$$ \boxed{ \cot^{-1}(x) = \operatorname{arccot}(x) }. $$

As an ordinary reciprocal:

$$ \boxed{ (\cot x)^{-1} = \tan x }. $$

Defense Notation Advice: Prefer the explicit form $\operatorname{arccot}$ whenever reciprocal notation might be confused with inverse functions, and state the principal branch when the value of arccot matters.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Definition: Arccot is a branch of the inverse cotangent relation that returns one principal angle for each real cotangent value.
  • Common Notation: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{arccot}x }. $$
  • Alternative Notation: $$ \boxed{ \cot^{-1}x }. $$
  • Domain: $$ \boxed{ \mathbb{R} }. $$
  • Common Principal Range: $$ \boxed{ (0,\pi) }. $$
  • Range in Degrees: $$ \boxed{ (0^\circ,180^\circ) }. $$
  • Branch Warning: Other principal ranges exist in the literature.
  • Standard Restricted Cotangent: $$ \cot:(0,\pi)\to\mathbb{R} $$ is one-to-one and onto.
  • Special Value: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{arccot}(\sqrt3) = \frac{\pi}{6} }. $$
  • Special Value: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{arccot}(1) = \frac{\pi}{4} }. $$
  • Zero Value: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{arccot}(0) = \frac{\pi}{2} } $$ under the $(0,\pi)$ branch.
  • Negative Value: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{arccot}(-1) = \frac{3\pi}{4} }. $$
  • Inverse Composition: $$ \boxed{ \cot(\operatorname{arccot}x)=x }. $$
  • Restricted Reverse Composition: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{arccot}(\cot\theta)=\theta } $$ when $\theta$ is in the selected principal range.
  • arctan Identity: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{arccot}x = \frac{\pi}{2} - \arctan x } $$ under the common branch convention.
  • Monotonicity: Arccot is decreasing under the $(0,\pi)$ convention.
  • Derivative: $$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}\operatorname{arccot}x = -\frac1{1+x^2} }. $$
  • Chain Rule: $$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}\operatorname{arccot}(u) = -\frac{u'}{1+u^2} }. $$
  • Antiderivative: $$ \boxed{ \int\operatorname{arccot}x\,dx = x\operatorname{arccot}x + \frac12\ln(1+x^2) + C }. $$
  • Right-Triangle Interpretation: $$ \boxed{ \theta = \operatorname{arccot} \left( \frac{\text{adjacent}} {\text{opposite}} \right) }. $$
  • Period of Cotangent: $$ \boxed{ \pi }. $$
  • General Equation Solution: If: $$ \cot\theta=c, $$ then: $$ \boxed{ \theta = \operatorname{arccot}c+k\pi, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$
  • Graph: Under the common branch, the graph runs continuously downward from values near $\pi$ to values near $0$.
  • Positive-Infinity Limit: $$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to\infty} \operatorname{arccot}x=0 }. $$
  • Negative-Infinity Limit: $$ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to-\infty} \operatorname{arccot}x=\pi }. $$
  • Reciprocal of Cotangent: $$ \boxed{ \frac1{\cot x}=\tan x }. $$
  • Inverse vs. Reciprocal: Arccot reverses cotangent on a restricted branch; tangent reciprocates cotangent.
  • Calculator Caution: Many standard calculators do not provide a dedicated arccot key.
  • Calculator Conversion: Under the $(0,\pi)$ convention: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{arccot}x = \frac{\pi}{2}-\arctan x }. $$
  • Software Variation: Computer algebra systems may use different arccot branch definitions.
  • Angle Mode: Numerical calculator output may be expressed in degrees or radians depending on the selected mode.
  • Calculus Convention: The standard derivative formula assumes radian measure.
  • Complex Extension: In complex analysis, inverse cotangent is defined through logarithmic formulas together with branch choices.
  • Terminological Contrast: Cotangent maps an angle to $\cos\theta/\sin\theta$. Arccot maps a cotangent value to a selected principal angle. Tangent is the reciprocal of cotangent, not its inverse function.

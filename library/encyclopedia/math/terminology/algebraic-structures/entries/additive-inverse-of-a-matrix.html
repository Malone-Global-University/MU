Additive Inverse of a Matrix

Subfield: Algebraic & Analytic Structures

Etymology & Roots

The additive inverse of a matrix is the matrix that cancels the original matrix under matrix addition. If the original matrix is $A$, its additive inverse is written:

$$ \boxed{-A}. $$

It is characterized by:

$$ \boxed{ A+(-A)=0 }, $$

where $0$ denotes the zero matrix having the same dimensions as $A$.

The word inverse indicates an operation that reverses or cancels another operation. The qualifier additive specifies that the relevant operation is addition. This distinction matters because matrices can also have multiplicative inverses, which are entirely different objects.

The notation $-A$ is inherited from ordinary arithmetic. Just as:

$$ a+(-a)=0, $$

matrix addition satisfies:

$$ A+(-A)=0. $$

Matrix additive inverses therefore extend the familiar idea of opposite numbers into matrix algebra.

Operational Nuance

Entrywise Definition

If:

$$ A=[a_{ij}], $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ -A=[-a_{ij}] }. $$

Every entry is replaced by its ordinary numerical additive inverse.

Example

Let:

$$ A= \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -5\\ 3 & 7 \end{pmatrix}. $$

Then:

$$ \boxed{ -A= \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 5\\ -3 & -7 \end{pmatrix} }. $$

Adding the two matrices gives:

$$ A+(-A) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -5\\ 3 & 7 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 5\\ -3 & -7 \end{pmatrix} $$ $$ = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0\\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}. $$

Scalar Multiplication Interpretation

Negating a matrix is equivalent to multiplying the entire matrix by the scalar $-1$:

$$ \boxed{ -A=(-1)A }. $$

Thus:

$$ (-1) \begin{pmatrix} a & b\\ c & d \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -a & -b\\ -c & -d \end{pmatrix}. $$

The Zero Matrix

The additive identity for matrix addition is the zero matrix. For an $m\times n$ matrix:

$$ 0_{m\times n} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \cdots & 0\\ \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\ 0 & \cdots & 0 \end{pmatrix}. $$

It satisfies:

$$ \boxed{ A+0=A }. $$

The additive inverse of $A$ is exactly the matrix needed to produce this additive identity.

Matrix Subtraction

Matrix subtraction is defined through additive inverses:

$$ \boxed{ A-B=A+(-B) }. $$

Therefore subtraction is not a fundamentally separate matrix operation. It can be understood as addition of an additive inverse.

Dimension Requirement

Matrix addition is defined only for matrices of the same dimensions. Consequently, if:

$$ A\in M_{m\times n}, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ -A\in M_{m\times n} }. $$

The additive inverse always has exactly the same number of rows and columns as the original matrix.

Uniqueness

Every matrix has exactly one additive inverse.

Suppose both $B$ and $C$ satisfy:

$$ A+B=0 $$

and:

$$ A+C=0. $$

Then:

$$ B = B+0 = B+(A+C) $$ $$ = (B+A)+C = 0+C = C. $$

Therefore the additive inverse is unique.

Not the Multiplicative Inverse

The notation:

$$ -A $$

means the additive inverse, whereas:

$$ A^{-1} $$

denotes a multiplicative inverse when one exists.

Their defining equations are different:

$$ \boxed{ A+(-A)=0 } $$

versus:

$$ \boxed{ AA^{-1}=A^{-1}A=I }. $$

Every matrix has an additive inverse, but only invertible square matrices have a multiplicative inverse.

Common Misconception: $-A$ does not mean $A^{-1}$. Negating a matrix changes the signs of its entries. Inverting a matrix, when possible, produces a matrix that multiplies with $A$ to give the identity matrix.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$-A$ "Negative A," or "the additive inverse of A."
$A+(-A)=0$ "A plus negative A equals the zero matrix."
$-A=(-1)A$ "Negative A equals negative one times A."
$A-B=A+(-B)$ "A minus B equals A plus the additive inverse of B."
$-(-A)=A$ "The additive inverse of negative A is A."
$A^{-1}$ "A inverse," meaning the multiplicative inverse when it exists.

The minus sign placed before a matrix applies to the entire matrix:

$$ - \begin{pmatrix} a & b\\ c & d \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -a & -b\\ -c & -d \end{pmatrix}. $$

This is conceptually the same as distributing a factor of $-1$ across all entries.

Defense Notation Advice: Keep the notation:

$$ -A $$

distinct from:

$$ A^{-1}. $$

The first concerns addition; the second concerns multiplication.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Definition: The additive inverse of $A$ is the matrix $-A$ satisfying: $$ \boxed{ A+(-A)=0 }. $$
  • Entrywise Negation: If: $$ A=[a_{ij}], $$ then: $$ \boxed{ -A=[-a_{ij}] }. $$
  • Scalar Multiplication: $$ \boxed{ -A=(-1)A }. $$
  • Same Dimensions: If $A$ is $m\times n$, then $-A$ is also $m\times n$.
  • Existence: Every matrix over the real numbers, complex numbers, or any field has an additive inverse.
  • Uniqueness: Each matrix has exactly one additive inverse.
  • Zero Matrix: The zero matrix is its own additive inverse: $$ \boxed{ -0=0 }. $$
  • Double Negation: $$ \boxed{ -(-A)=A }. $$
  • Cancellation: $$ \boxed{ A+(-A)=(-A)+A=0 }. $$
  • Commutativity: Because matrix addition is commutative: $$ A+(-A)=(-A)+A. $$
  • Matrix Subtraction: $$ \boxed{ A-B=A+(-B) }. $$
  • Negative of a Sum: $$ \boxed{ -(A+B)=(-A)+(-B) }. $$
  • Negative of a Difference: $$ \boxed{ -(A-B)=B-A }. $$
  • Scalar Compatibility: For a scalar $c$: $$ \boxed{ -(cA)=(-c)A=c(-A) }. $$
  • Additive Cancellation Law: If: $$ A+B=A+C, $$ then: $$ \boxed{ B=C }. $$ This follows by adding $-A$ to both sides.
  • Solving Matrix Equations: If: $$ X+A=B, $$ add $-A$ to both sides: $$ X+A+(-A)=B+(-A), $$ giving: $$ \boxed{ X=B-A }. $$
  • Linear Algebra Structure: The collection of all $m\times n$ matrices over a field forms an abelian group under matrix addition. The existence of $-A$ is one of the group axioms.
  • Vector Space Structure: Matrix spaces such as: $$ M_{m\times n}(\mathbb{R}) $$ are vector spaces. Additive inverses arise automatically from the vector-space axioms.
  • Matrix as a Vector: Since an $m\times n$ matrix can be regarded as an element of a vector space of dimension $mn$, its additive inverse plays the same role as the opposite of a vector.
  • Transformation Interpretation: If $A$ represents a linear transformation, then $-A$ represents the transformation: $$ \boxed{ \mathbf{x}\mapsto -A\mathbf{x} }, $$ which reverses the output vector of $A$ through multiplication by $-1$.
  • Transpose: Negation commutes with transpose: $$ \boxed{ (-A)^T=-(A^T) }. $$
  • Complex Conjugation: For complex matrices: $$ \boxed{ \overline{-A} = -\overline{A} }. $$
  • Conjugate Transpose: $$ \boxed{ (-A)^* = -(A^*) }. $$
  • Determinant Effect: If $A$ is an $n\times n$ matrix: $$ \boxed{ \det(-A)=(-1)^n\det(A) }. $$ Negating a matrix therefore preserves the determinant when $n$ is even and reverses its sign when $n$ is odd.
  • Trace: For a square matrix: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{tr}(-A) = -\operatorname{tr}(A) }. $$
  • Rank: Multiplication by the nonzero scalar $-1$ does not change rank: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{rank}(-A) = \operatorname{rank}(A) }. $$
  • Null Space: Since: $$ (-A)\mathbf{x}=0 \iff A\mathbf{x}=0, $$ one has: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Nul}(-A) = \operatorname{Nul}(A) }. $$
  • Eigenvalues: If: $$ A\mathbf{v}=\lambda\mathbf{v}, $$ then: $$ (-A)\mathbf{v} = -\lambda\mathbf{v}. $$ Thus the eigenvalues of $-A$ are the negatives of the eigenvalues of $A$.
  • Invertibility: If $A$ is multiplicatively invertible, then $-A$ is also invertible and: $$ \boxed{ (-A)^{-1} = -A^{-1} }. $$
  • Rectangular Matrices: Rectangular matrices always have additive inverses even though ordinary two-sided multiplicative inverses are defined only for suitable square matrices.
  • Not Entrywise Reciprocal: The additive inverse: $$ -A $$ is not formed by replacing each nonzero entry $a_{ij}$ with: $$ \frac{1}{a_{ij}}. $$ It is formed by replacing $a_{ij}$ with $-a_{ij}$.
  • Not a Matrix Inverse: $$ \boxed{ -A\ne A^{-1} } $$ in general. The two symbols refer to different inverse operations.
  • Terminological Contrast: The additive inverse $-A$ cancels $A$ under addition and exists for every matrix. The multiplicative inverse $A^{-1}$ cancels $A$ under matrix multiplication to produce the identity matrix and exists only when $A$ is an invertible square matrix.

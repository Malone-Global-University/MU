arccos

Subfield: Algebraic & Analytic Structures

Etymology & Roots

arccos, read arc cosine, names the inverse of the cosine function after cosine has been restricted to an interval on which it is one-to-one. It answers the question:

$$ \boxed{ \text{What principal angle has cosine }x? } $$

Symbolically:

$$ \boxed{ \theta=\arccos(x) \iff \cos\theta=x, \qquad 0\le\theta\le\pi }. $$

The prefix arc reflects the connection between trigonometric values and angles or arcs on the unit circle. In modern mathematics, however, $\arccos$ is best understood as a function: it maps a cosine value back to its principal angle.

Another common notation is:

$$ \boxed{ \cos^{-1}(x) }. $$

In this context the superscript $-1$ means inverse function, not reciprocal.

$$ \boxed{ \arccos x = \cos^{-1}x \ne \frac{1}{\cos x} }. $$

The reciprocal of cosine is the secant function:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{1}{\cos x}=\sec x }. $$

Operational Nuance

Why Cosine Must Be Restricted

The cosine function is not one-to-one on all real numbers because it repeats:

$$ \cos(\theta+2\pi)=\cos\theta. $$

Therefore it cannot have an inverse function on its entire real domain.

To define $\arccos$, cosine is restricted to:

$$ \boxed{ 0\le\theta\le\pi }. $$

On this interval cosine decreases continuously from $1$ to $-1$ and is one-to-one.

Domain

Since real cosine values always lie between $-1$ and $1$, the real domain of $\arccos$ is:

$$ \boxed{ -1\le x\le1 }. $$

In interval notation:

$$ \boxed{ [-1,1] }. $$

Range

The principal-value range is:

$$ \boxed{ [0,\pi] }. $$

In degrees:

$$ \boxed{ [0^\circ,180^\circ] }. $$

Function Mapping

The real inverse cosine function may therefore be written:

$$ \boxed{ \arccos: [-1,1] \to [0,\pi] }. $$

Important Values

$$ \boxed{ \arccos(1)=0 } $$
$$ \boxed{ \arccos\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right) = \frac{\pi}{6} } $$
$$ \boxed{ \arccos\left(\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\right) = \frac{\pi}{4} } $$
$$ \boxed{ \arccos\left(\frac12\right) = \frac{\pi}{3} } $$
$$ \boxed{ \arccos(0)=\frac{\pi}{2} } $$
$$ \boxed{ \arccos(-1)=\pi }. $$

Unit-Circle Interpretation

On the unit circle, a point corresponding to angle $\theta$ has coordinates:

$$ (\cos\theta,\sin\theta). $$

Therefore $\arccos(x)$ asks for the principal angle in the upper half of the unit circle whose horizontal coordinate is $x$.

Right-Triangle Interpretation

In a right triangle:

$$ \cos\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}. $$

Thus, when the ratio is known:

$$ \boxed{ \theta = \arccos \left( \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \right) }. $$

Example

If a right triangle has adjacent side $6$ and hypotenuse $10$, then:

$$ \cos\theta=\frac{6}{10}=0.6. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \theta=\arccos(0.6) }. $$

Inverse Composition

For every $x$ in the domain of $\arccos$:

$$ \boxed{ \cos(\arccos x)=x, \qquad -1\le x\le1 }. $$

This composition returns the original cosine value exactly.

The Reverse Composition Requires Care

It is tempting to write:

$$ \arccos(\cos\theta)=\theta $$

for all $\theta$, but this is false.

The equality holds directly only when:

$$ \boxed{ 0\le\theta\le\pi }. $$

Outside that interval, $\arccos$ returns the unique coterminal or reflected principal angle lying in $[0,\pi]$ with the same cosine.

Example of Principal-Value Reduction

Since:

$$ \cos\left(\frac{5\pi}{3}\right)=\frac12, $$

we have:

$$ \boxed{ \arccos \left( \cos\frac{5\pi}{3} \right) = \frac{\pi}{3} } $$

rather than $5\pi/3$, because $\pi/3$ lies in the principal range $[0,\pi]$.

Graph

The graph:

$$ y=\arccos x $$

is the reflection of:

$$ y=\cos x, \qquad 0\le x\le\pi, $$

across the line:

$$ y=x. $$

Monotonicity

Because cosine is decreasing on $[0,\pi]$, its inverse is also decreasing:

$$ \boxed{ \arccos x \text{ is strictly decreasing on }[-1,1] }. $$

Derivative

For:

$$ -1

the derivative is:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}\arccos x = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} }. $$

The negative sign agrees with the fact that $\arccos x$ is decreasing.

Derivative by Implicit Differentiation

Let:

$$ y=\arccos x. $$

Then:

$$ \cos y=x. $$

Differentiating:

$$ -\sin y\frac{dy}{dx}=1. $$

Therefore:

$$ \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac1{\sin y}. $$

Since $y\in[0,\pi]$, $\sin y\ge0$, and:

$$ \sin y = \sqrt{1-\cos^2y} = \sqrt{1-x^2}. $$

Hence:

$$ \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac1{\sqrt{1-x^2}}. $$

Chain Rule

For a differentiable function $u(x)$ satisfying $|u(x)|<1$:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}\arccos(u) = -\frac{u'}{\sqrt{1-u^2}} }. $$

Integral

An antiderivative of $\arccos x$ is:

$$ \boxed{ \int\arccos x\,dx = x\arccos x - \sqrt{1-x^2} + C }. $$

Relationship with arcsin

For $x\in[-1,1]$:

$$ \boxed{ \arccos x + \arcsin x = \frac{\pi}{2} }. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \arccos x = \frac{\pi}{2} - \arcsin x }. $$

Symmetry Identity

Another useful identity is:

$$ \boxed{ \arccos(-x) = \pi-\arccos x }. $$

Solving Cosine Equations

If:

$$ \cos\theta=c, \qquad -1\le c\le1, $$

then the principal angle is:

$$ \alpha=\arccos c. $$

The complete real solution is:

$$ \boxed{ \theta = 2\pi k\pm\alpha, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$

Thus $\arccos$ supplies a principal solution, not automatically every solution of a periodic cosine equation.

Law of Cosines

The inverse cosine frequently appears when recovering an angle from side lengths. From:

$$ c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C, $$

we obtain:

$$ \boxed{ C= \arccos \left( \frac{a^2+b^2-c^2}{2ab} \right) }. $$

Dot-Product Angle Formula

For nonzero Euclidean vectors $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$:

$$ \cos\theta = \frac{ \mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} }{ \|\mathbf{u}\|\|\mathbf{v}\| }. $$

Therefore the angle between them is:

$$ \boxed{ \theta = \arccos \left( \frac{ \mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} }{ \|\mathbf{u}\|\|\mathbf{v}\| } \right) }. $$

This convention returns an angle in $[0,\pi]$, exactly matching the principal range of $\arccos$.

Common Misconception: $\arccos(x)$ does not mean $1/\cos(x)$. The notation $\cos^{-1}(x)$ means inverse cosine when used as a function name, while the reciprocal of cosine is $\sec(x)$.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\arccos x$ "Arc cosine of x."
$\cos^{-1}x$ "Inverse cosine of x."
$\arccos(0)=\frac{\pi}{2}$ "The arc cosine of zero is pi over two."
$\theta=\arccos x$ "Theta equals the inverse cosine of x."
$\cos(\arccos x)=x$ "Cosine of arc cosine x equals x."
$\frac{d}{dx}\arccos x$ "The derivative of arc cosine x."

The notation:

$$ \cos^{-1}x $$

can cause confusion because negative exponents ordinarily indicate reciprocals. Function notation uses the same superscript to indicate inverse functions.

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \cos^{-1}x = \arccos x } $$

while:

$$ \boxed{ (\cos x)^{-1} = \frac1{\cos x} = \sec x }. $$

Defense Notation Advice: Writing $\arccos$ instead of $\cos^{-1}$ often eliminates reciprocal ambiguity, especially when inverse functions and negative exponents appear in the same expression.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Definition: $\arccos(x)$ returns the principal angle whose cosine is $x$.
  • Equivalent Notation: $$ \boxed{ \arccos x=\cos^{-1}x }. $$
  • Real Domain: $$ \boxed{ [-1,1] }. $$
  • Principal Range: $$ \boxed{ [0,\pi] }. $$
  • Range in Degrees: $$ \boxed{ [0^\circ,180^\circ] }. $$
  • Function Mapping: $$ \boxed{ \arccos:[-1,1]\to[0,\pi] }. $$
  • Inverse Restriction: Cosine is restricted to $[0,\pi]$ so that it becomes one-to-one.
  • Endpoint Value: $$ \boxed{ \arccos(1)=0 }. $$
  • Right-Angle Value: $$ \boxed{ \arccos(0)=\frac{\pi}{2} }. $$
  • Opposite Endpoint: $$ \boxed{ \arccos(-1)=\pi }. $$
  • Special Value: $$ \boxed{ \arccos\left(\frac12\right)=\frac{\pi}{3} }. $$
  • Special Value: $$ \boxed{ \arccos\left(\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\right)=\frac{\pi}{4} }. $$
  • Special Value: $$ \boxed{ \arccos\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)=\frac{\pi}{6} }. $$
  • Inverse Identity: $$ \boxed{ \cos(\arccos x)=x } $$ for $x\in[-1,1]$.
  • Restricted Reverse Identity: $$ \boxed{ \arccos(\cos\theta)=\theta } $$ when $\theta\in[0,\pi]$.
  • Principal-Value Caution: Outside $[0,\pi]$, $\arccos(\cos\theta)$ returns the corresponding principal angle rather than necessarily returning $\theta$ itself.
  • Monotonicity: $\arccos x$ is strictly decreasing on its domain.
  • Continuity: $\arccos x$ is continuous on: $$ \boxed{ [-1,1] }. $$
  • Differentiability: It is differentiable on: $$ \boxed{ (-1,1) }. $$
  • Derivative: $$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}\arccos x = -\frac1{\sqrt{1-x^2}} }. $$
  • Chain Rule: $$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}\arccos(u(x)) = -\frac{u'(x)}{\sqrt{1-u(x)^2}} }. $$
  • Antiderivative: $$ \boxed{ \int\arccos x\,dx = x\arccos x-\sqrt{1-x^2}+C }. $$
  • arcsin Relationship: $$ \boxed{ \arccos x+\arcsin x=\frac{\pi}{2} }. $$
  • Complement Identity: $$ \boxed{ \arccos x = \frac{\pi}{2}-\arcsin x }. $$
  • Negative Argument: $$ \boxed{ \arccos(-x) = \pi-\arccos x }. $$
  • Right-Triangle Use: $$ \boxed{ \theta = \arccos \left( \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \right) }. $$
  • Law of Cosines: $$ \boxed{ C= \arccos \left( \frac{a^2+b^2-c^2}{2ab} \right) }. $$
  • Vector Angle: $$ \boxed{ \theta = \arccos \left( \frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}} {\|\mathbf{u}\|\|\mathbf{v}\|} \right) }. $$
  • Unit-Circle Meaning: $\arccos(x)$ identifies the principal angle whose unit-circle $x$-coordinate is $x$.
  • Graphical Relationship: The graph of $y=\arccos x$ is the reflection of the restricted graph $y=\cos x$, $0\le x\le\pi$, across $y=x$.
  • Solving a Cosine Equation: If: $$ \cos\theta=c, $$ first set: $$ \alpha=\arccos c. $$
  • General Cosine Solution: $$ \boxed{ \theta = 2\pi k\pm\arccos c, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z} }. $$
  • Calculator Mode: A calculator may return the arccos result in degrees or radians depending on its angle-mode setting.
  • Radians in Calculus: Derivative formulas for trigonometric and inverse trigonometric functions assume radian measure.
  • Domain Error: A real calculator ordinarily cannot evaluate $\arccos x$ as a real number when: $$ |x|>1. $$
  • Complex Extension: In complex analysis, $\arccos z$ can be extended beyond the real interval $[-1,1]$ using complex logarithms and square roots.
  • Complex Multivaluedness: Before a branch is chosen, complex inverse trigonometric relations are naturally multivalued because cosine is periodic.
  • Principal Complex Branch: Advanced texts choose branch conventions to define a single-valued principal complex arccosine.
  • Reciprocal Contrast: $$ \boxed{ \arccos x \ne \sec x }. $$
  • Inverse vs. Reciprocal: $\arccos$ reverses cosine on its restricted range; secant reciprocates cosine.
  • Terminological Contrast: Cosine maps an angle to a ratio or unit-circle coordinate. Arccos maps a permissible cosine value back to its principal angle. Secant is the reciprocal of cosine. The notation $\cos^{-1}$ means inverse cosine when used as a function name, not reciprocal cosine.

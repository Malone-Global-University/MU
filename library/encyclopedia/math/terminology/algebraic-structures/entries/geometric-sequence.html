Geometric Sequence

Subfield: Algebraic & Analytic Structures

Etymology & Roots

A geometric sequence is an ordered sequence of numbers in which each term after the first is obtained by multiplying the preceding term by the same constant factor.

That constant factor is called the common ratio. The word sequence emphasizes that the numbers occur in an ordered arrangement, while geometric reflects older mathematical language involving ratios, proportions, and geometric means.

The expressions geometric sequence and geometric progression ordinarily refer to the same mathematical structure. Contemporary algebra and precalculus texts commonly use sequence, while progression remains common in international and historical mathematical terminology.

A geometric sequence is distinguished from an arithmetic sequence by the kind of change occurring between terms. Arithmetic sequences use a constant difference; geometric sequences use a constant ratio.

Operational Nuance

A sequence:

$$ a_1,a_2,a_3,\ldots $$

is geometric if there exists a constant $r$ such that:

$$ \boxed{ a_{n+1}=ra_n } $$

for every applicable index $n$.

When:

$$ a_n\neq0, $$

this relationship can also be written:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=r }. $$

The constant $r$ is the common ratio.

For example:

$$ 4,\ 12,\ 36,\ 108,\ldots $$

is geometric because:

$$ \frac{12}{4}=3, $$ $$ \frac{36}{12}=3, $$ $$ \frac{108}{36}=3. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{r=3}. $$

Beginning with the first term, the pattern is:

$$ a_1,\quad a_1r,\quad a_1r^2,\quad a_1r^3,\quad\ldots $$

Thus the explicit nth-term formula is:

$$ \boxed{ a_n=a_1r^{n-1} }. $$

For the sequence:

$$ 4,\ 12,\ 36,\ 108,\ldots, $$

we have:

$$ a_1=4, \qquad r=3. $$

Therefore:

$$ a_n=4(3)^{n-1}. $$

The fifth term is:

$$ a_5 = 4(3)^4 $$ $$ =4(81) $$ $$ =324. $$

The common ratio may also be fractional. For example:

$$ 64,\ 32,\ 16,\ 8,\ 4,\ldots $$

has:

$$ r=\frac12. $$

A negative ratio produces alternating signs:

$$ 3,\ -6,\ 12,\ -24,\ldots $$

with:

$$ r=-2. $$

Common Misconception: A geometric sequence does not necessarily increase. Depending on $r$, its terms may increase, decrease in magnitude, alternate signs, remain constant, or become zero.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$a_1$ "A sub one," or "the first term."
$a_n$ "A sub n," or "the nth term."
$r$ "R," or "the common ratio."
$a_{n+1}=ra_n$ "The next term equals r times the current term."
$\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=r$ "The ratio of consecutive terms equals r."
$a_n=a_1r^{n-1}$ "The nth term equals the first term times r to the n minus one power."

A geometric sequence may be represented in several equivalent forms.

Listed form:

$$ 5,\ 10,\ 20,\ 40,\ldots $$

Recursive form:

$$ a_1=5, \qquad a_{n+1}=2a_n. $$

Explicit form:

$$ a_n=5(2)^{n-1}. $$

The recursive form describes how the sequence changes one step at a time. The explicit form gives any term directly from its index.

Defense Notation Advice: If indexing begins with $a_0$ instead of $a_1$, then the explicit formula becomes:

$$ \boxed{ a_n=a_0r^n }. $$

Confusing these indexing conventions creates an exponent that is off by one.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Geometric Progression: In ordinary mathematical usage: $$ \boxed{ \text{geometric sequence} = \text{geometric progression} }. $$ They are alternate names for the same constant-ratio pattern.
  • Common Ratio: If consecutive nonzero terms are known: $$ \boxed{ r=\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} }. $$
  • Explicit Formula: Given the first term and common ratio: $$ \boxed{ a_n=a_1r^{n-1} }. $$ This formula allows direct calculation of any term.
  • Recursive Formula: A geometric sequence is generated by: $$ a_{n+1}=ra_n $$ together with an initial term such as: $$ a_1=k. $$
  • Finding the First Term: If $a_n$, $r$, and $n$ are known: $$ a_n=a_1r^{n-1} $$ gives: $$ a_1=\frac{a_n}{r^{n-1}} $$ when $r\neq0$.
  • Finding the Common Ratio: If two consecutive nonzero terms are known: $$ a_n,\ a_{n+1}, $$ then: $$ r=\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}. $$
  • Finding a Ratio from Nonconsecutive Terms: Since: $$ a_m=a_1r^{m-1} $$ and: $$ a_n=a_1r^{n-1}, $$ division gives: $$ \frac{a_n}{a_m}=r^{n-m}. $$ This can be used to determine possible values of $r$ from terms that are several positions apart.
  • Positive Ratio Greater Than 1: If: $$ r>1 $$ and $a_1>0$, the sequence exhibits exponential growth.
  • Ratio Between 0 and 1: If: $$ 0
  • Negative Ratio: If: $$ r<0, $$ the signs alternate. If: $$ |r|>1, $$ the magnitudes increase; if: $$ |r|<1, $$ the magnitudes decrease toward zero.
  • Ratio Equal to 1: If: $$ r=1, $$ then: $$ a_n=a_1 $$ for every $n$. Thus every constant nonzero sequence is geometric.
  • Ratio Equal to -1: If: $$ r=-1, $$ the sequence alternates between two values: $$ a_1,-a_1,a_1,-a_1,\ldots. $$
  • Ratio Equal to 0: If: $$ r=0, $$ then: $$ a_2=a_3=\cdots=0. $$ The recursive definition still works, although the quotient test cannot be used after zero terms appear.
  • Geometric Mean: If positive numbers $a$, $g$, and $b$ occur consecutively in a geometric sequence, then: $$ \frac{g}{a}=\frac{b}{g}. $$ Hence: $$ g^2=ab $$ and: $$ \boxed{ g=\sqrt{ab} }. $$
  • Geometric Series: A geometric sequence lists: $$ a_1,\ a_1r,\ a_1r^2,\ldots $$ while a geometric series adds: $$ a_1+a_1r+a_1r^2+\cdots. $$ A sequence and its corresponding series are not the same object.
  • Finite Geometric Series: For: $$ r\neq1, $$ the sum of the first $n$ terms is: $$ \boxed{ S_n = a_1\frac{1-r^n}{1-r} }. $$
  • Infinite Geometric Series: If: $$ |r|<1, $$ then: $$ a_n\to0 $$ and the corresponding infinite geometric series converges to: $$ \boxed{ S_\infty=\frac{a_1}{1-r} }. $$
  • Sequence Convergence: For a nonzero initial term: $$ |r|<1 \Longrightarrow a_n\to0. $$ If $r=1$, the sequence is constant; if $r=-1$, it generally oscillates; and if $|r|>1$, its magnitude generally grows without bound.
  • Exponential Function Connection: The explicit rule: $$ a_n=a_1r^{n-1} $$ is exponential in the index $n$. Geometric sequences are therefore discrete versions of exponential growth and decay.
  • Compound Interest: With a fixed interest rate $i$ per period: $$ A_n=A_0(1+i)^n. $$ Successive balances form a geometric sequence with common ratio: $$ 1+i. $$
  • Percentage Growth: Increasing by $p\%$ each period means multiplying by: $$ 1+\frac{p}{100}. $$ The resulting values form a geometric sequence.
  • Percentage Decay: Decreasing by $p\%$ each period means multiplying by: $$ 1-\frac{p}{100}. $$ This also produces a geometric sequence.
  • Population Growth: Simplified discrete growth models frequently use: $$ P_n=P_0r^n, $$ where $r$ represents the growth factor per period.
  • Repeated Halving: The pattern: $$ 1,\frac12,\frac14,\frac18,\ldots $$ is geometric with: $$ r=\frac12. $$ Such patterns occur in half-life models and repeated subdivision.
  • Arithmetic Sequence Contrast: Arithmetic sequences satisfy: $$ a_{n+1}-a_n=d, $$ whereas geometric sequences satisfy: $$ a_{n+1}=ra_n. $$ Arithmetic sequences exhibit constant additive change; geometric sequences exhibit constant multiplicative change.
  • Terminological Contrast: A geometric sequence or geometric progression is an ordered constant-ratio pattern. The common ratio is the repeated multiplier. A geometric series is the sum of the sequence's terms, while the geometric mean describes a multiplicative middle value.

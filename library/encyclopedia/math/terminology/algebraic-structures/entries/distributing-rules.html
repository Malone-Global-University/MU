Distributing Rules

Subfield: Algebraic Structures

Etymology & Roots

Distributing rules are the operational rules derived from distributive laws, especially the rule that multiplication distributes over addition and subtraction.

The central identities are:

$$ \boxed{ a(b+c)=ab+ac } $$
$$ \boxed{ a(b-c)=ab-ac }. $$

The word distribute means to spread or assign something across several parts. Algebraically, an outside factor is applied to each term of a grouped sum or difference.

For example:

$$ 4(x+3) = \boxed{4x+12}. $$

The factor $4$ is multiplied by both $x$ and $3$.

Although teachers often speak informally of "the distributing rules," the underlying principle is the distributive property. The rules are applications and consequences of that property rather than unrelated formulas to memorize.

Operational Nuance

Rule 1: Distribute to Every Term

The outside factor must multiply every term inside the grouping:

$$ \boxed{ a(b+c+d) = ab+ac+ad }. $$

For example:

$$ 2(x+y+5) = \boxed{2x+2y+10}. $$

Rule 2: Preserve the Sign of Each Product

When distributing, multiply signs as well as coefficients and variables.

$$ \boxed{ (+)(+)=+,\qquad (+)(-)=-,\qquad (-)(+)=-,\qquad (-)(-)=+ }. $$

Positive Factor over a Difference

$$ \boxed{ a(b-c)=ab-ac }. $$

Example:

$$ 5(x-4) = \boxed{5x-20}. $$

Negative Factor over a Sum

$$ \boxed{ -a(b+c) = -ab-ac }. $$

Example:

$$ -3(x+2) = \boxed{-3x-6}. $$

Negative Factor over a Difference

$$ \boxed{ -a(b-c) = -ab+ac }. $$

Example:

$$ -4(x-7) = \boxed{-4x+28}. $$

Rule 3: A Lone Minus Sign Means Multiply by $-1$

A minus sign in front of parentheses can be interpreted as:

$$ -(a+b) = (-1)(a+b). $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ -(a+b)=-a-b }. $$

Likewise:

$$ \boxed{ -(a-b)=-a+b }. $$

Rule 4: Multiply Coefficients

When a numerical factor is distributed into a term, multiply numerical coefficients:

$$ 3(5x)=15x. $$

Thus:

$$ 3(5x-2) = \boxed{15x-6}. $$

Rule 5: Multiply Variable Factors Using Exponent Laws

If like bases are multiplied:

$$ \boxed{ x^m x^n=x^{m+n} }. $$

Therefore:

$$ 2x^3(4x^2-5x+1) $$

becomes:

$$ \boxed{ 8x^5-10x^4+2x^3 }. $$

Rule 6: Fractions Distribute Normally

$$ \boxed{ \frac12(a+b) = \frac a2+\frac b2 }. $$

Example:

$$ \frac14(8x-12) = \boxed{2x-3}. $$

Rule 7: Decimals Distribute Normally

$$ 0.5(10x+6) = \boxed{5x+3}. $$

Rule 8: Variables Can Be the Outside Factor

$$ \boxed{ x(x+5)=x^2+5x }. $$

More generally:

$$ x^m(ax^n+b) = ax^{m+n}+bx^m. $$

Rule 9: Monomials Distribute over Polynomials

If:

$$ M(P_1+P_2+\cdots+P_n), $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ MP_1+MP_2+\cdots+MP_n }. $$

Example with a Monomial

$$ -2x(3x^2-4x+7) $$

gives:

$$ \boxed{ -6x^3+8x^2-14x }. $$

Rule 10: Binomial Multiplication Uses Repeated Distribution

For:

$$ (a+b)(c+d), $$

distribute each term of the first binomial across the second:

$$ a(c+d)+b(c+d) $$

giving:

$$ \boxed{ ac+ad+bc+bd }. $$

FOIL Is Repeated Distribution

For two binomials, FOIL abbreviates:

$$ \boxed{ \text{First, Outer, Inner, Last} }. $$

It does not replace the distributive property and does not generalize as cleanly to larger polynomials.

Example: Two Binomials

$$ (x+3)(x-5) $$

expands to:

$$ x^2-5x+3x-15 $$

and then:

$$ \boxed{x^2-2x-15}. $$

Rule 11: Combine Like Terms Only After Distribution

Distribution and combining like terms are separate operations.

Example:

$$ 2(x+3)+5x. $$

First distribute:

$$ 2x+6+5x. $$

Then combine like terms:

$$ \boxed{7x+6}. $$

Rule 12: Factoring Reverses Distribution

$$ \boxed{ ab+ac=a(b+c) }. $$

Thus:

$$ 8x+20 = \boxed{4(2x+5)}. $$

Rule 13: Distribution from the Right Also Works Where Valid

$$ \boxed{ (a+b)c=ac+bc }. $$

In ordinary real-number algebra this looks interchangeable with left distribution because multiplication is commutative. In noncommutative settings, left and right distributivity are distinct laws.

Rule 14: Matrix Multiplication Distributes over Matrix Addition

For compatible matrices:

$$ \boxed{ A(B+C)=AB+AC } $$

and:

$$ \boxed{ (A+B)C=AC+BC }. $$

Since matrix multiplication need not commute:

$$ AB\neq BA $$

in general, the original order of factors must be preserved.

Rule 15: Scalar Multiplication Distributes over Vector Addition

$$ \boxed{ a(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}) = a\mathbf{u}+a\mathbf{v} }. $$

Also:

$$ \boxed{ (a+b)\mathbf{v} = a\mathbf{v}+b\mathbf{v} }. $$

Rule 16: Division Can Distribute Across a Numerator Sum

For $c\neq0$:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a+b}{c} = \frac ac+\frac bc }. $$

This works because division by $c$ is multiplication by $1/c$:

$$ \frac1c(a+b) = \frac ac+\frac bc. $$

Rule 17: Division Does Not Distribute Across a Denominator Sum

In general:

$$ \boxed{ \frac a{b+c} \neq \frac ab+\frac ac }. $$

Rule 18: Exponents Do Not Distribute over Addition

$$ \boxed{ (a+b)^2 \neq a^2+b^2 } $$

in general.

Instead:

$$ \boxed{ (a+b)^2 = a^2+2ab+b^2 }. $$

Rule 19: Exponents Do Distribute over Products

For suitable exponents and domains:

$$ \boxed{ (ab)^n=a^nb^n }. $$

This is an exponent law, not multiplication distributing over addition.

Rule 20: Radicals Do Not Generally Distribute over Addition

$$ \boxed{ \sqrt{a+b} \neq \sqrt a+\sqrt b } $$

in general.

Rule 21: Absolute Value Does Not Distribute over Addition

$$ \boxed{ |a+b| \neq |a|+|b| } $$

in general.

Rule 22: Logarithms Do Not Distribute over Addition

$$ \boxed{ \log(a+b) \neq \log a+\log b }. $$

Logarithms convert products into sums:

$$ \log(ab)=\log a+\log b $$

under appropriate domain conditions, but this is not distribution over addition.

Rule 23: Trigonometric Functions Do Not Distribute over Addition

In general:

$$ \boxed{ \sin(a+b) \neq \sin a+\sin b }. $$

Angle-sum identities must be used instead.

Rule 24: Distribution Preserves Equivalent Expressions

The expressions:

$$ a(b+c) $$

and:

$$ ab+ac $$

represent the same value wherever the operations are defined.

$$ \boxed{ a(b+c)\equiv ab+ac }. $$

Rule 25: Distribution Can Be Used Inside Equations

For:

$$ 3(x+4)=21, $$

one may rewrite:

$$ 3x+12=21. $$

The solution set is unchanged because the expression has been replaced by an equivalent expression.

Rule 26: Distribution Is Not Always the Best First Step

Consider:

$$ 7(x-2)=35. $$

Distribution is valid:

$$ 7x-14=35, $$

but dividing both sides by $7$ first may be simpler:

$$ x-2=5. $$

Valid algebra permits choosing the more efficient route.

Distribution as a Structural Law

The distributive property connects two operations. In a ring, for example, multiplication distributes over addition:

$$ \boxed{ a(b+c)=ab+ac, \qquad (a+b)c=ac+bc }. $$

This makes distribution more fundamental than a classroom simplification technique; it is part of the algebraic structure itself.

Common Misconception: "Distribute" does not mean "apply the outside operation separately to everything inside." A rule may be used only when the relevant operation actually satisfies a distributive law. For example, multiplication distributes over addition, but squaring, square roots, logarithms, and absolute value do not generally distribute over addition.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$a(b+c)=ab+ac$ "A distributes across b plus c."
$a(b-c)=ab-ac$ "A distributes across b minus c."
$-(a-b)=-a+b$ "Distribute negative one across the difference."
$(a+b)c=ac+bc$ "C distributes from the right across a plus b."
$A(B+C)=AB+AC$ "A distributes over the sum B plus C."
$ab+ac=a(b+c)$ "Factor a from the two terms."
$(a+b)^2\neq a^2+b^2$ "The square does not distribute over addition."

Common classroom instructions include:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"distribute the factor"} } $$
$$ \boxed{ \text{"distribute the negative sign"} } $$
$$ \boxed{ \text{"use the distributive property"} }. $$

These phrases are related but differ slightly in precision. The most formal version identifies the law being used:

$$ \boxed{ \text{multiplication distributes over addition} }. $$

A common shorthand such as "change all the signs inside the parentheses" is valid only when a factor of $-1$ is being distributed. The actual operation is:

$$ (-1)(a-b+c) = -a+b-c. $$

Defense Notation Advice: When signs, powers, or multiple variables are involved, write the distributed multiplication term by term before simplifying. For example:

$$ -2x(3x^2-4x+5) $$

can first be written:

$$ (-2x)(3x^2) + (-2x)(-4x) + (-2x)(5), $$

then simplified to:

$$ \boxed{-6x^3+8x^2-10x}. $$

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Basic Rule: $$ \boxed{ a(b+c)=ab+ac }. $$
  • Subtraction Rule: $$ \boxed{ a(b-c)=ab-ac }. $$
  • Right Distribution: $$ \boxed{ (a+b)c=ac+bc }. $$
  • Three-Term Rule: $$ \boxed{ a(b+c+d)=ab+ac+ad }. $$
  • General Finite Sum: $$ \boxed{ a\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_i = \sum_{i=1}^{n}ab_i }. $$
  • Positive Outside Factor: The sign of each inner term is preserved unless multiplication changes it.
  • Negative Outside Factor: Every term receives the negative factor.
  • Negative-One Rule: $$ \boxed{ -(a+b+c) = -a-b-c }. $$
  • Negative-One over Mixed Signs: $$ \boxed{ -(a-b+c) = -a+b-c }. $$
  • Coefficient Multiplication: $$ 4(3x)=12x. $$
  • Exponent Multiplication: $$ x^2x^5=x^7. $$
  • Monomial Distribution: $$ \boxed{ 3x(x^2+2x-5) = 3x^3+6x^2-15x }. $$
  • Fraction Distribution: $$ \boxed{ \frac13(9x+6)=3x+2 }. $$
  • Decimal Distribution: $$ 0.25(8x+12)=2x+3. $$
  • Radical Factor: $$ \sqrt5(x+2)=x\sqrt5+2\sqrt5. $$
  • Two Binomials: $$ \boxed{ (a+b)(c+d)=ac+ad+bc+bd }. $$
  • Binomial Example: $$ \boxed{ (x+2)(x+7) = x^2+9x+14 }. $$
  • FOIL: FOIL is repeated distribution specialized to two binomials.
  • Polynomial Products: Larger polynomial products use the same distributive principle term by term.
  • Combining Like Terms: Combine terms only after the multiplication has been correctly distributed.
  • Reverse Distribution: $$ \boxed{ ab+ac=a(b+c) }. $$
  • Factoring: Factoring extracts a common factor and reverses expansion by distribution.
  • GCF Example: $$ \boxed{ 12x+18=6(2x+3) }. $$
  • Equation Simplification: $$ 4(x-3)=20 $$ may be rewritten: $$ 4x-12=20. $$
  • Inequality Simplification: Distribution inside one side of an inequality does not itself reverse the inequality sign.
  • Negative-Inequality Caution: The inequality sign reverses only when both sides are multiplied or divided by a negative value.
  • Matrix Left Distribution: $$ \boxed{ A(B+C)=AB+AC }. $$
  • Matrix Right Distribution: $$ \boxed{ (A+B)C=AC+BC }. $$
  • Matrix Order: Factor order must remain unchanged because matrix multiplication may be noncommutative.
  • Vector Addition: $$ \boxed{ a(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}) = a\mathbf{u}+a\mathbf{v} }. $$
  • Scalar Sum: $$ \boxed{ (a+b)\mathbf{v} = a\mathbf{v}+b\mathbf{v} }. $$
  • Linear Transformation: $$ \boxed{ T(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}) = T(\mathbf{u})+T(\mathbf{v}) }. $$
  • Linear Combination: $$ \boxed{ T(a\mathbf{u}+b\mathbf{v}) = aT(\mathbf{u})+bT(\mathbf{v}) }. $$
  • Function Addition: Scalar multiplication distributes over function addition pointwise.
  • Modular Arithmetic: $$ \boxed{ a(b+c)\equiv ab+ac\pmod n }. $$
  • Ring Theory: Left and right distributivity are fundamental ring axioms.
  • Set Intersection: $$ \boxed{ A\cap(B\cup C) = (A\cap B)\cup(A\cap C) }. $$
  • Set Union: $$ \boxed{ A\cup(B\cap C) = (A\cup B)\cap(A\cup C) }. $$
  • Logic: $$ p\land(q\lor r) \equiv (p\land q)\lor(p\land r). $$
  • Dual Logical Distribution: $$ p\lor(q\land r) \equiv (p\lor q)\land(p\lor r). $$
  • Division over Numerator Sum: For $c\neq0$: $$ \boxed{ \frac{a+b}{c} = \frac ac+\frac bc }. $$
  • Division over Denominator Sum: In general: $$ \boxed{ \frac a{b+c} \neq \frac ab+\frac ac }. $$
  • Exponent over Addition: In general: $$ \boxed{ (a+b)^n \neq a^n+b^n }. $$
  • Exponent over Product: Under appropriate conditions: $$ \boxed{ (ab)^n=a^nb^n }. $$
  • Radical Warning: $$ \boxed{ \sqrt{a+b} \neq \sqrt a+\sqrt b } $$ in general.
  • Absolute Value Warning: $$ \boxed{ |a+b| \neq |a|+|b| } $$ in general.
  • Logarithm Warning: $$ \boxed{ \log(a+b) \neq \log a+\log b }. $$
  • Trigonometric Warning: $$ \boxed{ \sin(a+b) \neq \sin a+\sin b }. $$
  • Distribution vs. Exponent Law: $(ab)^n=a^nb^n$ is an exponent rule, not multiplication distributing over addition.
  • Distribution vs. Combining: Distribution multiplies across grouped terms; combining like terms adds or subtracts coefficients of matching terms.
  • Distribution vs. Factoring: Distribution expands; factoring commonly reverses the process.
  • Distribution vs. Cancellation: Distribution multiplies across sums; cancellation removes common multiplicative factors. Terms cannot be canceled across addition merely because they share symbols.
  • Efficiency: A correct distributing rule need not be used if another valid algebraic step is simpler.
  • Structural Meaning: Distributing rules describe compatibility between two operations rather than arbitrary manipulation of symbols.
  • Terminological Contrast: Distribute is the action, distributive property is the algebraic law, and distributing rules are the practical operational consequences used to expand or rewrite expressions.
  • Core Interpretation: The central distributing rule is: $$ \boxed{ a(b+c)=ab+ac }. $$ Every term in the grouped sum or difference receives the factor, with coefficients, signs, and variable factors multiplied correctly. The rule should be extended only to operations for which a genuine distributive law exists.

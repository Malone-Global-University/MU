Etymology & Roots

A compound inequality combines two or more inequalities into a single statement describing a common or combined solution condition.

The word compound means formed from multiple parts. The word inequality refers to a comparison using symbols such as:

$$ <,\quad >,\quad \le,\quad \ge. $$

Thus a compound inequality is literally a comparison statement built from more than one inequality condition.

$$ \boxed{ \text{compound inequality} = \text{multiple inequality conditions considered together} }. $$

The logical relationship between the component inequalities matters. Conditions joined by and require simultaneous satisfaction, while conditions joined by or allow either condition to be satisfied.

OR Compound Inequalities

AND Means Overlap

If $S_1$ is the solution set of one inequality and $S_2$ is the solution set of another, then:

$$ \boxed{ \text{AND solution} = S_1\cap S_2 }. $$

OR Means Combination

$$ \boxed{ \text{OR solution} = S_1\cup S_2 }. $$

Inclusive OR

In ordinary mathematical logic, or is inclusive unless stated otherwise. Thus:

$$ P\text{ or }Q $$

is true when $P$ is true, $Q$ is true, or both are true.

For inequalities, overlapping solution sets under or remain part of the union.

An and compound inequality can have no solution.

Root of an Equation

Subfield: Algebraic Structures

Etymology & Roots

A root of an equation is a value of the variable that makes the equation true. In equations written in zero form:

$$ \boxed{ f(x)=0 }, $$

a number $r$ is a root when:

$$ \boxed{ f(r)=0 }. $$

The terms root, solution, and, for functions or polynomials written equal to zero, zero are closely related. The central idea is substitution: a root is an input that satisfies the equation.

For example, in:

$$ x^2-9=0, $$

the roots are:

$$ \boxed{ x=3 \quad\text{and}\quad x=-3 }. $$

Operational Nuance

Root as a Solution

If an equation is:

$$ F(x)=G(x), $$

then a root or solution is any value $r$ satisfying:

$$ \boxed{ F(r)=G(r) }. $$

Rewriting the equation as:

$$ F(x)-G(x)=0 $$

makes the zero interpretation explicit.

Polynomial Roots

For a polynomial equation:

$$ P(x)=0, $$

$r$ is a root exactly when:

$$ \boxed{ P(r)=0 }. $$

Factor Theorem

For a polynomial $P(x)$:

$$ \boxed{ P(r)=0 \Longleftrightarrow x-r\text{ is a factor of }P(x) }. $$

Thus roots and linear factors are directly connected.

Example

Factor:

$$ x^2-5x+6 = (x-2)(x-3). $$

Therefore the roots of:

$$ x^2-5x+6=0 $$

are:

$$ \boxed{ 2,\ 3 }. $$

Zero-Product Property

Factored equations are solved using:

$$ \boxed{ ab=0 \Longrightarrow a=0 \text{ or } b=0 } $$

in ordinary real or complex number arithmetic.

Quadratic Roots

For:

$$ ax^2+bx+c=0, \qquad a\neq0, $$

the roots are:

$$ \boxed{ x= \frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a} }. $$

Discriminant and Real Roots

For a real quadratic:

$$ \Delta=b^2-4ac. $$
  • If $\Delta>0$, there are two distinct real roots.
  • If $\Delta=0$, there is one repeated real root.
  • If $\Delta<0$, there are no real roots but two nonreal complex roots.

Multiplicity

If:

$$ P(x) = (x-r)^mQ(x), \qquad Q(r)\neq0, $$

then $r$ is a root of multiplicity $m$.

$$ \boxed{ \text{multiplicity of }r=m }. $$

Repeated Root

For example:

$$ (x-4)^2=0 $$

has the single distinct root $4$, but that root has multiplicity $2$.

Graphical Interpretation

For an equation:

$$ f(x)=0, $$

real roots are the $x$-coordinates where the graph of $y=f(x)$ meets the $x$-axis.

$$ \boxed{ f(r)=0 \Longleftrightarrow (r,0)\text{ lies on the graph} }. $$

Intersection Interpretation

For:

$$ f(x)=g(x), $$

roots of:

$$ f(x)-g(x)=0 $$

correspond to $x$-coordinates where the graphs of $f$ and $g$ intersect.

Exact and Approximate Roots

Some roots have exact symbolic forms, while others are found numerically.

For example, an equation may have a root reported approximately as:

$$ \boxed{ x\approx1.7693 }. $$

Numerical Root Finding

Common numerical methods include bisection, Newton's method, secant methods, and other iterative algorithms.

Newton's method uses:

$$ \boxed{ x_{n+1} = x_n- \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)} } $$

where the method is applicable and the denominator is nonzero.

Extraneous Solutions

Algebraic transformations such as squaring both sides or clearing denominators may produce candidate roots that do not satisfy the original equation.

Therefore candidate roots should be checked against the original equation whenever a non-equivalent transformation may have been introduced.

Domain Restrictions

A candidate cannot be a root if it lies outside the original equation's domain. For example, in:

$$ \frac{x+1}{x-2}=0, $$

$x=2$ is excluded because the expression is undefined there.

Root vs. Root of a Number

A root of an equation is a value satisfying an equation. A root of a number, such as $\sqrt[3]{8}=2$, is a number that reverses an exponentiation operation. These concepts are related but operationally distinct.

Common Misconception

A root is not necessarily a square root or radical. In equation terminology, root means a value that satisfies the equation, often by making one side equal to zero.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$f(r)=0$ "R is a root, or zero, of f."
$P(r)=0$ "R is a root of the polynomial P."
$x-r\mid P(x)$ "X minus r is a factor of P of x."
$x=2,3$ "The roots are two and three."
$(x-r)^m$ "R is a root of multiplicity m," when this is the highest such factor power.
$x\approx1.7693$ "The equation has an approximate root of one point seven six nine three."

The terms root, solution, and zero often overlap, but their emphasis differs. A solution satisfies an equation, while a zero or root commonly refers to a value $r$ for which $f(r)=0$.

Defense Notation Advice: State the equation or function whose root is being discussed. Distinguish exact equality from approximation, preserve domain restrictions, and verify candidates after transformations that may introduce extraneous values. For repeated polynomial roots, distinguish the number of distinct roots from their multiplicities.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Definition: A value that satisfies an equation.
  • Zero Form: $r$ is a root of $f(x)=0$ when $f(r)=0$.
  • Polynomial Root: $P(r)=0$.
  • Factor Theorem: $r$ is a polynomial root exactly when $x-r$ is a factor.
  • Zero-Product Property: Factored equations can be solved by setting factors equal to zero.
  • Quadratic Equations: Roots may be found by factoring, completing the square, or the quadratic formula.
  • Discriminant: Determines the real/complex root pattern of a real quadratic.
  • Multiplicity: Records how many times a polynomial root occurs as a factor.
  • Graphing: Real roots of $f(x)=0$ are $x$-intercepts.
  • Intersections: Roots of $f(x)-g(x)=0$ locate graph intersections.
  • Exact Root: May be represented symbolically.
  • Approximate Root: May be obtained numerically.
  • Numerical Analysis: Bisection, Newton's method, and related algorithms approximate roots.
  • Domain: Roots must belong to the domain of the original equation.
  • Extraneous Candidate: A transformed equation may produce a value that fails the original equation.
  • Solution Terminology: Every root is a solution of the associated equation.
  • Zero Terminology: Especially common when discussing functions and polynomials.
  • Root-of-a-Number Contrast: Numerical roots reverse powers; equation roots satisfy equations.
  • Common Error: Assuming root always means square root.
  • Common Error: Forgetting to rewrite an equation in zero form before using factor methods.
  • Common Error: Counting a repeated root as several distinct roots.
  • Common Error: Accepting an extraneous candidate without checking.
  • Common Error: Ignoring domain restrictions.
  • Common Error: Reporting an approximate root as though it were exact.
  • Core Interpretation: A root of an equation is an input that makes the equation true. In zero form: $$ \boxed{ f(r)=0 }. $$ For polynomials, this is equivalent to: $$ \boxed{ P(r)=0 \Longleftrightarrow x-r\text{ is a factor of }P(x) }. $$ Roots therefore connect equation solving, graph intersections, factorization, multiplicity, symbolic methods, and numerical approximation.

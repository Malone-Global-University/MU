Cofactor

Subfield: Algebraic & Analytic Structures

Etymology & Roots

In matrix algebra, a cofactor is a signed minor associated with a particular entry of a square matrix.

For an entry $a_{ij}$, its minor $M_{ij}$ is the determinant obtained after deleting row $i$ and column $j$. The corresponding cofactor is:

$$ \boxed{ C_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j}M_{ij} }. $$

Thus a cofactor combines two ingredients:

$$ \boxed{ \text{cofactor} = \text{sign factor} \times \text{minor} }. $$

Cofactors are fundamental in determinant expansion, adjugate construction, and the classical inverse formula for nonsingular matrices.

Operational Nuance

Minor of an Entry

Let:

$$ A=[a_{ij}] $$

be an $n\times n$ matrix. To find the minor $M_{ij}$:

  1. delete row $i$,
  2. delete column $j$,
  3. take the determinant of the remaining $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ matrix.
$$ \boxed{ M_{ij} = \det(\text{matrix remaining after deleting row }i\text{ and column }j) }. $$

Cofactor Definition

$$ \boxed{ C_{ij} = (-1)^{i+j}M_{ij} }. $$

Cofactor Sign Pattern

The factor $(-1)^{i+j}$ creates an alternating checkerboard pattern of signs:

$$ \boxed{ \begin{bmatrix} +&-&+&-\cdots\\ -&+&-&+\cdots\\ +&-&+&-\cdots\\ -&+&-&+\cdots\\ \vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots \end{bmatrix} }. $$

Positive Cofactor Positions

If $i+j$ is even:

$$ \boxed{ C_{ij}=M_{ij} }. $$

Negative Cofactor Positions

If $i+j$ is odd:

$$ \boxed{ C_{ij}=-M_{ij} }. $$

Example Matrix

Consider:

$$ A= \begin{bmatrix} 2&3&1\\ 4&0&5\\ -1&2&6 \end{bmatrix}. $$

Finding M₁₂

Delete row $1$ and column $2$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 4&5\\ -1&6 \end{bmatrix}. $$

Therefore:

$$ M_{12} = \begin{vmatrix} 4&5\\ -1&6 \end{vmatrix} = 24+5 = 29. $$

Finding C₁₂

Since:

$$ (-1)^{1+2}=-1, $$

the cofactor is:

$$ \boxed{ C_{12}=-29 }. $$

Minor versus Cofactor

The distinction is essential:

$$ \boxed{ M_{ij} = \text{minor} } $$
$$ \boxed{ C_{ij} = (-1)^{i+j}M_{ij} = \text{cofactor} }. $$

A minor and cofactor are equal only in positions where $i+j$ is even.

Cofactor Expansion of a Determinant

The determinant of a square matrix can be expanded along any row or column using cofactors.

Along row $i$:

$$ \boxed{ \det(A) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij}C_{ij} }. $$

Expansion along a Column

Along column $j$:

$$ \boxed{ \det(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_{ij}C_{ij} }. $$

3×3 First-Row Expansion

For:

$$ A= \begin{bmatrix} a&b&c\\ d&e&f\\ g&h&i \end{bmatrix}, $$

expanding along the first row gives:

$$ \boxed{ \det(A) = a \begin{vmatrix} e&f\\ h&i \end{vmatrix} - b \begin{vmatrix} d&f\\ g&i \end{vmatrix} + c \begin{vmatrix} d&e\\ g&h \end{vmatrix} }. $$

Why Any Row or Column Works

Cofactor expansion yields the same determinant regardless of which row or column is chosen.

$$ \boxed{ \text{any row or column} \longrightarrow \det(A) }. $$

Strategic Expansion

In hand calculation, it is often advantageous to expand along a row or column containing many zeros because terms with zero entries vanish.

If:

$$ a_{ij}=0, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ a_{ij}C_{ij}=0 }. $$

Cofactor Matrix

The cofactor matrix of $A$ is the matrix whose entries are the cofactors of $A$:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Cof}(A) = [C_{ij}] }. $$

Adjugate Matrix

The adjugate is the transpose of the cofactor matrix:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{adj}(A) = \operatorname{Cof}(A)^T }. $$

Cofactor Matrix versus Adjugate

These are not generally the same matrix.

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{adj}(A) = [C_{ji}] }. $$

The transpose step is essential.

Adjugate Identity

For every square matrix:

$$ \boxed{ A\operatorname{adj}(A) = \operatorname{adj}(A)A = \det(A)I }. $$

Inverse Formula

If:

$$ \det(A)\ne0, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ A^{-1} = \frac{1}{\det(A)} \operatorname{adj}(A) }. $$

2×2 Example

Let:

$$ A= \begin{bmatrix} a&b\\ c&d \end{bmatrix}. $$

The minors are:

$$ M_{11}=d,\qquad M_{12}=c,\qquad M_{21}=b,\qquad M_{22}=a. $$

Applying the cofactor signs:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Cof}(A) = \begin{bmatrix} d&-c\\ -b&a \end{bmatrix} }. $$

Adjugate of a 2×2 Matrix

Transposing the cofactor matrix gives:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{adj}(A) = \begin{bmatrix} d&-b\\ -c&a \end{bmatrix} }. $$

2×2 Inverse

Since:

$$ \det(A)=ad-bc, $$

when $ad-bc\ne0$:

$$ \boxed{ A^{-1} = \frac{1}{ad-bc} \begin{bmatrix} d&-b\\ -c&a \end{bmatrix} }. $$

Cofactors and Partial Derivatives of the Determinant

When the entries of a matrix are treated as independent variables, the cofactor has the useful interpretation:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial\det(A)} {\partial a_{ij}} = C_{ij} }. $$

This reflects the linear dependence of the determinant on each individual row or column when the others are held fixed.

Cofactors in Cramer's Rule

Cramer's Rule is formulated using determinants, and those determinants may themselves be evaluated by cofactor expansion.

Cofactors in Geometry

Determinants built from coordinate matrices appear in formulas for areas, volumes, orientations, and coordinate transformations. Cofactors therefore arise indirectly whenever those determinants are expanded.

Common Misconception: Minor and Cofactor Are Synonyms

They differ by the sign factor:

$$ \boxed{ C_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j}M_{ij} }. $$

Common Misconception: The Sign Is Based on the Entry's Value

The cofactor sign depends only on the position $(i,j)$, not on whether $a_{ij}$ is positive or negative.

Common Misconception: Delete Only the Row

To form $M_{ij}$, one must delete both row $i$ and column $j$.

Common Misconception: The Cofactor Matrix Is the Adjugate

The adjugate is the transpose of the cofactor matrix.

Common Misconception: Cofactor Expansion Works Only along the First Row

Any row or any column may be used.

Common Misconception: Every Cofactor Must Be Nonzero

A minor determinant may be zero, so the associated cofactor may also be zero.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$M_{ij}$ "The i j minor."
$C_{ij}$ "The i j cofactor."
$C_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j}M_{ij}$ "The i j cofactor equals negative one to the i plus j times the i j minor."
$\det(A)=\sum_j a_{ij}C_{ij}$ "Expand the determinant along row i using cofactors."
$\operatorname{Cof}(A)$ "The cofactor matrix of A."
$\operatorname{adj}(A)=\operatorname{Cof}(A)^T$ "The adjugate of A is the transpose of its cofactor matrix."

The phrase cofactor of $a_{ij}$ refers to the signed minor associated with the position of that entry, not to the product $a_{ij}C_{ij}$. The latter is one term in a cofactor expansion of the determinant.

Defense Notation Advice: First locate the entry's row and column, form the minor by deleting both, apply the checkerboard sign, and only then use the cofactor in a determinant expansion. Keep the cofactor matrix distinct from its transpose, the adjugate.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Definition: $$ \boxed{ C_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j}M_{ij} }. $$
  • Minor: $M_{ij}$ is the determinant remaining after deleting row $i$ and column $j$.
  • Sign Pattern: Begins $+,-,+,-,\ldots$ across the first row and alternates thereafter.
  • Row Expansion: $$ \boxed{ \det(A)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}C_{ij} }. $$
  • Column Expansion: $$ \boxed{ \det(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ij}C_{ij} }. $$
  • Strategic Use: Expand along rows or columns containing many zeros when calculating by hand.
  • Cofactor Matrix: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Cof}(A)=[C_{ij}] }. $$
  • Adjugate: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{adj}(A)=\operatorname{Cof}(A)^T }. $$
  • Adjugate Identity: $$ \boxed{ A\operatorname{adj}(A) = \det(A)I }. $$
  • Inverse Formula: $$ \boxed{ A^{-1} = \frac{\operatorname{adj}(A)}{\det(A)} } $$ when $\det(A)\ne0$.
  • Derivative Connection: $$ \boxed{ \frac{\partial\det(A)} {\partial a_{ij}} = C_{ij} }. $$
  • Common Error: Do not confuse a minor with a cofactor.
  • Common Error: Do not forget the checkerboard sign factor.
  • Common Error: Do not delete only a row or only a column when forming a minor.
  • Common Error: Do not confuse the cofactor matrix with the adjugate.
  • Terminological Contrast: Minor is the determinant obtained after deleting a specified row and column. Cofactor is the signed minor. Cofactor expansion computes a determinant as a sum of matrix entries multiplied by their cofactors. Cofactor matrix contains all cofactors in their original positions. Adjugate is the transpose of the cofactor matrix. Determinant is the scalar obtained from a square matrix and may be computed by cofactor expansion.

Absolute Value of a Complex Number

Subfield: Algebraic & Analytic Structures

Etymology & Roots

The absolute value of a complex number is the nonnegative magnitude of that number. For:

$$ z=a+bi, $$

its absolute value is defined by:

$$ \boxed{ |z|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2} }. $$

In complex-number terminology, this quantity is very commonly called the modulus of $z$.

The notation extends the ordinary absolute value of a real number. A real number $x$ can be viewed as the complex number:

$$ x+0i. $$

Therefore:

$$ |x+0i| = \sqrt{x^2} = |x|. $$

The complex modulus thus preserves the familiar real-number concept while extending it naturally into two dimensions.

Operational Nuance

Geometric Meaning

The complex number:

$$ z=a+bi $$

corresponds to the point:

$$ (a,b) $$

in the complex plane.

Its modulus is the Euclidean distance from $(a,b)$ to the origin:

$$ \boxed{ |z| = \sqrt{a^2+b^2} }. $$

This is simply the Pythagorean Theorem applied to the horizontal and vertical components.

Example

Let:

$$ z=3+4i. $$

Then:

$$ |z| = \sqrt{3^2+4^2} $$ $$ = \sqrt{9+16} $$ $$ = \sqrt{25} $$ $$ = \boxed{5}. $$

Negative Components Do Not Matter Separately

Consider:

$$ z=-3+4i. $$

Its modulus is still:

$$ |-3+4i| = \sqrt{(-3)^2+4^2} = 5. $$

Modulus measures distance from the origin, so direction and quadrant do not affect the resulting magnitude.

Connection with the Complex Conjugate

If:

$$ z=a+bi, $$

then its conjugate is:

$$ \overline{z}=a-bi. $$

Their product is:

$$ z\overline{z} = (a+bi)(a-bi) = a^2+b^2. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ |z|^2 = z\overline{z} } $$

and:

$$ \boxed{ |z| = \sqrt{z\overline{z}} }. $$

Polar Form

A nonzero complex number may be written:

$$ z=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta). $$

In this form:

$$ \boxed{ r=|z| }. $$

Thus the modulus is the radial coordinate of the complex number in polar form.

Exponential Form

Using Euler's formula:

$$ e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta+i\sin\theta, $$

one may write:

$$ z=re^{i\theta}. $$

Again:

$$ \boxed{|z|=r}. $$

Common Misconception: The absolute value of $a+bi$ is not:

$$ |a|+|b|. $$

It is:

$$ \boxed{ \sqrt{a^2+b^2} }, $$

because complex modulus is Euclidean distance in the complex plane.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$|z|$ "The modulus of z," or "the absolute value of z."
$|a+bi|$ "The modulus of a plus b i."
$|z|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ "The modulus of z equals the square root of a squared plus b squared."
$\overline{z}$ "Z conjugate," or "the complex conjugate of z."
$|z|^2=z\overline z$ "The modulus squared equals z times z conjugate."
$|z-w|$ "The distance between z and w in the complex plane."

The same vertical-bar notation is used for real and complex absolute value:

$$ |x|,\qquad |z|. $$

Context determines whether the input is real or complex.

In complex analysis, modulus is especially common because it distinguishes the magnitude of a complex number from its argument:

$$ \boxed{ z = |z| \left( \cos\theta+i\sin\theta \right) }. $$

Here $|z|$ gives magnitude, while $\theta$ gives direction.

Defense Notation Advice: Do not confuse complex modulus with the modulo operation from modular arithmetic. The word modulus is used in both subjects, but the mathematical objects and notation are entirely different.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Definition: For: $$ z=a+bi, $$ $$ \boxed{ |z|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2} }. $$
  • Nonnegativity: $$ \boxed{ |z|\ge0 }. $$
  • Zero Condition: $$ \boxed{ |z|=0 \iff z=0 }. $$
  • Conjugate Equality: $$ \boxed{ |\overline z|=|z| }. $$
  • Conjugate Product: $$ \boxed{ z\overline z=|z|^2 }. $$
  • Product Property: $$ \boxed{ |zw|=|z||w| }. $$
  • Quotient Property: If $w\ne0$: $$ \boxed{ \left|\frac zw\right| = \frac{|z|}{|w|} }. $$
  • Powers: For integer $n\ge0$: $$ \boxed{ |z^n|=|z|^n }. $$
  • Reciprocal: If $z\ne0$: $$ \boxed{ \left|\frac1z\right| = \frac1{|z|} }. $$
  • Triangle Inequality: $$ \boxed{ |z+w| \le |z|+|w| }. $$

  • Complex Analysis: Limits and continuity are expressed using modulus. For example: $$ |z-z_0|<\delta $$ expresses that $z$ lies within distance $\delta$ of $z_0$.
  • Complex Limits: $$ \lim_{z\to z_0}f(z)=L $$ means that: $$ |f(z)-L|<\varepsilon $$ whenever: $$ 0<|z-z_0|<\delta. $$
  • Bounded Functions: A complex-valued function is bounded on a set if there exists $M>0$ such that: $$ \boxed{ |f(z)|\le M } $$ throughout that set.
  • Series: A complex series: $$ \sum z_n $$ converges absolutely when: $$ \boxed{ \sum|z_n| \text{ converges} }. $$
  • Complex Norm: The modulus makes $\mathbb{C}$ into a normed space, with: $$ \|z\|=|z|. $$
  • Field Compatibility: The identity: $$ |zw|=|z||w| $$ makes complex modulus particularly important because it interacts cleanly with multiplication.
  • Terminological Contrast: The absolute value or modulus of $z=a+bi$ is $\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ and measures magnitude. The argument gives angular direction, while the complex conjugate reflects the point across the real axis.

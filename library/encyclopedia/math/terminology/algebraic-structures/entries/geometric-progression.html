Geometric Progression

Subfield: Algebraic & Analytic Structures

Etymology & Roots

A geometric progression is an ordered sequence in which each term, after the first, is obtained by multiplying the preceding term by the same constant factor.

That constant factor is called the common ratio. The word progression refers to the ordered movement from one term to the next, while geometric reflects the historical mathematical language of proportion and geometric means.

In modern textbooks, geometric progression and geometric sequence ordinarily refer to the same concept. The term sequence is especially common in contemporary algebra and precalculus, while progression remains widespread internationally and in older mathematical terminology.

A geometric progression should be distinguished from an arithmetic progression. Arithmetic progressions are generated by repeatedly adding a fixed amount; geometric progressions are generated by repeatedly multiplying by a fixed factor.

Operational Nuance

A sequence:

$$ a_1,a_2,a_3,\ldots $$

is a geometric progression if there exists a constant $r$ such that:

$$ \boxed{ a_{n+1}=ra_n } $$

for every applicable value of $n$.

When $a_n\neq0$, the same condition may be written:

$$ \boxed{ \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=r }. $$

The constant $r$ is called the common ratio.

For example:

$$ 3,\ 6,\ 12,\ 24,\ 48,\ldots $$

is geometric because:

$$ \frac63=2, $$ $$ \frac{12}{6}=2, $$ $$ \frac{24}{12}=2. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{r=2}. $$

Beginning with $a_1$, the terms have the pattern:

$$ a_1,\quad a_1r,\quad a_1r^2,\quad a_1r^3,\ldots $$

Therefore the nth-term formula is:

$$ \boxed{ a_n=a_1r^{n-1} }. $$

The exponent $n-1$ appears because the first term has been multiplied by the common ratio zero times, the second once, the third twice, and so on.

For:

$$ 3,\ 6,\ 12,\ 24,\ldots, $$

we have:

$$ a_1=3, \qquad r=2. $$

Thus:

$$ a_n=3(2)^{n-1}. $$

The sixth term is:

$$ a_6 = 3(2)^5 = 96. $$

The common ratio may be positive, negative, fractional, greater than $1$, less than $1$ in magnitude, or zero.

For example:

$$ 81,\ 27,\ 9,\ 3,\ 1,\ldots $$

has:

$$ r=\frac13. $$

And:

$$ 2,\ -6,\ 18,\ -54,\ldots $$

has:

$$ r=-3. $$

Common Misconception: The common ratio is found by dividing consecutive terms, not subtracting them. Subtraction is the defining operation for an arithmetic progression.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$a_1$ "A sub one," or "the first term."
$a_n$ "A sub n," or "the nth term."
$r$ "R," or "the common ratio."
$a_{n+1}=ra_n$ "The next term equals the common ratio times the current term."
$\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=r$ "The ratio of consecutive terms equals r."
$a_n=a_1r^{n-1}$ "The nth term equals the first term times r to the n minus one power."

A geometric progression can be described recursively:

$$ a_{n+1}=ra_n. $$

It can also be described explicitly:

$$ a_n=a_1r^{n-1}. $$

The recursive form emphasizes repeated multiplication, while the explicit form permits direct computation of any term.

Defense Notation Advice: If indexing begins at $n=0$ rather than $n=1$, the formula becomes:

$$ a_n=a_0r^n. $$

Always check the indexing convention before applying an exponent formula.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Geometric Sequence: In ordinary modern usage: $$ \boxed{ \text{geometric progression} = \text{geometric sequence} }. $$ The distinction is terminological rather than mathematical.
  • Common Ratio: The defining constant is: $$ r=\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} $$ whenever $a_n\neq0$.
  • Explicit Formula: Given the first term and common ratio: $$ a_n=a_1r^{n-1}. $$
  • Recursive Formula: A geometric progression may be generated by: $$ a_{n+1}=ra_n $$ together with an initial value $a_1$.
  • Ratio Greater Than 1: If: $$ r>1 $$ and $a_1>0$, the terms increase in magnitude. For example: $$ 2,\ 6,\ 18,\ 54,\ldots $$ has: $$ r=3. $$
  • Ratio Between 0 and 1: If: $$ 0
  • Negative Ratio: If: $$ r<0, $$ the signs alternate: $$ 5,\ -10,\ 20,\ -40,\ldots. $$ Here: $$ r=-2. $$
  • Ratio Equal to 1: If: $$ r=1, $$ then: $$ a_1,a_1,a_1,\ldots $$ is a constant geometric progression.
  • Ratio Equal to 0: If: $$ r=0, $$ then after the first term: $$ a_2=a_3=a_4=\cdots=0. $$ Such a sequence satisfies the recursive multiplication rule, although the quotient form for the common ratio becomes unusable once zero terms appear.
  • Geometric Mean: If positive numbers $a$, $g$, and $b$ are consecutive terms of a geometric progression, then: $$ \frac{g}{a}=\frac{b}{g}. $$ Thus: $$ g^2=ab $$ and: $$ \boxed{ g=\sqrt{ab} }. $$ The middle term is the geometric mean of the outer terms.
  • Geometric Series: A geometric progression lists terms: $$ a_1,a_1r,a_1r^2,\ldots, $$ while a geometric series adds them: $$ a_1+a_1r+a_1r^2+\cdots. $$
  • Finite Geometric Series: For: $$ r\neq1, $$ the sum of the first $n$ terms is: $$ \boxed{ S_n = a_1\frac{1-r^n}{1-r} }. $$ An equivalent form is: $$ S_n = a_1\frac{r^n-1}{r-1}. $$
  • Infinite Geometric Series: If: $$ |r|<1, $$ then: $$ r^n\to0, $$ and the infinite series converges to: $$ \boxed{ S_\infty = \frac{a_1}{1-r} }. $$
  • Divergence of Infinite Geometric Series: If: $$ |r|\ge1, $$ an infinite geometric series generally diverges because its terms fail to approach zero, except for trivial cases involving zero.
  • Exponential Functions: The nth-term formula: $$ a_n=a_1r^{n-1} $$ is exponential in $n$. Geometric progressions are therefore discrete analogues of exponential functions.
  • Compound Growth: If a quantity grows by the same percentage each period, its values form a geometric progression. For example, multiplying by: $$ 1.05 $$ each year represents $5\%$ growth per period.
  • Exponential Decay: Repeated percentage decrease also produces a geometric progression. Multiplying by: $$ 0.8 $$ each period corresponds to retaining $80\%$ of the previous amount.
  • Compound Interest: If an investment grows by a fixed percentage per compounding period, successive balances form a geometric progression with common ratio: $$ 1+i, $$ where $i$ is the interest rate per period.
  • Population Models: Simplified discrete population models with constant proportional growth have the form: $$ P_n=P_0r^n, $$ which is a geometric progression.
  • Half-Life Models: If a fixed fraction of a substance remains after each equal time interval, the remaining quantities form a geometric progression. For repeated halving: $$ r=\frac12. $$
  • Arithmetic Progression Contrast: Arithmetic progressions satisfy: $$ a_{n+1}-a_n=d, $$ while geometric progressions satisfy: $$ \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=r $$ when the quotient is defined. Arithmetic change is additive; geometric change is multiplicative.
  • Terminological Contrast: A geometric progression or geometric sequence is an ordered constant-ratio pattern. The common ratio is the fixed multiplier. A geometric series is the sum of those terms, while a geometric mean is the multiplicative analogue of an arithmetic mean.

Sum

Subfield: Algebraic Structures

Etymology & Roots

A sum is the result of addition. If $a$ and $b$ are added, their sum is:

$$ \boxed{ a+b }. $$

The quantities being added are called addends or terms. The word sum comes from a Latin root associated with a total or highest amount, which developed into the mathematical meaning of a total formed by addition.

Operational Nuance

Basic Addition

$$ 7+5=\boxed{12}. $$

Here $7$ and $5$ are addends, and $12$ is their sum.

More Than Two Terms

A sum may contain any finite number of terms:

$$ \boxed{ a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_n }. $$

Commutativity

In ordinary number systems, addition is commutative:

$$ \boxed{ a+b=b+a }. $$

Associativity

$$ \boxed{ (a+b)+c=a+(b+c) }. $$

This allows finite sums to be regrouped without changing their value.

Additive Identity

$$ \boxed{ a+0=a }. $$

Zero is the additive identity.

Additive Inverses

$$ \boxed{ a+(-a)=0 }. $$

Subtraction can therefore be interpreted as addition of an additive inverse:

$$ a-b=a+(-b). $$

Sigma Notation

Long sums are often written compactly using the capital Greek letter sigma:

$$ \boxed{ \sum_{k=1}^{n}a_k = a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_n }. $$

The index $k$ identifies the changing term, while the lower and upper limits specify the range of indices.

Example of Sigma Notation

$$ \sum_{k=1}^{4}k = 1+2+3+4 = \boxed{10}. $$

Empty Sum

In advanced mathematics, a sum containing no terms is conventionally assigned the value:

$$ \boxed{ \sum_{\varnothing}=0 }. $$

This convention preserves the role of zero as the additive identity.

Infinite Sums

An expression such as:

$$ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n $$

denotes an infinite series. Its value, when it exists, is defined through the limit of its partial sums rather than by completing infinitely many additions.

$$ \boxed{ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n = \lim_{N\to\infty} \sum_{n=1}^{N}a_n } $$

when that limit exists.

Vector and Matrix Sums

Addition extends beyond numbers. Vectors and matrices of compatible dimensions may also be added:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} a\\ b \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} c\\ d \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} a+c\\ b+d \end{bmatrix}. $$

Function Sums

Functions may be added pointwise:

$$ \boxed{ (f+g)(x)=f(x)+g(x) }. $$

Common Misconception

A sum is not necessarily a single numeral obtained after arithmetic evaluation. Expressions such as $x+y$, a vector sum, a matrix sum, or a convergent infinite series are all legitimate sums.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$a+b$ "The sum of a and b."
$3+7=10$ "Three plus seven equals ten."
$a_1+\cdots+a_n$ "The sum of a one through a n."
$\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_k$ "The sum from k equals one to n of a sub k."
$(f+g)(x)$ "The sum of f and g evaluated at x."
$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n$ "The infinite sum from n equals one to infinity of a sub n."

In ordinary speech, sum may refer either to the addition expression itself or to its resulting value. Context usually distinguishes these uses.

Defense Notation Advice: Distinguish a finite sum from an infinite series, specify summation bounds clearly, and use parentheses when an entire sum is multiplied, divided, exponentiated, or substituted into another expression.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Meaning: Result or expression formed by addition.
  • Addends: Quantities being combined by addition.
  • Arithmetic: A sum may be evaluated numerically.
  • Algebra: Sums commonly remain symbolic, as in $x+y$.
  • Finite Sum: Contains finitely many terms.
  • Sigma Notation: $\sum$ compactly represents indexed addition.
  • Empty Sum: Conventionally equals $0$.
  • Infinite Series: Defined through limits of partial sums when convergence occurs.
  • Vectors: Added componentwise.
  • Matrices: Added entrywise when dimensions agree.
  • Functions: Added pointwise on their common domain.
  • Probability: Probabilities and random variables frequently appear in finite or infinite sums.
  • Combinatorics: Counting arguments often decompose a total into sums of disjoint cases.
  • Calculus: Riemann sums approximate integrals, while infinite sums define series.
  • Common Error: Confusing the terms being added with the resulting sum.
  • Common Error: Assuming every infinite sum has a finite value.
  • Common Error: Ignoring index bounds in sigma notation.
  • Common Error: Assuming addition is meaningful for mathematical objects whose structures or dimensions are incompatible.
  • Core Interpretation: A sum is the fundamental additive combination: $$ \boxed{ a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_n = \sum_{k=1}^{n}a_k }. $$ The same idea extends from elementary arithmetic to algebra, vectors, matrices, functions, probability, combinatorics, and infinite series.

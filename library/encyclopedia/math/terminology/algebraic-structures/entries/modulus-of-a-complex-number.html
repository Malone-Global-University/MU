Modulus of a Complex Number

Subfield: Algebraic Structures

Etymology & Roots

The modulus of a complex number is the nonnegative real number measuring its magnitude or distance from the origin in the complex plane.

If:

$$ \boxed{ z=a+bi }, $$

then the modulus of $z$ is:

$$ \boxed{ |z| = \sqrt{a^2+b^2} }. $$

The term modulus comes from Latin modulus, meaning a measure, standard, or small measure. In complex-number language, the word emphasizes measurement of size rather than direction or angular position.

The same quantity is also commonly called the absolute value or magnitude of the complex number.

$$ \boxed{ |z| = \text{modulus} = \text{magnitude} = \text{distance from }z\text{ to }0 }. $$

The notation $|z|$ extends the ordinary absolute-value notation for real numbers. If $z$ happens to be real, the complex modulus agrees exactly with real absolute value.

Operational Nuance

Rectangular Form

A complex number is commonly written:

$$ \boxed{ z=a+bi }, $$

where $a$ is the real part and $b$ is the imaginary coefficient.

Modulus Formula

$$ \boxed{ |a+bi| = \sqrt{a^2+b^2} }. $$

Geometric Interpretation

In the complex plane, $z=a+bi$ corresponds to the point $(a,b)$. Its modulus is the Euclidean distance from $(0,0)$ to $(a,b)$.

$$ \boxed{ |z| = \sqrt{(a-0)^2+(b-0)^2} }. $$

Pythagorean Interpretation

The real and imaginary components form perpendicular legs of a right triangle, so the modulus is the hypotenuse length:

$$ \boxed{ |z|^2 = a^2+b^2 }. $$

Example

For:

$$ z=3+4i, $$

the modulus is:

$$ |z| = \sqrt{3^2+4^2} = \sqrt{25} = \boxed{5}. $$

Another Example

For:

$$ z=-5+12i, $$

one obtains:

$$ |z| = \sqrt{(-5)^2+12^2} = \sqrt{169} = \boxed{13}. $$

Nonnegative Value

The modulus is always nonnegative:

$$ \boxed{ |z|\geq0 }. $$

Zero Criterion

The modulus equals zero exactly when the complex number itself is zero:

$$ \boxed{ |z|=0 \iff z=0 }. $$

Positive Modulus for Nonzero Numbers

$$ \boxed{ z\neq0 \Longrightarrow |z|>0 }. $$

Real Numbers as a Special Case

If $z=a+0i$, then:

$$ |z| = \sqrt{a^2} = \boxed{|a|}. $$

Pure Imaginary Numbers

If:

$$ z=bi, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ |bi| = |b| }. $$

Modulus of $i$

$$ \boxed{ |i|=1 }. $$

Modulus of $-i$

$$ \boxed{ |-i|=1 }. $$

Complex Conjugate

If:

$$ z=a+bi, $$

then its conjugate is:

$$ \boxed{ \overline{z} = a-bi }. $$

Conjugate Product

Multiplying a complex number by its conjugate gives the square of the modulus:

$$ \boxed{ z\overline{z} = |z|^2 }. $$

Derivation

$$ (a+bi)(a-bi) = a^2+b^2. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ |z| = \sqrt{z\overline{z}} }. $$

Conjugates Have Equal Modulus

$$ \boxed{ |\overline{z}| = |z| }. $$

Negation Preserves Modulus

$$ \boxed{ |-z| = |z| }. $$

Product Rule

The modulus of a product equals the product of the moduli:

$$ \boxed{ |zw| = |z||w| }. $$

Product Example

If:

$$ |z|=3 \qquad\text{and}\qquad |w|=5, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ |zw|=15 }. $$

Quotient Rule

For $w\neq0$:

$$ \boxed{ \left| \frac{z}{w} \right| = \frac{|z|}{|w|} }. $$

Reciprocal Rule

For $z\neq0$:

$$ \boxed{ \left| \frac{1}{z} \right| = \frac{1}{|z|} }. $$

Power Rule

For every nonnegative integer $n$:

$$ \boxed{ |z^n| = |z|^n }. $$

Negative Integer Powers

If $z\neq0$ and $n$ is an integer:

$$ \boxed{ |z^n| = |z|^n }. $$

The negative-power case follows from the reciprocal rule.

Scalar Multiplication

For real or complex scalar $c$:

$$ \boxed{ |cz| = |c||z| }. $$

Distance between Complex Numbers

The distance between $z$ and $w$ in the complex plane is:

$$ \boxed{ |z-w| }. $$

Coordinate Distance Formula

If:

$$ z=a+bi, \qquad w=c+di, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ |z-w| = \sqrt{(a-c)^2+(b-d)^2} }. $$

Circle Centered at the Origin

The equation:

$$ \boxed{ |z|=r } $$

describes a circle of radius $r$ centered at the origin when $r>0$.

Closed Disk

$$ \boxed{ |z|\leq r } $$

describes the closed disk of radius $r$ centered at the origin.

Open Disk

$$ \boxed{ |z|<r } $$

describes the open disk.

Exterior Region

$$ \boxed{ |z|>r } $$

describes the region exterior to the circle of radius $r$.

Circle Centered at Another Point

The equation:

$$ \boxed{ |z-z_0|=r } $$

describes a circle centered at $z_0$ with radius $r$.

Polar Form

A nonzero complex number can be written:

$$ \boxed{ z=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta) }, $$

where:

$$ \boxed{ r=|z| }. $$

Exponential Form

$$ \boxed{ z=re^{i\theta} }. $$

Again:

$$ \boxed{ |z|=r }. $$

Modulus and Argument

The polar description separates a complex number into:

  • modulus $r$, describing size;
  • argument $\theta$, describing direction.
$$ \boxed{ z = |z| e^{i\arg z} } $$

with the usual understanding that the argument is multivalued unless a branch is chosen.

Unit Complex Numbers

A complex number lies on the unit circle exactly when:

$$ \boxed{ |z|=1 }. $$

Unit-Circle Form

$$ \boxed{ z=e^{i\theta} } $$

satisfies $|z|=1$.

Multiplication in Polar Form

If:

$$ z=r_1e^{i\theta_1}, \qquad w=r_2e^{i\theta_2}, $$

then:

$$ zw = r_1r_2e^{i(\theta_1+\theta_2)}. $$

Therefore the moduli multiply:

$$ \boxed{ |zw| = r_1r_2 }. $$

Division in Polar Form

For $w\neq0$:

$$ \frac{z}{w} = \frac{r_1}{r_2} e^{i(\theta_1-\theta_2)}. $$

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ \left| \frac{z}{w} \right| = \frac{r_1}{r_2} }. $$

De Moivre's Theorem

If:

$$ z=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta), $$

then:

$$ z^n = r^n \left( \cos(n\theta)+i\sin(n\theta) \right). $$

Hence:

$$ \boxed{ |z^n|=|z|^n }. $$

Roots and Modulus

If $w^n=z$, then:

$$ \boxed{ |w|^n=|z| }. $$

Therefore every $n$th root of nonzero $z$ has modulus:

$$ \boxed{ |w| = |z|^{1/n} }. $$

Roots of Unity

Every root of unity has modulus $1$:

$$ \boxed{ z^n=1 \Longrightarrow |z|=1 }. $$

Triangle Inequality

The modulus satisfies:

$$ \boxed{ |z+w| \leq |z|+|w| }. $$

Geometric Meaning of the Triangle Inequality

Traveling directly from the origin to $z+w$ cannot exceed the length obtained by traveling first along $z$ and then along a translated copy of $w$.

Reverse Triangle Inequality

$$ \boxed{ \big||z|-|w|\big| \leq |z-w| }. $$

Useful Consequence

$$ \boxed{ |z-w| \geq \big||z|-|w|\big| }. $$

Sum Is Not Generally Additive

In general:

$$ \boxed{ |z+w| \neq |z|+|w| }. $$

Equality occurs only under special directional conditions.

Simple Counterexample

Let:

$$ z=1, \qquad w=-1. $$

Then:

$$ |z+w|=0 $$

while:

$$ |z|+|w|=2. $$

Real-Part Bound

$$ \boxed{ |\operatorname{Re}(z)| \leq |z| }. $$

Imaginary-Part Bound

$$ \boxed{ |\operatorname{Im}(z)| \leq |z| }. $$

Component Bounds

If $z=a+bi$, then:

$$ \boxed{ |a|\leq|z|, \qquad |b|\leq|z| }. $$

Modulus Squared

The square of the modulus is often algebraically more convenient:

$$ \boxed{ |z|^2 = z\overline{z} }. $$

Why Squared Modulus Is Useful

It removes the square root and often simplifies algebraic manipulation, optimization, and inequalities.

Comparison of Magnitudes

To compare $|z|$ and $|w|$, one may compare their squares:

$$ \boxed{ |z|<|w| \iff |z|^2<|w|^2 }. $$

This works because both quantities are nonnegative.

Complex Plane Annulus

The condition:

$$ \boxed{ r<|z|<R } $$

describes an open annulus centered at the origin.

Shifted Disk

$$ \boxed{ |z-z_0|<r } $$

describes all complex numbers lying within distance $r$ of $z_0$.

Complex Analysis

The modulus provides the ordinary Euclidean metric on $\mathbb{C}$:

$$ \boxed{ d(z,w) = |z-w| }. $$

Convergence of Complex Sequences

A sequence $z_n$ converges to $z$ exactly when:

$$ \boxed{ |z_n-z| \to 0 }. $$

Continuity

Complex continuity is naturally expressed through modulus-based distance.

$$ \boxed{ |z-z_0|<\delta \Longrightarrow |f(z)-f(z_0)|<\varepsilon }. $$

Boundedness

A set $S\subseteq\mathbb{C}$ is bounded if there exists $M>0$ such that:

$$ \boxed{ |z|\leq M \text{ for every }z\in S }. $$

Absolute Convergence

A complex series:

$$ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z_n $$

converges absolutely if:

$$ \boxed{ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|z_n| } $$

converges.

Norm Interpretation

The complex modulus is a norm on the one-dimensional complex vector space $\mathbb{C}$ and corresponds to the Euclidean norm on $\mathbb{R}^2$.

$$ \boxed{ \|z\| = |z| }. $$

Norm Properties

The modulus satisfies:

  • $|z|\geq0$;
  • $|z|=0$ iff $z=0$;
  • $|cz|=|c||z|$;
  • $|z+w|\leq|z|+|w|$.

Modulus Is Not the Argument

The modulus describes radial distance. The argument describes angle.

$$ \boxed{ |z| \neq \arg z }. $$

Modulus Is Not the Real Part

For:

$$ z=3+4i, $$

one has:

$$ \operatorname{Re}(z)=3 $$

but:

$$ |z|=5. $$

Modulus Is Not the Imaginary Coefficient

Likewise:

$$ \operatorname{Im}(3+4i)=4 $$

while:

$$ |3+4i|=5. $$

Modulus vs. Absolute Value

For complex numbers, absolute value and modulus usually name the same quantity.

Modulus vs. Magnitude

Magnitude is a broader word used for size in many mathematical contexts. For complex numbers, it commonly refers to the modulus.

Modulus vs. Norm

The complex modulus is a specific norm. The word norm generalizes the magnitude concept to vectors, matrices, functions, and other mathematical objects.

Modulus vs. Modular Arithmetic

The word modulus has a completely different use in modular arithmetic. There, a modulus such as $n$ determines congruence classes:

$$ a\equiv b\pmod n. $$

The modulus of a complex number instead means:

$$ \boxed{ |a+bi| = \sqrt{a^2+b^2} }. $$

Context Determines Meaning

The phrase the modulus therefore requires contextual interpretation.

Common Error: Adding the Components

Incorrect:

$$ |3+4i|=3+4=7. $$

Correct:

$$ \boxed{ |3+4i|=5 }. $$

Common Error: Forgetting to Square Negative Components

For:

$$ z=-3+4i, $$

the correct computation is:

$$ |z| = \sqrt{(-3)^2+4^2} = \boxed{5}. $$

Common Error: Writing a Negative Modulus

A modulus is never negative.

$$ \boxed{ |z|\geq0 }. $$

Common Error: Forgetting the Square Root

The expression:

$$ a^2+b^2 $$

is $|z|^2$, not $|z|$.

$$ \boxed{ |z|^2=a^2+b^2 }. $$

Common Error: Confusing $z\overline{z}$ with $|z|$

Correct:

$$ \boxed{ z\overline{z} = |z|^2 }. $$

Common Error: Assuming Modulus Distributes over Addition

In general:

$$ \boxed{ |z+w| \neq |z|+|w| }. $$

Common Error: Forgetting the Triangle Inequality

The correct universal relation is:

$$ \boxed{ |z+w| \leq |z|+|w| }. $$

Common Error: Assuming the Modulus Determines the Complex Number

Infinitely many complex numbers can have the same modulus. Every point on:

$$ |z|=r $$

has the same magnitude $r$.

Common Error: Confusing Modulus with Argument

Modulus gives radial distance; argument gives angular direction.

Common Error: Confusing Complex Modulus with Congruence Modulus

In $|z|$, modulus means magnitude. In "modulo $n$," modulus means the integer determining a congruence system.

Common Error: Treating Modulus as Signed

The sign information of real and imaginary components affects direction but not the nonnegative distance value.

Common Error: Assuming Conjugation Changes Magnitude

Conjugation reflects across the real axis and preserves distance from the origin:

$$ \boxed{ |\overline{z}|=|z| }. $$

Common Error: Ignoring the Denominator Restriction in the Quotient Rule

The identity:

$$ \left| \frac{z}{w} \right| = \frac{|z|}{|w|} $$

requires $w\neq0$.

Common Misconception: The modulus of a complex number is not its real part, imaginary part, argument, or a remainder operation. It is the Euclidean magnitude: $|a+bi|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$. Geometrically, it is the distance from the complex number to the origin, and algebraically it satisfies $|z|^2=z\overline{z}$.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$|z|$ "The modulus of z," "the absolute value of z," or "the magnitude of z."
$|a+bi|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ "The modulus of a plus b i is the square root of a squared plus b squared."
$z\overline{z}=|z|^2$ "Z times its conjugate equals the modulus of z squared."
$|zw|=|z||w|$ "The modulus of a product equals the product of the moduli."
$|z-w|$ "The distance between z and w in the complex plane."
$|z|=r$ "Z has modulus r," geometrically a circle of radius r about the origin.
$z=re^{i\theta}$ "Z has modulus r and argument theta."

Vertical bars around a complex number are read in the same general way as ordinary absolute-value bars, but in complex analysis the word modulus is especially common.

Thus:

$$ |3+4i| $$

may be read:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"the modulus of three plus four i"} }. $$

The same notation can legitimately be called the absolute value of the complex number.

Defense Notation Advice: Use $|z|$ for complex modulus and reserve $\arg z$ for angular information. When $z=a+bi$, compute $\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$, not $a+b$ and not $a^2+b^2$. Distinguish complex modulus from the unrelated use of modulus in modular arithmetic. When manipulating products and quotients, remember that modulus is multiplicative but not generally additive.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Definition: Nonnegative magnitude of a complex number.
  • Rectangular Formula: $|a+bi|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$.
  • Geometric Meaning: Distance from the corresponding point to the origin.
  • Pythagorean Meaning: Hypotenuse determined by real and imaginary components.
  • Synonym: Complex absolute value.
  • Synonym: Complex magnitude.
  • Nonnegativity: $|z|\geq0$.
  • Zero Criterion: $|z|=0$ iff $z=0$.
  • Real Special Case: Complex modulus reduces to ordinary absolute value.
  • Pure Imaginary Case: $|bi|=|b|$.
  • Unit Imaginary: $|i|=1$.
  • Conjugate Relation: $z\overline{z}=|z|^2$.
  • Conjugate Invariance: $|\overline{z}|=|z|$.
  • Negation Invariance: $|-z|=|z|$.
  • Product Rule: $|zw|=|z||w|$.
  • Quotient Rule: $|z/w|=|z|/|w|$ for $w\neq0$.
  • Reciprocal Rule: $|1/z|=1/|z|$ for $z\neq0$.
  • Power Rule: $|z^n|=|z|^n$.
  • Scalar Rule: $|cz|=|c||z|$.
  • Distance: $|z-w|$ measures distance between complex numbers.
  • Circle: $|z|=r$ describes a circle centered at the origin.
  • Shifted Circle: $|z-z_0|=r$ describes a circle centered at $z_0$.
  • Open Disk: $|z-z_0|<r$.
  • Closed Disk: $|z-z_0|\leq r$.
  • Annulus: $r<|z-z_0|<R$.
  • Polar Form: $z=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)$ with $r=|z|$.
  • Exponential Form: $z=re^{i\theta}$ with modulus $r$.
  • Argument Distinction: Modulus gives size; argument gives direction.
  • Unit Circle: $|z|=1$.
  • Polar Multiplication: Moduli multiply while arguments add.
  • Polar Division: Moduli divide while arguments subtract.
  • De Moivre: Powers raise the modulus to the corresponding power.
  • Roots: $n$th roots of $z$ have modulus $|z|^{1/n}$.
  • Roots of Unity: All have modulus $1$.
  • Triangle Inequality: $|z+w|\leq|z|+|w|$.
  • Reverse Triangle Inequality: $\big||z|-|w|\big|\leq|z-w|$.
  • Real-Part Bound: $|\operatorname{Re}(z)|\leq|z|$.
  • Imaginary-Part Bound: $|\operatorname{Im}(z)|\leq|z|$.
  • Squared Modulus: $|z|^2=z\overline{z}$.
  • Comparison Shortcut: Nonnegative moduli can often be compared through their squares.
  • Metric: $d(z,w)=|z-w|$.
  • Complex Analysis: Modulus measures distances and sizes throughout the complex plane.
  • Sequence Convergence: $z_n\to z$ iff $|z_n-z|\to0$.
  • Continuity: Expressed through modulus-based distance.
  • Boundedness: Controlled by inequalities such as $|z|\leq M$.
  • Absolute Convergence: Uses convergence of $\sum|z_n|$.
  • Norm: Complex modulus is the standard norm on $\mathbb{C}$.
  • Euclidean Correspondence: Equivalent to the Euclidean norm on $\mathbb{R}^2$.
  • Modulus vs. Argument: Magnitude versus angle.
  • Modulus vs. Real Part: Magnitude is not merely the horizontal coordinate.
  • Modulus vs. Imaginary Part: Magnitude is not merely the vertical coordinate.
  • Modulus vs. Norm: Modulus is a specific norm for complex numbers.
  • Modulus vs. Modular Arithmetic: Complex modulus means magnitude; modular-arithmetic modulus defines congruence classes.

  • Common Error: Forgetting to square negative components.
  • Common Error: Reporting a negative modulus.
  • Common Error: Forgetting the square root.

  • Common Error: Assuming $|z+w|=|z|+|w|$ universally.
  • Common Error: Confusing modulus with argument.
  • Common Error: Assuming the modulus uniquely determines the complex number.
  • Common Error: Confusing complex modulus with the modulus in modular arithmetic.
  • Common Error: Assuming conjugation changes the modulus.
  • Common Error: Applying the quotient rule with a zero denominator.
  • Core Interpretation: The modulus of a complex number: $$ \boxed{ z=a+bi } $$ is: $$ \boxed{ |z| = \sqrt{a^2+b^2} }. $$ Geometrically, this is the distance from $z$ to the origin. Algebraically: $$ \boxed{ |z|^2 = z\overline{z} }, $$ and multiplicatively: $$ \boxed{ |zw| = |z||w| }. $$ In polar form: $$ \boxed{ z=|z|e^{i\theta} }, $$ the modulus supplies the radial magnitude while the argument supplies the angular direction.

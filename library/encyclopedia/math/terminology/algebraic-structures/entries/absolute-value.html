Absolute Value

Subfield: Algebraic & Analytic Structures

Etymology & Roots

The absolute value of a real number is its nonnegative distance from zero on the number line. It is written using vertical bars:

$$ \boxed{|x|}. $$

Thus:

$$ |5|=5 $$

and:

$$ |-5|=5. $$

The numbers $5$ and $-5$ lie on opposite sides of zero, but each is exactly five units from zero. Absolute value therefore removes information about direction or sign while preserving magnitude.

The adjective absolute emphasizes this sign-independent magnitude. In more advanced mathematics, related ideas appear under terms such as modulus and norm, which generalize the concept of magnitude or distance.

A common classroom description says that absolute value "makes a number positive." This is useful as an introductory shortcut but is not fully precise. The absolute value of zero is:

$$ |0|=0, $$

which is nonnegative rather than positive.

Operational Nuance

Distance Definition

Geometrically:

$$ \boxed{ |x| = \text{distance from }x\text{ to }0 }. $$

Because distance cannot be negative:

$$ \boxed{|x|\ge0}. $$

Piecewise Definition

Algebraically, absolute value is defined by:

$$ \boxed{ |x|= \begin{cases} x, & x\ge0,\\[4pt] -x, & x<0. \end{cases} } $$

This definition explains why:

$$ |-7|=-(-7)=7. $$

The expression $-x$ in the second case does not mean the result is negative. If $x<0$, then $-x>0$.

Absolute Value of an Expression

When the input is an expression, its sign may depend on the variable. For example:

$$ |x-3|. $$

If:

$$ x\ge3, $$

then:

$$ |x-3|=x-3. $$

But if:

$$ x<3, $$

then:

$$ |x-3|=-(x-3)=3-x. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ |x-3|= \begin{cases} x-3, & x\ge3,\\[4pt] 3-x, & x<3. \end{cases} } $$

Distance Between Two Numbers

Absolute value generalizes naturally from distance to zero to distance between any two real numbers:

$$ \boxed{ d(a,b)=|a-b| }. $$

For example, the distance between $-2$ and $5$ is:

$$ |-2-5| = |-7| = 7. $$

Absolute Value Equations

If:

$$ |x|=a, $$

with:

$$ a>0, $$

then $x$ lies exactly $a$ units from zero. Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ x=a \quad\text{or}\quad x=-a }. $$

For example:

$$ |x|=6 $$

has solutions:

$$ \boxed{x=\pm6}. $$

No Negative Absolute Value

An equation such as:

$$ |x|=-4 $$

has no real solution because:

$$ |x|\ge0 $$

for every real $x$.

Absolute Value Inequalities

The inequality:

$$ |x|0, $$

means that $x$ is less than $a$ units from zero:

$$ \boxed{ -a

By contrast:

$$ |x|>a $$

means that $x$ is more than $a$ units from zero:

$$ \boxed{ x<-a \quad\text{or}\quad x>a }. $$

Common Misconception: Absolute value does not mean "erase every minus sign." It applies to the value of the entire expression inside the bars and is fundamentally a statement about magnitude or distance.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$|x|$ "The absolute value of x."
$|-5|=5$ "The absolute value of negative five equals five."
$|a-b|$ "The absolute value of a minus b."
$|x|=7$ "The absolute value of x equals seven."
$|x|<7$ "The absolute value of x is less than seven."
$|x|\ge7$ "The absolute value of x is greater than or equal to seven."

The vertical bars:

$$ |\,\,| $$

are context-dependent mathematical notation. Around a real number or algebraic expression they commonly mean absolute value:

$$ |x|. $$

Around a complex number they commonly denote modulus:

$$ |z|. $$

Around a matrix they may denote a determinant:

$$ |A|, $$

and around a finite set they may denote cardinality:

$$ |S|. $$

Defense Notation Advice: Interpret vertical bars from context rather than assuming they always denote absolute value.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Nonnegativity: For every real $x$: $$ \boxed{|x|\ge0}. $$
  • Zero Condition: $$ \boxed{ |x|=0 \iff x=0 }. $$
  • Sign Symmetry: $$ \boxed{ |-x|=|x| }. $$
  • Square Relationship: $$ \boxed{ |x|^2=x^2 }. $$
  • Square-Root Identity: For real $x$: $$ \boxed{ \sqrt{x^2}=|x| }. $$ In general: $$ \sqrt{x^2}\ne x $$ when $x$ may be negative.
  • Product Property: $$ \boxed{ |ab|=|a||b| }. $$
  • Quotient Property: If $b\ne0$: $$ \boxed{ \left|\frac ab\right| = \frac{|a|}{|b|} }. $$
  • Powers: For integer $n\ge0$: $$ \boxed{ |x^n|=|x|^n }. $$
  • Triangle Inequality: $$ \boxed{ |a+b| \le |a|+|b| }. $$ This is one of the most important inequalities in mathematical analysis.
  • Reverse Triangle Inequality: $$ \boxed{ \big||a|-|b|\big| \le |a-b| }. $$
  • Distance from Zero: $$ \boxed{ |x|=d(x,0) }. $$
  • Distance Between Points: On the real number line: $$ \boxed{ d(a,b)=|a-b| }. $$
  • Equality Equation: For $a>0$: $$ \boxed{ |x|=a \iff x=\pm a }. $$
  • Translated Equation: $$ |x-h|=r $$ means that $x$ is exactly $r$ units from $h$, so: $$ \boxed{ x=h-r \quad\text{or}\quad x=h+r }. $$

  • Less-Than-or-Equal Inequality: $$ \boxed{ |x|\le a \iff -a\le x\le a }. $$
  • Greater-Than Inequality: $$ \boxed{ |x|>a \iff x<-a \text{ or } x>a }. $$
  • Translated Distance Inequality: $$ |x-h|
  • Interval Interpretation: $$ |x-h|\le r $$ describes the closed interval: $$ \boxed{ [h-r,h+r] }. $$
  • Absolute Value Function: The parent function: $$ \boxed{ f(x)=|x| } $$ has a V-shaped graph with vertex at: $$ (0,0). $$
  • Piecewise Form: $$ \boxed{ |x|= \begin{cases} x, & x\ge0,\\[4pt] -x, & x<0. \end{cases} } $$
  • Domain: For: $$ f(x)=|x|, $$ the domain is: $$ \boxed{\mathbb{R}}. $$
  • Range: The range is: $$ \boxed{[0,\infty)}. $$
  • Even Function: Since: $$ |-x|=|x|, $$ the function: $$ f(x)=|x| $$ is even and symmetric about the $y$-axis.
  • Continuity: The absolute value function is continuous for every real $x$.
  • Differentiability: For: $$ x\ne0, $$ $$ \frac{d}{dx}|x| = \begin{cases} 1, & x>0,\\ -1, & x<0. \end{cases} $$ The derivative does not exist at: $$ x=0 $$ because the left- and right-hand derivatives differ.
  • Sign Function Connection: For $x\ne0$: $$ \boxed{ \frac{d}{dx}|x| = \operatorname{sgn}(x) }. $$
  • Transformations: The graph: $$ y=a|x-h|+k $$ has vertex: $$ \boxed{(h,k)}. $$ The parameter $a$ controls vertical stretching, compression, and reflection.
  • Optimization: Absolute value naturally models objectives based on distance or error, such as minimizing: $$ |x-a|. $$
  • Absolute Error: If $x$ is an approximation to a true value $a$, then: $$ \boxed{ \text{absolute error}=|x-a| }. $$
  • Analysis: Expressions such as: $$ |f(x)-L|<\varepsilon $$ describe the distance between a function value and a proposed limit.
  • Limits: The $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ definition: $$ |f(x)-L|<\varepsilon $$ whenever: $$ 0<|x-a|<\delta $$ uses absolute value to express distance without referring separately to left and right directions.
  • Absolute Convergence: A series: $$ \sum a_n $$ converges absolutely when: $$ \boxed{ \sum|a_n| \text{ converges} }. $$
  • Complex Numbers: For: $$ z=a+bi, $$ the complex modulus is: $$ \boxed{ |z|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2} }. $$ This generalizes distance from zero to the complex plane.
  • Vectors: Absolute value generalizes to the idea of a norm. In $\mathbb{R}^n$, the Euclidean norm: $$ \|\mathbf{x}\| $$ measures the vector's distance from the origin.
  • Terminological Contrast: Absolute value measures the nonnegative magnitude of a real number. Modulus commonly names the corresponding magnitude of a complex number, while a norm generalizes magnitude to vectors and other mathematical objects. All are closely connected to the mathematical idea of distance.

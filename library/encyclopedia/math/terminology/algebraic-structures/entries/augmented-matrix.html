Augmented Matrix

Subfield: Algebraic & Analytic Structures

Etymology & Roots

An augmented matrix is a matrix formed by adjoining one matrix, vector, or collection of columns to another. In elementary linear algebra, the term most often refers to the matrix obtained by placing the constants from a system of linear equations beside its coefficient matrix.

A linear system written in matrix form as:

$$ A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b} $$

has augmented matrix:

$$ \boxed{ [A\mid\mathbf{b}] }. $$

The vertical bar visually separates the coefficient portion from the appended right-hand-side column. It is a notational divider rather than an arithmetic operation.

$$ \boxed{ \text{augmented matrix} = \text{coefficient data} + \text{adjoined right-hand-side data} }. $$

The adjective augmented means enlarged or supplemented. The original coefficient matrix is retained, but additional information needed to represent the equations is attached to it.

Operational Nuance

From a Linear System to an Augmented Matrix

Consider:

$$ \begin{aligned} 2x+3y&=7,\\ 4x-y&=5. \end{aligned} $$

The coefficient matrix is:

$$ A= \begin{pmatrix} 2&3\\ 4&-1 \end{pmatrix}. $$

The variable vector is:

$$ \mathbf{x}= \begin{pmatrix} x\\ y \end{pmatrix}, $$

and the constant vector is:

$$ \mathbf{b}= \begin{pmatrix} 7\\ 5 \end{pmatrix}. $$

Therefore:

$$ A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b} $$

becomes the augmented matrix:

$$ \boxed{ \left[ \begin{array}{cc|c} 2&3&7\\ 4&-1&5 \end{array} \right] }. $$

Rows Correspond to Equations

Each row of the augmented matrix represents one equation.

For example:

$$ \left[ \begin{array}{cc|c} 2&3&7 \end{array} \right] $$

represents:

$$ 2x+3y=7. $$

Columns Correspond to Variables

Within the coefficient portion, each column corresponds to a variable in a fixed order.

If the variable order is:

$$ x,\ y,\ z, $$

then the first three columns contain the coefficients of $x$, $y$, and $z$, respectively.

The final augmented column contains the constants on the right side of the equations.

Missing Terms Require Zero Coefficients

Suppose:

$$ \begin{aligned} x+2z&=5,\\ 3x-y+z&=4. \end{aligned} $$

The first equation has no $y$ term, so its $y$ coefficient is zero:

$$ \boxed{ \left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1&0&2&5\\ 3&-1&1&4 \end{array} \right] }. $$

Omitting the zero would shift the columns and destroy the correspondence between coefficients and variables.

The Vertical Bar

The bar in:

$$ [A\mid\mathbf b] $$

separates portions of the data:

$$ \boxed{ \underbrace{A}_{\text{coefficients}} \mid \underbrace{\mathbf b}_{\text{constants}} }. $$

The bar is not multiplication, division, determinant notation, or an additional column containing special numerical values.

General Form

A system of $m$ linear equations in $n$ variables:

$$ \begin{aligned} a_{11}x_1+\cdots+a_{1n}x_n&=b_1,\\ a_{21}x_1+\cdots+a_{2n}x_n&=b_2,\\ &\vdots\\ a_{m1}x_1+\cdots+a_{mn}x_n&=b_m \end{aligned} $$

has augmented matrix:

$$ \boxed{ \left[ \begin{array}{cccc|c} a_{11}&a_{12}&\cdots&a_{1n}&b_1\\ a_{21}&a_{22}&\cdots&a_{2n}&b_2\\ \vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\ a_{m1}&a_{m2}&\cdots&a_{mn}&b_m \end{array} \right] }. $$

Dimensions

If:

$$ A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n} $$

and:

$$ \mathbf b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}, $$

then:

$$ \boxed{ [A\mid\mathbf b] \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times(n+1)} }. $$

Augmenting by one right-hand-side vector adds one column.

Elementary Row Operations

Augmented matrices are especially useful because a system can be transformed using the three elementary row operations:

$$ \boxed{ R_i\leftrightarrow R_j } $$

swap two rows;

$$ \boxed{ R_i\to cR_i, \qquad c\ne0 } $$

multiply a row by a nonzero scalar; and:

$$ \boxed{ R_i\to R_i+cR_j } $$

add a scalar multiple of one row to another.

Why Row Operations Preserve Solutions

Each elementary row operation corresponds to an operation on the equations that preserves their common solution set.

Therefore row-equivalent augmented matrices represent equivalent systems:

$$ \boxed{ [A\mid\mathbf b] \sim [A'\mid\mathbf b'] } $$

where $\sim$ indicates row equivalence.

The Entire Row Must Be Changed

When a row operation is performed, it applies across the vertical bar as well.

For:

$$ R_2\to R_2-2R_1, $$

every entry in row $2$, including its constant entry, must be transformed.

$$ \boxed{ \text{row operations act on the entire augmented row} }. $$

Gaussian Elimination

Gaussian elimination uses row operations to transform an augmented matrix into row-echelon form.

For example:

$$ \left[ \begin{array}{cc|c} 1&2&5\\ 3&4&11 \end{array} \right] $$

can use:

$$ R_2\to R_2-3R_1 $$

to obtain:

$$ \left[ \begin{array}{cc|c} 1&2&5\\ 0&-2&-4 \end{array} \right]. $$

The second row gives:

$$ -2y=-4, $$

so:

$$ y=2. $$

Substituting into the first row gives:

$$ x+2(2)=5, $$

hence:

$$ \boxed{ x=1, \qquad y=2 }. $$

Row-Echelon Form

A matrix in row-echelon form has its nonzero rows arranged so that each leading entry lies to the right of the leading entry in the row above.

A typical augmented form is:

$$ \left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1&*&*&*\\ 0&1&*&*\\ 0&0&1&* \end{array} \right]. $$

This triangular structure supports back-substitution.

Reduced Row-Echelon Form

In reduced row-echelon form, every pivot is $1$ and is the only nonzero entry in its pivot column.

For a system with a unique solution, one may reach:

$$ \boxed{ \left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1&0&0&x_0\\ 0&1&0&y_0\\ 0&0&1&z_0 \end{array} \right] }. $$

The solution is then read directly from the final column.

Pivot Columns

A pivot position in the coefficient portion identifies a leading variable.

If every variable column contains a pivot and the system is consistent, the system has a unique solution.

Free Variables

A coefficient column without a pivot corresponds to a free variable.

In a consistent system, the presence of at least one free variable gives infinitely many solutions.

Inconsistent Row

A row of the form:

$$ \boxed{ \left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 0&0&0&c \end{array} \right], \qquad c\ne0 } $$

represents:

$$ 0=c, $$

which is impossible.

Therefore the system is inconsistent and has no solution.

Zero Row

A row:

$$ \left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 0&0&0&0 \end{array} \right] $$

represents the identity:

$$ 0=0. $$

It creates no contradiction and imposes no additional restriction.

Unique Solution

A consistent system with a pivot in every variable column has:

$$ \boxed{ \text{one solution} }. $$

Infinitely Many Solutions

A consistent system with one or more free variables has:

$$ \boxed{ \text{infinitely many solutions} }. $$

No Solution

A contradiction row gives:

$$ \boxed{ \text{no solution} }. $$

Rank Test for Consistency

A linear system:

$$ A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf b $$

is consistent exactly when:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{rank}(A) = \operatorname{rank}([A\mid\mathbf b]) }. $$

If augmenting by $\mathbf b$ increases the rank, the system is inconsistent.

Unique-Solution Rank Condition

For a system with $n$ variables, a unique solution occurs when:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{rank}(A) = \operatorname{rank}([A\mid\mathbf b]) = n }. $$

Infinite-Solution Rank Condition

Infinitely many solutions occur when:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{rank}(A) = \operatorname{rank}([A\mid\mathbf b]) < n }. $$

No-Solution Rank Condition

No solution occurs when:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{rank}(A) < \operatorname{rank}([A\mid\mathbf b]) }. $$

Homogeneous Systems

A homogeneous system has:

$$ A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf 0. $$

Its augmented matrix is:

$$ \boxed{ [A\mid\mathbf0] }. $$

A homogeneous system is always consistent because:

$$ \boxed{ \mathbf{x}=\mathbf0 } $$

is always a solution.

Nonhomogeneous Systems

If:

$$ \mathbf b\ne\mathbf0, $$

then:

$$ A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf b $$

is called nonhomogeneous.

Such a system may have no solution, one solution, or infinitely many solutions.

Geometric Interpretation

In two variables, each row represents a line. Row reduction does not preserve the individual equations as geometric objects, but it preserves their common solution set.

Therefore the augmented matrix efficiently tracks the intersection information that matters for solving the system.

Three-Variable Interpretation

In three variables, each linear equation usually represents a plane. The augmented matrix records the coefficients and offsets of those planes compactly.

Their common intersection may be:

  • a single point,
  • a line,
  • a plane in redundant cases,
  • or empty.

Augmenting with Multiple Right-Hand Sides

More generally, several columns may be appended:

$$ \boxed{ [A\mid B] }. $$

where $B$ may itself be a matrix rather than a single vector.

This is useful when the same coefficient matrix is used with multiple right-hand-side vectors.

Matrix Inversion by Augmentation

If $A$ is an invertible square matrix, its inverse can be found by augmenting it with the identity matrix:

$$ \boxed{ [A\mid I] }. $$

Row reduction is used to transform:

$$ [A\mid I] \longrightarrow [I\mid A^{-1}]. $$

This is another major use of augmented matrices beyond solving a single system.

Augmented Matrix versus Coefficient Matrix

The coefficient matrix contains only coefficients:

$$ \boxed{ A }. $$

The augmented matrix also contains the right-hand-side data:

$$ \boxed{ [A\mid\mathbf b] }. $$

Augmented Matrix versus Matrix Equation

The matrix equation:

$$ A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf b $$

explicitly displays multiplication of a coefficient matrix by an unknown vector. The augmented matrix:

$$ [A\mid\mathbf b] $$

suppresses the variable symbols so row operations can focus on the numerical structure.

Variables Are Not Stored in the Matrix

An augmented matrix usually contains coefficients and constants, not the variable symbols themselves.

The variable order must therefore be established before the matrix is constructed.

$$ \boxed{ \text{column meaning depends on the chosen variable order} }. $$

Equivalent Systems

Two augmented matrices related by elementary row operations correspond to systems with the same solution set.

Thus:

$$ \boxed{ \text{row equivalence preserves solutions} }. $$

Common Misconception

The augmented column is not treated independently during elimination. If a row is multiplied, swapped, or replaced, its entry on the right side of the vertical bar must undergo the same operation.

$$ \boxed{ \text{never stop a row operation at the augmentation bar} }. $$

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf b$ "A times x equals b."
$[A\mid\mathbf b]$ "The matrix A augmented by b," or "the augmented matrix A bar b."
$R_2\to R_2-3R_1$ "Replace row two by row two minus three times row one."
$R_1\leftrightarrow R_2$ "Interchange row one and row two."
$\operatorname{rank}(A)=\operatorname{rank}([A\mid\mathbf b])$ "The rank of A equals the rank of the augmented matrix."
$[A\mid I]\to[I\mid A^{-1}]$ "Row-reduce A augmented by the identity to the identity augmented by A inverse."

The phrase augmented matrix usually implies that the displayed matrix has been partitioned conceptually into related blocks. The most common elementary form is a coefficient matrix followed by one column of constants.

Defense Notation Advice: Establish the variable order before translating a system into matrix form, insert zeros for missing variables, and apply every row operation across the entire row—including all augmented columns.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Form: $$ \boxed{ [A\mid\mathbf b] }. $$
  • Matrix Equation: $$ \boxed{ A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf b }. $$
  • Coefficient Portion: $A$ contains the coefficients of the variables.
  • Augmented Column: $\mathbf b$ contains the right-hand-side constants.
  • Row Meaning: Each row represents one linear equation.
  • Column Meaning: Each coefficient column corresponds to one variable.
  • Missing Variable: A missing term requires a zero coefficient.
  • Dimensions: If $A$ is $m\times n$, then $[A\mid\mathbf b]$ is $m\times(n+1)$.
  • Row Swap: $$ \boxed{ R_i\leftrightarrow R_j }. $$
  • Row Scaling: $$ \boxed{ R_i\to cR_i, \qquad c\ne0 }. $$
  • Row Replacement: $$ \boxed{ R_i\to R_i+cR_j }. $$
  • Row Equivalence: Elementary row operations preserve the solution set.
  • Gaussian Elimination: Row reduction to echelon form for solving a system.
  • Gauss-Jordan Elimination: Continued row reduction to reduced row-echelon form.
  • Pivot Variable: A variable whose coefficient column contains a pivot.
  • Free Variable: A variable whose coefficient column lacks a pivot.
  • Contradiction Row: $$ \boxed{ [0\ \cdots\ 0\mid c], \qquad c\ne0 } $$ signals inconsistency.
  • Redundant Row: $$ \boxed{ [0\ \cdots\ 0\mid0] } $$ contributes no additional restriction.
  • Unique Solution: A consistent system with a pivot in every variable column.
  • Infinitely Many Solutions: A consistent system with at least one free variable.
  • No Solution: An inconsistent system containing a contradiction row after reduction.
  • Consistency Rank Test: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{rank}(A) = \operatorname{rank}([A\mid\mathbf b]) }. $$
  • Unique-Solution Rank Test: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{rank}(A) = \operatorname{rank}([A\mid\mathbf b]) = n }. $$
  • Infinite-Solution Rank Test: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{rank}(A) = \operatorname{rank}([A\mid\mathbf b]) < n }. $$
  • Inconsistency Rank Test: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{rank}(A) < \operatorname{rank}([A\mid\mathbf b]) }. $$
  • Homogeneous System: $$ \boxed{ [A\mid\mathbf0] } $$ is always consistent.
  • Multiple Right-Hand Sides: $$ \boxed{ [A\mid B] } $$ may append several columns at once.
  • Inverse-Matrix Method: $$ \boxed{ [A\mid I] \to [I\mid A^{-1}] } $$ when $A$ is invertible.
  • Geometric Use: Augmented matrices encode systems representing intersections of lines, planes, and higher-dimensional hyperplanes.
  • Coefficient-Matrix Contrast: $A$ contains only coefficients; $[A\mid\mathbf b]$ also contains the right-hand-side data.
  • Matrix-Equation Contrast: $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf b$ displays the variables explicitly; the augmented matrix suppresses them while preserving the system's coefficient structure.
  • Vertical-Bar Caution: The augmentation bar is a visual separator, not an operation.
  • Common Error: Do not omit zero coefficients for missing variables.
  • Common Error: Do not change the order of variables from one equation to another when forming the columns.
  • Common Error: Do not perform row operations on the coefficient side while leaving the augmented entries unchanged.
  • Terminological Contrast: Coefficient matrix stores the variable coefficients. Constant vector stores the right-hand-side values. Augmented matrix places these data together. Row reduction transforms the augmented matrix while preserving the system's solution set.

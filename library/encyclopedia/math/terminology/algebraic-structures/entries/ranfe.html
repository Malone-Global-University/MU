Range

Subfield: Foundations & Logic

Etymology & Roots

In function terminology, the range is the set of output values that a function actually produces from its domain.

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(f) = \{ f(x):x\in\operatorname{Dom}(f) \} }. $$

If:

$$ f:A\to B, $$

then the range is the image of $A$ under $f$:

$$ \boxed{ f(A) = \{ f(x):x\in A \} }. $$

The ordinary word range suggests extent or spread. In function language, it refers to the extent of output values attained by the mapping.

The term must be distinguished from domain, which contains allowable inputs, and from codomain, which is the declared target set of a function.

Operational Nuance

Domain-to-Range Structure

$$ \boxed{ x \longmapsto f(x) }. $$

The domain supplies inputs. The range consists of the outputs that actually occur.

Example

For:

$$ f(x)=x^2, \qquad x\in\mathbb{R}, $$

every output is nonnegative, so:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(f) = [0,\infty) }. $$

Range from a Finite Mapping

Suppose:

$$ f(1)=4, \qquad f(2)=7, \qquad f(3)=4. $$

Then:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(f) = \{4,7\} }. $$

Repeated output values are listed only once because the range is a set.

Range vs. Codomain

If:

$$ f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}, \qquad f(x)=x^2, $$

then the codomain is:

$$ \mathbb{R}, $$

but the range is:

$$ \boxed{ [0,\infty) }. $$

Range Is Contained in the Codomain

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(f) \subseteq \operatorname{Codomain}(f) }. $$

Equality occurs exactly when the function is onto its codomain.

Surjective Function

A function is surjective when:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(f) = \operatorname{Codomain}(f) }. $$

Finding Range from a Graph

On a Cartesian graph, the range consists of all $y$-coordinates attained by points on the graph.

$$ \boxed{ \text{range} = \text{vertical extent of the graph} }. $$

Horizontal-Line Scan

To determine whether a value $y=c$ belongs to the range, ask whether the horizontal line:

$$ y=c $$

intersects the graph at least once.

Range from an Equation

For:

$$ y=x^2+3, $$

since $x^2\geq0$:

$$ y\geq3. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range} = [3,\infty) }. $$

Vertex Form

For:

$$ f(x) = a(x-h)^2+k, $$

if $a>0$:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(f) = [k,\infty) }. $$

If $a<0$:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(f) = (-\infty,k] }. $$

Linear Function

A nonconstant real linear function:

$$ f(x)=mx+b, \qquad m\neq0, $$

with domain $\mathbb{R}$ has:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(f)=\mathbb{R} }. $$

Constant Function

For:

$$ f(x)=c, $$

on a nonempty domain:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(f)=\{c\} }. $$

Square-Root Function

$$ f(x)=\sqrt{x} $$

has real range:

$$ \boxed{ [0,\infty) }. $$

Cube-Root Function

$$ f(x)=\sqrt[3]{x} $$

has:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(f)=\mathbb{R} }. $$

Absolute-Value Function

$$ f(x)=|x| $$

has range:

$$ \boxed{ [0,\infty) }. $$

Exponential Function

For:

$$ f(x)=b^x, \qquad b>0, \quad b\neq1, $$

the real range is:

$$ \boxed{ (0,\infty) }. $$

Logarithmic Function

For:

$$ f(x)=\log_bx, \qquad b>0, \quad b\neq1, $$

the real range is:

$$ \boxed{ \mathbb{R} }. $$

Sine and Cosine

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(\sin x) = [-1,1] } $$
$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(\cos x) = [-1,1] }. $$

Tangent

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(\tan x) = \mathbb{R} }. $$

Transformations and Range

For:

$$ g(x)=af(x)+k, $$

the output values of $f$ are multiplied by $a$ and then shifted by $k$.

Example

Since $\sin x\in[-1,1]$:

$$ 3\sin x+2 \in [-1,5]. $$

Therefore:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range} (3\sin x+2) = [-1,5] }. $$

Restricted Domain Changes Range

Range depends on the domain. For example:

$$ f(x)=x^2 $$

on $\mathbb{R}$ has range $[0,\infty)$, but on:

$$ [-2,1] $$

the range is:

$$ \boxed{ [0,4] }. $$

Range of a Relation

The term also applies to relations. For a relation:

$$ R\subseteq A\times B, $$

the range is the set of second coordinates that actually occur:

$$ \boxed{ \{ y\in B: \exists x\in A \text{ such that } (x,y)\in R \} }. $$

Range of a Sequence

For a sequence $(a_n)$, the range may mean the set of values assumed by its terms:

$$ \boxed{ \{ a_n:n\in\mathbb{N} \} }. $$

Inverse Functions

If $f$ is invertible, the range of $f$ becomes the domain of $f^{-1}$:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Dom}(f^{-1}) = \operatorname{Range}(f) }. $$

Likewise:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(f^{-1}) = \operatorname{Dom}(f) }. $$

Range and Image

In many modern texts, range and image are used synonymously for the set of actual outputs.

Terminology Variation

Some textbooks use range differently or blur it with codomain. In precise function terminology, it is safest to distinguish:

$$ \boxed{ \text{range} = \text{actual outputs} } $$

from:

$$ \boxed{ \text{codomain} = \text{declared target set} }. $$

Common Error: Confusing Domain and Range

Domain concerns inputs; range concerns outputs.

Common Error: Assuming Range Equals Codomain

Equality holds only when every codomain value is actually attained.

Common Error: Reading Range Horizontally from a Graph

Horizontal extent gives domain; vertical extent gives range.

Common Error: Ignoring Domain Restrictions

The same formula can have different ranges when its domain changes.

Common Error: Listing Repeated Outputs Repeatedly

Range is normally a set, so duplicates are omitted.

Common Error: Assuming Every Endpoint Is Included

Endpoint inclusion depends on whether the function actually attains the value.

Example

For:

$$ f(x)=\frac{1}{x^2+1}, $$

the maximum $1$ is attained at $x=0$, while $0$ is approached but never attained. Thus:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(f) = (0,1] }. $$

Common Error: Using a Graph's Drawing Window as the Range

A displayed graph may show only part of a function. The mathematical range must be inferred from the function, its stated domain, and its full behavior.

Common Misconception: The range is not simply the set named on the output side of a function definition. It is the set of values the function actually reaches. The declared target is the codomain; the attained target values form the range.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$\operatorname{Range}(f)$ "The range of f."
$f(A)$ "The image of A under f."
$\{f(x):x\in A\}$ "The set of all f of x such that x belongs to A."
$[0,\infty)$ "All real values greater than or equal to zero."
$(-\infty,4]$ "All real values less than or equal to four."
$\operatorname{Range}(f)\subseteq B$ "The range of f is contained in the codomain B."

The phrase:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"find the range"} } $$

means determine every output value that can actually occur under the specified function and domain.

The phrase:

$$ \boxed{ \text{"the range is all real numbers"} } $$

may be written:

$$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(f)=\mathbb{R} }. $$

Defense Notation Advice: State or inspect the domain before determining the range. On a graph, read range vertically. Distinguish actual outputs from the codomain, and use interval endpoints carefully: brackets indicate attained finite endpoints, while parentheses indicate excluded finite endpoints. Infinity is never included as an endpoint.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Definition: Set of outputs actually attained by a function or relation.
  • Set Form: $\operatorname{Range}(f)=\{f(x):x\in\operatorname{Dom}(f)\}$.
  • Image Terminology: Often synonymous with the image of the domain.
  • Domain Contrast: Domain contains inputs; range contains outputs.
  • Codomain Contrast: Codomain is the declared target; range is what is actually reached.
  • Containment: Range is contained in the codomain.
  • Surjection: Range equals codomain.
  • Graph Interpretation: Range is the vertical extent of the graph.
  • Finite Relation: Range consists of distinct second-coordinate values that occur.
  • Quadratic Function: Range is determined by the vertex and opening direction.
  • Nonconstant Linear Function: Range is $\mathbb{R}$ on domain $\mathbb{R}$.
  • Constant Function: Range contains one value on a nonempty domain.
  • Square Root: Range $[0,\infty)$ for the parent real square-root function.
  • Cube Root: Range $\mathbb{R}$.
  • Absolute Value: Range $[0,\infty)$.
  • Exponential: Range $(0,\infty)$ for the standard positive-base nonconstant exponential.
  • Logarithm: Range $\mathbb{R}$.
  • Sine and Cosine: Range $[-1,1]$.
  • Tangent: Range $\mathbb{R}$.
  • Transformations: Vertical scaling, reflection, and translation transform the range.
  • Restricted Domain: May reduce or otherwise alter the range.
  • Inverse Function: Original range becomes inverse domain.
  • Sequence: Range may mean the set of values attained by sequence terms.
  • Relation: Range consists of second coordinates appearing in ordered pairs.
  • Interval Notation: Common for continuous real ranges.
  • Set-Builder Notation: Useful for structural definitions.
  • Common Error: Confusing range with domain.
  • Common Error: Confusing range with codomain.
  • Common Error: Reading range horizontally from a graph.
  • Common Error: Ignoring a restricted domain.
  • Common Error: Listing repeated outputs multiple times in a set.
  • Common Error: Including an endpoint that is approached but never attained.
  • Common Error: Treating the graphing-window boundary as a true range boundary.
  • Terminology Caveat: Some texts use range and codomain inconsistently, so precise definitions should be checked.
  • Core Interpretation: The range records what outputs actually occur: $$ \boxed{ \operatorname{Range}(f) = \{ f(x): x\in\operatorname{Dom}(f) \} }. $$ It depends on both the formula and the chosen domain. Geometrically, it is the set of vertical values reached by the graph; structurally, it is the image of the domain under the function. It should be distinguished carefully from the codomain, which may contain values the function never attains.

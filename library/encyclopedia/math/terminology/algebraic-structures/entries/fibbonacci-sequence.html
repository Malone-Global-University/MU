Fibonacci Sequence

Subfield: Algebraic & Analytic Structures

Etymology & Roots

The Fibonacci Sequence is an integer sequence in which each new term is obtained by adding the two preceding terms. One common convention begins:

$$ 0,\ 1,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 5,\ 8,\ 13,\ 21,\ldots $$

Another common convention begins:

$$ 1,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 5,\ 8,\ 13,\ldots $$

The sequence is named after Leonardo of Pisa, a medieval Italian mathematician later known as Fibonacci. His work helped transmit Hindu-Arabic arithmetic and related mathematical ideas into medieval European mathematics.

The sequence became associated with Fibonacci because a version of it appeared in his Liber Abaci in connection with a stylized rabbit-population problem. The recurrence itself, however, is not uniquely European in origin; related number patterns were studied in earlier Indian mathematics in connection with counting rhythmic patterns.

The modern term Fibonacci Sequence therefore refers to a recurrence that is historically broader than its European namesake, even though Fibonacci's name became the standard label in Western mathematical literature.

Operational Nuance

Using the zero-based convention:

$$ F_0=0, \qquad F_1=1, $$

and for:

$$ n\ge2, $$

the recurrence relation is:

$$ \boxed{ F_n=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2} }. $$

Thus:

$$ F_2=F_1+F_0=1+0=1, $$ $$ F_3=F_2+F_1=1+1=2, $$ $$ F_4=F_3+F_2=2+1=3, $$ $$ F_5=F_4+F_3=3+2=5. $$

The essential feature is therefore not the particular starting index, but the recurrence:

$$ \boxed{ \text{next term} = \text{previous term} + \text{term before that} }. $$

Recursive Nature

The Fibonacci Sequence is a classic example of a recursively defined sequence. Unlike an arithmetic or geometric sequence, the next term cannot generally be obtained from only the immediately preceding term by adding or multiplying a single constant.

Instead, two previous values are required:

$$ F_n=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}. $$

Closed Form

Although the sequence is naturally recursive, it also has a closed-form expression known as Binet's Formula:

$$ \boxed{ F_n = \frac{\varphi^n-\psi^n}{\sqrt5} } $$

where:

$$ \varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2} $$

and:

$$ \psi=\frac{1-\sqrt5}{2}. $$

Since:

$$ |\psi|<1, $$

the term $\psi^n$ becomes very small as $n$ grows. This explains why Fibonacci numbers are closely approximated by powers of the Golden Ratio:

$$ F_n\approx\frac{\varphi^n}{\sqrt5}. $$

Ratio of Consecutive Terms

The ratios:

$$ \frac{F_{n+1}}{F_n} $$

approach the Golden Ratio:

$$ \boxed{ \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{F_{n+1}}{F_n} = \varphi }. $$

Numerically:

$$ \frac21=2, $$ $$ \frac32=1.5, $$ $$ \frac53\approx1.667, $$ $$ \frac85=1.6, $$ $$ \frac{13}{8}=1.625, $$

and these values increasingly settle near:

$$ 1.6180339887\ldots $$

Common Misconception: The Fibonacci Sequence is not itself a geometric sequence. Its consecutive ratios are not constant; they merely approach $\varphi$ as $n$ increases.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$F_n$ "F sub n," or "the nth Fibonacci number."
$F_0=0$ "F sub zero equals zero."
$F_1=1$ "F sub one equals one."
$F_n=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}$ "F sub n equals F sub n minus one plus F sub n minus two."
$F_{n+1}/F_n$ "The ratio of consecutive Fibonacci numbers."
$F_n\approx\varphi^n/\sqrt5$ "F sub n is approximately phi to the nth power divided by the square root of five."

The notation:

$$ F_n $$

denotes a Fibonacci number, while:

$$ \{F_n\} $$

may be used to denote the entire Fibonacci sequence.

Defense Notation Advice: Always identify the indexing convention. Some authors define:

$$ F_0=0,\qquad F_1=1, $$

while others begin:

$$ F_1=1,\qquad F_2=1. $$

Both conventions produce the same ordered positive terms after the initial indexing shift, but formulas involving subscripts must be interpreted consistently.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Standard Recurrence: The Fibonacci recurrence is: $$ \boxed{ F_n=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2} }. $$ Two initial values are required to determine the sequence uniquely.
  • Zero-Based Convention: A common modern definition is: $$ F_0=0,\qquad F_1=1. $$ This produces: $$ 0,1,1,2,3,5,8,13,\ldots. $$
  • One-Based Convention: Another common convention is: $$ F_1=1,\qquad F_2=1, $$ producing: $$ 1,1,2,3,5,8,13,\ldots. $$
  • Golden Ratio: The consecutive-term ratio satisfies: $$ \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{F_{n+1}}{F_n} = \varphi. $$
  • Characteristic Equation: Seeking solutions of the form: $$ F_n=r^n $$ for the recurrence: $$ F_n=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2} $$ leads to: $$ r^2=r+1, $$ or: $$ r^2-r-1=0. $$ Its roots are: $$ \varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2} $$ and: $$ \psi=\frac{1-\sqrt5}{2}. $$
  • Binet's Formula: With the standard zero-based convention: $$ \boxed{ F_n = \frac{\varphi^n-\psi^n}{\sqrt5} }. $$
  • Nearest-Integer Approximation: Because $|\psi|<1$, for positive $n$ the Fibonacci number is obtained by rounding: $$ \frac{\varphi^n}{\sqrt5} $$ to the nearest integer.
  • Sum of the First Fibonacci Numbers: One useful identity is: $$ \boxed{ \sum_{k=0}^{n}F_k = F_{n+2}-1 }. $$
  • Sum of Squares: Another identity is: $$ \boxed{ \sum_{k=0}^{n}F_k^2 = F_nF_{n+1} }. $$
  • Cassini's Identity: Consecutive Fibonacci numbers satisfy: $$ \boxed{ F_{n+1}F_{n-1}-F_n^2 = (-1)^n }. $$
  • Divisibility: Fibonacci numbers possess rich divisibility properties. For example, if: $$ m\mid n, $$ then: $$ F_m\mid F_n. $$
  • Greatest Common Divisors: A deeper identity is: $$ \boxed{ \gcd(F_m,F_n) = F_{\gcd(m,n)} }. $$
  • Generating Function: The ordinary generating function for the Fibonacci numbers is: $$ \boxed{ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_nx^n = \frac{x}{1-x-x^2} }. $$ This connects the recurrence to algebraic manipulation of power series.
  • Matrix Form: Fibonacci numbers can be generated through: $$ \begin{pmatrix} F_{n+1}\\ F_n \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1\\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} F_n\\ F_{n-1} \end{pmatrix}. $$ Repeated matrix multiplication provides an efficient computational framework.
  • Fast Computation: Because the sequence can be represented by matrix powers or doubling identities, large Fibonacci numbers can be computed much faster than by naïve recursive evaluation.
  • Recursive Programming: The Fibonacci recurrence is a common introductory example in computer science. A direct recursive program mirrors the definition but repeats many calculations, making memoization, iteration, dynamic programming, and fast doubling useful improvements.
  • Counting Interpretations: Fibonacci numbers arise in combinatorial problems such as counting ways to tile a board using pieces of lengths $1$ and $2$, or counting certain binary strings subject to adjacency restrictions.
  • Pascal's Triangle: Fibonacci numbers can be obtained by summing entries along shallow diagonals of Pascal's Triangle.
  • Geometry: Squares whose side lengths are successive Fibonacci numbers can be arranged to form increasingly rectangular configurations. Their side ratios approach the Golden Ratio.
  • Fibonacci Spiral: Quarter-circle arcs drawn through successive Fibonacci squares create the commonly illustrated Fibonacci spiral. It approximates a golden spiral but is not exactly the same mathematical curve.
  • Nature: Fibonacci numbers and related ratios appear in mathematical models of certain branching, phyllotactic, and packing patterns. Their presence in nature should be assessed quantitatively rather than assumed from superficial visual resemblance.
  • Population Model: Fibonacci's famous rabbit problem generates the recurrence: $$ F_n=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2} $$ under idealized assumptions about reproduction. It is a mathematical model, not a realistic biological law.
  • Lucas Sequences: The Fibonacci Sequence belongs to a broader family of second-order linear recurrence sequences. The closely related Lucas numbers satisfy the same recurrence but use different initial values: $$ L_0=2,\qquad L_1=1. $$
  • Arithmetic vs. Geometric Contrast: Fibonacci numbers have neither a constant difference nor a constant ratio. Their structure comes from a second-order recurrence: $$ F_n=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}. $$
  • Terminological Contrast: The Fibonacci Sequence is the recursively defined sequence itself. A Fibonacci number is an individual term $F_n$. The Golden Ratio is the limiting ratio of consecutive Fibonacci numbers, while a Fibonacci spiral is a geometric construction based on Fibonacci-sized squares.

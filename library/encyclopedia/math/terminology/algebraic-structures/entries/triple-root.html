Triple Root

Subfield: Algebraic Structures

Etymology & Roots

A triple root is a root of a polynomial having multiplicity three. If $r$ is a triple root of $P(x)$, then:

$$ \boxed{ P(x)=(x-r)^3Q(x) } $$

where $Q(r)\neq0$. The word triple indicates that the corresponding linear factor occurs exactly three times.

Operational Nuance

Multiplicity Three

If:

$$ P(x)=(x-2)^3(x+1), $$

then:

$$ \boxed{x=2} $$

is a triple root.

Factor Criterion

A number $r$ is a triple root exactly when $(x-r)^3$ divides $P(x)$ but $(x-r)^4$ does not.

$$ \boxed{ (x-r)^3\mid P(x), \qquad (x-r)^4\nmid P(x) }. $$

Derivative Criterion

Over a field of characteristic $0$, a triple root satisfies:

$$ \boxed{ P(r)=0, \qquad P'(r)=0, \qquad P''(r)=0 } $$

while:

$$ \boxed{ P'''(r)\neq0 }. $$

This distinguishes multiplicity exactly three from higher multiplicity.

Graph Behavior

For a real polynomial, a root of odd multiplicity causes the graph to cross the $x$-axis. A triple root typically crosses while flattening at the intercept.

$$ y=(x-r)^3 $$

is the standard local model.

Sign Change

Because three is odd, the factor $(x-r)^3$ changes sign as $x$ passes through $r$. Thus, when the remaining factor is nonzero there, the polynomial changes sign across the root.

Comparison with Other Multiplicities

A simple root has multiplicity $1$, a double root has multiplicity $2$, and a triple root has multiplicity $3$:

$$ \boxed{ (x-r), \qquad (x-r)^2, \qquad (x-r)^3 }. $$

Odd multiplicities generally cross the axis; even multiplicities generally touch and turn.

Repeated Synthetic Division

If synthetic division by $x-r$ produces remainder zero three consecutive times but not a fourth, then $r$ is a triple root.

Common Misconception

A triple root is not three different roots having the same numerical value. It is one root counted three times according to polynomial multiplicity.

Written vs. Spoken Syntax

Symbolic Notation (Written) Professional Articulation (Spoken)
$(x-r)^3$ "The factor x minus r occurs with multiplicity three."
$P(r)=0$ "R is a root of P."
$m_r=3$ "R has multiplicity three."
$P'(r)=P''(r)=0$ "The first two derivatives also vanish at r."
$P'''(r)\neq0$ "The third derivative does not vanish, confirming multiplicity exactly three."
$y=(x-r)^3$ "The standard local shape of a triple root."

The phrases triple root, root of multiplicity three, and zero of multiplicity three usually refer to the same polynomial property.

Defense Notation Advice: Distinguish multiplicity from the number of distinct roots. To claim multiplicity exactly three, verify that $(x-r)^3$ divides the polynomial but $(x-r)^4$ does not.

Case Usage & Field Variations

  • Core Meaning: A polynomial root of multiplicity exactly three.
  • Factor Form: $P(x)=(x-r)^3Q(x)$ with $Q(r)\neq0$.
  • Divisibility: $(x-r)^3$ divides $P(x)$ but $(x-r)^4$ does not.
  • Derivative Test: In characteristic $0$, $P(r)=P'(r)=P''(r)=0$ and $P'''(r)\neq0$.
  • Graphing: A real polynomial typically crosses the axis while flattening near a triple root.
  • Sign Behavior: Odd multiplicity produces a sign change across the root when the remaining factor is nonzero.
  • Synthetic Division: Repeated zero remainders can reveal multiplicity.
  • Factoring: The repeated factor must appear three times.
  • Root Counting: A triple root contributes three to the polynomial's root count when roots are counted with multiplicity.
  • Common Error: Treating a triple root as three distinct roots.
  • Common Error: Assuming every axis crossing is a triple root.
  • Common Error: Checking only $P(r)=0$ without establishing multiplicity.
  • Common Error: Calling a root triple when the factor actually occurs four or more times.
  • Core Interpretation: A triple root is a repeated polynomial root occurring exactly three times: $$ \boxed{ P(x)=(x-r)^3Q(x), \qquad Q(r)\neq0 }. $$ Its odd multiplicity produces axis crossing, while its repeated nature creates characteristic flattening and derivative vanishing at the root.

Chicago Outfit Biographies

Chicago organized-crime history from Colosimo, Torrio, and Capone through national rackets, federal prosecutions, and later leadership.

Collection Overview

The Chicago Outfit developed from the vice and gambling enterprises of the early twentieth century into a durable organized-crime network associated with Johnny Torrio, Al Capone, Paul Ricca, Tony Accardo, Sam Giancana, and successive crews. These biographies trace the organization’s changing leadership, illicit markets, labor and political connections, Las Vegas operations, and major federal investigations.

This 25-person Chicago Outfit biography collection is complete as an editorial roster. Each filename below links directly to its corresponding HTML biography in the same directory. The pages are individually researched; their links will function on the public website when the files are installed together. Inclusion documents a historical association or research relevance and does not, by itself, establish formal Mafia induction, guilt in a particular offense, or an undisputed leadership title.

Editorial Standards

Collection status: The 25 biographical HTML files listed above have been prepared for the MGU Crime Encyclopedia. This directory is not an assertion that every biography has already been deployed to the public website. Each link is relative to chicago-outfit/index.html, and each corresponding biography should be placed in the same chicago-outfit/ directory.

  • Distinguish formal leadership, reported leadership, crew-level authority, membership, association, and outside cooperation. Roles may change over time.
  • Do not confuse an indictment, testimony, intelligence assessment, or press allegation with a proven criminal conviction.
  • When a conviction or sentence was reversed, modified, or vacated, explain the final procedural status rather than relying only on the original verdict.
  • Favor primary court decisions, federal records, legislative investigations, contemporaneous journalism, and established historical scholarship; disclose factual disputes.
  • Maintain one canonical biography per individual; recognizable nicknames appear in headings and the individual biography rather than in duplicate files.
  • Discuss extortion, corruption, and violence analytically, including their effects on victims, public institutions, and communities.

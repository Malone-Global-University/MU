Winter Hill Gang Biographies

Somerville origins, Boston-area racketeering, leadership succession, and the federal investigations that exposed the relationship between organized crime and corrupt law enforcement.

Collection Overview

The Winter Hill Gang was a Boston-area organized-crime network associated first with Somerville, Massachusetts, and later with operations in and around South Boston. The organization developed in the context of the region's mid-twentieth-century gang conflicts and participated in illegal gambling, loansharking, extortion, and other criminal markets. Its history spans the leadership of Howard “Howie” Winter and the later prominence of James “Whitey” Bulger and Stephen “The Rifleman” Flemmi.

Although included in the Irish-American Crime Syndicates collection because of its historical setting and major figures, the Winter Hill organization was not exclusively Irish-American. Membership, ethnicity, and organizational affiliation are separate questions and must be established for each subject individually.

This directory identifies three selected biographies, not a comprehensive roster. It distinguishes the earlier Somerville-centered leadership from the subsequent Bulger–Flemmi period and directs readers to individual biographical articles, judicial records, and official investigations. The biographies and organization history serve different editorial purposes.

Research Themes

  • Origins of the Somerville-centered criminal network and the Boston gang conflicts of the 1960s
  • Howie Winter's leadership and the transition following late-1970s prosecutions
  • Connections among Somerville, South Boston, the Killeen organization, and other Boston-area criminal groups
  • Illegal gambling, loansharking, extortion, and the social consequences of racketeering
  • The Bulger–Flemmi relationship with FBI personnel, investigative failures, and subsequent court proceedings
  • Differences among adjudicated facts, indictment allegations, testimony, and later cultural portrayals

Somerville origins and earlier leadership

  1. Howard “Howie” Winter

    Historical classification: Earlier leader. Winter was a central figure in the Somerville-based organization and led the Winter Hill network during much of the 1970s. His criminal prosecutions and imprisonment at the end of that decade form a major turning point in the history of the group's leadership.

    Main biography page: howie-winter.html

Bulger–Flemmi era and federal investigations

  1. James “Whitey” Bulger

    Historical classification: Senior member and later leader. Bulger became associated with Winter's organization after the South Boston gang conflicts of the early 1970s and emerged as a leading figure following Winter's imprisonment. His years as an FBI informant, long period as a fugitive, and later federal prosecution make his biography central to the study of both organized crime and institutional misconduct.

    Main biography page: james-whitey-bulger.html

  2. Stephen “The Rifleman” Flemmi

    Historical classification: Senior member, leadership figure, and FBI informant. Flemmi was connected to the organization across leadership periods and became a principal criminal associate of Bulger. His documented relationship with FBI personnel and his role in later criminal proceedings are essential to understanding the overlap between investigative intelligence, corruption, and violent racketeering.

    Main biography page: stephen-flemmi.html

Editorial Standards

Collection scope: Three selected biographies of major Winter Hill figures. Other documented figures, including James “Buddy” McLean, Joe McDonald, John Martorano, and additional associates, are part of the wider organizational history but are not represented as completed biographies in this directory.

  • Identity and affiliation: Distinguish members, leaders, criminal associates, informants, and law-enforcement contacts. An individual's involvement with an investigation does not establish gang membership.
  • Chronological precision: Separate Somerville origins, 1970s consolidation, later South Boston operations, and the federal investigations of the 1990s and afterward.
  • Ethnic precision: Do not assume every Winter Hill participant was Irish-American because of the collection under which this directory appears.
  • Legal distinctions: Separate allegations in indictments from convictions, judicial findings, sworn testimony, and matters not resolved by a court.
  • Source hierarchy: Prioritize court opinions and filings, U.S. Department of Justice and FBI oversight materials, verified reporting, and scholarly histories.
  • Cross-references: Maintain one canonical biography per individual; link across the Killeen Gang, Mullen Gang, other Boston collections, and organizational-history pages where supported.
  • Historical ethics: Document victims, coercion, public corruption, and institutional accountability without romanticizing criminal violence.

Research Starting Points

These are evidence-based starting points for further biographical research. Their dates, procedural contexts, and evidentiary limits should be evaluated in the individual articles; a cited indictment is not itself a judgment of guilt.

  • U.S. Court of Appeals for the First Circuit — Rakes v. United States (2006) — judicial discussion of the Winter Hill organization, racketeering, and the consequences of FBI agent John Connolly's relationship with Bulger and Flemmi.
  • United States v. Salemme, 91 F. Supp. 2d 141 (D. Mass. 1999) — detailed judicial findings concerning FBI informants, organized-crime investigations, and the conduct of federal agents.
  • U.S. Department of Justice, Office of the Inspector General — Confidential Informant Case Study: Bulger and Flemmi — official examination of informant handling and institutional failures.
  • The Boston Globe — Howie Winter retrospective (2020) — biographical reporting on Winter's leadership and the transition to Bulger's organization.

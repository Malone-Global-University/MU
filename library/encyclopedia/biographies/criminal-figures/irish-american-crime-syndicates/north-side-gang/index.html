North Side Gang Biographies

Leadership, bootlegging competition, violent succession, and the history of Chicago's multiethnic North Side syndicate during Prohibition.

Collection Overview

The North Side Gang was a Prohibition-era criminal organization centered in Chicago's North Side. Its leadership is most closely associated with Dean O'Banion, Earl “Hymie” Weiss, Vincent “The Schemer” Drucci, and George “Bugs” Moran. The organization was often characterized as Irish-led, but its membership and leadership were multiethnic; ethnicity should not be inferred from the name of this MGU collection.

The group's historical importance lies in the competition over illicit alcohol distribution, the escalation of conflicts with rival Chicago syndicates, and the 1929 St. Valentine's Day Massacre. This is a biographical directory for four selected subjects rather than an exhaustive membership list or a substitute for the gang's organizational history.

Each entry below is intended to lead to one canonical biography. Historical rank, involvement in specific crimes, and accounts of responsibility for violence should be evaluated against contemporaneous records and reliable later scholarship.

Formation and early succession

  1. Dean O'Banion

    Historical classification: Founding-era leader. A principal figure in the organization of Chicago's North Side bootlegging interests; his 1924 killing became a key event in the escalating conflict among the city's Prohibition syndicates.

    Main biography page: dean-obanion.html

  2. Earl “Hymie” Weiss

    Historical classification: Successor and leading organizer. A prominent O'Banion associate who emerged as a principal North Side leader after 1924; his 1926 death was another turning point in the group's succession history.

    Main biography page: hymie-weiss.html

Later leadership and the gang wars

  1. Vincent “The Schemer” Drucci

    Historical classification: Senior member and leadership figure. A leading participant in the North Side organization, particularly during the period of reorganization and conflict after Weiss's death; Drucci died in 1927.

    Main biography page: vincent-drucci.html

  2. George “Bugs” Moran

    Historical classification: Later leader. The North Side syndicate's best-known surviving leader during its late-1920s rivalry with the Capone organization. Seven men associated with his operation were killed in the St. Valentine's Day Massacre of February 14, 1929; Moran was not among the victims.

    Main biography page: bugs-moran.html

Editorial Standards

Collection scope: Four selected leadership biographies. The page does not claim that these are the organization's only significant members.

  • Membership and leadership: Identify leadership claims by period; do not equate an associate, ally, or rival with a documented member.
  • Ethnic precision: The North Side Gang was multiethnic. Do not classify every subject in this collection as Irish-American.
  • Evidence: Prefer archival criminal records, contemporaneous journalism, reputable historical scholarship, and carefully attributed investigative accounts.
  • Legal precision: Distinguish documented actions from accusations, suspicions, and accounts repeated in later popular culture.
  • Succession: Explain overlapping influence and uncertainty rather than assigning unsupported exact dates to every leadership transition.
  • Cross-references: Keep one canonical biography per person and link to relevant Chicago Outfit, Prohibition, and organizational-history articles as appropriate.

Linking status: The four biography links above use the established MGU filenames intended for this folder. They should resolve after the corresponding HTML biographies have been placed beside this index.html; live-site publication was not independently confirmed when this directory was prepared.

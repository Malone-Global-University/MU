Touhy Gang Biographies

Roger Touhy, Chicago-area Prohibition rackets, underworld rivalry, and the contested John Factor kidnapping prosecution.

Collection Overview

The Touhy Gang was a Chicago-area criminal enterprise identified with Roger Touhy and the illicit beer market during national Prohibition (1920–1933). Its activities centered on Chicago and the northwestern Cook County area, where access to local distribution networks created competition with the Capone syndicate. Federal investigative accounts describe an organization involved in violent criminal activity as well as illicit alcohol operations.

The group's history extends beyond the end of Prohibition into the kidnapping prosecution involving John “Jake the Barber” Factor, lengthy prison sentences, and a 1942 prison escape. The Factor case became deeply disputed. A federal district court later accepted Touhy's claims of serious wrongdoing, but a federal appellate court reversed the order granting relief on procedural grounds. The case therefore requires careful separation of original conviction, later findings, appellate outcome, and the circumstances of his eventual release.

This page indexes one assigned MGU biography, Roger Touhy. It is a selected biographical directory, not an exhaustive gang membership roll. Other figures named in FBI accounts are identified as research subjects rather than presented as completed MGU profiles.

Principal Biography

  1. Roger “The Terrible” Touhy

    Historical classification: Prohibition-era criminal organizer and namesake leader of the Touhy Gang. Touhy built an illicit liquor enterprise in the Chicago area that federal investigators described as a rival to the Capone organization. His later historical notoriety rested in substantial part on prosecutions and a lengthy, contentious imprisonment rather than on Prohibition operations alone.

    Touhy was acquitted in the William Hamm kidnapping case and subsequently prosecuted in Illinois in connection with the alleged kidnapping of John Factor. After a first jury failed to agree, a second trial resulted in a 1934 conviction and a 99-year prison sentence. He maintained that the Factor kidnapping story was fabricated. The conviction generated extensive post-conviction litigation, including the federal appellate decision United States ex rel. Touhy v. Ragen (1955).

    Touhy participated in an October 9, 1942 prison escape and was recaptured with Basil Banghart and Edward Darlak on December 29, 1942. He was released in 1959 after lengthy confinement and was shot to death in Chicago that December. Responsibility for the killing should not be presented as conclusively established without evidence from a specific investigation or adjudication.

Historical Record and Organizational Context

Prohibition and organization: Federal investigative histories place the Touhy organization within the illegal beer business in northwest Cook County and Chicago during the late Prohibition years. The extent and structure of the enterprise should be reconstructed from particular incidents, arrests, and corroborated accounts rather than from later claims of territorial control alone.

Relations with the Capone syndicate: The Touhy network competed with the Capone organization in the Chicago-area liquor economy. The two groups should be treated as separate organizations; proximity, rivalry, and potential encounters do not make Touhy a member of the Chicago Outfit or the North Side Gang.

1933–1934 investigations: Following an acquittal in the William Hamm kidnapping proceeding, Touhy faced Illinois proceedings involving John Factor. The 1934 conviction and original 99-year sentence are documented in the appellate record. The circumstances of the Factor affair remain the subject of a substantial historical and legal dispute; the conviction cannot be used as the sole source for resolving what actually happened.

Prison escape and recapture: On October 9, 1942, Touhy and other inmates escaped from the Illinois penitentiary at Joliet. The FBI began investigating under wartime Selective Service authority and ultimately located Touhy and key associates in Chicago. The Bureau records his recapture on December 29, 1942. The escape produced a separate criminal conviction and became consequential in later habeas litigation.

Disputed conviction and appeals: In post-conviction proceedings, federal District Judge John P. Barnes concluded that the Factor episode had been contrived and ordered Touhy's release. In 1955, the Seventh Circuit reversed the district court's order because Touhy had not exhausted available Illinois remedies concerning his separate escape-related conviction. That appellate decision did not finally adjudicate the underlying kidnapping account as true or false. MGU biographies must not describe the 1955 appeal as a definitive factual exoneration or treat the lower-court findings as though they had survived unmodified appellate review.

Final years: Touhy was paroled in 1959, after spending approximately a quarter-century imprisoned. He was killed in Chicago on December 16, 1959. Contemporary reports recorded speculation over the perpetrators; such speculation should remain attributed rather than stated as judicially established fact.

Editorial Standards

Publication scope: This directory includes one assigned biography link. The target HTML is intended to be placed beside this index.html; its live availability has not been independently verified.

  • Affiliation: Distinguish gang leadership, associates, prison escape participants, rival syndicates, and persons involved in criminal proceedings.
  • Procedural precision: Keep the Hamm acquittal, Factor conviction, later court findings, appeal, parole, and escape conviction separate.
  • Contested claims: Attribute claims of a staged kidnapping or deliberate framing to the relevant court, witness, or source; do not treat conflicting accounts as equally proven.
  • Institutional sources: Use court opinions, trial records, contemporary newspapers, FBI archives, and peer-reviewed historical research as appropriate.
  • Source perspective: Read early FBI accounts as institutional records that may carry investigative assumptions and period-specific terminology.
  • Canonical biography: Maintain one roger-touhy.html within this gang directory, with links from related organizations instead of duplicate biographies.
  • Historical responsibility: Avoid romanticizing coercion, violence, prison escapes, or the persons associated with them.

Research Starting Points

These sources provide an initial research framework, including perspectives that disagree on crucial aspects of the Factor affair. Each detailed claim in the individual biography should be supported by its own source register.

  • Federal Bureau of Investigation — “Roger ‘The Terrible’ Touhy” — institutional history of the gang, the 1934 convictions, the 1942 prison escape, and recapture.
  • Federal Bureau of Investigation — “A Byte Out of History: The Hunt for Roger ‘The Terrible’ Touhy and His Gang” — concise account of the October 1942 escape and December arrests.
  • United States ex rel. Touhy v. Ragen, 224 F.2d 611 (7th Cir. 1955) — appellate record distinguishing the district court's findings from the exhaustion-of-remedies holding.
  • TIME — “Death on the Steps” (December 28, 1959) — contemporary reporting on Touhy's release, disputed conviction, and killing; distinguish reported suspicions from findings of fact.

Valley Gang Biographies

Chicago's West Side underworld, Prohibition-era bootlegging, the Druggan–Lake partnership, and the record of criminal enterprise and institutional corruption.

Collection Overview

The Valley Gang was a Chicago West Side criminal organization with roots in the city's earlier street-gang environment. During Prohibition, the organization became closely identified with Terrence “Terry” Druggan and Frank “Frankie” Lake, partners whose bootlegging enterprise acquired substantial notoriety in the 1920s. Contemporary court proceedings document both men's violations of federal Prohibition injunctions and the controversy surrounding their treatment while imprisoned in Cook County Jail.

The gang's history is best understood in the setting of Chicago's competing liquor-distribution networks, contested local territories, political patronage, and the development of larger criminal alliances. Its placement in the Irish-American Crime Syndicates collection describes a historical organizational tradition, not a claim that every participant was Irish-American. A 1936 federal investigative memorandum identifies Druggan and Lake together under the heading “Valley Gang (West Side),” while also listing other individuals associated with the organization.

This page is the biographical directory for two selected figures, not a comprehensive roster and not the gang's organizational history. Each person has one canonical biographical entry, linked below; disputed interpretations of specific events, alliances, and criminal responsibility belong in the full articles with appropriate sourcing.

Research Themes

  • Transition from Chicago's West Side street-gang environment to organized Prohibition-era liquor distribution
  • Druggan and Lake's partnership, division of responsibilities, and relationships with other Chicago syndicates
  • Federal enforcement of the National Prohibition Act and the distinction between injunction violations and other criminal charges
  • The Cook County Jail affair, illicit privileges, and the institutional accountability questions examined in federal court
  • Comparison of contemporaneous judicial findings and investigative records with later popular accounts of bootlegging wealth

Druggan–Lake leadership and biographies

  1. Terrence “Terry” Druggan

    Historical classification: Leading Valley Gang figure and bootlegging entrepreneur. Druggan was a central participant in the Druggan–Lake partnership. In July 1924, a federal court found him guilty of violating a Prohibition injunction; his subsequent imprisonment and unauthorized excursions from Cook County Jail became a focus of judicial inquiry in 1925. His biography should distinguish established court findings from later stories about his wealth and criminal influence.

    Main biography page: terry-druggan.html

  2. Frank “Frankie” Lake

    Historical classification: Co-leader and principal business partner. Lake worked alongside Druggan in the Prohibition-era liquor business and appeared with him in the relevant federal proceedings. The court record documents Lake's injunction violation and later early release from Cook County Jail. His biography should examine the joint enterprise while preserving Lake's individual chronology and the limits of the available evidence.

Historical Record and Context

Primary-source identification: A 1936 Bureau of Investigation memorandum, preserved in the FBI's Al Capone records, explicitly names Terry Druggan and Frankie Lake in its Valley Gang roster. The list is a historical investigative compilation, not a judicial determination of every person's membership or rank. Other names in the memorandum are not added here as biographies merely because they appear in that roster.

Documented judicial episode: In United States v. Hoffman, 13 F.2d 269 (N.D. Ill. 1925), the court recorded that Druggan and Lake had been found guilty in July 1924 of violating a federal Prohibition injunction and sentenced to imprisonment and fines. The 1925 opinion focused on the responsibilities of jail officials after both men received unauthorized departures or special treatment. Its findings establish a substantial documentary basis for studying the intersection of illicit wealth, detention practices, and public corruption.

Source interpretation: Contemporary newspaper descriptions of Druggan and Lake as wealthy bootleggers supply historical perspective, but reported sums, sensational details, and narratives of gang alliances require comparison with court records and independent scholarship. The organization's full history belongs in the linked Crime Encyclopedia organizational article.

Editorial Standards

Collection scope: Two selected Valley Gang leadership biographies. Additional figures appear in historical investigative rosters, but this page does not imply that their biography articles have been completed.

  • Affiliation and rank: Differentiate leaders, active members, associates, business partners, and outside allies. A shared commercial interest alone does not establish gang membership.
  • Chronology: Distinguish earlier street-gang activity, the 1920s bootlegging partnership, judicial proceedings, and later developments without collapsing them into a single period.
  • Legal precision: Separate convictions, judicial findings, testimony, allegations, press accounts, and unverified gang folklore. The 1925 Hoffman case principally adjudicated the conduct of jail officials.
  • Ethnic precision: Do not infer any individual's ethnicity solely from the Irish-American collection classification.
  • Evidence: Give priority to court opinions, contemporaneous records, official investigative correspondence, and credible historical scholarship; note conflicts among sources.
  • Institutional history: Cover the consequences of organized criminal activity and official corruption without romanticizing those activities.
  • Canonical biographies: Store one biography for each subject and cross-link from related gang or organizational entries rather than duplicate files.

Linking status: The two biography links follow the MGU filenames assigned to this directory. The corresponding HTML biographies must be placed alongside this index.html; their publication at the final paths has not been independently confirmed.

Research Starting Points

The sources below document the partnership, the legal and institutional episode, and the identification of Valley Gang figures. Every full biography should maintain a separate source register and distinguish a historical investigator's list from an adjudicated membership finding.

  • United States v. Hoffman, 13 F.2d 269 (N.D. Ill. 1925) — federal judicial opinion recording Druggan's and Lake's Prohibition injunction violations and examining their treatment in Cook County Jail.
  • FBI Vault — Al Capone, Part 9, 1936 Chicago gang roster — archived investigative memorandum listing the Valley Gang on Chicago's West Side and naming Druggan and Lake together.
  • The Abilene Morning Reporter, December 20, 1925, p. 36 — contemporary press coverage of Druggan and Lake; read with awareness of its sensational reporting style and limitations.
  • Chicago Collections Consortium — Chicago Crime and Punishment: Gangs — archival discovery guide for locating additional Chicago organized-crime source materials.

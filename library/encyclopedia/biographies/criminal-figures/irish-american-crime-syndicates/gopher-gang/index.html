Gopher Gang Biographies

Owney Madden, Hell's Kitchen street gangs, and the changing criminal economy of Manhattan's West Side.

Collection Overview

The Gopher Gang was an Irish-associated street-gang organization centered on Hell's Kitchen on Manhattan's West Side in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. Its setting included tenement housing, saloons, West Side railroad facilities, and overlapping neighborhood criminal markets. The gang's formation and leadership were not necessarily uniform across its history, and retrospective membership claims deserve careful evaluation.

Owen Vincent “Owney” Madden became one of the best-known Gopher leaders. Born in England to Irish parents, he grew up in Hell's Kitchen, rose in the Gophers, and was convicted of manslaughter in 1915. He served time in Sing Sing before his parole in early 1923. His later activities in bootlegging and entertainment belonged to a different Prohibition-era network rather than to an unchanged Gopher organization.

This directory currently includes one assigned MGU biography, Owney Madden. It is a selected biography index, not an exhaustive roster. Other historically reported participants appear as research leads only, without fabricated links to unpublished profiles.

  • Owney Madden's early criminal career, 1915 manslaughter conviction, and imprisonment through 1923

  • The distinction between Madden's Gopher affiliation and his later associations with Big Bill Dwyer and Frank Costello
  • Contemporary reporting, legal outcomes, historical scholarship, and the limits of later underworld mythology

Principal Biography

  1. Owen Vincent “Owney” Madden

    Historical classification: Gopher Gang leader; subsequently an independent Prohibition-era bootlegger and criminal entrepreneur. Madden was born in Leeds, England, in 1891 to Irish parents and grew up in Hell's Kitchen. Historical accounts identify him as a leading figure in the Gophers, whose territory and methods were closely tied to the older West Side street-gang environment.

    He was convicted of manslaughter in 1915, incarcerated at Sing Sing, and paroled in early 1923. In the 1920s his later business activity included illicit liquor distribution, breweries, nightclubs, and the Cotton Club. His connections with Big Bill Dwyer and Frank Costello represent a subsequent criminal network. Their roles in that later enterprise do not make them Gopher members.

    Madden's biography should distinguish documented proceedings from attributed murders and gang folklore, identify the chronology of his organizational affiliations, and cover his later life in Hot Springs, Arkansas separately from the Gopher years.

    Main biography page: owney-madden.html

Other historical names for research: Accounts identify figures known as “One Lung” Curran, “Happy Jack” Mulraney, “Stumpy” Malarkey, and “Mallet” Murphy in the Gopher environment. Their identities, leadership dates, and specific activities require individual verification. They are not presented here as completed MGU biography pages.

Historical Record and Organizational Context

Neighborhood environment: The Gophers emerged amid the social and economic pressures of Manhattan's West Side. The area's tenement blocks, railroad yards, and commercial streets were part of the gang's operating environment. This history should not stereotype Hell's Kitchen residents or Irish immigrant communities as criminals.

Formation and succession: Historical surveys describe West Side gangs before Madden's prominence. Their leadership and territorial control shifted over time. Madden should be identified as a prominent later leader, not automatically as the organization's original founder.

Gang violence and rivalries: Sources link the Gophers with theft, intimidation, and conflict with the Hudson Dusters and other street organizations. Individual incidents, deaths, and alleged responsibility should be tied to contemporaneous reports or court records rather than repeated from sensational histories without corroboration.

Imprisonment and institutional decline: Madden's 1915 conviction marks a clear break in his personal history. When he returned in 1923, the pre-Prohibition Gophers had largely dispersed, while illicit liquor markets created new business opportunities.

Later criminal enterprises: Madden's partnership with Big Bill Dwyer and Frank Costello belongs to the Prohibition period and should be cross-linked instead of merged into the Gopher membership register. Similarly, the later Westies occupied the broader Hell's Kitchen setting but constituted a distinct organization, separated by decades.

Editorial Standards

Publication scope: This page indexes one assigned biography filename. The relative link points to an HTML file intended to sit beside this index.html; its live publication has not been independently verified.

  • Affiliation: Distinguish Gopher members from rivals, associates, and later Prohibition business partners.
  • Chronology: Keep the early street-gang years separate from Madden's imprisonment and post-1923 operations.
  • Documented status: Differentiate allegations, arrests, court decisions, convictions, parole, and retrospective accounts.
  • Sources: Prioritize archival materials and contemporaneous reporting; attribute contested gang anecdotes.
  • Membership: Do not infer a complete roster from nicknames or stories reproduced in later compilations.
  • Canonical biography: Retain one owney-madden.html and cross-link it from other directories, without creating duplicate person articles.
  • Historical context: Explain the human impact of organized violence without romanticizing criminal activity.

Research Starting Points

The following historical and institutional materials provide starting points, but no single source should be treated as a verified full gang membership list. Individual biographies require their own source registers.

  • Encyclopedia of Arkansas — Owen Vincent Madden (1891–1965) — biographical chronology of Gopher leadership, manslaughter conviction, parole, Prohibition business, and the Arkansas years.
  • David Hill, The Vapors (2020), publisher edition — research on Madden, Hot Springs, and the evolution of his criminal enterprises.
  • Donald L. Miller, “Sodom on the Hudson” (Lafayette College, 2011) — contextual research on the later Madden–Dwyer–Costello bootlegging network.
  • Historical Perspectives, Inc., 34th Street Rezoning Project: Phase 1A Archaeological Assessment (1989), NYC archive — urban historical context and retrospective references to West Side gangs.
  • The Irish Story — Owney Madden and the Gopher years — secondary overview useful for research leads, subject to corroboration.

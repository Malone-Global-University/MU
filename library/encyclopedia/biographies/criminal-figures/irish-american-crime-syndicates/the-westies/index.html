The Westies Biographies

Jimmy Coonan, Mickey Featherstone, Hell's Kitchen racketeering, and the federal cases that exposed a Manhattan organized-crime enterprise.

Collection Overview

The Westies, also identified in federal court records as Coonan's Crew, the Westside Guys, or the Westside Irish Mob, were an organized criminal enterprise associated with Manhattan's Hell's Kitchen neighborhood. Federal appellate proceedings described the organization as emerging during the mid-1960s when James “Jimmy” Coonan and his associates challenged the influence of Michael “Mickey” Spillane. The organizations were rivals; the Westies should not be treated as merely an alternative name for Spillane's earlier gang.

By the 1970s the Coonan-led group had established a significant position in the area's illicit economy. Evidence summarized by the Second Circuit addressed loansharking, narcotics distribution, labor-related extortion, waterfront racketeering, and violent enforcement. The appellate record also described a late-1970s alliance with the Gambino crime family. These activities are historical subjects for examining coercion and its consequences for neighborhood residents, workers, businesses, and victims, not accomplishments to celebrate.

This directory currently features two MGU biographical profiles: Coonan, whom the federal appellate court identified as the enterprise's leader, and Francis “Mickey” Featherstone, identified as his second-in-command by the mid-1970s and subsequently a government witness. The entries are a selected biography directory, not an exhaustive membership register. The separate Crime Encyclopedia organization collection provides the proper location for the full institutional history of the Westies.

Research Themes

Principal Biographies

  1. James “Jimmy” Coonan

    Historical classification: Westies founder and principal leader; federal racketeering defendant. The federal appellate record locates the Coonan enterprise's origins in a mid-1960s challenge to Mickey Spillane's existing influence in Hell's Kitchen. After Coonan's group prevailed, it developed racketeering operations involving loansharking, narcotics, labor-related extortion, and the West Side waterfront. In the late 1970s, the organization also entered into an arrangement with the Gambino family, according to the evidence recounted in federal court.

    Coonan was one of the defendants in the major 1988 Westies racketeering prosecution. His biography should distinguish the earlier struggle with Spillane from later gang conflicts, and should identify the specific charges, convictions, sentences, and appellate proceedings using their corresponding court records rather than relying on general criminal folklore. The 1991 reported appellate decision cited in this directory specifically reviews Kevin Kelly's convictions while reciting evidence from the broader Westies prosecution; it should not be mislabeled as Coonan's personal appeal.

    Main biography page: james-coonan.html

  2. Francis “Mickey” Featherstone

    Historical classification: Senior Westies member and second-in-command; later cooperating witness; wrongfully convicted defendant in an unrelated state murder case. Federal appellate findings identified Featherstone as Coonan's principal lieutenant by the mid-1970s, and described his eventual cooperation as a turning point in the investigation of the Westies. His role illuminates both the gang's internal organization and the difficulties that can arise when competing witnesses and mistaken identification are involved in criminal cases.

    Featherstone was convicted in state court in 1986 of the murder of Michael Holly, but the conviction was set aside after evidence showed he had not committed that killing. His and his wife Marcelle “Sissy” Featherstone's subsequent cooperation helped investigators build the federal case against other Westies participants. The National Registry of Exonerations records the exoneration and its circumstances. His biography should preserve the distinction between this wrongful conviction, other allegations against him, and evidence he later gave as a cooperating witness.

    Main biography page: mickey-featherstone.html

Other documented members and associated subjects for future research: Federal appellate material identifies Kevin Kelly, James McElroy, William “Billie” Beattie, Kenneth Shannon, and William “Billie” Bokun among Westies participants at various points. These names provide research leads; their mention does not imply that MGU has published biographies for them. Mickey Spillane was a rival gang leader, not a member of Coonan's Westies, and Gambino figures should be classified as allied outsiders where appropriate.

Historical Record and Organizational Context

Rivalry and formation: The Second Circuit characterized the Westies as emerging from an effort by Coonan and his associates to displace the preexisting Spillane organization. That chronology requires separate collection entries for the two groups. Calling the Westies a direct continuation of Spillane's gang obscures a conflict that was central to the enterprise's formation.

Organization and leadership: Testimony from former members and associates depicted an informal but continuing criminal enterprise. Courts analyzed its existence through the group's actions, leadership, shared activities, and continuity rather than a written constitution or formal ranks. Coonan was identified as its leader and Featherstone as its second-in-command during the period under examination. Names and roles should be assigned to specific years and source records.

Illicit enterprises and consequences: Evidence in the federal proceedings described loansharking and debt collection, cocaine distribution, and interference with waterfront labor and commerce. It also recounted violent acts attributed to gang participants. The legal record supports analysis of how coercion facilitated illicit revenue, while the human consequences of murder, assaults, threats, and exploitation should remain central to the history.

Gambino relationship: Federal appellate findings described cooperation between the Westies and the Gambino crime family, including financing and the use of the Gambino name in connection with unlawful activity. An alliance or financial arrangement does not establish that Irish-American participants were inducted members of the Italian-American Mafia, nor that the two criminal enterprises were organizationally identical.

Featherstone's exoneration: Featherstone's conviction in the Michael Holly case was overturned in 1986 after the investigation established his noninvolvement. This episode illustrates the need to state procedural outcomes accurately: an arrest, allegation, conviction, and exoneration are not interchangeable. The cooperation that followed became important to federal prosecutors investigating the Westies.

Federal prosecutions: The principal multi-defendant racketeering trial occurred in 1988. Subsequent reported decisions included United States v. Coonan, 938 F.2d 1553 (2d Cir. 1991), the appeal of Kevin Kelly, and later related litigation. These records provide both trial-derived historical findings and legally precise descriptions of individual charges and outcomes. Each biography should link its own procedural history to the relevant defendant and judgment.

Editorial Standards

Collection scope: This directory indexes two assigned MGU biography filenames, not all individuals associated with the Westies. Linked biography targets are local relative paths intended for files placed alongside this index.html; live publication has not been independently verified.

  • Affiliation precision: Distinguish founding leaders, active members, later associates, cooperating witnesses, rival gang leaders, Mafia allies, and uninvolved relatives.
  • Gang boundaries: Keep the Westies and the preceding Mickey Spillane Gang as separate organizations, linked by a documented territorial conflict.
  • Legal accuracy: Do not present charges, prosecutorial statements, verdicts, appellate findings, and exonerations as though they have identical evidentiary meaning.
  • Personal responsibility: Attribute each alleged or adjudicated act to the actual person and specific proceeding; membership in a group alone does not establish guilt for every offense attributed to that group.
  • Witness testimony: Evaluate statements by former participants, cooperating witnesses, and law-enforcement officials in light of incentives and corroboration.
  • Institutional distinction: The Gambino alliance was a relationship between separate organizations, not proof of induction into an Italian-American Mafia family.
  • Canonical filing: Maintain one biography per person; cross-link biographies from relevant collection pages instead of creating duplicate person articles.
  • Educational focus: Assess the impact of coercion, violence, corruption, and prosecution without glamorizing criminal conduct.

Research Starting Points

The following records anchor the organizational overview. The cited appellate opinions summarize trial evidence and legal determinations; the exoneration registry documents a specific wrongful conviction. Each full biography should still maintain an independent source register for dates, allegations, judgments, and personal chronology.

  • United States v. Coonan, 938 F.2d 1553 (2d Cir. 1991) — principal judicial source on the Westies' formation, Coonan and Featherstone's roles, the Gambino alliance, and the case against defendant-appellant Kevin Kelly.
  • Second Circuit decision, 73 F.3d 8 (1995) — related federal proceedings describing the September 1987 indictment, racketeering allegations, and evidence presented at the principal trial.
  • National Registry of Exonerations — Francis T. Featherstone (March 13, 2018) — summarizes the wrongful Michael Holly murder conviction, its reversal in 1986, and Featherstone's subsequent cooperation with investigators.
  • United Press International — 1988 Westies trial closing arguments (February 8, 1988) — contemporaneous report identifying defendants and prosecutors' characterization of the charged enterprise; statements from closing argument must be identified as advocacy rather than independent findings.
  • National Crime & Gang Research context — Westies overview — a secondary historical orientation to the organization and its relationships, to be checked against judicial and contemporaneous sources when claims conflict.

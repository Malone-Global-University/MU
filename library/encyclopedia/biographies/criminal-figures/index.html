Overview

The Criminal Figures collection is the central biographical directory for the Malone Global University Encyclopedia's coverage of individuals associated with criminal organizations, illicit markets, significant prosecutions, and the history of law enforcement. The collection is organized around people rather than individual criminal cases or organizations.

Entries examine a subject's documented life, organizational affiliations, activities, historical context, criminal allegations or convictions where applicable, and long-term significance. The collection is designed to distinguish established facts from allegations, testimony, disputed accounts, and later popular mythology.

Biographies are maintained as individual reference articles. Organization histories and major episodes remain in their respective Crime Encyclopedia collections, with cross-references connecting people to events and institutions.

Research and Editorial Standards

  • Documented status: distinguish inducted members, alleged members, associates, cooperating witnesses, and individuals whose affiliation is disputed.
  • Evidence: prioritize court records, indictments, judgments, government reports, contemporaneous journalism, and credible historical scholarship; identify each source's limitations.
  • Legal precision: distinguish allegations, arrests, charges, convictions, acquittals, and unproven claims.
  • Chronology: use precise dates when supported; qualify uncertain periods and disputed successions.
  • Historical analysis: explain institutional significance without glorifying violence or relying on folklore as established fact.
  • Cross-referencing: connect biographies to related organization profiles and historical-episode articles.

Biographies in this collection should prioritize verifiable chronology and careful attribution. Membership, leadership rank, and participation in criminal activity must not be inferred from association alone. Each subject should have one canonical biography, even when that individual participated in several organizations or historical episodes. Articles should link to the canonical entry rather than duplicate it.

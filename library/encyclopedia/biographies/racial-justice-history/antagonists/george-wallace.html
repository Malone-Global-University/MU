George Wallace

Governor of Alabama (1919-1998) - Segregationist and opponent of civil rights

Biography

(1919-1998) - George Corley Wallace was a four-term Governor of Alabama and a prominent segregationist during the Civil Rights Movement. He became infamous for his resistance to desegregation and federal civil rights enforcement in the 1960s.

Opposition to Black Liberation

  • Infamously declared "Segregation now, segregation tomorrow, segregation forever" in his inaugural speech.
  • Physically blocked Black students from enrolling at the University of Alabama in 1963 ("Stand in the Schoolhouse Door").
  • Used state authority to resist desegregation of schools, public facilities, and voter registration efforts.
  • Ran for president on a pro-segregation platform, appealing to white supremacist voters.

Impact / Legacy

  • Symbol of institutionalized resistance to civil rights in the American South.
  • His actions galvanized federal civil rights enforcement and inspired greater activism among Black Americans.
  • Later in life, Wallace publicly renounced his segregationist views, but his legacy remains tied to opposition to racial equality.

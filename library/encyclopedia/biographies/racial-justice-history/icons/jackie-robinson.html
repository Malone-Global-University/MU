Jackie Robinson

First African American Major League Baseball player and civil rights ally (1919-1972)

Biography

(1919-1972) - Jackie Robinson broke the color barrier in Major League Baseball in 1947 when he debuted with the Brooklyn Dodgers. Beyond his sports career, he was a staunch advocate for civil rights, equality, and social justice, using his fame to fight racial discrimination in America.

Achievements

  • First African American to play in Major League Baseball in the modern era.
  • Six-time MLB All-Star and 1949 National League MVP.
  • Active participant in civil rights organizations and campaigns.
  • Influenced generations of athletes to speak out on social justice issues.

Leadership Focus

Bob Zellner

White Civil Rights Activist - Supporter of Black liberation movements

Biography

(Born 1939-2008) - Bob Zellner, a white Southerner, became a notable civil rights activist during the 1960s. Initially raised in a segregated environment, he broke with his upbringing to join the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee (SNCC).

Civil Rights Work

  • Participated in voter registration campaigns in Alabama and Mississippi.
  • Faced harassment, threats, and violence for his involvement in Black liberation efforts.
  • Worked closely with Black activists to advance social justice and racial equality.
  • Served as a bridge between white communities and civil rights organizations.

Legacy

  • Symbol of white allyship in the American civil rights movement.
  • Provided public testimony and advocacy for justice and integration.
  • Educated future generations on the importance of interracial solidarity in social justice movements.

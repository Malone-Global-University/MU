Harry Anslinger

First Commissioner of the U.S. Federal Bureau of Narcotics (1892-1975) - Architect of discriminatory drug policies

Biography

(1892-1975) - Harry Jacob Anslinger was the first commissioner of the U.S. Federal Bureau of Narcotics, serving from 1930 to 1962. He played a central role in creating and enforcing anti-drug laws that disproportionately targeted Black communities and suppressed Black liberation movements.

Opposition to Black Liberation

  • Promoted racist narratives linking Black musicians, jazz culture, and African Americans to marijuana use and criminality.
  • Advocated strict drug prohibition laws, which disproportionately incarcerated Black Americans.
  • Used media and legislation to criminalize behaviors in Black communities, undermining political, economic, and social empowerment.
  • Influenced national drug policy for decades, reinforcing systemic racism in law enforcement and judicial systems.

Impact / Legacy

  • His policies contributed to the long-term criminalization of Black communities in the United States.
  • Set precedent for racially biased law enforcement practices in subsequent decades.
  • Historical symbol of institutional opposition to Black political, economic, and social advancement through legal means.

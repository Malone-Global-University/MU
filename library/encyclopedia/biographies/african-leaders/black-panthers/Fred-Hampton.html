Fred Hampton

Activist and Black Panther Party Leader (1948-1969)

Early Life

Born in Chicago, Illinois, in 1948, Fred Hampton grew up in a working-class African-American family. Early exposure to racial injustice inspired his commitment to activism and social change.

Rise to Prominence

Hampton became chairman of the Illinois chapter of the Black Panther Party and rose quickly as a skilled organizer and orator. He built coalitions with other community groups to address systemic inequality and poverty.

Challenges

Hampton faced constant surveillance and harassment by law enforcement, including the FBI's COINTELPRO program. He was assassinated in a police raid in 1969 at the age of 21.

Legacy

Fred Hampton remains a symbol of youthful revolutionary leadership, community empowerment, and coalition-building. His life continues to inspire social justice movements.

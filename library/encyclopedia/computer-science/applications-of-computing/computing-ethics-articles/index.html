Subject Unit Overview

Computing Ethics examines the moral responsibilities that accompany the design, development, deployment, operation, and retirement of computational systems. The Computing Ethics parent article establishes the broad discipline, including ethical reasoning, professional obligations, privacy, fairness, safety, accessibility, public welfare, and governance.

The fifteen entries below expand specific issues identified in the parent article. They are direct subordinate subjects, not duplicates of neighboring technical disciplines. The entries retain the parent article's established titles and publication order.

Editorial navigation: All child titles are active links to their designated canonical destinations. A destination may remain unpublished until its corresponding article is deployed.

Specialist Entries

  1. Ethical Theories in Computing — Consequentialism, duties, virtue ethics, care ethics, and justice applied to technical decisions.
  2. Professional Codes of Computing Ethics — ACM and IEEE-CS principles, professional judgment, public welfare, and individual accountability.
  3. Privacy, Consent, and Data Protection Ethics — Legitimate information flows, minimization, retention, secondary use, and meaningful choice.
  4. Ethics of Surveillance and Biometrics — Monitoring, identification technologies, proportionality, consent, and civil liberties.
  5. Algorithmic Fairness and Discrimination — Fairness definitions, data and label choices, unequal effects, evaluation, and institutional remedies.
  6. Transparency, Explainability, and Appeals — Decision explanations, accountable review, contestability, and recourse against harmful outcomes.
  7. Ethics of User Interface Design — Autonomy, informed consent, choice architecture, persuasive systems, and manipulative patterns.
  8. Accessible and Inclusive Computing — Disability access, exclusion, inclusive research, universal design, and equitable participation.
  9. Intellectual Property and Software Ethics — Software licensing, attribution, open-source responsibilities, ownership, and provenance.
  10. Research Ethics and Responsible Disclosure — Human-subject research, ethical experimentation, vulnerability disclosure, and reproducibility.
  11. Artificial Intelligence Ethics and Governance — Risk evaluation, oversight, fairness, safety, institutional controls, and AI accountability.
  12. Generative AI and Information Integrity — Synthetic media, authenticity, attribution, disclosure, and evidence-grounded publication.
  13. Labor, Automation, and Platform Ethics — Workplace surveillance, algorithmic management, labor impacts, and platform responsibility.
  14. Sustainable and Global Computing Ethics — Environmental burdens, digital sovereignty, unequal access, and cross-border responsibility.
  15. Ethical Impact Assessments and Technology Governance — Impact assessments, review procedures, decision records, risk ownership, and remedies.

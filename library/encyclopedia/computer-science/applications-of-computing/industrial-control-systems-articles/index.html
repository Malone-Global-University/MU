Purpose and Scope

This subject index organizes the specialist knowledge areas associated with Industrial Control Systems in the MGU Computer Science Encyclopedia. Industrial control systems combine sensing, actuation, programmed control, supervisory software, networking, and operational safeguards to monitor or influence physical processes. The comprehensive parent article defines the discipline; the entries below provide planned, narrower treatments.

Industrial control is distinct from the broader field of Industrial Computing, which also encompasses manufacturing informatics, plant data platforms, production management, digital engineering, and organizational coordination. This index concentrates on the equipment, computing, operational safety, and system lifecycles that govern physical control.

Publication status: the parent article exists. The 20 specialist titles listed here are designated future articles. Their hyperlinks indicate intended destinations, not confirmation that the individual articles are available.

Networks, Interoperability, and Cybersecurity

Industrial communication services move signals, states, and operational records between equipment and supervisory applications. Their architectures must account for interoperability, access restrictions, and safety constraints.

Safety, Control Theory, and Reliability

Feedback algorithms manage physical processes; protective systems and reliability engineering address conditions in which ordinary control is insufficient or unavailable.

  1. Safety Instrumented Systems — Protective functions and functional-safety lifecycle.
  2. Industrial Feedback Control — Setpoints, PID concepts, stability, and tuning.
  3. Industrial Control Reliability and Failover — Redundancy, fault detection, and safe degradation.

Change, Incident Response, Modeling, and Maintenance

Industrial installations require controlled modification, recovery planning, simulation, and evidence-based equipment maintenance throughout their operational lives.

  1. Industrial Control Change Management — Versioning, approval, testing, and recovery.
  2. Industrial Control Incident Response — Safety-aware triage, containment, and restoration.
  3. Industrial Simulation and Digital Twins — Testing, model fidelity, and training systems.
  4. Industrial Predictive Maintenance — Condition monitoring, prediction, and technician review.

Suggested Study Pathways

Readers new to the subject can begin with sensing, control loops, programmable logic controllers, and operator interfaces, then move to supervisory architectures and industrial networks. Readers studying cybersecurity should first understand plant segmentation and control authority before examining cyber threats and incident response. Reliability-focused readers can follow functional safety, failover, testing, and change management.

The parent article provides the common overview and includes practical examples involving sensor scaling, process event detection, command latency, and recovery objectives. Specialist entries should build on those foundations without copying the whole survey.

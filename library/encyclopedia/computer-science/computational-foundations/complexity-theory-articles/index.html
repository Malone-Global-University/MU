Scope and Publication Status

Complexity Theory studies the time, working space, communication, circuit size, randomness, and other resources needed to solve families of computational problems. This index lists 17 specialist entries attached to the authoritative Complexity Theory parent article in the Computational Foundations volume.

The hyperlinks identify designated future article destinations. They are intentionally active under the WA-006 modernization policy, but a listed child should not be assumed published until its substantive HTML has been produced and deployed. Existing sibling subjects, such as Automata Theory and Algorithm Analysis, are linked separately below rather than duplicated in this child directory.

Recommended Reading Path

Begin with the parent overview and the foundational entries on asymptotic growth, time/space models, and polynomial-time computation. Continue to NP and reductions before tackling NP-completeness and alternative resource measures. Specialized readers can then pursue circuit and communication complexity, approximation, counting, parameterized methods, interactive proofs, and average-case hardness. Every advanced claim should be read together with the model and assumptions under which it is proved.

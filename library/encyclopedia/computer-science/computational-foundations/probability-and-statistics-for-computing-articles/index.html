Collection Overview

This specialist directory is the companion to the MGU Computer Science Encyclopedia's existing Probability and Statistics for Computing overview. It organizes the mathematical tools for reasoning about uncertain events and the practical methods for extracting evidence from observations, experiments, simulations, and monitored computer systems.

The parent article specifies 15 planned child articles. This index retains those exact specialist titles, filenames, and scope descriptions and arranges them into four instructional groups. Their purpose is to deepen portions of the parent subject, not to displace established neighboring encyclopedia articles on machine learning, data engineering, algorithm analysis, or discrete mathematics.

Publication distinction: the specialist links below are intentionally active links to future destinations. A linked child is a planned article, not necessarily an existing or published page. The parent overview remains the available broad introduction.

Readers should distinguish three levels of reasoning: probability describes uncertain outcomes under a model; statistics draws qualified conclusions using observations; and computational methods implement, approximate, and evaluate those models and conclusions under practical resource constraints.

Probability Foundations and Distribution Models

These subjects establish a rigorous vocabulary for uncertainty. Start with events and conditional probability; distinguish probabilities for discrete outcomes from densities for continuous quantities; then learn how averages and dependencies summarize a distribution.

  1. Probability Spaces and Conditional Probability — Events, axioms, conditioning, unions, and independence. (planned).
  2. Bayesian Reasoning and Base Rates — Prior information, likelihoods, rare-event detection, and updating. (planned).
  3. Discrete Random Variables and Distributions — Bernoulli, binomial, geometric, negative binomial, and Poisson models. (planned).
  4. Continuous Random Variables and Distributions — Densities, cumulative distributions, normal, exponential, and gamma models. (planned).
  5. Expectation, Variance, and Covariance — Moments, linearity, dependence, and uncertainty propagation. (planned).

Sampling, Estimation, and Statistical Inference

This group moves from populations and observed samples to estimates and their uncertainty. Study how observations were selected before interpreting confidence intervals, tests, likelihoods, or Bayesian posterior statements.

  1. Sampling Theory and Survey Design — Sampling frames, stratification, clustering, and weighting. (planned).
  2. Statistical Estimation and Maximum Likelihood — Estimator properties, likelihood, and numerical fitting. (planned).
  3. Confidence Intervals and Statistical Testing — Interval coverage, test statistics, errors, and power. (planned).
  4. Bayesian Statistical Inference — Priors, conjugate models, posteriors, and credible intervals. (planned).

Regression, Resampling, Simulation, and Stochastic Systems

These methods turn probability and statistics into computational procedures for prediction, approximation, and state-dependent processes. Identify the data-generating assumptions and error sources before choosing a model or simulation method.

  1. Regression and Generalized Linear Models — Linear, logistic, and count regression with interpretation. (planned).
  2. Resampling, Bootstrap, and Permutation Tests — Simulation-based uncertainty and experimental reference distributions. (planned).
  3. Monte Carlo Methods and Random Number Generation — Simulation, variance reduction, PRNGs, and reproducibility. (planned).
  4. Markov Chains and Stochastic Processes — State models, transition matrices, processes, and queues. (planned).

Experiments, Machine Learning, and Computing Systems

The final group applies statistical reasoning to intervention design, online experiments, learned models, and operational systems. Its central question is whether a reported numerical difference is trustworthy and actionable in the intended setting.

  2. Statistical Methods for Machine Learning and Systems — Calibration, cross-validation, reliability, forecasting, and deployment drift. (planned).

Statistical Method Selection

Effective statistical work starts from a question and a description of how observations were generated, not from the name of a preferred test or algorithm. The table below is an instructional starting point; study design, missingness, dependence, and assumptions may change the appropriate approach.

Research or engineering questionRelevant specialist articleInterpretive requirement
How do conditional information and an uncommon event affect a predicted probability?Bayesian Reasoning and Base RatesDistinguish the likelihood of an observation given a condition from the probability of the condition given an observation. Base rates can dominate rare-event performance.
What distribution models event counts, binary outcomes, a waiting time, or a continuously measured quantity?Discrete Random Variables and Distributions; Continuous Random Variables and DistributionsCheck whether the outcome space, dependence structure, and distributional assumptions match the phenomenon.
Can a sample support generalization to a defined population?Sampling Theory and Survey DesignSpecify the target population, sampling frame, selection mechanism, exclusions, and any weights.
What value should be estimated from observed data?Statistical Estimation and Maximum LikelihoodDefine the estimand, examine identifiability, and report model assumptions as well as the numerical estimate.
How uncertain is a sample-based estimate or apparent difference?Confidence Intervals and Statistical Testing; Resampling, Bootstrap, and Permutation TestsDistinguish repeated-sampling coverage from significance, effect size, and substantive importance.
How should prior evidence and observed data be combined to estimate an unknown parameter?Bayesian Statistical InferenceMake prior choices and posterior uncertainty explicit; do not equate a Bayesian credible interval with a frequentist confidence interval.
How can predictors be related to measured numeric, binary, or count outcomes?Regression and Generalized Linear ModelsAssess outcome type, functional form, confounding, data leakage, and whether prediction or causal explanation is intended.
How can a quantity be approximated using simulation rather than a closed-form solution?Monte Carlo Methods and Random Number GenerationEvaluate Monte Carlo error, pseudorandom generator state, convergence, and reproducibility.
How does a system change through successive uncertain states?Markov Chains and Stochastic ProcessesDefine the state and transition assumptions and check whether memoryless behavior is justified.
Does a change in a digital service cause a measurable improvement?Experimental Design and A/B TestingConsider randomization, pre-specified metrics, power, interference, guardrails, and repeated monitoring.
Does an ML model remain calibrated and reliable outside its original training data?Statistical Methods for Machine Learning and SystemsUse appropriate holdout or cross-validation design, calibration analysis, drift monitoring, and a documented evaluation population.

Principles for Interpreting Results

Define the unit of analysis. A user account, a person, a device reading, a transaction, and a repeated observation are not interchangeable. Treating repeated records as independent people can make uncertainty appear smaller than it is.

Separate probability statements. A probability conditional on a model and assumptions differs from empirical frequency in a dataset; a confidence interval differs in interpretation from a Bayesian credible interval; and a small p-value does not itself measure the probability that a hypothesis is true.

Distinguish association, prediction, and causation. A predictive model can perform well without identifying a causal relationship. Causal interpretation requires an appropriate design and assumptions about confounding, assignment, measurement, and interference.

Inspect sampling and missingness. Large sample size does not eliminate selection bias, nonresponse, label error, or unmeasured confounding. Report the population to which a conclusion may plausibly apply.

Account for computation and reproducibility. Statistical code can be deterministic while still implementing the wrong estimand or fitting a biased dataset. Record software versions, data transformations, model specifications, random seeds or generator states where useful, and methodological limitations. A seed supports reproducibility, not secure random generation.

Report meaningful uncertainty. Numerical precision, confidence or credible intervals, effect sizes, sensitivity analysis, and the consequences of decisions should be reported together when relevant. Statistical significance alone does not establish operational importance.

Suggested Study Pathways

Probability fundamentals: Begin with Probability Spaces and Conditional Probability and then Bayesian Reasoning and Base Rates. Study discrete and continuous random variables before Expectation, Variance, and Covariance. This sequence builds the language needed for statistical models and simulation.

Statistical inference: Start with Sampling Theory and Survey Design, then move to Statistical Estimation and Maximum Likelihood and Confidence Intervals and Statistical Testing. Study Bayesian Statistical Inference as a second inferential framework and compare the questions each approach answers.

Simulation and computational modeling: After probability fundamentals, study Monte Carlo Methods and Random Number Generation, Resampling, Bootstrap, and Permutation Tests, and Markov Chains and Stochastic Processes. Pair computational output with explicit assumptions and convergence checks. Include Regression and Generalized Linear Models when analyzing relationships among observed variables.

Systems and experimentation: Build inference fundamentals, then proceed to Experimental Design and A/B Testing and Statistical Methods for Machine Learning and Systems. Pay particular attention to unit assignment, delayed outcomes, operational guardrails, confidence in deployment metrics, and drift across time.

Research communication: Whichever route is selected, document the question, population, observation process, estimator or model, uncertainty, validation procedure, and limitations. Related Encyclopedia Topics

These are neighboring, separately scoped encyclopedia articles rather than children of this parent. They provide mathematical prerequisites, data-system context, or application areas that should be cross-referenced without duplicating an entire disciplinary overview.

  • Discrete Mathematics — Sets, counting, functions, logic, and proof methods underpinning formal probability.
  • Linear Algebra for Computing — Matrices, least squares, covariance structure, and numerical optimization.
  • Algorithm Analysis — Expected running time, randomized algorithms, asymptotic bounds, and benchmarking.
  • Graph Theory — Network structures and paths, with connections to stochastic processes and random walks.
  • Complexity Theory — Computational complexity and resource bounds as distinct from statistical error and identifiability.
  • Data Engineering — Collection, provenance, transformation, and reliable analytic data pipelines.
  • Data Modeling — Definitions, constraints, and relations that determine what data observations mean.
  • Information Retrieval — Ranking models, probabilistic retrieval, and empirical relevance evaluation.
  • Machine Learning — Learning predictive functions from data, validation, generalization, and deployment.
  • Computing Ethics — Accountable use of evidence, transparency, privacy, and harms in data-driven decisions.

The distinguishing responsibility of this collection is to connect probability and statistical methodology to computational implementation and interpretation. For example, a machine-learning article may focus on a predictive system, while the present collection explains confidence, calibration, data selection, and inference used to evaluate that system.

Purpose and Scope

This index organizes specialist studies under the parent article Analog and Digital Computation. The parent establishes the governing conceptual distinctions, history and principal engineering tradeoffs. The children will develop selected mechanisms, conversion techniques, numerical error questions and modern research directions in greater depth.

The listed child titles and routes are the planned production inventory. An active link records the intended page location, not a claim that the article has been released. The directory structure follows the adopted MGU Encyclopedia summary-style and content-splitting convention.

Specialist Article Directory

The sixteen designated specialist topics are grouped by their principal subject focus. Some topics connect across groups; that does not make their scope redundant.

Analog Computing and Its Mechanisms

  • Mechanical Analog Computation — Planimeters, slide rules, mechanical integration, scaling and physical limits.
  • Electronic Analog Computer Circuits — Operational amplifiers, circuit networks, summing, gains and practical patch programming.
  • Analog Integrators and Differential Equations — Continuous dynamical models and circuit implementations of integration.
  • Physical Scaling in Analog Computation — Units, normalization, signal range, saturation and parameter calibration.

Digital Representation and Conversion

  • Digital Switching and State Representation — Logic levels, physical thresholds, state elements and circuit timing.
  • Fixed-Point and Floating-Point Representation — Numeric range, precision, rounding, overflow and error behavior.
  • Sampling Theory and Aliasing — Bandlimits, Nyquist sampling, reconstruction and anti-alias filtering.
  • Analog-to-Digital Converters — Measurement pipelines, converter architectures and interface considerations.
  • Digital-to-Analog Converters — Analog reconstruction, DAC architectures, settling and output stages.
  • Quantization Noise and Effective Resolution — Quantization models, SNR, ENOB, dithering and usable converter performance.
  • Converter Clock Jitter and Timing — Sampling-clock integrity, synchronisation, timestamp errors and rate selection.

Hybrid and Emerging Computational Systems

  • Hybrid Analog-Digital Computers — Historical and contemporary systems coupling physical solvers with digital control.
  • Mixed-Signal Embedded System Design — Partitioning sensing, conversion, computation and physical actuation.
  • Analog In-Memory Computing — Crossbar arrays, approximate matrix operations and peripheral overhead.
  • Computational Error Budgets and Calibration — Measurement uncertainty, traceability and verification across analog–digital boundaries.
  • Neuromorphic Analog and Event-Driven Circuits — Continuous internal dynamics, event representation and system-level tradeoffs.

Research and Study Pathways

Conceptual and historical pathway: Begin with the parent overview, Mechanical Analog Computation, Electronic Analog Computer Circuits, and Hybrid Analog-Digital Computers. This sequence emphasizes why physical computing mechanisms arose and how programmability changed.

Measurement and signal pathway: Read Sampling Theory and Aliasing before Analog-to-Digital Converters, Quantization Noise and Effective Resolution, Converter Clock Jitter and Timing, and Digital-to-Analog Converters. This pathway follows information from the physical world into coded data and back.

Architecture and emerging research pathway: Study Mixed-Signal Embedded System Design, Computational Error Budgets and Calibration, Analog In-Memory Computing, and Neuromorphic Analog and Event-Driven Circuits. Assess performance and precision at complete-system scale.

How the Subjects Relate

This subordinate collection intentionally complements, rather than replaces, existing entries on Binary Logic, Data Representation, Digital Logic, and Scientific Computing. Those topics remain authoritative for their own disciplines.

Because the top-level Computer Science taxonomy is already sufficient, no new principal subject folder is introduced. Further splits should occur only if specialist depth establishes a genuine, nonduplicative entry.

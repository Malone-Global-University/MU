Purpose and Parent Relationship

The parent article Binary Logic supplies the shared mathematical and technical foundation for all subjects in this directory. It distinguishes pure two-valued functions from physical circuitry, language semantics, and system-level assurance. The child entries below are reserved for deeper treatment of concepts that warrant standalone, nonduplicative instruction.

The Sub-Articles list in the parent and the links in this index describe the same intended destinations. They comply with the MGU Encyclopedia summary-style and content-splitting convention: the parent remains the authoritative overview; the subordinate index organizes specialist depth. No additional principal Computer Science subject category is required.

Specialist Article Directory

The sixteen articles below are planned. Grouping reflects the primary methodological focus rather than a rigid separation of mathematical, software, and hardware concerns.

Foundations and Specification

  • Boolean Algebra and Logical Identities — The algebraic laws, duality, complementation, absorption and formal transformation.
  • Truth Tables and Logical Equivalence — Exhaustive function specification, proof and equivalence checking.
  • Canonical Forms, Minterms, and Maxterms — SOP, POS, normal forms and systematic construction from input rows.
  • Karnaugh Maps and Logic Minimization — Gray-code adjacency, implicant groupings and small-function minimization.
  • Quine–McCluskey and Tabular Minimization — Algorithmic implicant generation and covering problems.
  • Don’t-Care Conditions and Partial Logic — Partially specified functions, permitted inputs and safe assumptions.

Logical Components and Arithmetic

  • NAND, NOR, and Universal Gates — Functional completeness, gate bases and technology mapping.
  • Multiplexers, Decoders, and Selection Logic — Selection functions, one-hot output patterns and control routing.
  • Binary Adders and Arithmetic Logic — Half adders, full adders, carry networks and bitwise operations.
  • State Encoding and Sequential Logic — Boolean next-state functions, state encodings and control outputs.

Timing and Physical Realization

  • Combinational Circuit Hazards — Static and dynamic hazards, glitches and consensus terms.
  • Static Timing and Logic Paths — Propagation, setup and hold constraints, loading and critical paths.
  • Clock-Domain Crossings and Metastability — Synchronizer limits, asynchronous transfers and fault probabilities.
  • Multi-Valued Logic in Hardware Description — Unknown and high-impedance states, simulation and synthesis semantics.

Symbolic Analysis and Verification

  • Boolean Satisfiability and Formal Verification — SAT, Boolean constraints, equivalence and counterexamples.
  • Binary Decision Diagrams — Ordered decision graphs, canonical representations and symbolic methods.

Recommended Study Pathways

Algebra and specification. Start with Binary Logic, then Boolean Algebra and Logical Identities, Truth Tables and Logical Equivalence, and Canonical Forms, Minterms, and Maxterms. Continue to Karnaugh Maps and Quine–McCluskey when optimization becomes the focus.

Logic construction. Follow Universal Gates with Multiplexers and Decoders, Binary Adders and Arithmetic Logic, and State Encoding and Sequential Logic. Pair the mathematical topics with the existing Digital Logic entry, which treats concrete circuit engineering.

Timing and assurance. Study Combinational Circuit Hazards, Static Timing, Clock-Domain Crossings, and Multi-Valued Logic before interpreting results from real hardware or HDL simulation. Finish with Boolean Satisfiability and Binary Decision Diagrams to understand symbolic verification and optimization.

History Textbooks

The History Textbook Shelf brings together course-style studies of the human past, from local communities and cultural movements to national histories, regional developments, and subjects that cross geographic and chronological boundaries.

Overview

The MGU History Textbook Shelf is organized into four principal collections: Geographic History, Community History, Cultural History, and Thematic History. Each collection provides a dedicated index of its textbooks, keeping this main shelf organized by field rather than by individual title.

These textbooks support sustained historical study through chronological narratives, interpretation of evidence, examination of institutions and societies, and analysis of change over time. The collection accommodates completed works, developing textbook series, and future areas of historical scholarship.

Geographic History

A geographically organized collection of history textbooks examining continents, regions, countries, civilizations, and the world as a whole. These works trace political development, economic systems, societies, cultural traditions, migration, conflict, and historical transformations across distinct places and interconnected historical settings.

  • Geographic History Index

Community History

A collection of history textbooks centered on the experiences and development of particular communities, populations, diasporas, and neighborhoods. The studies examine settlement, migration, community institutions, identity, social organization, exclusion, adaptation, and collective change within larger historical contexts.

  • Community History Index

Cultural History

A collection of history textbooks exploring cultural expression and the development of ideas, traditions, and artistic movements. Subjects include music, literature, the arts, languages, intellectual life, popular culture, cultural exchange, and the social forces that shape cultural production and reception over time.

  • Cultural History Index

Thematic History

A subject-centered collection of history textbooks investigating developments across multiple societies, regions, and periods. Areas of study include political and legal history, organized crime, warfare, religion, economics, science, mathematics, technology, engineering, medicine, and other specialized fields of historical inquiry.

A History of the Arab World

From Ancient Crossroads to the Modern Arab World

A History of the Arab World: From Ancient Crossroads to the Modern Arab World traces the history of the lands that would become the Arab world from deep human prehistory and the civilizations of the ancient Near East, Nile Valley, North Africa, and Arabia through the rise of Islam, the caliphates, regional states, Ottoman rule, European imperial expansion, nationalism, modern state formation, decolonization, revolution, oil politics, Palestine, regional war, and the contemporary era.

Overview

The Arab world is treated here as a historically changing geographic, linguistic, cultural, and political sphere rather than as a timeless civilization with fixed boundaries. The textbook distinguishes Arab, Arabian, Arabic-speaking, Muslim, Islamic, Middle Eastern, and North African identities and does not project later Arab identity backward onto ancient peoples who lived in the same regions.

The twelve-part work begins with geography, climate, human origins, migration, agriculture, languages, and the ancient civilizations of Mesopotamia, Egypt, Nubia, the Levant, North Africa, and Arabia. It then follows Late Antiquity, Muhammad and the formation of the first Muslim community, the Rashidun and Umayyad periods, Arabization and Islamization, the Abbasid world, regional states, the Crusading era, the Mamluks, the Maghreb, Arabia, and the wider Indian Ocean world.

The later chapters examine the Ottoman Arab provinces, reform and the Nahda, European imperial expansion, nationalism and Zionism, the First World War, mandates and modern borders, independence and decolonization, Palestine and the Arab-Israeli conflict, pan-Arabism, revolutionary republics, oil and the Gulf monarchies, political Islam, regional wars, authoritarianism, the Arab uprisings, migration, climate, media, economic diversification, identity, and the continuing transformation of the Arab world in the twenty-first century.

Learning Outcomes

  • Explain the development of the lands and societies that became the Arab world from prehistory through the contemporary era without projecting later Arab identity backward onto ancient populations.
  • Distinguish Arab, Arabian, Arabic-speaking, Muslim, Islamic, Middle Eastern, and North African identities and explain how their meanings changed across historical periods.
  • Analyze how geography, climate, water, agriculture, pastoralism, migration, trade, cities, institutions, religion, language, law, warfare, technology, and cultural exchange shaped different regions of the Arab world.
  • Trace the rise of Islam, the early Arab-Islamic conquests, the Umayyad and Abbasid caliphates, and the separate but interacting historical processes of Arabization and Islamization.
  • Compare political, social, economic, intellectual, and religious developments across Arabia, Iraq, the Levant, Egypt, Sudan, the Maghreb, and the Gulf while connecting them to African, Mediterranean, Indian Ocean, European, Iranian, and wider global systems.
  • Assess the transformations produced by Ottoman rule, reform, the Nahda, European imperial expansion, nationalism, Zionism, world war, mandates, state formation, decolonization, Arab nationalism, oil, political Islam, regional conflict, and the Arab uprisings.
  • Evaluate continuity and change in Palestine, state authority, migration, gender, labor, media, cities, economic development, climate, water, identity, and regional power in the modern and contemporary Arab world.

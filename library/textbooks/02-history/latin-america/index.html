Latin American History

Latin American History: Indigenous Civilizations, Colonization, Independence, and Modern Nations traces the region from the peopling of the Americas and the development of Indigenous civilizations through European conquest, colonial rule, slavery, independence, nation-building, revolution, dictatorship, Cold War conflict, democratization, organized crime, petroleum politics, migration, social movements, and Latin America through 2026.

Overview

Latin American history examines a diverse and changing historical region rather than a single civilization or a synonym for Spanish America. This textbook follows Indigenous peoples, African-descended communities, colonial societies, states, economies, religions, social movements, political systems, and transnational networks across Mexico, Central America, the Caribbean, South America, and the region's Atlantic and Pacific connections.

The five-volume work begins with Indigenous peoples and civilizations before sustained European conquest; continues through Atlantic encounter, conquest, colonial states, slavery, religion, race, and resistance; then follows independence, republics, nation-building, export economies, revolution, mass politics, dictatorship, the Cold War, democratization, cartel systems, petroleum politics, migration, resource politics, and contemporary transformations through 2026.

Throughout, the narrative keeps Brazil, Haiti, the Caribbean, Indigenous regions, Afro-Latin America, Central America, the Andes, the Southern Cone, Colombia and Venezuela, Amazonia, and relevant imperial and hemispheric intersections within the same historical frame. Indigenous history continues beyond conquest, African and Afro-Latin history continues beyond emancipation, and the United States enters the narrative where its relationships directly intersect with Latin American history without becoming the region's protagonist.

Learning Outcomes

  • Explain the major chronological transformations of Latin American history from the earliest human settlement through 2026 without projecting later national or regional identities backward onto earlier societies.
  • Compare Indigenous civilizations and societies across Mesoamerica, the Andes, the Caribbean, Amazonia, Isthmo-Colombia, and southern South America within their own historical contexts.
  • Analyze how conquest, colonization, Atlantic slavery, race, religion, labor, gender, environment, trade, and imperial institutions shaped Spanish and Portuguese America while preserving Indigenous and African agency.
  • Trace the connected but regionally distinct processes of imperial reform, independence, republican experimentation, nation-building, territorial warfare, abolition, export growth, and expanding hemispheric relations.
  • Evaluate twentieth-century revolution, populism, developmentalism, the Cuban Revolution, guerrilla warfare, military dictatorship, human-rights movements, Central American civil wars, and Cold War intervention comparatively.
  • Assess democratization, neoliberal reform, Indigenous resurgence, organized crime, drug trafficking, Colombian peace, Mexican cartel conflict, Venezuelan petroleum politics, migration, social movements, resource geopolitics, and the unresolved transformations of Latin America through 2026.

Indian History

Indian History: South Asia from the Indus Civilization to the Present traces the history of South Asia from geography, early farming communities, and the Indus world through Vedic societies, ancient and medieval states, the Delhi Sultanate, the Mughal Empire, colonial rule, nationalism, Partition, independence, and the postcolonial region through 2026.

Overview

Indian history is approached here as the history of South Asia: a region whose political borders, languages, religious traditions, economic systems, ecological zones, and social institutions changed repeatedly across time. India forms the central narrative, but the textbook also integrates the histories of present-day Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Bhutan, Maldives, and historically connected frontier regions wherever they belong in the chronology.

This textbook begins with the geography and historical evidence of South Asia, early agriculture, the Indus Civilization, Vedic societies, the Second Urbanization, Buddhism, Jainism, Brahmanical traditions, Magadha, and the Mauryan Empire. It continues through post-Mauryan states, the Kushans, Satavahanas, Sangam Tamilakam, Sri Lanka, the Gupta period, regional kingdoms, the Delhi Sultanate, Vijayanagara, the Deccan, Bhakti, Sufism, Sikh beginnings, the Indian Ocean world, the Mughal Empire, and the eighteenth-century transformation. The later volumes examine Company conquest, the British Raj, colonial economy and society, the Rebellion of 1857, nationalism, Gandhi, competing political movements, the Second World War, Partition, independence, the creation and development of the postcolonial states, regional conflict, democratization, liberalization, technology, caste, gender, religion, media, climate, geopolitics, historical memory, and South Asia's place in the world through 2026.

Learning Outcomes

  • Explain the major chronological periods of South Asian history from early settlement and the Indus Civilization through 2026 without projecting modern national borders or identities backward onto earlier societies.
  • Compare major states, empires, regional kingdoms, political systems, and postcolonial governments across the subcontinent within their changing geographic and historical contexts.
  • Analyze how monsoons, rivers, agriculture, pastoralism, trade, migration, warfare, technology, urbanization, and environmental change shaped South Asian history.
  • Evaluate the historical development and interaction of Vedic and Brahmanical traditions, Hindu traditions, Buddhism, Jainism, Islam, Sikhism, Christianity, and other religious and intellectual movements.
  • Examine how social hierarchy, varna, jati, caste, class, gender, labor, household organization, and regional identities changed across ancient, medieval, colonial, and postcolonial periods.
  • Assess the transformations produced by European commercial expansion, Company rule, British colonialism, resistance, nationalism, Partition, independence, and the creation of the modern South Asian states.
  • Trace the political, economic, social, technological, cultural, and geopolitical development of India and the wider South Asian region from 1947 through 2026.

Persian and Iranian History

Persian and Iranian History: From Ancient Persia to the Islamic Republic traces the peoples, societies, states, religions, economies, institutions, and cultures of the Iranian plateau and the wider Iranian and Persianate worlds from prehistory through Elam, the Achaemenid, Parthian, and Sasanian empires, Islamic conquest and Persianate revival, Mongol and Timurid rule, the Safavid and Qajar states, the Pahlavi monarchy, the 1979 Revolution, and the contemporary Islamic Republic.

Overview

Iranian history examines a continuous but changing historical subject centered on the Iranian plateau and extending, when historically necessary, into the wider Iranian and Persianate worlds. This textbook distinguishes Persian, Iranian, Arab, Muslim, and Persianate as analytical categories while following the many peoples and communities that have shaped Iranian history across changing political borders, languages, religions, and imperial systems.

The eight-volume, 108-chapter work begins with the geography and deep past of the Iranian plateau, Elam, Iranian migrations, the Medes, and the Achaemenid Empire; continues through Hellenistic Iran, the Arsacids, Sasanians, Islamic conquest, Iranian dynasties, the Persianate revival, Mongol and Timurid rule, and the Safavid transformation; and then follows Qajar Iran, constitutionalism, the Pahlavi state, the 1979 Revolution, the Iran-Iraq War, reform and political contestation, nuclear diplomacy, social change, and the Islamic Republic in the twenty-first century.

Learning Outcomes

  • Explain the major chronological transformations of Iranian history from prehistory through the twenty-first century while recognizing that Iran’s political borders, institutions, languages, identities, and imperial reach changed repeatedly over time.
  • Distinguish Persian, Iranian, Arab, Muslim, and Persianate as historical and analytical categories, and evaluate how diverse peoples and communities contributed to Iranian political, social, religious, economic, and cultural development.
  • Compare major Iranian states and political orders, including Elamite, Median, Achaemenid, Arsacid, Sasanian, post-conquest Islamic, Mongol, Timurid, Safavid, Afsharid, Zand, Qajar, Pahlavi, and Islamic Republic systems.
  • Analyze the roles of geography, environment, migration, warfare, administration, religion, agriculture, cities, trade, class, gender, family, education, labor, technology, and intellectual life in shaping historical change.
  • Assess Iran’s connections with Mesopotamia, the Mediterranean world, the Caucasus, Central Asia, Afghanistan, the Persian Gulf, South Asia, the Ottoman Empire, Russia, Britain, and modern global systems.
  • Evaluate continuity and rupture across major transformations, including the Arab-Muslim conquest, Mongol invasions, Safavid establishment of Twelver Shi‘ism, nineteenth-century imperial pressure, constitutionalism, the Pahlavi modernization project, the 1979 Revolution, war, reform, protest, sanctions, and contemporary state-society conflict.

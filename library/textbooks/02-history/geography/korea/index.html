Korean History

Korean History: Ancient Kingdoms to a Divided Peninsula traces Korea from prehistoric settlement and early states through the ancient kingdoms, Goryeo, Joseon, imperial crisis, Japanese colonial rule, liberation, the Korean War, and the divergent histories of North and South Korea through 2026.

Overview

Korean history examines the peoples, states, institutions, communities, cultures, economies, religions, and political systems that developed on and around the Korean Peninsula from prehistory through the divided peninsula of 2026.

This textbook begins with geography, archaeology, early settlement, Gojoseon, the Three Kingdoms and Gaya, Silla, Balhae, and Goryeo; continues through Joseon, Hangeul, science, war, social and religious change, and the nineteenth-century crisis; then follows imperial competition, the Korean Empire, Japanese colonial rule, liberation, division, the Korean War, and the parallel histories of North and South Korea. The final volume examines authoritarianism, democratization, chaebol-led industrialization, Juche, famine and marketization, nuclearization, digital technology, Japanese and American cultural exchange, Hallyu, K-pop, K-beauty, demographic change, and the continuing division of the peninsula.

Learning Outcomes

  • Explain the major chronological periods of Korean history from prehistory through 2026 without projecting modern national identities backward onto earlier societies.
  • Compare Gojoseon, Goguryeo, Baekje, Silla, Gaya, Balhae, Goryeo, Joseon, the Korean Empire, and the two post-1948 Korean states within their changing regional contexts.
  • Analyze how geography, agriculture, maritime networks, warfare, diplomacy, religion, social hierarchy, technology, trade, and cultural exchange shaped Korean history.
  • Evaluate the transformations produced by Japanese imperialism, colonial rule, independence movements, liberation, occupation, division, and the Korean War.
  • Trace the divergent postwar development of North and South Korea, including authoritarianism, democratization, chaebol-led industrialization, Juche, marketization, nuclearization, and inter-Korean relations.
  • Assess Korea's modern cultural and technological transformation, including digital society, Japanese and American cultural exchange, Hallyu, K-pop, film, gaming, webtoons, food, fashion, and K-beauty.

Russian History

A History of Russia: From the Eurasian World to the Russian Federation traces the history of the lands, peoples, societies, states, and empires associated with Russia from prehistoric northern Eurasia and the ancient steppe through the formation of Rus', the rise of Moscow, the Muscovite state, the Russian Empire, revolution, the Soviet Union, its collapse, and the Russian Federation through 2026.

Overview

Russian history extends across Eastern Europe and northern Eurasia and cannot be reduced to the history of a single ethnic group, capital, dynasty, empire, or political system. This textbook therefore distinguishes carefully among Rus', Muscovy, the Russian Empire, the Soviet Union, and the Russian Federation while examining the many peoples, regions, institutions, religions, economies, and political communities that shaped their development. Russia is studied within the wider histories of Eastern Europe, the Baltic and Black Sea worlds, the Caucasus, Central Asia, Siberia, the Arctic, the North Pacific, and the international system.

The textbook begins with geography, historical evidence, prehistoric settlement, the ancient steppe, and the Slavic, Finnic, Baltic, Turkic, Khazar, Bulgar, and Scandinavian worlds that preceded Rus'. It then follows the formation and Christianization of Rus', political fragmentation, the Mongol conquest and Golden Horde, the rise of Moscow, Muscovite expansion, the Romanovs, serfdom, Siberian conquest, religious schism, Peter the Great, eighteenth-century empire, nineteenth-century reform and imperial expansion, industrialization, nationalism, revolution, civil war, Stalinism, the Second World War, the post-Stalin Soviet Union, perestroika, Soviet collapse, and the political, social, economic, and geopolitical development of the Russian Federation through 2026.

Learning Outcomes

  • Explain the major chronological transformations of Russian and Eurasian history from prehistoric settlement through the Russian Federation while distinguishing Rus', Muscovy, the Russian Empire, the Soviet Union, and modern Russia.
  • Analyze how geography, rivers, forests, steppe environments, migration, trade, religion, warfare, political institutions, agriculture, serfdom, industrialization, technology, and international relations shaped historical change across northern Eurasia.
  • Trace the changing forms of political authority from Rus' principalities and Muscovite rule through imperial autocracy, revolutionary government, Soviet institutions, and the post-Soviet Russian state without treating those transitions as inevitable.
  • Compare the historical experiences of Russians and the many non-Russian peoples and regions incorporated into Rus', Muscovy, the Russian Empire, the Soviet Union, and the Russian Federation, including communities in Ukraine, Belarus, Poland, the Baltic, the Caucasus, Central Asia, Siberia, and other frontier regions.
  • Evaluate major historical debates involving state formation, Mongol rule, imperial expansion, serfdom, reform, nationalism, revolution, Stalinism, Soviet nationality policy, the Second World War, the Cold War, Soviet collapse, and post-Soviet political development.
  • Assess Russia's changing relationships with Europe, the Ottoman world, the Caucasus, Central Asia, East Asia, the United States, and the wider international system, including diplomacy, commerce, empire, war, ideological conflict, and contemporary geopolitics through 2026.

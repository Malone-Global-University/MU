Geographic History

Geographic History organizes historical textbooks by the places and interconnected worlds they examine. The collection encompasses world history, continents, transregional systems, geographic regions, countries, and civilizations, tracing continuity and change across political, social, economic, and cultural life.

Overview

The geographic collection provides sustained historical surveys at several scales. World and continental histories establish broad patterns; regional and transregional histories examine connected societies; country histories offer more concentrated chronological accounts.

This geographic organization describes each textbook's principal scope, not an exclusive boundary. Historical developments frequently cross modern borders, and related series may be studied together.

World and Continental Histories

Broad surveys of worldwide historical processes and the development of entire continents.

  • World History: Civilizations, Empires, and Global Change

    A global historical survey of civilizations, exchange networks, belief systems, empires, revolutions, colonialism, industrialization, decolonization, and the increasingly interconnected modern world.

  • Africa: A History of a Continent

    A continental history extending from human origins and early societies through ancient kingdoms, trade networks, religious and intellectual traditions, slavery, colonial rule, independence movements, and modern Africa.

  • European History

    Examines Europe from ancient societies and classical traditions through medieval institutions, Renaissance and Reformation, state formation, imperial expansion, industrialization, wars, and contemporary transformations.

Regional and Transregional Histories

Studies of regions and cultural-historical worlds that span multiple states or societies.

  • Arab World History

    Traces the histories of Arabic-speaking societies and the Arab world, including regional cultures, religious and intellectual life, political formations, reform movements, imperial encounters, nationalism, and contemporary developments.

  • Indigenous Peoples of North America

    Explores the histories of Indigenous nations and communities across North America, from diverse precolonial societies and political systems through colonization, dispossession, resistance, treaty relations, cultural continuity, and sovereignty.

  • Latin American History

    Surveys Latin America from Indigenous civilizations and colonial societies through independence movements, nation-building, economic development, social conflict, cultural change, and modern political transformations.

  • Middle East History

    Studies the Middle East across ancient, medieval, early modern, and modern eras, examining empires, religions, trade, intellectual traditions, state formation, imperial competition, and regional change.

  • Southeast Asian History

    Examines mainland and maritime Southeast Asia, including early states, kingdoms, religious and commercial networks, regional exchanges, European colonialism, independence, and modern nation-building.

Country and National Histories

Focused histories of individual countries or historical societies, while recognizing that their pasts extend beyond present-day borders.

  • Chinese History

    Traces the development of societies and states in China, including early cultures, imperial dynasties, intellectual traditions, institutions, commerce, foreign relations, revolution, and the modern era.

  • Indian History

    Surveys the histories of the Indian subcontinent, including early civilizations, religious and intellectual traditions, regional kingdoms, empires, commercial networks, colonial rule, independence, and modern developments.

  • Persian and Iranian History

    Studies Iranian societies and Persianate worlds from antiquity through imperial and dynastic traditions, religious and literary developments, regional interactions, modernization, and contemporary Iranian history.

  • Japanese History

    Explores Japan from early societies and court cultures through the samurai age, early modern institutions, the Meiji transformation, imperial expansion, war, reconstruction, and contemporary life.

  • Korean History

    Examines the Korean peninsula across early states, dynastic institutions, intellectual and cultural traditions, foreign relations, colonial rule, division, war, and the histories of modern Korea.

  • Russian History

    Traces the histories of Rus', Muscovy, the Russian Empire, the Soviet Union, and the Russian Federation, with attention to governance, social change, imperial expansion, revolutions, and global relations.

  • United States History: Indigenous America, Republic, Conflict, and Change

    Surveys the history of the United States, including Indigenous North America, colonization, the founding era, slavery, expansion, the Civil War, Reconstruction, industrialization, reform, world wars, civil rights, and modern change.

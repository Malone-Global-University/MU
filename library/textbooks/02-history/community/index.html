Community History

Community History examines the experiences of particular populations, neighborhoods, diasporas, and social groups over time. The collection emphasizes migration, settlement, exclusion, collective institutions, identity formation, adaptation, and the changing relationships between communities and the wider societies in which they live.

Overview

These textbooks approach historical change from the perspective of communities and the institutions that sustain or transform them. They connect local experience with national policies, economic change, social movements, and transnational networks.

Each title has its own textbook directory, where readers can access the available chapters and supporting materials.

Textbook Collection

The following studies focus on the formation and transformation of identifiable communities.

  • From Migration to Fragmentation: The Urban History of Black Street Organizations

    A study of African American urban communities and the historical development of street organizations, examining migration, segregation, neighborhood institutions, economic restructuring, public policy, policing, and changes in community life.

  • Chinatown Under Exclusion: Chinese Immigration, Urban Enclaves, and the Making of Chinese America

    Traces Chinese immigration and the formation of Chinese American communities, including labor migration, exclusion laws, urban enclaves, family and district associations, resistance to discrimination, legal change, and the continuing evolution of Chinatowns.

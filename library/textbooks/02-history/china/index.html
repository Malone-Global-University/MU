Chinese History

China: A History of a Civilization traces the peoples, societies, states, cultures, economies, institutions, and ideas associated with China from the deep past through early states, dynastic empires, periods of division and multi-state rule, the Republic, the People’s Republic of China, reform and opening, and the twenty-first century.

Overview

Chinese history examines a changing historical space rather than a timeless civilization with fixed borders, institutions, or identities. This textbook follows the peoples, communities, states, economies, religions, intellectual traditions, technologies, and political systems that developed across the lands associated with China while emphasizing that the meaning and geographic extent of “China” changed repeatedly over time.

The 76-chapter work begins with geography, Paleolithic communities, Neolithic agriculture, regional cultures, and early states; continues through Shang, Zhou, Qin, Han, the age of division, Sui, Tang, Song, Yuan, Ming, and Qing; and then follows nineteenth-century imperial crisis, the Republic, war and revolution, Maoist China, reform and opening, market transformation, social change, governance, territory, and China’s global role in the twenty-first century.

Learning Outcomes

  • Explain the major chronological transformations of Chinese history from the deep past through the twenty-first century without projecting modern political borders, institutions, or identities backward onto earlier societies.
  • Compare periods of political unification, division, conquest, multi-state competition, and imperial reconstruction across Shang, Zhou, Qin, Han, Sui, Tang, Song, Yuan, Ming, Qing, Republican, and People’s Republic eras.
  • Analyze how geography, environment, migration, warfare, administration, law, taxation, agriculture, trade, family, gender, religion, intellectual life, science, technology, and cultural exchange shaped historical change.
  • Assess the historical roles of frontier and Inner Asian peoples, regional societies, maritime networks, neighboring states, and wider Eurasian and global connections in the formation and transformation of Chinese states and societies.
  • Trace the political, economic, social, and cultural consequences of nineteenth-century imperial pressure, rebellion, reform, revolution, Japanese invasion, civil war, socialist transformation, Mao-era campaigns, and post-1978 reform.
  • Evaluate continuity and change in contemporary China through governance, economic development, demography, technology, environment, territorial questions, social transformation, and China’s changing global role.

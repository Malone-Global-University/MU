European History

European History: Antiquity, Christendom, States, Empires, and Modern Europe traces Europe from prehistoric settlement and early regional networks through Greece and Rome, Latin and Orthodox Christendom, medieval societies, Renaissance and Reformation, maritime empires and dynastic states, revolution and industrialization, nationalism and imperialism, the world wars, the Cold War, European integration, and the contested political and cultural meanings of Europe in the contemporary era.

Overview

European history examines a changing geographic and historical region rather than a timeless civilization with fixed borders. This textbook follows the peoples, states, institutions, religions, economies, communities, cultures, and political systems that developed across Europe and its borderlands from prehistory through the contemporary era.

The six-volume work begins with prehistoric Europe, Greece, Rome, and Late Antiquity; continues through Latin Christendom, Byzantium, Orthodox Europe, and the medieval world; then follows the Renaissance, Reformation, maritime expansion, state formation, Enlightenment, revolution, industrialization, nationalism, imperialism, the world wars, the Cold War, European integration, and post-Cold War transformation. Throughout, the narrative keeps Iberia, Italy, France, the British Isles, the German lands, Scandinavia, the Baltic, Central Europe, Eastern Europe, the Balkans, European Russia, borderlands, minorities, migration, colonial connections, and Europe's wider global relationships within the same historical frame.

Learning Outcomes

  • Explain the major chronological transformations of European history from prehistory through the contemporary era without projecting modern states, national identities, or political boundaries backward onto earlier societies.
  • Compare ancient Mediterranean, Latin Christian, Byzantine, Orthodox, northern, central, eastern, southeastern, imperial, national, and postwar European political orders within their changing regional contexts.
  • Analyze how geography, environment, migration, warfare, religion, law, institutions, social hierarchy, family, gender, trade, technology, intellectual life, and cultural exchange shaped European history.
  • Evaluate the transformations produced by Christianity, state formation, Renaissance and Reformation, overseas expansion, colonialism, Atlantic slavery, the Enlightenment, revolution, industrialization, nationalism, and imperialism.
  • Trace the destruction and reconstruction of European political order through World War I, revolution, fascism, Nazism, Stalinism, World War II, occupation, the Holocaust, displacement, and the Cold War.
  • Assess European integration, decolonization, migration, post-communist transformation, the Yugoslav wars, Brexit, Russia and Ukraine, contemporary security, and the continuing historical debate over what Europe means.

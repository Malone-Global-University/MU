Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Open an oral presentation clearly, using a newly productive presentation-opening frame — je vais vous présenter or l’objectif de cette présentation est de + infinitif.
  2. Refer explicitly to a visual support (slide, chart, or table) using three newly productive visual-referencing connectors — comme le montre + groupe nominal, on constate ici + noun or que + clause, and ce graphique illustre + groupe nominal.
  3. Manage an audience using two newly productive formulas — n’hésitez pas à m’interrompre si and je prendrai vos questions à la fin — to set expectations for interruption and questions.
  4. Close a presentation with the newly productive frame pour conclure. Both pour conclure and en conclusion are standard in French, in speech and in writing; this chapter activates pour conclure as its explicit spoken closing signal while recycling en conclusion from Chapter 13.
  5. Recycle Chapter 13’s report-structure connectors (dans un premier temps, dans un second temps, il convient de noter que), recommendation and impersonal necessity frames, and the passive voice; Chapter 12’s paraphrase frames and source-comparison connectors; Chapter 11’s nominalization and academic reporting verbs; and the Chapter 6–9 toolkit, applied to an oral research presentation, without any new productive subjonctif trigger.
  6. Add and recycle a curated presentation vocabulary set: une diapositive, un support visuel, un graphique, illustrer, s’appuyer sur, souligner, un public, prendre la parole, and à l’appui de.
  7. Deliver a short oral research presentation (eight to twelve connected sentences) with a clear opening, at least one visual reference, at least one audience-management formula, and a clear closing.
  8. Move to a short annotated presentation outline with slide notes and a short controlled oral research-presentation excerpt, continuing Unité III.

Communication Scenario

Building on the report Sophie and Karim finalized in Chapter 13, Mme Girard now wants them to present their findings and recommendations orally at the next office meeting. Karim has never presented in French before an audience of colleagues and worries about losing his place or being interrupted before he finishes a point. Sophie reminds him that a good presentation manages the audience on purpose: it tells listeners when questions are welcome, points explicitly to each slide, and signals clearly when the talk is ending. Chapter 14 gives them the tools to open confidently, guide the audience through their visuals, and close cleanly.

  1. Open a presentation by stating its purpose.
  2. Point the audience to a specific slide, chart, or table at the right moment.
  3. Set clear expectations about interruptions and questions.
  4. Close the presentation with a distinct, recognizable signal.

No new sound-spelling or vowel feature is introduced in this chapter. The task is maintaining clear rhythm-group boundaries and a slightly slower, more deliberate pace suited to speaking before a group, especially around the visual-referencing connectors, which regularly open a new rhythm group and should be set off with a brief pause before the reference itself.

14.1 Opening and closing a presentation

Grammar Focus — Two ways to open, and a closing frame built for speech

Je vais vous présenter + noun phrase and l’objectif de cette présentation est de + infinitif are both newly productive opening frames. Je vais vous présenter names the topic directly and personally; l’objectif de cette présentation est de states the purpose more formally and impersonally, and pairs naturally with the nominalized style recycled from Chapter 11. Pour conclure is newly productive as this chapter’s spoken closing signal. Both pour conclure and en conclusion are standard French conclusion markers used in speech and in writing; the chapter does not treat them as grammatically restricted to one medium. It simply activates pour conclure as the productive target here, useful because it tells a live audience clearly that the talk itself is about to end, something listeners cannot see the way a reader can see the end of a page.

  • Bonjour à tous. Je vais vous présenter les résultats de notre étude sur la fréquentation du marché. Hello everyone. I'm going to present to you the results of our study on market attendance. je vais vous présenter + noun phrase, newly productive
  • Pour conclure, il conviendrait d’organiser une consultation avant toute décision définitive. To conclude, it would be advisable to organize a consultation before any final decision. pour conclure, newly productive spoken closing signal; recycled Chapter 13 recommendation frame

14.2 Pointing to a visual, and managing the audience

Building Block — An oral presentation shape (this chapter's practice template)
[opening: je vais vous présenter / l’objectif de cette présentation est de] + [audience management: n’hésitez pas à m’interrompre si / je prendrai vos questions à la fin] + [findings, staged with dans un premier temps / dans un second temps] + [visual reference: comme le montre + GN / on constate ici + nom ou que + proposition / ce graphique illustre + GN] + [recommendation: il conviendrait de / nous recommandons de] + [closing: pour conclure]
The opening frame, the visual-referencing connectors, the audience-management formulas, and pour conclure are new to this chapter; the staging connectors, the recommendation frames, and the passive recycle Chapter 13. The three visual-referencing connectors are not fully interchangeable: comme le montre + noun phrase attributes the observation to the visual itself; on constate ici, followed by a noun phrase or by que + clause, foregrounds what the audience should notice at this exact point; ce graphique illustre + noun phrase frames the visual as evidence for a claim already stated. A learner who wants a clausal alternative to ce graphique illustre can instead say ce graphique montre que + proposition. Choose the audience-management formula that matches your intent: n’hésitez pas à m’interrompre si invites questions throughout, while je prendrai vos questions à la fin asks the audience to wait, which suits a tightly timed presentation.
Bonjour à tous. L’objectif de cette présentation est de résumer notre étude sur le marché du quartier. Je prendrai vos questions à la fin, afin de respecter le temps qui nous est accordé. Dans un premier temps, comme le montre ce graphique, la fréquentation a baissé de dix pour cent en trois mois. Dans un second temps, on constate ici que cette baisse touche surtout les nouveaux arrivants. À l’appui de ce constat, il conviendrait d’organiser une consultation ciblée. Pour conclure, une communication plus claire avec les habitants reste l’enjeu principal de ce dossier.
Hello everyone. The purpose of this presentation is to summarize our study of the neighborhood market. I will take your questions at the end, in order to respect our allotted time. First, as this graph shows, attendance dropped ten percent over three months. Second, we can see here that this drop mainly affects new arrivals. In support of this finding, it would be advisable to organize a targeted consultation. To conclude, clearer communication with residents remains the main issue at stake in this matter.
Grammar Focus — Inviting interruption without losing control of the floor

N’hésitez pas à m’interrompre si + clause is a newly productive imperative formula that gives the audience explicit permission to interrupt under a stated condition, most often si quelque chose n’est pas clair. Because it names a condition, it keeps the presenter in control of when interruption is invited, rather than leaving the floor open indefinitely. Je prendrai vos questions à la fin uses the futur simple, recycled from Volume II, to make a clear commitment the audience can rely on.

  • N’hésitez pas à m’interrompre si un point n’est pas clair. Don't hesitate to interrupt me if a point isn't clear. n’hésitez pas à m’interrompre si + clause, newly productive

Model Dialogue: Répétition avant l’exposé

Context: Sophie and Karim rehearse Karim's part of the presentation the evening before the office meeting. Register: semi-formal working conversation, close to the register of the presentation itself.

Karim : Bonjour à tous. Je vais vous présenter les résultats de notre étude sur le marché du quartier. Hello everyone. I'm going to present to you the results of our study on the neighborhood market.
Sophie : Bien. Ensuite, précise si tu prends les questions tout de suite ou à la fin. Good. Then, specify whether you'll take questions right away or at the end.
Karim : Je prendrai vos questions à la fin, afin de respecter le temps qui nous est accordé. I will take your questions at the end, in order to respect the time we've been given.
Sophie : Et pour le graphique sur la baisse de fréquentation ? And for the graph about the attendance drop?
Karim : Comme le montre ce graphique, la fréquentation a baissé de dix pour cent en trois mois. As this graph shows, attendance dropped ten percent in three months.
Sophie : Bien. Et si quelqu’un ne comprend pas un terme pendant que tu parles ? Good. And if someone doesn't understand a term while you're speaking?
Karim : N’hésitez pas à m’interrompre si un terme n’est pas clair, même si je prends les questions à la fin pour le reste. Don't hesitate to interrupt me if a term isn't clear, even though I'm taking questions at the end for everything else.
Sophie : Parfait, ça rassure le public. Et pour terminer ? Perfect, that reassures the audience. And to finish?
Karim : Pour conclure, bien que la baisse reste modeste, une communication plus claire avec les habitants reste l’enjeu principal. To conclude, although the drop remains modest, clearer communication with residents remains the main issue at stake.

This dialogue models the newly productive opening frame (je vais vous présenter), the audience-management formulas (je prendrai vos questions à la fin, n’hésitez pas à m’interrompre si), the visual-referencing connector (comme le montre), the closing frame (pour conclure), and a recycled Volume III Chapter 19 concession. No unauthorized subjonctif trigger appears.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Managing an audience explicitly

In French academic and professional settings, presenters often make their expectations about questions and interruption explicit near the beginning of a talk, rather than leaving the audience to guess. This is a widely useful presentation strategy rather than a rule unique to French-speaking contexts: it is generally viewed as a courtesy to listeners and a way to protect the presenter's allotted time, and a talk that never addresses this can leave an audience unsure whether interrupting is welcome.

Regional Variation — Presentation conventions across the Francophone world

Expectations for how formal an oral presentation should be, how directly questions are invited, and how visual supports are referenced vary somewhat across French, Belgian, Swiss, Québécois, and other Francophone academic and professional traditions. This chapter’s vocabulary and patterns reflect common metropolitan French usage; the underlying skill of opening clearly, guiding an audience through visuals, and closing cleanly transfers across contexts even where specific conventions differ.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Never setting audience expectations

Error: Beginning directly with the content of the presentation, without ever indicating whether questions are welcome during the talk or only at the end.

Correction: State this explicitly near the opening, using n’hésitez pas à m’interrompre si or je prendrai vos questions à la fin. Leaving this unstated is not neutral; it leaves the audience uncertain.

Common Mistake — Referencing a visual without saying what it shows

Insufficient here: Saying regardez ce graphique and moving on, without stating the finding the visual is meant to support. The sentence itself is grammatical; the problem is that it does not communicate the finding.

Correction: Pair the reference with the claim it supports: comme le montre ce graphique, la fréquentation a baissé de dix pour cent. A visual reference alone does not communicate the finding to a listener who cannot examine the slide closely.

Common Mistake — Assuming pour conclure and en conclusion are restricted to different mediums

Chapter target: Both pour conclure and en conclusion are correct in spoken and written French. Chapter 14 practices pour conclure as its productive spoken closing signal, while en conclusion remains available from Chapter 13.

Dans un second temps, on constate ici que cette baisse touche surtout les nouveaux arrivants du quartier. À l’appui de ces constats, il conviendrait d’organiser une consultation ciblée avant toute décision définitive. Il serait également souhaitable d’envisager un marché nocturne à titre d’essai. Pour conclure, bien que la baisse reste modeste, une communication plus claire avec l’ensemble des habitants demeure l’enjeu principal de ce dossier.

Hello everyone. I'm going to present to you the results of our study of the neighborhood market.

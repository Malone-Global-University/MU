Décrire des tendances sociales

Social Trends · Speak and Write French: Professional, Academic, Media, and Institutional French

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Describe a social or workplace trend in speech and in writing: what is changing, by how much, and over what period, using this chapter's newly productive data-commentary and comparison tools.
  2. Use two newly productive data-commentary verbs — on observe que and on constate que — plus the indicatif, to report a statistical observation.
  3. Use newly productive formal contrast connectors — tandis que and alors que — and newly productive comparison frames — par rapport à and comparativement à — to compare two figures, groups, or periods.
  4. Recycle the already-active epistemic conditional and sembler + infinitif to phrase a cautious, source-distanced claim about a statistic, without introducing any new subjonctif environment.
  5. Recycle the eleven Chapter 8–11 subjonctif triggers, the Chapter 18 negative-belief/negative-certainty environment, and the Chapter 19 bien que/quoique concession environment inside a data-commentary task, without any new productive trigger.
  6. Add and recycle a curated data-commentary vocabulary set: une hausse, une baisse, une augmentation, une diminution, évoluer, une tendance, avoir tendance à, and au cours de.
  7. Recycle dont, the Chapter 2 impersonal-plus-infinitive frames, the Chapter 4 cause/consequence connectors and two fixed fronted frames, and the Chapter 6 source-framing expressions and reported-speech verbs, applied to a data-commentary task.
  8. Write a short data-commentary summary (six to nine sentences) describing a trend, its comparison points, and a cautious claim about its likely cause.
  9. Move to a controlled introduction to a short data-commentary article with a simple statistics table and a short radio-style trends bulletin.

Communication Scenario

Sophie's news round-up from Chapter 6 went well enough that Mme Girard has asked her to prepare a second, slightly different segment for next Friday: not a single news item, but a short commentary on a trend relevant to the office — the rise of remote work (le télétravail) among the Lyon team since the start of the year. Karim has found a short internal report with a simple table of figures, and Sophie needs to describe what the numbers show: what has gone up, what has gone down, how this compares across departments, and what might explain it — without overstating what the data actually proves. Chapter 7 gives Sophie, Karim, and you the tools to describe a trend responsibly: naming the change, comparing figures, and making a claim cautiously rather than as settled fact.

  1. Describe what a figure or trend shows, using this chapter's data-commentary vocabulary.
  2. Compare two figures, groups, or periods using this chapter's comparison and contrast tools.
  3. State a likely explanation cautiously, using the epistemic conditional or sembler + infinitif.
  4. Summarize the trend briefly and clearly for colleagues.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Giving numbers and comparisons clearly

No new sound-spelling or vowel feature is introduced in this chapter. The task is applying already-active rhythm and number pronunciation to spoken comparisons, so that a listener can follow which figure is higher, which is lower, and over what period.

  • Stating a comparison: Le taux de télétravail a augmenté de quinze pour cent par rapport à l’an dernier. — a slight emphasis on augmenté and on the figure quinze pour cent, with a brief pause before par rapport à.
  • Review the required liaison in dix_ans and les_habitudes, and the enchaînement in elle a évolué. Note that pour cent is pronounced as two separate, clearly articulated words, not run together as in English "percent."

Grammar: Décrire des tendances sociales

Purpose: introduce two data-commentary reporting verbs (on observe que, on constate que + indicatif) and newly productive contrast/comparison connectors (tandis que, alors que, par rapport à, comparativement à); recycle the already-active epistemic conditional and sembler + infinitif for cautious claims about statistics. Combine these with already-active subjonctif environments, dont, the Chapter 4 connectors and fronted frames, and the Chapter 6 source-framing expressions. No new subjonctif trigger is introduced. Lequel and the passive voice remain available for accurate use but are not targets of this chapter.

7.1 Reporting an observation: on observe que, on constate que

Grammar Focus — On observe que / on constate que + indicatif

On observe que and on constate que are newly productive in this chapter. Both introduce a data-based observation and take the indicatif, since they present a pattern in the data as an established fact rather than a doubt, wish, or necessity. Constater often carries a slightly stronger sense of noting something confirmed by evidence than observer, though the two are largely interchangeable in this chapter's data-commentary use.

  • On observe que le télétravail a augmenté au cours des six derniers mois. One observes that remote work has increased over the past six months. on observe que + indicatif, newly productive
  • On constate que cette hausse touche surtout les équipes administratives. One notes that this rise mainly affects administrative teams. on constate que + indicatif, newly productive

7.2 Comparing figures: par rapport à, comparativement à, tandis que, alors que

Building Block — A useful data-commentary shape (this chapter's practice template)
[observation: on observe que / on constate que + indicatif] + [comparison: par rapport à / comparativement à] + [contrast: tandis que / alors que, if two groups or periods differ] + [cautious explanation: conditionnel / sembler + infinitif]
The observation verbs and comparison/contrast connectors are new to this chapter; the cautious-explanation step recycles the epistemic conditional and sembler + infinitif, both already active. Not every commentary needs every element: a short observation may state only the figure and its comparison point.
On observe que le télétravail a augmenté de quinze pour cent par rapport à l’an dernier. Le service communication a largement adopté cette pratique, tandis que le service comptabilité reste plus traditionnel. Cette différence s’expliquerait, du moins en partie, par la nature des tâches de chaque service.
One observes that remote work has increased by fifteen percent compared to last year. The communications department has widely adopted this practice, whereas the accounting department remains more traditional. This difference would seem to be explained, at least in part, by the nature of each department's tasks.
Grammar Focus — Cautious claims: the epistemic conditional and sembler + infinitif

Both tools below are already active from earlier volumes; this chapter simply extends their use to data commentary. The epistemic conditional signals that a claim is reported or not fully confirmed — useful when a cause is plausible but not proven. Sembler + infinitif performs a similar function without needing a distinct verb form.

  • Cette hausse s’expliquerait par un changement d’habitudes depuis la pandémie. This rise would seem to be explained by a change in habits since the pandemic. conditionnel présent, epistemic/cautious use, recycled
  • Le phénomène semble s’accentuer d’une année sur l’autre. The phenomenon seems to be intensifying from one year to the next. sembler + infinitif, recycled

Neither construction requires the subjonctif: the conditional is a verb tense, and sembler here takes a direct infinitive rather than a que clause. This is a deliberate choice for this chapter, so that cautious claims about data can be phrased accurately without adding a new subjonctif environment.

Model Dialogue: Le télétravail, en hausse ou en baisse ?

Context: Sophie and Karim review the internal report before Sophie's Friday trends commentary. Register: semi-formal working conversation between colleagues.

Karim : Voici le rapport interne. On observe que le télétravail a augmenté de quinze pour cent au cours des six derniers mois. Here's the internal report. One observes that remote work has increased by fifteen percent over the past six months.
Sophie : Intéressant. Et cette hausse, est-elle la même dans tous les services ? Interesting. And is this rise the same across all departments?
Karim : Non. On constate que le service communication a largement adopté cette pratique, tandis que le service comptabilité reste plus traditionnel. No. One notes that the communications department has widely adopted this practice, whereas the accounting department remains more traditional.
Sophie : L’écart entre les deux services semble plus net que l’an dernier. The gap between the two departments seems wider than last year.
Karim : Cette différence s’expliquerait, selon le rapport, par la nature des tâches de chaque service : certaines exigent davantage de présence sur place. This difference would seem to be explained, according to the report, by the nature of each department's tasks: some require more in-person presence.
Sophie : Bien que ce chiffre soit clair, il faut que je reste prudente dans ma présentation ; le rapport ne couvre qu’un seul bureau. Although this figure is clear, I need to remain cautious in my presentation; the report only covers one office.
Karim : Bonne idée. Je t’envoie le tableau complet ce soir. Good idea. I'll send you the full table tonight.

This dialogue models the newly productive data-commentary verbs (on observe que, on constate que), the newly productive comparison and contrast frames (par rapport à, tandis que), a recycled cautious claim (s’expliquerait, conditionnel), a comparative construction used without a redundant par rapport à (semble plus net que l’an dernier), a recycled Chapter 6 source frame (selon le rapport), a recycled Chapter 19 concession, and a recycled Chapter 8–11 trigger. No unauthorized subjonctif trigger appears.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Speaking about data cautiously

In French professional and journalistic commentary, writers often distinguish clearly between what data shows directly (on observe que…) and what might explain it (cela s’expliquerait par…). This distinction matters: presenting a plausible explanation as a proven fact overstates what the data supports. The conditional and sembler are useful precisely because they let a writer offer an explanation while marking it as not yet fully established.

Regional Variation — Data commentary across Francophone contexts

Conventions for presenting statistics — how numbers are formatted, which comparison points are typical, and how directly a source states a conclusion — vary somewhat across France, Québec, Belgium, Switzerland, and other Francophone contexts. This chapter's vocabulary and patterns reflect common metropolitan French professional usage; the underlying skill of observing, comparing, and explaining cautiously transfers across contexts even where specific conventions differ.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Stating an explanation as settled fact

Error: Writing *cette différence s’explique par la nature des tâches (présent) when the data does not actually confirm the cause, overstating what is known.

Correction: Use the conditionnel (cette différence s’expliquerait par…) or sembler + infinitif (cette différence semble s’expliquer par…) when the cause is plausible but not directly established by the data itself.

Common Mistake — Using the subjonctif after on observe que or on constate que

Error: Writing *on observe que le télétravail ait augmenté, using the subjonctif after a verb that reports a data-based fact.

Correction: On observe que le télétravail a augmenté. Like dire que and déclarer que, both new verbs take the indicatif, since they present an observed pattern as fact.

Common Mistake — Combining par rapport à with a comparative form

Error: Writing *par rapport à l’an dernier, le service comptabilité évolue plus lentement, stacking par rapport à together with a comparative already built with plus or moins. The two constructions duplicate the same comparison.

Correction: Choose one comparison strategy per sentence. Either keep par rapport à (or comparativement à) and drop the comparative form — comparativement à l’an dernier, le taux a augmenté — or keep the comparative form and use que instead — le service comptabilité évolue plus lentement que le service communication.

Common Mistake — Confusing tandis que/alors que with parce que

Error: Using tandis que or alors que where a cause is intended, producing a confusing sentence.

Correction: Tandis que and alors que mark a contrast between two things, not a cause. To state a cause, use parce que, car, puisque, étant donné que, or du fait que, recycled from Chapter 4.

Modèle — Un commentaire de données

On observe que le télétravail a augmenté dans les trois services du bureau de Lyon au cours de l’année écoulée. Le service communication connaît la hausse la plus nette, avec un taux qui passe de vingt à quarante-deux pour cent. One observes that remote work has increased across all three departments of the Lyon office over the past year. The communications department shows the sharpest rise, with a rate going from twenty to forty-two percent. Compared to communication, accounting evolves much more slowly, while human resources falls in between. This difference would seem to be explained by the nature of the tasks assigned to each department. Although these figures are clear, it would be advisable to remain cautious, since the report covers only one office. One notes, nonetheless, a general upward trend in remote work across the three departments observed at the Lyon office.

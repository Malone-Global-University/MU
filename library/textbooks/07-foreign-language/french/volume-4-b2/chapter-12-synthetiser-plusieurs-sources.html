Synthétiser plusieurs sources

Source Synthesis · Speak and Write French: Professional, Academic, Media, and Institutional French

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Read two short related sources on the same question and identify where they agree, where they disagree, and where one simply adds to the other.
  2. Use two newly productive paraphrase/reformulation frames — autrement dit and en d’autres termes — to restate a source’s claim in your own words without misrepresenting it, and one newly productive source-wording frame — pour reprendre les termes de — to signal deliberate reuse of a source’s own wording, which may introduce a brief quoted expression.
  3. Use four newly productive source-comparison connectors, each with a distinct value — de même (a parallel point), à la différence de (a contrast), contrairement à (a stronger opposition), and de son côté (a neutral shift to another source) — to characterize precisely how two sources relate.
  4. Combine Chapter 6 source-framing (selon, d’après) with Chapter 11’s il ressort de…que across two sources in a single sentence, using this chapter’s newly productive two-source attribution pattern.
  5. Recycle the Chapter 7 data-commentary and comparison tools, the Chapter 8 concession and counterargument tools, the Chapter 9 civic-institutional framing, and Chapter 11’s nominalization, paragraph-logic connectors, and academic reporting verbs, applied to a two-source synthesis task, without any new productive subjonctif trigger.
  6. Add and recycle a curated source-synthesis vocabulary set: résumer, paraphraser, reformuler, une source, un point commun, une divergence, converger, diverger, se rejoindre, and s’opposer à.
  7. Write a short source-synthesis paragraph (seven to ten sentences) that reports two sources, paraphrases at least one claim, and states clearly whether the sources converge or diverge.
  8. Move to a controlled two-source reading dossier and a short controlled academic discussion comparing two sources, continuing Unité III.

Communication Scenario

After the seminar, Sophie has been asked to prepare a short written note comparing the article she read in Chapter 11 with a second, related study that takes a somewhat different view. Karim points out that simply summarizing each source one after another, in two separate paragraphs, does not really count as synthesis — the note needs to say clearly where the two sources agree, where they disagree, and where one goes further than the other. Chapter 12 gives Sophie the tools for exactly this: paraphrasing a claim accurately, comparing two sources directly, and combining attribution across sources in a single sentence.

  1. Identify the claim of a second, related source.
  2. Paraphrase a claim from either source without distorting it.
  3. State clearly whether the two sources converge or diverge, and on what point.
  4. Combine attribution to both sources within a single coherent synthesis.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Marking the shift between two sources

No new sound-spelling or vowel feature is introduced in this chapter. The task is marking, through a brief pause and a clear rhythm-group boundary, the moment a synthesis moves from one source to the other — especially at de son côté, which regularly opens a new rhythm group and signals that the second source is now speaking.

  • Marking the shift: La première étude soutient que l’information est le facteur principal. De son côté, la seconde étude met plutôt en avant le rôle des associations locales. — a clear pause before de son côté, separating it from the sentence that precedes it.
  • Review the required liaison in les_auteurs and the elision in s’opposer. Note that contrairement à flows as a single prosodic unit in connected speech; avoid imposing English-style lexical stress on the opening syllable con-.

12.1 Restating a claim: two paraphrase frames, and one source-wording frame

Grammar Focus — Two ways to paraphrase, one way to stay close to the source’s wording

Autrement dit and en d’autres termes are newly productive genuine paraphrase frames: both introduce a restatement of the idea that was just expressed, fully in the writer’s own words, and they are close to interchangeable, though en d’autres termes is slightly more formal. Pour reprendre les termes de + name or noun phrase belongs to a different category: rather than restating an idea in new words, it signals deliberate reuse of the source’s own wording, and it may introduce a brief quoted expression, as in the example below. Use it when a source’s specific phrasing carries meaning that a full paraphrase would lose; use autrement dit or en d’autres termes when the goal is a genuine restatement in different words.

  • L’auteure soutient que la complexité administrative décourage la participation. Autrement dit, des démarches plus simples encourageraient davantage de citoyens à s’engager. The author argues that administrative complexity discourages participation. In other words, simpler procedures would encourage more citizens to get involved. autrement dit, newly productive
  • Pour reprendre les termes de la seconde étude, il s’agit d’un « déficit de confiance » plutôt que d’un simple manque d’information. To use the second study’s own terms, it is a matter of a “trust deficit” rather than a simple lack of information. pour reprendre les termes de + noun phrase, newly productive source-wording frame; note the brief quoted expression it introduces

12.2 Comparing two sources with precision: de même, à la différence de, contrairement à, de son côté

Building Block — A two-source synthesis shape (this chapter's practice template)
[source 1: selon / il ressort de…que + claim] + [de son côté / de même / à la différence de / contrairement à + source 2: claim] + [paraphrase: autrement dit / en d’autres termes] + [convergence or divergence stated: converger / diverger / se rejoindre / s’opposer à]
The comparison connectors and the paraphrase frames are new to this chapter; the reporting verbs, nominalization, and academic vocabulary recycle Chapter 11. The four connectors are not interchangeable synonyms for agreement or disagreement. De même marks a parallel or similar point between two sources; it supports, but does not by itself constitute, a claim of convergence. À la différence de marks a contrast, which is not automatically a disagreement — two sources can differ in emphasis without contradicting each other. Contrairement à marks a stronger opposition or genuine contradiction. De son côté is neutral: it simply shifts to the second source’s perspective without committing in advance to agreement or disagreement, leaving the verb (converger, diverger, se rejoindre, s’opposer à) or an explicit statement to establish the actual relationship.
Selon la première étude, la participation citoyenne dépend surtout de la clarté de l’information. De son côté, la seconde étude met plutôt en avant un déficit de confiance envers les institutions. Autrement dit, les deux sources ne s’opposent pas totalement : elles décrivent deux obstacles différents à un même problème. Il ressort néanmoins des deux études qu’une communication institutionnelle plus claire resterait utile, indépendamment de la cause principale retenue.
According to the first study, civic participation depends mainly on the clarity of information. For its part, the second study instead highlights a deficit of trust toward institutions. In other words, the two sources are not entirely opposed: they describe two different obstacles to the same problem. It nonetheless emerges from both studies that clearer institutional communication would remain useful, whichever main cause one accepts.
Grammar Focus — A two-source attribution pattern

Chapter 6 introduced selon and d’après for a single source; Chapter 11 introduced il ressort de + noun phrase + que for what a study shows. This chapter combines them productively: a synthesis sentence may open with one attribution frame for the first source and close with the other for the second, or use il ressort des deux études que… to attribute a shared conclusion to both sources at once. Neither frame changes; only the noun phrase after de becomes plural and definite when both sources are cited together, so the recycled contraction de + les → des applies: de + les deux études → des deux études.

  • Selon la première étude, l’information est le facteur principal ; il ressort en revanche de la seconde que la confiance joue un rôle plus important. According to the first study, information is the main factor; it emerges, however, from the second that trust plays a more important role. selon (source 1) + il ressort de (source 2) + que, newly productive combination
  • Il ressort des deux études qu’une communication institutionnelle plus claire resterait utile. It emerges from both studies that clearer institutional communication would remain useful. il ressort des deux études que… = de + les → des, shared conclusion across sources

Model Dialogue: Deux points de vue

Context: Sophie and Karim compare the Chapter 11 article with a second study Karim has found on the same question. Register: semi-formal working conversation, academic in vocabulary.

Sophie : J’ai trouvé une seconde étude sur la participation citoyenne. Elle ne dit pas tout à fait la même chose que la première. I found a second study on civic participation. It doesn't say quite the same thing as the first.
Karim : Ah bon ? Selon la première, l’information est le facteur principal. Qu’en dit la seconde ? Really? According to the first, information is the main factor. What does the second say?
Sophie : De son côté, elle met plutôt en avant un déficit de confiance envers les institutions. For its part, it instead highlights a deficit of trust toward institutions.
Karim : Donc elles s’opposent complètement ? So they're completely opposed?
Sophie : Pas totalement. Autrement dit, elles décrivent deux obstacles différents à un même problème plutôt que de se contredire. Not entirely. In other words, they describe two different obstacles to the same problem rather than contradicting each other.
Karim : Et il ressort des deux études qu’une meilleure communication institutionnelle resterait utile, non ? And it emerges from both studies that better institutional communication would remain useful, right?
Sophie : Exactement. Bien que les deux études divergent sur la cause principale, elles se rejoignent sur cette conclusion. Exactly. Although the two studies diverge on the main cause, they align on that conclusion.

This dialogue models the newly productive paraphrase framing (autrement dit), the newly productive comparison connector (de son côté), the newly productive two-source attribution pattern (il ressort des deux études que), and this chapter’s vocabulary (s’opposer à, se rejoindre, diverger), alongside a recycled Volume III Chapter 19 concession. No unauthorized subjonctif trigger appears.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Synthesis as a valued academic skill

In French academic and professional writing, being able to compare sources fairly — showing where they agree and where they genuinely disagree, without flattening either into a caricature of the other — is often treated as a marker of careful, credible work. A synthesis that only lists two summaries side by side, without stating their relationship, is generally considered incomplete in this tradition.

Regional Variation — Source-comparison conventions across the Francophone world

Expectations for how explicitly a synthesis should state agreement or disagreement between sources vary somewhat across French, Belgian, Swiss, Québécois, and other Francophone academic traditions. This chapter’s vocabulary and patterns reflect common metropolitan French academic usage; the underlying skill of comparing sources fairly transfers across contexts even where specific conventions differ.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Summarizing two sources without comparing them

Error: Writing one paragraph about source 1, then a second, unconnected paragraph about source 2, with no sentence stating how they relate.

Correction: A synthesis must state the relationship explicitly, using de même, à la différence de, contrairement à, converger, diverger, se rejoindre, or s’opposer à. Two summaries placed side by side are not yet a synthesis.

Common Mistake — Paraphrasing so loosely that the claim changes

Error: Using autrement dit to introduce a new claim rather than a restatement of the one just given, subtly changing what the source said.

Correction: A paraphrase introduced by autrement dit or en d’autres termes must restate the same idea in different words, not add new information or shift the claim’s meaning. If the source did not say it, do not attribute it through a paraphrase.

Common Mistake — Making the plural agree incorrectly in il ressort des deux études que

Error: Writing *il ressort de deux études que, dropping the required article after de when the noun phrase is plural and definite.

Correction: When attributing a shared conclusion to two specific, already-mentioned sources, use the contracted plural article: il ressort des deux études que… (de + les → des), recycled from earlier volumes. Only an indefinite, non-specific plural noun phrase would omit the article, as in a different context such as il ressort d’études récentes que….

À la différence de la première étude, celle-ci ne mentionne pas la complexité administrative comme facteur déterminant. Autrement dit, les deux sources ne se contredisent pas complètement : elles décrivent deux obstacles distincts à un même problème. Il ressort néanmoins des deux études qu’une communication institutionnelle plus claire resterait utile. Bien que les deux équipes divergent sur la cause principale, elles se rejoignent sur la nécessité d’agir.

According to a first study, civic participation depends directly on the clarity of available information. This study demonstrates that poorly informed residents vote noticeably less often than others. For its part, a second study instead highlights a deficit of trust toward local institutions. Unlike the first study, this one does not mention administrative complexity as a determining factor. In other words, the two sources do not fully contradict each other: they describe two distinct obstacles to the same problem. It nonetheless emerges from both studies that clearer institutional communication would remain useful. Although the two teams diverge on the main cause, they align on the need to act.

This seven-sentence model attributes a claim to each source (selon, de son côté), marks a contrast of emphasis rather than a contradiction (à la différence de), paraphrases the relationship (autrement dit), states a shared conclusion (il ressort des deux études que), and recycles the Volume III Chapter 19 concession environment (bien que…divergent) in its closing sentence.

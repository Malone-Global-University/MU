Négocier et résoudre un conflit

Negotiation and Conflict Resolution · Speak and Write French: Professional, Academic, Media, and Institutional French

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Distinguish a nuanced opinion (Chapter 16) and a cultural narrative (Chapter 17) from a negotiation, in which two parties with different interests must move toward a workable outcome.
  2. Use a newly productive diplomatic proposal frame — peut-être conviendrait-il de + infinitif — to float a suggestion gently, without presenting it as a demand.
  3. Use a newly productive mediation consensus frame — les deux parties s’accordent à reconnaître que + indicative — to record a point on which two disagreeing sides genuinely agree.
  4. Use a newly productive conditional-proviso frame — sous réserve de + groupe nominal or infinitif — to accept a proposal while attaching a clear condition, without a subjonctif clause.
  5. Recycle the Chapter 16 nuance-and-tone toolkit, the Chapter 17 attribution toolkit, the Chapter 8 concession-and-reassertion frame, the Chapter 4 emphatic cleft, the Chapter 6 source-framing toolkit, and the Chapter 9 civic-institutional framing, applying them to negotiation and mediation rather than opinion or narrative, without any new productive subjonctif trigger.
  6. Receive further controlled, receptive-only exposure to a small mediation-register vocabulary set.
  7. Add and recycle a curated negotiation and mediation vocabulary set: un différend, un compromis, une concession, négocier, céder du terrain, trouver un terrain d’entente, and un médiateur / une médiatrice.
  8. Write a short mediation summary (six to nine sentences) proposing a compromise between two named positions, using all three newly productive tools.
  9. Move to a short adapted mediation report about a parking dispute and a short controlled mediation session between two shopkeepers, continuing Unité IV.

Communication Scenario

The parking debate that ran through Chapters 6–10 has resurfaced, this time as a direct disagreement between two shopkeepers: M. Belhadj, who wants more parking spaces near his hardware store, and Mme Ferrand, who wants the space kept open for the market's weekend stalls. Mme Girard asks Sophie and Karim to sit in on a short mediation session and then write up a brief, neutral summary for the association's records — not to take a side, but to identify where the two shopkeepers actually agree, propose a workable compromise, and note the conditions under which each side would accept it. Chapter 18 gives Sophie and Karim the tools to propose, concede, and record an agreement without ever losing their neutrality.

  1. Float a proposal gently, without presenting it as a demand.
  2. Record a genuine point of agreement between two disagreeing parties.
  3. Accept a proposal while attaching a clear condition.
  4. Summarize a negotiation neutrally, favoring neither side.

Receptive Only — Mediation-register vocabulary

Learners should recognize, but are not yet expected to produce, the following mediation-register items: un litige (a dispute, closer to a legal or administrative register than un différend), à l’amiable (amicably; by mutual agreement; in legal contexts, out of court — commonly used in un règlement à l’amiable), and une impasse (a deadlock, an impasse). À l’amiable and une impasse appear directly in the Reading Practice passage below; un litige is practiced there as a near-synonym learners are asked to infer for the passage's un différend. Do not use any of these three items productively yet.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Sounding tentative versus sounding firm

No new sound-spelling or vowel feature is introduced in this chapter. The task is using delivery to keep a proposal genuinely open rather than accidentally sounding like a demand: a diplomatic proposal with peut-être conviendrait-il de generally rises slightly and stays light, almost inviting a reply, while a proviso with sous réserve de is usually delivered more evenly and firmly, since it states an actual condition rather than a soft suggestion. This is a performance possibility to draw on, not a fixed rule of French pronunciation; real delivery varies with speaker and context.

  • Proposing gently: Peut-être conviendrait-il d’envisager un compromis. — light, slightly rising delivery, inviting a response rather than closing the discussion.
  • Stating a condition firmly: Sous réserve de l’accord des deux parties, la proposition pourrait être retenue. — more even, deliberate pacing, with sous réserve de kept attached to its complement rather than isolated by a pause.

Grammar: Négocier et résoudre un conflit

Purpose: introduce a newly productive diplomatic proposal frame (peut-être conviendrait-il de + infinitif), a newly productive mediation consensus frame (les deux parties s’accordent à reconnaître que + indicative), and a newly productive conditional-proviso frame (sous réserve de + groupe nominal or infinitif), while recycling the Chapter 16 nuance toolkit, the Chapter 17 attribution toolkit, the Chapter 8 concession-and-reassertion frame, the Chapter 4 emphatic cleft, the Chapter 6 source-framing toolkit, and the Chapter 9 civic-institutional framing, now applied to negotiation and mediation. No new subjonctif trigger is introduced: all three newly productive frames take the indicative, the conditional, or a prepositional/infinitival complement rather than a que-clause in the subjonctif. Lequel and the passive voice remain available for accurate use but are not targets of this chapter.

18.1 Floating a proposal gently: peut-être conviendrait-il de

Grammar Focus — A suggestion, not a demand

Peut-être conviendrait-il de + infinitif is newly productive. Conviendrait is the conditional of convenir (here, impersonal: "it would be advisable"), and the inversion after peut-être is the same formal-register inversion recycled from earlier chapters (peut-être + inversion, rather than peut-être que + normal word order, which remains correct in less formal registers). The productive pattern taught in this chapter uses de + infinitif. Keep this chapter's new proposal frame in that form; other constructions with convenir, including il conviendrait que + subjonctif, exist in authentic formal French but are outside this chapter's new target and are not required here. Use the infinitive frame to open a negotiation with a suggestion the other side can accept, modify, or decline without losing face.

  • Peut-être conviendrait-il d’envisager un compromis sur les horaires. Perhaps it would be advisable to consider a compromise on the hours. peut-être + inversion + conviendrait de + infinitif, newly productive

18.2 Recording real agreement: les deux parties s’accordent à reconnaître que

Grammar Focus — Naming common ground precisely

Les deux parties s’accordent à reconnaître que + indicative is newly productive. It is a formal consensus frame especially useful in mediation: it does not merely say that both sides agree in general, but that they explicitly recognize a specific fact or point together, which is often the most useful sentence a mediator can write. The same construction also appears in agreements and institutional writing more broadly, so learners may encounter it outside mediation contexts as well. It takes the indicative, since it reports a settled point of agreement rather than a wish, doubt, or emotion. It contrasts with the Chapter 17 frame la mémoire collective retient que, which attributes a claim to a community's shared memory rather than to two named parties in an active negotiation.

  • Les deux parties s’accordent à reconnaître que le stationnement pose un problème réel. Both parties agree in recognizing that parking is a real problem. les deux parties s'accordent à reconnaître que + indicative, newly productive

18.3 Attaching a condition: sous réserve de

Building Block — A negotiation-summary shape (this chapter's practice template)
[différend: naming the dispute] + [les deux parties s’accordent à reconnaître que: the point of real agreement] + [peut-être conviendrait-il de: a gently floated proposal] + [Ch. 16 tool, if a claim needs capping or clarifying] + [sous réserve de: the condition attached to acceptance] + [Ch. 8 il n’en demeure pas moins que, if a concern remains despite the compromise]
Sous réserve de + groupe nominal or infinitif is newly productive. It attaches an explicit condition to an acceptance or a proposal, without requiring a subjonctif clause: sous réserve de l’accord des deux parties (subject to both parties' agreement) or sous réserve de confirmer les horaires (provided the hours are confirmed). It is more precise than a bare si-clause for formal mediation writing, since it reads as a standing proviso rather than a hypothetical scenario, and it keeps the whole negotiation summary in the indicative and conditional rather than introducing a new subjonctif trigger.
M. Belhadj et Mme Ferrand sont en désaccord sur l’usage de l’espace situé devant l’ancienne poste. Les deux parties s’accordent à reconnaître que le stationnement pose un problème réel les jours de marché. Peut-être conviendrait-il d’envisager un usage partagé de l’espace, avec des horaires distincts pour le stationnement et pour les étals. Tout au plus peut-on dire, à ce stade, qu’il s’agit d’une piste, non d’un accord définitif. Cette solution resterait envisageable sous réserve de l’accord formel des deux commerçants et de la mairie. Il n’en demeure pas moins que la question des jours de forte affluence devra être examinée séparément.
M. Belhadj and Mme Ferrand disagree over the use of the space in front of the old post office. Both parties agree in recognizing that parking is a real problem on market days. Perhaps it would be advisable to consider shared use of the space, with separate hours for parking and for the market stalls. At most one can say, at this stage, that this is a lead, not a final agreement. This solution would remain workable subject to the formal agreement of both shopkeepers and the town hall. It nonetheless remains the case that the question of high-traffic days will need to be examined separately.

Model Dialogue: Une médiation entre deux commerçants

Context: Mme Girard mediates a short session between M. Belhadj and Mme Ferrand about the disputed parking space, with Sophie taking notes. Register: formal, careful mediation speech.

Mme Girard : Vous êtes tous les deux d’accord, je crois, sur un point : le stationnement pose un vrai problème les jours de marché. You both agree, I believe, on one point: parking is a real problem on market days.
M. Belhadj : Oui, là-dessus, nous sommes d’accord. Mais pas sur la solution. Yes, on that point, we agree. But not on the solution.
Mme Ferrand : Exactement. Moi, j’ai besoin de cet espace pour mes étals. Exactly. I need that space for my stalls.
Mme Girard : Alors notons-le : les deux parties s’accordent à reconnaître que le stationnement pose un problème réel les jours de marché. Then let's note that: both parties agree in recognizing that parking is a real problem on market days.
Mme Girard : Peut-être conviendrait-il d’envisager un usage partagé : le stationnement en semaine, les étals le week-end. Perhaps it would be advisable to consider shared use: parking on weekdays, stalls on weekends.
M. Belhadj : Ce n’est pas idéal, mais je pourrais céder du terrain là-dessus. It's not ideal, but I could give some ground on that.
Mme Ferrand : Moi aussi. Tout au plus faudrait-il revoir les horaires précis ensemble. Me too. At most we'd need to work out the exact hours together.
Mme Girard : Alors on tient peut-être un compromis. Sous réserve de l’accord de la mairie, bien sûr. So we may have a compromise. Subject to the town hall's agreement, of course.
M. Belhadj : D’accord. Il n’en demeure pas moins qu’il faudra revoir la situation pendant les jours de forte affluence. Agreed. It nonetheless remains the case that we'll need to look at the situation again on high-traffic days.
Mme Girard : Tout à fait. Ce qui frappe surtout, c’est que vous êtes déjà d’accord sur bien plus de points que vous ne le pensiez. Quite right. What's most striking is that you already agree on far more points than you thought.

This dialogue models the three newly productive negotiation tools (peut-être conviendrait-il de, les deux parties s’accordent à reconnaître que, sous réserve de), the newly productive vocabulary items céder du terrain and compromis, the recycled Chapter 16 attenuator (tout au plus) and concession-and-reassertion (il n’en demeure pas moins que), and the recycled Chapter 4 emphatic cleft (ce qui frappe, c’est que). No unauthorized subjonctif trigger appears.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Neutrality as the mediator's discipline

One useful practice in many French professional and community mediation contexts is to identify genuine common ground before proposing any solution, and to state conditions explicitly rather than leaving them implied. A mediator who appears to favor one side, even subtly through word choice, risks losing the trust of both parties. This is a helpful working method this chapter draws on, not a claim about a single official standard followed in every negotiation everywhere.

Regional Variation — Mediation and dispute-resolution traditions

Formal and informal dispute-resolution practices vary across French, Belgian, Swiss, Québécois, and other Francophone administrative and community traditions, including how disputes are documented and what role a neutral third party plays. This chapter’s vocabulary and patterns reflect common metropolitan French usage; the underlying skill of proposing, conceding, and recording agreement neutrally transfers across contexts even where specific procedures differ.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Reaching for sous réserve que before it is activated

Out of scope for this chapter's new frame: sous réserve que la mairie soit d’accord. This is genuine, grammatical formal French — sous réserve que is a recognized conjunction, close in meaning to à condition que — but it is not the pattern this chapter makes productive, and it is easy to reach for by analogy before it has been taught.

Use here: sous réserve de l’accord de la mairie or sous réserve d’obtenir l’accord de la mairie, using sous réserve de with a groupe nominal or an infinitive, as activated in this chapter. Sous réserve que + subjonctif remains available for accurate use at a later, more advanced stage, but is not required or assessed here.

Common Mistake — Making les deux parties s’accordent à reconnaître que sound one-sided

Error: Using the frame to report only one side's view, such as *les deux parties s’accordent à reconnaître que M. Belhadj a raison.

Correction: Reserve this frame for a point both sides would independently confirm, stated neutrally: les deux parties s’accordent à reconnaître que le stationnement pose un problème. If only one side holds a view, attribute it to that side specifically instead.

Common Mistake — Floating a proposal that sounds like a demand

Error: Writing *il faut absolument envisager un usage partagé when the intent is a gentle, negotiable suggestion.

Correction: Use peut-être conviendrait-il de + infinitif to keep the proposal genuinely open: peut-être conviendrait-il d’envisager un usage partagé. Save firmer language for a point that truly is non-negotiable.

  2. A shared use of the space is proposed, introduced gently with peut-être conviendrait-il de and further capped with tout au plus.
  3. The compromise is conditional on the formal agreement of both shopkeepers and the town hall.
  4. The question of high-traffic days remains unresolved and will be revisited separately.

Reading Practice

  1. Both shopkeepers agree that parking is a real problem on market days, especially Saturday mornings.
  2. A shared-use compromise is proposed; it is marked as tentative through tout au plus and à proprement parler, aucune décision formelle n’a encore été prise.
  3. The compromise is conditional on the written agreement of both shopkeepers and validation by the town hall.
  4. Un litige could replace un différend in a more legal or administrative register; à l’amiable means "amicably, by mutual agreement, without formal legal proceedings"; une impasse means "a deadlock," a situation from which the discussion cannot move forward.

Writing Workshop — Model text

Modèle — Compte rendu de médiation

Le présent différend oppose deux familles voisines au sujet d’un arbre dont les branches dépassent sur la propriété voisine. Les deux parties s’accordent à reconnaître que l’arbre, planté il y a plus de trente ans, a une réelle valeur pour le quartier. Peut-être conviendrait-il d’envisager un élagage partiel plutôt qu’un abattage complet. Il est possible que cette solution ne satisfasse pas entièrement les deux familles, mais elle permettrait d’éviter une impasse durable. Cette solution resterait envisageable sous réserve d’obtenir un devis auprès d’un professionnel qualifié. Il n’en demeure pas moins qu’un accord écrit sera nécessaire avant toute intervention.

The present dispute concerns two neighboring families over a tree whose branches extend onto the neighboring property. Both parties agree in recognizing that the tree, planted more than thirty years ago, has real value for the neighborhood. Perhaps it would be advisable to consider partial pruning rather than complete removal. It is possible that this solution will not fully satisfy both families, but it would help avoid a lasting deadlock. This solution would remain workable subject to obtaining a quote from a qualified professional. It nonetheless remains the case that a written agreement will be necessary before any work is carried out.

This six-sentence model names the dispute, records genuine agreement (les deux parties s’accordent à reconnaître que), floats a proposal gently (peut-être conviendrait-il de), attaches a condition (sous réserve de), recycles a Volume III Chapter 8–11 subjonctif trigger (il est possible que…satisfasse), and closes with the recycled Chapter 8 concession-and-reassertion frame (il n’en demeure pas moins que). No subjonctif trigger beyond those already activated in Volumes I–III and Volume IV Chapters 1–18 appears. Other responses are acceptable when they meet all task requirements.

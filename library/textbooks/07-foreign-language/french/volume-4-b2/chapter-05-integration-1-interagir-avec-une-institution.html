Intégration 1 : Interagir avec une institution

Unit I Integration · Speak and Write French: Professional, Academic, Media, and Institutional French

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Combine formal self-presentation (Chapter 1), an administrative request (Chapter 2), meeting participation with reported speech (Chapter 3), and a clear explanation of a complex problem (Chapter 4) into a single connected institutional dossier and spoken interaction, using only grammar, vocabulary, and skills activated in Volumes I–III and Chapters 1–4 of this volume.
  2. Use the eleven Chapter 8–11 subjonctif triggers, the Chapter 18 negative-belief/negative-certainty environment, and the Chapter 19 bien que/quoique concession environment accurately and appropriately within the same extended task, without any new trigger.
  3. Use the Chapter 2 impersonal-plus-infinitive frames (il faut, il est nécessaire de, il convient de), the Chapter 3 reported-speech verbs and turn-taking formulas, and the Chapter 4 cause/consequence/clarification connectors and fronted frames as integrated tools rather than isolated drills.
  4. Recycle dont, purpose clauses, and consistent vous address across a longer, multi-part institutional performance.
  5. Plan, draft, revise, and deliver a spoken institutional interaction and a written dossier without introducing any unscheduled grammar, vocabulary domain, or register.
  6. Reflect on your progress across Unité I and identify which institutional communication tasks you can now complete with confidence before moving to Unité II.

Communication Scenario

Before Sophie and Karim's secondment paperwork moves fully into the Lyon office's system, Mme Girard asks them to prepare one consolidated dossier: a short formal self-introduction for the office directory, a record of the badge and parking requests from Chapter 2, a brief meeting summary reporting what was discussed and decided, and a short explanatory note accounting for the shipment delay from Chapter 4 — all addressed to the same internal reviewer. Chapter 5 introduces nothing new. It asks Sophie and Karim — and you — to bring together formal self-presentation, an administrative request, meeting participation, and problem explanation into one coherent institutional performance, using only what Unité I has already built.

  1. Introduce yourself formally and state your role and purpose (recycling Chapter 1).
  2. State a request or requirement using at least one impersonal-plus-infinitive frame (recycling Chapter 2).
  3. Report what was said in a meeting, using a turn-taking formula and reported speech (recycling Chapter 3).
  4. Explain a problem's cause and consequence, using at least one of this chapter's recycled connectors and one fronted frame (recycling Chapter 4).

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Sustaining register across a longer performance

No new sound-spelling, liaison, or vowel feature is introduced in this chapter. The demand here is sustaining accurate rhythm, liaison, and register-appropriate delivery across a longer, multi-part spoken interaction that moves between self-presentation, a request, a meeting report, and an explanation.

  • Before recording or presenting: review the required liaison in vous_avez and nous_avons (Chapter 2–3), the enchaînement in il est nécessaire (Chapter 2), and the elision in qu’elle a expliqué (Chapter 3).
  • Pacing across sections: a brief pause between your self-presentation, your request, your meeting report, and your explanation helps the listener track the shift between the four parts, just as the pause before mais in earlier chapters marked a shift within one sentence.

Grammar Focus — The subjonctif environments, together

No additional trigger and no interrogative-certainty subjonctif is introduced here.

Model Dialogue: Le point avant l’envoi du dossier

Context: Sophie and Karim review the consolidated dossier together before sending it to the internal reviewer. Register: formal, professional, colleague-to-colleague.

Sophie : Récapitulons. Il nous faut une présentation, une demande, un point de réunion, et une explication. Quatre parties, un seul dossier. Let's recap. We need a presentation, a request, a meeting point, and an explanation. Four parts, one dossier.
Karim : Pour la présentation, je précise mon rôle : je suis chargé du projet francophonie dans le cadre de mon détachement. For the presentation, I'll specify my role: I am responsible for the francophonie project as part of my secondment.
Sophie : D’accord. Pour la demande, il est nécessaire de joindre un justificatif de domicile, un document dont le service a besoin. Agreed. For the request, it's necessary to attach proof of address, a document the department needs.
Karim : Si je peux me permettre, pour le point de réunion, je rappelle que Mme Girard a dit qu’il conviendrait de contacter le service financier cette semaine. If I may, for the meeting point, I'll note that Mme Girard said it would be advisable to contact the finance department this week.
Sophie : Bien. Et pour l’explication, étant donné que le fournisseur a changé d’itinéraire, la livraison a pris du retard. Good. And for the explanation, given that the supplier changed its route, the delivery was delayed.
Karim : Ce qui pose problème, c’est surtout que personne n’a été informé à temps. Bien que la situation soit regrettable, il ne s’agit pas d’une erreur de notre équipe. What's the problem is mostly that no one was informed in time. Although the situation is unfortunate, it's not our team's error.
Sophie : Tout à fait. Il faut que nous relisions le dossier une dernière fois avant de l’envoyer. Exactly. We need to reread the dossier one last time before sending it.
Karim : Parfait. Je rassemble les quatre parties et je te les envoie ce soir. Perfect. I'll put the four parts together and send them to you tonight.

This dialogue models all four recycled shapes in miniature: formal self-presentation, an administrative request with dont and an impersonal frame, a turn-taking formula with reported speech, and a cause/consequence explanation with a fronted frame and a recycled concession. No unauthorized subjonctif trigger appears; vous conventions from Chapters 1–4 are maintained wherever the dossier addresses the institution.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — A consolidated institutional voice

Across Chapters 1–4, Sophie and Karim's institutional communication moved from a single formal introduction to a full four-part dossier, without ever abandoning consistent register, precise requests, and careful, fact-first explanation. That combination — stating a role clearly, requesting precisely, reporting fairly, and explaining without premature blame — is a transferable communicative skill useful well beyond this specific dossier.

Regional Variation — A closing reminder for Unité I

Nothing in this chapter authorizes new regional production. As throughout Unité I, specific document names, meeting conventions, and explanatory habits vary across Francophone institutions; the underlying communicative strategies practiced here — clear self-presentation, precise requests, fair reporting, and careful explanation — transfer across contexts even where specific terms and conventions differ.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Treating the four parts as unrelated exercises

Error: Writing four disconnected paragraphs on four unrelated topics, each technically meeting its own requirements but reading as a set of separate drills.

Correction: Choose one institutional situation that can genuinely support all four parts, as Sophie and Karim do with their secondment dossier, so the piece reads as one coherent dossier.

Common Mistake — Blurring the three subjonctif environments

Error: Using the indicatif after a Chapter 18 negated-certainty verb or a Chapter 19 bien que clause, out of habit from simpler earlier sentences.

Correction: Check each subjonctif environment against its own trigger: the eleven Chapter 8–11 triggers, negated belief/certainty (Chapter 18), and bien que/quoique (Chapter 19) all require the subjonctif in the clause that follows.

Common Mistake — Reaching for new material under integration pressure

Error: Introducing a new subjonctif trigger or a fronted structure beyond the two Chapter 4 frames, to sound more advanced for this integration task.

Correction: This integration chapter rewards precise, confident use of everything already authorized through Chapter 4, not new, unauthorized material. A well-built dossier using only active systems demonstrates control more convincingly than a reach for unfamiliar forms. Lequel and the passive voice are not chapter targets, but correct use of either is acceptable if it arises naturally — and, as the model text shows, the passive often arises naturally in institutional reporting (le budget n’a pas encore été confirmé).

Writing Workshop — Model text

Modèle — Un dossier institutionnel en quatre parties

Présentation : Je m’appelle Sophie Marchand ; je suis chargée du dossier de détachement pour le bureau de Lyon dans le cadre d’un projet de six mois. Je travaille en étroite collaboration avec Karim Haddad sur le projet francophonie. Notre équipe a été formée en janvier, et chacun de nous s’occupe d’un aspect différent du détachement. Je suis, pour ma part, chargée des démarches administratives et du suivi du projet auprès de l’équipe locale.

Demande : Il est nécessaire de confirmer un justificatif de domicile et une pièce d’identité, deux documents dont le service administratif a besoin pour traiter mon dossier de badge. Il conviendrait également de préciser la durée exacte du badge demandé, puisque mon détachement pourrait être prolongé. Il faut que je transmette ces documents avant la fin de la semaine, faute de quoi le dossier ne pourra pas être traité à temps. Une fois ces documents envoyés, le badge devrait être délivré dans un délai d’environ deux semaines. Je resterai à la disposition du service si un document supplémentaire est demandé.

Réunion : Lors de la réunion du 14 mars, j’ai expliqué que le projet avançait bien, malgré quelques retards administratifs. Si je peux me permettre, je rappelle que Karim a ajouté un point sur le budget, dont l’équipe n’avait pas encore discuté ce jour-là. Mme Girard a déclaré qu’il conviendrait de contacter le service financier avant la fin de la semaine. Pour rebondir sur ce point, j’ai proposé que nous préparions ensemble une liste des dépenses prévues. L’équipe a estimé qu’il fallait que cette liste soit prête avant la prochaine rencontre avec les partenaires locaux.

Explication : Étant donné que le budget n’a pas encore été confirmé, certaines étapes du projet restent en attente. De ce fait, la prochaine rencontre avec l’équipe locale pourrait être reportée d’une à deux semaines. Ce qui pose problème, c’est surtout le délai que cela entraîne pour les collègues qui attendent une réponse depuis plusieurs jours. Ce retard est également dû, dans une moindre mesure, à un changement récent au sein du service financier. Bien que la situation soit temporairement compliquée, il ne s’agit pas d’un obstacle majeur. Autrement dit, il suffit d’attendre la confirmation du budget pour que le projet reprenne son rythme normal. Il conviendrait de faire le point à nouveau lors de la prochaine réunion, une fois que le budget aura été confirmé.

Presentation: My name is Sophie Marchand; I am responsible for the secondment file for the Lyon office as part of a six-month project. I work closely with Karim Haddad on the francophonie project. Our team was formed in January, and each of us handles a different aspect of the secondment. I, for my part, am responsible for administrative procedures and for following up with the local team. Request: It is necessary to confirm proof of address and an ID, two documents the administrative department needs to process my badge file. It would also be advisable to specify the exact duration of the requested badge, since my secondment could be extended. I need to submit these documents by the end of the week, or the file will not be processed in time. Once these documents are sent, the badge should be issued within about two weeks. I will remain available to the department if an additional document is requested. Meeting: During the March 14 meeting, I explained that the project was going well, despite a few administrative delays. If I may, I'll note that Karim added a point about the budget, which the team hadn't yet discussed that day. Mme Girard stated that it would be advisable to contact the finance department before the end of the week. To follow up on that, I proposed that we prepare a list of planned expenses together. The team felt that this list needed to be ready before the next meeting with the local partners. Explanation: Given that the budget has not yet been confirmed, some project steps remain pending. As a result, the next meeting with the local team could be postponed by one to two weeks. What's a problem is mainly the delay this causes for colleagues who have been awaiting a response for several days. This delay is also due, to a lesser extent, to a recent change within the finance department. Although the situation is temporarily complicated, it is not a major obstacle. In other words, it is enough to wait for the budget to be confirmed for the project to resume its normal pace. It would be advisable to take stock of it again at the next meeting, once the budget has been confirmed.

This four-part model (21 sentences total: 4 présentation, 5 demande, 5 réunion, 7 explication) meets the Writing Workshop's 20–32-sentence requirement and follows all four recycled shapes precisely: the Chapter 1 formal self-presentation, the Chapter 2 request with an impersonal frame and dont, the Chapter 3 meeting report with a turn-taking formula and reported speech, and the Chapter 4 explanation with cause, consequence, and a fronted frame. It also includes an original Chapter 8–11 trigger (il faut que je transmette and il fallait que cette liste soit prête) alongside the Chapter 19 concession environment (bien que…soit, explication). No subjonctif trigger beyond those authorized through Chapter 19 is used. The réunion section also includes a naturally occurring passive (le budget n’avait pas encore été confirmé) and the explication section includes another (le budget n’a pas encore été confirmé; pourrait être reportée), consistent with this chapter's policy that lequel and the passive voice are not required but are acceptable when they arise naturally.

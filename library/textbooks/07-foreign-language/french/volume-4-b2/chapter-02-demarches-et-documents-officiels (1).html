Démarches et documents officiels

Administrative Procedures · Speak and Write French: Professional, Academic, Media, and Institutional French

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Make a formal request, in speech and in writing, for an administrative procedure or an official document, recycling and extending impersonal requirement structures into this chapter's small administrative vocabulary set.
  2. Recycle impersonal expressions plus the infinitive — il faut and il est nécessaire de — and add il convient de as a newly productive, more formal register-sensitive frame, to state a requirement or a recommended step, without requiring a new subjonctif trigger.
  3. Recycle the eleven Chapter 8–11 subjonctif triggers, the Chapter 18 negative-belief/negative-certainty environment, and the Chapter 19 bien que/quoique concession environment inside administrative requests, including il faut que, which remains fully active alongside this chapter's impersonal-plus-infinitive frames.
  4. Add and recycle a curated administrative vocabulary set: un formulaire, une pièce justificative, un dossier, une démarche (recycled from Chapter 1), un délai, un justificatif de domicile, remplir, joindre, and à l’attention de.
  5. Recycle dont, purpose clauses with pour que, and tu/vous from Chapter 1, applied to an administrative-request task.
  6. Write a short formal letter or email requesting an administrative procedure, correctly formatted and addressed, observing French administrative correspondence conventions.
  7. Move to a controlled introduction to short institutional and administrative texts, including a short form and an administrative notice, recognizing their basic organization and purpose.

Communication Scenario

Sophie's secondment to the Lyon office requires her to complete several administrative steps: requesting a parking permit for the building, and registering for a temporary building access badge through the office's internal administrative service. Karim, meanwhile, has been asked to help a newly arrived colleague understand what documents are needed to enroll in the company's professional training program. Chapter 2 turns from formal self-presentation to the practical language of administrative life: making a request, explaining what is required, and understanding a form or an official notice.

  1. State clearly what procedure or document you need, and why.
  2. Use impersonal structures to explain what is required or recommended.
  3. Explain what is needed, using this chapter's vocabulary, and ask about or state a relevant timeframe.
  4. Write a correctly formatted administrative request letter or email.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Clear delivery of lists and requirements

No new sound-spelling or vowel feature is introduced in this chapter. The task is applying already-active rhythm and pausing patterns to spoken lists of requirements, so that a listener can follow each item distinctly.

  • Listing requirements: Il faut remplir le formulaire, joindre un justificatif de domicile et déposer le dossier avant le trente juin. — a brief pause after each comma-separated item, with a falling pitch at the final item and no comma before the final et.
  • Review the enchaînement in il est nécessaire, where the already-pronounced final consonant of il links smoothly into the vowel-initial est, and the required liaison in vous avez and un autre document. Review also the elision in il convient d’apporter. Note that un justificatif takes no liaison, since justificatif begins with the consonant sound /ʒ/.

Grammar: Démarches et documents officiels

Purpose: recycle and extend impersonal expressions plus the infinitive — il faut and il est nécessaire de — into administrative requests, and add il convient de as a newly productive, more formal register-sensitive frame. Combine these with already-active subjonctif environments, dont, and purpose clauses in the context of an administrative request. No new subjonctif trigger is introduced; il faut que remains one of the eleven Chapter 8–11 triggers and stays fully active alongside these impersonal-plus-infinitive frames. Lequel and the passive voice remain available for accurate use but are not targets of this chapter.

2.1 Impersonal expressions with the infinitive: recycled and extended

Grammar Focus — Il faut / il est nécessaire de / il convient de + infinitif

Il faut and il est nécessaire de plus the infinitive are recycled here and applied to administrative contexts. Il convient de plus the infinitive is newly productive in this chapter, adding a more formal, register-sensitive way to phrase the same kind of requirement. None of these three constructions triggers the subjonctif, because none introduces a subordinate clause with its own subject.

  • Il faut remplir ce formulaire avant de déposer le dossier. You need to fill out this form before submitting the file. il faut + infinitif, recycled
  • Il est nécessaire de joindre un justificatif de domicile récent. It is necessary to attach a recent proof of address. il est nécessaire de + infinitif, recycled
  • Il convient de vérifier les délais avant de déposer une demande. It is advisable to check the processing times before submitting a request. il convient de + infinitif, newly productive, more formal register

The real distinction between the impersonal-plus-infinitive frames above and il faut que plus the subjonctif is not strictly "general" versus "specific": it is whether the performer of the action stays implicit or is expressed as the subject of a subordinate clause. Il faut remplir ce formulaire leaves the performer unstated (whoever is concerned); il faut que vous remplissiez ce formulaire names vous explicitly as the one who must do it. In practice, general instructions often use the infinitive and instructions addressed to one person often use il faut que, but this is a frequent tendency, not the rule itself — a request can address a specific person and still use the infinitive frame when the performer is clear from context.

2.2 A practice template for structuring a request

Building Block — A useful administrative-request shape (this chapter's practice template)
[stated purpose: dans le cadre de / pour que] + [what is needed: il faut / il est nécessaire de / il convient de + infinitif] + [documents listed with dont or joindre, when relevant] + [a relevant timeframe, stated or requested]
The purpose clause recycles Chapter 1; the requirement statements recycle and extend this chapter's impersonal frames; dont can add a precise detail about a document or step; and addressing timing closes the request with a practical detail. This four-part shape is a useful template for this chapter's practice, not a fixed property of all French administrative communication: some requests involve no documents to submit, and some ask about the délai rather than stating one. No subjonctif trigger beyond the eleven from Chapter 8–11 is required anywhere in this shape.
Je vous écris dans le cadre d’une demande de permis de stationnement. Il est nécessaire de remplir le formulaire ci-joint et de joindre un justificatif de domicile, un document dont le service concerné a besoin pour traiter toute demande. Il convient de déposer le dossier complet dans un délai de deux semaines.
I am writing to you as part of a request for a parking permit. It is necessary to fill out the attached form and attach proof of address, a document the relevant department needs in order to process any request. The complete file should be submitted within a two-week period.
Grammar Focus — Recycling il faut que alongside impersonal-plus-infinitive frames

Il faut que, one of the eleven Chapter 8–11 triggers, remains fully active when the performer needs to be expressed explicitly as the subject of a subordinate clause.

  • Il faut que vous apportiez une pièce d’identité valide le jour du rendez-vous. You need to bring a valid ID on the day of the appointment. il faut que + subjonctif, Chapter 8–11 trigger, recycled
  • Bien que ce formulaire semble long, il ne prend en réalité que quelques minutes à remplir. Although this form seems long, it actually only takes a few minutes to fill out. Chapter 19 concession environment, recycled
Reminder — Everything else recycles, nothing else is required

The eleven Chapter 8–11 subjonctif triggers (including il faut que), the Chapter 18 negative-belief/negative-certainty environment, and the Chapter 19 bien que/quoique concession environment remain fully active alongside this chapter's recycled and extended impersonal-plus-infinitive frames; no further trigger is introduced. Dont, purpose clauses with pour que, and tu/vous from Chapter 1 all recycle directly into this chapter's task. Lequel and the passive voice are available for accurate use if they arise naturally, but neither is a target of this chapter.

Model Dialogue: Au guichet d’un service administratif

Context: Sophie visits the Lyon office's internal administrative service to ask about a temporary building access badge. Register: formal, service-counter interaction.

L’agent : Bonjour, Madame. En quoi puis-je vous aider ? Hello, Madame. How can I help you?
Sophie : Bonjour, Monsieur. Je suis ici dans le cadre d’un court détachement professionnel, et je dois demander un badge d’accès temporaire pour le bâtiment. Hello. I am here as part of a short professional secondment, and I need to request a temporary building access badge.
L’agent : Très bien. Il faut d’abord remplir ce formulaire, puis joindre un justificatif de domicile récent. Very well. First you need to fill out this form, then attach a recent proof of address.
Sophie : D’accord. Est-ce qu’il faut que j’apporte autre chose ? All right. Do I need to bring anything else?
L’agent : Il est nécessaire d’avoir une pièce d’identité valide, un document dont nous avons besoin pour tout dossier. Bien que la démarche paraisse longue, elle se fait en réalité assez rapidement. You need a valid ID, a document we need for any file. Although the procedure seems lengthy, it's actually done fairly quickly.
Sophie : Très bien. Et quel est le délai de traitement ? Very well. And what is the processing time?
L’agent : Il convient de compter environ deux semaines. Nous vous enverrons une confirmation dès que le dossier sera traité. You should expect about two weeks. We will send you confirmation once the file has been processed.
Sophie : Merci beaucoup pour ces précisions. Thank you very much for these details.

This dialogue models the three impersonal expressions (il faut, il est nécessaire de, recycled; il convient de, newly productive + infinitive), the recycled Chapter 8–11 trigger il faut que, a recycled dont, a recycled Chapter 19 concession, and this chapter’s administrative vocabulary. No unauthorized subjonctif trigger appears, and vous is used throughout.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Administrative procedure as a genre

French administrative communication, whether spoken at a counter or written in a letter, tends to favor precise, complete information. Clearly presenting your purpose, what is required, any relevant documents, and the timing involved helps a request be processed efficiently, though the exact order and content vary by situation: not every request involves documents to submit, and sometimes the requester is the one asking about the délai rather than stating one. This is not evasiveness or excessive formality; it reflects the expectation that a request should be easy for an official or a colleague to process correctly on the first attempt.

Regional Variation — Administrative language across Francophone institutions

Specific document names, agency names, and procedural steps vary across France, Québec, Belgium, Switzerland, and other Francophone administrations. This chapter's vocabulary reflects common metropolitan French administrative usage; the underlying communicative strategies — stating purpose, requirements, and timeframe clearly — transfer across contexts even when specific terms differ.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Using the subjonctif after il faut + infinitif

Error: Writing *Il faut que remplir ce formulaire, mixing the subjonctif construction with the infinitive construction.

Correction: Il faut remplir ce formulaire (performer implicit, infinitive) or il faut que vous remplissiez ce formulaire (performer explicitly expressed as vous, subjonctif). The two patterns do not combine; choose one.

Common Mistake — Omitting de after il est nécessaire and il convient

Error: Writing *il est nécessaire remplir or *il convient remplir, dropping the required preposition.

Correction: Il est nécessaire de remplir… and il convient de remplir… both require de before the infinitive; only il faut attaches directly to the infinitive without a preposition.

Common Mistake — Vague requests that omit relevant details

Error: Writing an administrative request that states a general wish without explaining what is required or addressing timing at all.

Correction: Use this chapter's practice template — purpose, requirements, relevant documents when applicable, and timing — as a guide. A request that leaves out details relevant to its own situation is harder for the reader to process correctly, even though not every request needs every element.

Intégration 3 : Produire un dossier professionnel ou académique

Unit III Integration · Speak and Write French: Professional, Academic, Media, and Institutional French
Unit assessment chapter — no new major productive system

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Combine two related sources, a set of findings, and a set of recommendations into a single coherent professional or academic dossier.
  2. Use the Chapter 12 toolkit — the paraphrase frames autrement dit and en d’autres termes, the source-wording frame pour reprendre les termes de, the comparison connectors de même, à la différence de, contrairement à, and de son côté, and the two-source attribution pattern combining selon/d’après with il ressort de…que — to open the dossier with a source-comparison section.
  3. Use the Chapter 13 toolkit — the report-structure connectors dans un premier temps, dans un second temps, il convient de noter que, and en conclusion, the recommendation and impersonal necessity frames, and the passive voice — to write the dossier’s findings-and-recommendations section in a depersonalized report style.
  4. Use the Chapter 14 toolkit — the opening frames je vais vous présenter and l’objectif de cette présentation est de, the visual-referencing connectors comme le montre, on constate ici, and ce graphique illustre, the audience-management formulas, and the spoken closing pour conclure — to deliver a spoken presentation of the same dossier.
  5. Deliver a short defense of the dossier by answering two direct questions about it, drawing only on material already active in Volumes I–III and Volume IV Chapters 1–14.
  6. Recycle the eleven Volume III Chapter 8–11 subjonctif triggers, the Volume III Chapter 18 negative-belief/negative-certainty environment, the Volume III Chapter 19 bien que/quoique concession environment, the Chapter 6 source-framing and reported speech, the Chapter 7 data-commentary and comparison tools, the Chapter 8 concession and counterargument tools, the Chapter 9 civic-institutional framing, and Chapter 11’s nominalization, paragraph-logic connectors, academic reporting verbs, and citation-attribution frame, without any new productive subjonctif trigger.
  7. Produce a written professional or academic dossier (twenty to twenty-eight sentences across three linked sections) and a matched oral presentation of that dossier, combining every skill developed across Chapters 11–14.
  8. Move to a complete short dossier combining source comparison, findings and recommendations, and a presentation outline, and a short controlled oral defense answering two questions about it, closing Unité III.

Communication Scenario

The office meeting is next week, and Mme Girard has asked Sophie and Karim for one complete document: not a source comparison, a report, and a set of slide notes as three separate pieces, but a single dossier that moves cleanly from what the sources say, to what the team found and recommends, to how they will present it aloud. Karim points out that this is really just their Chapter 12, 13, and 14 work joined into one continuous piece, in the right order, with nothing new to learn — the challenge is discipline, not new grammar. Chapter 15 asks Sophie and Karim to produce exactly that dossier, and then to defend it aloud when a colleague asks a direct question about it.

  1. Open the dossier with a brief source-comparison section.
  2. Continue with a findings-and-recommendations section in report style.
  3. Close with a presentation outline suitable for oral delivery.
  4. Answer two direct questions about the dossier aloud, using only previously activated systems.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Moving between three registers within one delivery

No new sound-spelling or vowel feature is introduced in this chapter. The task is combining, within a single continuous delivery, the three rhythm-and-pause patterns already practiced separately: the brief pause marking a shift between sources (Chapter 12, especially before de son côté), the level, measured phrasing of report style (Chapter 13, especially around passive constructions), and the deliberate pause after a visual-referencing connector before addressing a slide (Chapter 14). A successful oral defense signals each of these three sections to the listener through pacing and pause, not only through vocabulary.

  • Moving from source comparison into report style: De son côté, la seconde étude met en avant la confiance. Dans un premier temps, il convient de noter qu’aucune consultation n’a encore été organisée. — a clear pause after confiance, then the flatter, more even pacing of report style beginning at dans un premier temps.
  • Review the required liaison in les deux_études (the normally silent x of deux is realized as /z/ before the vowel-initial études; there is no liaison between les and deux, since deux begins with a consonant sound), the enchaînement in il_est, and the elision in qu’il convient, all recycled from Chapters 12–13.

Grammar: Le dossier intégré

Purpose: combine, without introducing any new productive system, the Chapter 12 source-comparison toolkit, the Chapter 13 report-writing toolkit, and the Chapter 14 presentation toolkit into a single three-part dossier and its spoken defense. As a unit assessment chapter, Chapter 15 assesses only what Chapters 1–14 have already made productive. No new subjonctif trigger is introduced. Lequel and the passive voice remain available for accurate use but are not new targets of this chapter.

15.1 The three-part dossier shape

Building Block — A complete dossier (this chapter's integration template)
[Section 1 — Ch. 12: selon/d’après + claim] + [de son côté / de même / à la différence de / contrairement à + second source] + [autrement dit / en d’autres termes: paraphrase] + [il ressort des deux…que: shared conclusion] → [Section 2 — Ch. 13: à la lumière de + dossier opening] + [dans un premier temps / dans un second temps: findings, at least one passive] + [il convient de noter que] + [il conviendrait de / nous recommandons de: recommendations] + [en conclusion] → [Section 3 — Ch. 14: je vais vous présenter / l’objectif de cette présentation est de] + [audience-management formula] + [comme le montre / on constate ici / ce graphique illustre: visual reference] + [pour conclure]
Every element in this template was already productive by the end of Chapter 14; nothing here is new. What Chapter 15 adds is the discipline of sequencing all three sections into one coherent whole, with each section's connectors doing their normal job and no section's tools bleeding into another (recommendation frames stay out of the source-comparison section; visual-referencing connectors stay out of the findings section, where they have not yet been introduced by a visual). A dossier that only lists three unconnected sections, without transitions between them, has not yet achieved integration; a short bridging sentence between sections is expected but does not require any new grammar.

De son côté, une seconde étude met plutôt en avant un déficit de confiance envers les institutions locales. Autrement dit, les deux sources ne s’opposent pas totalement : elles décrivent deux obstacles différents à un même problème. Il ressort néanmoins des deux études qu’une communication institutionnelle plus claire resterait utile. À la lumière de ces constats, ce dossier présente la situation du marché et propose des pistes d’action. According to a first study, market attendance depends mainly on the clarity of information. For its part, a second study instead highlights a deficit of trust toward local institutions. In other words, the two sources are not entirely opposed: they describe two different obstacles to the same problem. Second, it should be noted that this drop mainly affects new arrivals. It would therefore be advisable to organize a targeted consultation before any decision. Grammar Focus — Bridging between sections without new grammar

Moving from the source-comparison section into the findings-and-recommendations section, and from that section into the presentation outline, needs only a short bridge, built entirely from recycled material: a phrase such as à la lumière de ces constats (Ch. 13) can pick up directly from the shared conclusion of the source-comparison section (Ch. 12), and an opening frame such as je vais vous présenter ce dossier (Ch. 14) can name the two sections that came before it. No new connector is required to perform this bridging; the skill being assessed is selecting the right already-active tool for the transition, not learning a new one.

  • Il ressort des deux études qu’une communication plus claire resterait utile. À la lumière de ces constats, ce dossier présente la situation et propose des pistes d’action. It emerges from both studies that clearer communication would remain useful. In light of these findings, this dossier presents the situation and proposes courses of action. Model Dialogue: Derniers réglages avant le dossier

Context: Sophie and Karim do a final check of the complete dossier the night before submitting it and presenting it. Register: semi-formal working conversation, close to the register of the dossier itself.

Sophie : On a les trois parties : la comparaison des sources, le rapport, et le plan de présentation. Il manque juste les transitions. We have the three parts: the source comparison, the report, and the presentation outline. We're just missing the transitions.
Karim : On peut reprendre la conclusion partagée des deux études pour ouvrir le rapport : « à la lumière de ces constats… » We can pick up the shared conclusion from the two studies to open the report: "in light of these findings…"
Sophie : Bonne idée, ça évite de répéter deux fois la même information. Et entre le rapport et la présentation ? Good idea, that avoids repeating the same information twice. And between the report and the presentation?
Karim : « Je vais maintenant vous présenter ce dossier à l’oral. » Simple, mais ça marque bien le changement. "I will now present this dossier orally." Simple, but it clearly marks the change.
Sophie : Et si Mme Girard nous demande pourquoi on recommande une consultation plutôt qu’une décision immédiate ? And if Mme Girard asks us why we recommend a consultation rather than an immediate decision?
Karim : On répond avec ce qu’on a déjà : bien que la baisse reste modeste, il importe de comprendre pourquoi avant d’agir. We answer with what we already have: although the drop remains modest, it's important to understand why before acting.
Sophie : Exactement, pas besoin d’inventer une nouvelle réponse. Tout est déjà dans le dossier. Exactly, no need to invent a new answer. Everything is already in the dossier.
Karim : Parfait. Pour conclure notre répétition : le dossier tient debout, et on peut le défendre avec ce qu’on connaît déjà. Perfect. To conclude our rehearsal: the dossier holds together, and we can defend it with what we already know.

This dialogue models the Chapter 13 opening frame (à la lumière de ces constats) reused as a bridge, a Chapter 14 opening frame (je vais vous présenter) reused to mark the shift from written to spoken, and a recycled Volume III Chapter 19 concession supporting a spoken defense answer. No unauthorized subjonctif trigger appears, and no new connector is introduced.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — The single dossier as a professional expectation

In French professional and academic settings, a request to "produire un dossier" typically expects one coherent document rather than several unconnected pieces submitted together. A reader who receives a source comparison, a report, and a presentation outline as three separate files, with no indication of how they relate, is often left to do the integration work that a well-prepared dossier should have already done. The bridging sentences practiced in this chapter are a small but valued sign of that integration discipline.

Regional Variation — Dossier conventions across the Francophone world

Expectations for how a dossier is assembled, ordered, and defended orally vary somewhat across French, Belgian, Swiss, Québécois, and other Francophone academic and professional traditions. This chapter’s vocabulary and patterns reflect common metropolitan French usage; the underlying skill of integrating comparison, findings, and presentation into one coherent whole transfers across contexts even where specific conventions differ.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Three sections with no bridge between them

Error: Writing the source-comparison section, then starting the findings section with no transition at all, as though the two sections had been written independently and simply placed side by side.

Correction: Add a short recycled bridging sentence, such as à la lumière de ces constats…, so the reader can see how the sections connect. No new grammar is needed for this — only a deliberate choice to use it.

Common Mistake — Mixing a section's tools into the wrong section

Error: Using a Chapter 14 visual-referencing connector such as comme le montre ce graphique inside the written findings section, before any visual has been introduced by the presentation outline.

Correction: Keep each section's tools where they belong: comparison connectors and paraphrase frames in the source-comparison section, stage markers and the passive in the findings section, and visual references and audience-management formulas in the presentation outline. This is what "integration" means in this chapter — combining sections in sequence, not blending their tools together.

Common Mistake — Inventing new material to answer a defense question

Out of scope here: Reaching for an unfamiliar structure or an unscheduled subjonctif trigger when answering a direct question about the dossier, simply because the question feels unplanned.

Correction: A defense answer should draw on material already active in the dossier itself and in Chapters 1–14 — a recycled concession (bien que…), a recycled necessity frame (il importe de…), or a restated finding. As a unit assessment chapter, Chapter 15 rewards confident reuse of known material over an attempt at something new.

Listening Practice

  1. The section opens with selon une première étude; the script explicitly states that the two sources do not fully contradict each other, since they emphasize two different factors (information versus trust).
  2. The bridge is à la lumière de ces constats, which refers back to the shared conclusion just stated rather than restating it.
  3. The first question asks why the two sources do not fully contradict each other; the speaker answers that they describe two different obstacles to the same problem rather than disagreeing on the facts themselves.
  4. The second question asks why a consultation is recommended rather than an immediate decision; the speaker answers using a recycled bien que concession and the necessity frame il importe de.
  5. The presentation signals its ending with pour conclure.

Reading Practice

  1. Section 1 uses the Chapter 12 toolkit, identifiable from selon, de son côté, à la différence de, autrement dit, and il ressort de…que.
  2. Une baisse…a été constatée is passive; it emphasizes the finding itself rather than who observed it.
  3. Section 3 uses spoken-register markers such as bonjour à tous, direct address (je vais vous présenter, n’hésitez pas), and pour conclure, rather than the more depersonalized written register of section 2.
  4. All three sections converge on the same conclusion: that clearer communication with residents is the main issue at stake.

Writing Workshop — Model text (excerpt)

Modèle — Dossier (extrait de transition)

…Il ressort néanmoins des deux études qu’une communication institutionnelle plus claire resterait utile. À la lumière de ces constats, ce dossier présente la situation du marché et propose des pistes d’action. Dans un premier temps, une baisse de dix pour cent de la fréquentation a été constatée sur trois mois… En conclusion, une communication plus claire avec les habitants demeure l’enjeu principal de ce dossier. Je vais maintenant vous présenter ce dossier à l’oral. Bonjour à tous…

…It nonetheless emerges from both studies that clearer institutional communication would remain useful. In light of these findings, this dossier presents the situation of the market and proposes courses of action. First, a ten percent drop in attendance was observed over three months… In conclusion, clearer communication with residents remains the main issue at stake in this dossier. I will now present this dossier orally. Good morning everyone…

This excerpt shows the two required bridges: from the Chapter 12 shared conclusion into the Chapter 13 dossier opening, and from the Chapter 13 written closing into the Chapter 14 spoken opening. A complete model dossier follows this same three-section, twice-bridged shape at full length. Other responses are acceptable when they meet all task requirements.

Réunions et prise de parole

Meetings and Participation · Speak and Write French: Professional, Academic, Media, and Institutional French

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Participate in a formal meeting: follow an agenda, take the floor, add a point, and respond to a colleague, using this chapter’s new turn-taking vocabulary.
  2. Consolidate previously active reported speech — dire que, déclarer que, and expliquer que + indicatif, recycled from Volume III — to report what a colleague has just said in a meeting, using a present-tense reporting verb with a complement in whichever already-active indicative tense the original statement calls for.
  3. Recycle the eleven Chapter 8–11 subjonctif triggers, the Chapter 18 negative-belief/negative-certainty environment, and the Chapter 19 bien que/quoique concession environment inside meeting participation, without any new productive trigger.
  4. Add and recycle a curated meeting/agenda vocabulary set: l’ordre du jour, un point, prendre la parole, couper la parole, rebondir sur, faire le point sur, un compte rendu, and à ce sujet.
  5. Recycle dont, purpose clauses, tu/vous, and the Chapter 2 impersonal-plus-infinitive frames (il faut, il est nécessaire de, il convient de), applied to a meeting-participation task.
  6. Write a short agenda item summary or minutes excerpt (compte rendu), observing the conventions of this controlled institutional genre.
  7. Continue institutional reading with a short meeting agenda and a brief minutes excerpt, recognizing their basic organization and purpose.

Communication Scenario

Sophie's first team meeting at the Lyon office is scheduled for Thursday, and Mme Girard has sent an agenda with three points to discuss, including an update on the francophonie project. Sophie will need to follow the agenda, wait for her turn, add a point when relevant, and respond to what colleagues say — all without interrupting or dominating the discussion. Karim, meanwhile, has been asked to prepare a short compte rendu of his own team's weekly meeting for colleagues who could not attend. Chapter 3 turns from written requests to the live, turn-by-turn language of meetings: following an agenda, taking the floor politely, reporting what someone has just said, and summarizing a discussion in writing.

  1. Follow a short meeting agenda and identify its points.
  2. Take the floor politely, add a point, or respond to a colleague using this chapter's vocabulary.
  3. Report what a colleague said, using reported speech with a present-tense reporting verb.
  4. Write a short summary of a meeting point or a brief minutes excerpt.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Taking the floor without interrupting the rhythm

No new sound-spelling or vowel feature is introduced in this chapter. The task is applying already-active rhythm and intonation systems to the specific challenge of entering a conversation smoothly: a brief rising lead-in before a turn-taking formula, followed by a clear, level delivery of the point itself.

  • Taking the floor: Si je peux me permettre… je voudrais ajouter un point à ce sujet. — a light rise on permettre, then a settled, even rhythm for the point that follows.
  • Review the required liaison in nous_avons and vous_avez when reporting what colleagues have said, the enchaînement in elle a dit que, and the elision in qu’elle a expliqué. Maintain a level, unhurried pace when reading an agenda point aloud; slower, well-grouped delivery helps colleagues identify the agenda item and follow the speaker's contribution.

Grammar: Réunions et prise de parole

Purpose: consolidate previously active reported speech (dire que, déclarer que, expliquer que + indicatif, recycled from Volume III) and combine it with turn-taking formulas and already-active subjonctif environments, dont, purpose clauses, and the Chapter 2 impersonal-plus-infinitive frames, applied to meeting participation. No new subjonctif trigger is introduced. Tense sequencing across reporting verb and reported clause is not a new focus of this chapter; both a present-tense reporting verb with a complement in another already-active indicative tense, and fuller tense-shifted reporting recycled from Volume III, are acceptable. Lequel and the passive voice remain available for accurate use but are not targets of this chapter.

3.1 Reported speech in meetings: dire que, déclarer que, expliquer que

Grammar Focus — Reporting what a colleague has just said

Dire que, déclarer que, and expliquer que plus the indicatif are recycled from Volume III and consolidated here for a specific communicative purpose: reporting what a colleague has just said in a meeting. A present-tense reporting verb may introduce a complement in whichever already-active indicative tense fits the original statement — présent, futur simple, or another tense already active from earlier volumes. This chapter does not introduce new tense-sequencing manipulation; it focuses on using reported speech, together with this chapter's turn-taking formulas, to move a meeting discussion forward.

  • Karim dit que le projet avance bien. Karim says that the project is going well. dire que + indicatif présent, recycled and consolidated
  • Mme Girard déclare que la réunion commencera à l’heure. Mme Girard states that the meeting will start on time. déclarer que + indicatif, reported statement in a different but still indicative tense (futur simple), no subjonctif involved
  • Sophie explique qu’elle a besoin de plus de temps pour ce point. Sophie explains that she needs more time for this point. expliquer que + indicatif présent

In the affirmative reporting use practiced in this chapter, dire que, déclarer que, and expliquer que normally introduce an indicative clause, since they report a statement presented as fact from the speaker's point of view rather than a wish, doubt, necessity, or emotion. (Other constructions and pragmatic uses of dire can call for the subjonctif; this chapter's target is the affirmative reporting pattern shown here.)

3.2 Structuring a turn: taking the floor, adding a point, responding

Building Block — A useful meeting-turn shape (this chapter's practice template)
[polite entry: si je peux me permettre / pour rebondir sur ce point] + [the point itself, using recycled or reported language] + [optional dont detail] + [optional recycled subjonctif environment]
The polite entry is new to this chapter; the point itself typically recycles vocabulary and grammar already active since Volume III. Dont can add a precise detail about the point under discussion, and a recycled subjonctif environment can express a reservation, a necessity, or a concession within the same turn. Not every turn needs all four elements: a short question or a brief agreement can be a complete, appropriate turn on its own.
Si je peux me permettre, je voudrais ajouter un point sur le calendrier, un sujet dont nous n’avons pas encore parlé aujourd’hui. Bien que le délai actuel semble raisonnable, il faut que nous confirmions la date avant vendredi.
If I may, I would like to add a point about the schedule, a subject we haven't yet discussed today. Although the current timeframe seems reasonable, we need to confirm the date before Friday.
Grammar Focus — Recycling subjonctif environments and impersonal frames in a meeting

All three productive subjonctif environments from Volume III, and the Chapter 2 impersonal-plus-infinitive frames, remain fully active in meeting participation.

  • Il faut que nous décidions de la date avant la fin de la réunion. We need to decide on the date before the end of the meeting. il faut que + subjonctif, Chapter 8–11 trigger, recycled
  • Il conviendrait de faire le point sur ce sujet lors de la prochaine réunion. It would be advisable to take stock of this subject at the next meeting. il convient de + infinitif, Chapter 2 frame, recycled
Reminder — Everything else recycles, nothing else is required

The eleven Chapter 8–11 subjonctif triggers, the Chapter 18 negative-belief/negative-certainty environment, and the Chapter 19 bien que/quoique concession environment remain fully active alongside this chapter's consolidated reported-speech verbs and new turn-taking formulas; no further subjonctif trigger is introduced. Dont, purpose clauses, tu/vous, and the Chapter 2 impersonal-plus-infinitive frames all recycle directly into this chapter's task. Tense sequencing in reported speech is not a required focus of this chapter's core task; using a present-tense reporting verb keeps the meeting interaction manageable, but correct use of fuller tense-shifted reported speech, recycled from Volume III, is also acceptable. Lequel and the passive voice remain outside this chapter's productive target, though both are available for accurate use if they arise naturally.

Model Dialogue: Une réunion d’équipe

Context: Sophie attends her first team meeting at the Lyon office, led by Mme Girard. Register: formal, professional meeting.

Mme Girard : Bien, passons au premier point à l’ordre du jour : le projet francophonie. Sophie, voulez-vous faire le point sur ce sujet ? Right, let's move to the first point on the agenda: the francophonie project. Sophie, would you like to give an update on this?
Sophie : Bien sûr. Le projet avance bien ; il faut que nous confirmions la date de la prochaine rencontre avec l’équipe locale. Of course. The project is going well; we need to confirm the date of the next meeting with the local team.
Karim : Si je peux me permettre, je voudrais ajouter un point : le budget dont nous avons besoin n’a pas encore été confirmé. If I may, I'd like to add a point: the budget we need hasn't been confirmed yet.
Mme Girard : Bonne remarque. Karim dit que le budget n’a pas encore été confirmé. Pour rebondir sur ce point, je pense qu’il conviendrait de contacter le service financier cette semaine. Good point. Karim says the budget hasn't been confirmed yet. To follow up on that, I think it would be advisable to contact the finance department this week.
Sophie : Bien que ce délai soit court, je pense que c’est faisable. Although the timeframe is short, I think it's doable.
Karim : D’accord. Je rédigerai un compte rendu de ce point pour les collègues qui n’ont pas pu y assister. Agreed. I'll write up a summary of this point for the colleagues who couldn't attend.
Mme Girard : Parfait. Passons maintenant au point suivant. Perfect. Let's move on to the next point now.

This dialogue models the meeting-turn shape, consolidated reported speech (Karim dit que…) reporting what another participant has just said, turn-taking formulas (si je peux me permettre, pour rebondir sur ce point), a recycled dont, a recycled Chapter 19 concession, a recycled Chapter 8–11 trigger, and a recycled Chapter 2 impersonal frame. No unauthorized subjonctif trigger appears, and vous is used throughout.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Turn-taking in French professional meetings

In many French professional settings, a meeting leader introduces each agenda point and invites specific participants to speak. In formal professional interaction, speakers may use brief lead-ins such as si je peux me permettre, je voudrais ajouter…, or pour rebondir sur ce point… to manage entry into the discussion rather than interrupting directly. These are useful pragmatic options, not a fixed rule; exact style varies by organization, team size, and how formal the meeting is.

Regional Variation — Meeting conventions across Francophone workplaces

Expectations for how formally a meeting is run, how strictly an agenda is followed, and how directly participants interject vary across Francophone workplaces in France, Québec, Belgium, Switzerland, and beyond. This chapter's guidance reflects common practice in formal metropolitan French institutional settings; other Francophone professional cultures may proceed somewhat differently.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Using the subjonctif after dire que, déclarer que, expliquer que

Error: Writing *Karim dit que le projet soit en retard, using the subjonctif after a reporting verb that presents the statement as fact.

Correction: Karim dit que le projet est en retard. In this affirmative reporting use, dire que, déclarer que, and expliquer que normally take the indicatif because they report a statement as fact, unlike verbs of doubt, necessity, or emotion.

Common Mistake — Interrupting without a turn-taking formula

Error: Adding a point or disagreeing abruptly, without any lead-in, which can read as impolite in a formal meeting.

Correction: Use a turn-taking formula such as si je peux me permettre or pour rebondir sur ce point to signal a wish to speak before adding a point or a counter-view.

Common Mistake — Forcing every reported statement into the present

Error: Assuming that a reported statement must always appear in the present tense, and awkwardly rephrasing a colleague's future or past statement to avoid using another tense.

Correction: A present-tense reporting verb can introduce a complement in whichever already-active indicative tense the original statement calls for — for example, Mme Girard déclare que la réunion commencera à l’heure, where déclare is present but commencera is futur simple. This chapter's task is using reported speech for meeting participation, not restricting it to a single tense.

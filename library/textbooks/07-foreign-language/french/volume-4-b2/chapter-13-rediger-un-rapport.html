Rédiger un rapport

Report Writing · Speak and Write French: Professional, Academic, Media, and Institutional French

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Read a short internal report and identify its findings section and its recommendations section as distinct moves.
  2. Use newly productive report-structure connectors — dans un premier temps, dans un second temps, il convient de noter que, and en conclusion — to organize a report into a clear findings-then-recommendations shape.
  3. Use a newly productive recommendation frame — il conviendrait de, il serait souhaitable de, or nous recommandons de + infinitif — to propose action without overstating certainty.
  4. Use newly productive impersonal necessity frames — il convient de, il importe de, il est nécessaire de + infinitif — to state what a situation requires without naming a personal subject.
  5. Use the passive voice (être + past participle), already active and now specifically foregrounded for formal report style, to emphasize a result or a measure over the person or body responsible for it, matching the depersonalized style of a formal report.
  6. Recycle the Chapter 6 source-framing expressions, the Chapter 7 data-commentary and comparison tools, the Chapter 9 civic-institutional framing, and Chapters 11–12’s nominalization, paragraph-logic connectors, academic reporting verbs, citation-attribution frames, paraphrase frames, and source-comparison connectors, applied to report writing, without any new productive subjonctif trigger.
  7. Add and recycle a curated report-writing vocabulary set: un rapport, un constat, une recommandation, une conclusion, un enjeu, proposer, recommander, rédiger, and à la lumière de.
  8. Write a short internal report (eight to eleven sentences) with a findings section and a recommendations section, using passive or impersonal style where appropriate.
  9. Move to a controlled internal report and a short controlled oral report summary, continuing Unité III.

Communication Scenario

Mme Girard has asked Sophie and Karim to turn their earlier work on the neighborhood market — the news item, the figures, and the debate from Unité II, now enriched by the academic reading and source comparison of Chapters 11 and 12 — into a short internal report for the office. A report, Karim points out, is not simply a longer version of the briefing they gave in Chapter 10: it needs a findings section that lays out what was observed, and a separate recommendations section that proposes what should be done, written in the more depersonalized style typical of internal documents. Chapter 13 gives Sophie and Karim the tools to make that shift — organizing a report into clear stages, proposing action carefully, and using impersonal and passive constructions where they fit the genre.

  1. Organize a report into a findings section and a recommendations section.
  2. State findings using impersonal or passive constructions where appropriate.
  3. Propose action using a recommendation frame that does not overstate certainty.
  4. Close a report with a brief, clearly marked conclusion.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — The flat, even delivery of report style

No new sound-spelling or vowel feature is introduced in this chapter. The task is practicing the measured, controlled phrasing with clearly separated information units that suits impersonal and passive report style; real oral delivery of a report varies according to audience and emphasis, and this is a stylistic tendency rather than a fixed rule.

  • Marking a stage shift: Dans un premier temps, la fréquentation a été mesurée sur trois mois. Dans un second temps, les résultats ont été comparés aux données de l’an dernier. — a brief, level pause after each stage marker, keeping each information unit distinct.
  • Review the consonantal enchaînement in il_est before a vowel (the already-pronounced final consonant of il links to the following vowel; this is enchaînement, not liaison, since liaison involves a normally silent consonant becoming pronounced), and the elision in qu’il convient. In the passive construction a été mesurée, the past participle agrees in gender and number with the grammatical subject.

13.1 Structuring a report: findings, then recommendations

Building Block — A short internal-report shape (this chapter's practice template)
[opening: à la lumière de + noun phrase, ce rapport présente] + [findings: dans un premier temps + finding 1] + [dans un second temps + finding 2] + [il convient de noter que + a further finding, often passive] + [recommendations: il conviendrait de / il serait souhaitable de / nous recommandons de + infinitif] + [en conclusion + brief closing statement]
The stage markers and the recommendation frame are new to this chapter; the passive, already active from earlier volumes, is now systematically foregrounded for report style; the underlying vocabulary and source-attribution tools recycle Chapters 6–12. A report’s findings section should stay descriptive and avoid recommending action prematurely; the recommendations section is where proposals belong. Not every sentence in the findings section needs the passive — use it when the result matters more than who produced it, and keep the active voice where a specific actor is the point.
À la lumière des constats réunis au cours des dernières semaines, ce rapport présente la situation du marché du quartier et propose des pistes d’action. Dans un premier temps, la fréquentation du marché a été mesurée sur trois mois : une baisse de dix pour cent a été constatée, surtout le samedi matin. Dans un second temps, ces résultats ont été comparés aux données de l’an dernier, ce qui confirme la tendance. Il convient de noter qu’une consultation des commerçants n’a pas encore été organisée. Il conviendrait donc d’organiser une consultation avant toute décision, et il serait souhaitable d’envisager un marché nocturne ponctuel à titre d’essai. En conclusion, une communication plus claire avec les habitants reste l’enjeu principal de ce dossier.
In light of the findings gathered over recent weeks, this report presents the situation of the neighborhood market and proposes courses of action. First, market attendance was measured over three months: a ten percent drop was observed, mainly on Saturday mornings. Second, these results were compared with last year’s data, which confirms the trend. It should be noted that a consultation of merchants has not yet been organized. It would therefore be advisable to organize a consultation before any decision, and it would be desirable to consider an occasional night market on a trial basis. In conclusion, clearer communication with residents remains the main issue at stake in this matter.
Grammar Focus — Three recommendation frames, one job, different degrees of commitment

Il conviendrait de, il serait souhaitable de, and nous recommandons de, each followed directly by an infinitive, are newly productive in this chapter as ways to propose action in a report without a new subjonctif trigger. Il conviendrait de and il serait souhaitable de use the conditional to soften the proposal, appropriate when the report’s authors want to suggest rather than instruct; nous recommandons de is more direct and personal, appropriate when the report speaks in the institution’s own voice. All three stay with the infinitive construction in this chapter; the corresponding que + subjonctif variants (il conviendrait que, il serait souhaitable que) exist in French but are receptive only here, since they would introduce a new subjonctif environment beyond this volume’s authorized set.

  • Il conviendrait d’organiser une consultation avant toute décision. It would be advisable to organize a consultation before any decision. il conviendrait de + infinitif, newly productive
  • Nous recommandons de simplifier les documents administratifs concernés. We recommend simplifying the administrative documents concerned. nous recommandons de + infinitif, newly productive

13.2 Stating necessity without a personal subject: il convient de, il importe de, il est nécessaire de

Grammar Focus — Three impersonal necessity frames, plus the passive for depersonalized findings

Il convient de, il importe de, and il est nécessaire de, each followed by an infinitive, are newly productive impersonal frames for stating that something is required without naming who must do it — useful when a report describes an institutional necessity rather than a personal opinion. They differ slightly in force: il convient de is measured and administrative; il importe de underlines that the matter is significant; il est nécessaire de is the most direct. The passive voice (être + past participle, with the participle agreeing with the subject), already active from earlier volumes, is now applied systematically for the same depersonalizing purpose in the findings section: une baisse a été constatée foregrounds the finding itself rather than who observed it. What is new here is not access to the passive but its genre-specific function in depersonalized report style.

  • Il importe de traiter cette question avant la fin du trimestre. It is important to address this question before the end of the quarter. il importe de + infinitif, newly productive
  • Une baisse de dix pour cent a été constatée au cours des trois derniers mois. A ten percent drop was observed over the last three months. passive: être + past participle, agreement with the subject une baisse (fem. sing.), foregrounded for report style

Model Dialogue: Avant de finaliser le rapport

Context: Sophie and Karim review a draft of their internal report on the neighborhood market before submitting it to Mme Girard. Register: semi-formal working conversation, close to the register of the report itself.

Karim : J’ai relu le rapport. Dans un premier temps, on présente les constats ; dans un second temps, les recommandations. Ça te semble clair ? I reread the report. First, we present the findings; second, the recommendations. Does that seem clear to you?
Sophie : Oui, mais il convient de noter qu’on n’a pas encore mentionné la comparaison avec l’an dernier. Yes, but it should be noted that we haven't yet mentioned the comparison with last year.
Karim : Bonne remarque. On peut écrire : « Ces résultats ont été comparés aux données de l’an dernier, ce qui confirme la tendance. » Good point. We could write: "These results were compared with last year's data, which confirms the trend."
Sophie : Parfait, le passif convient mieux ici que « nous avons comparé ». Et pour les recommandations ? Perfect, the passive fits better here than "we compared." And for the recommendations?
Karim : Il conviendrait d’organiser une consultation avant toute décision. Et il serait souhaitable d’envisager un marché nocturne à titre d’essai. It would be advisable to organize a consultation before any decision. And it would be desirable to consider a night market on a trial basis.
Sophie : Il importe aussi de préciser un calendrier, sinon la recommandation reste vague. It's also important to specify a timeline, otherwise the recommendation stays vague.
Karim : D’accord. En conclusion, on peut simplement écrire qu’une communication plus claire avec les habitants reste l’enjeu principal, bien que la mairie dispose déjà de plusieurs pistes. Agreed. In conclusion, we can simply write that clearer communication with residents remains the main issue at stake, although the town hall already has several options.

This dialogue models the newly productive report-structure connectors (dans un premier temps, il convient de noter que, en conclusion), the newly productive recommendation frames (il conviendrait de, il serait souhaitable de), the newly productive impersonal frame (il importe de), the passive now foregrounded for report style (ont été comparés), and a recycled Volume III Chapter 19 concession. No unauthorized subjonctif trigger appears.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Why French reports favor the impersonal

French institutional and professional writing often favors impersonal and passive constructions over a first-person voice, especially in the findings section of a report. This is not simply a stylistic preference: foregrounding the finding or the measure, rather than the person who observed or proposed it, is often read as more neutral and more credible in this genre. The recommendations section can shift toward a slightly more direct voice, as with nous recommandons de, without abandoning this overall register.

Regional Variation — Report conventions across the Francophone world

Expectations for report structure, the balance between passive and active voice, and the directness of recommendations vary somewhat across French, Belgian, Swiss, Québécois, and other Francophone administrative and professional traditions. This chapter’s vocabulary and patterns reflect common metropolitan French usage; the underlying skill of separating findings from recommendations transfers across contexts even where specific conventions differ.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Forgetting past participle agreement in the passive

Error: Writing *une baisse a été constaté, leaving the participle in its default masculine singular form.

Correction: In the passive, the past participle agrees in gender and number with the subject, exactly as it does elsewhere with être: une baisse a été constatée (feminine singular), les résultats ont été comparés (masculine plural).

Common Mistake — Mixing recommendations into the findings section

Error: Writing il conviendrait de… in the middle of the findings, before any recommendation has been signaled.

Correction: Keep the findings section descriptive; save recommendation frames (il conviendrait de, il serait souhaitable de, nous recommandons de) for the recommendations section, so the report’s two-part structure stays visible to the reader.

Common Mistake — Using a receptive-only frame productively

Out of scope here: Il conviendrait qu’on organise une consultation. This sentence is grammatically correct French — il conviendrait que genuinely takes the subjonctif — but it introduces a subjonctif environment beyond this volume’s authorized set, so it is not to be produced at this level.

Use instead: Il conviendrait d’organiser une consultation. This chapter's productive frame stays with the infinitive construction. Save que + subjonctif for a recycled Volume III trigger if a subjonctif clause is genuinely needed.

  2. "Une baisse…a été constatée" or "ces résultats ont été comparés"; both emphasize the finding or measure rather than the person responsible.
  3. Organize a targeted consultation; consider a trial night market; communicate the consultation results clearly.
  4. Although the drop is modest, it justifies prompt attention, and clearer communication with residents remains the main issue at stake.

Writing Workshop — Model text

Modèle — Un rapport interne

À la lumière des données réunies ce trimestre, ce rapport présente la situation du marché du quartier. Dans un premier temps, une baisse de dix pour cent de la fréquentation a été constatée au cours des trois derniers mois. Dans un second temps, ces résultats ont été comparés aux données de l’an dernier, ce qui confirme la tendance déjà observée. Il convient de noter qu’aucune consultation des habitants n’a encore été organisée à ce sujet. Il conviendrait donc d’organiser une consultation ciblée avant toute décision définitive. Il serait par ailleurs souhaitable d’envisager un marché nocturne ponctuel, à titre d’essai. Il importe enfin de communiquer clairement les résultats de cette consultation à l’ensemble du quartier. En conclusion, bien que la baisse reste modeste, elle justifie une attention rapide de la part de la mairie.

In light of the data gathered this quarter, this report presents the situation of the neighborhood market. First, a ten percent drop in attendance was observed over the last three months. Second, these results were compared with last year's data, which confirms the trend already observed. It should be noted that no consultation of residents has yet been organized on this subject. It would therefore be advisable to organize a targeted consultation before any final decision. It would furthermore be desirable to consider an occasional night market, on a trial basis. Finally, it is important to communicate the results of this consultation clearly to the whole neighborhood. In conclusion, although the drop remains modest, it justifies prompt attention from the town hall.

This eight-sentence model opens with à la lumière de, presents two findings using dans un premier temps / dans un second temps with the passive voice in both, notes a further finding with il convient de noter que, makes recommendations using all three recommendation/necessity frames, and closes with en conclusion, recycling the Volume III Chapter 19 concession environment (bien que…reste) in its final sentence. No subjonctif trigger beyond those already activated in Volumes I–III and Volume IV Chapters 1–12 appears. Other responses are acceptable when they meet all task requirements.

Practical Performance Task

Successful completion requires both components: a spoken summary meeting every listed element, and a written report of eight to eleven sentences meeting every required element listed in the rubric above. Partial completion of either component does not meet the task’s requirements.

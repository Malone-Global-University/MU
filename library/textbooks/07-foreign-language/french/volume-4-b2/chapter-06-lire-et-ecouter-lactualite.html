Lire et écouter l’actualité

News and Current Events · Speak and Write French: Professional, Academic, Media, and Institutional French

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Understand and summarize a short, controlled news item in speech and in writing, identifying what happened, who is involved, and what source the information comes from.
  2. Use source-framing expressions — selon, d’après, and à en croire — newly productive in this chapter, to attribute a piece of information to a named source, and combine them with the already-active reported-speech verbs dire que, déclarer que, and expliquer que (recycled from Chapter 3).
  3. Recycle the eleven Chapter 8–11 subjonctif triggers, the Chapter 18 negative-belief/negative-certainty environment, and the Chapter 19 bien que/quoique concession environment inside a news-based task, without any new productive trigger.
  4. Add and recycle a curated news/journalism vocabulary set: un titre, une source, un fait divers, une dépêche, annoncer, confirmer, démentir, and selon un communiqué.
  5. Recycle dont, the Chapter 2 impersonal-plus-infinitive frames, and the Chapter 4 cause/consequence connectors and fronted frames, applied to reporting news.
  6. Write a short news summary (six to nine sentences) that attributes information to at least one named source.
  7. Move to a controlled introduction to a short news article and a short radio-style news bulletin, recognizing their basic organization: headline, lead, source, and detail.

Communication Scenario

Unité II turns from institutional life toward the wider world: media, society, and public debate. Mme Girard's team has started a short Friday tradition — a five-minute round-up of one news item relevant to the office or the region, presented briefly at the start of the weekly meeting. Sophie is asked to present the first round-up, and Karim is asked to help by finding a short written item they can both draw from. Chapter 6 opens this new unit with the basic tools of following the news in French: reading a short article, listening to a short bulletin, and reporting what a source says, clearly and accurately.

  1. Identify the main fact of a short news item: what happened, and to whom.
  2. Attribute the information to its source, using selon or d’après.
  3. Report what an official or spokesperson said, using reported speech.
  4. Summarize the item briefly and clearly for colleagues.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — The clipped, level delivery of a news lead

No new sound-spelling or vowel feature is introduced in this chapter. The task is recognizing, in listening, the somewhat faster, more level delivery typical of news bulletins, where sentence stress falls on the key facts (who, what, when) rather than on emotional emphasis — a controlled, formulaic first step toward the fuller listening-pronunciation variation work later in the volume.

  • A news lead: Selon la mairie, le pont sera fermé à partir de lundi. — level, unhurried pace, with a slight emphasis on fermé and lundi, the two key facts.
  • Review the obligatory liaison in les_habitants (determiner + noun) and the elision in d’après. Note that selon does not take liaison before a following vowel (selon eux, no liaison); in connected speech, keep démentir within its rhythm group rather than giving it English-style word stress, since French stress falls on the last syllable of the rhythm group rather than on a fixed syllable of the verb itself.

Grammar: Lire et écouter l’actualité

Purpose: introduce source-framing expressions (selon, d’après, à en croire) as newly productive, and combine them with the already-active reported-speech verbs from Chapter 3 to attribute information in a news context. Combine these with already-active subjonctif environments, dont, and the Chapter 4 cause/consequence connectors and fronted frames. No new subjonctif trigger is introduced. Lequel and the passive voice remain available for accurate use but are not targets of this chapter.

6.1 Naming a source: selon, d’après, à en croire

Grammar Focus — Selon / d’après / à en croire + source

Selon and d’après are newly productive in this chapter and are largely interchangeable: both introduce the source of a piece of information and are typically placed at the start of the sentence, followed by a comma. À en croire is also newly productive and works the same way, but can carry a note of mild distance or reservation about the source, useful when a writer wants to attribute a claim without fully endorsing it. None of the three requires the subjonctif in the clause that follows, since they frame a stated fact rather than a doubt, wish, or necessity.

  • Selon la mairie, le pont sera fermé à partir de lundi. According to the town hall, the bridge will be closed starting Monday. selon + source, newly productive
  • D’après un communiqué de l’entreprise, la production reprendra la semaine prochaine. According to a company statement, production will resume next week. d’après + source, newly productive
  • À en croire certains habitants, les travaux ont déjà commencé. If some residents are to be believed, the work has already started. à en croire + source, newly productive, mild reservation implied

6.2 Combining source framing with reported speech

Building Block — A useful news-summary shape (this chapter's practice template)
[headline fact: selon / d’après + source] + [detail, using dont or a cause/consequence connector] + [an official statement: dire que / déclarer que / confirmer que + indicatif] + [optional denial: démentir + direct object] + [optional recycled subjonctif environment]
The source-framing frame is new to this chapter; the reported-speech verbs recycle Chapter 3 directly, now applied to attributing a statement to an official or spokesperson rather than a colleague in a meeting. Dont and the Chapter 4 connectors can add detail or explain a consequence. Not every summary needs every element: a short item may state only the fact and its source.
Selon la mairie, le pont sera fermé pendant trois semaines, un chantier dont plusieurs habitants se plaignent déjà. La mairie confirme que les travaux commenceront lundi. Bien que ce délai semble long, un responsable municipal déclare qu’il est nécessaire pour la sécurité du pont.
According to the town hall, the bridge will be closed for three weeks, a project several residents are already complaining about. The town hall confirms that the work will begin Monday. Although this timeframe seems long, a municipal official states that it is necessary for the bridge's safety.
Grammar Focus — Confirmer que, and démentir with a direct object

Confirmer que extends the Chapter 3 reported-speech pattern to a verb especially common in news contexts: confirming a piece of information. It normally takes the indicatif in this factual-reporting use, since it presents the speaker's stated position as fact, exactly like dire que and déclarer que.

Démentir ("to deny") is genuinely used with a que clause plus the subjonctif in authentic French (les autorités démentent qu’elles aient envoyé des troupes). This chapter, however, keeps démentir productive only with a direct object, so that no additional subjonctif environment is introduced here. To deny a specific claim, use démentir plus a noun phrase, and state the actual fact separately using an already-authorized reported-speech verb.

  • Le service confirme que le chantier commencera lundi. The department confirms that the project will begin Monday. confirmer que + indicatif, newly extended reported-speech verb
  • L’entreprise dément cette rumeur. Elle déclare que la production n’a pas été arrêtée. The company denies this rumor. It states that production was not halted. démentir + direct object (no que clause); déclarer que + indicatif carries the factual statement

Model Dialogue: Devant les infos du matin

Context: Sophie and Karim prepare Sophie's Friday news round-up before the team meeting. Register: semi-formal working conversation between colleagues.

Karim : J’ai trouvé un fait divers utile pour vendredi : la fermeture du pont de la Guillotière. I found a useful news item for Friday: the closure of the Guillotière bridge.
Sophie : Parfait. Selon la source, quand les travaux commencent-ils ? Perfect. According to the source, when does the work begin?
Karim : D’après un communiqué de la mairie, le pont sera fermé à partir de lundi, un chantier dont plusieurs habitants se plaignent déjà. According to a town hall statement, the bridge will be closed starting Monday, a project several residents are already complaining about.
Sophie : À en croire certains habitants, les travaux auraient déjà commencé. Est-ce confirmé ? According to some residents, the work has reportedly already begun. Is that confirmed?
Karim : Non, la mairie dément cette rumeur. Un responsable municipal déclare que les travaux commenceront bien lundi, et que la sécurité du pont exige ce délai. No, the town hall denies that rumor. A municipal official states that the work will indeed begin Monday, and that the bridge's safety requires this timeframe.
Sophie : Bien que ce délai semble long, il faut que je le présente clairement vendredi. Although this timeframe seems long, I need to present it clearly on Friday.
Karim : Je t’envoie la dépêche complète ce soir. I'll send you the full dispatch tonight.

This dialogue models the newly productive source-framing expressions (selon, d’après, à en croire), démentir used with a direct object rather than a que clause, the recycled reported-speech verb déclarer que + indicatif, a recycled dont, a recycled Chapter 19 concession, and a recycled Chapter 8–11 trigger. No unauthorized subjonctif trigger appears.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Attributing information carefully

In French-language professional journalism, as in responsible journalism more broadly, writers tend to attribute claims explicitly to a named source rather than presenting information as simple, unattributed fact. Distinguishing selon la mairie… from il est clair que… matters: the first flags whose claim it is, the second presents something as settled. This habit of careful attribution is a transferable skill, useful well beyond journalism.

Regional Variation — News style across the Francophone world

News conventions, source-attribution habits, and the balance between print, radio, and digital media vary across France, Québec, Belgium, Switzerland, and other Francophone regions. This chapter's vocabulary and patterns reflect common metropolitan French news usage; the underlying skill of tracking a source and a claim transfers across contexts even where specific conventions differ.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Extending démentir beyond this chapter’s pattern

Error: Writing *La mairie dément que les travaux ont commencé, attaching a que clause directly to démentir.

Correction: Authentic French does allow démentir que plus the subjonctif (les autorités démentent qu’elles aient envoyé des troupes), but this pattern is deliberately not activated in this chapter. Here, keep démentir productive only with a direct object — la mairie dément cette rumeur — and state the actual fact in a separate sentence with an already-authorized reported-speech verb: elle déclare que les travaux n’ont pas commencé. This keeps the denial clear without introducing a new subjonctif environment at this stage.

Common Mistake — Omitting the source

Error: Reporting a fact as settled without ever naming where it comes from, which can make a summary sound more authoritative than the evidence supports.

Correction: Use selon, d’après, or à en croire to name the source clearly, especially for claims that are not independently confirmed.

Common Mistake — Confusing un fait divers with une dépêche

Error: Using un fait divers and une dépêche interchangeably.

Correction: Un fait divers describes the type of story (local, human-interest news); une dépêche describes the format (a short, agency-issued item). A dépêche can report a fait divers, but the two terms describe different things.

Writing Workshop — Model text

Modèle — Un résumé d’actualité

Selon un communiqué de la mairie de Lyon, le pont de la Guillotière sera fermé à la circulation à partir de lundi prochain, un chantier dont plusieurs riverains se plaignent déjà. D’après le service municipal concerné, ces travaux sont nécessaires pour renforcer la structure du pont. La mairie confirme que des itinéraires de déviation seront mis en place dès le premier jour. Étant donné que certaines rumeurs circulaient cette semaine, la mairie dément ces rumeurs et déclare que les travaux n’ont pas encore commencé. Bien que ce délai semble long à certains habitants, un responsable municipal déclare qu’il ne peut pas être raccourci. Il conviendrait de suivre les prochaines communications de la mairie à ce sujet.

According to a statement from the Lyon town hall, the Guillotière bridge will be closed to traffic starting next Monday, a project several local residents are already complaining about. According to the relevant municipal department, this work is necessary to reinforce the bridge's structure. The town hall confirms that detour routes will be set up from the first day. Given that some rumors were circulating this week, the town hall denies that work began before the announced date. Although this timeframe seems long to some residents, a municipal official states it cannot be shortened. It would be advisable to follow the town hall's next communications on this subject.

This six-sentence model attributes the main fact to a source (selon), uses a second source frame (d’après), uses dont once, recycles a Chapter 4 cause connector (étant donné que), uses confirme que + indicatif, uses dément with a direct object followed by déclare que + indicatif for the factual statement (no que clause after démentir, so no additional subjonctif environment is introduced), recycles the Chapter 19 concession environment (bien que…semble), and closes with a Chapter 2 impersonal frame (il conviendrait de). No subjonctif trigger beyond those authorized through Chapter 19 appears. Other responses are acceptable when they meet all task requirements.

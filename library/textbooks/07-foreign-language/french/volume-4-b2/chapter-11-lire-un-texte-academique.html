Lire un texte académique

Academic Reading · Speak and Write French: Professional, Academic, Media, and Institutional French

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Read a short, controlled academic-style article and identify its thesis, its organizing paragraph logic, and its main supporting points.
  2. Use nominalization, newly productive in this chapter, to turn a familiar verb or adjective into an abstract noun (décider → une décision; complexe → la complexité) for a denser, more written academic style.
  3. Use newly productive academic paragraph-logic connectors — d’abord, ensuite, par ailleurs, en outre, and enfin — to organize the internal logic of a short academic-style paragraph.
  4. Use two newly productive academic reporting verbs — soutenir que and démontrer que — plus the indicatif, and the newly productive citation-attribution frame il ressort de + noun phrase + que, to report what a text argues or shows.
  5. Recycle the Chapter 6 source-framing expressions and reported speech, the Chapter 7 data-commentary and comparison tools, the Chapter 8 concession and counterargument tools, and the Chapter 9 civic-institutional framing, applied to an academic-reading task, without any new productive subjonctif trigger.
  6. Add and recycle a curated academic-discourse vocabulary set: une thèse, une problématique, un paragraphe, soutenir, démontrer, mettre en évidence, and en somme.
  7. Write a short academic-style paragraph (six to nine sentences) that states a thesis, develops it with paragraph-logic connectors, and closes with a summary frame.
  8. Move to a controlled introduction to a short academic-style article and a short controlled academic commentary, opening Unité III.

Communication Scenario

Unité III turns from media and public debate toward research, study, and professional communication. Sophie has been invited to sit in on a university seminar related to her office's work on civic participation, and Karim, who studied at the same university, offers to help her prepare by reading the short article assigned for the session. Chapter 11 opens this new unit with the basic tools of academic reading in French: identifying a thesis, following a paragraph's internal logic, and reporting what an article argues or demonstrates — skills Sophie will need before she can contribute to the seminar discussion.

  1. Identify the thesis and problématique of a short academic-style text.
  2. Follow a paragraph's internal logic using academic connectors.
  3. Report what an author argues or demonstrates, using this chapter's academic reporting verbs.
  4. Summarize the article's main point briefly and clearly.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — The measured pace of academic delivery

No new sound-spelling or vowel feature is introduced in this chapter. The task is practicing the controlled, measured academic delivery this chapter models, where paragraph-logic connectors receive a clear, separated pronunciation so a listener can track the stages of an argument; actual academic speech varies considerably in pace.

  • Marking a transition: D’abord, l’auteur pose la problématique. Ensuite, elle développe son argument. — a brief pause after each connector, keeping it in its own short rhythm group rather than blending it into the sentence that follows.
  • Review the required liaison in en_outre and the elision in qu’il ressort. Note that par ailleurs is pronounced as a single rhythm group, with the main prominence falling near the end, on -leurs, not on par.

11.1 Nominalization: turning a verb or adjective into an abstract noun

Grammar Focus — Nominalization for academic style

Nominalization is newly productive in this chapter: it is the process of turning a verb or an adjective already active from earlier volumes into a related abstract noun, a common feature of dense written academic style. Décider becomes une décision; complexe becomes la complexité; participer becomes la participation, already familiar from Chapter 9's la participation citoyenne. This chapter targets recognition and controlled production of nominalized forms already implicit in familiar vocabulary, not the invention of new abstract nouns from unfamiliar roots.

  • La complexité du dossier explique la lenteur de la décision. The complexity of the matter explains the slowness of the decision. complexe → la complexité; décider → la décision, newly productive
  • L’auteur insiste sur l’importance de la participation citoyenne. The author insists on the importance of civic participation. important → l’importance; participer → la participation, newly productive

11.2 Organizing an argument: d’abord, ensuite, par ailleurs, en outre, enfin

Building Block — A useful academic-paragraph shape (this chapter's practice template)
[thesis: l’auteur soutient que / il ressort de…que] + [d’abord + first point] + [ensuite + second point] + [par ailleurs / en outre + additional point] + [enfin + closing point] + [en somme + summary]
The connectors and reporting verbs are new to this chapter; the academic vocabulary and nominalized forms recycle familiar roots. Not every paragraph needs every connector: a short summary may use only d’abord and enfin.
L’auteur soutient que la participation citoyenne dépend directement de la clarté de l’information disponible. D’abord, elle démontre que les habitants mal informés votent moins souvent. Ensuite, elle met en évidence l’importance des consultations locales. Par ailleurs, il ressort de son étude que la complexité administrative décourage certains citoyens. En somme, une meilleure communication institutionnelle favoriserait une participation plus large.
The author argues that civic participation depends directly on the clarity of available information. First, she demonstrates that poorly informed residents vote less often. Then, she highlights the importance of local consultations. Furthermore, it emerges from her study that administrative complexity discourages some citizens. In sum, better institutional communication would encourage broader participation.
Grammar Focus — Soutenir que, démontrer que, and il ressort de…que

Soutenir que and démontrer que are newly productive in this chapter and take the indicatif in this factual-reporting use, parallel to Chapter 6's déclarer que and confirmer que: both report a claim or a finding as the source's stated position, not as a doubt, wish, or necessity. Il ressort de + noun phrase + que is also newly productive: it is an impersonal citation frame that attributes a conclusion to a text or study without naming a personal subject, useful when summarizing what a source shows rather than what an individual author states directly.

  • L’auteur soutient que la clarté de l’information favorise la participation. The author argues that clarity of information favors participation. soutenir que + indicatif, newly productive
  • Il ressort de cette étude que la complexité administrative décourage certains citoyens. It emerges from this study that administrative complexity discourages some citizens. il ressort de + noun phrase + que + indicatif, newly productive

Model Dialogue: Avant le séminaire

Context: Sophie and Karim discuss the article assigned for Sophie's upcoming seminar. Register: semi-formal, moving toward the academic register of the seminar itself.

Sophie : J’ai lu l’article, mais je ne suis pas sûre d’avoir bien compris la thèse. I've read the article, but I'm not sure I understood the thesis well.
Karim : L’auteur soutient que la participation citoyenne dépend surtout de la clarté de l’information. The author argues that civic participation depends mainly on the clarity of information.
Sophie : D’accord. Et comment développe-t-elle cette problématique dans le premier paragraphe ? Okay. And how does she develop this research question in the first paragraph?
Karim : D’abord, elle démontre que les habitants mal informés votent moins souvent. Ensuite, elle met en évidence l’importance des consultations locales. First, she demonstrates that poorly informed residents vote less often. Then, she highlights the importance of local consultations.
Sophie : Par ailleurs, il me semble qu’elle parle aussi de la complexité administrative. Furthermore, it seems to me she also talks about administrative complexity.
Karim : Exactement. Il ressort de son étude que cette complexité décourage certains citoyens, bien que la plupart restent motivés. Exactly. It emerges from her study that this complexity discourages some citizens, although most remain motivated.
Sophie : En somme, une meilleure communication institutionnelle favoriserait la participation. Je crois que je suis prête pour le séminaire. In sum, better institutional communication would encourage participation. I think I'm ready for the seminar.

This dialogue models the newly productive nominalized forms, the newly productive paragraph-logic connectors (d’abord, ensuite, par ailleurs, en somme), the newly productive academic reporting verbs and citation frame (soutenir que, démontrer que, il ressort de…que), and a recycled Volume III Chapter 19 concession (bien que la plupart restent motivés). No unauthorized subjonctif trigger appears.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — The shape of a French academic paragraph

French academic writing often favors an explicit, signposted structure: a stated thesis, clearly sequenced supporting points, and a summarizing conclusion. Connectors like d’abord, ensuite, and en somme are not mere decoration; they make a paragraph's logic visible to the reader, a convention especially valued in French secondary and university writing instruction.

Regional Variation — Academic writing conventions across the Francophone world

Expectations for paragraph structure, citation style, and argumentative organization vary somewhat across French, Belgian, Swiss, Québécois, and other Francophone academic traditions. This chapter's vocabulary and patterns reflect common metropolitan French academic usage; the underlying skill of stating a thesis and organizing supporting points transfers across contexts even where specific conventions differ.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Confusing par ailleurs and cependant

Error: Using par ailleurs where a contrast, not an addition, is intended.

Correction: Par ailleurs and en outre add a related point; they do not contrast one point with another. For a contrast, use the recycled Chapter 8 cependant, néanmoins, or toutefois instead.

Common Mistake — Adding an article before an impersonal il ressort de

Error: Writing *il ressort de la l’étude que, doubling the article after de.

Correction: Il ressort de + noun phrase + que: il ressort de l’étude que…. Here l’ is simply the elided definite article before a vowel, not a contraction; de itself does not change. True contractions of de with an article occur elsewhere, already active from earlier volumes: de + le → du, de + les → des (il ressort des données que…). No article at all is needed when the noun phrase doesn’t take one, as in il ressort de son analyse que….

Common Mistake — Forcing an unfamiliar nominalization

Error: Attempting to nominalize a verb or adjective whose noun form is unfamiliar or irregular, producing a form that does not exist in French.

Correction: This chapter targets nominalized forms built from already-familiar roots, such as décision, complexité, and participation. When a nominalized form is unfamiliar, keep the verb or adjective in its known form rather than guessing at an invented noun.

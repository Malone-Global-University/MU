Raconter avec précision

Precise Narration · Speak and Write French: Independent Communication and Advanced Expression

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Sequence a connected narrative precisely using d'abord, ensuite, puis, après (cela), and enfin, alongside pendant que and au moment où from Volume II.
  2. Use dès que and une fois que to mark immediate or completed sequence in a past narrative, choosing the tense appropriate to the relationship between the events.
  3. Move the plus-que-parfait from the formulaic, recognition-only stage of Chapter 1 into limited, controlled productive use, to state that something had already happened before another past event.
  4. Distinguish, in a connected narrative, among passé composé (foreground events), imparfait (background and ongoing states), and plus-que-parfait (an earlier, completed event), choosing correctly among the three.
  5. Continue to apply consistent tu/vous register while narrating a longer, more detailed account, and recognize how the same story shifts register between an informal and a formal version.
  6. Read and listen to a narrative with more than one time layer and identify which event happened first.
  7. Write a precise narrative of six to ten sentences that correctly layers passé composé, imparfait, and at least one controlled plus-que-parfait.

Communication Scenario

The conference from Chapter 1 has ended, and Karim's team's presentation is now behind him. Sophie calls to ask how it went, and Karim wants to tell the story properly — not just what happened, but in what order, and what had already been done beforehand to make it possible. In the Reading Practice, you will also see how the same underlying story looks in a short note to Madame Rousseau, in a more careful register. Chapter 1 asked you to reconnect; this chapter asks you to tell a story about what happened since, with enough precision that a listener can follow exactly what came first, what came next, and what had already taken place before the story even began.

  1. Tell a connected story in a clear order, using sequencing connectors rather than a flat list of sentences.
  2. Mark, at least once, that something had already happened before the main events of the story.
  3. Keep passé composé, imparfait, and plus-que-parfait clearly distinguished as you narrate.
  4. Maintain a single, appropriate register throughout your narration, and recognize how the same story shifts register in the paired reading texts.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Sustaining connected speech across a layered narrative

Chapter 1 introduced register-sensitive pitch and pacing. This chapter develops connected speech across a longer, layered narrative: as a story adds a "before the story" layer with the plus-que-parfait, your rhythm groups and liaison have more work to do, since each new clause needs to stay clearly attached to the moment it describes.

Two features deserve particular attention this chapter:

  • Obligatory liaison between the subject pronoun and the auxiliary that follows it: nous‿avions préparé (nou-z-avions) and ils‿étaient arrivés (il-z-étaient) both link smoothly, exactly as with other compound tenses you already control.
  • A brief pause before a sequencing connector that opens a new step — D'abord…, Ensuite…, Enfin… — helps a listener hear the story's structure instead of one long unbroken stream of clauses. With dès que and une fois que, keep the connector attached to the clause it introduces — do not pause between the connector and its clause — and instead place the pause after the completed subordinate clause, right before the main clause.

Practice the following aloud, keeping each connector attached to its own clause and pausing only where marked:

  • D'abord, ¦ nous avions déjà préparé nos diapositives. ¦ Ensuite, ¦ la présentation a commencé.
  • Dès que j'ai fini de parler, ¦ le public a applaudi.
  • Une fois que tout était terminé, ¦ nous sommes allés fêter ça.

No new sound-spelling feature is introduced. You are expected to sustain liaison, enchaînement, and rhythm-group pacing — carried forward from every earlier volume — over a narrative that now moves across more than one time layer.

Grammar: Raconter avec précision

Purpose: teach connectors that sequence a narrative precisely, and move the plus-que-parfait from Chapter 1's formulaic recognition into limited, controlled productive use for stating that one event had already happened before another past event. No subjonctif is introduced. The passive voice and reported speech remain formulaic and receptive, exactly as in Chapter 1.

2.1 Sequencing a narrative: d'abord, ensuite, puis, après, enfin

Building Block — Marking the steps of a story
D'abord, [événement 1]. Ensuite / Puis, [événement 2]. Après (cela), [événement 3]. Enfin, [événement final].
These connectors do not change any verb form; they simply signal, at the start of a sentence or clause, where that event falls in the sequence. Using them turns a list of passé composé sentences into a story a listener can follow without effort. They combine freely with everything you already control: passé composé for the events themselves, imparfait for background description woven in between.
D'abord, nous avons préparé la salle. Ensuite, les participants sont arrivés. Puis, notre équipe a commencé la présentation. Enfin, nous avons répondu aux questions.
First, we set up the room. Next, the participants arrived. Then, our team began the presentation. Finally, we answered questions.

2.2 The plus-que-parfait: from recognition to limited productive use

Language Focus

In Chapter 1, the plus-que-parfait appeared only as a fixed, whole expression to recognize. This chapter activates it productively, but within a deliberately narrow boundary: you are asked to use it only to say that something had already happened before the main events of a story you are narrating in the passé composé/imparfait.

Form: the plus-que-parfait is built exactly like the passé composé, except that the auxiliary (avoir or être) is in the imparfait instead of the présent. Auxiliary choice, reflexive verbs, and past participle agreement all follow the same rules you already control from compound-tense narration.

  • Nous avions déjà préparé nos diapositives quand la réunion a commencé. We had already prepared our slides when the meeting began. plus-que-parfait (earlier event) + passé composé (main-story event)
  • Mes collègues étaient déjà arrivés quand je suis entré dans la salle. My colleagues had already arrived when I entered the room. être auxiliary + agreement, exactly as in the passé composé

Notice the pattern in both examples: the plus-que-parfait clause states what had already happened, almost always signaled by déjà or by a clear "before" relationship to the main story-line, while the passé composé clause narrates the main story itself. This chapter asks you to produce this specific pattern — one earlier completed event set against the main narration — rather than to freely generate plus-que-parfait sentences in every context. A fuller, more independent system of plus-que-parfait use is developed later in the volume.

2.3 Dès que and une fois que: marking immediate or completed sequence

Language Note — Two precise time connectors

Dès que ("as soon as") links two events that happened in very quick succession; both clauses are usually in the passé composé in a past narrative. Une fois que ("once," "after") emphasizes that the first event was fully completed before the second began, and pairs naturally — though not obligatorily — with the plus-que-parfait pattern from section 2.2.

  • Dès que j'ai fini de parler, le public a applaudi. As soon as I finished speaking, the audience applauded.
  • Une fois que nous avions répondu aux questions, la session s'est terminée. Once we had answered the questions, the session ended.

2.4 Choosing among passé composé, imparfait, and plus-que-parfait

Review and Integration — Three tools, three jobs

A precise narrative now has three tools available, each with a distinct job: passé composé narrates the events that move the story forward; imparfait describes background, ongoing states, and how things were; and plus-que-parfait, used narrowly in this chapter, marks a single event that had already happened before the story's main timeline. Ask yourself, for each clause: is this the story moving forward (passé composé), the scene being described (imparfait), or something that had already happened earlier (plus-que-parfait)?

  • J'étais un peu nerveux (imparfait — background state), mais nous avions déjà répété plusieurs fois (plus-que-parfait — already done before), alors tout s'est bien passé (passé composé — main event). I was a bit nervous, but we had already rehearsed several times, so everything went well.

The subjonctif is still not active anywhere in this chapter. The passive voice and reported speech with tense shift remain formulaic and receptive only, exactly as authorized by the Chapter 2 matrix statuses; continue to treat the examples from Chapter 1 section 1.4 as fixed phrases rather than patterns to generate freely.

Model Dialogue: Ça s'est très bien passé

Context: The day after the conference, Sophie calls Karim to hear how the presentation went. Register: informal, close friends; tu throughout.

Sophie : Alors, raconte ! Comment s'est passée la présentation ? So, tell me! How did the presentation go?
Karim : Très bien, en fait ! J'avais un peu le trac le matin, mais nous avions déjà répété plusieurs fois la semaine dernière, donc j'étais assez préparé. Very well, actually! I had a bit of stage fright in the morning, but we had already rehearsed several times last week, so I was fairly well prepared.
Sophie : Et ça s'est déroulé comment, exactement ? And how did it go, exactly?
Karim : D'abord, nous avons installé la salle. Ensuite, les participants sont arrivés, et notre équipe a commencé à présenter. Dès que j'ai fini de parler, le public a applaudi — ça m'a fait très plaisir. First, we set up the room. Then, the participants arrived, and our team started presenting. As soon as I finished speaking, the audience applauded — that made me really happy.
Sophie : Et après ça ? And after that?
Karim : Une fois que nous avions répondu aux questions, la session s'est terminée, et enfin, tout le monde est allé fêter ça ensemble. Ah, et devine qui j'ai croisé là-bas ? Once we had answered the questions, the session ended, and finally, everyone went to celebrate together. Oh, and guess who I ran into there?
Sophie : Madame Rousseau, non ? Madame Rousseau, right?
Karim : Exactement ! Tu te souviens de tout, toi. Exactly! You remember everything.

This dialogue models the sequencing connectors d'abord, ensuite, après, and enfin; the time connectors dès que and une fois que; and two controlled, productive plus-que-parfait clauses (nous avions déjà répété…, une fois que nous avions répondu…) set against passé composé main-story events.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Making chronology explicit

A story told among close friends can often rely on shared context and a few simple connectors (et puis, après) because the listener can fill in gaps or simply ask. A story told to someone with less shared context — a new acquaintance, or an account written down for someone who was not there — usually benefits from making the order of events more explicit, which is exactly what the fuller set of connectors and the controlled plus-que-parfait in this chapter allow you to do. Choosing a denser or lighter set of sequencing tools is therefore a communicative choice tied to your audience, not simply a matter of correctness.

Regional Variation — Narrative connectors

D'abord, ensuite, puis, enfin, dès que, and une fois que are used and understood throughout the Francophone world in both speech and writing. Some informal spoken varieties favor après alone (without ensuite) as an all-purpose "then" connector; the fuller set taught in this chapter remains the safer, broadly recognized default for both speaking and writing.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Using the plus-que-parfait for ordinary past narration

Error: Writing *Hier, j'avais préparé la salle to mean simply "yesterday, I prepared the room," using the plus-que-parfait where the passé composé is required.

Correction: Use the plus-que-parfait only when an event happened before another past event you are narrating: j'avais déjà préparé la salle quand les participants sont arrivés. For an ordinary past event on its own timeline, use the passé composé: hier, j'ai préparé la salle.

Common Mistake — Stacking connectors without new information

Error: Opening every single sentence in a narrative with a connector (D'abord… Ensuite… Ensuite… Ensuite… Enfin…), which becomes repetitive rather than precise.

Correction: Use a connector at genuine turning points in the story, and let some sentences connect through content alone. A narrative with three or four well-placed connectors reads more naturally than one where every sentence begins with one.

Common Mistake — Forgetting auxiliary agreement inside the plus-que-parfait

Error: Writing *Mes collègues était déjà arrivé, forgetting subject-verb agreement on the imparfait auxiliary and participle agreement with an être verb.

Correction: The plus-que-parfait follows exactly the same auxiliary and agreement rules as the passé composé: mes collègues étaient déjà arrivés, with the imparfait auxiliary agreeing with the subject and the participle agreeing with a preceding être subject, exactly as you already practice in ordinary past narration.

  4. Dont is a relative pronoun replacing a complement introduced by de: la présentation dont je vous avais parlé corresponds to je vous avais parlé de la présentation (from parler de, recycled from Volume II). This is a relative-clause pattern, not reported speech; the verb inside the clause happens to be the controlled plus-que-parfait from this chapter's Grammar section 2.2.

Writing Workshop — Model text

Modèle — Récit d'un événement préparé

La semaine dernière, j'ai passé un examen important. J'étais assez nerveux le matin, mais j'avais déjà étudié pendant plusieurs semaines, donc je me sentais assez prêt. D'abord, je suis arrivé un peu en avance pour me calmer. Ensuite, l'examen a commencé, et dès que j'ai lu les premières questions, je me suis senti plus confiant. Une fois que j'avais fini toutes les questions, j'ai relu mes réponses attentivement. Enfin, je suis rentré chez moi, fatigué mais content.

Last week, I took an important exam. I was fairly nervous in the morning, but I had already studied for several weeks, so I felt fairly ready. First, I arrived a little early to calm down. Then, the exam began, and as soon as I read the first questions, I felt more confident. Once I had finished all the questions, I carefully reread my answers. Finally, I went home, tired but happy.

This six-sentence model includes three sequencing connectors, one imparfait background clause, two controlled plus-que-parfait clauses, and one use of dès que and une fois que each. No subjonctif, passive, or freely generated reported-speech form appears anywhere. Other responses are acceptable when they meet all task requirements and remain within the grammar and vocabulary activated through Chapter 2.

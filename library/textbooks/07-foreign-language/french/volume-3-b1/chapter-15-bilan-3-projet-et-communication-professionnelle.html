Bilan 3 : Projet et communication professionnelle

Unit III Integration · Speak and Write French: Independent Communication and Advanced Expression

Chapter Objectives

This chapter introduces no new productive grammar, pronunciation, orthography, vocabulary-domain, register, or skill system. By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Combine the education, workplace, project, and evaluation vocabulary recycled from Chapters 11–14 — goals, formal requests, project stages, and evaluation criteria — into a single sustained presentation and written report.
  2. Open a topic impersonally with il s’agit de, and state a recommendation impersonally with il convient de, exactly as authorized in Chapter 13.
  3. Sequence a multi-stage account using d’abord, ensuite, puis, and enfin, framed by pour commencer and pour conclure.
  4. Use the relative pronoun dont productively to link an idea back to a noun governed by de, exactly as authorized in Chapter 14; lequel and its family remain receptive only.
  5. Compare options with recycled comparative and superlative structures, and close with a nuanced recommendation using je recommanderais de or the conditionnel trigger il vaudrait mieux que.
  6. Continue productive use of all eleven Chapter 8–11 subjonctif triggers, the purpose-clause contrast (pour + infinitif versus pour que + subjonctif), article omission with professions, formal correspondence conventions, and already-consolidated double-object pronoun order.
  7. Maintain a single, deliberately chosen formal or semi-formal register across an extended spoken presentation and a linked written report.
  8. Listen to and read an integrated professional account and identify its goal, stages, criteria, and recommendation.
  9. Deliver a spoken presentation and write a linked report of eight to twelve sentences that together present a project, evaluate at least two options, and close with a clear, appropriately softened recommendation — using only grammar and vocabulary authorized through Chapter 14.

Communication Scenario

Three weeks have passed. Sophie's training plan is underway, and the outside trainer has been chosen. Monsieur Lefèvre now asks Sophie to close the loop for the wider team: a short spoken update at the next meeting, followed by a one-page written report for the file. Karim, who has watched Sophie grow more confident since Chapter 12's first formal email, helps her plan both pieces together — this bilan does not teach anything new; it asks Sophie to put everything from Unité III to work at once, cleanly and consistently.

  1. Open the update impersonally with il s’agit de.
  2. Sequence the project's stages with at least three connectors.
  3. Use dont at least once to link an idea back to a noun governed by de.
  4. Compare at least two aspects of the outcome and close with one nuanced recommendation formula.
  5. Maintain one consistent register across both the spoken update and the written report.

Pronunciation Review

Pronunciation Review — Discourse pacing and the pronunciations of plus

No new sound-spelling, liaison, or vowel feature is introduced in this chapter. Two features recycled from Chapters 13–14 remain especially relevant for a sustained presentation and are worth reviewing before the performance task.

  • Sequencing pause (Chapter 13): D’abord, // il s’agit de… A brief pause after a connector at presentation pace marks a new stage clearly for the listener.
  • The pronunciations of plus (Chapter 14): silent s before a consonant (plus fiable [ply]); liaison [z] before a vowel (plus avantageux [plyz]); pronounced [s] under contrastive emphasis (j’en veux plus [plys]).

Review the elisions in il s’agit and qu’il, and the optional liaison after dont, before completing the performance task.

As in every chapter since Chapter 8, no twelfth subjonctif trigger and no new irregular subjonctif paradigm may be used productively. Il s’agit de and il convient de remain limited to the infinitive (or a noun, for il s’agit de); il convient que plus the subjonctif is not assessed. Lequel and its family remain receptive only. Denser stylistic clitic stacking beyond the two Chapter 12 modeled patterns remains at the Introduced stage. The passive voice remains receptive only. Model Dialogue: Le point avec M. Lefèvre

Context: Three weeks after Chapter 14, Sophie updates the team at a meeting led by Monsieur Lefèvre, with Karim present. Register: formal throughout the update itself (vous); a brief private exchange with Karim beforehand uses tu, exactly as in Chapter 13.

Karim : Tu as tout préparé ? Le calendrier, les critères, la recommandation finale ? Have you got everything ready? The timeline, the criteria, the final recommendation?
Sophie : Mesdames, Messieurs, il s’agit de faire le point sur la formation et sur le choix de la formatrice. Ladies and gentlemen, this is about reviewing the training and the choice of trainer.
Sophie : D’abord, il convient de rappeler l’objectif : mieux gérer les projets d’équipe. Ensuite, la formatrice dont nous avions parlé a mené les trois premières séances exactement comme prévu. First, it's appropriate to recall the goal: to better manage team projects. Next, the trainer we had talked about led the first three sessions exactly as planned.
M. Lefèvre : Est-ce que le coût plus élevé reste justifié, selon vous ? Does the higher cost remain justified, in your view?
Sophie : Oui. Puis, en ce qui concerne les résultats, elle est plus expérimentée que l’autre candidate, et l’équipe est déjà plus à l’aise avec la gestion de projet. Je le leur ai déjà fait remarquer lors du dernier bilan intermédiaire. Yes. Then, regarding the results, she's more experienced than the other candidate, and the team is already more comfortable with project management. I already pointed that out to them at the last progress check.
Sophie : Enfin, je recommanderais de poursuivre ce partenariat l’an prochain, pour que l’équipe puisse continuer à progresser. Finally, I would recommend continuing this partnership next year, so that the team can keep improving.
M. Lefèvre : Il vaudrait mieux que nous confirmions cette décision par écrit. Pourriez-vous rédiger un court rapport ? It would be better if we confirmed this decision in writing. Could you draft a short report?
Sophie : Bien sûr. Pour conclure, je vous l’enverrai dès demain. Of course. To conclude, I will send it to you as soon as tomorrow.

This dialogue integrates, without introducing anything new: both impersonal structures (il s’agit de, il convient de), a full four-part sequence, one use of dont, a recycled comparative, both nuanced recommendation formulas, a recycled information question (est-ce que…selon vous, kept deliberately simple), a recycled purpose clause, and two recycled double-object confirmations (je le leur ai déjà fait remarquer; je vous l’enverrai). Sophie maintains a single formal register throughout the update; the opening exchange with Karim is clearly separated as informal planning talk.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Closing the loop: from spoken update to written record

In many French workplace settings, an oral update to a team is often followed by a short written record for the file, even when the content of the two is largely the same. This is not redundancy: the spoken version informs and allows questions in real time, while the written version creates a stable, shareable reference. This chapter's linked task — the same content delivered in spoken and written form — reflects that common professional pattern rather than introducing a new genre.

Regional Variation — Written follow-up conventions

Whether a written follow-up is expected, and how formal it needs to be, varies by workplace and by country across the Francophone world; some environments treat a brief email as sufficient, while others expect a more structured note. This chapter's pattern remains a safe, broadly transferable default when the expected convention is uncertain.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Letting the spoken and written registers drift apart

Error: Delivering a formal spoken update with vous and consistent structures, then writing a written report that slips into informal vocabulary or a looser structure.

Correction: As in Chapters 12 and 13, choose one register deliberately and keep both linked pieces of a task consistent with it. A written report following a formal spoken update should remain formal throughout.

Common Mistake — Reaching for a new trigger or structure under time pressure

Error: Introducing il convient que plus the subjonctif, a form of lequel, or a newly constructed passive sentence while trying to sound more sophisticated for this longer task.

Correction: This chapter assesses fluent combination of already-authorized material, not new material. A longer presentation is better served by using the existing eleven triggers, dont, and the two Chapter 14 recommendation formulas accurately and confidently than by reaching for an unauthorized form.

Common Mistake — Treating the written report as a simple copy of the spoken update

Watch for: Writing the report as a word-for-word transcript of the spoken presentation, including hesitations or the brief informal exchange with a colleague.

Correction: The written report should cover the same content in complete, well-punctuated sentences appropriate to a written document, omitting any informal aside and applying the formal writing conventions active since Chapter 12.

Writing Workshop — Model text

Modèle — Un rapport de bilan

Il s’agit de faire le bilan de notre formation en gestion de projet de ce trimestre. D’abord, il convient de rappeler l’objectif initial : mieux répartir les tâches entre les membres de l’équipe. Ensuite, la formatrice dont nous avions discuté en réunion a mené six séances, toutes conformes au calendrier prévu. Puis, les premiers retours de l’équipe sont plus positifs que prévu, surtout en ce qui concerne la communication interne. Un membre de l’équipe a même déclaré que les réunions sont désormais mieux organisées qu’avant. Enfin, je recommanderais de renouveler ce partenariat l’an prochain, pour que l’équipe puisse continuer à progresser. Il vaudrait mieux que nous confirmions cette décision avant la fin du mois, pour réserver les dates nécessaires. C’est, à notre avis, l’option dont les avantages sont les plus clairs pour l’ensemble du service. Je vous transmettrai le calendrier détaillé dès que nous confirmerons la décision.

This is about reviewing our project-management training this term. First, it is appropriate to recall the initial goal: to better divide tasks among team members. Next, the trainer we had discussed in the meeting led six sessions, all in line with the planned schedule. Then, the team's initial feedback is more positive than expected, especially regarding internal communication. One team member even said that meetings are now better organized than before. Finally, I would recommend renewing this partnership next year, so that the team can continue to improve. It would be better if we confirmed this decision before the end of the month, to reserve the necessary dates. This is, in our view, the option whose advantages are clearest for the whole department. I will send you the detailed schedule as soon as we confirm the decision.

This nine-sentence model includes both impersonal structures, all four sequencing connectors, two uses of dont, one comparative framing (plus positifs que prévu), both nuanced recommendation formulas, and one recycled purpose clause (pour que). No unauthorized trigger, no productive lequel, and no newly constructed passive sentence appears anywhere; the model avoids reduced participial constructions since the passive remains receptive only. Other responses are acceptable when they meet all task requirements and remain within the grammar and vocabulary activated through Chapter 14.

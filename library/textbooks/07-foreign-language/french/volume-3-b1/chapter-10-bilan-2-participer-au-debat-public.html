Bilan 2 : Participer au débat public

Unit II Integration · Speak and Write French: Independent Communication and Advanced Expression
Bilan chapter. This chapter introduces no major new productive grammar, pronunciation, orthography, vocabulary-domain, register, or skill system. It consolidates and assesses the language activated in Chapters 6–9: source citation, reported speech, indirect questions, the subjonctif présent after ten authorized triggers, both si-clause hypothesis patterns, controlled ni…ni, and consolidated ne…que. The passive voice remains receptive only throughout.

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Discuss a public issue by combining vocabulary and structures from Chapters 6–9: news and media (Ch. 6), sourcing and reported information (Ch. 7), community life and the subjonctif présent (Ch. 8), and problem-solving with hypothesis and possibility (Ch. 9).
  2. Cite a source accurately with selon or d’après and keep the indicatif, distinguishing sourcing from the subjonctif-triggering expressions practiced in Chapters 8 and 9.
  3. Report what someone said or asked, including at least one indirect question, using the reported-speech patterns from Chapter 7.
  4. Use the subjonctif présent correctly and only after one or more of the ten authorized triggers from Chapters 8 and 9 (necessity, emotion, recommendation, and possibility), with verbs whose stem is transparently present-derived or one of the five memorized irregular paradigms.
  5. Form a realistic hypothesis with si + présent/futur and a more distant hypothesis with si + imparfait/conditionnel, choosing the pattern that matches the intended likelihood.
  6. Recognize and, in the two modeled patterns only, produce ni…ni, and use ne…que to state a restriction.
  7. Maintain a single, consistent register (tu/vous, semi-formal public discussion, or formal written summary) across an extended spoken or written response.
  8. Read and listen to an extended public-issue text and identify sourced facts, reactions, hypotheses, and recommendations separately.
  9. Produce an extended spoken discussion and a written summary of eight to twelve sentences on a public issue, correctly combining evidence from a source, at least two subjonctif triggers, both hypothesis patterns, and a clear recommendation — using only material activated through Chapter 9.

Communication Scenario

The neighborhood festival is now confirmed for its new date, and Madame Dubois has invited Sophie and Karim to a slightly larger event: a quarterly meeting of the conseil de quartier (neighborhood council), where residents debate a genuine public issue affecting several streets — whether to convert an underused lot into a shared garden or a small parking area. This is the moment Unit II has been building toward: reporting what sources say, reacting to the situation, weighing possibilities, and proposing a way forward, all in one extended, connected discussion. Chapter 10 does not teach anything new; it asks you to bring together everything Chapters 6 through 9 activated.

  1. Summarize what a written source reports about the issue, using selon or d’après and the indicatif.
  2. Report what a resident said or asked at an earlier meeting, including at least one indirect question.
  3. React to the situation and state what is necessary, using an authorized subjonctif trigger.
  4. Propose at least one hypothesis about what will happen or would happen depending on the council's decision.
  5. Make a clear recommendation and, if useful, state a restriction with ne…que.

Pronunciation Review

Pronunciation Review — No new feature, full recycling

This chapter introduces no new pronunciation or sound-spelling feature. It recycles and consolidates everything active through Chapter 9: liaison and enchaînement in rhythm groups; the audibly distinct subjonctif forms (que je sois, que j’aie, que je fasse, que je puisse, que j’aille) alongside the less audible regular endings; the -ions/-iez endings shared by the subjonctif, the imparfait, and the conditionnel; and the présent-versus-imparfait contrast immediately after si.

  • Il faut que le conseil décide rapidement. (regular subjonctif ending, recognized by the trigger, not the sound)
  • Si le conseil choisit le jardin, nous serons ravis ; si le conseil choisissait le parking, nous serions déçus. (présent vs. imparfait contrast right after si)

Use this chapter's extended speaking and listening tasks to check that these contrasts remain clear across longer stretches of connected speech, not only in single isolated sentences.

Grammar Consolidation: Unité II

Purpose: bring together, without adding anything new, the four major systems activated across Chapters 6–9 — sourcing and reported speech, the bounded subjonctif présent, the two si-clause hypothesis patterns, and controlled restriction/negation — so that they can be combined fluently in a single extended discussion. The passive voice remains receptive only.

10.1 Sourcing and reported speech (recycled from Chapters 6–7)

Review — Reporting what a source or a person said

Selon and d’après report a stated fact and do not themselves trigger the subjonctif, even when the reported content sounds uncertain. In the straightforward sourced statements practiced in this chapter, the reported proposition stays in the indicatif unless another construction in the same sentence independently requires a different mood. Reported speech recycles the indirect-question patterns from Chapter 7, including reported yes/no questions with si and reported information questions with the retained question word.

  • Selon le bulletin du conseil, le terrain appartient toujours à la ville. According to the council bulletin, the lot still belongs to the city. selon + indicatif (sourcing)
  • Un voisin a demandé si le conseil avait déjà consulté les habitants. A neighbor asked whether the council had already consulted residents. reported yes/no question with si

Note the important contrast with si in a hypothesis (section 10.3, below): si introducing a reported yes/no question here has nothing to do with the hypothesis si-clauses reviewed later in this chapter; the two uses share only the word, not the grammar.

Sourcing expressions (selon, d’après) and general possibility (peut-être que) remain indicatif, exactly as established in Chapters 7 and 9.

10.3 The two hypothesis patterns (recycled from Chapter 9)

Review — Two clean, matched pairings
Si + [présent], + [futur simple]  |  Si + [imparfait], + [conditionnel présent]
The realistic pattern presents a likely condition and its probable result; the more distant pattern presents a less likely or contrary-to-fact condition and its hypothetical result. Si is never directly followed by a conditionnel verb in either pattern.
Si le conseil choisit le jardin communautaire, plusieurs voisins s’impliqueront tout de suite. Si le conseil choisissait le parking, certains habitants seraient déçus.
If the council chooses the community garden, several neighbors will get involved right away. If the council chose the parking area, some residents would be disappointed.

10.4 Restriction and controlled negation (recycled from Chapter 9)

Review — Ne…que and the two modeled ni…ni patterns

Ne…que remains fully productive for stating a limit. Ni…ni remains limited to the two patterns modeled in Chapter 9, section 9.4: ne + verb + ni + adjective + ni + adjective, and ne + avoir + ni + noun + ni + noun.

  • Le conseil n’a que deux options réalistes. The council only has two realistic options.
  • Pour l’instant, nous n’avons ni décision ni date précise. For now, we have neither a decision nor a precise date. ne + avoir + ni + nom + ni + nom (Chapter 9 pattern)
Reminder — Still receptive, still bounded

The passive voice remains receptive only, exactly as in Chapters 6–9: recognize it in the reading passage below, but continue to prefer active constructions in your own speech and writing. No system beyond what Chapters 1–9 authorized may be assessed productively in this chapter.

Model Dialogue: Un débat au conseil de quartier

Context: Sophie, Karim, and Madame Dubois attend the quarterly neighborhood council meeting, where residents debate what to do with an underused lot. Register: semi-formal public discussion; vous throughout the formal part of the meeting, tu between Sophie and Karim aside.

Mme Dubois : Selon le bulletin municipal, le terrain de la rue Voltaire appartient toujours à la ville, et la mairie envisage deux options : un jardin communautaire ou un petit parking. According to the municipal bulletin, the lot on Rue Voltaire still belongs to the city, and city hall is considering two options: a community garden or a small parking area.
Karim : Un voisin m’a demandé hier si la mairie avait déjà pris une décision. Je lui ai répondu que non, que la réunion d’aujourd’hui allait justement en discuter. A neighbor asked me yesterday whether city hall had already made a decision. I told him no, that today's meeting was precisely going to discuss it.
Sophie : C’est bien que la mairie nous laisse un vrai choix. Il est important que nous partagions notre avis clairement aujourd’hui. It's good that city hall is leaving us a real choice. It's important that we share our opinion clearly today.
Mme Dubois : Si le conseil choisit le jardin communautaire, plusieurs bénévoles s’impliqueront tout de suite, d’après les réponses au questionnaire. If the council chooses the community garden, several volunteers will get involved right away, according to the survey responses.
Karim : Et si le conseil choisissait le parking à la place ? Certains habitants seraient déçus, je crois, mais d’autres apprécieraient sans doute plus de places pour se garer. And if the council chose the parking area instead? Some residents would be disappointed, I think, but others would probably appreciate more parking spaces.
Sophie : C’est dommage que nous n’ayons ni sondage complémentaire ni discussion approfondie sur le parking avant aujourd’hui. It's a shame that we have neither a follow-up survey nor an in-depth discussion about the parking option before today.
Mme Dubois : Il se peut que nous devions organiser une deuxième réunion avant le vote final. Pour l’instant, je propose que chacun donne son avis, puis que nous votions une première fois à titre indicatif. It may be that we need to organize a second meeting before the final vote. For now, I propose that everyone give their opinion, then that we vote once as an initial indication.
Karim : D’accord. Il vaut mieux que nous commencions maintenant : nous n’avons qu’une heure avant la fin de la réunion. Agreed. It's better that we start now: we only have one hour before the meeting ends.

This dialogue recycles sourcing (selon, d’après) and reported speech, including one reported yes/no question (si la mairie avait déjà pris une décision); six of the ten authorized subjonctif triggers (c’est bien que, il est important que, c’est dommage que, il se peut que, je propose que, il vaut mieux que); both hypothesis patterns; one Chapter 9 ni…ni pattern; and ne…que. No new trigger, pattern, or vocabulary domain appears anywhere in this dialogue.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Participatory local governance

Neighborhood councils and comparable consultative bodies exist across many French-speaking cities and towns, giving residents a structured way to weigh in on local decisions such as land use, public space, and municipal budgets. The vocabulary and structures practiced across Chapters 6–9 — sourcing information accurately, reporting what others have said, expressing necessity and reaction, and reasoning through possible outcomes — are the everyday tools of this kind of civic participation, not specialized political language.

Regional Variation — Naming local consultative bodies

Le conseil de quartier is a common and widely understood term, but the exact name and structure of neighborhood-level consultation varies by country and municipality (for example, un conseil citoyen or une commission de quartier in some contexts). The vocabulary in this chapter remains the safer, broadly recognized default across the Francophone world.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Mixing up the two uses of si

Error: Treating every si the same way, and mistakenly applying hypothesis tense-pairing rules to a reported yes/no question, or vice versa.

Correction: Reported si (Chapter 7) introduces an indirect yes/no question and follows normal indicatif sequencing: il a demandé si le conseil avait décidé. Hypothesis si (Chapter 9) follows one of the two fixed pairings and is never directly followed by a conditionnel verb: si le conseil décidait…, nous serions…. Identify which function si is serving before choosing the verb form.

Common Mistake — Letting a sourced fact drift into the subjonctif

Error: Writing *Selon le bulletin, la décision soit prise dans un mois, applying the subjonctif to information that is simply being reported.

Correction: Selon and d’après do not themselves trigger the subjonctif. In this straightforward sourced statement, use the indicatif: selon le bulletin, la décision sera prise dans un mois. The subjonctif belongs only after this volume's ten authorized triggers.

Common Mistake — Overloading a single sentence with every system at once

Watch for: Trying to combine sourcing, a subjonctif trigger, and a hypothesis inside one very long, tangled sentence, which usually breaks down grammatically before the end.

Correction: As modeled throughout this chapter's dialogue and reading passage, use separate sentences for each function — one for sourcing, one for reaction or necessity, one for each hypothesis — and let connectors link them. Clarity across several well-formed sentences communicates better than a single overloaded one.

Modèle — Résumé d’un enjeu de quartier

D’après le compte rendu de la dernière réunion, le conseil de quartier envisage deux options pour le terrain de la rue Voltaire : un jardin communautaire ou un petit parking. Plusieurs habitants ont demandé si la mairie avait déjà consulté les riverains directs, et la réponse a été que oui, mais seulement de manière informelle. Il est important que le conseil prenne une décision qui reflète vraiment l’avis de la majorité.

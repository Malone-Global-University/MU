Exprimer son point de vue

Giving a Viewpoint · Speak and Write French: Independent Communication and Advanced Expression

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. State an opinion clearly using je pense que, je trouve que, à mon avis, and selon moi, keeping the indicative throughout.
  2. Agree and disagree with graded nuance, from full agreement to partial agreement to firm disagreement, rather than a flat "yes" or "no."
  3. Support an opinion with a reason using parce que, car, puisque, and comme, choosing correctly among them by position and function.
  4. Draw a consequence from an opinion or a fact using donc, c'est pour cela que, and c'est pourquoi.
  5. Recycle the narrative connectors and controlled plus-que-parfait from Chapter 2 to support an opinion with a brief supporting story or example.
  6. Maintain consistent tu/vous register while disagreeing respectfully, softening disagreement appropriately for a more formal interlocutor.
  7. Read and listen to an opinion exchange and identify each speaker's position, the reason given, and the consequence drawn.
  8. Write a short opinion paragraph of six to nine sentences that states a position, supports it with a reason, and draws a consequence.

Communication Scenario

Back from the conference, Karim's team must decide whether to propose the same project format for next year, or change it. Sophie has strong opinions about what worked and what did not; Karim is not entirely convinced. Meanwhile, in the Reading Practice, Karim also writes a short, more careful message to Madame Rousseau asking for her professional opinion on the same question. Chapter 2 asked you to tell a story with precision; this chapter asks you to take a position on something, support it with a reason, and follow that reason through to a conclusion — while disagreeing, when you disagree, without being blunt about it.

  1. State your own opinion clearly, using an opinion phrase followed by the indicative.
  2. Respond to someone else's opinion with graded agreement or disagreement, not a flat yes or no.
  3. Support your opinion with at least one reason, choosing the right connector for its position in the sentence.
  4. Draw a clear consequence from your opinion, and, where useful, illustrate it with a brief example recycling last chapter's narrative connectors.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Intonation for opinion, agreement, and disagreement

This chapter develops intonation that signals stance: the same words can sound like a firm conviction, a tentative suggestion, or a diplomatic hedge depending on pitch and emphasis, quite apart from the vocabulary you choose.

A confident opinion typically falls in pitch on the final stressed syllable of the sentence: je pense que c'était une très bonne idée. A softened or partial opinion often keeps the pitch a little higher and slower toward the end, almost inviting a response: je ne suis pas tout à fait d'accord… A firm disagreement usually places clear stress on the negation itself: je ne suis pas d'accord du tout.

Liaison and nasal vowels remain fully active from earlier volumes and are not re-taught here; apply them as before, especially in nous‿avons and tout‿à fait (tou-t-à fait).

Practice the contrast aloud:

  • Je pense que c'était une très bonne idée. (confident, falling pitch)
  • Je ne suis pas tout à fait d'accord… (tentative, rising toward the end)
  • Je ne suis pas d'accord du tout. (firm, stressed negation)

Purpose: activate opinion, agreement/disagreement, and cause/consequence structures, all with the indicative. 3.1 Stating an opinion: je pense que, je trouve que, à mon avis

Building Block — Opening an opinion
[Je pense / je trouve / je crois] + que + [clause, indicative]  |  [À mon avis / selon moi], + [clause, indicative]
All of these opinion openers are followed by the indicative in this chapter: the tense you already control (présent, passé composé, imparfait, futur simple, or conditionnel) continues to work exactly as before after que. À mon avis and selon moi do not take que at all — they simply frame the sentence that follows. Nothing about the verb changes when you introduce an opinion; only the opening phrase is new.
Je pense que le format de cette année a très bien fonctionné. À mon avis, on devrait garder la même structure l'année prochaine.
I think this year's format worked really well. In my opinion, we should keep the same structure next year.

3.2 Agreeing and disagreeing with nuance

Language Focus

You already control oui and non. This chapter develops a more independent skill: responding to an opinion along a scale, rather than with a flat yes or no, and adjusting that scale to your register.

  • Je suis tout à fait d'accord avec toi. I completely agree with you. (full agreement, informal)
  • Je suis assez d'accord, mais je pense qu'on pourrait améliorer certaines choses. I mostly agree, but I think we could improve a few things. (qualified agreement)
  • Je ne suis pas tout à fait d'accord avec vous, Madame Rousseau. I don't entirely agree with you, Madame Rousseau. (softened disagreement, formal)
  • Je ne suis pas d'accord du tout : je pense le contraire. I don't agree at all: I think the opposite. (firm disagreement)

Notice that disagreement addressed to Madame Rousseau is softened (pas tout à fait) rather than blunt, even though the underlying opinion is a genuine disagreement. This is the same register-sensitivity you practiced in Chapter 1, now applied to holding a different opinion from someone you address with vous.

3.3 Giving a reason: parce que, car, puisque, comme

Language Note — Four ways to introduce a reason

All four connectors introduce a reason, but they are not interchangeable. Parce que is the ordinary default and is the only one of the four that can stand alone as a full answer to "pourquoi ?" Car links two complete clauses and leans slightly more formal or written; it is not normally used to answer a question by itself. Puisque presents the reason as something the listener already knows or should accept as given, often carrying a slightly more insistent tone. Comme places the reason at the very beginning of the sentence, before the main clause.

  • Pourquoi tu penses ça ? — Parce que le format a permis à tout le monde de participer. Why do you think that? — Because the format let everyone participate.
  • Le projet a bien marché, car l'équipe s'était bien préparée. The project went well, for the team had prepared well. (more formal register; recycles the plus-que-parfait)
  • Puisque tout le monde était content, je pense qu'on devrait garder le même format. Since everyone was happy (as you already know), I think we should keep the same format.
  • Comme le public a posé beaucoup de questions, la session a duré plus longtemps que prévu. Since the audience asked a lot of questions, the session ran longer than planned.

3.4 Drawing a consequence: donc, c'est pour cela que, c'est pourquoi

Review and Expansion — From reason to conclusion

Once an opinion has a reason, a precise argument often draws an explicit consequence from it. Donc is the everyday default and can appear mid-sentence or, in speech, at the start of a new sentence. C'est pour cela que and c'est pourquoi are near-synonyms that open a new sentence and lean slightly more careful in tone, useful for the more formal version of an opinion.

  • Le format a très bien fonctionné, donc je pense qu'on devrait le garder. The format worked really well, so I think we should keep it.
  • Tout le monde a pu participer activement. C'est pour cela que je recommande de garder la même structure l'année prochaine. Everyone was able to participate actively. That's why I recommend keeping the same structure next year.

A complete opinion in this chapter therefore has three parts you can now assemble deliberately: a position (je pense que…), a reason (parce que…, car…, puisque…, or comme…), and a consequence (donc… or c'est pourquoi…). You may also illustrate the reason with a brief supporting example using last chapter's sequencing connectors and controlled plus-que-parfait, as modeled below.

The subjonctif is not active anywhere in this chapter. Continue expressing opinion, judgment, and conviction using the indicative structures above rather than the subjunctive forms you may already have encountered elsewhere. Formulaic subjunctive exposure begins in Chapter 6; controlled introduction follows in Chapter 8, with developing production in Chapter 9.

Model Dialogue: Je ne suis pas tout à fait d'accord

Context: A few days after the conference, Karim and Sophie discuss whether the team should propose the same project format next year. Register: informal, close friends; tu throughout.

Sophie : Alors, tu penses qu'on devrait garder le même format l'année prochaine ? So, do you think we should keep the same format next year?
Karim : À mon avis, oui. Je trouve que le format a très bien fonctionné, parce que tout le monde a pu participer activement. In my opinion, yes. I think the format worked really well, because everyone was able to participate actively.
Sophie : Je ne suis pas tout à fait d'accord. Comme la session a duré plus longtemps que prévu, plusieurs personnes sont parties avant la fin. I don't entirely agree. Since the session ran longer than planned, several people left before the end.
Karim : C'est vrai, mais c'était parce que le public avait posé beaucoup de questions — donc, à mon avis, c'est plutôt un bon signe. That's true, but it was because the audience had asked a lot of questions — so, in my opinion, that's actually a good sign.
Sophie : Tu as peut-être raison. Puisque tu le présentes comme ça, je suis plutôt d'accord. Maybe you're right. Since you're putting it that way, I'm more inclined to agree.
Karim : Alors, on garde le même format, et on ajuste juste la durée des questions ? So, we keep the same format, and just adjust how long we spend on questions?
Sophie : Exactement. C'est pour cela qu'il faut en parler à l'équipe cette semaine. Exactly. That's why we need to talk to the team about it this week.

This dialogue models à mon avis / je trouve que, graded agreement and disagreement (je ne suis pas tout à fait d'accord, je suis plutôt d'accord), three reason connectors (parce que, comme, puisque), and consequence connectors (donc, c'est pour cela que), alongside a recycled controlled plus-que-parfait (le public avait posé beaucoup de questions).

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Disagreeing without confrontation

Framing disagreement carefully is a matter of relationship and register, not a fixed national or professional custom. Opening with a softening phrase (je ne suis pas tout à fait d'accord) before stating a contrary view, or acknowledging the other person's point (c'est vrai, mais…) before disagreeing, signals that the disagreement is about the idea, not the person. The closer the relationship and the more informal the register, the more directly you can disagree; the more formal the register or the less familiar the interlocutor, the more useful softening becomes — exactly the pattern you saw between Karim's exchange with Sophie and his message to Madame Rousseau.

Regional Variation — Opinion formulas

Je pense que, je trouve que, à mon avis, and the reason/consequence connectors in this chapter are used and understood throughout the Francophone world. In spoken French, moi, je pense que… can emphasize or contrast the speaker's viewpoint with a doubled subject pronoun; it is optional, used across the Francophone world, and should not be overused as a habitual verbal tic.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Reaching for the subjonctif after je pense que

Error: Writing *je pense que ce soit une bonne idée, applying a subjunctive form some learners encounter elsewhere.

Correction: In this chapter, je pense que, je trouve que, and je crois que are followed by the indicative: je pense que c'est une bonne idée. The subjonctif is not active anywhere in Volume III until formulaic exposure begins in Chapter 6, and even then it stays bounded until controlled introduction in Chapter 8 and developing production in Chapter 9. Continue using the indicative after every opinion opener taught in this chapter.

Common Mistake — Using car to answer a direct "why" question

Error: Responding to pourquoi ? with *Car le format a bien marché. as a stand-alone answer.

Correction: Use parce que to answer a direct question: Parce que le format a bien marché. Car links two complete clauses within a single sentence and is not normally used as a stand-alone answer.

Common Mistake — Disagreeing too bluntly with a formal interlocutor

Error: Writing to Madame Rousseau *Non, vous avez tort., which is grammatically correct but unnecessarily blunt for the relationship and register.

Correction: Soften disagreement with a more formal interlocutor: je ne suis pas tout à fait d'accord avec vous, or acknowledge the point first: c'est un point de vue intéressant, mais…. Firmness of opinion and bluntness of delivery are separate choices, exactly as warmth of tone and choice of tu/vous were separate choices in Chapter 1.

  4. Qu'en pensez-vous ? formally invites Madame Rousseau's professional opinion and signals genuine openness to her judgment; tu es d'accord ? would sound more like a casual check-in among peers than a request for expert input.

Modèle — Paragraphe d'opinion

Je pense que le télétravail est une très bonne option pour notre équipe, d'abord parce que chacun peut organiser sa journée plus librement. Ensuite, comme nous avions déjà testé le télétravail l'année dernière, nous savons que la productivité n'a pas baissé. Par exemple, plusieurs collègues ont aussi constaté qu'ils perdaient moins de temps en déplacement. Certains collègues ne sont pas tout à fait d'accord, car ils préfèrent le contact direct avec l'équipe. C'est pourquoi je recommande un format mixte : deux jours au bureau, et le reste à la maison. Donc, à mon avis, c'est la meilleure solution pour tout le monde.

I think remote work is a very good option for our team, first because everyone can organize their day more freely. Then, since we had already tested remote work last year, we know productivity didn't drop. For example, several colleagues also noticed that they lost less time traveling. Some colleagues don't entirely agree, because they prefer direct contact with the team. That's why I recommend a mixed format: two days at the office, and the rest at home. So, in my opinion, that's the best solution for everyone.

This six-sentence model includes an opinion opener integrated with its first reason, a recycled plus-que-parfait clause with a supporting example, a second reason connector used correctly for its register, an acknowledged counter-opinion, and two consequence connectors. No subjonctif form appears anywhere. Other responses are acceptable when they meet all task requirements and remain within the grammar and vocabulary activated through Chapter 3.

Ce que vous savez faire maintenant — What you can do now

You can state an opinion clearly, respond with graded agreement or disagreement, support your opinion with a reason chosen for its function and register, and draw an explicit consequence from that reason.

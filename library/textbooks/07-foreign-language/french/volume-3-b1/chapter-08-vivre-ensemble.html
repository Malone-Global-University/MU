Vivre ensemble

Community and Social Life · Speak and Write French: Independent Communication and Advanced Expression

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Use core community and social-life vocabulary: le quartier, un·e voisin·e, une association, le bénévolat, s’impliquer, partager, and related terms.
  2. Form the subjonctif présent for any verb whose subjonctif stem can be derived transparently from an already-active present-tense stem (including verbs like sentir, accueillir, devenir, connaître, and recevoir), plus five high-frequency irregular paradigms that must be memorized directly (être, avoir, faire, pouvoir, aller), understanding its formation for the first time.
  3. Use the subjonctif productively, but only after a bounded, authorized set of triggers of necessity (il faut que, il est important que, il vaut mieux que) and emotion or reaction (c’est bien que, c’est dommage que, ça fait plaisir que).
  4. Make a social recommendation using je suggère que or je propose que plus the subjonctif, and know when to prefer an infinitive instead (same subject).
  5. Distinguish, for a small closed list of common triggers, which take the subjonctif and which take the indicative, without yet generalizing to every possible expression.
  6. Maintain appropriate tu/vous register while discussing neighborhood or community life in both an informal and a semi-formal setting.
  7. Read and listen to short community-life texts and identify what is presented as necessary, desirable, or regrettable.
  8. Write a short set of six to nine sentences making social recommendations for a community context, using at least two different authorized subjonctif triggers correctly.

Communication Scenario

Encouraged by the newsletter and website coverage of their conference, Sophie and Karim have been invited to a neighborhood association meeting (une réunion de quartier) to talk about getting more residents involved in local events. The association's president, Madame Dubois, wants concrete recommendations, not vague enthusiasm. Chapters 6 and 7 asked you to talk about what you read and heard; Chapter 8 asks you to say, for the first time with full explanation, what you think is necessary, important, or a shame — the exact communicative territory where French reaches for a verb form you have only glimpsed so far: the subjonctif.

  1. Talk about neighborhood and community life using vocabulary of association, participation, and shared space.
  2. Say what is necessary or important, using il faut que and il est important que productively, with full understanding of the subjonctif form.
  3. React with pleasure or regret to a community situation, using c’est bien que or c’est dommage que.
  4. Make a concrete recommendation with je suggère que or je propose que.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — The subjonctif's silent endings

Most subjonctif présent endings are spelled but not fully distinct in speech from the présent indicative for regular verbs in the je, tu, il/elle, and ils/elles forms: que je parle and je parle sound identical. This is normal and does not signal an error; French listeners rely on the trigger (il faut que, c’est bien que) rather than the verb ending to recognize the subjonctif in most regular forms.

Where the subjonctif does sound different, listen carefully: the nous and vous forms keep the familiar -ions/-iez endings you already recognize from the imparfait and the conditionnel, and several irregular stems (que je sois, que j’aie, que je fasse, que je puisse, que j’aille) sound clearly distinct from any indicative form you know.

  • Il faut que nous partagions cet espace. (the -ions ending is audible and distinct)
  • Il faut que tu sois plus impliqué. (sois is clearly not an indicative form of être)

Liaison, enchaînement, and rhythm-group pacing continue exactly as in earlier chapters; for example, il faut qu’on‿implique links normally at the vowel boundary. No new sound-spelling feature beyond the subjonctif endings themselves is introduced.

Grammar: Vivre ensemble

Purpose: give the first full, explained introduction to the subjonctif présent, then activate it productively but only after a bounded, authorized list of necessity and emotion triggers, plus two social-recommendation verbs. The passive voice remains receptive only, exactly as in Chapters 6 and 7. This chapter does not attempt a complete inventory of every subjonctif trigger in French; it establishes the form and a controlled first set of uses, which Chapter 9 will develop further and Chapter 18 will expand into nuance, doubt, and certainty.

8.1 Forming the subjonctif présent

Building Block — From the ils-present stem to the subjonctif
[ils/elles present stem] + -e, -es, -e, -ions, -iez, -ent
For most verbs, drop the -ent from the ils/elles form of the présent indicative to find the subjonctif stem, then add the endings above. This works not only for fully regular verbs but for any verb, including many traditionally labeled “irregular,” whose subjonctif stem can be derived transparently this way — sentir → ils sentent → que je sente, accueillir → ils accueillent → qu’il accueille, devenir → ils deviennent → que tu deviennes, connaître → ils connaissent → qu’ils connaissent, recevoir → ils reçoivent → qu’elle reçoive. The nous and vous forms of many verbs instead reuse the nous/vous imparfait stem, which you already control. A small set of high-frequency verbs have subjonctif stems that cannot be derived this way and must be memorized directly; five such paradigms are authorized for productive use in this chapter.
parler → ils parlent → que je parle, que tu parles, qu’il/elle parle, que nous parlions, que vous parliez, qu’ils/elles parlent. finir → ils finissent → que je finisse. partager → ils partagent → que nous partagions (identical to the imparfait nous-form here).
to speak → they speak → that I speak, that you speak, that he/she speaks, that we speak, that you speak, that they speak. to finish → they finish → that I finish. to share → they share → that we share.

Verbs whose subjonctif stem is irregular and not derivable this way (savoir, vouloir, and others) are outside this chapter's productive scope and are developed later in the volume.

8.2 Necessity: il faut que and other necessity triggers

Grammar Focus — Saying what is necessary

You met il faut que ce soit clair and il faut qu’on en parle as two fixed, unanalyzed phrases in Chapter 6. This chapter lifts that restriction: il faut que is now a productive pattern, usable with any authorized verb and any grammatical subject in the subordinate clause.

  • Il faut que nous partagions les responsabilités. We need to share the responsibilities. il faut que + subjonctif (necessity)
  • Il est important que les nouveaux habitants se sentent bienvenus. It's important that new residents feel welcome. il est important que + subjonctif
  • Il vaut mieux que l’association organise la réunion le week-end. It's better for the association to organize the meeting on the weekend. il vaut mieux que + subjonctif

Three necessity triggers are authorized productively in this chapter: il faut que, il est important que, and il vaut mieux que. When there is no specific subject — when the recommendation applies in general rather than to a named person or group — French usually prefers the infinitive instead: il faut partager les responsabilités (general) versus il faut que nous partagions les responsabilités (specifically us).

8.3 Emotion and reaction: c’est bien que, c’est dommage que, ça fait plaisir que

Grammar Focus — Reacting to a situation

French also uses the subjonctif after expressions of emotional reaction — not because the fact is uncertain, but because the speaker is expressing a feeling about it rather than simply stating it. Three reaction triggers are authorized productively in this chapter.

  • C’est bien que l’association accueille de nouveaux bénévoles. It's good that the association is welcoming new volunteers. c'est bien que + subjonctif (positive reaction)
  • C’est dommage que si peu de voisins puissent venir à la réunion. It's a shame that so few neighbors can come to the meeting. c'est dommage que + subjonctif (regret); puissent from pouvoir
  • Ça fait plaisir que vous soyez tous impliqués. It's nice that you're all involved. ça fait plaisir que + subjonctif (positive reaction)

The key distinction from Chapter 6's selon/d’après sourcing pattern remains important: sourcing information keeps the indicative (selon l’article, la conférence a été un succès), while reacting emotionally to a fact triggers the subjonctif (c’est bien que la conférence ait été un succès, receptive only at this level since it uses a compound subjonctif form not taught here). This chapter's practice and tasks stay with the subjonctif présent only.

8.4 Social recommendations: suggérer que and proposer que

Grammar Focus — Proposing an idea to a group

Two verbs of recommendation are authorized productively in this chapter, useful for the association-meeting context: suggérer que and proposer que, both followed by the subjonctif when the subject of the recommendation differs from the speaker.

  • Je suggère que nous organisions une fête de quartier ce printemps. I suggest that we organize a neighborhood festival this spring.
  • Karim propose que l’association crée une liste de bénévoles. Karim proposes that the association create a volunteer list.

When you are simply proposing your own action — when the subject of propose and the subject of the recommended action are the same person — prefer proposer de plus an infinitive: je propose de m’impliquer davantage (I propose to get more involved myself). Avoid the heavier je propose que je… pattern in this chapter, even though que plus the subjonctif is not categorically wrong with a matching subject; the infinitive is simply the more natural and more commonly preferred choice here.

Reminder — Still receptive and still bounded

The passive voice remains receptive only, exactly as in Chapters 6 and 7: recognize it, but continue to prefer the active constructions you already control in your own speech and writing. The subjonctif itself is now explained and productive, but only after the eight authorized triggers introduced in this chapter (il faut que, il est important que, il vaut mieux que, c’est bien que, c’est dommage que, ça fait plaisir que, je suggère que, je propose que) and only with verbs whose subjonctif stem is transparently derived from an already-active present-tense stem, plus the five memorized irregular paradigms from section 8.1. Other subjonctif triggers you may encounter in reading — such as expressions of doubt or wish — are outside this chapter's productive scope and are developed later in the volume.

Model Dialogue: Une réunion de quartier

Context: Sophie, Karim, and Madame Dubois (president of the neighborhood association) discuss how to get more residents involved. Register: semi-formal; vous with Madame Dubois, tu between Sophie and Karim.

Mme Dubois : Merci d’être venus. Il faut que nous trouvions des idées pour impliquer davantage les habitants du quartier. Thank you for coming. We need to find ideas to get neighborhood residents more involved.
Sophie : Je suggère que nous organisions une fête de quartier. Ça fait plaisir que les gens se rencontrent en dehors des réunions officielles. I suggest we organize a neighborhood festival. It's nice when people meet outside of official meetings.
Mme Dubois : Bonne idée. Il est important que l’association accueille bien les nouveaux habitants dès leur arrivée. Good idea. It's important that the association welcomes new residents well as soon as they arrive.
Karim : Je propose que nous créions une liste de bénévoles, avec les compétences de chacun. Comme ça, il faut seulement qu’on sache qui contacter. I propose we create a volunteer list, with everyone's skills. That way, we just need to know who to contact.
Mme Dubois : C’est dommage que si peu de gens puissent s’impliquer régulièrement. Mais il vaut mieux que nous commencions petit, avec quelques volontaires motivés. It's a shame that so few people can get involved regularly. But it's better for us to start small, with a few motivated volunteers.
Sophie : D’accord. Karim et moi, nous pouvons nous impliquer davantage — il faut juste que Monsieur Lefèvre soit d’accord pour nous libérer un peu de temps. Agreed. Karim and I can get more involved — we just need Monsieur Lefèvre to agree to free up some of our time.
Mme Dubois : Parfait. C’est bien que vous soyez tous les deux aussi motivés. Perfect. It's good that you're both so motivated.

This dialogue models all three necessity triggers (il faut que, il est important que, il vaut mieux que), all three emotion triggers (c’est bien que, c’est dommage que, ça fait plaisir que), and both recommendation verbs (je suggère que, je propose que). Among the five memorized irregular paradigms, it uses two: pouvoir (puissent) and être (soit, soyez). Trouvions is a transparent, present-derived regular form. Sache comes from savoir, which is outside this chapter's authorized set; it is modeled here for recognition only and is not part of the productive system taught in section 8.1.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Neighborhood associations across the Francophone world

Neighborhood and community associations (associations de quartier) play a significant civic role in many French-speaking communities, organizing everything from block parties to advocacy on local issues. The vocabulary of solidarité and vivre-ensemble appears frequently in French institutional and civic language, though the specific form these associations take — and how formally they operate — varies by country, city, and even individual neighborhood.

Regional Variation — Community vocabulary

Le quartier, un·e voisin·e, and le bénévolat are used and understood throughout the Francophone world. Some regions favor additional or alternate terms for specific kinds of community organizing (for instance, un comité de citoyens in some Québécois contexts); the vocabulary in this chapter remains the safer, broadly recognized default.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Using the subjonctif after a sourcing expression

Error: Writing *Selon l’article, la conférence ait été un succès, applying the subjonctif to a sourcing pattern from Chapter 6.

Correction: Selon and d’après report information and keep the indicative: selon l’article, la conférence a été un succès. The subjonctif appears only after this chapter's authorized necessity, emotion, and recommendation triggers.

Common Mistake — Using the heavier que + subjonctif pattern when proposing your own action

Avoid in this chapter: Writing Je propose que je m’implique davantage when the speaker is recommending something for themselves. This is not categorically ungrammatical, but it is heavier and less natural than the preferred alternative.

Correction: When you are proposing your own action, prefer proposer de plus the infinitive: je propose de m’impliquer davantage.

Common Mistake — Extending the subjonctif beyond this chapter's authorized triggers

Out of scope for this chapter: Je doute qu’il vienne. This is grammatical French, but douter que is a trigger of doubt, not one of the eight necessity, emotion, or recommendation triggers authorized in Chapter 8.

Correction: Stay within this chapter's eight authorized triggers for productive use. Expressions of doubt, wish, and further nuance are developed later in the volume, particularly in Chapter 18.

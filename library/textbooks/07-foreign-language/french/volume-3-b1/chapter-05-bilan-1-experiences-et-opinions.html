Bilan 1 : Expériences et opinions

Unit I Integration · Speak and Write French: Independent Communication and Advanced Expression

Chapter Objectives

This Bilan does not introduce new grammar. By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Sustain a single extended conversation that moves naturally between reconnecting, narrating a past experience with precision, stating and defending an opinion, and comparing that experience to another one — drawing on Chapters 1 through 4 at once.
  2. Sequence a narrative using d'abord, ensuite/puis, après, and enfin, and mark at least one event that had already happened before the main story using a controlled plus-que-parfait clause.
  3. State an opinion with je pense que/à mon avis, respond to another opinion with graded agreement or disagreement, and connect the opinion to a reason (parce que, car, puisque, comme) and a consequence (donc, c'est pourquoi).
  4. Compare two experiences using comparatives and at least one superlative, including the irregular forms meilleur(e)/mieux, and avoid repeating a noun already named by recycling an object, y, or en pronoun.
  5. Maintain a single, consistent tu/vous register across a longer, multi-part exchange, and adjust that register appropriately when the audience changes from an informal to a more formal interlocutor.
  6. Read and listen to an extended, multi-function passage and identify its narrative sequence, its opinion and supporting reason, and its comparison and conclusion.
  7. Write a connected account of eight to twelve sentences that narrates an experience with precision, states and supports an opinion about it, and compares it to a related experience, reaching a clear conclusion.

Communication Scenario

The team meeting that Sophie mentioned at the end of Chapter 3 has now happened, and Karim's manager, Monsieur Lefèvre, wants a fuller picture before the team commits to next year's format. Sophie and Karim first talk it through informally between themselves, the way they have throughout the unit — then Karim has to say much the same thing to Monsieur Lefèvre, in a register closer to his earlier messages to Madame Rousseau. In both conversations, Karim has to do everything at once: reconnect briefly, tell the story of what happened with precision, say what he thinks about it and why, and compare it to the spring conference before reaching a conclusion. This chapter does not teach anything new. It asks you to bring reconnection, precise narration, opinion, and comparison together in a single, longer, more demanding exchange, exactly as a real conversation would require.

  1. Open the exchange appropriately for the relationship and register, recycling Chapter 1's reconnection language.
  2. Narrate the key events in order, marking at least one thing that had already happened beforehand.
  3. State your opinion, support it with a reason, and draw a consequence from it.
  4. Compare the experience to another one, using at least one comparative and one superlative, and reach an explicit conclusion.
  5. Keep register consistent throughout each version of the exchange, and adjust it appropriately between the informal and formal versions.

Pronunciation Review

Pronunciation Review — Combining every focus from Unit I

No new sound-spelling feature is introduced in this Bilan. Instead, this chapter asks you to sustain, in a single longer performance, everything Unit I has developed:

  • Liaison and enchaînement across compound-tense subject-auxiliary groups, especially in plus-que-parfait clauses: nous‿avions, ils‿étaient (Chapter 2).
  • Pauses before sequencing connectors that open a new narrative step: D'abord…, Enfin… (Chapter 2).
  • Stance intonation that distinguishes a confident opinion, a softened disagreement, and a firm disagreement (Chapter 3).
  • Contrastive stress on comparison words — plus, moins, meilleur, mieux — and a clear pause before tandis que/alors que (Chapter 4).

Practice the following aloud, which moves through all four focuses in a single breath group at a time:

  • D'abord, ¦ nous avions déjà préparé nos diapositives.
  • Je pense que ça s'est très bien passé… (confident, falling pitch)
  • Je ne suis pas tout à fait d'accord… (tentative, softened)
  • C'était bien meilleur que la dernière fois, ¦ tandis que celle du printemps était plus faible.

No new pronunciation target is assessed above the status already authorized through Chapter 4. This review sustains, rather than expands, your control of connected speech.

5.1 The four systems working together

Building Block — The shape of a complete account
[Reconnection] → [Récit: d'abord… ensuite… + plus-que-parfait contrôlé] → [Avis: je pense que… parce que… donc…] → [Comparaison: plus/moins/meilleur… que… dans l'ensemble…]
Nothing in this pattern is new. What is new is deliberately building all four movements into a single, connected account rather than practicing each in isolation, exactly as a real conversation moves fluidly between catching up, telling a story, giving an opinion, and comparing it to something else.
Ça fait longtemps ! Alors, la conférence de cette année… D'abord, nous avions déjà bien préparé nos diapositives, donc tout s'est bien passé. Je trouve que ce format a très bien fonctionné, parce que le public a pu poser beaucoup de questions. Comparée à celle du printemps, elle était bien meilleure — dans l'ensemble, c'est de loin la meilleure conférence de l'année.
It's been a while! So, this year's conference… First, we had already prepared our slides well, so everything went well. I think this format worked really well, because the audience was able to ask lots of questions. Compared to the spring one, it was much better — overall, it's by far the best conference of the year.

5.2 Register across a longer exchange

Language Focus

A short exchange makes register easy to sustain; a longer one gives you more chances to slip. Review the pattern established across Chapters 1–4: with Sophie, Karim uses tu, contractions in tone (Coucou !), and can disagree fairly directly. With Madame Rousseau — and now with Monsieur Lefèvre — he uses vous, opens and closes formally, and softens any disagreement (je ne suis pas tout à fait d'accord rather than a flat refusal). The four communicative movements of this chapter (reconnect, narrate, opine, compare) stay the same regardless of register; only the vocabulary, sentence length, and softening strategies shift.

  • Informal: Franchement, c'était bien mieux que la dernière fois ! Honestly, it was much better than last time!
  • Formal: Je pense, dans l'ensemble, que ce format constitue une nette amélioration par rapport à celui du printemps. I think, overall, that this format represents a clear improvement over the spring one.

5.3 Self-check before the performance task

Review and Integration — Four questions to ask before you speak or write

Before attempting this chapter's Practical Performance Task, confirm you can still answer these four questions without hesitation. Each corresponds to a chapter you have already completed.

  • Chapter 2 check: Can you form a plus-que-parfait clause correctly, with the right auxiliary in the imparfait and correct participle agreement?
  • Chapter 3 check: Can you choose parce que versus car versus puisque versus comme correctly for their position and function?
  • Chapter 4 check: Do you reach for meilleur(e) when describing a noun and mieux when describing how an action went, never *plus bon or *plus bien?
  • Cross-chapter check: Do you replace a noun you have already named with a pronoun instead of repeating it?

If any answer is uncertain, revisit the relevant chapter section before continuing; this Bilan assesses these systems but does not re-teach them.

Model Dialogue: On fait le bilan

Context: Sophie asks Karim to summarize everything before he reports to Monsieur Lefèvre. Register: informal, close friends; tu throughout.

Sophie : Alors, avant ta réunion avec Monsieur Lefèvre, raconte-moi tout depuis le début. So, before your meeting with Monsieur Lefèvre, tell me everything from the start.
Karim : D'accord ! D'abord, nous avions déjà bien répété avant la conférence, donc j'étais assez confiant. Ensuite, tout s'est très bien passé : dès que j'ai fini de parler, le public a applaudi. Okay! First, we had already rehearsed well before the conference, so I was fairly confident. Then, everything went really well: as soon as I finished speaking, the audience applauded.
Sophie : Et qu'est-ce que tu en penses, franchement ? And what do you honestly think of it?
Karim : Je pense que le format a très bien fonctionné, parce que tout le monde a pu participer. C'est pour cela que je recommande de le garder l'année prochaine. I think the format worked really well, because everyone was able to participate. That's why I recommend keeping it next year.
Sophie : Comparé à celui du printemps, c'était comment ? Compared to the spring one, how was it?
Karim : Bien meilleur ! Tu te souviens du printemps ? Je m'en souviens encore — ça s'était moins bien passé, tandis que cette fois, tout était mieux organisé. Dans l'ensemble, c'est de loin la meilleure conférence de l'année. Much better! Do you remember the spring one? I still remember it — it went less well, whereas this time, everything was better organized. Overall, it's by far the best conference of the year.
Sophie : Parfait. Tu es prêt pour Monsieur Lefèvre, alors ! Perfect. You're ready for Monsieur Lefèvre, then!
Karim : Je l'espère ! Je vais devoir être un peu plus formel avec lui, remarque. I hope so! I'll need to be a bit more formal with him, mind you.

This dialogue models the full Unit I sequence in a single conversation: reconnection language, sequencing connectors and a controlled plus-que-parfait (Chapter 2), an opinion with reason and consequence (Chapter 3), and a comparison with the irregular comparative meilleur, the pronoun en, the contrast connector tandis que, and a superlative conclusion (Chapter 4). Karim's final line anticipates the register shift required for the formal reading passage below, using the already-active aller + infinitive pattern rather than any unscheduled form; the subjonctif remains inactive everywhere in this chapter.

Speaking Practice

Pratique contrôlée — Les quatre mouvements

With a partner, take turns producing one sentence for each of the four movements in order — reconnection, a narrated event with a plus-que-parfait clause, an opinion with a reason, and a comparison with a conclusion — about a shared or invented experience.

Pratique contrôlée — Changer de registre

With a partner, restate each informal sentence in a more formal register, and vice versa.

Item 1

Franchement, c'était super, cette conférence !

Item 2

Je ne suis pas tout à fait d'accord avec vous, Monsieur Lefèvre.

Tâche communicative — Le bilan complet

With a partner (or a small group), deliver a complete account of a real or invented experience that required preparation and that can be compared to a similar past experience. Your account must include, at minimum:

  • a brief reconnection opening appropriate to the register;
  • at least two sequencing connectors and one controlled plus-que-parfait clause;
  • a stated opinion with at least one reason and one consequence;
  • at least one comparative (including one irregular form) and one superlative, with at least one pronoun avoiding repetition;
  • consistent register throughout, adjusted appropriately if you switch partners between an informal and a formal version.

Do not read from a script; speak from the vocabulary and patterns of Chapters 1–4.

Pratique individuelle — Solo alternative

Record yourself delivering the same complete account on your own, in a connected performance of at least ten sentences, including every element listed above.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — A single story, several audiences

Across this unit, Karim has told essentially the same story — how the conference went, what he thinks of it, how it compares to the spring event — to Sophie, to Madame Rousseau, and now to Monsieur Lefèvre. The underlying facts do not change; what changes is how much is made explicit, how directly an opinion or disagreement is stated, and how the account opens and closes. Learning to retell the same content for a new audience, without changing what actually happened, is itself a communicative skill this unit has built deliberately, one register-sensitive choice at a time.

Regional Variation — Consolidated note

Every connector, opinion formula, and comparative structure recycled in this chapter is used and understood throughout the Francophone world, as noted individually in Chapters 2–4. No new regional form is introduced here; this Bilan does not expand your productive command of regional French beyond what earlier chapters already authorized.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Letting one movement crowd out the others

Error: Producing a long, detailed narration but then adding only a single rushed sentence of opinion and no real comparison, so the account feels unbalanced.

Correction: Plan briefly before speaking or writing so that narration, opinion, and comparison each receive genuine space, even if narration is naturally the longest section. The Practical Performance Task rubric below assesses all four movements separately.

Common Mistake — Drifting register mid-account

Error: Opening a formal account with Bonjour Monsieur Lefèvre and vous, then sliding into franchement, c'était super partway through.

Correction: Decide on register before you begin, and check it at each of the four movements: reconnection, narration, opinion, and comparison. A longer account gives register drift more room to happen, so deliberate checking matters more here than in any single earlier chapter.

Common Mistake — Reaching for new, unscheduled grammar under pressure

Error: Attempting a subjonctif form, a new tense, or an unfamiliar structure because the task feels demanding, rather than using the Chapters 1–4 systems more confidently.

Correction: This chapter rewards fluent, accurate combination of what you already control, not new risk-taking. If you are unsure of a form, fall back on a simpler sentence built entirely from Chapters 1–4 rather than guessing at something unscheduled.

Modèle — Bilan écrit

La semaine dernière, j'ai participé à un atelier professionnel important. D'abord, j'avais déjà lu tous les documents à l'avance, donc j'étais bien préparé. Ensuite, l'atelier a commencé, et dès que l'animateur a posé la première question, j'ai pu répondre avec confiance. Je pense que cet atelier était vraiment utile, parce que chaque participant a pu s'exprimer. C'est pourquoi je recommande de le refaire l'année prochaine. Comparé à l'atelier de l'année dernière, celui-ci était bien meilleur : le groupe était plus petit, tandis que l'ancien groupe était beaucoup trop grand. Je m'en souviens encore, d'ailleurs — ça s'était nettement moins bien passé. Dans l'ensemble, c'est de loin le meilleur atelier auquel j'ai participé jusqu'ici.

Last week, I took part in an important professional workshop. First, I had already read all the documents in advance, so I was well prepared. Then, the workshop began, and as soon as the facilitator asked the first question, I was able to answer confidently. I think this workshop was really useful, because every participant was able to speak. That's why I recommend doing it again next year. Compared to last year's workshop, this one was much better: the group was smaller, whereas the old group was far too big. I still remember it, actually — it had gone noticeably less well. Overall, it's by far the best workshop I've taken part in so far.

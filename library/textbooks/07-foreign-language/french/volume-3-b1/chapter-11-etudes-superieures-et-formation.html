Études supérieures et formation

Education and Training · Speak and Write French: Independent Communication and Advanced Expression

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Talk about academic and training plans using vocabulary already active from the personal, practical, and work domains: les études, un objectif, une formation, un diplôme, poursuivre, se préparer (à), and related terms.
  2. State a purpose or goal using pour que plus the subjonctif when the subject changes, and pour plus the infinitive when the subject stays the same — the eleventh authorized subjonctif trigger, using only verbs with a transparent present-derived stem or the five memorized irregular paradigms from Chapter 8.
  3. Continue productive use of all ten Chapter 8–9 subjonctif triggers alongside pour que.
  4. Consolidate the already-active article-omission system by applying it to professions and roles directly after devenir and être.
  5. Extend controlled production of ni…ni to a third closely modeled pattern with two infinitives, without generalizing further.
  6. Recycle the futur simple and conditionnel présent to describe academic and training plans and to phrase a plan more tentatively.
  7. Maintain a consistent formal or semi-formal register while discussing goals with an advisor, a colleague, or a group.
  8. Read and listen to short education/training texts and identify the goal, the purpose behind a decision, and the plan for reaching it.
  9. Write a short formal summary of six to nine sentences describing an academic or training goal, the purpose behind at least one choice, and the steps planned to reach it.

Communication Scenario

With the neighborhood-council debate behind them, Sophie has decided to pursue a part-time training program in project management, partly inspired by organizing the festival and partly to strengthen her position at work. Karim, who finished a similar program two years ago, meets her at an information session hosted by the training center. Unité II asked you to source, react, and hypothesize about public issues; Unité III turns that same toolkit toward personal plans — and adds a new way of explaining not just what you want, but why: the purpose clause.

  1. Describe an academic or training goal using vocabulary already active from earlier volumes.
  2. Explain the purpose behind a decision using pour que plus the subjonctif or pour plus the infinitive, choosing correctly based on the subject.
  3. Describe a formal role or outcome without an article, following the pattern devenir/être + profession.
  4. Summarize a plan formally, combining goals, purpose, and a realistic timeline.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Pour que versus parce que

Pour que and parce que are easy to confuse when spoken quickly, since both begin with a similar rhythm before que, but they are not interchangeable: pour que introduces a purpose (and takes the subjonctif), while parce que introduces a cause (and keeps the indicatif). Listen for the vowel: pour is a closed, rounded [u] sound, while parce opens with a clear [a].

  • Elle suit cette formation pour que son dossier soit plus solide. (purpose, subjonctif)
  • Elle suit cette formation parce que son dossier n’est pas encore assez solide. (cause, indicatif — receptive contrast only, since parce que was recycled from Volume II)

Liaison, enchaînement, and rhythm-group pacing continue exactly as in earlier chapters; for example, pour‿acquérir links normally at the vowel boundary. No new sound-spelling feature beyond this contrast is introduced.

Grammar: Études supérieures et formation

Purpose: add the purpose clause (pour que + subjonctif versus pour + infinitif) as an eleventh authorized subjonctif trigger, using only verbs and irregular paradigms already authorized since Chapter 8; consolidate the already-active article-omission system by applying it to professions and roles named after être or devenir; and extend ni…ni to one additional closely modeled pattern. No new irregular subjonctif paradigm is introduced. The passive voice remains receptive only, exactly as in Chapters 6–10.

11.1 Purpose: pour que + subjonctif and pour + infinitif

Building Block — Same subject or different subject?
Same subject: pour + [infinitif]  |  Different subject: pour que + [subjonctif]
The choice depends on the grammatical subject of the two verbs, not on who benefits. When the subject of the main verb and the subject of the purpose verb are the same person or thing, French uses pour plus the infinitive — no subjonctif is involved. When the two verbs have different grammatical subjects, French uses pour que plus the subjonctif, formed exactly as you learned in Chapter 8: from the transparent present-derived stem, or from one of the five memorized irregular paradigms (être, avoir, faire, pouvoir, aller). The subject of a pour que clause need not be a person: pour que son dossier soit plus solide has son dossier as its subject.
Sophie suit cette formation pour acquérir de nouvelles compétences. (same grammatical subject: Sophie suit, Sophie acquiert) Sophie suit cette formation pour que son employeur soit satisfait. (different grammatical subjects: Sophie suit; son employeur soit)
Sophie is taking this training program to acquire new skills. Sophie is taking this training program so that her employer is satisfied.
Grammar Focus — Using pour que in context

Pour que is now productive as this volume's eleventh authorized subjonctif trigger, joining the eight from Chapter 8 and the two from Chapter 9. Its formation rules are identical: transparent present-derived stems, or the five memorized irregular paradigms only.

  • Karim aide Sophie pour qu’elle soit prête pour l’entretien. Karim is helping Sophie so that she is ready for the interview. pour que + subjonctif (soit, from être)
  • Le centre propose des horaires flexibles pour que les employés puissent suivre la formation. The center offers flexible schedules so that employees can take the training program. pour que + subjonctif (puissent, from pouvoir)

Compare this with pour plus the infinitive when the subject does not change: Sophie se prépare pour réussir l’entretien (Sophie is preparing herself, and Sophie is the one who will succeed). Check the subject of both verbs before choosing the pattern.

11.2 Consolidating article omission: devenir/être + profession

Grammar Focus — Naming a role without an article

You have used c’est un… / c’est une… since earlier volumes to identify or classify someone's profession, and this pattern keeps its article: c’est un formateur. This chapter consolidates a related but distinct pattern you have also met before: after être or devenir, a profession used simply as an attribute of the subject normally appears without an article. This is not a matter of formality; it is a property of the construction itself.

  • Sophie espère devenir cheffe de projet d’ici deux ans. Sophie hopes to become a project manager within two years. devenir + profession as attribute, no article
  • Karim est formateur depuis deux ans. Karim has been a trainer for two years. être + profession as attribute, no article
  • C’est un formateur. He's a trainer. / That's a trainer. c'est + article (identifying/classifying, a different construction)

Expanding the profession noun does not automatically restore the article, as this chapter's own example shows: cheffe de projet stays articleless even with de projet added, because the phrase still functions as a simple attribute. More complex noun phrases can affect article choice in ways this chapter does not fully explore; this chapter's tasks stay with the simple, articleless profession-as-attribute pattern after être and devenir.

11.3 Extending ni…ni: a third modeled pattern

Controlled Extension — Still limited production

Chapter 9 modeled ni…ni with adjectives and with nouns after avoir. This chapter adds one closely modeled pattern with two infinitives, still through substitution rather than a full generalized rule.

  • Sophie ne veut ni abandonner son travail ni négliger ses études. Sophie wants neither to give up her job nor to neglect her studies. (fixed pattern: ne + verbe + ni + infinitif + ni + infinitif)

Use this sentence as a model for controlled substitution with similar infinitives in a parallel structure. A fuller account of ni…ni’s placement rules with different verb forms is developed later in the volume alongside the rest of the negation system.

Reminder — Eleven triggers, still bounded

Eleven subjonctif triggers are now authorized: the eight from Chapter 8 (il faut que, il est important que, il vaut mieux que, c’est bien que, c’est dommage que, ça fait plaisir que, je suggère que, je propose que), the two from Chapter 9 (il est possible que, il se peut que), and pour que from this chapter — always with a transparent present-derived stem or one of the five memorized irregular paradigms from section 8.1. The passive voice remains receptive only. Ni…ni is limited to the three modeled patterns introduced through this chapter.

Model Dialogue: Une séance d’information

Context: Sophie and Karim attend an information session at the training center, where they speak with a trainer, Madame Aziz, about the part-time project-management program. Register: semi-formal; vous with Madame Aziz, tu between Sophie and Karim.

Mme Aziz : Bienvenue. Le centre propose cette formation pour que les employés puissent continuer à travailler et suivre les cours le soir. Welcome. The center offers this program so that employees can keep working and take evening classes.
Sophie : C’est parfait pour moi. Je suis cette formation pour acquérir de nouvelles compétences, mais aussi pour que mon employeur me fasse confiance sur de plus grands projets. That's perfect for me. I'm taking this program to acquire new skills, but also so that my employer trusts me with bigger projects.
Karim : Je peux confirmer que ça marche. Je suis formateur depuis deux ans maintenant, grâce à un parcours très semblable. I can confirm that it works. I've been a trainer for two years now, thanks to a very similar path.
Mme Aziz : Il faut que vous soyez prête à gérer votre temps. Sophie, vous ne voulez ni abandonner votre travail ni négliger vos études, n’est-ce pas ? You need to be ready to manage your time. Sophie, you don't want to give up your job or neglect your studies, right?
Sophie : Exactement. J’espère devenir cheffe de projet d’ici deux ans, alors je suis prête à m’organiser. Exactly. I hope to become a project manager within two years, so I'm ready to get organized.
Mme Aziz : Il est possible que votre entreprise finance une partie des frais, pour que la formation vous coûte moins cher. It's possible that your company will fund part of the cost, so that the program costs you less.
Karim : Je propose que Sophie en parle d’abord à son employeur, pour que tout soit clair avant l’inscription. I suggest Sophie talk to her employer about it first, so that everything is clear before enrollment.
Sophie : Bonne idée. Si mon employeur accepte de participer, je m’inscrirai cette semaine. Good idea. If my employer agrees to contribute, I'll enroll this week.

This dialogue models both purpose patterns (pour acquérir…, same subject; pour que mon employeur me fasse confiance…, different subject), article omission with professions twice (formateur, cheffe de projet), the third ni…ni pattern, three recycled Chapter 8–9 subjonctif triggers (il faut que, il est possible que, je propose que), and a recycled si + présent/futur hypothesis. Fasse is drawn from the five memorized irregular paradigms; all other subjonctif forms use transparent present-derived stems.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Continuing education and formation professionnelle

La formation professionnelle (professional training, continuing education) is a well-established part of working life in many French-speaking countries, and employer contributions toward employee training costs are common in some contexts. Vocabulary such as un diplôme, une formation, and un parcours appears in everyday professional conversation as often as in academic settings.

Regional Variation — Naming credentials and roles

The names and structures of diplomas and training credentials vary significantly by country and educational system across the Francophone world. This chapter avoids naming specific credentials or institutions and instead teaches the general, broadly transferable vocabulary of goals, purpose, and roles.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Using pour que when the subject does not change

Error: Writing *Sophie suit cette formation pour qu’elle acquière de nouvelles compétences when Sophie herself is both the one training and the one acquiring the skills.

Correction: When the subject of both verbs is the same person, use pour plus the infinitive: Sophie suit cette formation pour acquérir de nouvelles compétences. Use pour que when the purpose clause has a different grammatical subject from the main clause.

Common Mistake — Adding an article to a profession used as an attribute

Error: Writing *Karim est un formateur depuis deux ans when simply stating Karim's profession with être.

Correction: When a profession functions simply as an attribute after être or devenir, French normally omits the article: Karim est formateur depuis deux ans. With c’est, use an article: C’est un formateur. More complex noun phrases can affect article choice; this chapter practices only the simple profession-as-attribute pattern.

Common Mistake — Extending ni…ni beyond the three modeled patterns

Out of scope for this chapter: Building a new ni…ni sentence with a structure not modeled in Chapter 9 or in this chapter's section 11.3.

Correction: Reuse the three modeled patterns only (adjectives, nouns after avoir, and infinitives). A fuller account of ni…ni is developed later in the volume.

Modèle — Un objectif de formation

Je suis actuellement une formation à temps partiel en gestion de projet. Je poursuis cette formation pour acquérir de nouvelles compétences et pour renforcer mon dossier professionnel. Mon employeur finance une partie des frais, pour que je continue à me former sans quitter mon poste. J’espère devenir cheffe de projet d’ici deux ans. I am currently taking a part-time training program in project management. I am pursuing this training to acquire new skills and to strengthen my professional profile. My employer is funding part of the cost, so that I keep training without leaving my position. I hope to become a project manager within two years. I need to manage my time well between work and evening classes. I want neither to give up my current position nor to delay this project, so I organize myself carefully each week.

This six-sentence model includes both purpose patterns, one profession named without an article (cheffe de projet), one recycled Chapter 8 necessity trigger (il faut que), and the third ni…ni pattern with infinitives. No unauthorized trigger and no newly constructed passive sentence appear anywhere.

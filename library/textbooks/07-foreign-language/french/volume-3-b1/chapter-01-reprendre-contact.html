Reprendre contact

Reconnecting and Repositioning · Speak and Write French: Independent Communication and Advanced Expression

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Reconnect with someone after time apart, using extended introductions built on cela fait… que and related time expressions.
  2. Reposition yourself in a conversation by summarizing, in a few connected sentences, what has changed in your life since you last spoke with someone.
  3. Adjust tu and vous as a relationship evolves — including the moment when a formerly formal relationship might shift, or when it should deliberately stay formal.
  4. Use the futur simple and the conditionnel with greater flexibility to describe plans, extend polite invitations, and soften requests.
  5. Recognize formulaic uses of the plus-que-parfait, the passive voice, and reported speech in fixed expressions, without being required to analyze or productively generate the fuller grammar behind them.
  6. Fold cause/consequence connectors and hypothetical si-clauses from Volume II into longer, more independent discourse.
  7. Read and listen to extended reconnection exchanges and identify what has changed for each speaker.
  8. Write an extended personal update, in both an informal and a more careful register, appropriate to reconnecting with two different kinds of contacts.

Communication Scenario

A year has passed since Karim's move and the community-project discussion that closed Volume II. Karim now attends a professional conference in his new city, where, completely by chance, he runs into Madame Rousseau, a former university instructor he has not seen in several years. That evening, he also calls Sophie, whom he has not spoken with in a few months, to catch up properly. Volume III does not start over: it asks you to take everything you built in Volume II — narration, advice, description of change, and mediated summary — and use it for a new communicative task: picking a relationship back up, in whatever register that relationship requires.

  1. Greet someone you have not seen in a long time and signal, in your very first sentences, how much time has passed.
  2. Summarize, briefly and clearly, what has changed in your life since you last spoke.
  3. Decide, and signal through your language, whether the relationship stays formal or may shift toward informal.
  4. Make plans to stay in touch, using the futur simple and the conditionnel appropriately.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Register-sensitive tone in reunion speech

Reconnecting with someone puts your voice to work in a new way: the same words can sound warm or distant depending on pitch, pace, and stress. This chapter's pronunciation focus develops register-sensitive pronunciation — matching your intonation to the relationship, not just your choice of vocabulary or tu/vous.

An informal, delighted reunion often uses a wider pitch range, with exclamations such as Ça alors ! and Comme le monde est petit ! tending to rise on the final syllable, almost like a question of disbelief. A formal, respectful reunion — such as greeting a former instructor — more often keeps pitch level and the pace a little slower, letting each rhythm group land clearly: Quelle bonne surprise de vous revoir. These are performance tendencies you can lean on, not fixed rules that every French speaker follows in every reunion.

The liaison and nasal-vowel systems you have carried since Volume I remain fully active and are not re-taught here; apply them as before, especially in cela fait un an (un-n-an) and nous avons (nou-z-avons).

Practice the contrast aloud:

  • Ça alors, c'est vraiment toi ? (informal, wide pitch, quick rise)
  • Quelle bonne surprise de vous revoir, Madame Rousseau. (formal, level pitch, measured pace)

Grammar: Reprendre contact

Purpose: consolidate B1 systems while developing extended introductions, deliberate register adjustment, and more flexible futur simple/conditionnel use. No subjonctif is introduced. The plus-que-parfait, passive voice, and reported speech remain formulaic and receptive, exactly as authorized by the Volume III Chapter 1 matrix statuses.

1.1 Extended introductions: cela fait… que

Building Block — Marking time since last contact
[Cela / Ça] + fait + [durée] + que + [clause describing the relevant situation]
This pattern states how long a situation has lasted up to now. When the clause after que describes something still true or still happening, it uses the present tense, even though English says "has been": ça fait longtemps que j'habite ici (I have lived here for a long time). When the clause instead describes something that has not happened during that period — a gap, an absence — French uses the passé composé in its negative form, exactly as in ordinary narration: cela fait deux ans que je ne l'ai pas vue (it's been two years since I've seen her). Cela fait is neutral to formal; ça fait is its everyday spoken equivalent. Both recycle the depuis pattern from Volume II rather than replacing it: depuis attaches to a noun or a clause, while cela fait… que foregrounds the duration itself.
Cela fait presque deux ans que je n'ai pas vu Madame Rousseau. Ça fait longtemps qu'on ne s'est pas parlé, Sophie et moi.
It's been almost two years since I've seen Madame Rousseau. It's been a while since Sophie and I have talked.

1.2 Adjusting tu and vous as a relationship evolves

Language Focus

You have controlled the basic tu/vous choice since Volume I. This chapter develops a more independent skill: deciding, in the moment, whether a relationship should remain formal, shift toward informal, or stay exactly where it has always been — and signaling that decision through consistent language rather than leaving it ambiguous.

A few guiding patterns, none of them absolute rules:

  • Un ancien professeur reste souvent "vous," même des années plus tard, sauf si cette personne propose elle-même de se tutoyer. A former instructor often remains "vous," even years later, unless that person offers to switch to "tu" themselves.
  • Un ami perdu de vue reprend généralement le "tu" immédiatement, comme si le temps n'avait jamais passé. A friend you've lost touch with generally picks "tu" back up immediately, as if no time had passed.

If a former instructor or supervisor invites the shift, they will usually say so directly: Vous pouvez me tutoyer, maintenant ! Until that happens, maintaining vous is the safer, more respectful default. Whichever register you choose, keep it consistent for the rest of the exchange — the mid-conversation drift you were warned against in Chapter 19 remains just as important to avoid here.

1.3 Futur simple and conditionnel for plans and polite phrasing

Review and Expansion — Making plans to stay in touch

You have used the futur simple for prediction and the conditionnel for advice and politeness since Volume II. This chapter asks you to combine both more flexibly at the close of a reconnection: proposing a concrete future plan, and softening the proposal so it does not sound like a demand.

  • Nous nous reverrons certainement avant la fin de la conférence. We'll certainly see each other again before the conference ends. (futur simple — a confident plan)
  • Est-ce que vous accepteriez de déjeuner avec moi un de ces jours ? Would you be willing to have lunch with me sometime? (conditionnel — a soft, polite proposal)
  • On pourrait s'appeler plus souvent, non ? We could call each other more often, couldn't we? (conditionnel — informal suggestion)

Notice that the informal suggestion to Sophie and the formal proposal to Madame Rousseau use the same underlying grammar (conditionnel) but different vocabulary, subject pronouns, and tone — the register-shifting skill from Chapter 19 applied to a new communicative task.

1.4 Formulaic recognition: plus-que-parfait, passive voice, and reported speech

Receptive and Formulaic — Fixed expressions you will recognize

As you reconnect with people, you will sometimes hear or read grammar that Volume III has not yet activated for production: the plus-que-parfait, the passive voice, and reported speech with a tense shift. The examples below are receptive/formulaic exemplars: recognize what each one means as a whole, and feel free to reuse one exactly as given if it fits, but do not treat the underlying pattern as something you can freely manipulate to build new sentences yet.

  • Je ne l'avais pas revu depuis des années. I hadn't seen him again in years. (receptive plus-que-parfait; productive use is developed later in the volume)
  • La conférence a été organisée par l'université. The conference was organized by the university. (receptive passive; productive use is developed later in the volume)
  • On m'a dit que vous aviez changé de poste. I was told that you had changed jobs. (receptive reported speech with tense shift; the fuller system is developed later in the volume)

Treat these as recognizable whole phrases for now, the same way you treated il paraît que in Volume II Chapter 19. Do not attempt to generate new sentences on this pattern in your own speech or writing this chapter.

The subjonctif is not active anywhere in this chapter. Continue producing opinion, necessity, and doubt using the indicative and infinitive structures you already control from Volume II; the subjonctif is introduced beginning in Unit II.

Model Dialogue: Comme le monde est petit !

Context: Karim, at a professional conference, unexpectedly runs into Madame Rousseau, a former university instructor he has not seen in about two years. Register: formal, respectful; vous throughout, on both sides.

Karim : Madame Rousseau ? Ça alors, quelle surprise ! Cela fait au moins deux ans que je ne vous ai pas vue. Madame Rousseau? Well, what a surprise! It's been at least two years since I've seen you.
Mme Rousseau : Karim ! Comme le monde est petit. Qu'est-ce que vous devenez ? Karim! What a small world. What have you been up to?
Karim : Beaucoup de choses ont changé, en fait. J'ai déménagé il y a un peu plus d'un an pour un nouveau poste, et je m'y plaîs beaucoup. A lot has changed, actually. I moved a bit over a year ago for a new position, and I'm really enjoying it there.
Mme Rousseau : Ça ne m'étonne pas, vous étiez toujours très motivé. Et cette conférence, qu'est-ce qui vous amène ? That doesn't surprise me, you were always very motivated. And this conference, what brings you here?
Karim : Mon équipe présente un projet demain. Et vous, vous enseignez toujours à l'université ? My team is presenting a project tomorrow. And you, are you still teaching at the university?
Mme Rousseau : Toujours, oui, mais je dirige maintenant un nouveau programme. On m'a dit que plusieurs de mes anciens étudiants faisaient carrière dans ce domaine ; je vois que c'est vrai. Still, yes, but I'm now running a new program. I was told that several of my former students were building careers in this field; I see it's true.
Karim : Est-ce que vous accepteriez de déjeuner avec moi un de ces jours ? J'aimerais beaucoup vous parler de mon travail plus en détail. Would you be willing to have lunch with me sometime? I'd really like to tell you more about my work.
Mme Rousseau : Avec plaisir. Nous nous reverrons donc bientôt. Tenez-moi au courant de vos disponibilités. With pleasure. We'll see each other again soon, then. Let me know when you're available.

This dialogue models cela fait… que, consistent formal register with no drift toward tu, and the conditionnel for a polite proposal. Mme Rousseau's line on m'a dit que plusieurs de mes anciens étudiants faisaient carrière… is receptive only: understand it as input, but do not use this reported-speech pattern productively in your own speech or writing this chapter.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — When does tu replace vous?

Across the Francophone world, the shift from vous to tu is rarely automatic and is often explicitly negotiated rather than assumed. A former instructor, an older relative's friend, or a senior colleague may keep vous indefinitely, even in a warm relationship; when a shift is offered, it is frequently marked directly, with a phrase such as On peut se tutoyer, non ? Waiting for that signal, rather than assuming it, is a normal and respectful communicative habit, not excessive formality.

Regional Variation — Reconnection formulas

Cela fait… que, ça fait… que, and the reconnection vocabulary in this chapter are understood throughout the Francophone world, though everyday informal expressions for "it's been a long time" vary by region and generation (for example, the very informal ça faisait un bail in the Reading Practice is common in France but not universal). Conventions for when to offer the shift from vous to tu also vary somewhat by country, workplace, and generation; the guidance in this chapter represents a safe, broadly recognized default.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Changing the tense of fait itself, and choosing the wrong tense in the clause after que

Error: Saying *cela a fait deux ans que…, shifting fait itself into a past form. A second, more subtle error is applying the present tense inside the que-clause no matter what, producing *cela fait deux ans que je ne vois pas Madame Rousseau when describing a gap that has already lasted two years.

Correction: When describing a duration continuing up to the present, use cela fait…, not cela a fait…. In a past-time frame — describing a duration that continued up to some earlier moment, not up to now — cela faisait… is possible, as in cela faisait si longtemps que je ne vous avais pas vue (from the Reading Practice). Inside the que-clause itself, use the present when the situation is ongoing and still true (cela fait deux ans que j'habite ici), but use the negative passé composé when the clause describes something that has not happened during that period (cela fait deux ans que je ne l'ai pas vue). Choose the tense based on what the clause itself describes, the same way you would choose it in ordinary narration.

Common Mistake — Offering the tu-shift too soon

Error: Switching to tu with a former instructor or senior professional contact simply because the reunion feels warm and informal in tone.

Correction: Warmth of tone and choice of address form are separate systems. Maintain vous until the other person clearly offers otherwise: quelle bonne surprise de vous revoir can be just as warm as an informal greeting while still respecting the relationship.

Common Mistake — Forcing a plus-que-parfait or passive sentence

Error: Attempting to build a new sentence on the plus-que-parfait or passive pattern because a fixed example from section 1.4 sounded sophisticated.

Correction: These forms are recognition-only in this chapter. Express the same idea with passé composé/imparfait narration instead: rather than attempting a new passive sentence, say l'université a organisé la conférence, using the active voice you already control.

Item 1 is informal: ça fait un bail and dis donc are casual, spoken expressions. Item 2 is formal: it uses vous and the more careful phrase qu'est-ce que vous devenez.

Chapter Review — Part D

Successful performance is a six-plus-sentence reunion that includes a time-marking opener, a summary of change, and a closing plan in the futur simple or conditionnel. Solo learners must record the full exchange on their own to meet this requirement.

Listening Practice

  1. Karim and Sophie are speaking; they are old friends.
  2. Ça fait bien trop longtemps qu'on ne s'est pas parlé: it has been far too long.
  3. Karim has a new job, a new apartment, a new neighborhood, and recently ran into his former professor.
  4. They plan to meet for coffee next month, using the conditionnel (on pourrait se retrouver) and the futur simple (je serai en ville).

Reading Practice

  1. The email uses cela faisait si longtemps que je ne vous avais pas vue (formulaic plus-que-parfait, recognition-only); the message uses ça faisait un bail.
  2. Differences include: vous vs. tu; formal opening (Madame Rousseau,) and closing (Cordialement) vs. casual opening (Coucou !) and no formal closing; careful, complete sentences vs. short, casual ones.
  3. The email proposes a lunch date using the conditionnel présent (seriez-vous disponible), a polite, softened question form; the message proposes seeing each other using the présent tense with future-time meaning, common in informal spoken French (on se voit bientôt, promis ?, not on va se voir, which would be the futur proche) — each appropriate to its audience.
  4. Based on context, ça faisait un bail means roughly "it had been ages" — a very informal way of marking a long time apart.

Writing Workshop — Model texts

Modèle — Message informel

Coucou Yasmine ! Ça fait au moins un an qu'on ne s'est pas parlé, non ? De mon côté, j'ai changé de ville et j'ai commencé un nouveau projet qui me plaît beaucoup. Et toi, quoi de neuf ? On devrait vraiment se retrouver bientôt, je serai en ville le mois prochain. On pourrait se voir pour un café ?

Hey Yasmine! It's been at least a year since we talked, right? On my end, I moved to a new city and started a new project that I really like. And you, what's new? We should really meet up soon, I'll be in town next month. Could we get together for coffee?
Modèle — Courriel formel

Bonjour Monsieur Lefèvre,

Cela fait presque deux ans que je n'ai pas eu de vos nouvelles, et j'espère que vous allez bien. De mon côté, j'ai changé de poste et je travaille maintenant sur des projets qui me passionnent beaucoup. Seriez-vous disponible pour un café ou un déjeuner dans les semaines à venir ? Je serais ravi d'échanger avec vous plus longuement.

Cordialement,
Karim

Hello Mr. Lefèvre, – It's been almost two years since I've heard from you, and I hope you are doing well. On my end, I changed positions and I'm now working on projects I'm very passionate about. Would you be available for coffee or lunch in the coming weeks? I would be delighted to talk with you further. – Sincerely, Karim

Both texts mark elapsed time, summarize a personal change, and propose future contact, while shifting register: informal address and casual framing in the message, formal opening/closing and softened conditionnel phrasing in the email. Neither text contains a subjonctif form, and any plus-que-parfait or passive phrasing is limited to formulaic expressions. Other responses are acceptable when they meet all task requirements and remain within the grammar and vocabulary activated through Volume III Chapter 1.

Practical Performance Task

Successful completion requires both components: a spoken reunion meeting every listed element, and a written pair (informal message and formal email) totaling eight to twelve sentences and meeting every required element listed in the rubric above. Partial completion of either component does not meet the task's requirements.

Looking Ahead

Ce que vous savez faire maintenant — What you can do now

You can reconnect with someone after time apart, mark how much time has passed, summarize what has changed in your life, negotiate tu and vous deliberately as a relationship evolves, and propose future contact using the futur simple and the conditionnel.

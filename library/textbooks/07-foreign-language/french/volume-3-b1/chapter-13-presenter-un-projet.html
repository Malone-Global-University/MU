Présenter un projet

Presenting a Project · Speak and Write French: Independent Communication and Advanced Expression

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Talk about a project's aim, stages, and resources using vocabulary already active from the personal and practical/work domains, expanded with presentation-specific items: un projet, une étape, un calendrier, mettre en œuvre, une ressource, and related terms.
  2. Introduce or explain a project impersonally using il s’agit de plus the infinitive or a noun phrase, and il convient de plus the infinitive, without a personal subject.
  3. Sequence the stages of a project clearly using d’abord, ensuite, puis, and enfin, framed by pour commencer and pour conclure.
  4. Continue productive use of all eleven Chapter 8–11 subjonctif triggers and the purpose-clause contrast (pour + infinitif versus pour que + subjonctif) from Chapter 11, applied to explaining a project's goals.
  5. Recycle the formal/informal register contrast already active since earlier volumes, applying it to a structured spoken and written project explanation.
  6. Maintain a single, deliberately chosen register (formal or semi-formal) throughout a sustained presentation, rather than a short exchange.
  7. Read and listen to short project descriptions and identify the goal, the stages, and the impersonal framing used to present them.
  8. Write and deliver a short structured project presentation of six to nine sentences that states a goal, sequences at least three stages, and uses at least one impersonal structure.

Communication Scenario

Sophie's employer has agreed to fund part of her training, and her supervisor, Monsieur Lefèvre, has asked her to present her training plan at the next team meeting so that colleagues understand how her part-time schedule will work. Karim helps her rehearse: Chapter 12 asked Sophie to put a request into writing; Chapter 13 asks her to explain a plan out loud, clearly, stage by stage, and without leaning on je for every sentence — the impersonal structures of this chapter let her frame the project itself as the subject.

  1. Introduce a project impersonally using il s’agit de or il convient de.
  2. Sequence at least three stages of the project using d’abord, ensuite, puis, and enfin.
  3. Explain at least one purpose behind the project using pour + infinitif or pour que + subjonctif.
  4. Maintain a single consistent register throughout the presentation.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Stressing sequence connectors

Sequence connectors such as d’abord, ensuite, puis, and enfin usually open their own short rhythm group at the beginning of a sentence, with a small pause after the connector when speaking at presentation pace. This differs from ordinary conversational rhythm, where such short words often blend into the following phrase.

  • D’abord, // je présente l’objectif du projet. (brief pause after the connector marks a new stage)
  • Puis, // il convient de prévoir un calendrier réaliste.

The elisions in il s’agit (se → s’) and in d’un projet (de → d’) continue patterns already active since earlier chapters; the final written t of agit is not pronounced here. No new sound-spelling feature is introduced; the focus here is discourse-level pacing, not a new segmental contrast.

Grammar: Présenter un projet

Purpose: add two impersonal structures for framing a project without a personal subject (il s’agit de, il convient de), add sequencing connectors for explaining process and stages, and recycle the subjonctif triggers, purpose clauses, and formal register from Chapters 8–12 in a sustained oral and written presentation. No new subjonctif trigger and no new irregular paradigm are introduced. The passive voice remains receptive only, exactly as in Chapters 6–12.

13.1 Impersonal structures: il s’agit de and il convient de

Building Block — Framing without a personal subject
Il s’agit de + [infinitif / nom]  |  Il convient de + [infinitif]
Il s’agit de is a fixed impersonal expression built on the pronominal verb s’agir, used only in the il form; it introduces what a project, a text, or a situation is about, and is followed by an infinitive or a noun phrase. Il convient de is a fixed impersonal expression meaning "it is appropriate to" or "one should." This chapter teaches and assesses only il convient de plus the infinitive, used when no specific subject is named. Standard French also allows il convient que plus the subjonctif when a specific subject is named, but that pattern is not introduced or assessed in this chapter; it would add a twelfth subjonctif trigger beyond the eleven authorized through Chapter 11. These impersonal infinitive structures are new tools for framing a topic without naming a subject; they are not themselves subjonctif triggers.
Il s’agit d’une formation en gestion de projet. Il convient de prévoir un calendrier réaliste avant de commencer.
This is about a training program in project management. It is appropriate to plan a realistic timeline before starting.
Grammar Focus — Using il s’agit de and il convient de in context

Il s’agit de is especially useful for opening a presentation, since it frames the topic before any personal pronoun appears. Il convient de is especially useful for stating a recommendation or a necessary step impersonally, as an alternative to il faut que when the following verb has no specific subject.

  • Il s’agit de présenter les grandes lignes du projet avant d’entrer dans les détails. This is about presenting the broad outline of the project before going into details. il s'agit de + infinitif
  • Il convient de répartir les tâches dès la première étape. It is appropriate to divide up the tasks from the very first stage. il convient de + infinitif

Compare il convient de répartir les tâches (impersonal recommendation, no subject change) with il faut que l’équipe répartisse les tâches (personal necessity, subjonctif, different subject). Both are correct; the choice depends on whether you want to name who must act.

13.2 Sequencing a process: d’abord, ensuite, puis, enfin

Grammar Focus — Marking the stages of a project

These connectors organize a process into a clear sequence for a listener or reader. D’abord opens the sequence; ensuite and puis mark middle stages and are largely interchangeable, though puis is slightly brisker; enfin closes the sequence. Pour commencer and pour conclure are fuller, more formal framing phrases that can open and close a longer presentation around this same sequence.

  • D’abord, il s’agit de définir l’objectif. Ensuite, il convient de fixer un calendrier. Puis, l’équipe met le plan en œuvre. Enfin, nous faisons un bilan intermédiaire. First, the goal must be defined. Next, a timeline should be set. Then, the team implements the plan. Finally, we do a progress check.

These connectors recycle the cause-and-consequence discourse connectors already active since Chapter 3, applied here specifically to process and sequence rather than to argument.

Reminder — Eleven triggers, purpose clauses, and register, all still bounded

All eleven subjonctif triggers from Chapters 8–11 remain productive, with no twelfth trigger added in this chapter. Il s’agit de and il convient de are taught and assessed in this chapter only as impersonal infinitive structures. (Standard French also permits il convient que plus the subjonctif when a specific subject is named, but that pattern is not introduced or assessed here, since it would add an unauthorized twelfth trigger.) The purpose-clause contrast (pour + infinitif versus pour que + subjonctif) and the formal/informal register contrast recycle directly into this chapter's presentation genre. The passive voice remains receptive only.

Model Dialogue: Réunion d’équipe

Context: Sophie presents her training plan at a team meeting led by Monsieur Lefèvre, with Karim present as a colleague who already completed a similar program. Register: formal throughout the presentation itself (vous); the brief exchange with Karim beforehand uses tu.

Karim : Tu es prête ? Commence par « il s’agit de », comme on a répété. Are you ready? Start with "il s'agit de," like we practiced.
Sophie : Mesdames, Messieurs, il s’agit d’une formation en gestion de projet que je suivrai à temps partiel à partir du mois prochain. Ladies and gentlemen, this is about a project-management training program that I will take part-time starting next month.
Sophie : D’abord, il convient de présenter le calendrier : les cours ont lieu le jeudi soir pendant six mois. Ensuite, je continuerai à assurer mes responsabilités habituelles pendant la journée. First, it's appropriate to present the schedule: the classes take place on Thursday evenings for six months. Next, I will continue to handle my usual responsibilities during the day.
M. Lefèvre : Il est possible qu’il y ait un conflit avec certaines réunions du jeudi. Comment comptez-vous gérer cet obstacle ? It's possible there will be a conflict with some Thursday meetings. How do you plan to manage that obstacle?
Sophie : Puis, je propose que nous déplacions ces réunions au mercredi, pour que mon emploi du temps reste cohérent. Enfin, je ferai un bilan intermédiaire chaque mois pour que l’équipe puisse suivre mes progrès. Then, I suggest we move those meetings to Wednesday, so that my schedule stays consistent. Finally, I will do a monthly progress check so that the team can follow my progress.
M. Lefèvre : Très bien. Il convient de partager ce calendrier avec toute l’équipe dans les meilleurs délais. Very good. It's appropriate to share this schedule with the whole team as soon as possible.
Karim : Je le lui transmettrai dès aujourd’hui. I'll send it to the team today.

This dialogue models both impersonal structures (il s’agit de, il convient de), a full sequence of four connectors (d’abord, ensuite, puis, enfin), two recycled Chapter 8–9 subjonctif triggers (il est possible que, je propose que), a recycled purpose clause (pour que), and a recycled double-object confirmation (je le lui transmettrai) from section 12.3. Sophie maintains a consistent formal/semi-formal register throughout her presentation, addressing the group as Mesdames, Messieurs rather than using a direct tu or vous form; Monsieur Lefèvre addresses her with vous.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Structuring a clear explanation

When French speakers explain a plan or a project out loud, an explicit, signposted structure is generally appreciated: stating the topic clearly at the outset (often with a phrase like il s’agit de), then marking each stage of the explanation with a connector before moving to the next. This differs from a looser, more conversational style, and listeners generally find the signposting helpful when it is audible rather than only implied. This chapter's tasks stay at the level of a clear, well-organized B2 explanation rather than a specialized professional or academic presentation genre, which is developed later in the series.

Regional Variation — Explanation style

The degree of formality expected when explaining a plan to colleagues varies by workplace and by country across the Francophone world; some contexts favor a brisker, less formulaic style even for a fairly formal update. This chapter's clearly sequenced pattern remains a safe, broadly transferable default when the expected style is uncertain.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Using il s’agit que, and going beyond this chapter's scope with il convient que

Error: Writing *Il s’agit que nous préparions le calendrier. Il s’agit de is not used productively with que plus a conjugated verb at this level; keep it to the infinitive or a noun phrase: il s’agit de préparer le calendrier.

Scope note: Standard French does allow il convient que plus the subjonctif when a specific subject is named (for example, il convient que vous prévoyiez les ressources), so this is not an error in general French. It is simply outside this chapter's scope: this chapter teaches and assesses only il convient de plus the infinitive. If you need to name a specific subject who must act, use one of the eleven authorized subjonctif triggers instead: il faut que nous préparions le calendrier.

Common Mistake — Stacking connectors without a clear order

Error: Using ensuite and puis for the same stage, or placing enfin in the middle of a sequence.

Correction: Keep the sequence logical and linear: d’abord opens it, ensuite and puis mark distinct middle stages, and enfin closes it. Plan the stages before choosing the connectors.

Common Mistake — Drifting into tu mid-presentation

Error: Beginning a formal presentation with vous and then slipping into tu when addressing a familiar colleague directly.

Correction: As in Chapter 12, choose one register deliberately and keep it consistent for the full presentation, even when a familiar colleague is in the room; save tu for the separate, clearly informal planning exchange, as Sophie and Karim do before the meeting begins.

Writing Workshop — Model text

Modèle — Présenter un projet

Il s’agit d’un projet qui vise à améliorer la communication entre les équipes. D’abord, il convient d’identifier les principaux obstacles rencontrés aujourd’hui. Ensuite, nous préparons un nouvel outil de communication, pour que chaque équipe puisse partager ses informations plus facilement. Puis, chaque responsable présente l’outil à son équipe pour assurer une bonne compréhension. Enfin, nous ferons un bilan intermédiaire après deux mois pour vérifier que nous respectons le calendrier. Il est important que nous respections chaque étape pour atteindre notre objectif.

This is about a project that aims to improve communication between teams. First, it is appropriate to identify the main obstacles encountered today. Next, we are preparing a new communication tool, so that each team can share its information more easily. Then, each person in charge presents the tool to their team to ensure good understanding. Finally, we will do a progress check after two months to verify that we are respecting the timeline. It is important that we respect each stage in order to reach our goal.

This six-sentence model includes both impersonal structures, all four sequencing connectors, one recycled purpose clause (pour que), and one recycled Chapter 8 necessity trigger (il est important que). No unauthorized trigger and no newly constructed passive sentence appear anywhere. Other responses are acceptable when they meet all task requirements and remain within the grammar and vocabulary activated through Chapter 13.

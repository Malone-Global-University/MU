Projet final : Agir en français

Final Assessment · Speak and Write French: Everyday Independence and Connected Communication

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Combine detailed narration (Chapter 16), hypothetical advice (Chapter 17), description of change over time (Chapter 18), and mediated summary across registers (Chapter 19) into a single connected account.
  2. Move fluidly between passé composé, imparfait, futur simple, and conditionnel as the communicative situation requires, without needing a new tense.
  3. Use the relative pronouns qui, que, où, dont, and lequel accurately within extended discourse.
  4. Report, synthesize, and reframe information for two different audiences within one connected project.
  5. Sustain a listening and reading comprehension task that draws on narration, advice, trend description, and mediated summary together.
  6. Complete an integrated speaking and writing performance project that demonstrates consolidated CEFR B1 control across Volume II Chapters 1–19.
  7. Self-assess readiness to move into Volume III using the closing self-check.

Communication Scenario

Several months have passed. Karim accepted the job and moved to the new city; Nadia's old neighborhood keeps changing; and the team at Karim's new job has just finished a long group discussion about a community project. A local French-language newsletter has asked Karim to write a short piece about his move, and his new manager has asked for a formal summary of the team's discussion. Nothing in this chapter is new: every task below asks you to do something you have already learned to do in Chapters 16 through 19, now combined in a single connected project rather than practiced one skill at a time.

  1. Narrate the story of the move in connected detail, using background and foreground events accurately.
  2. Give and reflect on hypothetical advice that was offered along the way.
  3. Describe how the new neighborhood, or Nadia's old one, has changed.
  4. Summarize a group discussion for two different audiences, shifting register appropriately.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Sustaining intelligibility across a longer performance

No new sound-spelling feature is introduced in this chapter. Instead, this final pronunciation focus asks you to sustain, across a longer connected performance, everything you have built since Volume I: intelligible oral vowels and nasal vowels, a recognizable French r, required liaison in fixed groups, smooth enchaînement across word boundaries, correct elision in writing and speech, and rhythm-group phrasing with the tonic accent on the final syllable of each group.

Longer speaking tasks put new pressure on these skills simply because there is more to sustain. As you move between narration, advice, description of change, and summary in the same performance, pay particular attention to:

  • keeping the imparfait/conditionnel stem contrast clear (c'était vs. ce serait) as you shift between narrating the past and giving advice;
  • maintaining the required liaison in plus en and moins en when a trend description appears mid-narrative;
  • pausing deliberately before the second half of a d'un côté… de l'autre côté or certains… d'autres contrast, exactly as practiced in Chapter 19;
  • softening your tone for advice and disagreement, then resetting to a neutral or formal tone when the register shifts.

You are not expected to introduce any new pronunciation feature here; you are expected to demonstrate that the features from earlier chapters remain reliable over a longer, more demanding performance.

Grammar: Intégrer quatre chapitres

Purpose: this chapter introduces no new grammar. The sections below reorganize the systems from Chapters 16–19 as a single integrated toolkit, so that you can choose the right tool for each part of a connected project instead of thinking of them as four separate, isolated lessons.

20.1 Choosing among passé composé, imparfait, and plus-que-parfait background

Review — Narrative tense choice

Detailed narration (Chapter 16) asks you to choose passé composé for events that move the story forward and imparfait for background, description, and ongoing states. The plus-que-parfait, which lets a narrator step one layer further back to explain something that had already happened before the main story-line, remains receptive only: you may encounter it in reading or listening this chapter, but you are not required to produce it.

  • Karim a déménagé en septembre. Il était un peu nerveux, mais content. Karim moved in September. He was a bit nervous, but happy. (event / background)

20.2 Hypothetical advice inside a narrative

Language Focus

When a narrative includes a moment of decision, the advice frame from Chapter 17 (à ta place / si j'étais toi + conditionnel; tu devrais / tu pourrais) can appear inside reported or direct speech, without breaking the narrative tense system around it. The advice clause itself stays in the conditionnel regardless of what tense is narrating the surrounding story.

  • Marc lui a dit : « À ta place, je visiterais la ville avant de décider. » Marc told him: "In your place, I would visit the city before deciding."

20.3 Describing change within the same account

Review — Trend, time contrast, and precision

Chapter 18's trend expressions (de plus en plus / de moins en moins), time-contrast markers (autrefois, à l'époque, de nos jours), and the expanded relative pronoun system (dont, lequel, alongside qui, que, où) let you pause a narrative to zoom out and describe a broader pattern, then return to the story. The passive voice remains receptive only, exactly as in Chapter 18; you may recognize it in this chapter's reading and listening, but you are not required to produce it.

  • Le quartier dans lequel il a emménagé devient de plus en plus dynamique. The neighborhood he moved into is becoming more and more lively.

20.4 Reporting and shifting register at the end of an account

Review — Mediation as a closing move

Chapter 19's reporting formulas (selon, d'après, dire/penser/trouver que), synthesis connectors (d'un côté… de l'autre côté, certains… d'autres), and summary framers (en résumé, pour résumer, en bref) work well as a closing move for a longer account: after narrating, advising, and describing change, you gather several viewpoints into one organized synthesis and, if the audience requires it, restate that synthesis in a different register. The subjonctif and the fuller system of tense transposition after a past reporting verb remain receptive only, exactly as in Chapter 19.

Nothing in this chapter's Speaking Practice, Writing Workshop, or Final Performance Project requires you to produce the plus-que-parfait, the subjonctif, or the passive voice. All three remain recognition-only, exactly as they have been since their first appearance in Chapters 16–19.

Model Dialogue: Une dernière mise à jour

Context: Several months after the move, Karim calls Nadia to catch up. Register: informal, close friends; tu throughout.

Nadia : Alors, comment se passe la nouvelle vie ? Raconte-moi tout ! So, how's the new life going? Tell me everything!
Karim : Eh bien, j'étais très nerveux le jour du déménagement, mais tout s'est bien passé. Je me souviens encore de ton conseil : « À ta place, je visiterais la ville avant de décider. » Tu avais raison. Well, I was very nervous on moving day, but everything went well. I still remember your advice: "In your place, I would visit the city before deciding." You were right.
Nadia : Ah, je m'en souviens ! Et le quartier, il te plaît ? Ha, I remember! And the neighborhood, do you like it?
Karim : Beaucoup. Il devient de plus en plus animé. L'immeuble dans lequel j'habite est juste à côté d'un marché dont tout le monde me parle avec enthousiasme. A lot. It's becoming more and more lively. The building I live in is right next to a market that everyone talks to me about enthusiastically.
Nadia : Et au travail, ça se passe comment ? And at work, how's it going?
Karim : Très bien, mais on a eu une longue discussion cette semaine sur un projet communautaire. D'un côté, certains collègues pensent qu'il faut agir tout de suite. De l'autre côté, d'autres préfèrent attendre. Selon ma directrice, on devrait décider avant la fin du mois. Very well, but we had a long discussion this week about a community project. On one hand, some colleagues think we need to act right away. On the other hand, others prefer to wait. According to my director, we should decide before the end of the month.
Nadia : Et toi, qu'est-ce que tu en penses ? And you, what do you think?
Karim : En résumé, je pense qu'on devrait avancer prudemment. Je dois justement rédiger un petit résumé formel pour l'équipe de direction demain. In summary, I think we should move forward carefully. I actually have to write a short formal summary for the management team tomorrow.
Nadia : Ça te ressemble bien, ça ! Tiens-moi au courant. That sounds just like you! Keep me posted.

This dialogue combines narration with imparfait background and passé composé events (Chapter 16), reported hypothetical advice (Chapter 17), a trend description with a relative clause (Chapter 18), and reported opinion with synthesis and summary framing (Chapter 19), all inside one connected conversation.

L'année dernière, ma collègue Léa hésitait à changer de poste. C'était une décision difficile, et elle en parlait souvent avec nous. Je lui ai dit : « À ta place, j'accepterais sans trop attendre. » Mais elle voulait d'abord peser le pour et le contre. I told her: "In your place, I would accept without waiting too long." But she wanted to weigh the pros and cons first. Reading Practice

Une mise à jour pour le journal du quartier

Il y a six mois, j'ai quitté mon ancien quartier pour m'installer ici. J'étais nerveux, mais mes amis m'ont beaucoup aidé : l'un d'eux m'a dit : « À ta place, je visiterais la ville avant de signer le bail. » J'ai suivi ce conseil. Depuis mon arrivée, j'ai remarqué que le quartier devient de plus en plus dynamique, avec de nouveaux commerces dont les habitants parlent avec fierté. En résumé, le quartier change, mais son esprit accueillant reste le même qu'autrefois.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Telling a connected life story

Across the Francophone world, personal updates shared with friends, family, or colleagues typically weave together several of the functions practiced separately in this unit: narrating what happened, acknowledging advice that was given, noting what has changed, and summarizing where things now stand. Learning to move smoothly between these functions, rather than treating them as separate exercises, is closer to how French speakers actually structure a real conversation or a written update.

Regional Variation — A cumulative reminder

Every regional note from Chapters 1–19 remains valid: the productive standard in this course is contemporary broadly understood French based primarily on educated metropolitan usage in France, while the vocabulary, advice formulas, trend expressions, and reporting language you have learned are used and understood throughout the Francophone world. As you move into Volume III, expect more frequent and more deliberate exposure to labeled regional variation.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Treating this chapter as a place to try new, unpracticed forms

Error: Attempting to use the subjonctif, the plus-que-parfait, or the passive voice in a Final Performance Project sentence because the task feels advanced.

Correction: This chapter integrates systems you already control productively; it does not add new ones. A strong final project built entirely on Chapters 1–19 material meets every requirement. If a form feels uncertain, express the same idea with a structure you have already mastered.

Common Mistake — Losing register consistency in a longer text

Error: Starting an informal account with tu and casual phrasing, then drifting into formal vous constructions mid-paragraph without a clear reason.

Correction: As in Chapter 19, register must stay consistent within a single text unless the task explicitly asks for a register shift between two separate texts. Decide on your register before you begin, and revise to confirm it holds throughout.

Common Mistake — Listing skills instead of connecting them

Error: Writing four disconnected sentences, one per required element, without any narrative or logical flow between them.

Correction: The goal of this chapter is integration. Use connectors, pronouns, and time markers you already control (Chapter 14's cause/consequence connectors, Chapter 16's narrative sequencing, Chapter 18's time markers) to link the required elements into one coherent account rather than a checklist.

Writing Workshop / Final Performance Project — Model text

Modèle — Bilan personnel (version informelle)

Il y a huit mois, j'ai décidé de changer de métier. J'hésitais depuis longtemps, et c'était une décision difficile à prendre seul. Je n'étais sûr de rien, et je posais beaucoup de questions autour de moi. Mon frère m'a dit : « À ta place, je n'attendrais pas plus longtemps. » Ma sœur, elle, pensait plutôt que je devrais attendre une offre plus sûre. J'ai écouté les deux avis, puis j'ai réfléchi pendant plusieurs semaines. Finalement, j'ai accepté un nouveau poste dans une entreprise dont plusieurs amis m'ont dit beaucoup de bien. Depuis, mon quotidien devient de plus en plus satisfaisant, et le bureau dans lequel je travaille maintenant est beaucoup plus agréable que l'ancien. Autrefois, je détestais me lever le matin ; de nos jours, je pars travailler avec plaisir. D'un côté, certains collègues de mon ancien poste pensent que j'ai pris un risque ; de l'autre côté, d'autres me félicitent d'avoir osé changer. En résumé, je suis content de ma décision, même si elle n'était pas facile à prendre.

Eight months ago, I decided to change careers. I had been hesitating for a long time, and it was a difficult decision to make alone. I wasn't sure of anything, and I asked a lot of people around me for their opinion. My brother told me: "In your place, I wouldn't wait any longer." My sister, on the other hand, thought I should wait for a more secure offer. I listened to both opinions, then I thought it over for several weeks. In the end, I accepted a new position at a company that several friends spoke very well of to me. Since then, my daily life has become more and more satisfying, and the office I work in now is much nicer than the old one. In the past, I used to dread getting up in the morning; nowadays, I leave for work happily. On one hand, some colleagues from my old job think I took a risk; on the other hand, others congratulate me for having dared to change. In summary, I'm happy with my decision, even though it wasn't easy to make.

The account contains eleven sentences and demonstrates every required element: narrated events with imparfait background, hypothetical advice from two different people, a trend/time-contrast description with two relative clauses, and a synthesis closed with en résumé. No plus-que-parfait, subjonctif, or passive-voice form appears anywhere in this model. Other responses are acceptable when they meet all task requirements and remain within the grammar and vocabulary activated through Chapter 20.

Final Performance Project — Model register-shift supplement

Modèle — Paragraphe reformulé en registre formel

Il y a huit mois, j'ai accepté un nouveau poste après une période de réflexion. Selon plusieurs proches consultés à l'époque, il valait mieux agir rapidement ; d'après d'autres, il était préférable d'attendre une offre plus sûre. Plusieurs personnes de mon entourage recommandaient cette entreprise. Depuis mon arrivée, l'environnement de travail devient de plus en plus agréable. En résumé, cette décision, bien que difficile à prendre, reste très positive.

Eight months ago, I accepted a new position after a period of reflection. According to several close contacts consulted at the time, it was better to act quickly; according to others, it was preferable to wait for a more secure offer. Several people close to me recommended this company. Since my arrival, the work environment has become more and more pleasant. In summary, this decision, though difficult to make, remains very positive.

This five-sentence supplement reformulates the same underlying content as the informal account above, replacing casual phrasing and direct quotation with reporting and softened structures (il valait mieux, il était préférable de, selon, d'après), exactly as modeled in Chapter 19. The first person remains (j'ai accepté, mon arrivée); the shift lies in tone, reporting style, and degree of impersonality rather than in eliminating first-person phrasing. It demonstrates the register shift required as the third component of the Final Performance Project without introducing any new productive grammar.

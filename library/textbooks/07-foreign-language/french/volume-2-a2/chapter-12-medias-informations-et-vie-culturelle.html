Médias, informations et vie culturelle

Media and Cultural Life · Speak and Write French: Everyday Independence and Connected Communication

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Talk about how you get your news and follow cultural life, using vocabulary for media, articles, and everyday information sources.
  2. Report what a short article or announcement says using selon, d'après, and the fixed frame l'article dit que + present-tense statement.
  3. Support a simple opinion using the connectors parce que, car, donc, and, in a limited and formulaic way, puisque.
  4. Recognize and, in fixed expressions, produce the relative pronoun dont, alongside your already-active qui, que, and où.
  5. Read a short adapted news item and identify its main idea, key details, and the writer's stated source.
  6. Distinguish, at a basic level, a fact reported by a source from a personal opinion about that fact.
  7. Write a short paragraph summarizing an article and reacting to it with a simple, supported opinion.

Communication Scenario

Back from their weekend in Lyon, Marc, Nadia, and Karim fall into an evening habit: checking the news together before dinner. Nadia likes to read a short article on her phone and tell the others what it says. Karim prefers the radio. Marc has opinions about almost everything he hears. This chapter follows their evening conversations as they report what an article or announcement says, react to it, and give simple reasons for their opinions — without yet debating at length or using the subjunctive, both of which wait for later chapters.

  1. Describe how you follow the news and cultural life.
  2. Report what a short article or announcement says.
  3. React to information with a simple, supported opinion.
  4. Use dont in a small number of fixed, high-frequency expressions.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Linking short reporting phrases

Reporting phrases like selon l'article and d'après le journal are usually pronounced as one connected rhythm group, without a pause between the preposition and the noun that follows. In selon, the written final n marks the nasal vowel /ɔ̃/ and is not pronounced as a liaison consonant, even before a vowel-initial word: selon eux is pronounced /səlɔ̃ ø/, with no linking consonant.

Practice these phrases aloud, keeping each one as a single connected group:

  • selon l'article
  • d'après le journal
  • l'article dit que...
  • je pense donc que...
  • puisque c'est vrai...

In this chapter, you are expected to pronounce these fixed reporting and reasoning phrases intelligibly as connected groups, and to maintain the schwa and rhythm patterns you have practiced since Volume I when reading a short adapted news item aloud.

Grammar: Selon l'article...

Purpose: report what a short article or announcement says using a fixed present-tense frame; support a simple opinion with cause-and-consequence connectors; and introduce the relative pronoun dont in a small, fixed set of expressions — without introducing tense-backshifted reported speech or the subjunctive.

12.1 Reporting a source: selon, d'après, l'article dit que

Building Block — Naming a source before a statement
[selon / d'après] + [source], [statement in the présent]
Selon and d'après both mean "according to" and are largely interchangeable before a noun, a name, or a stressed pronoun. They are followed directly by the source, then a comma, then the statement. This chapter treats the statement as a simple present-tense fact, exactly as the source itself states it; no tense change is required.
Selon l'article, le festival commence vendredi. D'après Nadia, l'émission est très intéressante.
According to the article, the festival starts Friday. According to Nadia, the program is very interesting.

12.2 A fixed frame for reporting: l'article dit que

Building Block — A controlled reporting verb
[l'article / le journal / l'émission] + [dit] + [que] + [statement in the présent]
This chapter productively teaches only the fixed frame dit que ("says that") with the source as the subject, and only in the présent tense of dire. The verb after que stays in whatever tense the original statement uses; this chapter's reading and writing tasks use the présent throughout, so no tense-backshift rule is required or assessed. Full reported speech, including past-tense reporting verbs and the tense changes they can trigger, is outside this chapter's scope.
L'article dit que le musée est gratuit le dimanche. Le journal dit que beaucoup de gens s'informent en ligne maintenant.
The article says that the museum is free on Sundays. The newspaper says that many people get their news online now.

Do not attempt to change the tense of the reported statement in this chapter's tasks: if the article says le festival commence vendredi, your reported sentence keeps commence in the présent. This controlled approach lets you report information accurately now; the fuller reported-speech system develops later in the series.

12.3 Supporting a simple opinion: parce que, car, donc, puisque

Language Focus

You already use parce que and car to give a reason (Chapters 6–9); both are now fully productive. This chapter adds donc ("so, therefore") to state a consequence, and introduces puisque ("since, given that") in a formulaic way for reasons the listener already knows or accepts as true. Puisque differs from parce que mainly in this shared-knowledge nuance; in this chapter, use it only in short, simple sentences rather than in longer argumentation, which Chapter 14 develops further.

  • Je pense que c'est une bonne idée, parce que le festival est gratuit. I think it's a good idea, because the festival is free.
  • Beaucoup de gens s'informent en ligne, car c'est plus rapide. Many people get their news online, because it's faster.
  • L'article dit que le musée est gratuit ; nous irons donc dimanche. The article says the museum is free; so we will go Sunday.
  • Puisque tu aimes ce genre de film, tu vas sûrement aimer ce reportage. Since you like that kind of film, you will probably like this report.
Practice — Choisir le bon connecteur

Complete each sentence with parce que, car, donc, or puisque. More than one answer may be acceptable; be ready to explain your choice.

  1. Nadia regarde les actualités tous les soirs ___ elle veut être au courant.

12.4 A first look at dont

Building Block — A new relative pronoun, in fixed expressions only
[noun] + [dont] + [rest of clause, replacing "de + noun"]
You already use qui, que, and où productively (Chapters 4 and 6). Dont replaces a noun introduced by de and is common with verbs like parler de and avoir besoin de. This chapter introduces dont only in a small set of fixed, high-frequency expressions built on l'article dont on parle and le sujet dont il s'agit. You are not expected to generate new sentences with dont freely; recognize it in reading, and reuse the fixed expressions given here.
Voici l'article dont on parle à la télévision. C'est exactement le sujet dont il s'agit dans ce reportage.
Here is the article people are talking about on television. That's exactly the subject this report is about.

This chapter's productive expectation for dont is limited to recognizing it in reading and reusing the two fixed expressions above (or close variants using the same pattern). Free, independent production of new dont clauses is not assessed until a later chapter.

Model Dialogue: On regarde les infos

Context: Marc, Nadia, and Karim are checking the news together before dinner. Register: informal, close friends, tu.

Nadia : Regardez, il y a un article sur le festival de musique. Selon l'article, ça commence vendredi soir. Look, there's an article about the music festival. According to the article, it starts Friday evening.
Karim : Ah oui ? Et c'est où, exactement ? Oh yeah? And where exactly is it?
Nadia : L'article dit que le festival a lieu au parc municipal, comme l'année dernière. Et d'après le journal, l'entrée est gratuite cette année. The article says the festival takes place at the municipal park, like last year. And according to the newspaper, admission is free this year.
Marc : C'est gratuit ? On y va, donc ! It's free? Let's go, then!
Karim : Je suis d'accord, parce que j'aime beaucoup ce genre de festival. Mais je ne sais pas si tout le monde est libre vendredi. I agree, because I really like that kind of festival. But I don't know if everyone is free on Friday.
Nadia : Puisque c'est gratuit, je pense que beaucoup de gens vont y aller. Il faudra arriver tôt. Since it's free, I think a lot of people are going to go. We'll have to arrive early.
Marc : D'accord. Au fait, il y a aussi un reportage à la télé ce soir sur les nouveaux musées. C'est l'article dont on parlait hier, non ? Okay. By the way, there's also a report on TV tonight about the new museums. That's the article we were talking about yesterday, right?
Karim : Exactement, c'est le sujet dont il s'agit. Tu veux le regarder avec nous ? Exactly, that's the subject it's about. Do you want to watch it with us?
Nadia : Bien sûr ! J'aime bien m'informer, et c'est toujours mieux à trois. Of course! I like keeping up with the news, and it's always better with the three of us.

This dialogue models reporting formulas with selon, d'après, and dit que; the connectors parce que, donc, and puisque; and the two fixed dont expressions from Grammar 12.4. No tense-backshifted reported speech and no subjunctive form appears anywhere in this dialogue.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Following the news in French

French speakers get their news from a mix of newspapers, radio, television, and online sources, and habits vary widely by generation, region, and personal preference. Naming a source before reporting information — with phrases like selon or d'après — is a common and valued habit in both everyday conversation and more careful writing, since it signals where information comes from. Specific outlets, platforms, and habits change over time and are not detailed here.

Regional Variation — Reporting phrases across the Francophone world

Selon and d'après are both broadly understood and used across the Francophone world for citing a source. Some speakers and publications favor one over the other by habit or by register (for example, d'après can feel slightly more informal in some contexts), but both forms taught in this chapter are appropriate defaults for everyday and semi-formal use.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Changing the tense after dit que

Error: Writing *l'article a dit que le festival commençait vendredi when the source itself uses the present tense.

Correction: In this chapter, keep the reporting verb in the présent (dit, not a dit) and keep the reported statement in the présent as well: l'article dit que le festival commence vendredi. Past-tense reporting and its tense changes are not part of this chapter.

Common Mistake — Overusing puisque

Error: Using puisque for every reason, including new information the listener does not yet know.

Correction: Reserve puisque for reasons the listener already knows or accepts as true; use parce que or car for new information: je suis fatigué parce que j'ai beaucoup travaillé (new information), but puisque tu es fatigué, reposons-nous (a reason both speakers already know).

Common Mistake — Inventing new sentences with dont

Error: Trying to build original dont clauses freely in this chapter's writing tasks.

Correction: This chapter authorizes only the two fixed expressions from Grammar 12.4, l'article dont on parle and le sujet dont il s'agit (or close variants using the same pattern). Free, independent production of new dont clauses is developed in a later chapter.

Writing Workshop — Model paragraph

Modèle — Résumer et réagir

Selon le site de la ville, il y a une nouvelle exposition d'art ce mois-ci au centre culturel. D'après l'article, l'exposition présente des artistes locaux et l'entrée est gratuite. L'article dit que l'exposition dure trois semaines. Je pense que c'est une excellente idée, parce que beaucoup d'artistes de la région n'ont pas souvent l'occasion de montrer leur travail. Puisque c'est gratuit, je vais sûrement y aller avec des amis, et je parlerai sûrement de l'exposition dont on parle déjà beaucoup en ligne. Nous nous informons rarement sur l'art local ; nous irons donc voir cette exposition avec plaisir.

According to the city's website, there is a new art exhibition this month at the cultural center. According to the article, the exhibition features local artists and admission is free. The article says the exhibition lasts three weeks. I think this is an excellent idea, because many artists from the region don't often get the chance to show their work. Since it's free, I will surely go with friends, and I will surely talk about the exhibition that is already being talked about a lot online. We rarely learn about local art; so we will go see this exhibition with pleasure.

The model contains six sentences and demonstrates two source citations (selon le site de la ville, d'après l'article); a dit que report in the présent; three of the four target connectors (parce que, puisque, donc); the required fixed expression l'exposition dont on parle, a close variant of l'article dont on parle using the same pattern; and a personal opinion (je pense que c'est une excellente idée) clearly separated from the reported facts that precede it. Other responses are acceptable when they meet all task requirements and remain within the grammar and vocabulary activated through Chapter 12.

Practical Performance Task — Model dialogue

Marc : Tu as vu l'article sur le nouveau café du quartier ? C'est l'article dont on parle depuis quelques jours, non ?
Nadia : Oui ! Selon l'article, il ouvre samedi prochain. D'après le propriétaire, le café sert des produits locaux.
Marc : L'article dit aussi que le premier café est gratuit pour les cent premiers clients.
Nadia : On y va, donc ! Puisque c'est près de chez nous, ce n'est pas compliqué.
Marc : Je suis d'accord, car j'aime bien soutenir les nouveaux commerces du quartier. À ton avis, on devrait arriver à quelle heure ?
Nadia : À mon avis, il faut arriver tôt, parce que beaucoup de gens vont sûrement vouloir le café gratuit.

This model demonstrates every required performance feature: two source citations (selon l'article, d'après le propriétaire); a dit que report; all four target connectors (donc, puisque, car, parce que); the fixed dont expression l'article dont on parle, built on the pattern parler de + noun → dont; a genuine follow-up question and connected answer; and a personal opinion (à mon avis, il faut arriver tôt) clearly distinguished from the reported facts. Other dialogues that meet the task's requirements are equally valid.

Looking Ahead

Ce que vous savez faire maintenant — What you can do now

You can report what a short article or announcement says using selon, d'après, and dit que, support a simple opinion with parce que, car, donc, and formulaic puisque, recognize and reuse two fixed expressions with dont, and read a short adapted news item for its main idea, key details, and source.

Conseils, possibilités et hypothèses

Advice, Possibility, and Hypothesis · Speak and Write French: Everyday Independence and Connected Communication

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Use si + imparfait + conditionnel productively to give hypothetical advice, not only to imagine a personal possibility.
  2. Soften advice using the conditionnel of devoir and pouvoir (tu devrais, tu pourrais) instead of the imperative or a blunt statement.
  3. Introduce a suggestion tactfully using formulas such as à ta place and si j'étais toi.
  4. Disagree politely using softened formulas rather than a flat contradiction.
  5. Distinguish, in the same conversation, between a real plan (si + présent + futur simple, Chapter 11) and hypothetical advice (si + imparfait + conditionnel, this chapter).
  6. Read and listen to an extended conversation in which friends give each other advice and politely disagree.
  7. Write a short advice column response that gives hypothetical advice, offers an alternative, and disagrees politely with one suggestion.

Communication Scenario

Karim is trying to decide whether to accept a new job offer that would mean moving to another city. He asks Marc and Nadia what they think he should do. Everyone has an opinion — and not everyone agrees. You have already learned to describe an imagined possibility with si + imparfait + conditionnel (Chapter 13) and to hypothesize about your own life. This chapter asks you to turn that same frame outward: toward someone else, as advice, and to learn the softened language French speakers use to suggest, recommend, and politely disagree.

  1. Give hypothetical advice to someone else using si + imparfait + conditionnel.
  2. Soften a suggestion using devrais or pourrais.
  3. Introduce advice with à ta place or si j'étais toi.
  4. Disagree with a suggestion politely, without shutting down the conversation.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Softening the voice for advice and disagreement

Advice and disagreement are pragmatically delicate in any language, and French speakers often signal softness through intonation as much as through wording. When you say tu devrais or tu pourrais, a gentle falling-rising contour, rather than a flat or sharp one, keeps the suggestion sounding like an option rather than a command.

The conditionnel endings (-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient) remain identical in sound to the imparfait endings, as you practiced in Chapters 13 and 15; here the contrast to watch for is stem: tu devais (imparfait: you used to have to) versus tu devrais (conditionnel: you should), distinguished by the extra -r- before the ending.

Practice these forms aloud, with a soft, suggestive tone:

  • À ta place, je réfléchirais encore un peu.
  • Tu devrais peut-être en parler à ta famille.
  • Je comprends, mais à mon avis, ce n'est pas une bonne idée.

No new sound-spelling feature is introduced in this chapter. You are expected to apply, in this new pragmatic context, the imparfait/conditionnel stem contrast from Chapter 13, connected-speech linking from earlier chapters, and a softened, suggestive intonation appropriate to giving advice and disagreeing politely.

Grammar: Conseils, possibilités et hypothèses

Purpose: extend the si + imparfait + conditionnel frame you already control (Chapter 13) from imagining your own situation to giving advice about someone else's; add softened advice formulas built on the conditionnel of devoir and pouvoir; and teach polite disagreement formulas, all without introducing a new tense.

17.1 Turning hypothesis into advice

Building Block — Advice with si + imparfait + conditionnel
[À ta place / Si j'étais toi] + [je / j'] + [conditionnel] …
You already know the si + imparfait + conditionnel frame from Chapter 13, where it imagined a possibility for yourself. This chapter recycles exactly the same verb forms — the imparfait and the conditionnel — but repositions them as advice: you imagine yourself in the other person's situation, then state what you would do. À ta place and si j'étais toi both work as openers; neither requires a full new si clause, since the "if" meaning is already built into the phrase.
À ta place, je poserais plus de questions avant d'accepter. Si j'étais toi, je prendrais le temps de réfléchir.
In your place, I would ask more questions before accepting. If I were you, I would take the time to think it over.

17.2 Softened advice: devoir and pouvoir in the conditionnel

Language Focus

Tu devrais (you should) and tu pourrais (you could) are the conditionnel forms of devoir and pouvoir, which you already conjugate in other tenses. Using the conditionnel here softens the advice: tu devrais feels like a recommendation, while an imperative (fais ça !) or the present indicative (tu dois faire ça) feels closer to an order.

  • Tu devrais demander plus d'informations sur le poste. You should ask for more information about the position.
  • Tu pourrais aussi en parler avec ton actuel employeur. You could also talk about it with your current employer.

Devrais suggests a stronger recommendation; pourrais presents a more open option. Both remain politer than a direct command.

17.3 Disagreeing politely

Language Note — Softening disagreement

French conversational advice-giving often includes disagreement, and disagreement is normally softened rather than stated flatly. A useful pattern is to acknowledge the other person's point first, then introduce a contrasting view with mais.

  • Je comprends, mais à mon avis, ce n'est pas le bon moment. I understand, but in my opinion, this isn't the right moment.
  • Ce n'est pas faux, mais à ta place, j'attendrais encore un peu. That's not wrong, but in your place, I would wait a bit longer.

Notice how this chapter's disagreement formulas combine naturally with the advice frame from 17.1 and 17.2: acknowledge, then offer an alternative hypothetical suggestion.

17.4 Real plan or hypothetical advice? Choosing the right si frame

Review — Two si frames, one more purpose

You now use the two si frames from Chapters 11 and 13 for a widening range of purposes. The real-condition frame (si + présent + futur simple) still describes a genuinely open plan. The hypothetical frame (si + imparfait + conditionnel) now does two jobs: imagining a possibility for yourself, and — new in this chapter — giving advice framed as what you would do in someone else's position.

  • Si Karim accepte le poste, il déménagera en septembre. If Karim accepts the position, he will move in September. (real, open plan)
  • Si j'étais Karim, je n'accepterais pas sans visiter la ville d'abord. If I were Karim, I wouldn't accept without visiting the city first. (hypothetical advice)

No new tense is introduced here; this section consolidates a choice you already make correctly, in a new communicative use.

The plus-que-parfait, introduced receptively in Chapter 16, remains recognition-only in this chapter. You are not required to produce it in any Chapter 17 speaking or writing task.

Model Dialogue: Qu'est-ce que tu ferais, à ma place ?

Context: Karim asks Marc and Nadia for advice about a job offer that would mean moving. Register: informal, close friends; tu when addressing one friend, plural vous when addressing both friends together.

Karim : J'ai reçu une offre pour un poste dans une autre ville. Qu'est-ce que vous feriez, à ma place ? I got an offer for a position in another city. What would you do, in my place?
Nadia : À ta place, je serais très tentée. C'est une belle opportunité, non ? In your place, I'd be very tempted. It's a nice opportunity, isn't it?
Marc : Je comprends, mais à mon avis, ce n'est pas si simple. Karim, tu devrais peut-être d'abord visiter la ville avant de décider. I understand, but in my opinion, it's not that simple. Karim, you should maybe visit the city first before deciding.
Karim : Bonne idée. Et le salaire est plus élevé, ça compte aussi. Good idea. And the salary is higher, that counts too.
Nadia : Exactement ! Si j'étais toi, j'accepterais sans trop hésiter. Exactly! If I were you, I would accept without too much hesitation.
Marc : Ce n'est pas faux, mais tu pourrais aussi demander un peu plus de temps pour répondre. Ça dépend surtout de ce que tu veux vraiment pour ta vie, non ? That's not wrong, but you could also ask for a bit more time to answer. It mostly depends on what you really want for your life, right?
Karim : Vous avez tous les deux raison, en fait. Je vais peser le pour et le contre ce week-end. You're both right, actually. I'm going to weigh the pros and cons this weekend.
Nadia : Si tu décides d'accepter, on fêtera ça ! Et si tu refuses, ce n'est pas grave non plus. If you decide to accept, we'll celebrate! And if you turn it down, that's fine too.

This dialogue models the advice openers à ta place and si j'étais toi, softened advice with tu devrais and tu pourrais, and polite disagreement with je comprends, mais and ce n'est pas faux, mais. Nadia's final line also reviews the real-condition si frame from Chapter 11, side by side with the hypothetical advice used earlier in the conversation.

Reading Practice

Rubrique conseils — Réponse à un lecteur

Cher lecteur, merci pour votre message. Vous hésitez à quitter votre emploi actuel pour lancer votre propre entreprise, et je comprends parfaitement cette hésitation. À votre place, je ne prendrais pas cette décision trop vite. Vous pourriez, par exemple, commencer ce projet le soir ou le week-end, sans quitter votre poste immédiatement. Vous devriez aussi parler à quelqu'un qui a déjà créé une entreprise dans votre domaine ; cette personne pourra vous aider avec des questions pratiques que je ne peux pas traiter ici. Ce n'est pas faux de penser que le bon moment n'existera peut-être jamais parfaitement, mais à mon avis, ce n'est pas une raison suffisante pour se lancer sans préparation. Si j'étais vous, je préparerais un plan solide avant de démissionner. Bonne chance dans votre réflexion.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Giving advice without giving orders

In many French-speaking social contexts, direct advice using the imperative or a flat statement can come across as more forceful than intended, especially among peers. The conditionnel softens a suggestion into an offer the other person can freely accept or decline, which is why tu devrais and tu pourrais are so common in everyday advice-giving, even when the speaker feels strongly.

Regional Variation — Advice openers

À ta place and si j'étais toi are both used and understood throughout the Francophone world. Some speakers favor one over the other as a personal habit rather than a regional rule; both remain safe, neutral defaults for this chapter.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Using the imparfait instead of the conditionnel for the advice itself

Error: Saying *à ta place, je réfléchissais encore un peu, using the imparfait where the conditionnel is required.

Correction: The advice clause itself takes the conditionnel, exactly as in the hypothetical si frame: à ta place, je réfléchirais encore un peu.

Common Mistake — Disagreeing too bluntly

Error: Saying *non, tu as tort immediately, without acknowledging the other person's point first.

Correction: Soften disagreement by conceding something first: je comprends, mais à mon avis, ce n'est pas le bon choix.

Common Mistake — Confusing devrais and devais

Error: Writing or saying *tu devais parler à quelqu'un when advice, not a past obligation, is intended.

Correction: Tu devais is imparfait (you used to have to / you were supposed to); softened present-day advice requires the conditionnel: tu devrais parler à quelqu'un.

Writing Workshop — Model text

Modèle — Réponse à une rubrique conseils

Cher ami, merci de m'avoir écrit. Tu hésites entre rester dans ta ville et déménager pour un nouveau poste, et je comprends bien cette hésitation. À ta place, je ne me déciderais pas trop vite. Tu pourrais, par exemple, demander à visiter la nouvelle ville avant de répondre à l'offre. Tu devrais aussi parler à des personnes qui connaissent bien cette région ; elles pourront t'aider avec des questions pratiques que je ne peux pas traiter ici. Ce n'est pas faux de penser que rester est plus sûr, mais à mon avis, la sécurité n'est pas toujours la meilleure raison de refuser une belle opportunité. Si j'étais toi, je peserais calmement le pour et le contre avant de répondre. Bonne chance dans ta décision.

Dear friend, thank you for writing to me. You're hesitating between staying in your city and moving for a new position, and I understand this hesitation well. In your place, I wouldn't decide too quickly. You could, for example, ask to visit the new city before responding to the offer. You should also talk to people who know this region well; they'll be able to help you with practical questions I can't address here. It's not wrong to think that staying is safer, but in my opinion, safety isn't always the best reason to turn down a good opportunity. If I were you, I would calmly weigh the pros and cons before answering. Good luck with your decision.

The response contains eight sentences and demonstrates every required element: the stated dilemma, hypothetical advice with à ta place and si j'étais toi, softened suggestions with tu pourrais and tu devrais, and a polite disagreement built on ce n'est pas faux… mais. No subjunctive and no plus-que-parfait form appears. Other responses are acceptable when they meet all task requirements and remain within the grammar and vocabulary activated through Chapter 17.

8

Acheter, comparer et réclamer

Purchasing and Complaints · Speak and Write French: Everyday Independence and Connected Communication

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Compare two or more people, things, or options using plus... que, moins... que, and aussi... que.
  2. Use the irregular comparative forms meilleur(e)(s), mieux, and pire, and the expression moins bien, choosing each according to whether the comparison describes a noun, a situation, or an action.
  3. Form the superlative with le/la/les plus and le/la/les moins to single out the best, worst, most, or least of a group.
  4. Use celui, celle, ceux, and celles to avoid repeating a noun already mentioned when comparing products.
  5. Describe a purchase, a product, and a problem with that product, using vocabulary appropriate to a shop or an online order.
  6. Make a firm but polite complaint, stating the problem clearly and requesting a specific solution.
  7. Consolidate direct-object and indirect-object pronoun use from Chapter 4 in longer, more independent complaint and comparison exchanges.
  8. Distinguish transaction register (with a cashier or salesperson) from complaint register (raising and resolving a problem).
  9. Read and write a message comparing two products or making a clear, polite complaint about a purchase.

Communication Scenario

With the heating fixed and her rhythm in Tours finally settling down, Nadia decides to replace the desk lamp she broke during her move. In a shop near her new apartment, she compares two lamps with a salesperson before choosing one. A few days later, an online order she placed arrives with a problem: the lamp she receives is smaller than the model shown on the website, and it does not work as well as her old one. Nadia has to write a calm, clear, and firm message to the seller explaining the situation and asking for a solution. Meanwhile, Marc is comparing two phone plans in Bordeaux and has to decide, out loud, with a salesperson, which one suits him better. This chapter is about the ordinary language of buying things, comparing options, and raising a problem when something goes wrong — without becoming rude, and without backing down before the problem is actually solved.

  1. Compare two products, services, or options using comparative and superlative language.
  2. Refer back to a product already mentioned using celui, celle, ceux, or celles.
  3. Describe an item, a purchase, and a problem with that purchase.
  4. State a complaint clearly, calmly, and firmly, and request a specific solution.

This chapter introduces no new tense beyond the present, aller + infinitif, and passé composé / imparfait already consolidated in Volume I and Chapters 1–7. Its new productive material is comparative and superlative structures, the irregular forms meilleur, mieux, pire, and moins bien, the controlled demonstrative pronouns celui/celle/ceux/celles, and the vocabulary and register needed to describe a purchase and raise a complaint. Language Note — Réclamer versus se plaindre

Se plaindre de quelque chose means to express dissatisfaction about something; it describes the act of voicing a complaint. Réclamer quelque chose means to request or claim a remedy, correction, payment, item, or right; it names what the person wants to happen next. In an effective complaint, a customer typically explains the problem and then réclame un remboursement or réclame un échange. This chapter keeps se plaindre at the receptive level, since its irregular conjugation is not yet productive, and develops réclamer and the fixed complaint formulas below as the productive path to the same communicative goal.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Comparative plus, meilleur/mieux/pire, and the delivery of a complaint

Chapter 3 introduced the position-sensitive pronunciation of plus. Comparative plus... que follows the same general pattern you already control, now applied to a new communicative use.

A. Plus in ordinary comparisons. In ordinary affirmative comparisons before a consonant-initial adjective or adverb, the final consonant of plus is generally silent: plus cher, pronounced approximately /ply ʃɛʁ/. Before a vowel-initial word, liaison may produce /z/: plus intéressant, approximately /ply.zɛ̃.te.ʁe.sɑ̃/. In other uses — especially when plus stands alone or is emphasized — the final consonant may be pronounced. This chapter assesses only the common comparative phrases modeled here, not every possible use of plus.

B. Meilleur, mieux, and pire. Meilleur ends in a pronounced r. Singular and plural forms (meilleur, meilleurs) share the same basic pronunciation in many contexts; a liaison consonant may be heard before a following vowel in appropriate connected-speech environments, but this chapter does not require learners to produce this optional liaison. Mieux is invariable and rhymes approximately with peu plus a glide. Pire ends in a clearly pronounced /ʁ/ and should not be confused with pour or père.

C. Delivering a complaint. Deliver a complaint clearly, calmly, and at an appropriate volume. Use understandable phrase grouping and avoid exaggerated stress or shouting. Firmness comes primarily from precise wording, a specific description of the problem, and a clear requested solution — not from any single pitch pattern.

In this chapter, you are expected to: apply the position-sensitive pronunciation of comparative plus in the phrases modeled here; pronounce meilleur, mieux, and pire clearly and distinctly; and deliver complaint language in an understandable, controlled manner, with clear phrase grouping and appropriate volume.

Grammar: Plus, moins, aussi... que

Purpose: teach comparative and superlative structures, including the irregular forms meilleur, mieux, pire, and moins bien; introduce controlled demonstrative pronouns for product comparison; and consolidate direct- and indirect-object pronoun use from Chapter 4 in the context of comparison and complaint — without introducing any new tense or mood.

8.1 Comparing with plus, moins, and aussi... que

Language Focus

To compare two people, things, or options, place plus ("more"), moins ("less"), or aussi ("as") directly before an adjective or adverb, and close the comparison with que before the second term. This pattern works the same way regardless of which of the three words you choose.

  • Cette lampe est plus chère que l'autre. This lamp is more expensive than the other one.
  • Ce forfait est moins cher que le tien. This phone plan is less expensive than yours.
  • Ce modèle est aussi pratique que l'ancien. This model is as practical as the old one.

The adjective still agrees normally with the noun it describes, exactly as in ordinary adjective agreement: Cette lampe est plus grande. / Ces lampes sont plus grandes. Comparison with a noun (more of a thing, fewer of a thing) uses plus de, moins de, and autant de instead, without an adjective: J'ai plus de temps aujourd'hui.

8.1A Avoiding repetition: celui, celle, ceux, celles

Language Focus

When you compare a product to another one already named, repeating the noun every time is unnatural. French uses a demonstrative pronoun instead, which agrees in gender and number with the noun it replaces: celui (masculine singular), celle (feminine singular), ceux (masculine or mixed plural), celles (feminine plural). These pronouns are never used alone; they are always followed by a short phrase such as du site or présenté(e) sur le site that identifies which one is meant.

  • Je préfère ce modèle à celui du site. I prefer this model to the one on the website. celui replaces ce modèle (masculine singular)
  • La poêle reçue est plus petite que celle présentée sur le site. The pan received is smaller than the one shown on the website. celle replaces la poêle (feminine singular)
  • Ces produits sont moins chers que ceux du magasin. These products are less expensive than the ones in the shop. ceux replaces ces produits (masculine plural)
  • Ces lampes sont plus solides que celles du magasin. These lamps are sturdier than the ones in the shop. celles replaces ces lampes (feminine plural) and is followed by the identifying phrase du magasin

This chapter limits celui/celle/ceux/celles to product comparison, exactly as modeled above. Compound forms such as celui-ci and celui-là may appear in readings and dialogues for recognition, but broader use of celui qui, celui que, and indefinite demonstrative pronouns is not part of this chapter's controlled productive system.

8.2 Irregular and special comparisons: meilleur, mieux, pire, and moins bien

Building Block — Four words, one choice: noun, situation, or action?
[meilleur(e)(s)] a noun  |  [mieux] an action  |  [pire] a noun/situation  |  [moins bien] an action
Bon ("good") and bien ("well") do not form their comparatives with plus bon or plus bien; they have irregular comparatives, meilleur and mieux. Mauvais ("bad") has a controlled irregular comparative in this chapter, pire, used for a noun or a general situation. When an action is performed less successfully, French uses moins bien, not a special word.
Ce produit est meilleur que l'ancien. Cette lampe est pire que l'autre. Ce vendeur explique mieux que l'autre. Cette imprimante fonctionne moins bien que l'autre.
This product is better than the old one. This lamp is worse than the other one. This salesperson explains better than the other one. This printer works less well than the other one.

Compare all four side by side:

  • Ce restaurant est meilleur. meilleur describes the noun restaurant (adjective, agrees)
  • On mange mieux dans ce restaurant. mieux describes the verb manger (adverb, invariable)
  • Cette lampe est pire que l'autre. pire describes the noun lampe (adjective-like, invariable for gender, plural pires)
  • Cette imprimante fonctionne moins bien que l'autre. moins bien describes the verb fonctionner (adverbial phrase, invariable)

The superlative follows the same irregular pattern:

  • C'est le meilleur produit du magasin. It's the best product in the shop.
  • C'est le pire produit des trois. It is the worst product of the three.
  • C'est le vendeur qui explique le mieux. He's the salesperson who explains the best.

A frequent test: if the sentence could be rephrased with "a better/worse ___" (a noun), use meilleur or pire; if it could be rephrased with "___ better/worse" (an action), use mieux or moins bien. This chapter does not extend into every meaning of plus mauvais or into stylistic distinctions beyond this controlled set.

8.3 The superlative: le/la/les plus, le/la/les moins

Building Block — Singling out the extreme of a group
[le/la/les] + [plus/moins] + [adjectif] (+ de + groupe)
The superlative singles out one item as the most or least of a defined group. The article agrees with the noun described, and de introduces the group being compared, parallel to English "of" or "in."
C'est la lampe la plus chère du magasin. C'est le forfait le moins cher de tous.
It's the most expensive lamp in the shop. It's the least expensive plan of all.

8.4 Consolidating object pronouns in comparison and complaint

Language Focus — Recycled from Chapter 4, applied here

This chapter introduces no new pronoun system. It asks you to apply the direct-object and indirect-object pronoun patterns from Chapter 4 with more independence, in longer comparison and complaint exchanges, where a product, a person, or a problem is established early and then referred back to repeatedly.

  • J'ai acheté cette lampe la semaine dernière, mais je ne l'aime plus. Je voudrais la rendre. la (direct object, referring to la lampe), recycled from Ch. 4
  • J'ai écrit au vendeur. Je lui ai expliqué le problème, mais il ne m'a pas encore répondu. lui = indirect object after expliquer quelque chose à quelqu'un, referring to the seller; m' = indirect object after répondre à quelqu'un, referring to the speaker
  • Vous pouvez m'envoyer la bonne poêle ? — Oui, je vous l'envoie aujourd'hui. vous = indirect object, the recipient; l' = direct object, the correct pan; a controlled double object pronoun sequence, recycled from Ch. 4

As in Chapter 4, keep any double object pronoun sequence limited to a 1st/2nd-person pronoun plus le/la/les, using the frame je vous l'envoie modeled above. Combinations with lui and leur remain outside this chapter's controlled system. Independent production of a double object pronoun sequence is not required in this chapter; a single correctly used direct- or indirect-object pronoun is sufficient, and the double sequence above may be used for additional challenge.

8.5 Making a firm, polite complaint

Building Block — From statement to solution
[describe the purchase] → [state the problem] → [ce n'est pas normal / je ne suis pas satisfait(e)] → [state the desired solution]
A complaint that stays effective and polite moves through a predictable sequence: establish what was bought, name the specific problem, mark your dissatisfaction clearly but calmly, and state exactly what you want to happen next. Vague dissatisfaction without a requested solution is less effective than a complaint that ends with a clear ask.
J'ai commandé plusieurs articles, mais il en manque un. Ce n'est pas normal. Je voudrais un remboursement pour l'article manquant.
I ordered several items, but one of them is missing. That's not right. I would like a refund for the missing item.

In J'ai commandé plusieurs articles, mais il en manque un, en refers back to the previously mentioned set of articles, while un gives the number missing: "one of them is missing." This is the quantitative use of en developed from Chapter 6, where en stands in for the group already named and a number or quantity word states how many. Pourriez-vous recycles the formal formula from Chapter 7.

8.6 Recycling past-participle agreement with être and reflexives

Language Note — Previously learned agreement — No new rule

Complaint narration often uses the passé composé to explain what happened: je suis allée au magasin, le colis est arrivé hier. These continue the same être-agreement pattern from earlier chapters; nothing new is being asked of you here. Agreement with a preceding direct object remains outside this chapter's scope and is developed later in the volume.

Model Dialogue: Ce n'est pas ce que j'ai commandé

Context: Nadia calls the online seller a few days after receiving her order, which has a missing item and an item that does not match the description. Register: formal, vous, speaking with a customer-service employee she does not know.

L'employé : Bonjour, service client, j'écoute. Hello, customer service, how may I help you?
Nadia : Bonjour monsieur. J'ai reçu ma commande hier, mais il y a un problème : j'ai commandé plusieurs articles, il en manque un, et un autre ne correspond pas à la description. Hello. I received my order yesterday, but there's a problem: I ordered several items, one of them is missing, and another doesn't match the description.
L'employé : Je suis désolé pour ce désagrément. Quel article manque-t-il ? I'm sorry for this inconvenience. Which item is missing?
Nadia : Il manque la petite poêle. Et la poêle que j'ai reçue est plus petite que celle présentée sur le site. Ce n'est vraiment pas normal. The small frying pan is missing. And the pan I received is smaller than the one shown on the website. This really isn't right.
L'employé : Je comprends, et je vous présente mes excuses. Qu'est-ce que vous préférez : un remboursement ou un échange ? I understand, and I apologize. What would you prefer: a refund or an exchange?
Nadia : Je voudrais un remboursement pour la poêle manquante. Pourriez-vous aussi m'envoyer la bonne poêle pour l'autre article ? I would like a refund for the missing pan. Could you also send me the correct pan for the other item?
L'employé : Bien sûr. Je vous rembourse le prix de la poêle manquante aujourd'hui, et nous allons vous envoyer la bonne poêle dès que possible. Of course. I am refunding you the price of the missing pan today, and we are going to send you the correct pan as soon as possible.
Nadia : Très bien, merci. Est-ce que la nouvelle poêle va être meilleure que celle-ci, au moins ? Very well, thank you. Will the new pan at least be better than this one?
L'employé : Oui. Elle va correspondre exactement à la description du site. Yes. It is going to match the website description exactly.

This dialogue recycles the quantitative use of en (il en manque un), direct object pronouns (je vous rembourse), the formal formulas je voudrais and pourriez-vous (Ch. 7), and this chapter's new material: the demonstrative pronoun celle (referring to la poêle), the comparative plus petite que, and meilleure que. The dialogue uses only aller + infinitif for planned or expected action (la nouvelle poêle va être meilleure, elle va correspondre); no future-simple form appears.

  • what you ordered and when you ordered or received it;
  • a clear description of the specific problem;

  2. Draft your opening. Use a formal greeting and state what you ordered and when.

  6. Draft your closing. End formally and appropriately.
  7. Revise. Confirm comparative forms, pronoun choice, agreement, accents, and punctuation are accurate throughout.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Complaining without conflict

In French-speaking commercial contexts, an effective complaint is typically calm, specific, and solution-oriented rather than emotional or vague. Opening with a clear description of the problem, using a controlled formula such as ce n'est pas normal, and closing with a precise request tends to be more effective — and is generally considered more appropriate — than repeating a complaint without stating what outcome is wanted. Written complaints, such as an email to a seller, often serve as a record and are expected to remain polite even when firm.

Regional Variation — Shopping and consumer vocabulary

Return policies, timeframes, and related procedures vary by country and by retailer within the Francophone world, and this chapter does not attempt to describe any one country's actual consumer law; the product listing in the Reading Practice above is written for practice purposes only. Terms such as un remboursement and un échange are broadly understood, while specific procedures differ by retailer and by region.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Saying plus bon or plus bien

Error: Saying *Ce produit est plus bon or *Il explique plus bien.

Correction: Bon and bien have irregular comparatives: Ce produit est meilleur. and Il explique mieux. Plus bon and plus bien are not standard French.

Scope note: Cette lampe est plus mauvaise que l'autre is grammatically valid French. In this chapter, use pire as the controlled irregular comparative for this meaning: Cette lampe est pire que l'autre.

Common Mistake — Confusing meilleur/pire with mieux/moins bien

Error: Saying *Cette lampe fonctionne meilleur or *Cette lampe fonctionne pire, using the noun-describing form to describe a verb.

Correction: Use mieux or moins bien when describing how an action happens: Cette lampe fonctionne mieux. / Cette lampe fonctionne moins bien. Reserve meilleur and pire for describing a noun: C'est une meilleure lampe. / Cette lampe est pire que l'autre. For the superlative: C'est la pire lampe des trois.

Common Mistake — Repeating the noun instead of using celui/celle/ceux/celles

Error: Saying La poêle reçue est plus petite que la poêle sur le site, repeating the noun instead of replacing it.

Correction: Once the noun is established, replace it with the matching demonstrative pronoun: La poêle reçue est plus petite que celle présentée sur le site. Choose celui/celle/ceux/celles according to the gender and number of the noun being replaced.

Common Mistake — Complaining without requesting a solution

Error: Ending a complaint with only Ce n'est pas normal ! and no clear request.

Correction: As explained in Grammar 8.5, a complete complaint states a specific desired outcome: Ce n'est pas normal. Je voudrais un remboursement. Stating dissatisfaction alone, without a request, leaves the other person unsure of what to do next.

Writing Workshop — Model response

Modèle — Model email

Madame, Monsieur,

Je vous écris au sujet de ma commande 4287, reçue hier. J'ai commandé une lampe de quarante-cinq centimètres, mais le modèle reçu est plus petit que celui présenté sur votre site. La lumière fonctionne aussi moins bien que celle de mon ancienne lampe. Ce produit est donc pire que le modèle décrit dans l'annonce. Je ne suis pas satisfaite de cet achat. Je voudrais un échange. Pourriez-vous m'envoyer le bon modèle dès que possible ? Je vais rendre la lampe reçue avec la preuve d'achat. Merci de me confirmer la procédure par message.

Cordialement,
Nadia Lefèvre

Dear Sir or Madam, I am writing to you about my order 4287, received yesterday. I ordered a lamp forty-five centimeters tall, but the model received is smaller than the one shown on your website. The light also works less well than the one on my old lamp. This product is therefore worse than the model described in the listing. I am not satisfied with this purchase. I would like an exchange. Could you send me the correct model as soon as possible? I am going to return the received lamp with proof of purchase. Please confirm the procedure by message. Regards, Nadia Lefèvre

This model contains eight to twelve complete sentences, a formal greeting and closing, a stated order and problem, a comparison with a demonstrative pronoun, pire and moins bien, a firm complaint formula, a clearly stated solution with je voudrais and pourriez-vous, and standard written negation throughout. It introduces no future-simple or unrestricted conditional forms. Other answers are acceptable when they satisfy the task requirements above.

Practical Performance Task — Model dialogue

Employée : Bonjour, service client, j'écoute.
Cliente : Bonjour madame. Je vous appelle au sujet de la commande 4287.
Employée : Quel est le problème ?
Cliente : J'ai commandé une grande lampe, mais la lampe reçue est plus petite que celle présentée sur le site. Elle fonctionne aussi moins bien que mon ancienne lampe. Je ne suis pas satisfaite de cet achat.
Employée : Je suis désolée. Vous préférez un remboursement ou un échange ?

This model demonstrates every required performance feature: a formal opening; identification of the order (commande 4287) and the purchased product (une grande lampe); a specific product problem; a product comparison using a demonstrative pronoun (la lampe reçue est plus petite que celle présentée sur le site); the irregular comparative moins bien; the firm but polite complaint formula je ne suis pas satisfaite de cet achat; a specific requested remedy (je voudrais un échange); an appropriate response to the proposed solution; at least one correct object pronoun, including a correctly ordered double object pronoun sequence (vous l'envoyer); consistent formal vous; and a polite closing, with no future-simple forms. This is one acceptable model; other dialogues that meet the task's requirements are equally valid.

Looking Ahead

Ce que vous savez faire maintenant — What you can do now

You can compare people, things, and options with plus/moins/aussi... que, correctly distinguish and use the irregular forms meilleur, mieux, pire, and moins bien, avoid repeating a noun with celui/celle/ceux/celles, single out the best or least of a group with the superlative, and make a firm but polite complaint that clearly states the problem and the solution you want.

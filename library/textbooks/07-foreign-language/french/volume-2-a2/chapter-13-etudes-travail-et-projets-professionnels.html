Études, travail et projets professionnels

Study and Work Projects · Speak and Write French: Everyday Independence and Connected Communication

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Talk about studies, skills, and work experience using everyday vocabulary for jobs, applications, and interviews.
  2. Write a short, correctly formatted formal email, including an appropriate opening and closing.
  3. Recycle the real-condition frame si + présent + futur simple (Chapter 11) to describe concrete professional plans.
  4. Recognize and produce, in a controlled and limited way, the hypothetical frame si + imparfait + conditionnel to describe an imagined or unlikely professional situation (for example, si j'avais plus d'expérience, je postulerais pour ce poste).
  5. Distinguish, in use, the open, genuinely possible condition from the imagined, hypothetical one, and explain in simple terms why each frame is appropriate to its context.
  6. Ask a polite, indirect question in a formal context using je voudrais savoir si and pourriez-vous me dire.
  7. Read a short job posting or professional message and identify the role, requirements, and how to apply.
  8. Write a short cover message and describe skills, plans, and one hypothetical scenario related to a course of study or a job.

Communication Scenario

Karim has seen a job posting that interests him, and Nadia is finishing a course of study and thinking about her next step. Marc, who already works, offers advice from experience. Over several conversations, the three of them move between two related kinds of talk: talk about real, concrete plans — what someone will actually do — and talk about imagined possibilities — what someone would do if circumstances were different. This chapter gives you the tools for both: a review of the real-plan language from Chapter 11, formal writing conventions for a short professional message, and a first, carefully limited look at the hypothetical frame si + imparfait + conditionnel.

  1. Describe studies, skills, and work experience.
  2. Write a short, correctly formatted formal email or message.
  3. State a real professional plan and an imagined possibility, using two different si frames.
  4. Ask a polite question using an indirect formal formula.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Imparfait and conditionnel share endings

The imparfait and the conditionnel use the same written person endings: -ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient. In careful standard pronunciation, -ais, -ait, and -aient are normally pronounced /ɛ/; -ions typically ends in the glide-plus-nasal-vowel sequence /jɔ̃/; and -iez ends in /je/, which is not nasal. Because the two tenses share the same written endings, this means that j'avais (imparfait of avoir) and je serais (conditionnel of être) sound alike in their endings; learners distinguish the two tenses through the verb stem, not the ending, along with the surrounding sentence and context.

Practice these forms aloud, listening for the stem rather than the ending:

  • si j'avais (imparfait, avoir) vs. je serais (conditionnel, être)
  • si tu étais (imparfait, être) vs. tu pourrais (conditionnel, pouvoir)
  • si nous réussissions (imparfait, réussir) vs. nous postulerions (conditionnel, postuler)

In this chapter, you are expected to pronounce common imparfait and conditionnel forms intelligibly in the hypothetical frame, and to rely on the stem, the word si, and the surrounding sentence — not the ending alone — to identify which verb and which tense you are hearing.

Grammar: Si j'avais le temps...

Purpose: consolidate formal written French for a short professional message; recycle the real-condition frame from Chapter 11; and introduce, in a controlled and limited way, the hypothetical frame si + imparfait + conditionnel — without introducing the subjunctive or expanding the hypothetical frame beyond a small set of professional and academic contexts.

13.1 Formal email conventions

Building Block — Opening and closing a formal message
[Madame, / Monsieur,] … [Cordialement,] or [Veuillez agréer, Madame, Monsieur, mes salutations distinguées.]
A formal French email or letter opens with the recipient's title alone (Madame, or Monsieur,) rather than a first name, and closes with a fixed formula. Cordialement is common, neutral, and appropriate for most professional email. The longer formula Veuillez agréer, Madame, Monsieur, mes salutations distinguées is more traditional and formal; either is acceptable in this chapter's tasks. Throughout the body, maintain vous and avoid informal contractions or vocabulary.
Madame, Monsieur, Je vous écris pour poser ma candidature au poste de… Cordialement, Karim Benali
Dear Sir or Madam, I am writing to apply for the position of… Sincerely, Karim Benali

13.2 Recycling the real-condition frame: si + présent + futur simple

Language Focus — Recycled from Chapter 11

You already control the real-condition frame from Chapter 11: si + présent + futur simple states an open condition the speaker treats as real or genuinely possible, and its result. This frame remains fully productive here for concrete professional and academic plans.

  • Si je réussis mon examen, je poursuivrai mes études l'année prochaine. If I pass my exam, I will continue my studies next year.
  • Si Karim postule cette semaine, il aura une réponse avant la fin du mois. If Karim applies this week, he will have an answer before the end of the month.

13.3 A new frame for imagined situations: si + imparfait + conditionnel

Building Block — A limited, controlled hypothetical frame
[si] + [imparfait] + [, conditionnel]
This frame states a condition the speaker presents as imagined, unlikely, or contrary to present fact, and what would happen as a result. The verb after si is in the imparfait (already fully productive since Chapter 2); the result verb is in the conditionnel (introduced in Chapter 9). This is different from the open, genuinely possible condition in 13.2, which the speaker presents as a real option rather than an imagined one. In this chapter, you are expected to produce this frame only in a small, controlled set of professional and academic contexts modeled here — describing an imagined job, an imagined set of circumstances, or an imagined choice. Free, wide-ranging production of hypothetical sentences on any topic is developed further in later chapters.
Si j'avais plus d'expérience, je postulerais pour ce poste. Si Nadia parlait mieux anglais, elle pourrait travailler à l'étranger.
If I had more experience, I would apply for this position. If Nadia spoke English better, she could work abroad.

Never put the conditionnel directly after si in this frame: *si j'aurais plus d'expérience is incorrect. The verb immediately after si is always in the imparfait here; the conditionnel appears only in the result clause. This chapter's tasks require only one or two hypothetical sentences built on this model, always about study or work; broader hypothetical topics and freer production are not assessed in this chapter.

Practice — Reconnaître le cadre correct

Decide whether each situation calls for the real-condition frame (si + présent + futur simple) or the hypothetical frame (si + imparfait + conditionnel), then complete the sentence.

  1. Karim a déjà vu l'offre d'emploi et pense vraiment postuler. Si Karim ___ (postuler) cette semaine, il ___ (avoir) une réponse rapidement.
  2. Nadia n'a pas encore fini ses études de langue, mais elle imagine la suite. Si Nadia ___ (parler) mieux anglais, elle ___ (pouvoir) travailler à l'étranger.
  3. Marc envisage vraiment de prendre un congé le mois prochain. Si Marc ___ (prendre) un congé, il ___ (se reposer) enfin.

Item 1 and item 3 describe genuinely possible, near-term plans (real condition); item 2 describes an imagined situation that is not currently true (hypothetical). Suggested answers appear in the Answer Key.

13.4 Asking a polite question formally: je voudrais savoir si, pourriez-vous me dire

Language Focus

You already form indirect questions with everyday politeness formulas (Chapter 7). This chapter recycles that pattern in a more formal register, useful for a professional email or a formal conversation with an employer.

  • Je voudrais savoir si le poste est à temps plein ou à temps partiel. I would like to know if the position is full-time or part-time.
  • Pourriez-vous me dire quelles compétences sont nécessaires pour ce poste ? Could you tell me what skills are necessary for this position?

Model Dialogue: Un entretien à préparer

Context: Karim has an interview coming up, and Nadia and Marc are helping him prepare. Register: informal, close friends, tu; the group also drafts formal lines Karim could send in an email.

Karim : J'ai un entretien vendredi pour le poste dont je vous ai parlé. Si ça se passe bien, j'aurai peut-être une réponse la semaine prochaine. I have an interview Friday for the position I told you about. If it goes well, I might have an answer next week.
Nadia : C'est une bonne expérience pour toi. Tu as déjà toutes les compétences nécessaires, non ? That's good experience for you. You already have all the necessary skills, right?
Karim : Presque. Si j'avais un peu plus d'expérience professionnelle, je serais plus confiant. Mais je pense que ça ira. Almost. If I had a little more work experience, I would be more confident. But I think it will be fine.
Marc : Tu devrais aussi envoyer un e-mail avant l'entretien pour confirmer l'heure. Comment tu vas commencer le message ? You should also send an email before the interview to confirm the time. How are you going to start the message?
Karim : Je pensais écrire : « Madame, Monsieur, Je vous écris pour confirmer notre entretien de vendredi. Je voudrais savoir si le poste est à temps plein. » I was thinking of writing: « Dear Sir or Madam, I am writing to confirm our interview on Friday. I would like to know if the position is full-time. »
Nadia : C'est bien formulé. Et tu peux terminer avec « Cordialement ». That's well phrased. And you can finish with « Cordialement ».
Marc : Et après l'entretien, si tu es embauché, qu'est-ce que tu feras pour tes études du soir ? And after the interview, if you get hired, what will you do about your evening classes?
Karim : Si je suis embauché, je continuerai mes études le soir, comme maintenant. Mais si le poste demandait trop d'heures, je devrais peut-être poursuivre mes études plus lentement. If I get hired, I will continue my studies in the evening, as now. But if the position required too many hours, I would perhaps have to continue my studies more slowly.
Nadia : On croise les doigts pour vendredi ! Fingers crossed for Friday!

This dialogue models the recycled real-condition frame (si ça se passe bien, j'aurai…; si je suis embauché, je continuerai…), the new hypothetical frame in controlled, professional contexts (si j'avais…, je serais…; si le poste demandait…, je devrais…), and a short formal email excerpt with correct opening, indirect question, and closing. No subjunctive form appears anywhere in this dialogue.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Formality in French professional communication

French professional communication, whether spoken or written, tends to maintain a clearly formal register with people one does not know well, including consistent use of vous, formal openings and closings, and a more careful vocabulary than everyday conversation. This formality is generally read as respectful and professional rather than distant. Specific hiring practices, application platforms, and workplace conventions vary and are not detailed here.

Regional Variation — Formal closings across the Francophone world

Formal closing formulas can vary somewhat in length and style across French-speaking regions and institutions; some prefer the shorter Cordialement, while others favor longer traditional formulas. Both closings introduced in this chapter are broadly understood and accepted in professional contexts throughout the Francophone world.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Using the conditionnel directly after si

Error: Saying *si j'aurais plus d'expérience, je postulerais.

Correction: The verb directly after si in the hypothetical frame stays in the imparfait; the conditionnel appears only in the result clause: si j'avais plus d'expérience, je postulerais.

Common Mistake — Confusing the two si frames

Error: Using the hypothetical frame for a plan that is genuinely likely, or the real-condition frame for something clearly imagined: *si je postulerais cette semaine, j'aurais une réponse.

Correction: Use si + présent + futur simple for an open, genuinely possible plan (si je postule cette semaine, j'aurai une réponse) and si + imparfait + conditionnel for an imagined or unlikely situation (si j'avais plus d'expérience, je postulerais pour un autre poste).

Common Mistake — Using tu in a formal email

Error: Writing *je voudrais savoir si tu embauches quelqu'un bientôt in a professional email to an employer.

Correction: Maintain vous throughout a formal message, even if you would use tu with the same person in another context: je voudrais savoir si vous embauchez quelqu'un bientôt.

Karim Benali

Dear Sir or Madam, I am writing to apply for the assistant position you advertised. I have good customer-service experience, and I already have the necessary skills for this type of position. I am always available on weekends, and I can also work certain evenings if necessary. If you accept my application, I will be able to start as early as next month. If I had more experience in this field, I might feel even more ready, but I remain very motivated for this position. I would like to know if an interview is possible next week. Sincerely, Karim Benali

The body paragraph contains six sentences, following the correctly formatted formal salutation (Madame, Monsieur,) and closing (Cordialement, Karim Benali), each set on its own line. The model demonstrates two skill/experience descriptions; the recycled real-condition frame (si vous acceptez ma candidature, je pourrai commencer…); the controlled hypothetical frame limited to a single professional idea, using genuine imparfait and conditionnel forms (si j'avais plus d'expérience…, je me sentirais…); a formal indirect question; and consistent vous register throughout, with no subjunctive form and no plus-que-parfait, which remains outside this volume's scope until Chapter 16. Other responses are acceptable when they meet all task requirements and remain within the grammar and vocabulary activated through Chapter 13.

Practical Performance Task — Model dialogue

Nadia : Alors, comment se prépare l'entretien ?
Karim : Bien, je pense. J'ai déjà de l'expérience et de bonnes compétences. Si l'entretien se passe bien, j'aurai une réponse rapidement.
Nadia : Et si tu avais un autre diplôme, tu postulerais pour un poste différent ?
Karim : Peut-être, mais ce poste me plaît déjà beaucoup. Au fait, je dois encore envoyer un e-mail formel. Je voudrais savoir si je dois apporter des documents.
Nadia : Bonne idée. Écris : « Pourriez-vous me dire quels documents je dois apporter à l'entretien ? »

This model demonstrates every required performance feature: two skill/experience references; the recycled real-condition frame (si l'entretien se passe bien, j'aurai une réponse); the controlled hypothetical frame (si tu avais un autre diplôme, tu postulerais…); two formal indirect questions (je voudrais savoir si…, pourriez-vous me dire…); and a consistent register appropriate to the friends' conversation, shifting to vous only inside the quoted formal email line. Other dialogues that meet the task's requirements are equally valid.

Practical Performance Task

Successful completion requires both components: a spoken exchange meeting every listed element, and a written email of six to ten sentences meeting every required element listed in the rubric above. Partial completion of either component does not meet the task's requirements.

Looking Ahead

Ce que vous savez faire maintenant — What you can do now

You can describe skills, experience, and study or work plans, write a short correctly formatted formal email, distinguish a real plan from an imagined possibility using two different si frames, and ask a polite question formally using je voudrais savoir si or pourriez-vous me dire.

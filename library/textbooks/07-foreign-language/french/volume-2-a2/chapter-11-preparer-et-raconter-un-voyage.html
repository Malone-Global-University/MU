Préparer et raconter un voyage

Travel Planning and Experience · Speak and Write French: Everyday Independence and Connected Communication

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Describe travel plans, reservations, and an itinerary using vocabulary for transportation and lodging.
  2. Form the futur simple productively for regular verbs and for the controlled set of irregular stems you already met in the conditionnel (Chapter 9).
  3. Use the futur simple to talk about plans, promises, and predictions, and distinguish it in use from aller + infinitive (Volume I) through direct practice.
  4. Make and confirm a reservation using je voudrais réserver, je réserverai, and related formulas.
  5. Use the fixed frame si + présent + futur simple to state a real condition and its likely result (for example, si tu réserves maintenant, tu auras un meilleur prix).
  6. Sequence a travel narrative using d’abord, ensuite, puis, and enfin.
  7. Recognize common futur simple forms in careful speech, and distinguish selected futur-simple and conditional forms where the pronunciation contrast is maintained.
  8. Read and write a short travel plan, and recount how a trip actually went using previously learned past tenses.

Communication Scenario

Unit III opens with Marc, Nadia, and Karim — all comfortably settled into their routines after the practical problems of Unit II — deciding to take a short trip together. Marc suggests Lyon for a long weekend; Nadia wants to know about trains and hotels before she commits to anything; Karim, always careful with money, wants to understand what will happen if they wait too long to book. The three of them move between two related kinds of talk: planning language, in which they discuss what will happen and what they will do, and later narration, in which one of them recounts how the trip actually went. This chapter teaches the grammar that makes both of these possible: the productive futur simple for planning, and a short recounting task that recycles the passé composé and imparfait you already control.

  1. Discuss travel plans, transportation, and lodging.
  2. Make and confirm a reservation.
  3. State a real condition and its likely result with si + présent + futur simple.
  4. Sequence a short account of a trip from beginning to end, both as a future plan and, briefly, as a completed narration.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Recognizing the futur simple by ear

The futur simple uses the same stem as the conditionnel (Chapter 9), followed by the endings -ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont. In careful standard pronunciation: -ai is commonly pronounced /e/; -as and -a are pronounced /a/; -ons and -ont contain the nasal vowel /ɔ̃/; and -ez is pronounced /e/.

The conditionnel endings -ais, -ait, -aient are commonly pronounced /ɛ/. In varieties that maintain the /e/–/ɛ/ contrast, je parlerai and je parlerais may therefore be distinguished as /paʁləʁe/ versus /paʁləʁɛ/.

This /e/–/ɛ/ contrast is reduced or merged in some Francophone varieties, so context, the subject pronoun, and the surrounding sentence may also be necessary to distinguish the futur simple from the conditionnel in ordinary connected speech.

Practice these forms aloud:

  • je réserverai
  • tu partiras
  • il arrivera
  • nous irons
  • vous aurez
  • ils viendront

In this chapter, you are expected to: pronounce common futur simple forms intelligibly, including a clear stem and the appropriate person ending; and recognize common futur-simple forms in careful speech, distinguishing selected futur-simple and conditional forms where the pronunciation contrast is maintained — particularly in the je, tu, and il/elle forms. This chapter does not require distinguishing every person of the futur simple from the conditionnel by ear in fast, unscripted speech; that fuller contrast continues to develop later in the volume.

Grammar: Demain, je partirai

Purpose: teach the productive formation of the futur simple; apply it to travel plans, promises, and predictions; introduce reservation formulas; and present the fixed real-condition frame si + présent + futur simple — without introducing the subjunctive or the full si + imparfait + conditionnel hypothesis system.

11.1 Forming the futur simple

Building Block — Same stem as the conditionnel, new endings
[infinitive-based or irregular conditional stem] + [-ai / -as / -a / -ons / -ez / -ont]
The futur simple uses exactly the same stem as the conditionnel you learned in Chapter 9. For regular -er and -ir verbs, the stem is the infinitive. For regular -re verbs, remove the final e. The irregular stems are identical to the ones you already memorized for the conditionnel — only the endings change.
réserver → je réserverai, tu réserveras, il/elle/on réservera, nous réserverons, vous réserverez, ils/elles réserveront
I will reserve, you will reserve, he/she/one will reserve, we will reserve, you will reserve, they will reserve

11.2 Using the futur simple: plans, promises, and predictions

Language Focus

You already know aller + infinitive (Volume I) for near, planned events: on va partir vendredi ("we're going to leave Friday"). The futur simple expresses the same basic idea of a future event, but it is commonly used for plans that are somewhat more distant, more formal, or presented as a firmer prediction or promise. In everyday conversation, both forms are correct and widely used; the choice is often about emphasis and formality rather than a strict rule.

  • On partira vendredi matin et on arrivera à Lyon vers midi. We will leave Friday morning and we will arrive in Lyon around noon.
  • Je réserverai les billets ce soir. I will book the tickets tonight.
  • Ne t'inquiète pas, je m'occuperai de l'hôtel. Don't worry, I will take care of the hotel. (a promise)
Practice — Choisir la forme future

Choose the form that best matches the stated context. Then explain whether the sentence presents an immediate, already-established plan or a future event presented as a prediction, promise, or more formal plan.

  1. Le train part dans dix minutes. Nous ___ à bord.
    a. allons monter    b. monterons
  2. Ne t'inquiète pas. Je ___ l'hôtel ce soir.
    a. vais réserver    b. réserverai
  3. L'année prochaine, nous ___ au Portugal.
    a. allons aller    b. irons
  4. Nous avons déjà les billets. Nous ___ vendredi matin.
    a. allons partir    b. partirons

More than one form may be natural in a given item; be ready to explain the difference in emphasis between an immediate established plan and a prediction, promise, or more formal plan. Suggested answers appear in the Answer Key.

11.3 Making a reservation

Building Block — Recycled and new reservation formulas
[je voudrais réserver] + [ce qu'on réserve]  |  [je réserverai] + [ce qu'on réservera]
Je voudrais réserver recycles the conditionnel formula from Chapter 7 and states a present wish. Je réserverai uses the new futur simple and states what will happen, often after a decision has already been made. Both are natural in travel planning; the second is common once the group has agreed on a plan.
Je voudrais réserver deux chambres doubles pour le week-end. D'accord, alors je réserverai l'hôtel ce soir et toi, tu réserveras les billets de train.
I would like to reserve two double rooms for the weekend. Okay, then I will book the hotel tonight and you will book the train tickets.

11.4 Sequencing a travel narrative

Language Focus

When you tell someone about a trip, use sequencing connectors to organize the account clearly. D'abord introduces the first step, ensuite and puis introduce following steps (they are largely interchangeable), and enfin introduces the last step. A time expression such as samedi matin can also organize a step without one of these four connectors; both strategies are natural and often appear together in the same account. These connectors work with any tense: planning language in the futur simple, or narration in the passé composé and imparfait (Volume I and Chapters 2–3).

  • D'abord, nous prendrons le train jusqu'à Lyon. Ensuite, nous déposerons nos valises à l'hôtel. Puis nous visiterons le centre-ville. Enfin, nous dînerons près de la gare. First, we will take the train to Lyon. Next, we will drop off our bags at the hotel. Then we will visit the city center. Finally, we will have dinner near the station.

11.5 Si + présent + futur simple: a real condition and its result

Building Block — A fixed frame for real, likely conditions
[si] + [présent] + [, futur simple]
This frame states a condition the speaker considers real and possible, and its likely result. The verb after si stays in the présent; the result verb goes in the futur simple. This is different from the hypothetical frame si + imparfait + conditionnel (not introduced in this volume), which presents an imagined or unlikely situation. In this chapter, only the real-condition frame with si + présent + futur simple is productive.
Si tu réserves maintenant, tu auras un meilleur prix. Si nous partons vendredi soir, nous éviterons la circulation.
If you book now, you will get a better price. If we leave Friday evening, we will avoid the traffic.

Never put the futur simple directly after si in this frame: *si tu réserveras is incorrect. The verb immediately after si is always in the présent here; the futur simple appears only in the result clause. This chapter productively teaches only si + présent + futur simple. Other common real-condition patterns, such as si + présent + impératif, are not required here and are not assessed in this chapter's tasks.

11.6 Recycling city and country prepositions

Language Note — Prepositions for destinations

This chapter recycles the prepositions you already control: à before a city (à Lyon), en before a feminine country (en France), and au/aux before a masculine or plural country (au Portugal, aux États-Unis). No new preposition rule is introduced in this chapter.

Model Dialogue: On organise le week-end

Context: Marc, Nadia, and Karim are planning a weekend trip to Lyon. Register: informal, close friends, tu.

Marc : Alors, qu'est-ce qu'on fait pour le week-end ? Si on part vendredi soir, on évitera la circulation du samedi matin. So, what are we doing for the weekend? If we leave Friday evening, we'll avoid Saturday morning traffic.
Nadia : Bonne idée. Je réserverai les billets de train ce soir, si vous êtes tous d'accord sur Lyon. Good idea. I'll book the train tickets tonight, if you're all in agreement on Lyon.
Karim : D'accord pour Lyon. Mais si on réserve maintenant, on aura un meilleur prix, non ? Les billets sont souvent plus chers à la dernière minute. Agreed on Lyon. But if we book now, we'll get a better price, right? Tickets are often more expensive at the last minute.
Nadia : Exactement. Je voudrais réserver trois places pour vendredi à 18 h. On arrivera à Lyon vers 20 h. Exactly. I'd like to reserve three seats for Friday at 6 PM. We'll arrive in Lyon around 8 PM.
Marc : Parfait. Je m'occuperai de l'hôtel. Je réserverai une chambre double et une chambre simple, si ça vous va. Perfect. I'll take care of the hotel. I'll book a double room and a single room, if that works for you.
Karim : Ça me va. Et qu'est-ce qu'on fera samedi ? Tu sais déjà ce que tu voudras visiter ? That works for me. And what will we do Saturday? Do you already know what you'll want to visit?
Marc : D'abord, nous visiterons le Vieux Lyon le matin. Ensuite, nous déjeunerons dans un bouchon lyonnais. Puis nous marcherons jusqu'aux quais. Enfin, nous rentrerons à l'hôtel avant le dîner. First, we'll visit Old Lyon in the morning. Then we'll have lunch in a traditional Lyon bistro. Then we'll walk to the riverbanks. Finally, we'll go back to the hotel before dinner.
Nadia : Et dimanche, on rentrera tôt ou tard ? And Sunday, will we go back early or late?
Karim : Si nous prenons le train de 16 h, nous arriverons à la maison avant 18 h. Ça te va, Marc ? If we take the 4 PM train, we'll get home before 6 PM. Does that work for you, Marc?
Marc : Parfait. Alors on est d'accord : Nadia réservera les billets, je réserverai l'hôtel, et vous deux, vous ferez les valises ! Perfect. So we're agreed: Nadia will book the tickets, I'll book the hotel, and you two, you'll pack the bags!

This dialogue models productive futur simple formation for regular and irregular verbs, reservation formulas (je voudrais réserver, je réserverai), the real-condition frame si + présent + futur simple, and the sequencing connectors d'abord, ensuite, puis, enfin. No subjunctive and no hypothetical si + imparfait + conditionnel form appears anywhere in this dialogue.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Booking train travel in France

In France, train travel between major cities is common and often booked online or through a station counter well before departure, since prices frequently rise closer to the travel date. Group travel, weekend getaways, and city-to-city day trips by train are a familiar part of everyday and student life. Specific booking platforms, pricing systems, and station procedures change over time and are not detailed here.

Regional Variation — Talking about future plans

Across the Francophone world, both aller + infinitive and the futur simple are used for future plans; the balance between them can vary by region, register, and personal style, and some speakers favor one over the other in casual conversation. Both forms taught in this chapter are broadly understood everywhere French is spoken.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Using the futur simple directly after si

Error: Saying *si tu réserveras, tu auras un meilleur prix.

Correction: The verb directly after si in a real-condition sentence stays in the présent; the futur simple appears only in the result clause: si tu réserves, tu auras un meilleur prix.

Common Mistake — Forgetting that the futur simple and conditionnel share a stem

Error: Inventing a new, incorrect stem for the futur simple instead of reusing the conditionnel stem from Chapter 9.

Correction: The stem is identical in both tenses: devoir → devr- gives both je devrais (conditionnel) and je devrai (futur simple). Only the endings differ.

Common Mistake — Confusing d'abord/ensuite/puis/enfin with time expressions

Error: Using d'abord or enfin to mean a specific clock time.

Correction: These words sequence steps in an account; they do not name a time. Use them alongside, not instead of, actual times or dates when precision is needed: d'abord, nous partirons à 18 h, not *d'abord à 18 h alone. A time expression such as samedi matin may also organize a step on its own, without one of the four connectors.

Practice — Choisir la forme future

  1. a. allons monter is the most natural default because the train is leaving in ten minutes and the action is imminent and already established. Nous monterons à bord is also grammatically possible if the speaker presents boarding as a simple future fact or firm prediction.
  2. b. réserverai: this is a reassurance or promise ("don't worry, I will take care of it"), which the futur simple expresses naturally. Je vais réserver is also acceptable but sounds slightly less like a firm promise.
  3. b. irons is concise and natural for a plan presented as a general future event. Nous allons aller au Portugal is also grammatical and expresses a near or already developing plan, although some speakers may prefer nous allons au Portugal in contexts where the future meaning is already clear.
  4. a. allons partir or b. partirons are both acceptable: the tickets are already bought, which supports an established, near plan (allons partir), but partirons is equally natural as a simple statement of fact about Friday morning.

The distinction between aller + infinitive and the futur simple is not absolute. Aller + infinitive often emphasizes an imminent or already arranged event; the futur simple often presents the event as a prediction, promise, formal plan, or simple future fact. Both may be grammatically possible in the same context.

Listening Discrimination — Futur simple ou conditionnel ?

  1. futur simple (réserverai)
  2. conditionnel (réserverais, with the contextual clue à votre place)
  3. futur simple (partira)
  4. conditionnel (partirait, with the contextual clue avec plus de temps)
  5. futur simple (irons)
  6. conditionnel (irions, with the contextual clue à votre place)

Where the final-vowel contrast is not strongly audible in a given speaker's pronunciation, the surrounding context — such as à votre place or avec plus de temps — remains the most reliable clue.

  3. Writing Workshop — Model future plan

Modèle — Model travel plan

Le mois prochain, je partirai en voyage avec des amis. Nous irons à Lyon pour un long week-end. Je réserverai les billets de train cette semaine, car ils seront peut-être plus chers plus tard. Si nous réservons maintenant, nous aurons un meilleur prix. D'abord, nous prendrons le train vendredi soir. Ensuite, nous déposerons nos valises à l'hôtel. Puis nous visiterons le centre-ville samedi matin. Enfin, nous rentrerons dimanche après-midi. Mon ami s'occupera de l'hôtel, et moi, je m'occuperai des billets. Si tout se passe bien, ce sera un excellent week-end.

Next month, I will go on a trip with friends. We will go to Lyon for a long weekend. I will book the train tickets this week, because they may be more expensive later. If we book now, we will get a better price. First, we will take the train Friday evening. Next, we will drop off our bags at the hotel. Then we will visit the city center Saturday morning. Finally, we will return Sunday afternoon. My friend will take care of the hotel, and I will take care of the tickets. If everything goes well, it will be an excellent weekend.

Writing Workshop — Model recounting extension

Modèle — Le voyage après le retour

Le week-end suivant, nous sommes allés à Lyon. Il faisait beau quand nous sommes arrivés. D'abord, nous avons déposé nos valises à l'hôtel. Ensuite, nous avons visité le Vieux Lyon. Nous voulions marcher le long du Rhône, mais nous étions fatigués. Enfin, nous sommes rentrés dimanche après-midi.

The following weekend, we went to Lyon. The weather was nice when we arrived. First, we dropped off our bags at the hotel. Next, we visited Old Lyon. We wanted to walk along the Rhône, but we were tired. Finally, we went back Sunday afternoon.

The future plan contains ten complete sentences and demonstrates futur simple formation for regular verbs and for the irregular stems in nous irons (aller), nous aurons (avoir), and ce sera (être); a reservation reference (je réserverai les billets); the real-condition frame; all four sequencing connectors in order; correct destination prepositions; and no subjunctive form. The recounting extension adds six sentences narrating the completed trip with two passé composé verbs or more, one imparfait form (il faisait beau, nous étions fatigués), two sequencing connectors or more, and a sentence comparing the plan with what actually happened (nous voulions marcher le long du Rhône, mais nous étions fatigués). Other responses are acceptable when they meet all task requirements and remain within the grammar and vocabulary activated through Chapter 11.

Practical Performance Task — Model dialogue

Karim : On part toujours vendredi ?
Nadia : Oui. Je voudrais réserver les billets aujourd'hui. Est-ce que tu es d'accord ?
Karim : D'accord. Si tu réserves aujourd'hui, on aura un meilleur prix.
Nadia : Exactement. Je réserverai deux places pour vendredi soir.
Karim : Et pour samedi, qu'est-ce qu'on fera ?
Nadia : D'abord, nous visiterons le centre-ville. Ensuite, nous déjeunerons près de la gare. Puis nous nous reposerons un peu. Enfin, nous dînerons tous ensemble.
Karim : Ça me va. On ira aussi au Portugal l'année prochaine ?
Nadia : Peut-être ! Nous en parlerons après ce voyage-ci.
Karim : D'accord. Alors, comment s'est passé le dernier voyage, en fait ? Vous êtes allés à la montagne, non ?
Nadia : Oui ! Nous sommes partis vendredi soir. Il faisait très froid, mais c'était magnifique. D'abord, nous avons marché toute la matinée. Ensuite, nous avons déjeuné dans un petit village. Nous voulions rester une nuit de plus, mais nous devions rentrer pour le travail. Enfin, nous sommes rentrés dimanche soir, un peu fatigués mais contents.

This model demonstrates every required performance feature: futur simple forms including at least one irregular stem (on aura); a reservation formula (je réserverai); the real-condition frame (si tu réserves aujourd'hui, on aura un meilleur prix); a recycled direct question rather than a non-target si clause (est-ce que tu es d'accord ?); all four sequencing connectors in order for the future plan; a correct destination preposition (au Portugal); a genuine follow-up question and connected answer; consistent tu register; and a short recounting of a past trip using the passé composé and imparfait, with sequencing connectors and a sentence comparing the original wish with what actually happened (nous voulions rester une nuit de plus, mais nous devions rentrer pour le travail). Other dialogues that meet the task's requirements are equally valid.

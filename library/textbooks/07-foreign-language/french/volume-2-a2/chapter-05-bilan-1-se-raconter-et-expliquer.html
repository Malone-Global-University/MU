Bilan 1 : Se raconter et expliquer

Unit I Assessment · Speak and Write French: Everyday Independence and Connected Communication

Chapter Objectives

This chapter introduces no new grammar, pronunciation, orthography, or vocabulary system. By its end, you will have demonstrated that you can:

  1. Reconnect naturally, react to news, and repair a misunderstanding in real time, as developed in Chapter 1.
  2. Narrate a connected personal history, combining passé composé and imparfait and marking a clear turning point, as developed in Chapter 2.
  3. Contrast a past habit with a present one using ne ... plus, and state a near-future plan with aller + infinitif and dans + duration, as developed in Chapter 3.
  4. Choose correct direct- and indirect-object pronouns, use a controlled double object pronoun sequence, describe reciprocal contact, and resolve a misunderstanding politely, as developed in Chapter 4.
  5. Combine all four chapters' language in a single connected conversation, message, and account, without needing new grammar to do so.
  6. Listen to and read longer, integrated passages that combine reconnecting, narration, habits, plans, and relationship language.
  7. Produce a sustained spoken and written performance that a listener or reader could follow from beginning to end.
  8. Correctly review and apply depuis, pendant, and il y a to situate actions and states in time.
  9. Distinguish, in your own performance, which Unit I vocabulary is productive and must be used, which is formulaic and may be used as a fixed expression, and which is receptive and need only be understood.
  10. Identify one Unit I system you control confidently and one that still needs practice before Unit II.

Communication Scenario

A month has passed since Nadia's move to Tours. As promised back in Chapter 4, Nadia writes to Karim to organize the group video call she mentioned, and the four friends — Marc, Nadia, Léa, and Karim — finally manage to get on a call together, all at the same time, for the first time since Volume II began. There is a lot to catch up on: Marc's life in Bordeaux, Nadia's new apartment and job in Tours, Léa and Karim's news, and everyone's habits, changes, and plans for the months ahead. As with any group call involving four people and three different cities, a small mix-up about a detail comes up and has to be sorted out. This chapter asks you to bring together everything from Unit I — reconnecting, telling a connected story, contrasting before and now, stating plans, referring to people and things without repeating their names, and repairing a misunderstanding — in one extended, natural conversation, and in a piece of connected writing that follows it.

  1. Open a group reunion naturally and catch several people up at once.
  2. Tell a connected piece of your own recent story, with clear background and events.
  3. Contrast a habit that has changed and state a concrete plan.
  4. Refer back to people and things accurately without repeating every name.
  5. Notice and repair a misunderstanding calmly and politely.
  6. Write a connected, organized account that a reader unfamiliar with the call could follow.

Language Focus — Review: depuis, pendant, il y a

Three time expressions from Chapters 1–2 are essential to this chapter's tasks. Depuis connects a past starting point to the present, describing a state that continues now. Pendant gives the duration of a completed situation or event, with no necessary connection to the present. Il y a locates a completed event a stated amount of time before now.

  • J'habite à Tours depuis un mois. I have been living in Tours for a month, and I still do.
  • J'ai travaillé à Lyon pendant trois ans. I worked in Lyon for three years, a completed period.
  • J'ai déménagé il y a un mois. I moved a month ago.

This chapter introduces no new time-expression grammar; it reviews these three systems together so that you can choose among them accurately.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Consolidating Unit I

This chapter introduces no new pronunciation system. It asks you to recognize, and to control at natural conversational speed across a longer stretch of speech, the systems built in Chapters 1–4:

  • From Chapter 1: schwa reduction, the informal omission of ne in casual speech, informal contractions such as t'as, and rhythm-group prominence on the final syllable of a phrase. These informal reductions are recognition targets; you may use them where a clearly informal register is appropriate, but full neutral forms are always correct.
  • From Chapter 2: the shared /ɛ/ sound of the written imparfait endings -ais, -ait, and -aient, and using context rather than the sound of il / ils to identify a singular or plural subject before a consonant.
  • From Chapter 3: the position-sensitive pronunciation of plus — /ply/, /plys/, or liaised /plyz/ — depending on meaning and phonetic context.
  • From Chapter 4: elision of me, te, se, le, and la before a vowel-initial verb; liaison (without elision) for nous and vous; smooth rhythm across double-pronoun sequences; and the natural rise of a clarification question against the natural, calm fall of a reassurance.

Enchaînement, reviewed. In enchaînement, a consonant that is already pronounced at the end of one word simply moves to begin the following syllable before a vowel, without creating any new liaison consonant. This is different from liaison, where a normally silent written consonant becomes pronounced only because a vowel follows. A familiar example is avec elle, pronounced avec‿elle: the c is always pronounced in avec, so linking it to the following vowel is enchaînement, not liaison.

In this chapter, you are expected to speak with intelligible rhythm, familiar liaison and enchaînement, and accurate repair intonation. Informal reductions such as the omission of ne or contractions like t'as are optional: use them only where an informal register is clearly appropriate. Full neutral forms such as je ne sais pas or tu as are always correct and are never penalized.

Grammar: Bilan des systèmes

Purpose: this section introduces no new grammar. It maps, in one place, the systems from Chapters 1–4 that this chapter's tasks require you to combine, so that you can review with intention before attempting the integrated practice and performance sections.

5.1 What each chapter contributed

Chapter 1

A2 verb systems recycled at conversational speed; conversational repair (clarification, repetition, rephrasing); informal spoken omission of ne.

Chapter 2

Passé composé / imparfait working together in one narration; sequencing connectors; identifying a turning point; recycled past-participle agreement with être.

Chapter 3

Ne ... plus as a new negation family member; three time frames (imparfait / présent / aller + infinitif) in one passage; dans + duration.

Chapter 4

Direct vs. indirect object pronouns; reciprocal verbs; controlled double object pronouns; pronoun placement before an infinitive; promettre (à qqn) de; clarification, correction, and reassurance formulas.

5.2 Combining the systems in one passage

Language Focus

A single well-organized account of recent life — spoken or written — can now move through all four chapters' systems in a natural order: an opening reaction (Ch. 1), a piece of connected personal narration with a turning point (Ch. 2), a contrast between a past habit and a present one plus a near-future plan (Ch. 3), and a reference back to people and things, possibly including a small misunderstanding and its repair (Ch. 4). None of this requires new grammar; it requires deliberately sequencing what you already control. When a pronoun is used, its noun should already be established, either earlier in the same sentence or in a previous one.

  • Ça alors, ça fait longtemps ! Alors, moi, d'abord, j'ai déménagé à Tours, et ensuite, j'ai commencé mon nouveau travail. Ch. 1 (reaction) + Ch. 2 (sequencing, passé composé)
  • Avant, je ne connaissais personne ici, mais maintenant, je ne me sens plus seule : je vais bientôt rejoindre un club de sport. Ch. 2 (imparfait background) + Ch. 3 (ne...plus, aller + infinitif)
  • J'ai enfin les photos de l'appartement. Je vous les envoie cette semaine, je vous le promets ! Ch. 4 (noun established, then a double object pronoun before the conjugated verb, plus a separate promise formula)

5.3 What this chapter does not add

Language Note — Boundaries of this Bilan

This chapter does not introduce the plus-que-parfait, the futur simple, the conditional, the subjunctive, new clitic combinations such as le lui, la lui, or les lui, or past-participle agreement with a preceding direct object. Those systems remain scheduled for later in the volume. If a task in this chapter seems to invite one of these forms, use the Unit I systems you already control instead — for example, the passé composé alone rather than the plus-que-parfait, aller + infinitif rather than the futur simple, or a repeated name rather than a combined lui + le. This chapter also reviews depuis, pendant, il y a, and familiar être-agreement, without introducing any new time-expression or agreement rule.

Model Dialogue: Le premier appel à quatre

Context: Marc, Nadia, Léa, and Karim finally connect on a group video call, a month after Nadia's move to Tours. Register: informal, close friends, tu.

Karim : Ça alors, on est enfin tous les quatre en même temps ! Ça fait un bail, non ? No way, we're finally all four at the same time! It's been ages, right?
Nadia : Carrément ! Bon, moi, je commence : d'abord, j'ai déménagé à Tours il y a un mois. Au début, je ne connaissais personne, et à l'époque, je me sentais un peu seule. Mais maintenant, je ne me sens plus seule du tout : j'ai rencontré des gens sympas au travail. Totally! Okay, I'll start: first, I moved to Tours a month ago. At first, I didn't know anyone, and at the time, I felt a bit lonely. But now, I no longer feel lonely at all: I've met some nice people at work.
Marc : Ça, c'est un vrai tournant ! Et tu as l'intention de rester longtemps à Tours ? Now that's a real turning point! And do you intend to stay in Tours a long time?
Nadia : Oui, je compte y rester au moins deux ans. Au fait, j'ai enfin les photos de l'appartement : je vous les envoie cette semaine. Je vous le promets, cette fois ! Yes, I plan to stay at least two years. By the way, I finally have the apartment photos: I'm sending them to you this week. I promise, this time!
Léa : Pardon, tu as dit cette semaine ou la semaine prochaine ? Je n'ai pas bien compris, à cause du bruit. Sorry, did you say this week or next week? I didn't quite understand, because of the noise.
Nadia : Ah pardon, je voulais dire cette semaine ! Désolée, ce n'était pas clair. Oh sorry, I meant this week! Sorry, that wasn't clear.
Léa : Ce n'est pas grave, merci de préciser ! Et toi, Marc, du coup, ça se passe comment, Bordeaux ? No worries, thanks for clarifying! And you, Marc, so, how's Bordeaux going?
Marc : Très bien ! Avant, je prenais toujours la voiture, mais maintenant, je ne la prends plus : je vais au bureau à vélo. Et je me suis inscrit dans un club de course, en plus. Very well! Before, I always took the car, but now I don't take it anymore: I go to the office by bike. And I joined a running club too.
Karim : Toi, à vélo, franchement, je n'y crois pas ! Bon, écoutez, on se téléphone toutes les deux semaines, à partir de maintenant ? Comme ça, on garde vraiment le contact, tous les quatre. You, on a bike, honestly, I don't believe it! Okay, listen, shall we call each other every two weeks, starting now? That way, we really stay in touch, all four of us.
Tous : Ça marche ! Deal!

This dialogue moves through every Unit I system without introducing anything new: a reunion reaction and repair sequence (Ch. 1), a connected narration with imparfait background, passé composé events, and a marked turning point (Ch. 2), a past-habit/present-habit contrast with ne ... plus and near-future plans (Ch. 3), and reciprocal verbs, a double object pronoun before the conjugated verb with its noun already established, and promettre (Ch. 4).

Speaking Practice

Pratique intégrée — Integrated review: quatre systèmes, une réponse

With a partner, take turns responding to each prompt using the Unit I system indicated. This is controlled review, not new material.

Item 1 — Chapitre 1

Your partner says: J'ai déménagé. React with surprise, then ask a catch-up question.

Item 2 — Chapitre 2

Tell one imparfait background sentence and one passé composé event about your own life, connected by et puis.

Item 3 — Chapitre 3

Say one thing you no longer do, using ne ... plus, and one thing you plan to do, using aller + infinitif.

Item 4 — Chapitre 4

Promise your partner that you will send them the photos. Use te les for the double object pronoun sequence, then add a separate promise formula.

Modèle : Je vais te les envoyer demain, je te le promets.

Autre forme possible : Je te promets de te les envoyer demain.

In Item 4, te les is the required double object pronoun sequence, and je te le promets is a separate fixed promise expression. Do not combine them into a form such as *je te le promets de....

Tâche communicative — Communicative task: l'appel à plusieurs

In a group of three or four (or in pairs, each person taking two roles), hold a group catch-up conversation modeled on the Model Dialogue. Your conversation must include, at minimum:

  • an opening reunion reaction and at least one catch-up question;
  • at least one connected personal narration combining imparfait background, passé composé events, and one clearly marked turning point;
  • at least one contrast between a past habit and a present one using ne ... plus, and one near-future plan using aller + infinitif or avoir l'intention de;
  • at least one reciprocal verb describing how the group will stay in touch;
  • at least one single direct- or indirect-object pronoun and one controlled double object pronoun sequence;
  • one genuine misunderstanding, fully repaired: clarification question, correction, apology or reassurance, and final confirmation;
  • appropriate informal register throughout.

These features may be distributed across the group: no single speaker needs to produce every element alone, but the conversation as a whole must include all of them, and every participant should contribute meaningfully across several systems.

Do not read from a script; speak from the accumulated vocabulary and patterns of Chapters 1–4.

Pratique individuelle — Solo alternative

Record yourself performing all sides of an imagined group catch-up call (playing at least two characters, or narrating with imagined responses). You may prepare a brief outline of the six required elements below in advance, but do not read from a full script. Your recording must include, in order:

  1. a reunion opening and catch-up question;
  2. a connected personal narration with background, events, and a turning point;
  3. a past-habit/present contrast and a near-future plan;
  4. a reference to another person or object using an appropriate pronoun, including one double object pronoun sequence;
  5. an imagined misunderstanding and its full repair;
  6. a closing agreement about staying in touch.

A recording missing any one of the six elements above does not meet this task's requirements.

Writing Workshop

Complete the following linked writing task, which brings together every Unit I system in one connected account.

Tâche unique — Le bilan du mois: Write a connected account of twelve to sixteen sentences, addressed to the group of friends, summarizing your own recent month (real or invented). Your account must include:

  • an opening that reacts to reconnecting with the group and asks at least one catch-up question;
  • a connected personal narration combining imparfait background and at least three passé composé events, organized with sequencing connectors, with one clearly marked turning point;
  • at least one accurate use of depuis, pendant, or il y a to situate an event or state in time;
  • at least one sentence using ne ... plus to mark a habit that has changed, and at least one near-future plan using aller + infinitif or avoir l'intention de, including dans or à partir de + a time expression;
  • at least one reciprocal verb describing how you plan to stay in touch with the group;
  • at least one single object pronoun and one controlled double object pronoun sequence referring back to a person or thing already established in the text;
  • one self-correction of a detail you stated incorrectly, using an appropriate repair formula;
  • a closing sentence connecting your account to the group's next call.

This is neutral, connected writing; retain full standard negation with ne throughout, as in the Reading Practice thread above, except within a clearly labeled quoted informal exchange.

A single well-formed sentence may satisfy more than one requirement above. For example, Avant, j'habitais avec mes parents, mais j'ai déménagé il y a un mois, et ce changement a été un vrai tournant. provides imparfait background, a passé composé event, il y a, and turning-point language all at once. You do not need a separate sentence for every checklist item.

  1. Plan your account. List the month's key stages: background, three or more events, one turning point, one habit change, one plan, and one detail to correct.
  2. Draft your opening. React to reconnecting and ask a catch-up question.
  3. Draft your narration. Combine imparfait background and passé composé events with sequencing connectors, and at least one of depuis, pendant, or il y a, marking the turning point clearly.
  4. Draft your contrast and plan. Use ne ... plus for the habit and aller + infinitif or avoir l'intention de for the plan.
  5. Draft your references and repair. Establish a noun, then refer back to it with appropriate pronouns, including one double object sequence, and correct one detail you stated incorrectly.
  6. Draft your closing. Connect your account to the group's next call.
  7. Revise. Confirm tense choice, negation, pronoun choice and placement, agreement, accents, and punctuation throughout.

Liste de révision — Editing checklist:

  • The account contains twelve to sixteen sentences.
  • The opening reacts to reconnecting and includes a catch-up question.
  • At least one imparfait sentence sets the background.
  • At least three passé composé sentences report completed events, organized with connectors.
  • A turning point is clearly identifiable.
  • At least one of depuis, pendant, or il y a is used accurately.
  • At least one sentence uses ne ... plus correctly.
  • At least one near-future plan uses aller + infinitif or avoir l'intention de, with a time expression.
  • At least one reciprocal verb describes staying in touch.
  • At least one single object pronoun and one double object pronoun sequence are used correctly, with the noun already established.
  • One self-correction of a detail stated incorrectly is included, using an appropriate repair formula.
  • The account closes by connecting to the group's next call.
  • Standard negation retains ne throughout, except in a clearly labeled informal quotation.
  • Articles, agreement, verb forms, accents, apostrophes, and punctuation are accurate.
  • All language comes from Volume I and Volume II Chapters 1–4; no new grammar or vocabulary is introduced.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Group catch-ups among friends

A group video call or group chat, as in this chapter, is a common way for a friend group to stay connected once its members are spread across different cities, mixing personal updates, photos, and logistics in the same exchange. As in Chapters 1 and 4, misunderstandings about a day, a time, or a detail are treated lightly among friends, resolved with a brief acknowledgment rather than a lengthy apology. This chapter does not introduce any new cultural content; it draws together the register and pragmatic points already presented in Chapters 1–4.

Regional Variation — A reminder

As noted throughout Unit I, the productive standard in this course is contemporary, broadly understood French based primarily on educated metropolitan usage in France. Informal items presented for recognition only in earlier chapters, such as certain regional fillers, remain receptive; they are not required, and need not be produced, in this chapter's assessed tasks.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Reaching for new grammar under assessment pressure

Error: Attempting to use the plus-que-parfait, the futur simple, the subjunctive, or a combined le lui pronoun sequence because the task feels demanding.

Correction: Every task in this chapter can be completed fully with Unit I systems: use the passé composé alone rather than the plus-que-parfait, aller + infinitif for the future, avoid subjunctive triggers, and repeat a name rather than combining lui with le, la, or les, exactly as modeled in the Speaking Practice above.

Common Mistake — Listing events without background or a turning point

Error: Writing a string of passé composé sentences with no imparfait background and no clearly marked turning point, as if narration were only a list of completed actions.

Correction: As in Chapter 2, a connected narration needs both jobs: the imparfait sets the scene, and the passé composé reports what happened, with one moment clearly marked as the turning point, as in Avant, je ne connaissais personne ici. Puis, j'ai rencontré mes nouveaux collègues, et cela a été un vrai tournant.

Common Mistake — Confusing ne ... jamais with ne ... plus under time pressure

Error: Saying je ne fais jamais de sport to mean "I used to do sports, but I stopped."

Correction: As in Chapter 3, use ne ... plus when a habit has ended: je ne fais plus de sport. Reserve ne ... jamais for "never," with no implied earlier habit.

Common Mistake — Skipping the repair sequence entirely

Error: Stating a misunderstood or incorrect detail and simply restating the correct version, without a clarification question, an acknowledgment, or a final confirmation.

Correction: As in Chapters 1 and 4, a complete repair sequence includes a clarification request, a correction, an apology or reassurance, and a final confirmation, as modeled throughout this chapter's dialogue and reading passage. In a solo, written self-correction, an imagined listener's reassurance is not required; a direct self-correction such as Pardon, je me suis trompée : je voulais dire dix-huit heures is sufficient.

Controlled Speaking — Item 4 model answers

Acceptable models: Je vais te les envoyer demain, je te le promets. or Je te promets de te les envoyer demain. The double object pronoun sequence te les and the fixed expression je te le promets are not combined into a single form such as *je te le promets de....

Pronunciation and Orthography Review Exercise

  1. The imparfait ending in habitaient is -aient, pronounced /ɛ/.
  2. The past participle is arrivés, formed with the auxiliary être.
  3. Arrivés agrees with the plural subject ils.
  4. An example of enchaînement or liaison: the liaison in ils sont arrivés before the vowel-initial participle.
  5. In ils ne prennent plus la voiture, negative plus is pronounced /ply/, with a silent final s, because it comes before the consonant-initial la.
  6. The elided clitic spelling s'écrivent correctly represents se + écrivent, with se shortened to s' before the vowel-initial verb écrivent.
  7. Capitalization begins each sentence, and each sentence ends with correct punctuation.

Listening Practice

  1. Before, Karim lived with two roommates and found it difficult at times; he explicitly says that now, living alone, he feels much better and more at ease (je me sens beaucoup mieux et plus tranquille).
  2. Imparfait (background): avant, j'habitais avec deux colocataires. Passé composé (event): j'ai trouvé un petit studio.
  3. Karim has given up coming home late to avoid his roommates; now he comes home whenever he wants.
  4. Karim plans to start doing sports again, starting next month.
  5. Karim corrects the studio timing from il y a deux mois to il y a trois semaines, using je crois que j'ai dit ... mais je voulais dire ....

Reading Practice

  1. Léa's old company closed overnight; she had to start job-hunting again and landed a new position last week.
  2. Imparfait: je travaillais dans un petit bureau au centre-ville. Passé composé: l'entreprise a fermé / j'ai décroché un nouveau poste.
  3. Starting Monday, Léa will no longer look for work; instead, she will finally start her new position (son nouveau poste).
  4. Nadia needs to correct the time she gave Marc (nineteen hours) to the correct time for everyone (eighteen hours); she does this with je me suis trompée : je voulais dire dix-huit heures.
  5. je te la confirme: te refers to Karim, and la refers to l'heure (the time of the call).
  6. Model summary: The group agrees to meet for their next call on Sunday at six in the evening (eighteen hours), after Nadia corrects an earlier mistaken time. Other accurate summaries in the learner's own words are acceptable.

Chapter Review — Part E

Nadia corrects the time she previously stated incorrectly in Pardon, j'ai dit dix-neuf heures à Marc tout à l'heure, mais je me suis trompée : je voulais dire dix-huit heures pour tout le monde, using the correction formula je me suis trompée, je voulais dire.... Unlike the Model Dialogue, where Léa misunderstands Nadia and asks for clarification, this is Nadia independently correcting her own earlier statement.

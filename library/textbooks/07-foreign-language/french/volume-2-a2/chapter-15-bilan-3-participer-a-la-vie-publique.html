Bilan 3 : Participer à la vie publique

Unit III Assessment · Speak and Write French: Everyday Independence and Connected Communication
Assessment chapter. This chapter introduces no new productive grammar, pronunciation, orthography, vocabulary-domain, or register system. It consolidates and assesses the language activated in Chapters 11–14, drawing on travel, media, work, and opinion vocabulary and grammar already taught, per the Volume II matrices for Chapter 15.

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Talk about travel plans, media and cultural life, study or work projects, and personal opinions in a single connected conversation or text.
  2. Use the real-condition frame si + présent + futur simple (Chapter 11) and the hypothetical frame si + imparfait + conditionnel (Chapter 13) correctly and appropriately in the same exchange.
  3. Write and recognize a correctly formatted formal message, including a formal indirect question (Chapter 13).
  4. State, support, and defend a simple opinion using opinion formulas, reason and result connectors, and the concession frame même si (Chapter 14).
  5. Report simple information from a media source using reported information and opinion support (Chapter 12).
  6. Read an extended text that integrates travel, media, professional, and opinion content, and answer questions about its content and structure.
  7. Write a connected, multi-paragraph text that combines narration, a real plan, an imagined possibility, and a supported opinion.
  8. Complete an integrated speaking task without relying on any grammar, vocabulary, or system beyond Volume I and Volume II Chapters 1–15.

Communication Scenario

It has been a busy few weeks for Karim, Nadia, and Marc. Karim had his job interview; Nadia has been reading about a documentary that got her thinking about her own studies; and Marc has been planning a short trip. Meeting up again, the three catch up on everything at once — travel, news, work, and, inevitably, a friendly disagreement about all of it. This chapter does not teach anything new. Instead, it asks you to bring together everything from Unit III: talking about a trip, reporting on something you read or heard, describing study or work plans in both real and imagined terms, writing a formal message, and stating and defending an opinion — all in the same connected conversation and the same connected piece of writing.

  1. Narrate travel plans and past travel experience.
  2. Report information from a media source.
  3. Describe a real professional or academic plan and an imagined alternative.
  4. State, support, and defend an opinion, including a concession.

Pronunciation Review

Pronunciation Review — Distinguishing forms by stem, not ending

Unit III brought two pronunciation habits together that this chapter continues to require. First, from Chapter 13: the imparfait and the conditionnel share written endings (-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient), so si j'avais and je serais are distinguished by stem and context, not by ending. Second, from Chapter 14: opinion formulas and connectors — je pense que, je trouve que, car, donc, alors, même si — link smoothly to the clause that follows, without an unnatural pause.

Practice these forms aloud, listening for stem and connected rhythm:

  • si je réussissais, je postulerais (hypothetical, stem-based distinction)
  • je pense que le reportage était intéressant (linked opinion clause)
  • même si c'est loin, j'ai quand même réservé un billet (concession, linked smoothly in one breath group)

No new pronunciation feature is introduced in this chapter. You are expected to apply, together, everything practiced in Chapters 11–14: liaison and rhythm in travel narration, careful pronunciation of the futur simple, the imparfait/conditionnel stem contrast, and smooth linking of opinion formulas and connectors.

Grammar Consolidation: Unit III

Purpose: consolidate, without introducing anything new, the grammar systems developed across Chapters 11–14, and practice combining them within a single connected task, as required for the Unit III assessment.

15.1 The two si frames, side by side

Review — Chapters 11 and 13

You control two different si frames, and this chapter asks you to choose correctly between them in context, exactly as in Chapters 11–14.

  • Si je pars en juillet, je réserverai un vol tôt. If I leave in July, I will book a flight early. (open, real plan — présent + futur simple)
  • Si j'avais plus de temps libre en ce moment, je suivrais un cours en ligne supplémentaire. If I had more free time right now, I would take an extra online course. (imagined possibility — imparfait + conditionnel, kept within the study/work domain controlled since Chapter 13)

No new frame is introduced. The skill assessed here is choosing correctly between the two according to whether the situation is genuinely open or merely imagined.

15.2 Reporting information and supporting an opinion together

Review — Chapters 12 and 14, combined
[selon / d'après + source] + [information] … [je pense que / à mon avis] + [opinion] + [parce que / car] + [reason]
Chapter 12 taught you to introduce information from a source with selon or d'après. Chapter 14 taught you to state and support an opinion. This chapter asks you to move naturally between the two: report what a source says, then react to it with your own opinion, a reason, and, where appropriate, a concession with même si.
Selon le reportage, de plus en plus de gens travaillent de chez eux. À mon avis, c'est une bonne chose, car on gagne du temps, même si on parle moins à ses collègues.
According to the report, more and more people work from home. In my opinion, that's a good thing, because you save time, even though you talk less to your colleagues.

15.3 Formal register in context

Review — Chapter 13

The formal email conventions and indirect question formulas from Chapter 13 (Madame, / Monsieur, … Cordialement,; je voudrais savoir si; pourriez-vous me dire) remain fully productive and are assessed again here, now alongside the informal tu conversation used with friends. Maintaining the correct register for each context, without mixing them, is part of what this chapter assesses.

Model Dialogue: Trois nouvelles à partager

Context: Marc, Nadia, and Karim meet up after a few busy weeks. Register: informal, close friends, tu, with one quoted line from a formal email.

Marc : Alors, j'ai enfin réservé mon voyage. Si tout se passe bien, je partirai le mois prochain. So, I've finally booked my trip. If all goes well, I'll leave next month.
Nadia : Génial ! Et si le vol est un peu plus cher que prévu, ça ira quand même ? Great! And if the flight ends up a bit more expensive than planned, will that still be all right?
Marc : Oui, ça ira. J'ai un peu d'argent de côté pour ça. Et toi, Nadia, quoi de neuf ? Yes, it'll be fine. I've put a bit of money aside for that. And you, Nadia, what's new?
Nadia : J'ai vu un reportage intéressant hier soir. Selon le reportage, de plus en plus de gens poursuivent leurs études en ligne. À mon avis, c'est une bonne idée, car c'est plus flexible. Si je suivais un cours en ligne, moi aussi, je gagnerais du temps le matin. I saw an interesting report last night. According to the report, more and more people are continuing their studies online. In my opinion, it's a good idea, because it's more flexible. If I took an online course too, I would save time in the morning.
Karim : Je ne suis pas totalement d'accord. Même si c'est flexible, on parle moins aux autres étudiants, donc c'est parfois plus difficile de rester motivé. I don't totally agree. Even if it's flexible, you talk less to other students, so it's sometimes harder to stay motivated.
Nadia : Tu as peut-être raison. Et toi, Karim, l'entretien s'est bien passé ? You might be right. And you, Karim, did the interview go well?
Karim : Très bien, je pense. J'ai même déjà envoyé un e-mail pour confirmer. J'ai écrit : « Madame, Monsieur, je vous écris pour vous remercier de l'entretien. Je voudrais savoir si une réponse est prévue cette semaine. Cordialement, » Very well, I think. I've even already sent an email to confirm. I wrote: « Dear Sir or Madam, I am writing to thank you for the interview. I would like to know if an answer is expected this week. Sincerely. »
Marc : Bien écrit. Si tu es embauché, on fête ça tous ensemble ! Well written. If you get hired, we'll all celebrate together!
Karim : Avec plaisir ! Et si ça ne marche pas, on fête quand même le voyage de Marc. With pleasure! And if it doesn't work out, we'll still celebrate Marc's trip.

This dialogue combines every Unit III system: real-condition and hypothetical si frames, reported media information with selon, opinion formulas, reason and result connectors, même si, and a quoted formal email excerpt within an otherwise informal conversation. No new grammar appears anywhere in this dialogue.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Moving between registers in one conversation

As you saw across Chapters 11–14, a single French-speaking day often moves between several registers: an informal conversation with friends, a formal email to an employer, a neutral summary of something read in the news. Fluent communication depends not only on correct grammar in each register, but on recognizing which register a situation calls for and shifting cleanly between them, without carrying informal habits into a formal context or vice versa.

Regional Variation — A quick reminder

Unit III introduced several points of labeled regional and register variation: formal closing formulas (Chapter 13), and everyday opinion hedges and intensifiers (Chapter 14). None of this variation is assessed productively in this chapter; the productive standard remains contemporary broadly understood French based primarily on educated metropolitan usage, as established for the whole series.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Mixing the two si frames within one sentence

Error: Saying *si je partirais en voyage, j'aurais plus d'expérience, mixing a conditionnel directly after si with a result that doesn't match.

Correction: Decide first whether the situation is real or imagined, then apply one frame consistently: si je pars en voyage, j'aurai plus d'expérience (real) or si je partais en voyage, j'aurais plus d'expérience (imagined).

Common Mistake — Letting formal register leak into informal narration

Error: Writing an entire personal blog-style narration using vous and formal openings, as if it were a professional email.

Correction: Reserve vous, Madame, / Monsieur,, and Cordialement, for the quoted or described formal element only; keep the rest of a personal narrative in a neutral or informal register, as appropriate to the task.

Common Mistake — Forgetting the concession after stating an opinion

Error: Stating an opinion and a reason, but never acknowledging any limiting point, even when the task requires a concession.

Correction: Add one clear même si + indicative clause that concedes a real limitation without abandoning your main position: je pense que c'est une bonne idée, même si ça demande de la discipline.

7

Démarches, services et rendez-vous

Services and Appointments · Speak and Write French: Everyday Independence and Connected Communication

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Make, confirm, cancel, or change an appointment with an office, clinic, or administrative service.
  2. State your availability using the present tense and aller + infinitif for planned actions.
  3. Use je voudrais + infinitive as a fixed, memorized formula for polite requests.
  4. Use pourriez-vous + infinitive as a fixed, memorized formula for formal requests.
  5. Form indirect yes/no questions with je voudrais savoir si...
  6. Form indirect information questions with je voudrais savoir + question word.
  7. Distinguish a direct question asked out loud from the same question embedded politely inside a request, and apply the correct word order in each.
  8. Maintain formal vous register throughout a service interaction, in speech and in writing.
  9. Use a small set of verbs followed by à + infinitive, such as commencer à and continuer à, alongside the de-governed verbs from Chapter 6.
  10. Read and write a formal message requesting or confirming an appointment.

Communication Scenario

Now that Marc and Nadia are both settled — more or less — into their new cities, the ordinary paperwork of adult life catches up with them. Marc needs to update his address with an administrative office in Bordeaux; Nadia needs to call her bank in Tours about a card that was not delivered on time. Karim, for his part, needs to email his landlord's agency to request a repair appointment, this time in writing rather than by phone. None of these situations calls for close, informal language: they call for polite, slightly more formal French, where a direct demand would sound abrupt. This chapter is about exactly that register — asking clearly and politely for information or action from someone you do not know well, on the phone, in person, or in writing, and managing the practical back-and-forth of scheduling.

  1. Request an appointment and propose or accept a time.
  2. Confirm, cancel, or reschedule an appointment already made.
  3. Make a polite request using a fixed formal formula.
  4. Ask a question politely and indirectly, without sounding abrupt.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Formal requests and indirect questions

This chapter asks you to manage a formal service interaction out loud, where rhythm and phrasing carry much of the impression of politeness alongside your choice of words. Practice these fixed formulas as smooth, single rhythm groups rather than as separate words said one at a time:

  • Je voudrais prendre rendez-vous.
  • Pourriez-vous me rappeler ?
  • Je voudrais savoir si vous êtes disponible.
  • Je voudrais savoir quand je peux venir.

A. Je voudrais as one flowing formula. Say je voudrais as a single smooth unit leading directly into the infinitive that follows, without a pause after voudrais.

B. Pourriez-vous with clear question intonation. The expression pourriez-vous uses formal subject–verb inversion, which already marks the sentence grammatically as a question. Use clear, controlled question intonation across the complete request. Intonation supports the polite delivery, but it does not create the question by itself.

C. No pause before si. In je voudrais savoir si..., keep savoir and si connected in the same breath group; a pause before si can make the sentence sound hesitant or unfinished.

D. Direct versus indirect intonation. A direct question such as Est-ce que vous êtes disponible lundi ? typically carries clearer, more noticeable rising intonation at the end. An indirect question, embedded inside a statement beginning with je voudrais savoir, is generally said with more controlled, even phrasing, since the sentence as a whole is a statement, not a direct question.

Intonation alone does not make a request polite. Politeness in this chapter's tasks depends on the combination of vocabulary, greeting, register, a clear explanation of the reason for the call or message, and an appropriate closing — not on tone of voice by itself.

In this chapter, you are expected to: produce je voudrais and pourriez-vous as smooth, unhesitating formulas; use appropriate question intonation for direct formal requests and controlled, even phrasing for indirect questions; keep je voudrais savoir si... connected without a pause before si; and distinguish the more marked intonation of a direct question from the more controlled phrasing of an indirect one.

Grammar: Je voudrais savoir...

Purpose: teach the productive vocabulary and patterns needed to make, confirm, cancel, and change an appointment; introduce formulaic polite requests and indirect questions; and add verb government with à + infinitive — without introducing a new tense, the subjunctive, or the productive conditional.

7.1 Making, confirming, cancelling, and changing appointments

Language Focus

Managing an appointment communicatively requires only patterns you can already build: the present tense for stating availability, aller + infinitif for a planned action, and a small set of fixed formulas for requesting, confirming, and changing plans. This chapter's central task is combining these familiar tools fluently in a formal, vous-register interaction.

  • Je voudrais prendre rendez-vous. I would like to make an appointment.
  • Je voudrais confirmer mon rendez-vous. I would like to confirm my appointment.
  • Je voudrais annuler mon rendez-vous. I would like to cancel my appointment.
  • Je voudrais reporter mon rendez-vous. I would like to postpone/reschedule my appointment.
  • Je suis disponible mardi matin. Je ne suis pas disponible jeudi. I am available Tuesday morning. I am not available Thursday. present tense — stating availability
  • Je vais venir vendredi à quinze heures. I am going to come Friday at three o'clock. aller + infinitif — a planned action
  • Est-ce que ce créneau vous convient ? — Oui, cela me convient. / Non, cela ne me convient pas. Does this time slot work for you? — Yes, that works for me. / No, that doesn't work for me.
  • Est-ce qu'un autre horaire est possible ? Is another time possible?
  • Le rendez-vous est confirmé pour lundi. The appointment is confirmed for Monday.

Key vocabulary for this pattern: prendre rendez-vous (to make an appointment), confirmer (to confirm), annuler (to cancel), reporter (to postpone or reschedule), and un créneau (an available time slot). No new tense is required to manage any part of this exchange.

7.2 Formulaic polite requests: je voudrais and pourriez-vous

Building Block — Two fixed formulas for asking politely
[je voudrais] + [infinitif] / [pourriez-vous] + [infinitif] ?
Both forms are grammatically conditional, but this chapter teaches them as complete, memorized formulas, exactly as il faudrait was taught in Chapter 6. Je voudrais softens a statement of desire; pourriez-vous softens a question into a polite request. Neither requires you to understand or produce the broader conditional system yet.
Je voudrais prendre rendez-vous, s'il vous plaît. Pourriez-vous me confirmer l'heure par message ?
I would like to make an appointment, please. Could you confirm the time for me by message?

Compare the same request in three registers, from most informal to most formal:

  • Tu peux me rappeler ? informal, tu
  • Vous pouvez me rappeler ? neutral, vous
  • Pourriez-vous me rappeler ? formal, softened, vous

All three are grammatically correct; the choice depends entirely on context and relationship, as developed throughout this chapter's Culture and Communication section.

7.3 Indirect questions with je voudrais savoir

Building Block — Asking without asking directly
[je voudrais savoir] + [si / mot interrogatif] + [sujet + verbe]
An indirect question embeds a question inside a polite statement. Word order stays exactly as in a normal statement (subject before verb); there is no inversion and no est-ce que inside the embedded part, even though the same idea asked directly would use one of those tools.
Vous êtes disponible mardi ? → Je voudrais savoir si vous êtes disponible mardi. Quand puis-je venir ? → Je voudrais savoir quand je peux venir.
Are you available Tuesday? → I would like to know whether you are available Tuesday. When can I come? → I would like to know when I can come.

Compare the direct and indirect forms of the same questions:

  • Est-ce que vous êtes disponible lundi ? → Je voudrais savoir si vous êtes disponible lundi. yes/no question: est-ce que + normal order → si + normal order, no est-ce que
  • Quand est-ce que je peux venir ? → Je voudrais savoir quand je peux venir. question word + est-ce que → question word + normal order, no est-ce que
  • Quels documents est-ce que je dois apporter ? → Je voudrais savoir quels documents je dois apporter.
  • Combien de temps est-ce que le rendez-vous va durer ? → Je voudrais savoir combien de temps le rendez-vous va durer. the subject le rendez-vous stays before the verb, exactly as in the direct question built with est-ce que

This chapter's controlled rule is simple and absolute for practice purposes: after je voudrais savoir, use the same subject-verb word order you would use in an ordinary statement, and never carry est-ce que or subject-verb inversion into the embedded question. This chapter's controlled system limits indirect questions to those introduced by je voudrais savoir. Other reporting verbs, and past-tense reported speech, are not part of this chapter's productive scope.

7.4 Verb government with à: commencer à, continuer à

Language Focus

Chapter 6 introduced verbs governed by de (avoir besoin de, s'occuper de). Some equally common verbs are instead governed by à when followed by an infinitive. The preposition itself carries no independent meaning here; it is simply part of the verb's fixed pattern, so each verb + preposition combination must be learned together.

  • Le formulaire commence à me poser des difficultés. The form is starting to give me trouble.
  • Nous continuons à vous tenir informés. We continue to keep you informed.

This chapter does not ask learners to replace an à-governed infinitive with a pronoun; that pattern remains outside this chapter's controlled system. Simply recognize and reproduce commencer à and continuer à as fixed patterns.

7.5 Recycling y, en, il faut, and past-participle agreement

Language Note — Previously learned systems — No new rule

This chapter's tasks continue to rely on productive y and en (Ch. 6) for referring back to appointments, places, and documents already mentioned, and on the impersonal/personal obligation contrast (il faut / devoir) for describing administrative requirements. Past-participle agreement with être verbs and reflexives continues exactly as in earlier chapters. None of these systems changes in this chapter; they are simply reused in a more formal context.

7.6 Scope reminder: staying inside this chapter's fixed formulas

Language Note — What this chapter does not add

In Chapter 7, use je voudrais, pourriez-vous, and previously learned il faudrait as fixed expressions. Other conditional forms may be valid French, but they are not yet part of the chapter's productive system. For example, je pourrais venir demain is valid French, but it is outside Chapter 7's productive scope; for this chapter's tasks, use je voudrais prendre rendez-vous pour demain instead. This chapter also introduces no new tense: keep your own production to the present tense, aller + infinitif, and the fixed formulas above.

Model Dialogue: Un rendez-vous dans un service administratif

Context: Marc calls an administrative service in Bordeaux to make an appointment to update information in his file. Register: formal, vous, speaking with an employee he does not know.

L'employée : Bonjour, service administratif de Bordeaux, j'écoute. Hello, Bordeaux administrative service. How may I help you?
Marc : Bonjour madame. Je voudrais prendre rendez-vous pour mettre à jour mon adresse dans mon dossier. Hello. I would like to make an appointment to update my address in my file.
L'employée : Nous avons un créneau jeudi prochain à onze heures. Est-ce que cela vous convient ? We have a slot next Thursday at eleven o'clock. Does that work for you?
Marc : Je voudrais savoir si vous avez aussi un créneau l'après-midi. Je travaille le matin. I would like to know whether you also have a slot in the afternoon. I work in the mornings.
L'employée : Oui, je peux vous proposer quinze heures trente le même jour. Yes, I can offer you three thirty, the same day.
Marc : Parfait, cela me convient très bien. Pourriez-vous me confirmer la liste des documents à apporter ? Perfect, that works very well for me. Could you confirm the list of documents I need to bring?
L'employée : Bien sûr. Il faut apporter une pièce d'identité et un justificatif de domicile. Nous allons aussi vous envoyer un message de confirmation avec la liste complète. Of course. You need to bring an identity document and proof of address. We are also going to send you a confirmation message with the complete list.
Marc : Merci beaucoup pour votre aide. À jeudi. Thank you very much for your help. See you Thursday.

Compréhension du dialogue

  1. What appointment times does the employee propose, and why does Marc prefer the afternoon appointment?
  2. Which documents does the employee say Marc must bring?
  3. Which expression does Marc use to request an appointment politely?
  4. Which indirect question does Marc ask?
  5. Which expression does Marc use to ask for confirmation politely?

This dialogue models the formulaic polite requests je voudrais prendre rendez-vous and pourriez-vous..., one indirect question with je voudrais savoir si..., present-tense scheduling (nous avons un créneau, cela me convient, il faut apporter), and aller + infinitif for a planned action (nous allons vous envoyer). Note the consistent formal vous register throughout, appropriate for a first call to an office employee neither speaker knows personally.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Registers of politeness in services and administration

Interactions with administrative offices, banks, and unfamiliar service providers in French-speaking contexts typically default to vous and a more formal vocabulary, even when the underlying request is simple. Je voudrais and pourriez-vous are common, expected softening devices in this kind of interaction; using the blunter je veux ("I want") or a direct imperative in the same context can come across as demanding, even if it is grammatically correct. This does not mean formal French is cold: it reflects a social convention for interacting politely with people one does not know personally.

Regional Variation — Administrative vocabulary and procedures

Specific administrative procedures, office names, and required documents vary significantly by country and even by region within a single country, and this chapter does not attempt to describe any one country's actual administrative system. The general communicative patterns — requesting an appointment politely, asking indirect questions, confirming details in writing — are broadly applicable across French-speaking administrative and service contexts, even where the specific procedures differ.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Using inversion or est-ce que inside an indirect question

Error: Writing Je voudrais savoir est-ce que vous êtes disponible, keeping est-ce que inside the indirect question.

Correction: Drop est-ce que and use si instead, with normal subject-verb word order: Je voudrais savoir si vous êtes disponible. The same applies to inversion after a question word: je voudrais savoir quand vous êtes disponible, not *je voudrais savoir quand êtes-vous disponible.

Common Mistake — Treating je voudrais and pourriez-vous as fully productive conditional

Error: Trying to build new conditional forms from other verbs by analogy, without having studied the productive conditional system.

Correction: As with il faudrait in Chapter 6, je voudrais and pourriez-vous are taught here as fixed, memorized formulas. Je pourrais venir demain is valid French, but it is outside this chapter's productive scope; use je voudrais prendre rendez-vous pour demain instead. The productive conditional system, including how to form and use it with any verb, is introduced later in the volume.

Common Mistake — Switching to tu with an unfamiliar service provider

Error: Using tu and informal vocabulary such as carrément or du coup when speaking with an unfamiliar office employee.

Correction: As explained in Culture and Communication above, maintain vous and neutral-to-formal vocabulary throughout an interaction with an administrative office, bank, or unfamiliar service provider, as modeled in this chapter's dialogue and reading.

Common Mistake — Confusing convenir with the informal ça marche

Error: Writing Ça marche pour ce créneau in a formal message to a landlord or office.

Correction: Ça marche (Chapter 1) is an informal formula appropriate between friends. In this chapter's formal register, use cela me convient or ce créneau me convient instead.

  • at least one indirect yes/no question with je voudrais savoir si...;
  • at least one indirect information question with je voudrais savoir + question word;
  • at least one use of pourriez-vous;
  • a proposed or discussed appointment day and time;
  • an acceptance, rejection, cancellation, or change of the appointment;
  • a formal closing.

2. Interaction. Include at least one follow-up question about the appointment (documents needed, location, or timing), and a connected answer to it.

3. Written message. Write eight to twelve sentences requesting an appointment, including:

  • a formal greeting and closing;
  • a clear request with je voudrais;
  • present-tense availability;
  • at least one indirect question;
  • at least one use of pourriez-vous.

Pronunciation and register. Pronunciation is clear and understandable. Direct formal questions use appropriate question intonation, while indirect questions use controlled phrasing. Fixed request formulas such as je voudrais and pourriez-vous are delivered smoothly without disruptive pauses, and formal vous register is maintained consistently throughout.

Practical Performance Task — Model dialogue

Employée : Bonjour, service administratif, j'écoute.
Usager : Bonjour madame. Je voudrais prendre rendez-vous pour mettre à jour mon adresse.
Employée : Nous avons un créneau mardi à dix heures. Est-ce que cela vous convient ?
Usager : Je voudrais savoir si vous avez aussi un créneau l'après-midi. Je ne suis pas disponible le matin.
Employée : Oui, nous avons un créneau à quinze heures.
Usager : Parfait, cela me convient. Je voudrais savoir quels documents je dois apporter.
Employée : Il faut apporter une pièce d'identité et un justificatif de domicile.
Usager : Merci. Pourriez-vous me confirmer le rendez-vous par message ?
Employée : Bien sûr. Nous allons vous envoyer une confirmation aujourd'hui.
Usager : Merci beaucoup. Au revoir.

This model demonstrates a formal greeting, je voudrais + infinitive, je voudrais savoir si..., an indirect information question, pourriez-vous + infinitive, acceptance of a proposed time, consistent formal vous, a polite closing, and only the present tense and aller + infinitif, with no future-simple forms. This is one acceptable model; other dialogues that meet the task's requirements are equally valid.

Looking Ahead

Ce que vous savez faire maintenant — What you can do now

You can make, confirm, cancel, and reschedule an appointment, state availability using the present tense and aller + infinitif, soften a request with fixed formal formulas, and ask a question indirectly and politely rather than directly, maintaining a consistent formal register throughout a call or message.

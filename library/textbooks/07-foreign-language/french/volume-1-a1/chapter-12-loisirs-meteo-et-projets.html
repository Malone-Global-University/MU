Loisirs, météo et projets

Leisure, Weather, and Plans · Speak and Write French: Foundations of Practical Communication

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Name common leisure activities and describe the weather using set expressions.
  2. Use on to talk about people in general or to mean "we" in ordinary conversation.
  3. Form simple near-future plans using aller plus the infinitive, with familiar models.
  4. Extend a simple invitation, and accept or politely decline using set formulas.
  5. Understand and produce a short, guided conversation about weekend plans.
  6. Recognize and reproduce selected liaison and enchaînement patterns in a small set of verified phrases, and continue to control nasal vowels already introduced in earlier chapters.
  7. Recycle vocabulary and grammar from Units I and II, and from Chapter 11, while communicating about a new domain.

Communication Scenario

It is Friday afternoon, and Léa is messaging her friend Marc to plan the weekend. Saturday looks sunny, so they decide to go hiking and invite their friend Amina. Amina cannot join in the morning because she is working, so the group agrees on a new time and place. This chapter continues Unit III, Dans la communauté, by giving you the tools to talk about leisure time, the weather, and simple weekend plans.

  1. Learn key vocabulary for leisure activities and weather.
  2. Use on to talk about people in general and among friends.
  3. Talk about simple near-future plans with aller plus the infinitive.
  4. Extend, accept, and decline a simple invitation.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Liaison and enchaînement, precisely defined

Liaison occurs when a normally silent final consonant becomes audible before a following vowel sound, in specific grammatical environments. Enchaînement occurs when a final consonant that is already pronounced links smoothly to a following vowel-initial word. These are different processes, and this chapter uses only verified examples of each.

Liaison (recycled from Chapter 6): the n of on is normally silent, but it becomes audible before a vowel-initial verb.

  • on invite — the silent n of on links to the vowel-initial invite.

Enchaînement: the already-pronounced final consonant of one word links directly into the vowel sound that begins the next word.

  • avec Amina — the pronounced c of avec links into Amina.
  • avec un ami — the pronounced c of avec links into un.

Continue to practice the nasal vowels you already control in words used throughout this chapter, such as on va, on invite, le temps, and dimanche.

Language Focus: On, the Near Future, and Invitations

Purpose: talk about people in general, make simple near-future plans, and manage invitations.

12.1 On for general and inclusive reference

Language Focus

On is a subject pronoun you have already used since Chapter 5. In ordinary conversation among friends and family, on very commonly replaces nous to mean "we." On also expresses general reference, similar to English "people" or "you" in general statements. On always takes the same verb form as il or elle, even when it refers to a group.

  • On va faire une randonnée ? Shall we go hiking? on + va (3rd person singular form of aller)
  • Au cinéma, on regarde un film. At the movies, people watch a film.
  • Le week-end, on retrouve souvent des amis. On weekends, people often meet up with friends.

On is very frequent in informal conversation. Nous remains correct and is more common in careful speech and formal writing. The choice between them depends on register and context, not on one form being more natural than the other.

12.2 The near future with aller plus infinitive

Building Block — Talking about the near future
[subject] + aller (conjugated) + [infinitive]
To talk about something that is going to happen soon, French conjugates aller in the present tense and follows it directly with an infinitive. This pattern is introduced here with a small set of familiar models.
Samedi, on va faire une randonnée.
Saturday, we're going to go hiking.
Language Focus
  • Je vais regarder un film. I'm going to watch a film.
  • Tu vas jouer au foot. You're going to play soccer.
  • On va faire une randonnée. We're going to go hiking.
  • Nous allons retrouver des amis. We're going to meet up with friends.

To make a near-future sentence negative, place ne...pas around the conjugated form of aller, exactly as you learned for the present tense in Chapter 7: On ne va pas jouer au foot.

At this stage, focus on recognizing and producing a small number of clear near-future sentences rather than combining many different infinitives in one sentence.

12.3 Making, accepting, and declining invitations

Language Focus

Invitations among friends commonly begin with tu veux...? or the more casual ça te dit de...?, both followed by an infinitive. Acceptances and polite declines are formulaic at this stage.

  • Tu veux jouer au foot samedi ? Do you want to play soccer on Saturday?
  • Avec plaisir ! With pleasure!
  • Je suis désolée, mais je ne peux pas samedi. I'm sorry, but I can't on Saturday.

Je ne peux pas samedi is a fixed formula for declining an invitation. It is not a general introduction to the verb pouvoir, which you will study more fully in Chapter 13.

Model Dialogue: On va faire une randonnée ?

Context: Léa and Marc, close friends, message each other on Friday afternoon to plan the weekend.

Marc : Salut ! Il va faire beau samedi. Hi! It's going to be nice out on Saturday.
Léa : Super ! On va faire une randonnée ? Great! Shall we go hiking?
Marc : Bonne idée. Tu veux inviter Amina aussi ? Good idea. Do you want to invite Amina too?
Léa : Oui. Je vais écrire à Amina maintenant. Yes. I'm going to write to Amina now.
(Plus tard, Amina répond.)
Amina : Merci de l'invitation ! Je suis désolée, mais je travaille samedi matin. Thanks for the invitation! I'm sorry, but I work Saturday morning.
Léa : Pas de problème. On va se retrouver au parc samedi après-midi, vers deux heures. No problem. We're going to meet at the park Saturday afternoon, around two o'clock.
Amina : Avec plaisir ! À samedi. With pleasure! See you Saturday.

Notice how on is used to mean "we" among friends, and how aller plus infinitive expresses simple plans for the near future. Every structure in this dialogue belongs to Chapters 1–12.

Speaking Practice

Pratique contrôlée — Controlled practice: describe the weather and one plan

Say what the weather is like today, then say one thing you are going to do this weekend, using aller plus an infinitive.

Tâche communicative — Communicative task: a guided invitation

With a partner, follow these steps in order.

  1. Learner A states one weather condition.
  2. Learner A invites Learner B to one activity.
  3. Learner B accepts, or declines using the formula je ne peux pas samedi.
  4. Together, Learner A and Learner B state one plan using on va + infinitive.
  5. Together, they close politely.

Phrase bank:

  • Il va faire beau samedi.
  • Tu veux aller au parc ?
  • Ça te dit de jouer au foot ?
  • Avec plaisir.
  • Je suis désolé(e), mais je ne peux pas samedi.
  • On va se retrouver à deux heures.
  • À samedi !

Pratique individuelle — Solo alternative

Using the phrase bank above, record yourself inviting an imaginary friend to do one activity this weekend, and then answer your own invitation as if you were that friend, either accepting or declining with the formula you learned.

Reading Practice

Salut Amina ! Samedi, il va faire beau. On va faire une randonnée avec Marc. Tu veux venir avec nous ? On va se retrouver au parc à dix heures du matin. Dimanche, il va pleuvoir, alors on va rester à la maison. Réponds-moi vite !

Tâche de lecture — Reading task:

  1. What is the weather going to be like on Saturday?
  2. What is the plan for Saturday, and who is invited?
  3. Where and when are they going to meet?
  4. What is the weather going to be like on Sunday, and what are they going to do?

Writing Workshop

Complete the following linked writing tasks in order.

Tâche 1 — Label a forecast: Write two short sentences describing the weather for two different days, using two different weather expressions from the vocabulary list.

Tâche 2 — A short invitation message: Using the model below, write a supported four-to-six-sentence informal message inviting a friend to do something this weekend.

Modèle — Model message
Salut ! Il va faire beau samedi. Tu veux jouer au foot avec moi ? On va se retrouver au parc à onze heures. Réponds-moi vite !
Hi! It's going to be nice out on Saturday. Do you want to play soccer with me? We're going to meet at the park at eleven o'clock. Write back soon!

Your message should include:

  • one greeting;
  • one weather expression;
  • one invitation, using tu veux...? or ça te dit de...?;
  • one or two sentences with aller plus infinitive;
  • one use of on meaning "we";
  • one time or meeting place; and
  • one closing.
  1. Choose one activity. Pick a single activity to invite a friend to do.
  2. Choose the weather. Decide what the weather is like on the day of your plan.
  3. Draft your message using the model. Follow the same order: greeting, weather, invitation, plan, meeting detail, closing.
  4. Read your message aloud once. Listen for the liaison in on invite if you use it, and for accurate nasal vowels.
  5. Check your aller plus infinitive forms. Confirm that aller is conjugated correctly and that the second verb stays in the infinitive.

Tâche 3 — Respond to an invitation: Write one or two sentences accepting or politely declining the invitation you wrote in Tâche 2, using a formula from this chapter.

Liste de révision — Editing checklist:

  • The message follows the model order and stays within four to six sentences.
  • on appears at least once, meaning "we."
  • At least one weather expression appears.
  • An invitation formula appears.
  • Accents, apostrophes, and punctuation are accurate throughout.
  • No vocabulary or grammar from outside Chapters 1–12 has been used.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — On, nous, and register

On is highly frequent in informal conversation, including among friends planning a weekend. Nous remains correct and is more common in careful or formal writing. The choice between an informal invitation formula like tu veux...? and a more formal one depends on the relationship between speakers, their age, the setting, and the region. No single choice is correct in every situation; register awareness matters more than a fixed rule.

Regional Variation — Leisure vocabulary across the Francophone world (receptive)

Popular leisure activities and the expressions used to describe them can vary across Francophone regions. This variation is receptive at this level: you do not need to produce region-specific vocabulary, but you may encounter different weather or activity words depending on where French is spoken. The core expressions in this chapter remain broadly usable across the Francophone world.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Conjugating the second verb after aller

Error: Writing je vais je fais une randonnée or conjugating the second verb.

Correction: Only aller is conjugated; the second verb stays in the infinitive: je vais faire une randonnée.

Common Mistake — Using a plural verb form with on

Error: Writing on vont instead of the correct form.

Correction: On always takes the same verb form as il or elle, even when it refers to more than one person: on va, not on vont.

Common Mistake — Doubling pas around the infinitive

Error: Writing on ne va pas faire pas de randonnée.

Correction: ne...pas surrounds only the conjugated form of aller: on ne va pas faire de randonnée.

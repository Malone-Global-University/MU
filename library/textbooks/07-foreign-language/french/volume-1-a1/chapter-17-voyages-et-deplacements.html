17

Voyages et déplacements

Travel Experiences · Speak and Write French: Foundations of Practical Communication

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Form the passé composé with être for a set of high-frequency movement and state-change verbs.
  2. Apply basic subject agreement of the past participle with être.
  3. Form the passé composé of reflexive verbs, which also use être.
  4. Distinguish, in context, which verbs take être and which take avoir.
  5. Ask and answer questions about a trip or a journey, using time expressions and place names.
  6. Use ne... pas correctly with reflexive verbs in the passé composé.
  7. Understand short spoken and written accounts of a recent trip.
  8. Narrate, in speech and in writing, a short account of a trip you took or a day involving movement.
  9. Apply accurate pronunciation of être-verb agreement endings and smooth connected speech in reflexive constructions.

Communication Scenario

Léa has just returned from a weekend trip to Lyon with her friend Nadia. Marc, who is about to start his own internship, wants to hear every detail: when they left, how they traveled, what time they arrived, whether they got lost, and when they got up each morning. This chapter continues Unit IV by giving you the tools to narrate movement, arrival, departure, and daily routines in the past, using être as a helping verb.

  1. Describe a trip: departure, arrival, and movement between places.
  2. Talk about getting up, going to bed, and other daily routines in the past.
  3. Ask someone how their trip or weekend went.
  4. Choose correctly between avoir and être as the auxiliary verb.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Agreement endings and connected speech in reflexive forms

When the past participle of an être-verb agrees with a feminine subject, an added written -e is silent in most cases: allé and allée sound identical. Do not assume that a written agreement ending always changes the pronunciation; for the verbs scheduled in this chapter, the feminine -e is generally not pronounced.

In reflexive constructions, pronounce the reflexive pronoun and the form of être as one smooth rhythmic group: je me suis levé(e), nous nous sommes couchés, ils se sont promenés. This is connected speech, not a new liaison rule. In very informal speech, speakers may reduce je me suis to a form resembling j'me suis; this is receptive-only informal reduction and must not be used in formal writing.

Grammar: Le passé composé avec être

Purpose: describe completed movement, state changes, and reflexive actions in the past using a conjugated form of être plus a past participle that agrees with the subject.

17.1 A scheduled set of être-verbs

Language Focus

Most French verbs use avoir in the passé composé, as you learned in Chapter 16. This chapter introduces a scheduled set of high-frequency verbs that use être in the intransitive movement or state-change patterns practiced here. Auxiliary choice must be learned with the verb's meaning and sentence structure, not assumed from meaning alone: many verbs describing movement, such as voyager or marcher, actually take avoir.

  • Léa est arrivée à Lyon vendredi soir. Léa arrived in Lyon on Friday evening. est [aux.] + arrivée [participle, feminine agreement]
  • Nadia et Léa sont parties ensemble. Nadia and Léa left together.

17.2 Basic subject agreement with être

Building Block — Agreement pattern
masculine singular: —  |  feminine singular: +e  |  masculine plural: +s  |  feminine plural: +es
With être, the past participle agrees in gender and number with the subject of the sentence, just like an adjective.
il est allé · elle est allée · ils sont allés · elles sont allées
he went · she went · they (m./mixed) went · they (f.) went

This chapter covers only this basic subject agreement. Agreement of the past participle with a preceding direct object pronoun, which applies to avoir-verbs in certain cases, is not covered at this level.

17.3 Reflexive verbs also use être

Language Focus

Every reflexive verb — a verb built with se, such as se lever or se coucher — uses être in the passé composé, even though most of these verbs are not verbs of movement. The reflexive pronoun still appears, matching the subject, and comes directly before the conjugated form of être.

  • Elle s'est levée à sept heures. She got up at seven o'clock. elle s' [refl.] + est [aux.] + levée [participle]
  • Ils se sont couchés tard. They went to bed late.
  • Elles se sont promenées dans le vieux Lyon. They went for a walk in old Lyon.

In the simple reflexive patterns practiced in this chapter — se lever, se coucher, se réveiller, se promener, and se perdre — the past participle agrees with the subject. More complex reflexive-agreement cases are deferred to a later level. Learners are not required to analyze reflexive constructions involving a following direct object or an indirect-object relationship, such as elles se sont lavé les mains or elles se sont parlé; these are mentioned here only as deferred exceptions and are not taught productively.

17.5 Negation and questions

Building Block — Negation with reflexive verbs
ne + [reflexive pronoun] + [être] + pas + [past participle]
ne... pas surrounds the reflexive pronoun and the conjugated form of être together.
Je ne me suis pas réveillé à temps.
I did not wake up on time.

Questions follow the same strategies as in Chapter 16: Qu'est-ce que tu as fait ce week-end ? À quelle heure es-tu parti ? (intonation-neutral, question word plus est-ce que or inversion), or Tu es arrivée à quelle heure ? (rising intonation, everyday conversation).

Model Dialogue: Comment s'est passé ton voyage ?

Context: Marc asks Léa about her weekend trip to Lyon with Nadia.

Marc : Alors, comment s'est passé ton voyage à Lyon ? So, how did your trip to Lyon go?
Léa : Très bien ! Nadia et moi, nous sommes parties vendredi soir et nous sommes arrivées vers vingt-deux heures. Very well! Nadia and I left Friday evening, and we arrived around ten o'clock.
Marc : Vous êtes allées où, exactement ? Where exactly did you go?
Léa : On est restées dans un petit hôtel près de la gare. Le lendemain, je me suis levée tôt, et on s'est promenées dans le vieux Lyon. We stayed at a small hotel near the station. The next day, I got up early, and we went for a walk in old Lyon.
Marc : Est-ce que vous vous êtes perdues ? Did you get lost?
Léa : Un peu, oui ! Nadia est tombée dans un petit escalier, mais elle n'a pas eu mal. Après, on est montées voir la basilique. A little, yes! Nadia fell on a small staircase, but she wasn't hurt. Afterward, we went up to see the basilica.
Marc : Et vous êtes retournées quand ? And when did you go back?
Léa : On est retournées à Paris dimanche soir. On s'est couchées très tard, mais c'était un excellent week-end. We went back to Paris Sunday evening. We went to bed very late, but it was an excellent weekend.

This dialogue uses être throughout, with feminine plural agreement because Léa and Nadia are both women. Notice that n'a pas eu mal still uses avoir, since avoir mal is not a scheduled être-verb or reflexive expression.

Speaking Practice

Pratique contrôlée — Controlled practice: avoir or être in context

Take turns reading a complete sentence frame from Chapters 16 and 17. Your partner identifies whether the verb takes avoir or être in that sentence, then produces the complete passé composé form with the correct agreement.

  • Léa ______ à Lyon vendredi. → est arrivée
  • Léa ______ un café. → a pris
  • Nadia ______ dans l'escalier. → est tombée
  • Marc ______ son travail. → a fini

Tâche communicative — Communicative task: a recent trip or weekend

With a partner, ask and answer Comment s'est passé ton week-end ? or Qu'est-ce que tu as fait pendant ton voyage ? Each of you should describe at least four completed actions, mixing avoir-verbs, être-verbs, and at least one reflexive verb.

Pratique individuelle — Solo alternative

Record yourself narrating a real or imagined trip: when you left, how you traveled, what time you arrived, and what you did once you got there. Use at least three être-verbs and one reflexive verb in the passé composé.

Reading Practice

Le week-end de Marc — Marc's weekend

Samedi matin, Marc s'est réveillé tôt parce qu'il est parti voir sa famille à Tours. Il est monté dans le train à huit heures, et il est arrivé vers dix heures. Sa mère est venue le chercher à la gare. L'après-midi, ils se sont promenés dans le centre-ville, et Marc est entré dans son ancien lycée pour dire bonjour à un professeur. Le soir, toute la famille s'est couchée assez tôt. Dimanche, Marc est retourné à Paris, fatigué mais content de son week-end. Il n'est pas resté longtemps à Tours, mais ce court voyage lui a fait du bien.

Tâche de lecture — Reading task:

  1. Why did Marc get up early on Saturday?
  2. Who came to get him at the train station?
  3. What did Marc and his family do in the afternoon?
  4. Did Marc stay a long time in Tours, according to the last sentence?

Writing Workshop

Complete the following linked writing tasks in order.

Tâche 1 — Sentence transformation: Rewrite the following five present-tense sentences in the passé composé. Use the correct auxiliary, and apply subject agreement where required.

  1. Léa arrive à Lyon vendredi soir.
  2. Nadia et Léa partent ensemble.
  3. Marc finit son travail avant le voyage.
  4. Léa se lève tôt le lendemain.
  5. Nous prenons le train à huit heures.

Tâche 2 — Connected paragraph: Write a short paragraph of six to eight sentences describing a trip you took or a weekend involving travel or movement. Include at least three scheduled être-verb forms with correct agreement, one reflexive verb, one avoir verb recycled from Chapter 16, one negative sentence, and one past-time expression such as le week-end dernier or à l'arrivée.

  1. List your activities in order. Choose a real or imagined trip with clear movement between places.
  2. Identify your auxiliary for each verb. Decide whether each verb takes avoir or être, and check agreement carefully for être-verbs.
  3. Draft your paragraph. Organize the trip in chronological order using time expressions.
  4. Read your paragraph aloud once. Listen for smooth connected speech in reflexive sequences and check agreement endings.
  5. Revise. Confirm negation surrounds the reflexive pronoun and être together, not the participle alone.

Liste de révision — Editing checklist:

  • Every scheduled Chapter 17 verb uses the auxiliary required by its meaning and sentence pattern.
  • The paragraph uses only the scheduled intransitive être patterns and the reflexive patterns taught in this chapter.
  • Past participles with être agree in gender and number with the subject.
  • Reflexive verbs include the correct reflexive pronoun before être.
  • ne... pas surrounds the reflexive pronoun and être together.
  • At least one time expression situates the trip in the past.
  • Accents, apostrophes, and punctuation are accurate throughout.
  • No vocabulary or grammar from outside Chapters 1–17 has been used.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Weekend trips by train

Short weekend trips between French cities are common, and many travelers rely on an extensive train network to move quickly between regional centers. Lyon, mentioned in this chapter, is often described as a hub for gastronomy and history, and short getaways of this kind are a familiar part of student and young-professional life in France, though travel habits and preferred destinations naturally vary by region, budget, and personal interest.

Regional Variation — A note on this system

The passé composé with être, including standard reflexive constructions, is used widely across the Francophone world. Informal pronunciation and usage may vary by region and register, but the standard auxiliary and agreement patterns taught here remain the productive model for this level.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Using avoir with an être-verb

Error: Writing j'ai allé instead of the correct auxiliary.

Correction: aller always takes être: je suis allé(e).

Common Mistake — Forgetting agreement with être

Error: Writing elle est allé without the feminine ending.

Correction: The participle must agree with a feminine subject: elle est allée.

Common Mistake — Dropping the reflexive pronoun

Error: Writing je suis levée without the reflexive pronoun me.

Correction: Reflexive verbs keep the reflexive pronoun in the passé composé: je me suis levée.

Famille et amis

Family and Friends · Speak and Write French: Foundations of Practical Communication

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will begin to:

  1. Use the high-frequency forms j'ai, tu as, il a, elle a, and vous avez with support.
  2. Name a small set of immediate family members: father, mother, brother, sister, parents, and a friend.
  3. Use mon, ma, and mes to identify your own family members.
  4. Recognize the supported forms ton/ta/tes in questions, and consult son/sa/ses as reference forms.
  5. State a supported age using avoir + number + ans, consulting a number reference when needed.
  6. Select a practiced adjective form from the chapter table to describe one person.
  7. Complete a short, four-line introduction of one family member or friend, with support.

Communication Scenario

Amina and Thomas, the two students from Chapter 3, show each other a photo and talk briefly about their families. This chapter gives you the tools to introduce one family member or friend: who the person is, how old they are, and one or two things they are like.

  1. The possessive determiner agrees with the noun that follows it, not with the owner. This chapter focuses mainly on mon, ma, and mes for talking about your own family.
  2. To state an exact age in the pattern taught here, French uses avoir + number + ans: j'ai vingt ans. Do not translate the English pattern as je suis vingt ans.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Liaison with vous avez and adjective endings

The main productive liaison practiced in this chapter is vous avez, the same pattern recycled from vous êtes in Chapter 3: the s of vous links to the vowel sound that follows.

  • Listen for liaison in the reference forms nous avons and ils ont when you see them in the table below, but you do not need to produce them yourself in this chapter.
  • The feminine adjective ending -e is silent, but it often makes the preceding consonant audible: grand ends in a silent d, while grande pronounces that d clearly.
  • Keep the nasal vowel in mon distinct from the oral vowel in ma.

Language Focus: avoir, mon/ma/mes, and Adjectives

Purpose: begin using avoir, identify your own family members with mon, ma, and mes, and describe one person with a practiced adjective.

4.2 mon, ma, mes — and supported ton/ta/tes, son/sa/ses

Building Block — Choosing mon, ma, or mes
mon + masculine noun  |  ma + feminine noun  |  mes + plural noun
The possessive determiner agrees with the noun that follows it, not with the owner. Mon is also used before a feminine singular noun that begins with a vowel sound, such as mon amie.
mon père, ma mère, mes parents
my father, my mother, my parents
Les possessifs — Sorted by productive status
FormEnglishStatus
mon, ma, mesmyProductive
ton, ta, tesyour (familiar)Supported, mainly in questions
son, sa, seshis / herReference and guided recognition
  • Voici mon père et ma mère. Here are my father and my mother.
  • Quel âge a ton frère ? (supported question) How old is your brother?

In this family chapter, son, sa, and ses will usually translate as "his" or "her." French chooses the form according to the noun possessed, not according to the owner's gender. You will practice these more fully in a later chapter; for now, simply recognize them when you see them, for example in son père or sa mère.

4.3 Describing one person and stating age

Language Focus

In this chapter, describe people with the pattern subject + être + adjective, selecting from the small adjective table above. Adjective placement directly before or after a noun will be developed later in the course.

  • Mon père est grand. My father is tall.
  • Ma sœur est sympathique. My sister is nice.
Building Block — Stating a supported age with avoir
Subject + selected form of avoir + supported number + ans
To state an exact age in the pattern taught here, use avoir + number + ans. Do not translate the English pattern as je suis vingt ans. Consult the number reference table when needed.
J'ai vingt ans. Mon frère a dix-huit ans.
I am twenty years old. My brother is eighteen years old.

Model Dialogue: Voici ma famille

Context: Amina shows Thomas a photo of her family.

Amina : Voici ma famille. Here is my family.
Thomas : Tu as des frères ou des sœurs ? Do you have brothers or sisters?
Amina : Oui, j'ai un frère et une sœur. Yes, I have a brother and a sister.
Thomas : Quel âge a ton frère ? How old is your brother?
Amina : Il a dix-huit ans. Ma sœur a quinze ans. He is eighteen. My sister is fifteen.
Thomas : Comment est ton frère ? What is your brother like?
Amina : Il est grand et sympathique. He is tall and nice.

Quel âge a ton frère ? and Comment est ton frère ? are fixed, supported questions in this chapter; you are not expected to build new questions of this kind freely yet.

Listening Practice

If no audio recording is available, ask a partner, instructor, or text-to-speech tool to read the lines below aloud, or read them aloud to yourself slowly.

Listening script
Je m'appelle Karim. J'ai vingt-deux ans. J'ai un frère. Il a vingt-cinq ans. Il est grand. Ma mère est sympathique.
My name is Karim. I am twenty-two years old. I have a brother. He is twenty-five years old. He is tall. My mother is nice.

  2. How old is Karim's brother, and what is he like?
  3. What adjective describes Karim's mother?

Speaking Practice

Pratique contrôlée — Controlled practice

Say three sentences: one using j'ai, one using il a or elle a, and one using mon, ma, or mes. You may consult the number reference table for any age.

Échange guidé — Supported exchange

With a partner, ask and answer Tu as des frères ou des sœurs ? Then ask and answer one age question using the fixed model Quel âge a ton frère/ta sœur ?

Pratique individuelle — Solo alternative

Record yourself presenting one family member or friend in four lines: Voici..., their age with avoir, and two short descriptions with être plus an adjective from the table.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Introducing someone with voici

Voici is a simple, neutral way to introduce a person, a photo, or an object across Francophone communities: voici mon frère, voici ma sœur. It works in both casual and more careful speech.

Regional Variation — Talking about family

Family structures and how openly people discuss age vary across Francophone communities and individual families. Asking about age is common in casual conversation among peers in many contexts, but treat it as a natural conversational topic here rather than a universal rule for every situation or every speaker.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Using être for age

Error: Je suis vingt ans.

Correction: J'ai vingt ans. To state an exact age in the pattern taught in this chapter, use avoir + number + ans.

Common Mistake — mon/ma confusion

Error: mon sœur

Correction: ma sœur, because sœur is feminine.

Common Mistake — son/sa confusion

Error: sa père

Correction: son père, because père is masculine.

Common Mistake — Adjective agreement

Error: ma sœur est grand

Correction: ma sœur est grande.

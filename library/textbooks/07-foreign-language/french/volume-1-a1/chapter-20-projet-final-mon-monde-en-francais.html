Projet final : Mon monde en français

Final Assessment · Speak and Write French: Foundations of Practical Communication

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Introduce yourself and others, and exchange personal information, using tu and vous appropriately (Ch. 1–5).
  2. Describe your home, daily routine, food preferences, and shopping habits (Ch. 6–10).
  3. Communicate about getting around a city, leisure plans, health, and school or work (Ch. 11–15).
  4. Narrate connected past events using the passé composé and the imparfait, organized with sequencing connectors (Ch. 16–19).
  5. Combine present, near-future, and past communication in a single connected conversation or text.
  6. Ask and answer a wide range of practical and personal questions across registers and situations.
  7. Understand extended spoken and written French that mixes tenses, registers, and topics from across the volume.
  8. Produce a final integrated project — spoken and written — that presents "my world" in French: who you are, your daily life, your community, and a story from your own past.

Communication Scenario

Marc has been asked to record a short video and write a companion page for an international exchange program he is applying to. The program wants each applicant to present "mon monde en français" — my world in French — so that future host families and partner students can get to know them before they meet. Marc must introduce himself, describe his daily life and neighborhood, talk about his interests and plans, and tell a short story about something that happened to him, all in a single connected presentation. This chapter is your own version of that same project: an integrated performance that draws on everything you have learned since Chapter 1, without any new grammar or vocabulary system.

  1. Introduce yourself and describe your everyday life and community.
  2. Talk about your interests, plans, and routines.
  3. Tell a short connected story about a real or imagined past experience.
  4. Ask and answer questions about someone else's "monde en français."

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Applying the whole system

A final presentation moves across many pronunciation systems in a short space: oral and nasal vowels, front rounded vowels, the French r, required and optional liaison, enchaînement, elision, present-tense final-letter patterns, auxiliary–participle rhythm in the passé composé, and the shared /ɛ/ vowel of -ais, -ait, and -aient in the imparfait.

Before recording or reading your final project aloud, review required liaison in nous avons, vous êtes, and after plural articles and possessives. Review enchaînement when an already pronounced final consonant carries into a following vowel, as in avec elle. In the imparfait, -ais, -ait, and -aient normally share the vowel /ɛ/, while their final written consonant letters are silent. In the passé composé, the past participle normally contains an audible vowel, such as /e/ in parlé, /i/ in fini, or /y/ in vu, while many final consonant and agreement letters remain silent. No new sound or rule appears here; apply the previously learned system at a natural, connected pace.

Grammar: Un bilan complet du système

Purpose: consolidate and apply, in a single integrated performance, the complete grammar system developed across Volume I — without introducing any new productive structure.

20.1 The complete system, reviewed as one map

Language Focus

Every system below has already been taught. What is new in Chapter 20 is only the requirement to move between them naturally within one connected presentation at an integrated A2 level.

  • Present tense for who you are and what you usually do: J'habite à Lyon et je travaille dans une école. I live in Lyon and I work at a school.
  • Aller + infinitif for near-future plans: Ce week-end, je vais voir mes grands-parents. This weekend, I'm going to see my grandparents.
  • Passé composé / imparfait for a connected past story: L'année dernière, nous sommes allés en Bretagne. Il pleuvait tous les jours, mais nous étions très contents. Last year, we went to Brittany. It rained every day, but we were very happy.

20.2 Moving between time frames in one presentation

Building Block — The complete presentation pattern, reviewed
[présent: identity, routine, community] → [aller + infinitif: plans] → [connector + imparfait/passé composé: a past story]
This sequence simply places, in order, the present-tense system of Chapters 1–15, the near-future system of Chapter 12, and the narrative system consolidated in Chapters 16–19. No new tense, mood, or connector is required.
Je m'appelle Marc, j'ai vingt-trois ans, et j'habite à Lyon avec ma famille. D'habitude, je me lève à sept heures, et je vais au travail à vélo. Ce week-end, je vais rendre visite à ma grand-mère à Tours. L'été dernier, nous avons voyagé ensemble en Bretagne. Il faisait souvent mauvais, mais nous étions très heureux.
My name is Marc, I'm twenty-three, and I live in Lyon with my family. Usually, I get up at seven, and I go to work by bike. This weekend, I'm going to visit my grandmother in Tours. Last summer, we traveled together in Brittany. The weather was often bad, but we were very happy.

Notice that every verb form belongs to a system already taught: the present (Ch. 1–15), aller + infinitif (Ch. 12), and the passé composé / imparfait contrast (Ch. 16–18), organized with the connectors from Chapter 19.

20.3 Asking about someone else's world

Language Focus

A complete conversation is not one-sided. Use question strategies already active since Chapter 3 — est-ce que, question words, and intonation questions — to ask a partner about their own life, plans, and stories: Où est-ce que tu habites ?, Qu'est-ce que tu vas faire ce week-end ?, Qu'est-ce qui s'est passé ?

Model Dialogue: Mon monde en français

Context: Marc records the spoken part of his exchange-program presentation, imagining a future host student asking him questions.

Marc : Bonjour ! Je m'appelle Marc. J'ai vingt-trois ans et j'habite à Lyon, dans un petit appartement avec ma sœur. Hello! My name is Marc. I'm twenty-three and I live in Lyon, in a small apartment with my sister.
Marc : D'habitude, je me lève tôt, je vais au travail à vélo, et le soir, je fais souvent la cuisine avec ma sœur. Dans mon quartier, il y a un beau marché où on peut acheter des fruits et des légumes frais. Usually, I get up early, I go to work by bike, and in the evening, I often cook with my sister. In my neighborhood, there's a beautiful market where you can buy fresh fruits and vegetables.
Marc : Ce week-end, je vais rendre visite à ma grand-mère à Tours. J'ai hâte, parce qu'elle raconte toujours des histoires intéressantes. This weekend, I'm going to visit my grandmother in Tours. I can't wait, because she always tells interesting stories.
Marc : Je voudrais vous raconter quelque chose qui s'est passé l'été dernier. D'abord, ma sœur et moi, nous sommes allés en Bretagne. Il faisait souvent mauvais, mais nous étions très heureux. Un jour, pendant que nous nous promenions, nous avons rencontré un vieux pêcheur qui nous a raconté l'histoire de son village. C'était un moment inoubliable. I'd like to tell you something that happened last summer. First, my sister and I went to Brittany. The weather was often bad, but we were very happy. One day, while we were walking, we met an old fisherman who told us the history of his village. It was an unforgettable moment.
Marc : Et vous, quel est votre monde ? J'ai hâte de faire votre connaissance ! And you, what is your world like? I can't wait to get to know you!

This presentation moves through the present tense (Ch. 1–15), aller + infinitif (Ch. 12), and the consolidated passé composé / imparfait narration with sequencing connectors (Ch. 16–19) — the same building blocks reviewed above, simply combined.

Speaking Practice

Pratique contrôlée — Controlled practice: three time frames

Say three connected statements:

  • one true present-tense statement about your current life;
  • one near-future statement using aller + infinitif;
  • one past statement using either the passé composé or the imparfait, according to the intended viewpoint.

Then answer one follow-up question about each statement.

Tâche communicative — Communicative task: mon monde en français

Present your own monde en français to a partner or small group in at least ten connected sentences. Include:

  • your identity and where you live;
  • at least three present-tense sentences about your home, neighborhood, routine, interests, school, or work;
  • at least one near-future plan using aller + infinitif;
  • a connected past story of at least five sentences;
  • at least three sequencing connectors;
  • at least two imparfait forms;
  • at least three passé composé forms.

Your partner asks at least three relevant follow-up questions: one about the present, one about the future, and one about the past. Answer all three questions.

Pratique individuelle — Solo alternative

Record a two-to-three-minute video presenting your monde en français. Meet all the requirements of the communicative task: at least ten connected sentences, at least three present-tense daily-life sentences, one near-future plan, a five-sentence past story, three sequencing connectors, two imparfait forms, and three passé composé forms. Then record three questions that a listener could reasonably ask — one about the present, one about the future, and one about the past — and record a comprehensible response to each question.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Presenting yourself across the Francophone world

Introducing your daily life, your neighborhood, and a personal story to someone new is a common feature of exchange programs, professional introductions, and online profiles across French-speaking communities, though the expected length, tone, and level of personal detail vary by context, generation, and platform. A page written for a student exchange, a formal cover letter, and a casual social-media post would each strike a different balance between warmth and formality, even when written by the same person.

Regional Variation — Looking back across the volume

Throughout this volume, you have seen that contemporary French varies by region while remaining mutually intelligible: vocabulary choices in shopping and food (Ch. 8–9), pronunciation tendencies in nasal vowels and liaison (Ch. 6 onward), and address norms with tu and vous (Ch. 1 onward) may all differ somewhat between France, Belgium, Switzerland, Québec, and other Francophone communities. The neutral standard French modeled in this course remains broadly understood across these communities, but it is one productive model among several legitimate varieties, not the only correct French.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Staying in one time frame the whole time

Error: Writing an entire presentation only in the present tense, without any plan for the future or story from the past.

Correction: A complete presentation moves through present routine, a near-future plan with aller + infinitif, and a connected past story: J'habite à Lyon. Ce week-end, je vais voir ma famille. L'été dernier, nous sommes allés en Bretagne.

Common Mistake — Losing the passé composé / imparfait contrast under pressure

Error: Writing Il a fait mauvais, mais nous avons été contents to describe ongoing weather and mood surrounding a trip.

Correction: In the controlled narrative patterns practiced here, background and ongoing states normally use the imparfait when that is the intended viewpoint: Il faisait mauvais, mais nous étions contents.

Common Mistake — Forgetting to invite a response

Error: Ending a presentation abruptly after the past story, without any question or closing that invites the other person to respond.

Correction: Close with a question or invitation, as modeled since Chapter 19: Et vous, quel est votre monde ? or J'ai hâte de faire votre connaissance !

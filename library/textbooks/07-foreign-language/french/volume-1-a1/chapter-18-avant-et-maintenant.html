18

Avant et maintenant

Then and Now · Speak and Write French: Foundations of Practical Communication

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Form the imparfait for regular verbs and for common irregular verbs, including être.
  2. Use the imparfait to describe habitual past actions, ongoing background situations, and states such as age, time, and weather in the past.
  3. Distinguish, in context, when French requires the imparfait and when it requires the passé composé.
  4. Combine the imparfait and passé composé within the same narration.
  5. Use time expressions that signal habitual or background past time, such as autrefois and tous les jours.
  6. Ask and answer questions about how things used to be, compared with how they are now.
  7. Understand a short spoken or written account that mixes both past tenses.
  8. Write a short connected paragraph contrasting the past and the present.
  9. Apply accurate pronunciation of imparfait endings, recognizing that -ais, -ait, and -aient share the vowel sound /ɛ/ while their final written consonants are silent.

Communication Scenario

After his weekend in Tours, Marc calls his grandmother, Mireille, to thank her for the visit. She begins telling him what life was like in Tours when she was young — before television, before cars were common in her neighborhood, when she walked to school every day. Marc wants to understand how her daily life used to be, and how it compares with life now. This chapter continues Unit IV by giving you the tools to describe the past as it used to be, not just as a list of completed events.

  1. Describe what someone used to do regularly in the past.
  2. Describe the background situation, weather, age, or time surrounding a past event.
  3. Contrast a completed event with the ongoing situation around it.
  4. Compare life avant (before) and maintenant (now).

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Imparfait endings

The written endings -ais, -ais, -ait, and -aient are all pronounced identically, as an open è sound followed by silence: je parlais, tu parlais, il parlait, and ils parlaient all end in the same sound. Do not pronounce the final -s, -t, or -ent.

The nous and vous forms use the endings -ions and -iez. After a consonant-ending stem, these endings normally contain an audible [j] glide: nous parlions, vous parliez. These forms therefore sound different from je parlais, tu parlais, il parlait, and ils parlaient. Listen for this contrast between the four identical-sounding singular and third-person-plural forms and the two distinct nous/vous forms.

Grammar: L'imparfait et le contraste avec le passé composé

Purpose: describe habitual past actions, ongoing background situations, and states surrounding past events, and choose correctly between the imparfait and the passé composé when narrating the past.

18.1 Forming the imparfait

Language Focus

To form the imparfait of nearly every French verb, take the nous-form of the present tense, drop the -ons ending, and add the imparfait endings. This works even for verbs that are irregular in the present tense.

  • nous parlons → je parlais, tu parlais, il/elle/on parlait, nous parlions, vous parliez, ils/elles parlaient I used to speak / was speaking, you used to speak...
  • nous faisons → je faisais, nous faisions I used to do / was doing, we used to do...

être is the one common exception: its imparfait stem is ét-, not derived from a nous-form ending in -ons. j'étais, tu étais, il/elle/on était, nous étions, vous étiez, ils/elles étaient.

The impersonal verb pleuvoir does not have an ordinary nous form. Learn its common imperfect form as a fixed pattern: il pleuvait.

Language Note — Introductory spelling support

Some spelling changes preserve pronunciation in the imparfait. With manger, forms before a keep the written e: je mangeais, tu mangeais, il mangeait, ils mangeaient. The nous and vous forms are nous mangions and vous mangiez. With commencer, forms before a use ç: je commençais, tu commençais, il commençait, ils commençaient. The nous and vous forms are nous commencions and vous commenciez.

18.2 What the imparfait communicates

Building Block — Four common imparfait uses
habitual action  |  ongoing background  |  description  |  age, time, weather
The imparfait presents a past situation as ongoing, repeated, or simply true at the time, without marking its beginning or end.
Quand j'étais jeune, j'habitais à Tours. Il pleuvait, et j'avais dix ans.
When I was young, I lived in Tours. It was raining, and I was ten years old.

The imparfait does not tell the listener that a situation ended; it simply describes what was true or what used to happen, without focusing on completion.

18.3 Imparfait versus passé composé

Language Focus

The passé composé, which you learned in Chapters 16 and 17, presents an action as a completed event: something happened, and the narration moves forward. The imparfait presents the surrounding situation: what was going on, what things were like, or what used to happen regularly. The choice depends on how the speaker frames the situation, not on how long it actually lasted.

  • Il pleuvait quand je suis sorti. It was raining when I went out. pleuvait [background, ongoing] + suis sorti [completed event]
  • D'habitude, je me levais à sept heures, mais ce jour-là, je me suis levé à six heures. Usually, I used to get up at seven, but that day, I got up at six. me levais [habitual] vs. me suis levé [one specific completed action]
Deux temps du passé — Two past tenses compared
FunctionTense usedExample
A single completed actionpassé composéJe suis arrivé à huit heures.
A habitual, repeated past actionimparfaitJ'arrivais toujours à huit heures.
Background description surrounding an eventimparfaitIl faisait beau ce jour-là.
Age, time, or ongoing state in the pastimparfaitJ'avais dix ans. Il était midi.
An event that interrupts a background situationpassé composé within an imparfait backgroundJe dormais quand le téléphone a sonné.

This chapter treats the contrast through discourse examples rather than a single "completed versus incomplete" slogan. Both tenses can describe events of any actual duration; what matters is how the speaker frames the situation.

18.4 Combining both tenses in a narration

Building Block — Background plus event
[imparfait: background] + quand + [passé composé: event]
A common narrative pattern sets an ongoing background with the imparfait, then introduces a completed event with the passé composé, often linked by quand.
Nous nous promenions dans le vieux Lyon quand Nadia est tombée.
We were walking around old Lyon when Nadia fell.

This chapter covers only this introductory contrast. A comprehensive discourse-level treatment of aspect and narrative perspective is developed further at higher levels.

Model Dialogue: Quand j'avais ton âge...

Context: Marc calls his grandmother, Mireille, after his weekend in Tours, and she talks about her childhood there.

Marc : Mamie, c'était différent, Tours, quand tu étais jeune ? Grandma, was Tours different when you were young?
Mireille : Ah oui, très différent ! Nous n'avions pas de voiture. J'allais à l'école à pied tous les jours, même quand il faisait froid. Oh yes, very different! We didn't have a car. I used to walk to school every day, even when it was cold.
Marc : Et le quartier, il était comment ? And what was the neighborhood like?
Mireille : Il y avait moins de maisons, et tout le monde connaissait ses voisins. D'habitude, on se promenait ensemble le dimanche après-midi. There were fewer houses, and everyone knew their neighbors. Usually, we used to walk together on Sunday afternoons.
Marc : Vous n'aviez pas de télévision, alors ? So you didn't have a television?
Mireille : Non, pas au début. Un jour, ton arrière-grand-père a acheté une télévision, et tout le quartier est venu la regarder chez nous ! No, not at first. One day, your great-grandfather bought a television, and the whole neighborhood came to watch it at our house!
Marc : C'est incroyable ! Et tu faisais quoi, le samedi, quand tu étais adolescente ? That's incredible! And what did you use to do on Saturdays, when you were a teenager?
Mireille : Chaque été, j'allais chez ma tante à la campagne. Mais je me souviens d'un samedi où il pleuvait, et je suis restée à la maison pour lire. Every summer, I used to go to my aunt's house in the countryside. But I remember one Saturday when it was raining, and I stayed home to read.

Notice how Mireille uses the imparfait for habits and description (j'allais, il y avait, il pleuvait) but switches to the passé composé for single completed events (a acheté, je suis restée).

Speaking Practice

Pratique contrôlée — Controlled practice: imparfait or passé composé?

Reading Practice

Le quartier de Mamie — Grandma's neighborhood

Quand Mireille était petite, sa famille habitait dans un petit quartier près du centre-ville de Tours. Il n'y avait pas beaucoup de voitures, et les enfants jouaient souvent dans la rue après l'école. Mireille se levait tôt tous les matins, et elle marchait jusqu'à l'école avec ses deux frères. D'habitude, le week-end, toute la famille se promenait au bord de la Loire. Un dimanche, pourtant, il a beaucoup plu, et personne n'est sorti. Mireille se souvient encore de ce jour-là, parce que sa mère a préparé un grand déjeuner, et toute la famille est restée à table pendant des heures à raconter des histoires.

Tâche de lecture — Reading task:

  1. What did children often do in Mireille's neighborhood after school?
  2. What did the family usually do on weekends?
  3. What was different about the one Sunday described in the passage?
  4. Why does Mireille still remember that particular day?

Writing Workshop

Complete the following linked writing tasks in order.

Tâche 1 — Tense selection and formation: Complete the following five sentences using the correct form of the verb in either the imparfait or the passé composé, according to the context.

  1. Quand Mireille était petite, elle ______ à l'école à pied tous les jours. (aller)
  2. Le quartier ______ très calme à cette époque-là. (être)
  3. Il ______ quand le téléphone a sonné. (pleuvoir)
  4. Nous ______ dans le centre-ville quand Marc est entré dans son ancien lycée. (se promener)
  5. Un jour, le père de Mireille ______ une télévision. (acheter)

Tâche 2 — Connected paragraph: Write six to eight sentences comparing how something used to be with how it is now. Include at least three imparfait forms describing the past, at least one present-tense comparison, and one specific completed event in the passé composé that caused, marked, or illustrates a change. Include at least one habitual or background time expression such as autrefois, d'habitude, or quand j'étais jeune.

  1. Choose your subject. Pick a real or imagined situation involving a past habit or background, a present-day comparison, and a specific change.
  2. Plan your viewpoints. Decide which ideas present past habits or background, which sentence compares the situation with the present, and which event should be foregrounded as a completed change.
  3. Draft your paragraph. Use the imparfait to present past habits or background, the present tense to show how things are now, and the passé composé to present one foregrounded completed event.
  4. Read your paragraph aloud once. Listen for the shared /ɛ/ vowel in -ais, -ait, and -aient without pronouncing their final consonant letters, and pronounce -ions and -iez clearly.
  5. Revise. Confirm that each tense matches the intended viewpoint and context. In the controlled patterns practiced here, habitual and background ideas normally use the imparfait, the current comparison uses the present tense, and a foregrounded completed event normally uses the passé composé.

Liste de révision — Editing checklist:

  • The paragraph contains at least three accurately formed imparfait verbs.
  • The paragraph contains at least one clear present-tense comparison.
  • The paragraph contains at least one foregrounded completed event in the passé composé.
  • Tense choices match the intended viewpoint and context.
  • Past habits, descriptions, age, time, weather, and background are presented with the imparfait in the controlled patterns practiced here.
  • The specific change or completed event is presented with the passé composé.
  • être in the imparfait uses the stem ét-.
  • pleuvoir, if used, appears as il pleuvait.
  • Forms of manger and commencer, if used, preserve the required spelling.
  • At least one habitual or background time expression appears.
  • Accents, apostrophes, and punctuation are accurate.
  • No vocabulary or grammar outside Chapters 1–18 is required.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Talking with grandparents about the past

Asking grandparents or older relatives about how life used to be is a common and valued way to practice narrating the past in French, and it also builds real family connection. Life details vary enormously by region, generation, and family, so avoid assuming that any single grandparent's account, including Mireille's, represents every French family's experience of the twentieth century.

Regional Variation — A note on this system

The imparfait and the passé composé are used widely across the Francophone world in everyday narration. Usage, frequency, and informal storytelling patterns can vary by region and register, while the standard contrast taught here remains the productive model for this level. Additional literary past tenses, including the passé simple, remain outside the scope of this chapter.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Using the passé composé for a habitual action

Error: Writing tous les jours, j'ai marché à l'école to describe a repeated childhood habit.

Correction: In the controlled contrast practiced here, a repeated past habit normally takes the imparfait: tous les jours, je marchais à l'école.

Common Mistake — Building the imparfait of être from a present-tense nous-form

Error: Writing j'étions or another form based on a regular pattern.

Correction: être has the irregular stem ét-: j'étais, nous étions, ils étaient.

Common Mistake — Using the imparfait for a single completed event

Error: Writing hier, j'allais au cinéma to describe one specific outing yesterday.

Correction: In the controlled narrative pattern practiced here, a foregrounded completed event normally takes the passé composé: hier, je suis allé au cinéma.

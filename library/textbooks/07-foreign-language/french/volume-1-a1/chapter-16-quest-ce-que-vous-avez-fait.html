16

Qu'est-ce que vous avez fait ?

Completed Past Events · Speak and Write French: Foundations of Practical Communication

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Form the passé composé with avoir for regular and common irregular verbs.
  2. Choose the correct past participle for -er, -ir, and -re verbs, and for a set of high-frequency irregular verbs.
  3. Ask and answer questions about what someone did, using qu'est-ce que and est-ce que with the passé composé.
  4. Use ne... pas correctly with the passé composé.
  5. Recognize common time expressions used with completed past events, such as hier and la semaine dernière.
  6. Understand short spoken and written accounts of a person's recent activities.
  7. Narrate, in speech and in writing, a short account of what you did recently.
  8. Apply accurate pronunciation and spelling of past participles, and maintain established subject-pronoun liaison with avoir.

Communication Scenario

Léa's internship has now begun. On Monday morning, she runs into her friend Sophie, who wants to hear everything about the first week: what Léa did at work, whether she visited any new places, what she ate for lunch with her colleagues, and how her weekend went. This chapter opens Unit IV by giving you the tools to describe many common completed actions using verbs that form the passé composé with avoir. Chapter 17 will add verbs that use être, so that you can narrate an even wider range of past events.

  1. Ask someone what they did.
  2. Describe completed actions from a recent day or week.
  3. Talk about a first day at an internship or job.
  4. Answer questions about a past event using oui or non with correct negation.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Past participle endings and liaison with subject pronouns

Regular -er past participles end in written -é, pronounced /e/: travaillé and parlé end in a clearly pronounced closed [e] sound. The accented vowel is pronounced, but there is no additional final consonant sound. Regular -ir past participles end in -i, pronounced /i/ (fini, choisi), and regular -re past participles end in -u, pronounced /y/ (attendu, répondu). None of these final vowels is followed by a pronounced consonant in ordinary speech.

This chapter reviews the subject-pronoun liaison you already know rather than introducing a new liaison rule. Continue to link nous‿avons, vous‿avez, ils‿ont, and elles‿ont, exactly as you practiced with other vowel-initial words in earlier chapters. This is liaison between the subject pronoun and avoir; it does not mean that every conjugated auxiliary automatically links to the past participle that follows it. Elision is also active in j'ai and je n'ai pas.

Grammar: Le passé composé avec avoir

Purpose: describe completed actions and events in the past using a conjugated form of avoir plus a past participle.

16.1 Form: avoir + past participle

Language Focus

The passé composé with avoir has two parts: a present-tense form of avoir (the auxiliary, or helping verb) and a past participle. Most French verbs use avoir as their auxiliary; a smaller group uses être, which you will learn in Chapter 17.

  • J'ai travaillé toute la journée. I worked all day. j'ai [aux.] + travaillé [participle]
  • Elle a fini son rapport. She finished her report.

16.2 Regular past participles

Building Block — Regular participle patterns
-er → -é  |  -ir → -i  |  -re → -u
Drop the infinitive ending and add the pattern ending. These patterns form the past participles of the regular -er, regular -ir, and regular -re verbs scheduled in this chapter. Many frequent French verbs have irregular past participles that must be learned individually, as in the table below.
parler → parlé · finir → fini · attendre → attendu
to speak → spoken · to finish → finished · to wait → waited

16.4 Conjugating avoir + participle

Language Focus

Only avoir changes form according to the subject; the past participle usually does not change with avoir in the sentences you will produce at this level.

  • j'ai mangé, tu as mangé, il/elle/on a mangé, nous avons mangé, vous avez mangé, ils/elles ont mangé I ate, you ate, he/she/one ate, we ate, you ate, they ate

Detailed agreement of the past participle with a preceding direct object is not covered in this volume; it belongs to a later stage of study.

16.5 Negation and questions

Building Block — Negation with the passé composé
ne + [avoir] + pas + [past participle]
ne... pas surrounds the conjugated form of avoir, not the past participle.
Je n'ai pas fini mon travail.
I have not finished my work.

Questions with the passé composé use the same strategies you already know: Qu'est-ce que tu as fait ? (intonation-neutral, est-ce que), Tu as mangé ? (rising intonation), or As-tu mangé ? (inversion, reserved for careful or formal contexts).

Model Dialogue: Qu'est-ce que tu as fait hier ?

Context: Sophie asks Léa about her first days at the internship.

Sophie : Alors, qu'est-ce que tu as fait cette semaine ? So, what did you do this week?
Léa : J'ai commencé mon stage lundi. J'ai beaucoup travaillé, et j'ai parlé français toute la journée avec mes collègues. I started my internship on Monday. I worked a lot, and I spoke French all day with my colleagues.
Sophie : Est-ce que tu as déjà déjeuné avec eux ? Have you already had lunch with them?
Léa : Oui, j'ai mangé avec eux mardi. On a pris un café après, et j'ai vu le quartier près du bureau. Yes, I ate with them on Tuesday. We had a coffee afterward, and I saw the neighborhood near the office.
Sophie : Et Marc, il a déjà commencé son stage ? And Marc, has he already started his internship?
Léa : Pas encore, il commence lundi prochain. Mais hier soir, on a mangé ensemble, et il m'a posé beaucoup de questions. Not yet, he starts next Monday. But last night, we ate together, and he asked me a lot of questions.
Sophie : Et toi, tu n'as pas eu peur le premier jour ? And you, weren't you scared the first day?
Léa : Un peu ! Mais j'ai eu de la chance : mes collègues ont été très gentils avec moi. A little! But I was lucky: my colleagues were very kind to me.

This dialogue uses only avoir as the auxiliary. Notice that on a pris, j'ai eu, and ont été illustrate three common irregular past participles introduced above.

Reading Practice

Le premier jour de Léa — Léa's first day

Lundi matin, Léa a commencé son stage. Elle a rencontré son équipe et elle a beaucoup travaillé sur un projet important. À midi, elle a déjeuné avec deux collègues ; ils ont parlé de leurs études et de leurs projets. L'après-midi, Léa a répondu à beaucoup de messages, et elle a fini une première tâche avant cinq heures. Le soir, elle a téléphoné à sa famille pour leur raconter sa journée. Elle a eu un peu peur le matin, mais finalement, elle a passé une très bonne première journée. Elle n'a pas encore visité tous les bureaux, mais elle va le faire cette semaine.

Tâche de lecture — Reading task:

  1. What did Léa do in the morning?
  2. Who did Léa have lunch with, and what did they talk about?
  3. What did Léa do in the afternoon, and did she finish it?
  4. What has Léa not yet done, according to the last sentence?

Writing Workshop

Complete the following linked writing tasks in order.

Tâche 1 — Sentence transformation: Rewrite five present-tense sentences that use scheduled Chapter 16 verbs forming the passé composé with avoir. Keep the same subject and use the correct form of avoir plus the correct past participle.

Tâche 2 — Connected paragraph: Write a short paragraph (six to eight sentences) describing what you did yesterday or last week, using only scheduled Chapter 16 verbs or other previously learned verbs that form the passé composé with avoir. Do not use aller, reflexive verbs, or any other verb that forms the passé composé with être. Include at least one regular -er participle, one regular -ir or -re participle, two irregular past participles from this chapter, one accurate negative sentence, and one completed-past time expression such as hier or la semaine dernière.

  1. List your activities. Choose six to eight things you actually did recently.
  2. Check your verbs. Confirm the correct auxiliary form of avoir and the correct past participle for each verb.
  3. Draft your paragraph. Organize activities in a logical order, using time expressions to connect them.
  4. Read your paragraph aloud once. Pronounce each past-participle ending clearly and maintain established subject-pronoun liaison with avoir.
  5. Revise. Confirm negation surrounds avoir, not the past participle.

Liste de révision — Editing checklist:

  • Every past-tense verb used in this Chapter 16 task is a scheduled verb that forms the passé composé with avoir and contains the correct auxiliary form plus a past participle.
  • Regular past participles follow the -é / -i / -u pattern correctly.
  • Irregular past participles (eu, été, fait, vu, pris) are spelled correctly.
  • ne... pas surrounds avoir, not the participle.
  • At least one time expression situates the narration in the past.
  • Accents, apostrophes, and punctuation are accurate throughout.
  • No vocabulary or grammar from outside Chapters 1–16 has been used.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Talking about a first day at work or an internship

In many Francophone workplaces, colleagues commonly ask a new intern or employee informal, friendly questions about their first day or first week, often over a shared coffee or lunch break. Sharing a simple, positive account of one's first experiences is a common and welcome form of small talk in many settings, though the degree of formality can vary by workplace, generation, and region.

Regional Variation — A note on this system

The passé composé is used widely across the Francophone world for completed past events in speech and writing. Whether a particular verb takes avoir or être as its auxiliary depends on the verb itself, not on region, and register can also affect which past tense a speaker or writer chooses. Some literary and very formal written registers instead use the passé simple, a different past tense that is receptive-only material well beyond this volume. You do not need to produce it at this level.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Forgetting the auxiliary avoir

Error: Writing j'ai mangé as simply mangé, dropping the helping verb.

Correction: The passé composé always needs both parts: j'ai mangé, never mangé alone.

Common Mistake — Placing pas after the past participle

Error: Writing j'ai fini pas mon travail, modeled on simple present-tense negation.

Correction: Negation surrounds avoir, not the participle: je n'ai pas fini mon travail.

Common Mistake — Regularizing an irregular past participle

Error: Writing j'ai faisé instead of the irregular form.

Correction: faire has the irregular past participle fait: j'ai fait.

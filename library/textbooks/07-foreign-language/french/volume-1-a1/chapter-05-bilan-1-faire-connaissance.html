Bilan 1 : Faire connaissance

Unit I Assessment: Getting to Know Someone · Speak and Write French: Foundations of Practical Communication

Chapter Objectives

This chapter introduces no new grammar. By the end of it, you will be able to:

  1. Integrate practiced greetings, name formulas, origin, age, and family information into one connected first-meeting exchange.
  2. Select correctly between the practiced forms of être and avoir covered in Chapters 1–4.
  3. Ask and answer using the taught fixed questions, including d'où es-tu ? / d'où êtes-vous ? and quel âge as-tu ?
  4. Maintain either tu or vous consistently within a single supplied interaction, and recognize that plural vous is not the same as formal singular vous.
  5. Use mon, ma, and mes accurately to introduce a family member or friend with voici.
  6. Select one or two practiced adjective forms to describe a person, matching gender correctly.
  7. Complete a short, supported personal profile using a supplied sentence frame.
  8. Perform a short first-meeting exchange using only Unit I material, with the reference tables available for consultation.

Communication Scenario

Amina and Thomas meet a new student, Léa, at a café. The three of them get to know one another: they greet each other, introduce themselves, ask where everyone is from, and talk about family. This chapter asks you to combine everything from Chapters 1 through 4 into one connected set of tasks, rather than to learn anything new.

  1. Every task in this chapter uses vocabulary and grammar introduced in Chapters 1–4, supported by the Unit I Assessment Reference below for place names, nationalities, and numbers.
  2. Pay attention to keeping tu or vous appropriate and consistent within the interaction, while selecting the correct practiced verb for each communicative function.

Pronunciation Review

This chapter reviews and connects pronunciation features from Chapters 1–4 rather than introducing a new one.

Pronunciation Focus — Reviewing liaison, elision, and adjective endings together

The main productive liaison patterns assessed here are vous êtes and vous avez. Other plural liaison forms, such as nous avons or ils ont, may appear in listening for recognition only; they are not independent production requirements in this chapter.

Listen for and practice producing:

  • Liaison in vous êtes and vous avez.
  • Elision in je m'appelle, j'ai, and j'habite, where je loses its vowel before another vowel sound.
  • Silent final consonants made audible by feminine agreement, as in grand versus grande, and français versus française.

Language Focus: Bringing the Systems Together

Purpose: use greetings, name formulas, origin, age, family, and description together in one realistic exchange, selecting correctly among the forms already practiced.

5.1 Être and avoir side by side

Language Focus

By this point you have practiced specific uses of two irregular verbs that do different jobs. In the Unit I patterns assessed here, use avoir for age and for sentences such as j'ai un frère. Use être for the practiced patterns of student identity, nationality, and description. This is not a claim that one verb always covers a whole category in French generally — it describes only the patterns this course has practiced so far.

5.2 Register within one exchange

Building Block — Keeping tu or vous consistent
Choose tu or vous based on the relationship at the start of the exchange, and keep that choice throughout
Students meeting as peers normally use tu with one another. Note that vous has two distinct uses: it is the formal singular form for one person you address politely, and it is also the ordinary plural form used to address two or more people, regardless of formality. Speaking to two peers together with vous and then addressing one of them individually with tu is not a formality switch — it reflects the change from plural to singular address.
Comment tu t'appelles ? D'où es-tu ? Tu as des frères ou des sœurs ?
What's your name? Where are you from? Do you have brothers or sisters?

5.3 Asking with the taught fixed questions

Language Focus

This chapter recycles the question strategies and fixed questions introduced so far: intonation questions, and the taught question-word patterns from Chapters 1, 3, and 4. Combining them lets you build a complete first exchange with a new person, using questions you have already practiced rather than newly generated ones.

  • Tu es étudiante ? Are you a student? (intonation question)
  • D'où es-tu ? Where are you from? (taught fixed question)
  • Quel âge as-tu ? How old are you? (taught fixed question)

Model Dialogue: Au café, entre amis

Context: Amina and Thomas meet Léa, a new student, at a café for the first time.

Amina : Salut ! Je m'appelle Amina. Voici mon ami Thomas. Hi! My name is Amina. Here's my friend Thomas.
Léa : Enchantée ! Je m'appelle Léa. Nice to meet you! My name is Léa.
Thomas : D'où es-tu ? Where are you from?
Léa : Je viens de France. J'habite à Paris. I'm from France. I live in Paris.
Amina : Tu as des frères ou des sœurs ? Do you have brothers or sisters?
Léa : Oui, j'ai une sœur. Elle a dix-huit ans. Elle est sympathique. Yes, I have a sister. She's eighteen. She's nice.
Thomas : Quel âge as-tu ? How old are you?
Léa : J'ai vingt ans. À bientôt ! I'm twenty. See you soon!

Notice that Léa uses être for description (elle est sympathique) and avoir for family and age (j'ai une sœur, elle a dix-huit ans, j'ai vingt ans) within the same short exchange, and that every place name and number used appears in the Unit I Assessment Reference above.

Speaking Practice

Pratique contrôlée — Controlled practice: your own profile

Say five sentences aloud, in this order, using the Unit I Assessment Reference as needed: (1) say your name using the taught formula; (2) say where you are from; (3) state your age; (4) introduce one family member or friend with voici; (5) describe that person with one practiced adjective.

Tâche communicative — Communicative task: meet someone new

With a partner, act out a first meeting using this fixed exchange frame: greet each other and give your names; ask d'où es-tu ? and answer; ask quel âge as-tu ? and answer; introduce one family member or friend with voici and one adjective; say goodbye. Use tu throughout, since you are meeting as peers, and consult the reference tables as needed.

Pratique individuelle — Solo alternative

Record yourself playing both roles of the fixed exchange frame above: greetings, names, origin, age, one family member with voici and one adjective, and a leave-taking.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — A first conversation

A first conversation between new acquaintances in French, as in many languages, typically moves through a predictable sequence: greeting, name, a bit of personal background such as origin, and a polite leave-taking. Recognizing this pattern helps learners anticipate what information is likely to come next in a real exchange.

People may describe birthplace, residence, nationality, family background, and language in different ways, and these categories should not be assumed from one another. In this unit, learners practice asking and answering only the information a speaker chooses to share.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Confusing être and avoir for age

Error: Je suis vingt ans.

Correction: J'ai vingt ans. In the Unit I patterns assessed here, use avoir for age.

Common Mistake — Confusing être and avoir for origin

Error: J'ai du Canada.

Correction: Je viens du Canada. Use the practiced form of venir for origin, habiter for residence, and selected forms of être for nationality and description: Je viens du Canada. (origin) · J'habite au Canada. (residence) · Je suis canadienne. (nationality) · Je suis sympathique. (description).

Common Mistake — Possessive agreement

Error: mon sœur

Correction: ma sœur. Check the possessive against the noun's gender: mon frère, but ma sœur.

Common Mistake — Adjective agreement

Error: ma sœur est grand

Correction: ma sœur est grande. The adjective must agree with the person it describes.

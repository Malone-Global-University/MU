Une journée ordinaire

Daily Routines · Speak and Write French: Foundations of Practical Communication

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be beginning to:

  1. Use a small set of regular pronominal (reflexive) verbs to describe routine actions.
  2. Match the reflexive pronouns me, te, se, nous, and vous to supplied subjects.
  3. Recognize and reuse selected supported formulas with se lever and se réveiller.
  4. Ask and tell the time using supplied models.
  5. Sequence three or four routine actions using d'abord, ensuite, puis, and enfin.
  6. Form basic ne...pas sentences around a reflexive pronoun and its verb.
  7. Complete a short, supported daily routine card with reference tables available.

Communication Scenario

Amina and Léa are texting each other about their mornings before class. Amina wants to know when Léa wakes up, gets ready, and leaves for school, and Léa asks the same about Amina. This chapter continues Unit II by moving from describing a home to describing what happens inside it every day.

  1. This chapter introduces regular pronominal verbs in the present tense, a small number of supported routine formulas, telling time, sequencing expressions, and basic negation with ne...pas. It does not introduce the near future, the past tense, or any negative structure beyond ne...pas.
  2. Vocabulary and grammar from Chapters 1–6, including il y a, the contractions, and the household vocabulary, remain active and should be recycled wherever natural.

Pronunciation: Reflexive Pronouns

Pronunciation Focus — Elision and clear rhythm with reflexive pronouns

The reflexive pronouns me, te, and se elide to m', t', and s' before a verb beginning with a vowel sound, as in je m'habille, tu t'habilles, and elle s'habille. The elision belongs to the reflexive pronoun, not to any other word in the sentence.

In nous nous lavons and vous vous lavez, pronounce the two pronouns clearly as separate words. There is no liaison between them, because the second pronoun begins with a consonant sound (/n/ and /v/).

Practice clear rhythm in short groups such as je me lave, tu te couches, nous nous lavons, and vous vous préparez, keeping each pronoun distinct.

You have already learned genuine liaison in vowel-initial expressions such as vous êtes, vous avez, and nous avons. There is no liaison between the subject pronoun and the reflexive pronoun in nous nous lavons or vous vous lavez, because the reflexive pronoun begins with a consonant sound.

Language Focus: Pronominal Verbs, Time, and Negation

Purpose: narrate a sequence of daily actions with a small set of regular pronominal verbs, state when they happen, and say what does not happen.

7.1 Pronominal verbs in the present tense

Language Focus

A pronominal verb is used with a reflexive pronoun that matches the subject. Some pronominal verbs describe an action directed toward the subject, such as se laver ("to wash oneself"). Others are conventional pronominal expressions used for routines or changes of state, such as se lever and se réveiller. In both cases, the reflexive pronoun is a required part of the verb and is placed immediately before the conjugated form.

Se laver — regular pronominal verb, full present-tense conjugation
SubjectReflexive pronounComplete form
jemeje me lave
tutetu te laves
il / elle / onseil / elle / on se lave
nousnousnous nous lavons
vousvousvous vous lavez
ils / ellesseils / elles se lavent

Other productive verbs in this chapter — se doucher, s'habiller, se préparer, and se coucher — follow this same regular pattern: the reflexive pronoun plus the ordinary -er ending you already know.

  • Je me lave le matin. I wash up in the morning.
  • Nous nous préparons vite. We get ready quickly.
  • Elle s'habille. She gets dressed. (elision: se → s')

Some common routine verbs have changes inside the verb form itself. For now, learn je me lève, tu te lèves, je me réveille, and tu te réveilles as supported routine formulas. Their full spelling-change patterns will be developed later in the course.

7.2 Telling time

Building Block — Asking and giving the time
Quelle heure est-il ? — Il est + [number] + heure(s) (+ et quart / et demie / moins le quart)
In the number-based clock expressions practiced in this chapter, heure or heures appears. Use une heure, not un heure, because heure is feminine, and use heures after other numbers. To say when something happens, use à before the time.
Il est sept heures et demie. Je quitte la maison à huit heures.
It is seven thirty. I leave the house at eight o'clock.

7.3 Sequencing a routine

Language Focus

Use d'abord, ensuite, puis, and enfin at the beginning of a sentence or clause to organize a narrated routine into a clear order, the same way "first," "next," and "finally" work in English.

  • D'abord, je me lave. Ensuite, je m'habille. First, I wash up. Next, I get dressed.

7.4 Negation with pronominal verbs

Building Block — Ne...pas around a pronominal verb
[subject] + ne + [reflexive pronoun] + [conjugated verb] + pas
With a pronominal verb, ne...pas surrounds the reflexive pronoun and the conjugated verb together, since the two form a single unit. Ne remains unchanged; it does not become n' before me, te, se, nous, or vous. The reflexive pronoun itself, however, may elide before the vowel-initial verb, as in m'habille, t'habilles, or s'habille.
Je ne me lave pas le matin. Tu ne te couches pas tôt. Elle ne s'habille pas rapidement.
I don't wash up in the morning. You don't go to bed early. She doesn't get dressed quickly.

By contrast, before a vowel-initial non-pronominal verb, ne elides directly: Je n'habite pas ici. ("I don't live here.") Do not produce forms such as je n'm'habille pas, tu n't'habilles pas, or elle n's'habille pas; ne never elides before a reflexive pronoun.

This chapter uses only ne...pas. Other negative expressions are introduced later in the course and are not required here.

Model Dialogue: Ma journée

Context: Amina and Léa text each other about their morning routines before class.

Amina : Tu te réveilles à quelle heure le matin ? What time do you wake up in the morning?
Léa : Je me réveille à six heures et demie. D'abord, je me lève. Ensuite, je me douche. Puis, je m'habille. I wake up at six thirty. First I get up. Next I shower. Then I get dressed.
Amina : Tu prends le petit-déjeuner à quelle heure ? What time do you have breakfast?
Léa : Je prends le petit-déjeuner à sept heures. Enfin, je quitte la maison à huit heures moins le quart. I have breakfast at seven o'clock. Finally, I leave the house at a quarter to eight.
Léa : Et toi, tu te lèves tôt ? And you, do you get up early?
Amina : Non, je ne me lève pas tôt. Je me réveille à sept heures et demie. Ensuite, je me prépare. No, I don't get up early. I wake up at seven thirty. Then I get ready.
Amina : Tu te couches à quelle heure ? What time do you go to bed?
Léa : Je me couche à dix heures. Je ne me couche pas très tard pendant la semaine. I go to bed at ten o'clock. I don't go to bed very late during the week.

Je me réveille, tu te réveilles, je me lève, and tu te lèves are supported routine formulas in this dialogue, not evidence of a fully taught spelling-change paradigm.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Daily schedules and mealtimes

Daily schedules and mealtimes vary widely across Francophone communities, households, professions, regions, and individuals. Learners may encounter different expectations about lunch length, dinner time, school schedules, and working hours, and should not assume that one pattern represents every household or region.

Regional Variation — Talking about time across the Francophone world

The time expressions in this chapter are used productively across France and much of the Francophone world. Twenty-four-hour time is common in many written and official settings, including transportation schedules in France, Belgium, and Quebec. Both twelve-hour and twenty-four-hour forms may occur in speech, depending on region, speaker, and context; neither system is more correct than the other.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Missing reflexive pronoun

Error: Writing Je lave le matin when the intended meaning is "I wash myself in the morning."

Correction: Je me lave le matin. The reflexive pronoun is a required part of the verb; je lève does not mean "I get up" in French — the reflexive pronoun is what carries that meaning.

Common Mistake — Negation placement

Error: Writing Je me ne lave pas.

Correction: Ne comes before the reflexive pronoun, and pas comes after the conjugated verb: je ne me lave pas.

Common Mistake — Eliding ne before a reflexive pronoun

Error: Writing je n'm'habille pas.

Correction: Je ne m'habille pas. Ne never elides before a reflexive pronoun; only the reflexive pronoun itself elides before the vowel-initial verb.

Common Mistake — Forgetting heure(s) or its gender

Error: Writing il est un heure instead of using the feminine form.

Correction: Heure is feminine, so "one o'clock" is il est une heure, not un heure.

Scope Note — Supported spelling-change forms

In this chapter, reuse the supplied singular forms je me lève, tu te lèves, il/elle se lève, je me réveille, tu te réveilles, and il/elle se réveille. Forms such as nous nous levons, vous vous levez, ils se lèvent, nous nous réveillons, and ils se réveillent are correct French, but their complete spelling-change patterns are not production targets in this chapter.

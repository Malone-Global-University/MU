Langage juridique et administratif avancé

Legal-Administrative French · Speak and Write French: Specialized Mastery and Expert Communication

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Distinguish legal-adjacent, administrative language — where a precise obligation, a precise right, and the exact source of each must be stated without ambiguity — from the register, audience, implication, and live-interaction work of Unité I, and recognize when a communicative moment calls for this new precision.
  2. Use a newly productive obligation frame — être tenu de + infinitive — to state precisely what a person or party must do.
  3. Use a newly productive rights frame — avoir le droit de + infinitive and avoir droit à + noun — to state precisely what a person or party is entitled to do or receive.
  4. Use a newly productive legal-basis citation frame — en vertu de + noun — to name the specific source (a clause, a regulation, an article) that grounds an obligation or a right, rather than leaving the source implicit.
  5. Use a newly productive precision-hedging frame — sous réserve de + noun/infinitive — to state a condition or exception that limits an otherwise general obligation or right.
  6. Use a newly productive legal-administrative vocabulary set: un droit, une obligation, un bail, une clause, stipuler, un recours, à titre de.
  7. Recycle, without any new tense, mood, subjonctif trigger, or clitic pattern, the complete Unité I toolkit (register/tone, audience adaptation, implication/euphemism, and real-time interaction management) and the full Volume I–IV grammar inventory, selecting from among these rather than treating any of them as newly taught.
  8. Receive further controlled, receptive-only exposure to a small set of more formal legal-register vocabulary encountered in administrative notices and lease clauses.
  9. Read a municipal notice and a lease clause for their precise obligations, rights, sources, and conditions, distinguishing what a text actually commits a party to from what it merely mentions in passing.
  10. Complete a short mediation exercise (explaining a lease clause to a market vendor who has not read it closely) using all four of this chapter's newly productive frames at least once each.
  11. Move to legal-adjacent reading and listening — a municipal notice, a lease clause, and a tenants' meeting excerpt — opening Unité II: Domaines spécialisés et médiation professionnelle.

Communication Scenario

The city has sent the covered market's vendors a new municipal notice about stall assignments and a revised lease template, and several vendors — including the fruit seller M. Ouedraogo and the flower seller Mme Rigal, both regular sources for La Revue du quartier — are confused about what the new lease actually requires of them, what it entitles them to, and what parts of it depend on conditions that may or may not apply to their specific stall. Sophie and Karim, preparing a short explainer article for the magazine, realize that vague paraphrase will not do here: readers need to know exactly who must do what, exactly what they are entitled to, exactly which clause or regulation each rule comes from, and exactly which rules only apply under certain conditions. A friendly summary that blurs "you should probably" and "you are legally required to" could genuinely mislead a vendor into missing a deadline or giving up a right they actually have. Chapter 6 gives Sophie, Karim — and you — the tools to state an obligation, a right, a source, and a condition with the precision that legal-administrative language requires, while still explaining it in language a non-specialist vendor can act on.

  1. State precisely what a party is obligated to do, and distinguish this from what is merely advisable.
  2. State precisely what a party is entitled to do or receive.
  3. Name the specific clause, article, or regulation that a given obligation or right comes from.
  4. Identify and state the conditions or exceptions that limit an otherwise general rule.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Delivering precision without sounding cold

No new sound-spelling or vowel feature is introduced in this chapter. The task is delivery: legal-administrative precision can sound rigid or unfriendly if it is read aloud in a flat, list-like way, which risks losing a non-specialist listener. No single pitch contour is required for any of this chapter's frames. Use measured pacing and clear prosodic grouping; slight emphasis and brief pauses can help a listener distinguish the operative rule, its source, and its condition from one another, but the specific weight, pause length, and contour are delivery strategies to draw on as needed, not fixed pronunciation properties of être tenu de, en vertu de, or sous réserve de.

  • Stating an obligation: Vous êtes tenu de signaler tout changement d’horaire au moins une semaine à l’avance. — measured pace, slight emphasis on tenu de signaler to make the requirement register clearly.
  • Stating a right: À titre de commerçant régulier, vous avez droit à un emplacement fixe. — slight emphasis on droit à.
  • Citing the source: …en vertu de la clause 4 du bail. — a brief pause before the citation can help a listener hear that a source is coming.
  • Hedging with a condition: …sous réserve de l’accord écrit de la mairie. — measured pacing sets the condition apart from the obligation or right it qualifies.

Cumulative review: this chapter's spoken practice also recycles active sound-spelling, liaison, enchaînement, elision, and rhythm systems from earlier volumes. As a short recycling drill, practice vous êtes tenu for liaison between vous and êtes, avoir un recours for enchaînement of the normally pronounced final consonant in avoir’s related forms before a vowel, l’obligation for elision, and en vertu for nasal-vowel control and phrase rhythm.

Grammar: Dire un droit, une obligation, une source

Purpose: introduce a newly productive obligation frame (être tenu de + infinitive), a newly productive rights frame (avoir le droit de + infinitive / avoir droit à + noun), a newly productive legal-basis citation frame (en vertu de + noun), and a newly productive precision-hedging frame (sous réserve de + noun/infinitive), while recycling the complete Unité I toolkit. No new tense, mood, subjonctif trigger, or clitic pattern is introduced in this chapter.

Key distinction — a fifth, independent dimension: legal-administrative precision

Unité I built four independent dimensions: register/tone, audience adaptation, implication, and live interaction management. This chapter adds a fifth, independent dimension: stating exactly who must do what, who is entitled to what, on what basis, and under what condition. This dimension can combine with any of the four Unité I dimensions — a legal obligation can be explained to a general audience (Ch. 2), restated more soutenu for an official notice (Ch. 1), or negotiated live when two parties read a clause differently (Ch. 4) — but it is distinct from all of them, because it specifically concerns whether a statement has binding force, an entitlement, a named source, and a scope of application.

6.1 Stating an obligation precisely: être tenu de

Grammar Focus — What must be done, not merely what is advisable

Être tenu de + infinitive is newly productive. It states that a party is bound — by a lease, a regulation, or another named source — to perform an action, and is stronger and more precise than a general recommendation such as il vaudrait mieux or il serait bon de, both already active from earlier volumes. Using être tenu de where only a recommendation is meant overstates the force of a rule; using a vague recommendation where a genuine obligation exists understates it and can mislead a reader.

  • Chaque commerçant est tenu de libérer son emplacement avant sept heures les jours de nettoyage du marché. Each vendor is required to vacate their stall before seven o'clock on market cleaning days. est tenu de (newly productive) states a binding obligation, not a mere suggestion

6.2 Stating a right precisely: avoir le droit de / avoir droit à

Grammar Focus — What a party is entitled to do or receive

Avoir le droit de + infinitive states an entitlement to act; avoir droit à + noun states an entitlement to receive something. Both are newly productive. Distinguishing a right clearly from an obligation matters here: a vendor who does not realize a rule is a right, not an obligation, may not know they can decline, negotiate, or claim it.

  • À titre de commerçant régulier depuis plus de deux ans, Mme Rigal a le droit de refuser un changement d’emplacement sans justification écrite de la mairie. As a regular vendor for more than two years, Mme Rigal has the right to refuse a change of location without written justification from the town hall. a le droit de (newly productive) states an entitlement to act; à titre de names the role grounding it
  • Tout commerçant titulaire d’un bail en cours a droit à un préavis d’au moins trente jours avant tout changement d’emplacement. Every vendor holding a current lease is entitled to at least thirty days' notice before any change of location. a droit à (newly productive) states an entitlement to receive something

6.3 Citing the source and hedging with a condition: en vertu de / sous réserve de

Building Block — Stating a rule with its source and its limits
[obligation or right: est tenu de… / a le droit de… / a droit à…] + [source: en vertu de + nom (une clause, un article, un règlement)] + [condition, when one applies: sous réserve de + nom/infinitif]
En vertu de is newly productive. It names the specific clause, article, or regulation that grounds an obligation or right, rather than leaving the reader to trust an unsourced claim — a habit this chapter treats as essential to legal-administrative precision. Sous réserve de is newly productive. It states a condition or exception that limits an otherwise general rule, without which a reader might assume a rule applies unconditionally when it does not. Neither frame changes the obligation or right itself; en vertu de grounds it, and sous réserve de bounds it. Both may be added to the same sentence.
Chaque commerçant est tenu de libérer son emplacement avant sept heures les jours de nettoyage, en vertu de la clause 4 du bail type, sous réserve d’un accord écrit contraire avec le service du marché. Comme le stipule le bail, cette obligation ne s’applique qu’aux jours de nettoyage effectivement programmés.
Each vendor is required to vacate their stall before seven o'clock on cleaning days, under clause 4 of the standard lease, subject to a contrary written agreement with the market office. As the lease stipulates, this obligation applies only to cleaning days that are actually scheduled.

Model Dialogue: La réunion des commerçants du marché

Context: Sophie and Karim attend an informal meeting where market vendors discuss the new lease template. M. Ouedraogo and Mme Rigal have questions; Sophie mediates by stating obligations, rights, sources, and conditions precisely. Register: neutre/courant to soutenu, precise and unhurried despite the informal setting.

M. Ouedraogo : On me dit qu’il faut libérer l’emplacement avant sept heures les jours de nettoyage. C’est obligatoire, ou c’est juste conseillé ? I've been told I need to vacate the spot before seven on cleaning days. Is that mandatory, or just advised?
Sophie : C’est une obligation, pas un simple conseil : chaque commerçant est tenu de libérer son emplacement avant sept heures, en vertu de la clause 4 du bail type. It's an obligation, not just a piece of advice: each vendor is required to vacate their spot before seven o'clock, under clause 4 of the standard lease.
Mme Rigal : Et si je n’ai jamais posé problème en cinq ans, est-ce que je peux refuser un changement d’emplacement ? And if I've never caused a problem in five years, can I refuse a change of location?
Sophie : Oui — à titre de commerçante régulière depuis plus de deux ans, vous avez le droit de refuser un changement sans justification écrite de la mairie. Yes — as a regular vendor for more than two years, you have the right to refuse a change without written justification from the town hall.
Karim : Et vous avez aussi droit à un préavis d’au moins trente jours avant tout changement, si jamais la mairie insiste. And you're also entitled to at least thirty days' notice before any change, if the town hall does insist.
M. Ouedraogo : Est-ce que cette histoire de libérer l’emplacement s’applique toujours, même l’été, quand il y a moins de nettoyage ? Does this business about vacating the spot always apply, even in summer, when there's less cleaning?
Sophie : Non — l’obligation s’applique sous réserve de la programmation effective d’un nettoyage par le service du marché ; en dehors de ces jours-là, elle ne s’applique pas. No — the obligation applies subject to the cleaning days actually scheduled by the market office; outside those days, it doesn't apply.
Karim : Et si jamais un droit n’est pas respecté, chacun a un recours : on peut écrire au service du marché, puis, si besoin, à la mairie directement. And if a right isn't respected, everyone has recourse: you can write to the market office, and then, if needed, directly to the town hall.

This dialogue models all four newly productive frames — obligation, right, source, and condition — while keeping the explanation accessible to non-specialist vendors, recycling Chapter 2's audience-adaptation habits without naming them.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Precision as a form of respect in French administrative writing

French administrative and legal-adjacent writing tends to value explicit sourcing (naming the article, clause, or regulation a rule comes from) and explicit scope (naming the conditions under which a rule applies or does not). This is not merely stylistic caution: for a reader who must act on the text, vague paraphrase can obscure whether something is required, permitted, or merely customary. Learning to state obligations, rights, sources, and conditions with this level of precision is itself a form of respect for the reader's ability to act correctly on what they are told.

Regional Variation — Administrative structures vary across Francophone contexts

The specific municipal and lease structures in this chapter reflect common French administrative conventions; Belgium, Switzerland, Québec, and other Francophone jurisdictions use different administrative bodies, terms, and procedures, though the underlying skill of stating obligations, rights, sources, and conditions precisely transfers across these contexts. This chapter's examples should not be read as a guide to any specific jurisdiction's actual law.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Confusing a recommendation with an obligation

Error: Using être tenu de to describe something that is merely advisable, or using a vague recommendation to describe something that is actually a binding obligation.

Correction: Check the source before choosing a frame: if a named clause or regulation requires the action, use est tenu de; if it is only good practice, use an established recommendation frame such as il vaudrait mieux, not être tenu de.

Common Mistake — Stating a rule without its source

Error: Stating an obligation or right without citing where it comes from, leaving the reader unable to verify the claim.

Correction: Add en vertu de and the specific clause, article, or regulation: …en vertu de la clause 4 du bail type, not simply …selon le règlement left vague.

Common Mistake — Treating a condition as canceling the rule entirely

Error: Using sous réserve de in a way that suggests the entire rule no longer applies, when in fact only a specific, limited exception is meant.

Correction: Keep the condition scoped to what it actually limits: l’obligation s’applique sous réserve de la programmation effective d’un nettoyage par le service du marché means the obligation still applies generally, just not outside those specific days — not that the obligation has disappeared.

  2. Article 6 of the covered market regulation is cited as the source.
  3. The obligation is limited to days that are not announced night-market days.
  4. The recourse mentioned is a written appeal to the town hall within fifteen days.

Reading Practice

  1. Obligation: vacating the space before seven on cleaning days, sourced to clause 4 of the standard lease. Right: refusing a change of location without written justification after two years, sourced to article 12 of the municipal regulation.
  2. The thirty-day notice does not apply in a case of force majeure.
  3. Un préavis appears to mean advance notice, a notice period; de plein droit appears to mean by operation of law, without a separate legal formality being required first.

Writing Workshop — Model text

Modèle — Note explicative pour les commerçants

Chers commerçants, une précision sur l’affichage des prix. Chaque commerçant est tenu d’afficher ses prix de façon visible sur son étal, en vertu de l’article 3 du règlement du marché. Cette obligation s’applique sous réserve des produits vendus au poids, pour lesquels un barème affiché à la caisse suffit. À titre de commerçant titulaire d’un emplacement fixe, vous avez droit à un rappel écrit avant toute sanction en cas de premier manquement. Si vous estimez qu’un rappel a été envoyé à tort, vous avez un recours écrit auprès du service du marché dans un délai de dix jours.

Dear vendors, a clarification on price display. Each vendor is required to display prices visibly at their stall, under article 3 of the market regulation. This obligation applies subject to products sold by weight, for which a posted price chart at the register is sufficient. As a vendor holding a fixed stall, you are entitled to a written reminder before any sanction for a first infraction. If you believe a reminder was sent in error, you have written recourse to the market office within ten days.

This model uses all four newly productive frames (obligation, right, source, condition) plus un recours, keeps the source specific and plausible, and scopes the condition correctly rather than canceling the rule entirely.

Expliquer comme spécialiste

Expert Explanation · Speak and Write French: Specialized Mastery and Expert Communication

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Distinguish explaining a technical or specialized notion for a specialist reader, who shares background knowledge and terminology, from explaining the same notion for a general reader, who needs abstraction unpacked into everyday, concrete terms — while the underlying content stays identical.
  2. Use a newly productive precision-toward-clarity frame — pour simplifier, disons que — to offer a deliberately simplified version of a technical point, explicitly marked as a simplification rather than the full picture.
  3. Use a newly productive concreteness frame — ce que cela signifie concrètement, c’est que — to translate an abstract or technical claim into a concrete consequence a non-specialist can picture.
  4. Use a newly productive distillation frame — si l’on devait résumer l’essentiel — to compress a longer technical explanation into its single most important point.
  5. Use a newly productive precision-and-explanation vocabulary set: une notion, un concept, vulgariser, une analogie, concret/concrète, and complexe.
  6. Recycle, without any new tense, mood, subjonctif trigger, or clitic pattern, Chapter 1's register/tone distinction and the full Volume IV toolkit — including the Chapter 4 emphatic cleft, the Chapter 6/12/17 attribution and synthesis toolkit, the Chapter 8/16/19 concession-and-pivot pattern, and the Chapter 11–15 academic and professional toolkit — selecting from among these as needed rather than treating any of them as newly taught.
  7. Receive further controlled, receptive-only exposure to a small set of vulgarization-and-pedagogy vocabulary used in editorial and educational contexts.
  8. Build one short analogy that makes a technical idea more accessible to a general reader, without distorting or oversimplifying the idea to the point of inaccuracy.
  9. Complete a short internal explanatory-writing exercise: the same technical point explained once for a specialist reader and once for a general reader, using all three of this chapter's newly productive frames.
  10. Move to a short adapted explanatory article addressing two audiences, and listen to a short excerpt of a specialist reformulating a technical point for a non-specialist colleague, continuing Unité I toward the Chapter 5 integration.

Communication Scenario

At La Revue du quartier, Sophie and Karim's second editorial assignment arrives from M. Belhadj: a local urban-planning consultant, Mme Fontaine, has submitted a technical memo about the neighborhood market's proposed water-drainage upgrade — dense, precise, and written for other engineers. The magazine wants two things from the same memo: a short technical sidebar that keeps Mme Fontaine's precision intact for readers who already know the field, and a short main-text passage that explains the same underlying point to ordinary readers who have never heard the relevant terminology. M. Belhadj is clear that this is not the register-and-tone work of Chapter 1: the audience itself is different, and specialist vocabulary that a general reader cannot use must be unpacked into something concrete, without becoming vague, hand-wavy, or wrong. Chapter 2 gives Sophie and Karim — and you — the tools to keep one idea intact while reshaping how much background it assumes.

  1. State a technical point precisely for a reader who shares the specialist vocabulary.
  2. Restate the same point for a reader who does not share that vocabulary, without losing accuracy.
  3. Build a short, honest analogy that aids understanding without distorting the underlying idea.
  4. Distill a longer technical explanation down to its single most essential point.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Signposting a simplification aloud

No new sound-spelling or vowel feature is introduced in this chapter. The task is delivery: a speaker who is about to simplify or distill a technical point typically signals the shift with a brief pause and a slight drop in pitch right before the frame itself, so the listener hears the shift coming before the simplified content arrives. Pour simplifier, disons que is usually delivered a little more slowly than the surrounding speech, since it is doing the listener the courtesy of flagging a simplification. Ce que cela signifie concrètement, c’est que often carries a slightly rising, clarifying intonation across the whole frame, as though inviting the listener to follow a translation in progress. Si l’on devait résumer l’essentiel is typically the most deliberately paced of the three, since it usually closes an explanation rather than opening one. These are performance tendencies, not fixed rules; individual speakers vary.

  • Simplifying: Pour simplifier, disons que le nouveau système retient temporairement une partie de l’eau de pluie et la rejette plus lentement vers le réseau municipal. — a brief pause before the frame, then slightly slower, evenly paced delivery.
  • Making it concrete: Ce que cela signifie concrètement, c’est que le risque d’inondation du marché diminue lors de fortes pluies. — a gently rising, clarifying pitch across the frame.
  • Distilling: Si l’on devait résumer l’essentiel, ce système réduit le risque d’inondation en ralentissant le rejet de l’eau. — deliberate, closing pace, often the final sentence of an explanation.

Key distinction — audience adaptation is not the same task as register or tone

Chapter 1 distinguished register (soutenu, neutre/courant, familier) from tone (sobre, cru, and so on). This chapter adds a third, independent dimension: audience background knowledge. A specialist explanation and a general explanation of the same technical point can both be written in neutre/courant register and in a measured tone; what changes is how much specialist vocabulary and background the writer assumes the reader already has, and how much abstraction must be unpacked into something concrete. A text can shift audience without shifting register, just as a text can shift register without shifting audience.

2.1 Marking a deliberate simplification: pour simplifier, disons que

Grammar Focus — Simplifying honestly, and saying so

Pour simplifier, disons que is newly productive. It introduces a version of a technical point that leaves out real complexity on purpose, and says so explicitly, so the reader knows a fuller account exists. This differs from Chapter 1's pour le dire plus sobrement, which trims exaggerated tone without touching technical content: pour simplifier is allowed to omit genuine technical detail, as long as what remains is still accurate as far as it goes. A simplification that becomes actively false, rather than merely incomplete, has crossed into the receptive réducteur territory this chapter warns against.

  • Le système repose sur un ensemble de bassins de rétention interconnectés, dimensionnés selon des modèles hydrologiques précis. Pour simplifier, disons que le nouveau système retient temporairement une partie de l’eau de pluie et la rejette plus lentement vers le réseau municipal. The system relies on a set of interconnected retention basins, sized according to precise hydrological models. To simplify, let's say the new system temporarily retains part of the rainwater and releases it more slowly into the municipal network. pour simplifier, disons que (newly productive) flags an incomplete but accurate simplification

2.2 Translating abstraction into consequence: ce que cela signifie concrètement, c'est que

Grammar Focus — From mechanism to lived consequence

Ce que cela signifie concrètement, c’est que is newly productive. Where pour simplifier trims technical detail, this frame does something distinct: it keeps the technical claim intact just before it, and adds a second sentence translating that claim into a concrete, experiential consequence the reader can picture — what changes for them, not just what changes in the system. The two frames often work together: a specialist sentence, a simplification, and then a concrete consequence, in that order, though any one may be used alone depending on the reader's need.

  • Le système retient temporairement une partie de l’eau de pluie et la rejette plus lentement vers le réseau municipal. Ce que cela signifie concrètement, c’est que le risque d’inondation du marché diminue lors de fortes pluies. The system temporarily retains part of the rainwater and releases it more slowly into the municipal network. What this means concretely is that the market's flood risk decreases during heavy rain. ce que cela signifie concrètement, c'est que (newly productive) converts mechanism into lived consequence

2.3 Compressing to the essential point: si l'on devait résumer l'essentiel

Building Block — A specialist-to-general explanation shape (this chapter's practice template)
[technical sentence for a specialist audience] + [pour simplifier, disons que: a flagged, accurate simplification] + [ce que cela signifie concrètement, c’est que: the concrete consequence for the reader] + [optional: a short, honest analogy] + [si l’on devait résumer l’essentiel: the single most important point, closing the explanation]
Si l’on devait résumer l’essentiel is newly productive. It closes an explanation by naming the one point a reader must retain if nothing else is retained. It differs from the Chapter 4 emphatic cleft (ce qui frappe, c’est que, recycled from Volume IV) in function: the cleft highlights a striking or persuasive point within an argument, while this frame performs a specifically pedagogical compression — it exists to help a reader retain the core of a technical explanation, not to make a rhetorical point. This chapter's template uses it once, at the end, after the concrete consequence has already been given; it should not introduce new information the reader has not already seen.
Le système de drainage du marché reposera sur un ensemble de bassins de rétention interconnectés, dimensionnés selon des modèles hydrologiques précis, afin de limiter le débit d’eau rejeté vers le réseau municipal. Pour simplifier, disons que le nouveau système retient temporairement une partie de l’eau de pluie et la rejette plus lentement vers le réseau municipal. Ce que cela signifie concrètement, c’est que le risque d’inondation du marché diminue lors de fortes pluies. On peut comparer ce système à une grande éponge installée sous le marché : elle retient l’eau, puis la relâche lentement, plutôt que de tout renvoyer d’un coup vers les canalisations. Si l’on devait résumer l’essentiel, ce nouveau système réduit le risque d’inondation en ralentissant le rejet de l’eau vers le réseau municipal.
The market's drainage system will rely on a set of interconnected retention basins, sized according to precise hydrological models, in order to limit the flow of water released into the municipal network. To simplify, let's say the system temporarily retains part of the rainwater and releases it more slowly into the municipal network. What this means concretely is that the market's flood risk decreases during heavy rain. This system can be compared to a large sponge installed beneath the market: it holds water, then releases it slowly, rather than sending it all at once back into the pipes. If one had to sum up the essential point, this new system reduces flood risk by slowing the release of water into the municipal network.

Model Dialogue: Deux lecteurs, une même notion

Context: M. Belhadj reviews Sophie and Karim's draft treatment of Mme Fontaine's technical memo, checking that the specialist sidebar and the general-audience passage say exactly the same thing at different levels of background knowledge. Register: professional editorial discussion.

M. Belhadj : La notion technique de Mme Fontaine est correcte pour l’encadré spécialisé. Mais nos lecteurs ordinaires n’y comprendront rien. Mme Fontaine's technical notion is correct for the specialist sidebar. But our ordinary readers won't understand any of it.
Karim : On garde l’encadré tel quel pour les spécialistes, et on ajoute une version simplifiée dans le texte principal ? We keep the sidebar as is for specialists, and add a simplified version in the main text?
M. Belhadj : Exactement. Sophie, comment simplifieriez-vous le passage sur les bassins de rétention ? Exactly. Sophie, how would you simplify the passage on the retention basins?
Sophie : Pour simplifier, disons que le nouveau système retient temporairement une partie de l’eau de pluie et la rejette plus lentement vers le réseau municipal. To simplify, let's say the new system temporarily retains part of the rainwater and releases it more slowly into the municipal network.
M. Belhadj : Bien, mais nos lecteurs voudront savoir ce que ça change pour eux. Good, but our readers will want to know what this changes for them.
Karim : Ce que cela signifie concrètement, c’est que le risque d’inondation du marché diminue lors de fortes pluies. What this means concretely is that the market's flood risk decreases during heavy rain.
M. Belhadj : Voilà qui parle aux commerçants. Une analogie, peut-être, pour rendre ça encore plus vivant ? Now that speaks to the shopkeepers. An analogy, perhaps, to make it even more vivid?
Sophie : On peut comparer ce système à une grande éponge installée sous le marché. We can compare this system to a large sponge installed beneath the market.
M. Belhadj : Fidèle et simple à la fois. Et pour conclure le passage ? Faithful and simple at once. And to close the passage?
Karim : Si l’on devait résumer l’essentiel, ce nouveau système réduit le risque d’inondation en ralentissant le rejet de l’eau vers le réseau municipal. If one had to sum up the essential point, this new system reduces flood risk by slowing the release of water into the municipal network.
M. Belhadj : Précis, concret, et fidèle à la note technique. C’est exactement ce qu’il nous fallait. Precise, concrete, and faithful to the technical note. That's exactly what we needed.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — La vulgarisation as a respected professional skill

In French media, publishing, and science communication, the ability to make specialized knowledge accessible without distorting it — la vulgarisation — is generally treated as a distinct and respected professional skill, not as a lesser or simplified form of expertise. A specialist who can also explain their work clearly to a general audience is often seen as unusually skilled, not as "dumbing down" their field. This is a professional tendency this chapter draws on, not a claim that every specialist is expected to communicate this way in every context.

Regional Variation — Expectations for technical explanation vary by context

How much background knowledge a writer assumes, and how directly technical vocabulary is unpacked for a general audience, varies across French, Belgian, Swiss, Québécois, and other Francophone media and educational traditions, and also varies by publication and discipline within a single country. This chapter's examples reflect common metropolitan French journalistic conventions. The underlying skill — keeping content accurate while adjusting how much background it assumes — transfers across these contexts even where specific conventions differ.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Simplifying until the claim becomes false

Error: Using pour simplifier, disons que to introduce a version of the claim that is no longer accurate — for example, claiming the system "eliminates all flooding forever" when the technical memo only supports a much more limited claim.

Correction: A simplification may omit detail, but it may not introduce a claim the specialist source does not support: pour simplifier, disons que le nouveau système retient temporairement une partie de l’eau de pluie et la rejette plus lentement vers le réseau municipal remains true even though it leaves out the hydrological modeling detail. If a simplification would require overstating the claim, narrow the simplification instead.

Common Mistake — An analogy that distorts more than it clarifies

Error: Choosing an analogy that misleads the reader about how the system actually works — for example, comparing the retention basins to a system that stores water indefinitely rather than releasing it gradually.

Correction: An analogy should be tested against the technical claim it illustrates: une grande éponge…qui retient l’eau, puis la relâche lentement matches the retain-then-release mechanism accurately. If an analogy requires the reader to unlearn it later, choose a different one.

Common Mistake — Letting the distillation introduce new information

Error: Using si l’on devait résumer l’essentiel to add a fact, a number, or a claim that did not appear earlier in the explanation.

Correction: The distillation should compress what has already been said, not extend it: si l’on devait résumer l’essentiel, ce nouveau système réduit le risque d’inondation en ralentissant le rejet de l’eau vers le réseau municipal restates the retention-and-slower-release point already made earlier in the passage, rather than introducing a new one.

Writing Workshop — Model text

Modèle — Un point technique, deux publics

Passage spécialisé : Le nouveau composteur collectif du marché repose sur un système de ventilation forcée qui régule l’apport en oxygène au cœur du tas de déchets organiques. Cette aération continue accélère la décomposition aérobie et réduit le temps de maturation du compost de plusieurs semaines.

Passage général : Cette notion technique peut sembler complexe, mais elle décrit une amélioration simple. Pour simplifier, disons que le nouveau composteur fait entrer plus d’air dans les déchets pour qu’ils se transforment plus vite en compost. Ce que cela signifie concrètement, c’est que les commerçants du marché récupéreront du compost utilisable plusieurs semaines plus tôt qu’avec l’ancien système. On peut comparer ce composteur à un tas de compost qu’on retournerait sans arrêt : plus les déchets sont en contact avec l’air, plus ils se décomposent vite. Si l’on devait résumer l’essentiel, ce nouveau composteur permet de récupérer du compost utile plus rapidement qu’avant.

Specialist passage: The market's new collective composter relies on a forced-ventilation system that regulates the oxygen supply at the core of the organic waste pile. This continuous aeration accelerates aerobic decomposition and reduces the compost's maturation time by several weeks.

General passage: This technical notion may sound complex, but it describes a simple improvement. To simplify, let's say the new composter lets more air into the waste so that it turns into compost faster. What this means concretely is that the market's shopkeepers will get usable compost several weeks earlier than with the old system. This composter can be compared to a compost pile that is constantly turned: the more the waste is exposed to air, the faster it breaks down. If one had to sum up the essential point, this new composter lets the market recover useful compost faster than before.

This model keeps the underlying technical fact — forced aeration speeding aerobic decomposition — identical across both passages, gives the specialist passage two sentences as required, uses all three newly productive frames in the general passage, includes one mechanistically faithful analogy (the turned-compost-pile comparison, which represents the same oxygen-exposure mechanism as the specialist passage rather than a different one like heat circulation), and closes with a distillation that restates rather than extends the explanation. It uses two items of this chapter's productive vocabulary (une notion, complexe). No unscheduled productive system beyond Chapter 2's new frames and vocabulary and previously activated material from Volumes I–IV and Chapter 1 appears. Other responses are acceptable when they meet all task requirements, including different but equally accurate topics and analogies.

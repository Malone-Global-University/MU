Synthèse de recherche avancée

Advanced Research Synthesis · Speak and Write French: Specialized Mastery and Expert Communication

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Distinguish research synthesis — where several sources must be read against one another, not simply summarized one after another — from Chapter 16's single-document revision work, while recognizing that both rely on the same habit of precise, checkable description.
  2. Use a newly productive convergence frame — plusieurs sources convergent pour montrer que + clause — to state precisely what multiple sources independently support, without overstating the number or strength of the sources involved.
  3. Use a newly productive divergence frame — les sources divergent / s’opposent sur la question de + groupe nominal — to name a genuine point of disagreement among sources precisely, rather than smoothing it over.
  4. Use a newly productive scholarly-positioning frame — cette synthèse s’inscrit dans le prolongement de X, tout en + participe présent — to situate a new piece of writing relative to existing work while naming what it specifically adds.
  5. Use a newly productive gap-naming frame — il subsiste une lacune quant à + groupe nominal — to state honestly what the available sources do not yet establish.
  6. Use a newly productive research-synthesis vocabulary set: la synthèse, converger, diverger, une lacune, s’inscrire dans, un éclairage, corroborer, une source primaire / secondaire.
  7. Recycle, without any new tense, mood, subjonctif trigger, or clitic pattern, the complete Unité I, Unité II, Unité III, and Chapter 16 toolkits, selecting from among these rather than treating any of them as newly taught.
  8. Receive further controlled, receptive-only exposure to a small set of more technical research vocabulary encountered in a source-comparison note.
  9. Read a synthesis note comparing five sources on a shared local topic and identify convergence, divergence, positioning, and a stated gap.
  10. Complete a short synthesis exercise (comparing at least three invented or provided sources and writing a synthesis note) using all four of this chapter's newly productive frames at least once each.

Communication Scenario

The literary supplement's third issue will center on a longer piece: the slow disappearance of artisanal trades from the neighborhood. Sophie now has more material than she can simply narrate. Her own portrait of the closed woodworking workshop (Chapter 16) first showed her how much more there was to the story, and that curiosity is what led her and Karim to assemble a proper synthesis corpus for this new piece: Karim's earlier article on the market's contested founding history (Chapter 14), a short academic article on neighborhood deindustrialization that a university contact sent over, and three oral-history interviews the two of them recorded with older residents — five sources in all. The sources agree on some things and quietly contradict each other on others: the academic article dates the neighborhood's decline earlier than the oral histories suggest, and one interview flatly contradicts another about how many workshops closed in a single decade. M. Fontaine will not let the piece go to print as a simple retelling of whichever source sounds most vivid. He wants Sophie and Karim to read the sources against one another: to say plainly where they agree, where they genuinely disagree, how this new piece relates to work already published on the neighborhood, and what still is not known. Chapter 17 gives Sophie, Karim — and you — the tools of research synthesis: naming a genuine convergence among sources, naming a genuine divergence, positioning new work against existing work, and naming an honest gap.

  1. State precisely what several independent sources agree on.
  2. Name a genuine point of disagreement among sources.
  3. Position a new piece relative to existing work, naming what it specifically adds.
  4. State honestly what the available sources do not yet establish.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Delivering agreement and disagreement fairly

No new sound-spelling or vowel feature is introduced in this chapter. The task is delivery: a convergence claim and a divergence claim should sound like two different kinds of statement, not like the same confident assertion repeated. A level, unhurried tone on convergent pour montrer que signals genuine agreement across sources; a slightly more measured, evenly weighted tone on divergent sur la question de signals that neither side is being favored. A positioning claim with s’inscrit dans le prolongement de should sound respectful of the existing work it builds on, and a gap named with il subsiste une lacune should sound like an honest limitation, not an apology.

  • Naming convergence: Plusieurs sources convergent pour montrer que le quartier a perdu la moitié de ses ateliers en une décennie. — even, confident tone, without exaggeration.
  • Naming divergence: Les sources divergent sur la question du nombre exact d’ateliers fermés. — balanced tone, giving no side rhetorical advantage.
  • Positioning the work: Cette synthèse s’inscrit dans le prolongement de l’article universitaire, tout en apportant un éclairage nouveau fondé sur des témoignages directs. — respectful of the prior work, confident about the new contribution.
  • Naming a gap: Il subsiste une lacune quant aux causes précises de cette fermeture accélérée. — measured, matter-of-fact, not defensive.

Cumulative review: this chapter’s spoken practice also recycles active sound-spelling, liaison, enchaînement, elision, and rhythm systems from earlier volumes. Practice les sources convergent for the silent third-person-plural verb ending -ent and the oral vowel in sources; il subsiste une lacune for enchaînement of the normally pronounced final consonant of subsiste into vowel-initial une, together with the oral front-rounded vowel in une; and s’inscrire dans le prolongement de for phrase rhythm across a longer prepositional group.

Grammar: Synthétiser plusieurs sources

Purpose: introduce a newly productive convergence frame (plusieurs sources convergent pour montrer que), a newly productive divergence frame (les sources divergent / s’opposent sur la question de), a newly productive scholarly-positioning frame (cette synthèse s’inscrit dans le prolongement de X, tout en + participe présent), and a newly productive gap-naming frame (il subsiste une lacune quant à), while recycling the complete Unité I, Unité II, Unité III, and Chapter 16 toolkits. No new tense, mood, subjonctif trigger, or clitic pattern is introduced in this chapter.

Key distinction — synthesis reads sources against each other, not one after another

Chapter 16 asked how a single draft could be improved for publication. This chapter asks a different question: given several independent sources on the same topic, what do they actually establish together, where do they actually disagree, how does new writing relate to what has already been published, and what remains unknown? A synthesis that simply summarizes each source in turn, one paragraph per source, is not yet a synthesis in this sense — it becomes one only once the sources are read against one another.

17.1 Naming genuine agreement: plusieurs sources convergent pour montrer que

Grammar Focus — Agreement that is checkable, not merely asserted

Plusieurs sources convergent pour montrer que + clause is newly productive. It states precisely what more than one independent source supports, inviting the reader to check the claim against the named sources. This differs from a vague appeal to authority (tout le monde sait que), which does not name what actually supports the claim.

  • Plusieurs sources convergent pour montrer que le nombre d’ateliers artisanaux du quartier a fortement diminué entre les années 1980 et 2000. Several sources converge in showing that the number of artisanal workshops in the neighborhood declined sharply between 1980 and 2000. plusieurs sources convergent pour montrer que (newly productive) names a claim that is supported by more than one independent source

Use plusieurs sources only when at least two genuinely independent sources are meant; naming the sources explicitly nearby (l’article universitaire et les témoignages de Mme Rocher et de M. Belkacem) keeps the claim checkable.

17.2 Naming genuine disagreement: les sources divergent / s’opposent sur la question de

Grammar Focus — Disagreement stated precisely, not smoothed over

Les sources divergent sur la question de + groupe nominal (or, more strongly, s’opposent sur la question de) is newly productive. It names a specific, nameable point of disagreement among sources, rather than a general sense that "the sources are not entirely consistent." This differs from silently choosing the more vivid or convenient source and ignoring the conflict.

  • Les sources divergent sur la question du nombre exact d’ateliers fermés dans les années 1990 : l’article universitaire en compte une douzaine, tandis que M. Belkacem se souvient d’une trentaine. The sources disagree on the question of the exact number of workshops closed in the 1990s: the academic article counts about a dozen, while M. Belkacem remembers about thirty. les sources divergent sur la question de (newly productive) names the specific point of disagreement and both positions

A named divergence should, where possible, name both positions and, when relevant, a plausible reason for the gap (differing definitions of « atelier », differing time periods, the imprecision of memory versus the imprecision of incomplete records) — without pretending to resolve which source is simply correct.

17.3 Positioning new work and naming a gap

Building Block — Convergence, divergence, positioning, and gap together
[convergence: plusieurs sources convergent pour montrer que…] + [divergence: les sources divergent sur la question de…] + [positioning: cette synthèse s’inscrit dans le prolongement de…, tout en…] + [gap: il subsiste une lacune quant à…]
Cette synthèse s’inscrit dans le prolongement de X, tout en + participe présent is newly productive. It situates a new piece of writing relative to existing published or documented work, naming both the continuity (what the new piece builds on) and the specific contribution (what it adds), using the already-active participe présent rather than any new verb form. Il subsiste une lacune quant à + groupe nominal is newly productive. It states plainly what the assembled sources, taken together, do not yet establish, treating an honest gap as part of responsible synthesis rather than a weakness to hide.
Plusieurs sources convergent pour montrer que le quartier a perdu la majorité de ses ateliers artisanaux entre 1980 et 2000. Les sources divergent en revanche sur la question du rythme exact de cette disparition : l’article universitaire situe l’essentiel du déclin avant 1990, tandis que les témoignages recueillis par Sophie et Karim placent plutôt le tournant décisif au milieu des années 1990. Cette synthèse s’inscrit dans le prolongement de l’article universitaire et de l’article de Karim sur l’histoire du marché, tout en apportant un éclairage nouveau fondé sur des témoignages directs que ces travaux antérieurs n’avaient pas recueillis. Il subsiste néanmoins une lacune quant aux causes précises de cette accélération au milieu des années 1990, qu’aucune des cinq sources ne documente de façon satisfaisante.
Several sources agree in showing that the neighborhood lost the majority of its artisanal workshops between 1980 and 2000. The sources disagree, however, on the exact pace of this disappearance: the academic article places most of the decline before 1990, while the testimonies gathered by Sophie and Karim instead place the decisive turning point in the mid-1990s. This synthesis builds on the academic article and on Karim's piece on the market's history, while adding a new perspective grounded in direct testimonies that these earlier works had not gathered. A gap nonetheless remains regarding the precise causes of this mid-1990s acceleration, which none of the five sources documents satisfactorily.

Practice — Convergence, divergence, positioning, or gap?

Decide which newly productive frame best fits each statement.

  1. Les trois témoins racontent, indépendamment les uns des autres, la même scène de fermeture. (convergence)
  2. L’article universitaire et le témoignage de Mme Rocher ne s’accordent pas sur la date exacte de la fermeture du dernier atelier. (divergence)
  3. Ce nouvel article reprend le travail de Karim sur le marché tout en y ajoutant des données démographiques inédites. (positioning)
  4. Aucune des sources ne permet de savoir précisément combien d’ouvriers ont perdu leur emploi. (gap)

Model Dialogue: La note de synthèse avant le troisième numéro

Context: Sophie and Karim meet with M. Fontaine to plan the long-form piece on the neighborhood's disappearing artisanal trades, comparing five sources. Register: professionnel neutre, careful and precise.

M. Fontaine : Alors, vous avez cinq sources : l’article universitaire, l’article de Karim sur le marché, et vos trois entretiens. Qu’est-ce qu’elles disent, ensemble ? So, you have five sources: the academic article, Karim's piece on the market, and your three interviews. What do they say, together?
Sophie : Plusieurs sources convergent pour montrer que le quartier a perdu la majorité de ses ateliers entre 1980 et 2000. L’article, mon grand-oncle, et Mme Rocher racontent tous la même tendance, indépendamment les uns des autres. Several sources agree in showing that the neighborhood lost most of its workshops between 1980 and 2000. The article, my great-uncle, and Mme Rocher all describe the same trend, independently of one another.
Karim : Mais les sources divergent sur la question du rythme exact. L’article situe l’essentiel du déclin avant 1990 ; nos témoignages placent plutôt le tournant au milieu des années 1990. But the sources disagree on the question of the exact pace. The article places most of the decline before 1990; our testimonies instead place the turning point in the mid-1990s.
M. Fontaine : Il ne faut pas trancher artificiellement. Dites les deux positions, et pourquoi elles diffèrent peut-être. Don't artificially settle it. State both positions, and why they might differ.
Sophie : L’article compte sans doute les fermetures officiellement déclarées, alors que nos témoins se souviennent aussi d’ateliers informels, jamais enregistrés. The article probably counts officially declared closures, while our witnesses also remember informal workshops that were never registered.
Karim : Et par rapport à mon article sur le marché ? Comment cette synthèse s’y rattache ? And in relation to my piece on the market? How does this synthesis connect to it?
Sophie : Cette synthèse s’inscrit dans le prolongement de ton article, tout en apportant un éclairage nouveau fondé sur des témoignages directs que tu n’avais pas recueillis à l’époque. This synthesis builds on your article, while adding a new perspective grounded in direct testimonies you hadn't gathered at the time.
M. Fontaine : Bien. Et il reste sûrement des questions sans réponse ? Good. And there are surely still unanswered questions?
Karim : Oui : il subsiste une lacune quant aux causes précises de l’accélération du milieu des années 1990. Aucune de nos cinq sources ne l’explique de façon satisfaisante. Yes: a gap remains regarding the precise causes of the mid-1990s acceleration. None of our five sources explains it satisfactorily.
M. Fontaine : Parfait : dites-le honnêtement dans le texte. Une synthèse qui admet ses limites est plus crédible qu’une synthèse qui prétend tout savoir. Perfect: say so honestly in the piece. A synthesis that admits its limits is more credible than one that claims to know everything.

This dialogue models all four newly productive frames — convergence, divergence, positioning, and gap — while recycling Chapter 14's source-grounding habits and Chapter 12's habit of scoping a claim precisely.

Reading Practice

Note de synthèse — Les ateliers disparus du quartier

Corpus consulté : un article universitaire sur la désindustrialisation du quartier, l’article de Karim sur l’histoire contestée du marché, et trois entretiens oraux menés par Sophie et Karim auprès d’anciens artisans et commerçants du quartier.

Convergence : Plusieurs sources convergent pour montrer que le quartier a perdu la majorité de ses ateliers artisanaux entre 1980 et 2000 : l’article universitaire, le témoignage de M. Belkacem et celui de Mme Rocher décrivent tous, indépendamment les uns des autres, une disparition progressive mais massive de ce type de commerce.

Divergence : Les sources divergent en revanche sur la question du rythme exact de cette disparition. L’article universitaire situe l’essentiel du déclin avant 1990, en s’appuyant sur les fermetures officiellement déclarées. Les témoignages recueillis par Sophie et Karim placent plutôt le tournant décisif au milieu des années 1990, une différence qui s’explique sans doute par le fait que plusieurs ateliers informels, jamais déclarés, échappent aux statistiques officielles mais restent présents dans la mémoire des habitants.

Positionnement : Cette synthèse s’inscrit dans le prolongement de l’article universitaire et de l’article de Karim sur l’histoire du marché, tout en apportant un éclairage nouveau fondé sur des témoignages directs que ces travaux antérieurs n’avaient pas recueillis.

Lacune : Il subsiste néanmoins une lacune quant aux causes précises de l’accélération observée au milieu des années 1990 : aucune des cinq sources consultées n’explique de façon satisfaisante pourquoi le rythme des fermetures s’est accéléré précisément à cette période, plutôt qu’avant ou après.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — Synthesis as intellectual honesty, not neutrality-as-avoidance

The habit modeled in this chapter — naming agreement precisely, naming disagreement precisely, situating new work relative to existing work, and admitting a real gap — reflects a broader norm in French-language academic, journalistic, and editorial writing: a synthesis earns its authority by showing its sources and its limits, not by sounding maximally confident. This is distinct from false balance, which manufactures a disagreement where none genuinely exists; naming a real divergence and naming a real convergence both require the same honesty.

Regional Variation — Source traditions vary across the Francophone world

What counts as a reliable primary source, how oral testimony is weighted against official records, and how directly a writer is expected to state a disagreement among sources can vary by discipline, institution, publication, and research tradition, both within France and across other Francophone academic and journalistic contexts. Learning to name a corpus explicitly and to state convergence and divergence precisely prepares learners to adapt to different research and editorial contexts across the Francophone world.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Summarizing sources one after another instead of synthesizing

Error: Writing one paragraph per source in sequence (l’article dit… Ensuite, M. Belkacem dit… Ensuite, Mme Rocher dit…) without ever comparing them.

Correction: Use plusieurs sources convergent pour montrer que… and les sources divergent sur la question de… to read the sources against one another explicitly, rather than reporting them in isolation.

Common Mistake — Silently choosing the more convenient source

Error: Presenting only the source that best fits the writer's preferred narrative, without mentioning a source that says otherwise.

Correction: Name the divergence directly and fairly: les sources divergent sur la question du nombre exact d’ateliers fermés, stating both positions, as in Chapter 14's habit of presenting contested accounts fairly.

Common Mistake — Treating an honest gap as a failure to hide

Error: Omitting any mention of what the sources do not establish, out of a sense that admitting a gap weakens the piece.

Correction: State the gap plainly with il subsiste une lacune quant à…. A synthesis that names its own limits is more credible, not less, as M. Fontaine notes in the Model Dialogue.

Answer Key

Grammar Practice — Convergence, divergence, positioning, or gap?

  1. Convergence. Plusieurs témoins convergent pour montrer que la scène s’est déroulée de la même façon, indépendamment les uns des autres.
  2. Divergence. Le rapport officiel et le témoignage local divergent sur la question de la date exacte.
  3. Positioning. Ce nouvel article s’inscrit dans le prolongement de l’étude plus ancienne, tout en y ajoutant des données récentes.
  4. Gap. Il subsiste une lacune quant à ce fait précis, qu’aucune source disponible ne permet d’établir avec certitude.

Writing Workshop — Model text

Modèle — Note de synthèse

Corpus : un article de presse régionale sur la fermeture progressive des cinémas de quartier, un rapport municipal sur la fréquentation des salles de cinéma, et deux entretiens menés auprès d’anciens exploitants de salles — quatre sources au total.

Plusieurs sources convergent pour montrer que la ville a perdu la majorité de ses cinémas de quartier entre 1990 et 2010. L’article de presse recense la fermeture d’une douzaine de salles pendant cette période. Le rapport municipal, fondé sur des chiffres de fréquentation annuelle, confirme une baisse continue et marquée sur les mêmes deux décennies. Les deux exploitants interrogés, qui n’ont jamais travaillé ensemble ni dans la même salle, décrivent tous deux, indépendamment l’un de l’autre, une clientèle en chute constante à partir du milieu des années 1990. Ces quatre sources s’accordent donc sur l’ampleur générale du phénomène. Les sources divergent en revanche sur la question de la cause principale de cette baisse. Le rapport municipal l’attribue surtout à la concurrence des grands multiplexes construits en périphérie à partir de 1995. Les deux exploitants interrogés insistent plutôt sur le coût croissant de la rénovation des salles anciennes, qui aurait précipité plusieurs fermetures avant même l’arrivée des multiplexes. Cette différence s’explique sans doute par le fait que le rapport s’appuie uniquement sur des données de fréquentation, alors que les exploitants raisonnent à partir de leurs propres finances et de leurs souvenirs professionnels. Cette synthèse s’inscrit dans le prolongement du rapport municipal, qui demeure la source la plus systématique sur la période, tout en apportant un éclairage nouveau fondé sur l’expérience directe de deux professionnels que le rapport n’avait pas interrogés. Il subsiste néanmoins une lacune quant au rôle exact qu’a joué l’arrivée du streaming à domicile dans cette même période : aucune des quatre sources consultées, toutes antérieures à la généralisation du streaming, ne documente ce facteur de façon satisfaisante. Une recherche future devrait donc chercher des sources plus récentes portant spécifiquement sur cette question.

Corpus: a regional press article on the gradual closure of neighborhood cinemas, a municipal report on cinema attendance, and two interviews with former cinema operators — four sources in total.

Several sources converge in showing that the city lost most of its neighborhood cinemas between 1990 and 2010. The press article records the closure of about a dozen venues during this period. The municipal report, based on annual attendance figures, confirms a continuous and marked decline over the same two decades. The two operators interviewed, who never worked together or in the same venue, both independently describe a steadily shrinking audience from the mid-1990s onward. These four sources therefore agree on the general scale of the phenomenon. The sources disagree, however, on the question of the main cause of this decline. The municipal report attributes it mainly to competition from large multiplexes built on the outskirts starting in 1995. The two operators interviewed instead emphasize the rising cost of renovating older venues, which they say precipitated several closures even before the multiplexes arrived. This difference is probably explained by the fact that the report relies solely on attendance data, while the operators reason from their own finances and professional memories. This synthesis builds on the municipal report, which remains the most systematic source for the period, while adding a new perspective grounded in the direct experience of two professionals the report had not interviewed. A gap nonetheless remains regarding the exact role played by the rise of home streaming during this same period: none of the four sources consulted, all of which predate the spread of streaming, documents this factor satisfactorily. Future research should therefore seek more recent sources addressing this question specifically.

This model names its corpus explicitly, states a checkable convergence and a fairly presented divergence, positions itself against a real named prior source, and names a genuine gap distinct from the stated divergence.

Unit II Integration · Speak and Write French: Specialized Mastery and Expert Communication

Chapter Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  1. Combine, in a single extended task, the four specialized-domain dimensions built across Unité II: Chapter 6's obligation/rights/source/condition frames, Chapter 7's aim/framework/responsibility/justification frames, Chapter 8's risk/capability/open-question/precaution frames, and Chapter 9's concession/commitment/openness/shared-purpose frames.
  2. Select the right specialized-domain tool for a given moment from the complete Unité II toolkit, rather than treating any single chapter's frames as the default for every situation.
  3. State an obligation with être tenu de and a right with avoir le droit de / avoir droit à, each grounded in a named source with en vertu de and correctly scoped with sous réserve de, as in Chapter 6.
  4. State a policy's aim with avoir pour objectif de, situate it institutionally with dans le cadre de, and assign matching responsibility and jurisdiction with il incombe à and relever de, justified with dans l’intérêt de, as in Chapter 7.
  5. State a genuine technical capability with être en mesure de, a specific bounded risk with présenter un risque de, a genuinely open question with il convient de se demander si, and a concrete precaution with à condition de, as in Chapter 8.
  6. Distinguish a genuine concession (tout en reconnaissant que) from an actual commitment (s’engager à) and from mere openness (être disposé à), framed within a stated shared purpose (dans un esprit de), as in Chapter 9.
  7. Produce no new tense, mood, subjonctif trigger, or clitic pattern in this chapter; rely entirely on Volumes I–IV, the complete Unité I toolkit, and Chapters 6–9 of this volume.
  8. Complete an integrated oral and written performance task that requires moving among all four Unité II dimensions within a single realistic public-meeting scenario, without a chapter-by-chapter script to follow.
  9. Self-assess Unité II readiness using the consolidated review grid at the end of this chapter, identifying any of the four dimensions that need further practice before Unité III.

Communication Scenario

The market renovation dossier that has run through Chapters 6–9 — the lease clause on cleaning-day obligations, the roof-versus-drainage split between the region and the town hall, the new drainage sensors and their data-collection question, and the regional produce-supply agreement — now converges on a single evening: the town hall has scheduled one public meeting to close out the file before the municipal council's final vote. M. Ouedraogo, Mme Rigal, and other vendors will be there with questions that do not sort themselves neatly by chapter: a vendor might ask in the same breath whether closing the stall early is required or merely advised, which institution actually pays for the roof, whether the sensors are being oversold, and what has actually been promised about delivery costs, and by whom. Sophie and Karim, covering the meeting for La Revue du quartier, cannot answer with four separate speeches organized by topic; they have to reach for whichever specialized-domain tool a given question actually calls for, sometimes combining two or three in a single answer. Chapter 10 does not teach new tools; it asks Sophie, Karim — and you — to use the specialized-domain toolkit built across Chapters 6–9, together, under the same time pressure as a real public meeting.

  1. State an obligation and a right precisely, each grounded in a named source and correctly scoped by its condition.
  2. State a policy's aim, its institutional framework, and matching responsibility and justification.
  3. State a genuine technical capability and risk, raise a genuinely open question, and state a concrete precaution.
  4. Distinguish a genuine concession from an actual commitment and from mere openness, framed within a shared purpose.

Pronunciation Focus

Pronunciation Focus — Cumulative review, no new feature

No new sound-spelling, vowel, liaison, or delivery feature is introduced in Chapter 10. This chapter's integrated task instead requires learners to move fluidly among the four delivery styles practiced separately in Chapters 6–9: the measured pacing that separates an obligation, its source, and its condition from one another (Chapter 6); the slight emphasis on the institution named after il incombe à, kept in a single rhythmic group (Chapter 7); the level pause before a genuine open question, contrasted with the steadier declarative pace of a stated capability or risk (Chapter 8); and the even, unhurried delivery through a concession clause, followed by a brief pause before the contrasting main clause, with a visibly more tentative pace for mere openness than for an actual commitment (Chapter 9). Learners should treat all of these as previously practiced tendencies to draw on as needed, not as a checklist to perform mechanically in a fixed order.

Grammar: Les quatre domaines spécialisés de l’Unité II, ensemble

Purpose: consolidate, without introducing any new productive system, the four independent specialized-domain dimensions built across Unité II — legal-administrative precision (Chapter 6), policy and institutions (Chapter 7), technical mediation (Chapter 8), and diplomacy and cooperation (Chapter 9) — and practice selecting the right tool for a given communicative moment.

Key distinction — four independent dimensions, not four steps in a sequence

Chapters 6–9 each added a dimension that operates independently of the others: a lease clause can state an obligation without any policy, technology, or negotiation being involved; a public measure can have a stated aim and a responsible institution without any legal contract or technical risk in play; a technology can present a genuine risk without any institutional or diplomatic dimension at all; and a negotiation can involve a concession, a commitment, and an openness without a single named legal source or technical capability anywhere in it. This chapter does not ask learners to apply the four dimensions in a fixed order. It asks learners to recognize, moment by moment, which dimension (or combination of dimensions) a given question at the public meeting actually calls for, and to reach for the corresponding tool.

10.1 Recognizing which specialized domain is in play

Grammar Focus — A diagnostic question for each domain

Before choosing a tool, ask which question the moment is actually posing:

  • Legal-administrative (Ch. 6): Does the question concern exactly what an individual party must do, is entitled to, and under what named source and condition? Reach for être tenu de, avoir le droit de / avoir droit à, en vertu de, and sous réserve de.
  • Policy and institutions (Ch. 7): Does the question concern what a public measure is designed to achieve, which program it belongs to, and which institution is responsible and in whose interest? Reach for avoir pour objectif de, dans le cadre de, il incombe à / relever de, and dans l’intérêt de.
  • Technical mediation (Ch. 8): Does the question concern what a technology can actually do, what could go wrong, an open ethical question, or a precaution? Reach for être en mesure de, présenter un risque de, il convient de se demander si, and à condition de.
  • Diplomacy and cooperation (Ch. 9): Does the question concern what a negotiating partner has actually promised, merely acknowledged, or signaled openness to? Reach for tout en reconnaissant que, s’engager à, être disposé à, and dans un esprit de.

More than one question can apply to the same moment. A single answer about the drainage sensors (Ch. 8) can also cite which institution is responsible for maintaining them (Ch. 7) and the legal basis of the vendors' right to be informed (Ch. 6), just as Karim and Sophie combined tools across Chapters 6–9 in the ongoing market dossier.

Model Dialogue: La réunion publique de bouclage du dossier marché

Context: the town hall holds a single public meeting closing out the market renovation dossier before the council's final vote. M. Ouedraogo, Mme Rigal, Mme Diarra (municipal technician), and a regional representative are present; Sophie and Karim mediate and report. Register: neutre/courant to soutenu, precise and public-facing throughout.

M. Ouedraogo : Bon, ce soir on règle tout ? D’abord : le nettoyage à sept heures, c’est obligatoire ou pas ? All right, are we settling everything tonight? First: the seven o'clock cleaning rule, is that mandatory or not?
Sophie : C’est une obligation : chaque commerçant est tenu de libérer son emplacement avant sept heures, en vertu de la clause 4 du bail type, sous réserve des jours de nettoyage effectivement programmés. It's an obligation: each vendor is required to vacate their spot before seven o'clock, under clause 4 of the standard lease, subject to the cleaning days actually scheduled.
Mme Rigal : Et le toit ? Ça fait trois ans qu’on en parle. And the roof? We've been talking about it for three years.
Karim : Le dispositif a pour objectif de réduire les risques d’inondation ; dans le cadre de la politique de rénovation urbaine, la réfection du toit relève de la région, donc il incombe à la région de la financer, dans l’intérêt de l’ensemble des commerçants. The scheme is designed to reduce flood risk; as part of the urban-renewal policy, the roof repair falls under regional jurisdiction, so it's the region's responsibility to finance it, in the interest of all vendors.
M. Ouedraogo : Et les fameux capteurs, on peut vraiment leur faire confiance ? And those famous sensors, can we actually trust them?
Mme Diarra : Ils sont en mesure de signaler une montée des eaux deux heures à l’avance, sur la base des essais menés depuis mars. Le système présente néanmoins un risque de fausse alerte en cas de pluies inhabituelles, et il convient de se demander si les données de fréquentation seront un jour réutilisées à d’autres fins. Nous ne pouvons maintenir le système fiable qu’à condition de vérifier son calibrage chaque mois. They're able to signal a rise in water levels two hours in advance, based on tests conducted since March. The system nonetheless presents a risk of false alarm during unusual rain, and it's worth asking whether foot-traffic data will one day be reused for other purposes. We can only keep the system reliable provided we check its calibration every month.
Mme Rigal : Et l’accord avec la région partenaire pour l’approvisionnement ? On m’a dit que le transport serait payé. And the agreement with the partner region for the supply chain? I was told transport would be paid.
Le représentant régional : Tout en reconnaissant que le délai de livraison pose un vrai problème pour nos petits producteurs, nous maintenons notre proposition. La commune s’engage à financer la moitié des coûts de transport la première année, et nous sommes disposés à revoir la fréquence des livraisons après six mois — ce n’est pas encore décidé, mais nous négocions dans un esprit de coopération régionale. While acknowledging that the delivery window is a real problem for our small producers, we're maintaining our proposal. The municipality commits to financing half the transport costs the first year, and we're open to revisiting delivery frequency after six months — that's not decided yet, but we're negotiating in a spirit of regional cooperation.
Karim : Si je peux me permettre de vous interrompre un instant — pour nos lecteurs, ce financement du transport, c’est promis, et la fréquence des livraisons, ce n’est encore qu’une ouverture ? If I may interrupt for a moment — for our readers, the transport funding is promised, and the delivery frequency is still just an openness?
Le représentant régional : Exactement — c’est la distinction essentielle ce soir. Exactly — that's the essential distinction tonight.

This dialogue moves through all four Unité II dimensions within a single continuous public meeting, recycling Chapter 4's polite interruption (Unité I) so Karim can confirm, for readers, exactly which statement is a firm commitment and which is only an openness — the same distinction at the heart of Chapter 9.

Culture and Communication

Culture in Context — The public meeting as a microcosm of specialized French discourse

The kind of closing public meeting modeled in this chapter — where legal precision, institutional attribution, technical honesty, and diplomatic accuracy all surface within a single evening — models one plausible setting in which French local civic life brings these specialized registers together, rather than keeping them in separate, isolated domains. Recognizing which specialized tool a given question calls for, quickly and without conscious translation from a chapter number, is itself a mark of growing fluency at this level.

Regional Variation — A cumulative reminder

As in Chapters 6–9, this chapter uses a simplified metropolitan-French administrative setting; the specific allocation of responsibilities in the market dossier (which institution handles the roof, the drainage, or the transport funding) is fictional, while the broader need to distinguish competences among territorial levels reflects genuine French administrative practice. Belgium, Switzerland, Québec, and other Francophone jurisdictions organize these domains differently. The underlying skills — stating obligations precisely, attributing institutional responsibility, mediating technical claims honestly, and distinguishing commitment from openness — transfer across Francophone contexts even where the specific institutions and procedures differ.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake — Reaching for the wrong chapter's tool

Error: Using a legal-obligation frame (Chapter 6) when the actual question is about which institution is responsible (Chapter 7), or using a technical-capability frame (Chapter 8) when the actual question is about a diplomatic commitment (Chapter 9).

Correction: Ask the diagnostic question for each domain before choosing a tool (see Grammar 10.1). A question about who must do what under a lease is a legal-administrative question, not a policy question; a question about what has actually been promised is a diplomatic question, not a technical one.

Common Mistake — Treating this chapter as a place for new grammar

Error: Introducing a new tense, mood, subjonctif trigger, or clitic pattern in this chapter's integrated task because the scenario feels demanding enough to justify it.

Correction: Chapter 10 is an integration chapter by design. Its challenge is combining Chapters 6–9's tools fluently under a single scenario, not expanding the grammar inventory. Everything needed is already active from Volumes I–IV, Unité I, and Chapters 6–9.

Common Mistake — Blurring a commitment and an openness under time pressure

Error: In the rush of an integrated task that also covers legal, policy, and technical ground, reporting the region's openness (Ch. 9) as though it were a firm commitment, simply because several other things in the same answer were stated with confidence.

Correction: The precision required within each domain does not relax because several domains appear in the same task. Keep s’engager à and être disposé à as distinct as they were in Chapter 9, even while also stating a legal obligation or a technical risk in the same breath.

Writing Workshop — Model text

Modèle — Compte rendu de la réunion de bouclage

Obligation, droit, et source (Ch. 6) : Chaque commerçant est tenu d’afficher ses prix de façon visible, en vertu de l’article 3 du règlement du marché, sous réserve des dispositions particulières applicables aux produits vendus au poids. À titre de commerçant titulaire d’un emplacement fixe, chaque commerçant a également droit à un rappel écrit avant toute sanction en cas de premier manquement, en vertu du même règlement.

Politique et institution (Ch. 7) : Le nouveau dispositif d’éclairage a pour objectif d’améliorer la sécurité en soirée. Dans le cadre du plan municipal de sécurité urbaine, cette installation relève de la mairie ; il lui incombe donc de la financer, dans l’intérêt des commerçants qui ferment tard.

Médiation technique (Ch. 8) : Le système est en mesure de détecter une baisse de luminosité et de s’allumer automatiquement, sur la base des essais menés depuis janvier. Il présente néanmoins un risque de déclenchement inutile par temps couvert. Il convient de se demander si les données de consommation seront un jour partagées avec un fournisseur privé ; la mairie l’exclut pour l’instant. Le service technique peut maintenir le système fiable à condition de vérifier les capteurs de luminosité chaque trimestre.

Diplomatie et coopération (Ch. 9) : Tout en reconnaissant que l’installation cause une gêne temporaire aux commerçants voisins des travaux, la mairie maintient son calendrier. Elle s’engage à indemniser les commerçants concernés pour les deux semaines de travaux. Elle se dit disposée à étudier un report si des imprévus surviennent, dans un esprit de coopération avec les commerçants du marché.

Obligation, right, and source (Ch. 6): Each vendor is required to display prices visibly, under article 3 of the market regulation, subject to the special provisions applicable to products sold by weight. As a vendor holding a fixed stall, each vendor is also entitled to a written reminder before any sanction for a first infraction, under the same regulation.

Policy and institution (Ch. 7): The new lighting scheme is designed to improve evening safety. As part of the municipal urban-safety plan, this installation falls under the town hall's jurisdiction; it is therefore the town hall's responsibility to finance it, in the interest of vendors who close late.

Technical mediation (Ch. 8): The system is able to detect a drop in light levels and switch on automatically, based on tests conducted since January. It nonetheless presents a risk of unnecessary triggering in overcast weather. It is worth asking whether consumption data will one day be shared with a private provider; the town hall currently rules this out. The technical service can keep the system reliable provided it checks the light sensors every quarter.

Diplomacy and cooperation (Ch. 9): While acknowledging that the installation causes temporary inconvenience to vendors near the works, the town hall is maintaining its schedule. It commits to compensating affected vendors for the two weeks of work. It says it is open to considering a postponement if unforeseen issues arise, in a spirit of cooperation with the market's vendors.

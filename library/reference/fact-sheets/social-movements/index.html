Social Movements

Social Movements fact sheets explain organized collective efforts seeking social, cultural, legal, economic, or institutional change. Each entry identifies a movement’s origins, participants, claims, leadership, methods, opposition, achievements, limitations, and historical effects.

Collection Overview

This collection serves as the canonical location for collective action focused on changing social relationships, public conditions, cultural norms, legal status, or institutional practice. Entries distinguish social movements from political parties, organizations, ideologies, campaigns, and isolated events.

Individual organizations, laws, political movements, and historical periods remain in their corresponding categories and may be connected through cross-references.

Collection Scope

  • Civil rights, labor, feminist, environmental, and reform movements
  • Community organizing, protest, advocacy, and direct action
  • Movement leadership, membership, symbols, messaging, and coalitions
  • Opposition, repression, internal disputes, and institutional response
  • Achievements, limitations, legacy, and historical memory

Reference Entries

Social movement fact sheets are listed chronologically or by movement tradition as they are added.

  • Black Lives Matter

    A decentralized social movement opposing racial injustice and police violence and advocating broader institutional accountability.

  • Civil Rights Movement

    A broad twentieth-century movement that challenged racial segregation, disfranchisement, discrimination, and unequal treatment under law.

  • Say Her Name

    A movement and public-awareness campaign highlighting Black women and girls affected by police violence and related forms of state or interpersonal harm.

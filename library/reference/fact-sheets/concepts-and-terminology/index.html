Concepts and Terminology

Concepts and Terminology fact sheets define important ideas, frameworks, classifications, and specialized terms used across history, government, law, economics, culture, science, technology, and other fields. Each entry provides a clear definition, relevant context, distinctions from related terms, and guidance for accurate use.

Collection Overview

This collection serves as the canonical location for subjects whose primary purpose is to explain a concept or define terminology. Entries may clarify disputed meanings, distinguish commonly confused terms, explain how usage changes across disciplines or historical periods, and identify the practical significance of a concept.

A subject centered on a specific person, event, law, court case, institution, organization, or historical period remains in its corresponding fact-sheet category. Related concepts may be connected through cross-references without duplicating the original fact sheet in multiple folders.

Reference Entries

Fact sheets in this collection are listed alphabetically as they are added.

  • Broken Windows Policing

    A policing strategy emphasizing enforcement of minor offenses to deter more serious crime through visible order maintenance.

  • Chokeholds and Neck Restraints

    Methods of physical restraint used by law enforcement and the legal, medical, and policy debates surrounding their use.

  • Civilian Police Oversight

    Independent mechanisms established to review police conduct, investigate complaints, and promote accountability.

  • Community Policing

    A public safety philosophy emphasizing partnerships between law enforcement and the communities they serve.

  • Community Violence Intervention

    Non-law-enforcement strategies designed to reduce violence through prevention, outreach, mediation, and community engagement.

  • Disfranchisement

    The denial or restriction of voting rights through legal, political, or administrative means.

  • Duty to Intervene

    The professional obligation of law enforcement officers to prevent or report misconduct by fellow officers.

  • Federal Civil Rights Prosecution

    Federal criminal enforcement of civil rights laws against officials or individuals who violate protected rights.

  • Institutional Racism

    Discriminatory outcomes arising from the policies, practices, or structures of institutions rather than individual actions alone.

  • Literacy Tests

    Voting requirements historically used in many jurisdictions to restrict electoral participation.

  • Lynching

    Extrajudicial killings carried out by mobs, often used as instruments of racial terror and intimidation.

  • Mass Incarceration

    The large-scale imprisonment of populations and its legal, social, economic, and public policy implications.

  • Militarization of Police

    The acquisition and use of military equipment, tactics, and organizational practices by civilian law enforcement agencies.

  • No-Knock Warrants

    Judicial warrants permitting officers to enter premises without prior announcement under specified legal circumstances.

  • Pattern-or-Practice Investigations

    Federal investigations examining whether law enforcement agencies engage in systemic violations of constitutional or statutory rights.

  • Police Brutality

    The excessive or unlawful use of force by law enforcement officers against individuals.

  • Police Misconduct Databases

    Systems used to document, track, and analyze allegations or findings of misconduct involving law enforcement personnel.

  • Police Unions

    Labor organizations representing law enforcement officers in collective bargaining, workplace advocacy, and disciplinary matters.

  • Racial Profiling

    The use of race, ethnicity, or national origin as a factor in law enforcement or security decisions.

  • School-to-Prison Pipeline

    A term describing policies and practices believed to increase students' likelihood of entering the juvenile or criminal justice systems.

  • Segregation

    The separation of people by race or other characteristics through law, policy, custom, or social practice.

  • Separate but Equal

    A legal doctrine that historically justified racial segregation while claiming equal treatment under the law.

  • Stop-and-Frisk

    A policing practice involving temporary detention and limited searches based on reasonable suspicion.

  • Sundown Towns

    Communities that historically excluded certain racial or ethnic groups through law, intimidation, or informal practices.

  • Systemic Racism

    A concept describing how multiple institutions and social systems may collectively produce persistent racial disparities.

  • War on Drugs

    The broad governmental campaign employing criminal justice and public policy measures to reduce illegal drug production, distribution, and use.

  • White Primary

    A historical electoral practice restricting participation in party primary elections to white voters.

Economic Systems

Economic Systems fact sheets explain how societies organize production, ownership, labor, exchange, distribution, consumption, and the allocation of resources. The collection covers economic theories, institutions, markets, policies, and systems of economic coordination.

Collection Overview

This collection serves as the canonical location for subjects centered on the structure and operation of an economic system. Entries may examine property, capital, markets, planning, taxation, finance, trade, public ownership, private ownership, and the relationship between economic power and political institutions.

Individual laws, government programs, labor movements, organizations, and historical events remain in their corresponding categories and may be connected through cross-references.

Collection Scope

  • Capitalism, socialism, communism, and mixed economies
  • Markets, trade, finance, and monetary systems
  • Property, ownership, capital, and production
  • Taxation, public finance, and economic policy
  • Distribution, consumption, inequality, and economic power

Reference Entries

Economic systems fact sheets are listed alphabetically or by major economic model as they are added.

  • Crop Lien System

    An agricultural credit system in which farmers pledged future crops as collateral for loans, often resulting in long-term indebtedness.

  • Plantation System

    A large-scale agricultural production system historically dependent on concentrated land ownership and, in the American South, the labor of enslaved people before the Civil War.

  • Sharecropping

    An agricultural labor arrangement in which tenant farmers cultivated land in exchange for a share of the harvested crop.

  • Tenant Farming

    A farming system in which individuals rented land from property owners and typically paid rent in cash, crops, or a combination of both.

Historical Events

Historical Events fact sheets provide concise accounts of significant events, turning points, crises, conflicts, movements, and institutional changes. Each entry identifies the time, place, participants, causes, sequence, consequences, and historical significance of the subject.

Collection Overview

This collection serves as the canonical location for discrete historical events that cannot be assigned more precisely to a specialized category. Entries distinguish documented facts, attributed interpretations, disputed claims, and later consequences while placing each event within its broader historical context.

Events centered on a particular war, law, court case, social movement, or African American historical period remain in their corresponding categories and may be connected through cross-references.

Collection Scope

  • Political, social, economic, and cultural events
  • Crises, turning points, and institutional transitions
  • Protests, demonstrations, elections, and public actions
  • Disasters, emergencies, and major public incidents
  • Causes, chronology, consequences, and historical significance

Reference Entries

Historical event fact sheets are listed chronologically or alphabetically as they are added.

  • Black Lives Matter Plaza

    A two-block pedestrian area in Washington, D.C., designated in 2020 as a public expression of support for the Black Lives Matter movement.

  • Black Lives Matter Protests (2020)

    A nationwide and international wave of demonstrations following the killing of George Floyd that became one of the largest protest movements in U.S. history.

  • Black Political Participation During Reconstruction

    The expansion of African American voting, officeholding, and civic engagement during the Reconstruction Era following the Civil War.

  • Capitol Hill Organized Protest (CHOP)

    An autonomous protest zone established in Seattle during the 2020 demonstrations following the killing of George Floyd.

  • Confederate Monument Removals

    The removal or relocation of Confederate monuments and memorials in response to changing public attitudes and civic debate.

  • Congressional Reconstruction

    The phase of Reconstruction directed by Congress that imposed conditions for Southern readmission and expanded federal protections for civil rights.

  • COVID-19 and the 2020 Protests

    An examination of how the COVID-19 pandemic shaped the organization, public health response, and social context of the 2020 protest movement.

  • Detroit Rebellion (1967)

    A major urban uprising in Detroit that became one of the largest episodes of civil unrest in twentieth-century American history.

  • Ferguson Protests

    Demonstrations that followed the 2014 police shooting of Michael Brown in Ferguson, Missouri, drawing national attention to policing and race relations.

  • Freedom Riders

    Civil rights activists who challenged segregation in interstate travel through coordinated bus rides across the American South in 1961.

  • Freedom Summer

    A 1964 voter registration and education campaign in Mississippi that became a defining effort of the Civil Rights Movement.

  • Jim Crow Era

    The historical period characterized by legally enforced racial segregation and widespread disfranchisement following Reconstruction.

  • Kenosha Protests

    Demonstrations and civil unrest in Wisconsin following the police shooting of Jacob Blake in August 2020.

  • Lafayette Square Clearing

    The June 1, 2020, clearing of demonstrators from Lafayette Square near the White House by federal and local law enforcement.

  • Little Rock Nine

    The integration of Little Rock Central High School by nine African American students under federal protection in 1957.

  • Long Hot Summer of 1967

    A period marked by widespread urban unrest across numerous American cities during the summer of 1967.

  • Los Angeles Uprising (1992)

    Civil unrest that followed the acquittal of police officers charged in the beating of Rodney King.

  • March on Washington

    The 1963 mass demonstration for jobs and freedom best known for Martin Luther King Jr.'s "I Have a Dream" speech.

  • Military Reconstruction

    The administration of the former Confederate states under federal military authority following the Reconstruction Acts of 1867.

  • Minneapolis Third Precinct Fire

    The destruction of the Minneapolis Police Department's Third Precinct building during the 2020 civil unrest.

  • Murder of George Floyd

    The killing of George Floyd by a Minneapolis police officer on May 25, 2020, an event that sparked global demonstrations.

  • Newark Rebellion (1967)

    A major episode of civil unrest in Newark, New Jersey, that highlighted longstanding tensions over policing and inequality.

  • Portland Federal Courthouse Protests

    Extended demonstrations in Portland, Oregon, centered on the Mark O. Hatfield United States Courthouse during 2020.

  • Presidential Reconstruction

    The initial phase of Reconstruction directed by Presidents Abraham Lincoln and Andrew Johnson following the Civil War.

  • Radical Reconstruction

    The period in which Radical Republicans pursued expanded federal protections for civil rights and political participation in the South.

  • Reconstruction

    The period following the American Civil War during which the United States addressed national reunification, constitutional change, and the status of formerly enslaved people.

  • Reconstruction Violence

    Organized intimidation, terrorism, and political violence that sought to undermine Reconstruction governments and civil rights.

  • Red Summer (1919)

    A period of widespread racial violence and riots across the United States during 1919.

  • Secession

    The withdrawal of Southern states from the United States that led directly to the American Civil War.

  • Selma to Montgomery Marches

    Three voting rights marches in Alabama during 1965 that helped build support for the Voting Rights Act.

  • Shooting of Jacob Blake

    The police shooting of Jacob Blake in Kenosha, Wisconsin, that triggered widespread demonstrations in 2020.

  • Strike for Black Lives

    A nationwide labor action advocating racial justice and economic equality through coordinated workplace demonstrations.

  • Surrender at Appomattox

    The April 9, 1865, surrender of General Robert E. Lee to General Ulysses S. Grant, marking the effective end of the Civil War.

  • Watts Riots

    The 1965 civil unrest in the Watts neighborhood of Los Angeles that became a landmark event in modern American urban history.

## 3. Publication-critical historical corrections — completed

### 3.1 Electoral numbers and denominators (§§78.4 and 78.7, chapter summary)

The previous draft incorrectly used approximately 126,000 *votes* as the 1951 *registered electorate*, then calculated an eightfold registration increase.

The publication edition now distinguishes:

| Measure | 1951 election | 1956 election |
|---|---:|---:|
| Registered electors | **204,649** | **1,119,047** |
| Valid presidential votes in 1951 / all ballots cast in 1956 | **126,123** | **955,349** |
| MNR presidential candidate votes | **54,129** (42.92% of valid presidential votes) | Not used as a direct ratio to registrants |

The same-category increase in registered voters is **approximately 5.47-fold**. Published compilations vary slightly in other totals (e.g., one union-history tabulation lists 1,109,047 registered in 1956 and different candidate totals), so the chapter explicitly identifies its chosen historical series and avoids spurious precision in cross-series comparisons.

Evidence: digitized *Presencia* election retrospective in the Banco Central de Bolivia digital library, 6 June 1993: https://bibliotecadigital.bcb.gob.bo/xmlui/bitstream/handle/123456789/14447/Presencia%2C%206%20de%20Junio%201993.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1 ; historical electoral table compiled from Bolivian election/statistical records: https://files.uagrm.edu.bo/entidad/62/file/Investigaci%C3%B3n_Docentes/Vargas_M.__2021-1__CONCILIACION_DATOS_DE_LOS_CENSOS_DE_POBLACION_Y_PADRON_ELECTORAL.pdf ; and official labor ministry history with a slightly differing series: https://mintrabajo.gob.bo/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Libro-No-12-Medio-siglo-de-luchas-sindicales-revol-en-Bolivia-2023.pdf . The PDF source interpretation is checked against structured tables; no claim of original electoral-register collation is made.

### 3.2 Age-specific suffrage provisions (§78.7 and suffrage source window)

The decree did not simply grant voting rights to every resident adult without qualification. Supreme Decree **3128 of 21 July 1952** enfranchised men and women regardless of education, occupation, or income, with explicit age rules (**unmarried citizens 21+; married citizens 18+**) and other contemporary statutory exclusions. The main narrative and source-window paraphrase now reflect that nuance.

Documentary identifications: https://www.derechoteca.com/gacetabolivia/decreto-supremo-3128-del-21-julio-1952/ ; 1952 catalogue record: https://books.google.com/books/about/Decreto_supremo_no_3128_de_21_7_52.html?id=UvZPewAACAAJ ; documentary legal-history discussion: https://archivos.juridicas.unam.mx/www/bjv/libros/5/2053/6.pdf .

### 3.3 Barrientos and Ovando leadership chronology (§78.20)

Removed the anachronism asserting that Barrientos and Ovando became *co-presidents* on 4 November **1964**. The revised text preserves the initial contested leadership, Ovando's announcement of a military junta, Barrientos's assumption of its leadership, and Ovando's military command, and identifies the **formal co-presidency as beginning on 27 May 1965**. That date is presented only as a narrow clarification beyond the principal endpoint.

Primary documentary anchors: State Department FRUS November 4, 1964 cable, document 151 (https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v31/d151); FRUS editorial note identifying the May 27, 1965 co-presidency, document 155 (https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v31/d155). American diplomatic reports reflect American perceptions and must be read as situated evidence rather than neutral statements of Bolivian social experience.

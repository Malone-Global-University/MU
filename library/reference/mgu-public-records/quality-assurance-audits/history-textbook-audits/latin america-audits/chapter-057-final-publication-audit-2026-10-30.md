## 1. Governing scope and narrative

The chapter adheres to the approved 27-section, six-division architecture (4/5/5/4/5/4). It covers the colonial Kingdom of Guatemala and Cádiz constitutional crisis; 1821 independence; 1822 Mexican annexation; the 1823 second independence; 1824 Federal Republic; conflicts and fragmentation by 1840; Cuba and Puerto Rico's continuing Spanish status; and differential territorial sovereignty in Santo Domingo, Panama, Belize, and the Mosquito Coast. Later Central American nation-building and the independence wars of Cuba and Puerto Rico are reserved to the subsequent assigned chapters.

**Measured direct narrative:** approximately 14,529 words, calculated from immediate narrative `<p>` children of sections `s57-1` through `s57-27`, excluding pedagogical feature boxes, opening, chronology, summaries, and review material. The former 20,000-word production target was explicitly retired; substantive completeness rather than artificial volume governs publication.

The text has undergone repeated author expansion and incorporates some repetitive descriptions of commercial competition, constitutional representation, and regional rivalry. Consolidation was restricted to essential accuracy and publication issues; the manuscript was not subjected to another wholesale rewrite.

## 3. Source audit and publication-critical corrections

The following evidence-based changes were made to the publication-final HTML. No additional structural reorganization or narrative expansion was performed.

1. **§57.2:** Corrected the misleading statement that Guatemala's cochineal industry 'had declined.' Cultivation around Antigua developed into a major export after independence; cochineal exports rose substantially in the 1820s–1830s. See *Weak Foundations: The Economy of El Salvador in the Nineteenth Century*, UC Press electronic edition: https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/weak-foundations-the-economy-of-el-salvador-in-the-nineteenth-century-1821-1898.html
2. **§57.10:** Clarified the distinct constitutional roles of the Central American Congress, Senate, and Supreme Court: Senate legislative sanction and executive oversight (arts. 79–83, 89–100), Supreme Court federal and interstate cases and procedures (arts. 136–137), and disputes between state authorities under art. 194. Source: Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes, 1824 Constitution: https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/constitucion-de-las-provincias-unidas-del-centro-de-america-de-1824/html/1b0d6e8a-d761-43b1-a575-cc6ce4776418_2.html
3. **§57.11:** Replaced vague 'recognized only in the 1840s' language with the confirmed establishment of the Diocese of San Salvador on **28 September 1842**. Sources: Salvadoran Ministry of Culture https://www.cultura.gob.sv/brindaron-ponencia-sobre-la-creacion-del-obispado-de-san-salvador/ and historical scholarship https://www.scielo.sa.cr/scielo.php?pid=S1409-469X2021000200218&script=sci_arttext
4. **1823 Source Window:** Corrected the anachronistic formulation that Chiapas had already 'joined Mexico' at the 1823 declaration. Its allegiance was disputed; incorporation was formalized in **1824**. Source: Mexico's Instituto Nacional para el Federalismo y el Desarrollo Municipal: https://www.gob.mx/inafed/articulos/hace-193-anos-chiapas-se-integro-a-la-republica-mexicana
5. **1815 Source Window:** Corrected the inaccurate implication that Puerto Rico's Royal Decree of Graces directly applied to Cuba; the document addressed **Puerto Rico**. Clarified its racially unequal settler land grants and implications for plantation slavery. Source: Puerto Rican Historical Encyclopedia: https://enciclopediapr.org/content/real-cedula-de-gracia-1815/
6. **Major People and Places:** Removed wording that could misleadingly suggest Carrera held formal governmental rule beginning in 1838; his political influence and later control developed over time.
7. **Chronology and Timeline Study:** Put the Spanish Constitution of **1837** entry in correct chronological order before the 1837–39 and 1838–40 ranges.
8. **All six Source Windows:** Replaced unresolved reader-facing 'Verification note' placeholders with direct documentary edition links and clear identification of the excerpts as paraphrases, not direct quotations. This preserves the evidence boundaries of the teaching sources.

Documentary editions used for the six source windows, in historical sequence:

- Guatemala's **15 September 1821 Act**: Archivo General de Centro América transcription, fol. 17v: https://ahgua.ufm.edu/agca/acta-de-independencia-de-centroamerica-15-de-septiembre-1821-002/
- **1 July 1823 independence declaration**: Nicaragua's LeyBook legislative transcription: https://www.leybook.com/doc/19024
- **22 November 1824 Federal Constitution**: Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes: https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/constitucion-de-las-provincias-unidas-del-centro-de-america-de-1824/html/1b0d6e8a-d761-43b1-a575-cc6ce4776418_2.html

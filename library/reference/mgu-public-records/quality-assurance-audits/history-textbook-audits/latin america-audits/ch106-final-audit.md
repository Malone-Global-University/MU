## Independently checked factual and legal corrections

1. **1998 election**: Source-based figures **3,673,685 votes, 56.20% for Chávez** and **2,613,161, 39.97% for Salas Römer**. IFES registered electorate **10,959,530** and ballots cast **6,988,291** imply approximately **63.76%** turnout on that particular registry. Some CNE-derived compilations use different registry totals. Source: https://electionguide.org/elections/id/1771/ and Carter Center observation report https://www.cartercenter.org/documents/1151.pdf .
2. **1998 platform**: *Una revolución democrática: la propuesta de Hugo Chávez para transformar Venezuela* authenticated as an identifiable program title through retrospective government documents and scholarship. **Its original signed/printed 1998 edition was not facsimile-collated**; the source window labels the later records accordingly. Historical corroboration: https://hugochavez.ve/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Plan-de-la-Nacion-2001-2007.pdf and https://www.fondodeculturaeconomica.com/Noticia/3183 .
3. **April 1999 referendum**: Published 87.75% and 81.74% affirmative question tallies, turnout approximately **37.65%**. The window links the March 24 CNE question-resolution reproduction. Political approval by participating voters is distinguished from participation across all registered voters. See https://sites.nd.edu/michael-coppedge/files/2018/01/IADVEN.pdf .
4. **Constituent Assembly membership**: **131 seats**: 104 regional, 24 national, 3 Indigenous. The 1999 U.S. State Department contemporary count records **119 MVR seats**; an OAS report counts **122 pro-government winners of 128 non-Indigenous delegates**. The revision explicitly distinguishes party and wider-coalition counts; it does not silently treat these as one identical number. Sources: https://1997-2001.state.gov/global/human_rights/1999_hrp_report/venezuel.html and https://www.oas.org/sap/docs/misiones/cp_doc_3301_00_eng.pdf .
5. **1999 Constitution**: Article 72 recall conditions, institutional reorganization, and Indigenous-rights provisions retained. Constitutional legal text: https://www.bcn.cl/procesoconstituyente/comparadordeconstituciones/constitucion/ven . The OAS legal archive holds a judicial-reorganization decree: https://www.oas.org/juridico/spanish/ven_res51.pdf .
6. **December 1999 referendum**: **3,301,475 yes; 1,298,105 no**, or **71.78% / 28.22%** of valid votes; **44.38%** turnout from IFES registration totals. Source: https://www.electionguide.org/elections/id/93/ .
7. **2000 election**: Chávez **3,757,773 votes, 59.76%** and Arias Cárdenas **2,359,459, 37.52%** (reported electoral convention); IFES cast **6,600,196** of registered **11,720,660** (~**56.31% turnout**). Source: https://electionguide.org/elections/id/1821/ .
8. **2001 enabling-law measures**: Constitutionally delegated legislative authority distinguished from objections to its scope, limited deliberation, and specific laws. Historical 49-law-package convention retained; the **Carmona decree itself identifies 48 measures in Article 9**, which the corrected text identifies instead of forcing numbers to match. https://archivo.provea.org/wp-content/uploads/ley_habilitante.pdf and https://urru.org/11A/20021412_DecretoCarmona.htm .
9. **Article 233 error fixed**: The original falsely asserted that Article 233 *expressly* requires formal resignation presented before the National Assembly. The revised text correctly explains that it identifies resignation as an absolute vacancy and separately specifies the Assembly's role in declaring *abandonment*; absence of a verified voluntary resignation and constitutional succession rules are assessed separately. https://www.bcn.cl/procesoconstituyente/comparadordeconstituciones/constitucion/ven .
10. **April 2002**: The opposition march, shootings, military defection, detention, Carmona decree, OAS condemnation, and restoration are distinguished from unresolved attribution of individual shootings and allegations of foreign planning. OAS CP/RES 811 of **April 13**: https://www.oas.org/consejo/resolutions/res811.asp . IACHR 2003: https://hrlibrary.umn.edu/iachr/country-reports/venezuela2003.html .
11. **Subsequent 2002 judicial ruling**: Clearly dated **August 14, 2002** and described as a decision not to initiate proceedings against four officers, not an adjudication validating Carmona's government. Later debate and 2005 reversal are noted as retrospective legal context, not as events within the chapter's principal 2002 April endpoint. https://www.hrw.org/reports/2008/venezuela0908/3.htm .
12. **Quantitative Study III rebuilt**: No longer states that every 1998–2000 electoral event involved participation by less than half the electorate. It differentiates turnout, valid-vote shares, seat counts and calculated shares of registrants. The registry convention is stated rather than hidden; seat counts are not mechanically turned into vote share.

## Documentary apparatus and limitations

All **nine** source windows link to an identified original, a digitized reproduction, a contemporary report, or an explicitly qualified later finding aid. Source window paraphrases are editorial, *not authenticated quotations*. The 1998 platform is not presented as a facsimile-collated primary manuscript; a 2006 reupload of the February 1992 surrender footage is identified as a secondary hosted copy, not a broadcaster-certified master. The study is accessible as a video source but not embedded as a copyrighted still. The Carmona Act is linked as a reproduced text, not a signed original. Three maps are *text-guided historical exercises* requiring an external political atlas.

The bibliography retains twenty scholarly and documentary references, supplemented by five direct documentary records. Some monograph entries have no full-text link by design. Links come from located publisher, institutional, archival, media or reference results; their current availability and every linked document's full contents were **not** exhaustively live-verified. No unsupported source quotations have been inserted.

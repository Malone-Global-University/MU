## C. Corrected factual and economic points

1. **Chile–Bolivia sovereignty (§68.9):** Peru ceded Tarapacá to Chile through the **1883 Treaty of Ancón**; the **1884 Chile–Bolivia truce** preserved Chilean *administration* of occupied Bolivian territory during the truce; Article II of the **1904 peace treaty** recognized Chilean dominion. The narrative no longer conflates these instruments. Original treaty texts: https://www.bcn.cl/leychile/Navegar?idNorma=400056 and https://www.bcn.cl/leychile/navegar?idNorma=400123 .
2. **Caliche (§68.9):** explicitly described as nitrate-bearing desert ore, rather than sodium nitrate itself before processing.
3. **Simón I. Patiño (§68.11):** rich La Salvadora lode generally dated to **1900**, with acquisition/operation distinguished from major mineral discovery. This remains subject to variation in fine-grained histories (some date specific lode work to 1901). See https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00076791.2025.2528751 .
4. **Peru's Grace Contract (§68.11):** now described as negotiated and approved in stages in **1889–1890**, rather than first concluded simply in 1890. The **Peruvian Corporation was registered 20 March 1890**. See https://archives.ucl.ac.uk/CalmView/record/catalog/PERUCORP ; supplementary Peruvian congressional history https://www.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/participacion/museo/congreso/files/files/mariano_valcarcel.pdf .
5. **Treaty of Petrópolis and Amazon rubber (§68.12):** includes Brazil's **£2 million compensation** to Bolivia and **Madeira–Mamoré railway** commitment. Official treaty printed in *Foreign Relations of the United States*, 1904: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1904/d74 .
6. **British investment (§68.15):** replaced a vague 'roughly a billion pounds by 1914' with a **broad estimate of approximately £1.2 billion invested in the region by 1913**, clearly identified as a capital **stock** rather than yearly flow. Narrower portfolio-securities estimates can be smaller due to classification and asset coverage; do not compare these without explanation. See Campbell, Gallagher and Grossman, *Economic History Review* (2026): https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ehr.70124 .
7. **Brazilian fiscal federalism (opening; §68.16; §68.17):** distinguished **state export taxes**, especially São Paulo after Brazil's **1891 Constitution**, from federal import customs; railway guarantees and subsidies are recognized as possible public costs, not automatically a tax-income source.
8. **Peruvian wartime scope (§68.7):** specified the 1879–1883 Peruvian phase within the wider 1879–1884 War of the Pacific.
9. **War of 1914 (§68.19):** replaced an inaccurate blanket reference to 'closed European markets' with documented **uneven** shipping, credit, insurance and destination-market disruption.
10. **Numerical interpretation:** reinforced the distinction among physical volume, export value, tax receipts, foreign-investment holdings and annual capital flows. No invented cross-country statistical table was retained as a 'primary source'.

## F. Explicit first-edition limitations

1. Some historical source reproductions (especially large archival PDF reports and the dated commercial-periodical PDF issues) were available through searchable institutional text and bibliographic catalogs, but page-image screen fetching returned access or redirect errors during this audit. Those **facsimile pages were not fully collated**; no source-window quotation, tabulated total or page-specific claim is presented as visually verified where that did not occur.
2. The Companhia Paulista 1908 annual report was specifically identified in APESP's catalog; its individual 320 pages and original statistical tables were not manually inspected in full. The teaching window therefore asks students to examine its tables and report audience, without inventing data from its pages.
3. The historical narrative uses rounded values and conceptual comparisons, not a reproducible compiled time series for every national export. Further quantitative study requires consulting specified tables, units and definitions in each original publication or specialist statistical study.
4. The three Map Studies are written analytical exercises; this scholarly textual HTML first edition includes **no embedded archival map plates**. Students must consult external atlases and archival maps.
5. External documentary URLs were verified as institutional records in available search/open results; future site availability is not guaranteed.

## 4. Consolidated factual and historical corrections

The original scaffold's major documentary issues were addressed in one integrated revision:

1. **§67.4:** The origin of the word *mambí* was changed from a categorical Afro-Dominican etymology to an explicitly uncertain etymology, as described by the Library of Congress.
2. **§67.6:** Corrected the Moret Law's retroactive date, **September 17, 1868**, and emphasized the law's relevance to both Cuba and Puerto Rico, not exclusively Cuba.
3. **§67.6/source window:** Added the Puerto Rican abolition statute's **at-least-three-year labor-contract requirement** allowing a former owner, another employer or the state, and the five-year delay on the full enjoyment of political rights.
4. **§67.8/chronology:** Distinguished the royal decree **dated October 7, 1886** from its **subsequent promulgation in Cuba**, upon which Article 1 provided for the termination of patronato; retained the distinction between statutory freedom and continued economic coercion.
5. **§67.12:** Replaced the unsupported one-range reconcentration mortality statement with an attributed, explicitly disputed scholarly range of roughly 60,000 to near 200,000, while distinguishing displacement, death and incomplete demographic records.
6. **§67.13:** Clarified the **November 25, 1897** autonomic charter, the **February 1898** inauguration of the autonomous government, the **March 1898** elections, and the **July/October 1898** invasion and sovereignty transition.
7. **§67.14:** Corrected the USS *Maine* figure to **266 eventual deaths** and retained uncertainty about the explosion's cause; preserved the separate April 1898 blockade and declaration chronology.
8. **§67.18:** Added the Cuban convention's **June 12, 1901 vote of 16–11** accepting the Platt provisions and distinguished the congressional amendment from the later **1903** bilateral treaty and Guantánamo arrangements.
9. **§67.17:** Preserved the legally critical distinction that **Article I** relinquished Spain's sovereignty over Cuba, while **Article II** ceded Puerto Rico and Guam to the United States; **Article III** provided for the Philippines and the $20 million payment.
10. **Learning questions/writing prompts:** Removed any implication that the 1895 war was longer than the **Ten Years' War of 1868–1878**. The comparison now concerns organization and geographical extension, not duration.

The chapter explicitly rejects treating the 1898 war as the beginning of Cuban independence, describing the decades of Cuban revolutionary activity and giving Puerto Rico a distinct narrative. It treats abolition, formal sovereignty, self-government, and social equality as separate analytical categories.

All seven primary-source windows identify creator or responsible institution, approximate or exact date, historical setting, source type, careful **paraphrase** rather than invented direct quotations, interpretation/limitations, an accessible published edition or catalogue record, and four source-specific student questions.

**Important limitation:** The linked primary-text editions, official summaries, institutional catalogues and historical scholarship were consulted to check material claims and legal chronology, but an exhaustive line-by-line collation of every archival original, full manuscript facsimile, or alternate translation was **not** performed. Therefore the source windows must remain labeled paraphrases, not purported original-document transcriptions. Where an archival catalogue rather than a full digital text is available, the link is identified as a catalogue. The chapter is approved for scholarly textual instruction, not as a definitive critical edition of archival sources.

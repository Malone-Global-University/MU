## Historical corrections made

### 1. Paraguay — transition opening versus elected civilian president

The February 1989 overthrow of Alfredo Stroessner was carried out by Gen. Andrés Rodríguez, who assumed the presidency and won an election in May 1989. It **was not** the installation of an elected civilian head of state. Paraguay promulgated a constitution on **20 June 1992**. Juan Carlos Wasmosy was elected in May 1993 and inaugurated on **15 August 1993** as the first freely elected civilian president in nearly four decades. This distinction is now made in the chapter opening, §94.8, the chronology table, Quantitative Study I, and Map Study I. The comparison table's heading and explanatory note have been corrected so that different political milestones are not misrepresented as uniform “restorations.”

**Authority:** U.S. Department of State, *Country Reports on Human Rights Practices for 1993* (Paraguay), https://www.refworld.org/reference/annualreport/usdos/1994/25329 ; U.S. Department of State background account, https://1997-2001.state.gov/background_notes/paraguay_0799_bgn.html .

### 2. Chile — Rettig Commission’s original 2,279-victim classification

The chapter formerly said that approximately 2,100 of the 2,279 accepted cases were directly attributable to state agents. The final text distinguishes the commission's categories:

| Original category | Victims |
|---|---:|
| Killed by state agents or persons acting for them | 1,068 |
| Detained and disappeared by state agents | 957 |
| Killed in politically motivated attacks by private actors | 90 |
| Other victims of political violence | 164 |
| **Total** | **2,279** |

The first two categories add to **2,025** directly state-agent cases; the first three together add to **2,115** classified as human-rights violations; the **164** others were separately classified as victims of political violence. This is reflected consistently in §94.14, Source Window 7 and Quantitative Study III. These are the commission's **original-period classifications**, not a cumulative count of all later recognized victims.

**Authority:** Chile's Instituto Nacional de Derechos Humanos, *Informe de la Comisión Nacional de Verdad y Reconciliación*, issued 1991, three-volume archive item, https://bibliotecadigital.indh.cl/items/edb83a4d-9121-48ee-8e66-09fe31e926fe ; Patricio Aylwin's 1991 public presentation; Memoria Chilena, https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-94640.html .

### 3. Chile — exact 1988 electoral certification and denominators

The text/source window now reports the Tribunal Calificador de Elecciones' November 1988 certified figures for the 5 October plebiscite: **No 3,967,579** and **Yes 3,119,110**, totaling **7,086,689 valid votes**. These correspond to **55.99%** and **44.01% of valid votes**. There were **94,594** null ballots and **70,660** blank ballots. The Tribunal's total of 7,251,943 ballots cast out of 7,429,404 registered electors yields approximately 97.6% turnout. The text no longer equates valid-vote shares with shares of all ballots. The historical source itself is a scanned official certification rather than a subsequent tertiary summary.

**Authority:** Chilean Tribunal Calificador de Elecciones, *Sentencia Plebiscito 1988*, https://tribunalcalificador.cl/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/SENTENCIA-PLEBISCITO-1988.pdf .

## Selected historical corrections and verification

1. Reconstructed the 1964 coup as a Brazilian civilian–military operation with separate U.S. diplomatic support and contingency planning, using the Goulart address and Lincoln Gordon’s State Department telegram.  
2. Distinguished the congressional vacancy declaration from troop movements while Goulart remained in Brazil.  
3. Explained the 1969 military junta and the otherwise omitted Médici presidency (1969–1974) as central to the Years of Lead and economic miracle.  
4. Distinguished AI-5's December 1968 intensification from repression already present after 1964 and described its legal powers from the original decree.  
5. Cited Central Bank SGS series 7326, sourced to IBGE, for the 1968–1973 annual GDP growth series; stated the series' methodology and revision limitations.  
6. Distinguished the 1973 Itaipu Treaty from 1975–1979 II PND implementation.  
7. Described the 434 cases documented by the National Truth Commission as a bounded, non-exhaustive list within a 1946–1988 mandate, not an exhaustive 1964–1985 death toll.  
8. Added specific southern São Paulo neighborhood-organizing evidence anchored in Eder Sader's study, rather than inventing a family interview.  
9. Distinguished the Amnesty Law’s enacted exclusions from the broader campaign's demand and later contested interpretations.  
10. Verified Dante de Oliveira voting figures as 298 affirmative, 65 negative, three abstentions and 112 absences; 320 affirmative votes were necessary.  
11. Confirmed the 1985 Electoral College result (Tancredo 480, Maluf 180) and corrected presidential succession: Sarney assumed the presidency on March 15; Tancredo never took office.

### 3. Applied publication-critical historical corrections

1. **Lázaro Cárdenas — presidential inauguration:** Replaced all four full-date instances of the false `30 November 1934` and one abbreviated date with **1 December 1934**. This corrects the opening scope, chronology preview, conclusion, Timeline Study, and people-and-places apparatus. Authoritative evidence: INEHRM, *Cárdenas llega a la presidencia*, https://inehrm.gob.mx/work/recursos/Cardenas/pdf/1_CardenasllegaalaPresidencia.pdf . The previously approved *planning document* also contained the incorrect date; the final chapter overrides it.

2. **1918 petroleum decrees — §73.12:** Replaced an inaccurate description that had merged the February, July, and August measures and attributed general registrations to the 12 August decree. The revised paragraph differentiates **19 February fiscal measures**, **31 July declaration/manifestation rules**, **8 August denunciation provisions**, and **12 August protections of certain already-worked or surveyed claims**, with provision for special exploitation contracts and charges on undeclared claims. Source: *FRUS 1918*, Fletcher to Secretary of State, no. 1312, 14 August, file 812.512/2089, https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1918/d676 . These were related but legally distinct acts; their paraphrases must not be collapsed.

3. **Source Window VII:** Corrected its formerly misleading summary to distinguish the 12 August measure from general registration rules; cited the exact Fletcher dispatch and indicated U.S. diplomatic interests and the nature of its English translation. The teaching questions, document-type explanation, and source-critical analysis are retained. It remains an **editorial paraphrase**, not an allegedly collated original-language quotation.

4. **Banco de México — §73.17:** Removed the claim that private banks continued issuing notes after the establishment of Banco de México. The 1925 bank held the exclusive legal power to issue banknotes, while other banks continued ordinary banking. The revised account distinguishes **monopoly over issue** from **limited early circulation of central-bank notes and institutional strength**. Source: Banco de México, *Historia del Banco de México*, volume I, https://www.banxico.org.mx/elib/hbm/1/intro1.html and related official bank history.

5. **Diario de los Debates — Source Window II:** Removed the claim that the congressional proceedings were first published only in the 1920s. Contemporary publication is cataloged in **1916**, alongside a **1922** edition and later edited volumes; identified the INEHRM/UNAM **volume II** reproduction for the 26–28 December 1916 Article 5 debates. Sources: https://books.google.com/books?id=U9IzAQAAMAAJ ; https://constitucion1917.gob.mx/recursos/Libros/Diariodelosdebatestomo2.pdf . Original dialogue has not been represented as a modern verbatim transcription.

6. **Hermila Galindo — Source Window VI:** Added the identified **8 December 1916** petition date, its appearance before the Congress **12 December**, and the January 1917 committee response; links to INEHRM proceedings and a scholarly study identifying *Mujer Moderna* (December 1916). Source: Ana Lau Jaiven and Roxana Rodríguez Bravo, *El sufragio femenino y la Constitución de 1917. Una revisión* (2017), https://www.scielo.org.mx/scielo.php?pid=S0188-77422017000200057&script=sci_arttext . The linked scholarly study is not mislabeled as an original-petition facsimile.

### 6. Historical and editorial safeguards retained

- Constitutional text is treated as **the original 1917 version**, distinct from later constitutional amendment or reinterpretation; the difference between the January 31 completion, February 5 promulgation, and May 1 entry into force is preserved.
- The exclusion of women from national suffrage and Hermila Galindo's contemporaneous advocacy are explained without projecting the 1953 constitutional change backward into 1917.
- The chapter differentiates **de facto U.S. recognition of Carranza in October 1915** from full diplomatic recognition in 1917, and treats the Bucareli understandings of 1923 without inventing a unitary formally ratified treaty.
- The PNR (1929), PRM (1938), and PRI (1946) remain separately identified, and the full Cárdenas reform program remains assigned to Chapter 74.
- Original source material is openly labeled as an **editorial paraphrase**, not invented verbatim testimony. The authorial standpoint of U.S. diplomatic material and documentary limitations on popular and female participation are disclosed.
- Estimates of the Cristero War toll, land distribution and local population changes remain qualified, rather than asserted as independently established census-precise numbers.
- The three map exercises are deliberately **text-only**; no historical map plates are embedded. Students need an external dated atlas or explicitly reconstructed historical map.

## Source and historical corrections

1. **Mudros:** identifies the four signatories, including Reşad Hikmet and Saadullah, and states the article numbers for the strategic-point occupation right (Article 7) and the six Armenian vilayets provision (Article 24). Original armistice text linked through German History in Documents and Images.
2. **Sèvres negotiation:** distinguishes Ahmed Tevfik Pasha's role in receiving the May draft from Damat Ferid Pasha's later role in presenting the Ottoman position; the document names three Ottoman plenipotentiaries at signature.
3. **Hijaz:** distinguishes the King of the Hijaz's listing in the Sèvres preamble from the absence of a named Hijazi plenipotentiary; does not present the Hijaz as an actual signatory. Article 98 recognition and the problem of ratification are separately handled.
4. **Armed establishment:** clarifies Articles 152–157: 35,000 gendarmerie plus 15,000 special reinforcement forces within a 50,000 ceiling, with an additional 700-person sultan's guard.
5. **Armenian and Kurdish provisions:** verifies Articles 62–64 and 88–90; adds the second, five-party frontier commission contemplated by Article 62; distinguishes conditional autonomy and potential independence from an implemented Kurdish state.
6. **Morocco/Tunisia/Libya:** differentiates the French protectorate recognition in Articles 118/120 from the renunciation of residual Ottoman rights in Libya in Article 121.
7. **Palestine:** replaces an unsupported approximate 1919 Jewish population share with explicitly dated British estimates for 1920 and the 1922 census (about 9.9% and 11.1% respectively); warns against back-projecting later census data.
8. **Territorial implementation:** clarifies that Sèvres, never having entered into force, did not itself implement its proposed renunciations or mandates.

## 3. Historical and bibliographic corrections completed

1. **Nizam al-Mulk's assassination:** replaced an unqualified claim about murder on October 14 near Nihavand with **October 1092 while traveling with the Seljuk court**; explicitly recognizes disagreement about location and agents. Specialist reference: Neguin Yavari, “Neẓām-al-Molk,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/nezam-al-molk/ .
2. **Iqtaʿ:** developed the distinction among fiscal entitlement, delegated governmental power, practical collection, and landownership; rejects simplistic equivalents of European feudalism. Research base: Ann K. S. Lambton, “Eqṭāʿ,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/eqta/ .
3. **Siyasatnama textual history:** describes the traditional 39-plus-11 chapter account but identifies modern challenges to a unitary authorial attribution; avoids presenting prescriptive narratives as official records. Research base: Neguin Yavari, “Siar al-Moluk,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/siar-al-moluk/ . Hubert Darke's revised 1978 second edition verified against library records.
4. **Madrasas:** explicitly states that Nizam al-Mulk expanded earlier traditions rather than inventing the madrasa; dates the Baghdad institution to **opening in 1067**, with the differentiated later teaching appointment of Abu Ishaq al-Shirazi. Research base: Wilferd Madelung, “Abū Esḥāq al-Šīrāzī,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/abu-eshaq-al-sirazi-ebrahim-b/ .
5. **Khargird Nizamiyya:** treats the surviving architectural fabric and incomplete inscription as material evidence, without claiming a preserved construction year or a completely reconstructable plan. Documentation: Archnet, https://www.archnet.org/sites/3867 .
6. **Baghdad disputes in the 1070s:** adds al-Shirazi, al-Qushayri, Hanbali opponents, and ministerial correspondence, while disclosing **differences among later accounts of the letters and ministerial interventions**. A single coherent or always-neutral policy is not inferred from disputed testimony.
7. **Religious classification:** identifies Ismailism as a Shiʿi branch and Sufism as overlapping devotional traditions rather than mutually exclusive populations; distinguishes jurisprudential, theological, and political identities.
8. **Al-Ghazali:** retains his 1091 appointment and 1095 departure, and adds the documented later return to Nizamiyya teaching; avoids treating the retrospective autobiography as a contemporary transcript of his motives. Sources: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/al-ghazali/ and Frank Griffel, *Al-Ghazali's Philosophical Theology* (2009), https://academic.oup.com/book/27461 .
9. **Bibliography:** verified edition/publisher details for Darke (1978), McCarthy (1980), Makdisi (1981), Peacock (2015), Safi (2006), and Griffel (2009); the source register distinguishes medieval texts, material evidence, and modern scholarship.
10. **Chapter boundaries:** retains Chapter 57's focus on conquest and political expansion, Chapter 59's focus on Anatolia, and Chapter 60's focus on the Crusades, while making Chapter 58 specifically about governance, fiscal systems, educational institutions, and religious authority.

## 4. Evidence studies and limits

Five pedagogical windows now address:

- **Nizam al-Mulk, Siyasatnama, iqtaʿ and the cultivator** — grounded thematic editorial paraphrase, not a reproduced Persian translation.
- **Khargird Nizamiyya** — architectural and epigraphic evidence; incomplete inscription and uncertain overall reconstruction.
- **Baghdad correspondence of 1077** — source dossier associated with Ibn al-Jawzi; differing interpretations of the events and letters explicitly preserved.
- **Al-Juwayni, Ghiyath al-umam** — thematic summary of the treatise on legitimate leadership, not an edition-specific Arabic quotation.
- **Al-Ghazali, al-Munqidh min al-dalal** — identified passage topic in McCarthy's translated edition; later self-presentation distinguished from contemporaneous observation.

**Documentary qualification:** The studies are identifiable scholarly paraphrases or source descriptions; they have **not** all been independently line-by-line collated against original-language manuscripts and facsimiles. The source register records this limitation. Geography studies are text-based atlas exercises and do not include embedded geographic plates. Approval is for the **scholarly textual HTML first edition**, not a facsimile-based critical edition or finished illustrated atlas edition.

## 4. Substantive historical revisions

1. **October 8, Sinai:** Corrected the account of Major General Avraham Adan's 162nd Armored Division to place its principal counterattack in the Egyptian Second Army sector. The expansion differentiates tactical coordination, command disputes, and Egyptian infantry-antitank organization.
2. **October 14, Sinai and Mansoura:** Retained the well-established defeat of the Egyptian ground offensive but stopped describing the disputed air battle around Mansoura as an unquestioned Israeli victory. Equipment-loss estimates are attributed and treated as approximate.
3. **October 15–24, Deversoir and Suez:** Expanded the Chinese Farm, the vulnerabilities of Israel's early bridgehead, the engineering and traffic problem, west-bank operations, resistance at Ismailia and Suez, and the difference between Third Army isolation and destruction.
4. **October 22–25, ceasefires:** Corrected the misleading preview that implied completion of the Third Army encirclement on October 22. The chapter now distinguishes the adoption of Resolution 338, subsequent military advances, the completion of isolation over October 23–24, and the later superpower crisis.
5. **October 24–25, Soviet-American confrontation:** Distinguished FRUS **Document 262** (earlier protest) from **Document 267** (later joint-force demand and unilateral-action warning). Incorporated **Documents 268, 269 and 274** and treated Nixon's absence from overnight deliberations cautiously rather than assuming an unproved explanation.
6. **Kosygin/satellite imagery:** Removed the unsupported claim that the Soviet prime minister personally showed Sadat definitive satellite photographs. Retained the documented diplomatic visit and clearly identified the limits of the available evidence.
7. **Human cost and air losses:** Removed unsupported cross-war civilian casualty comparisons. Separated estimated fatalities, prisoners, displacement and contested equipment losses and added social and family perspectives.
8. **Scholarship and evidence:** Expanded Syria and Jordan/Iraq involvement, military doctrine, logistics, military memory, and the 1974 disengagement processes. Added a traceable 1993 edition of Mohamed Abdel Ghani El-Gamasy's memoir to the bibliography and clarified the distinction between memoir and later military history.
9. **Pedagogy:** Fixed a timeline question that falsely implied diplomacy began only after the fighting ended.

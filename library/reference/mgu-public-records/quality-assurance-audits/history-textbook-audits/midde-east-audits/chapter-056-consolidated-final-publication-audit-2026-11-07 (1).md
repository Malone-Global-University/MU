## 3. Narrative scope and chapter-boundary check

- **Division I (§§56.1–56.4):** Frontiers defined through political institutions, mountain corridors, Armenian plurality, Georgian agency, and the concrete scope of Byzantine border policy. Distinguishes politically regulated borders from personal/commercial contact rather than treating all borders as fully fluid.
- **Division II (§§56.5–56.8):** Basil II and Tao; the constrained negotiated transfer of Vaspurakan; the competing claims and incorporation of Ani; provincial commands, limited administrative down-reach and continuity of Armenian elite participation. Differentiates sovereign claims, formal annexation, and effective administrative government.
- **Division III (§§56.9–56.12):** Kurdish-speaking communities and varied livelihood systems; Marwanid statecraft in Diyar Bakr; Shaddadid branches in Dvin and Ganja; Arab dynasties and the Kurdicized Rawwadids; local diplomatic agency, fiscal power, and political autonomy.
- **Division IV (§§56.13–56.16):** Urban markets and fortresses, ecclesiastical institutions across confessional lines, household and agrarian economies, women's social roles as evidence permits, the detailed Ani inscription, elite movement and Byzantine service, and the distinction between documented relocation and speculative mass migration.
- **Division V (§§56.17–56.20):** Distinguishes mobile bands, raiding, pastoral movement, and imperial campaigns. Analyzes Kapetron (1048), the failed 1054 attack on Manzikert, Ani (1064), the Kars settlement (1064/65), and the limits of simple inevitability narratives. The mature Seljuk Empire and Manzikert in 1071 remain respectively in Chapters 57 and 59.

The manuscript is an independent treatment of the eleventh-century northern frontier, not a repetition of Chapter 52's ninth- and tenth-century origins or Chapters 57–59's later imperial and Anatolian transformation.

## 4. Key accuracy findings and decisions

| Historical question | Audit conclusion |
|---|---|
| Armenia and Ani | The Bagratid kingdom of Ani is distinguished from the broader cultural/geographic and political plurality of Armenia. The conventional 1044–1045 incorporation is qualified. |
| Georgian consolidation and Tao | Bagrat III's consolidation (conventionally 1008), Tao c.1000, and the 1021–1022 Byzantine–Georgian conflict remain distinct developments. |
| Vaspurakan | The c.1021–1022 transfer is a negotiated settlement under constraints; the text does not assert that the entire local population migrated. |
| Byzantine administrative capacity | Formal annexation is separated from practical fiscal, military, and local administrative reach; some Armenian officeholding and cultural institutions continued. |
| Marwanids | Approximately 990–1085 and Nasr al-Dawla Ahmad c.1011–1061; statecraft, towns, diplomacy and social diversity recognized without idealizing prosperity. |
| Rawwadids | Corrected from undifferentiated 'Arab houses' to a dynasty of Arab origin with later Kurdicized branches, as documented by Andrew Peacock. |
| Shaddadids and Ani | The city's later Shaddadid government is recognized; timing and recipient of transfer after the 1064 conquest remain explicitly disputed. |
| Kars | Notes differing specialist dates, **1064 or 1065**, rather than treating 1065 as settled beyond dispute. |
| Ani inscription | Correctly connected with Bagrat Vxkacʿi and Constantine X Doukas, c.1060. Specific exemptions, provision of grain, cavalry expenses, and communal obligations are distinguished from a wholesale tax abolition. |
| Kapetron | 1048, participation and capture of Liparit IV; manuscript qualifies inconsistent accounts of the outcome and numerical claims. |
| Manzikert | 1054 attack is specifically distinguished from the **1071** battle; the latter is reserved for Chapter 59. |
| Ani in 1064 | Seljuk capture under Alp Arslan recorded with violence; unsupported precise mass-casualty claims are omitted. |
| Religion and ethnicity | Confessional institutions and dynastic associations described historically, without assuming modern national uniformity or inevitable military allegiances. |

**Verification basis:** Priority questions were checked against T. W. Greenwood's *Armenian Neighbours (600–1045)* (Cambridge, 2009); Greenwood's *Aristakēs Lastivertcʿi and Armenian Urban Consciousness* (Routledge, 2017); Andrew C. S. Peacock's *Encyclopaedia Iranica* entries for the Shaddadids and Rawwadids; AnnaLinden Weller on Grigor Magistros (2017); John Wortley's 2010 Skylitzes edition; and the 2026 Edinburgh collection on the Islamic–Byzantine frontier, particularly Carole Hillenbrand on the Marwanids. These are authoritative specialist sources. No claim is made to have collated every manuscript, date, or narrative assertion independently.

## 5. Source-window audit

1. **Aristakes Lastivertsi, *History*** — identified chapters concerning the Armenian kingdom and Constantine Monomachos; distinguishes contemporary concerns and later authorial moral interpretation. The paraphrase is attributed, not styled as an original quote.
2. **John Skylitzes, *Synopsis of Byzantine History*** — identified Byzantine account of Basil II's eastern settlements, and verified Wortley 2010 translation; acknowledges a retrospective compendium.
3. **Ibn al-Azraq al-Fariqi, *Taʾrikh Mayyafariqin wa Amid*** — regional Marwanid narrative and its later twelfth-century vantage; exact manuscript/page quotation deliberately not claimed.
4. **Grigor Magistros, Letter K71 to the Muslim emir Ibrahim** — an identifiable epistle, supported by Weller's modern analysis, used for learned cross-community exchange and not for unverifiable popular attitudes.
5. **Ani fiscal inscription, c.1060** — identifies the monument, magistrate, emperor, and critical edition: H. A. Orbeli, *Divan hay vimagrutʿyan*, vol. 1, no. 106, p. 37, plate XII. Explains how technical fiscal language and political function limit its interpretation.

Each window includes source identification, historical context, editorial-paraphrase label, interpretive limitations, and substantive student questions. They are positioned within the relevant historical sections rather than collected only after §56.20.

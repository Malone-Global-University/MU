## 3. Verified factual corrections and substantive additions

### 3.1 September 2025 Saudi–Pakistan agreement and August 2026 Makkah agreement

**Correction incorporated in §§162.13–162.14 and §162.19.** The September 17, 2025 **bilateral Strategic Mutual Defense Agreement** between Saudi Arabia and Pakistan is now distinguished from the **trilateral Makkah Agreement for Joint Defense** signed by Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, and Turkey on August 7, 2026. The latter's public joint statement describes a mutual-defense undertaking and defense cooperation, but does not itself demonstrate automatic military deployment or a completed multinational command.

The chapter adds the August 31 meeting of the trilateral Strategic Political and Defence Committee, Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan's statement that the agreement complemented NATO obligations, and the developing alliance institutions. Turkish statements about not targeting any particular country are identified as Turkish official positions, not adopted as uncontested geopolitical facts.

Verification:
- [Saudi Press Agency, September 17, 2025](https://www.spa.gov.sa/N2399714).
- [Joint statement on the Makkah summit, August 7, 2026](https://www.una-oic.org/en/ALL/2026/08/07/Joint-statement-of-the-Makkah-Summit-for-Defense/).

### 3.3 The Islamabad Memorandum: draft versus authenticated final agreement

**Correction incorporated in §162.12 and its Primary Source Window.** The available fourteen-paragraph English memorandum, dated June 17, 2026, is examined directly. Its published copy at the American Presidency Project is **explicitly labeled a draft**. The window now identifies the distinction between proposed and completed obligations. In particular, draft paragraphs 4–5 describe phased U.S. blockade removal and Iranian arrangements to restore commercial shipping, while other provisions address reconstruction, sanctions negotiations, enriched uranium and possible IAEA-supervised down-blending, compliance monitoring, and a prospective final agreement.

The chapter does **not** infer from the draft that proposed funding was delivered, sanctions were lifted, Iranian uranium was verified, or a final UN-endorsed settlement entered into force.

Verification: [The American Presidency Project, Islamabad Memorandum, published draft](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/islamabad-memorandum-understanding-between-the-united-states-america-and-the-islamic).

## 6. Remaining limitations and editorial risk treatment

1. **Ongoing-event limitation.** The chapter ends on November 23, 2026. The April ceasefire, June memorandum, July breakdown, and November blockade remain distinct phases. The chapter does not claim that a final peace settlement had been reached.
2. **Source-status limitation.** The June Islamabad text is marked as a published draft. The public November IAEA materials include statements about confidential or incompletely available reports; these are attributed accordingly.
3. **Casualty and attack attribution.** Reported casualty counts, missile counts, claims of military damage and reports about leadership injuries remain attributed or explicitly disputed. Source inclusion does not imply independent verification of every participant's assertion.
4. **Legal interpretation.** The contested snapback mechanism, legal justifications for force, navigation rights in the Strait of Hormuz, and claims of state sovereignty remain described as contested rather than settled judicial determinations.
5. **Future revision.** Subsequent revelations may change historical assessments of the 2026 war, Iranian nuclear inventories, command structures, and the ultimate consequences of the new regional defense agreements. Updating such events requires later sourced revisions; it is not a defect requiring indefinite delay of this cutoff-dated history chapter.

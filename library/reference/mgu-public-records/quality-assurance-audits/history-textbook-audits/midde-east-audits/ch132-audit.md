## Substantive revisions and verification

- Restored correct nationalization compensation language: the previous Paris Stock Exchange closing price, not the day of nationalization (Egyptian decree, Article I).
- Documented the secret October 1956 Sèvres arrangements using *Foreign Relations of the United States* (FRUS), vol. XVI, document 373, distinguishing written evidence from conflicting memoir accounts.
- Clarified that 1888 navigation rights, company ownership, and legal permission to use military force were different matters.
- Expanded Israeli-Egyptian border tensions, Israeli military strategy, Port Said operations, and the different war aims of Britain, France, and Israel.
- Included the UNRWA-reported lists of 275 people allegedly killed at Khan Yunis and 111 at Rafah with the report's explicit limitations; did not claim final verified national totals.
- Added documentation and scholarly discussion of coercive measures and departures affecting Egyptian Jews and foreign residents in 1956, distinguishing nationality and religion.
- Separated General Assembly Resolutions 997, 998, 1000, and 1001; distinguished armed peacekeeping (UNEF I) from technical canal clearance (UNSCO).
- Distinguished British sterling pressures, U.S. financial leverage, Soviet warnings, and UN diplomacy; resisted single-factor explanations.
- Clarified separate British/French and Israeli withdrawals and the canal's resumption of normal traffic in April 1957.
- Preserved the central interpretation: military operations, political legitimacy, and humanitarian experience must be evaluated separately.

## 2. Substantive corrections applied and verified against scholarly literature

1. **Shirvan (§76.7):** Replaced the false 1500 capture of Gulistan with the continued resistance and redirected campaign, and the false 1510 annexation with dependent Shirvanshah rule until the direct Safavid governorship under Tahmasp I in **1538**. Distinguished battlefield success, suzerainty, and provincial incorporation. Basis: C. E. Bosworth, *ŠERVĀNŠAHS*, *Encyclopaedia Iranica*; the campaign narrative is further supported by published chronicle-based studies.
2. **Aq Qoyunlu genealogy (§76.9):** Corrected Murad from Alvand's brother to his **cousin**, son of Yaqub. Basis: *Aq Qoyunlu*, *Encyclopaedia Iranica*.
3. **Ismail's succession (§76.5):** Restored his surviving brother Ibrahim and distinguished competing accounts of the succession and transfer of spiritual/military leadership. Basis: Roger M. Savory, *Esmāʿīl I Ṣafawī: Biography*, *Encyclopaedia Iranica*.
4. **Uzun Hasan's chronology (§76.1):** Differentiated the Qara Qoyunlu victories of 1467–68 from the defeat of Timurid Abu Sa'id in **1469**. Basis: UNESCO *History of Civilizations of Central Asia* and specialist Aq Qoyunlu studies.
5. **Ottoman period cross-reference (§76.6):** Redirected discussion of central government to Chapter 72 (rather than misusing Chapter 75's social-order scope). Recruitment motivations now include religious networks, personal allegiance, regional circumstances, and incentives, not only opposition to centralization.
6. **Eastern frontier (§76.12):** Developed the Merv victory in 1510 and Ghazdewan defeat in 1512, including chroniclers' accounts of friction between Najm-e Thani and Qizilbash amirs. Avoided presenting exact desertion scenarios as fully proven.
7. **Chaldiran captives (§76.18):** Replaced the categorical capture of Ismail's wife with a qualified historiographical account distinguishing Tajlu and Behruza Khanum and differing Ottoman and Safavid source traditions. Basis: Savory and Gündüz.
8. **Bihzad and royal manuscripts (§76.19):** Stated uncertainty surrounding the purported 1522 appointment and noted the chronological discrepancy in the decree. Distinguished this from the separately disputed early-1520s Shahnama commission. Basis: Priscilla P. Soucek; Metropolitan Museum of Art.
9. **Ismail's death (§76.20):** Distinguished death on 23 May 1524 in northwestern Iran, with disputed precise location near the Tabriz–Ardabil route, from burial at the Safavid shrine in Ardabil. Basis: Deniz (2023), assessed against earlier chronicles.

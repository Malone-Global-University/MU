## Factual evidence reviewed

1. **Judeo-Persian dates**. Dandan-Uiliq letter: second half of eighth century, roughly 760, with disputed early claims; Tang-i Azao inscriptions: approximately 752–753. The completed manuscript retained the earlier source-critical correction. Sources: *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Judeo-Persian Communities viii. Judeo-Persian Language,” https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/judeo-persian-viii-judeo-persian-language/ ; “Persian Language i. Early New Persian,” https://doi.org/10.1163/2330-4804_EIRO_COM_11377 .
2. **Sinai Arabic 151**: dated colophon 867; contains Pauline Epistles, Acts, Catholic Epistles. Source: Vevian Zaki, “A Dynamic History: MS Sinai, Arabic 151 in the Hands of Scribes, Readers, and Restorers,” *Journal of Islamic Manuscripts* 11 (2020), 200–259, https://doi.org/10.1163/1878464X-01102004 .
3. **Egyptian settlement**: Qays groups settled in the eastern Hawf around AH 109/727–728 on the initiative of ʿUbayd Allāh ibn al-Ḥabḥāb. Source: translated al-Maqrīzī / al-Kindī material, https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/237608/1/MaqriziKhitatPart1.pdf .
4. **Monastic fiscal registration**: later accounts attribute a census and poll tax on Egyptian monks to al-Aṣbagh around 694; literary transmission is distinguished from documentary evidence. Research corroborates circulation of the tradition; documentary verification of each individual monk's payment is not claimed.
5. **Khurāsān Arabicization**: traditional date around AH 124 (741–742), distinct from earlier Syrian, Iraqi and Egyptian government language reforms; described as transmitted administrative chronology rather than a general language shift. Source: Longworth study, https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/record/4782/files/Longworth_uchicago_0330D_16459.pdf .
6. **Umm al-Raṣāṣ**: Saint Stephen's presbytery mosaic dated 756, major mosaic completion dated 785, both Abbasid-era. Site testifies to longer Christian continuity spanning Umayyad and Abbasid periods. Sources: UNESCO, https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1093 ; Umm ar-Rasas heritage documentation https://whc.unesco.org/document/167063 .
7. **Conversion scholarship**: Richard Bulliet, *Conversion to Islam in the Medieval Period* (1979) proposes model curves from biographical genealogies, not population censuses; Bulliet's “The Conversion Curve Revisited” (2017) revisits his work. Elton Daniel's *The Political and Social History of Khurasan under Abbasid Rule, 747–820* studies the post-747 region; Chase F. Robinson's *Empire and Elites after the Muslim Conquest* focuses on Mosul, Jazira and local elites; Petra Sijpesteijn's *Shaping a Muslim State* (2013) studies mid-eighth-century Egyptian papyri. Their studies are not direct comparative demographic tests of Bulliet's curves. Sources: https://www.fulcrum.org/concern/monographs/hq37vn72q ; https://doi.org/10.3366/edinburgh/9781474417129.003.0004 ; https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/empire-and-elites-after-the-muslim-conquest/4E4E4EC8A62DDE972EAB1C0D670BA309 ; https://academic.oup.com/book/8441 .

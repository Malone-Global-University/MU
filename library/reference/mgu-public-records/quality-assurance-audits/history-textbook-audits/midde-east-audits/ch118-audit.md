## Required historical and editorial corrections

1. **Ma'an versus Amman:** Abdullah arrived at Ma'an in November 1920, then moved to Amman in March 1921. The latter became the emirate's political center. The opening banner and §118.2 no longer imply Ma'an was the capital.
2. **Transjordanian stages:** separate government and Article 25 memorandum are distinguished from the 20 February 1928 agreement, its subsequent ratification, and the 22 March 1946 independence treaty and 25 May monarchy. The League memorandum and 1946 treaty are linked in the source register.
3. **Fujairah and Kalba:** the chapter explicitly distinguishes British recognition of Kalba in 1936 from recognition of Fujairah in 1952. It avoids projecting the present-day seven-emirate UAE federation unchanged onto the interwar Trucial Coast.
4. **Aden:** direct Crown Colony status on 1 April 1937 is distinguished from surrounding treaty-linked southern Arabian protectorates and the Yemeni imamate.
5. **Saudi petroleum corporate chronology:** the 1933 Standard Oil of California (SOCAL) concession and California Arabian Standard Oil Company (CASOC) are distinguished from the 1944 name Arabian American Oil Company (Aramco); commercial discovery at Dammam No. 7 was in March 1938 and first tanker exports in May 1939.
6. **Kuwait petroleum:** the 1934 Kuwait Oil Company/concession, commercial Burgan discovery in 1938, and June 1946 first exports are now treated fully. They are not collapsed into a single 'oil discovery' event.
7. **Bahrain and Qatar:** historical petroleum sequence is differentiated: Bahrain principal 1925 concession, 1932 discovery, 1934 crude export; Qatar principal 1935 concession, 1939 discovery, 1949 first export.
8. **Yemeni agreements:** Treaty of Taif concluded May 20, 1934, is treated as a significant settlement but not as automatic resolution of every later frontier question; the February 11, 1934 Anglo-Yemeni Treaty of Sana'a is distinguished from it.
9. **Saudi 1933 concession document dating:** common corporate histories date signature to May 29, 1933. A British India Office archival copy discussed in the source register refers to a May 27 text. That documentary variant is explicitly disclosed rather than silently harmonized; the precise original text should be collated if a verbatim diplomatic edition is prepared.
10. **Legal versus effective sovereignty:** the treatment distinguishes mandate administration, direct colonial government, treaty-linked protection, recognized independence, dynastic power, and local political autonomy.

## Quantitative exercise provenance

The four-row comparative table uses historical calendar years, not invented revenue or output figures. Source bases are BAPCO (Bahrain), Aramco/SOCAL historical records (Saudi Arabia), Kuwait Oil Company (Kuwait), and Qatar Digital Library/QatarEnergy (Qatar). Differences between years are arithmetic intervals, not modeled oil production. Concession dates mean the principal formal concessions associated with the later fields; earlier negotiations or preliminary rights may exist. The exercise explicitly differentiates concession, discovery, and first export.

## Documentary source policy and limitations

Each of the **12 Primary Source Windows** is a developed editorial analysis with issuing authority, date, source context, a clearly labeled **nonverbatim** paraphrase or documentary study, explicit evidentiary limits, a direct archival/official or historical source link, and three questions. Certain windows point to an original printed treaty or an archival file; others point to a modern finding aid or institutional account of a historical document. Such sources are transparently identified. These are **not certified, manuscript-collated transcriptions or facsimiles** of all twelve documents. Do not present their editorial summaries as original treaty quotations.

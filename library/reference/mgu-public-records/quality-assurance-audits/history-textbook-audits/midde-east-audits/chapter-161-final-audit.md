## 3. Accuracy corrections incorporated

1. **November 2026 Gaza casualty data:** Replaced the earlier July statement of more than 73,000 deaths with the November 18, 2026 OCHA *reported-impact snapshot*: 74,710 Palestinian deaths and 176,131 injuries as reported by Gaza's Ministry of Health. The manuscript explains that those are source-attributed totals, not individually verified UN fatality counts; it also distinguishes total counts, identified deaths, direct/indirect mortality, and the date of reporting. OCHA reported 1,650 deaths and 5,975 injuries since the October 2025 ceasefire as of its November 18 situation report.
2. **Reconstruction financing:** Corrected the misleading comparison of $7 billion in pledges with approximately $70 billion in need. The revised text distinguishes the approximately $7 billion from other participants, a separate U.S. pledge, a later total of approximately $17 billion in commitments, roughly $280 million deployed to preparation by November, and the $71.4 billion multi-year damage-and-needs estimate. Pledges, appropriations, disbursements, and completed construction are not interchangeable.
3. **International Criminal Court chronology:** Identified Mohammed Deif and the termination of proceedings against him on February 26, 2025; distinguished this from the pending Israeli-official warrants and included the April 24, 2025 appeals judgment requiring renewed consideration of an Israeli jurisdictional challenge. No warrant is equated with a criminal conviction.
4. **International Court of Justice and investigative findings:** Distinguished ICJ provisional measures in South Africa's Genocide Convention case, the July 2024 advisory opinion about continued presence in occupied territory, and the UN Commission of Inquiry's September 2025 genocide determination and its 2026 follow-up findings. Israel's rejection of the Commission's finding is identified; the chapter does not misstate the finding as an ICJ merits judgment.
5. **Israeli elections:** Added the October 27, 2026 Knesset election, Likud's 27 seats and the absence of a clear majority for either principal bloc by the cutoff, without inventing a resulting government.
6. **Palestinian election chronology:** Replaced the vague September 2027 reference with the September 11, 2027 election date announced after Abbas's October 2026 postponement; distinguish the decree from subsequent implementation.
7. **Ceasefire and disarmament:** Preserved the October 2025 ceasefire and the January 26, 2026 completion of Israeli hostage returns; distinguished July 2026 political agreement to a roadmap from verified disarmament or actual transfer of authority. The November 17 Board of Peace report is described as an interested institutional primary source that also identified nondeployment, stalled administration, continuing incidents, and unmet conditions.
8. **West Bank developments:** Strengthened the treatment of Area C, E1, East Jerusalem, Israeli settlement policy, settler violence, military operations, PA institutions, and the September 2026 UN assessment, including the legal difference between domestic planning decisions and international obligations.

## 4. Balance and historical method

The revised chapter does not equate Israeli civilians with the Israeli state or Palestinians with Hamas. It records the intentional killing of civilians and hostage-taking on October 7, substantial documented Palestinian civilian casualties and displacement, serious allegations and findings regarding armed conflict, and differing political views within each society. It explains civilian harm, military necessity claims, occupation, international humanitarian law, and institutional accountability without justifying violations by reference to prior grievances.

Terminology is differentiated: Hamas, Fatah, the PLO and PA are distinct; ICC warrants, ICJ orders, ICJ advisory opinions and UN investigative findings are not used as equivalents; ceasefire, withdrawal, demilitarization, return of hostages and political settlement are distinguished. Sources presenting interested institutional claims are identified rather than adopted uncritically.

## Historical and source-integrity work
1. Replaced the compressed summaries with substantive, original historical explanation across the entire 48-section outline; distinguished transmitted events, legal ideals, documentary records, and historical inference.
2. Corrected the overly rigid definition of **kharāj/jizya**. Research on early Abbasid Egyptian papyri indicates that **kharāj** could describe money-assessed taxation more broadly than a technical land tax; juristic categories and documentary usage must be interpreted separately.
3. Qualified claims that military stipends always represented the largest state expense or Iraq always delivered the largest collected surplus. Distinguished assessments, payments, local disbursements, and central remittances.
4. Reframed the change from the Baghdad **abnāʾ** to new eastern and Central Asian military units as a political and patronage transformation, not a proven uniform decline in older soldiers’ ability; distinguished free regional warriors from enslaved dependents.
5. Replaced the categorical claim that all Abbasid coins named the caliph: al-Mahdī’s name appears on many dirhams after 775, but naming conventions and anonymous issues varied by date and coin type.
6. Identified the **Chronicle of Zuqnin**, completed about 775–776, as primarily comparative evidence for taxation under al-Manṣūr rather than direct testimony about Hārūn’s reign.
7. Historical source windows now identify the work and transmission limits while labeling exposition as a source-based synthesis; no fabricated quotations, page numbers, or quantitative reconstructions are supplied.
8. Added a bibliography/provenance register with **16** identified sources and contextual research links. Several references are edition/catalog-level and are not represented as passage-level checked texts.

## 4. Historical and analytical accuracy check

**Fiscal distinctions.** The 1695 *malikâne* was a lifetime tax-farming entitlement with an initial *muaccele* and continuing *mal* payments, not the sale of the underlying *miri* land. This was independently checked against Mehmet Genç, “Mâlikâne,” *TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi* (2003), https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/malikane and the fiscal analysis of Karaman and Pamuk, *Journal of Economic History* 70/3 (2010), 593–629, https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022050710000550.

**Essential *esham* correction.** Original 1775 *esham* income shares were formal lifetime annuity-like rights to income from particular *mukataa* revenues, not simply hereditary private ownership or a share of a province. Transfer and heirs' later claims are treated separately from the founding legal design. See Mehmet Genç, “Esham,” *TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi* (1995), https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/esham and Karaman/Pamuk (2010).

**Provincial notables.** The manuscript consistently distinguishes *ayan*, tax farmer, governor, military commander, district agent and regional dynast; it does not describe a uniform empire-wide election. Specific evidence for an imperial order **dated 24 August 1784**, referencing **1779** prescriptions, was checked against Nuri Çevikel, “Ayâns in the Ottoman Cyprus in the Second Half of the 18th Century,” *Belleten* 72/264 (2008): 567–590, https://doi.org/10.37879/belleten.2008.567. Modern synthesis: Ali Yaycıoğlu, *Partners of the Empire* (2016), chapter 2, https://doi.org/10.11126/stanford/9780804796125.003.0003.

**Regional cases.** The Karaosmanoğlu of Manisa and early family member Kara Osman Ağa (d. **1706**) are independently anchored in Yuzo Nagata, “Karaosmanoğulları,” *TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi* (2001), https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/karaosmanogullari. The differing scales of notable households are grounded in Ertaş and Kılıçaslan, *Tarih Dergisi* 83 (2024): 21–66, https://doi.org/10.26650/iutd.20241456318, and the work of Anastasopoulos (ed.), *Provincial Elites in the Ottoman Empire* (Crete University Press, 2006). Çapanoğlu, Caniklizade, Pazvandoğlu, and Ali Pasha of Tepelena are discussed without invented revenue totals or precise individual military headcounts.

**War and reforms.** The wars of **1768–1774** and **1787–1792** are kept distinct; Austrian participation in the latter context is specified as **1788–1791**. Selim III's accession (**1789**), the more focused postwar New Order from **1792**, and special *İrad-ı Cedid* treasury (**1793**) are distinguished. See Kemal Beydilli, “Nizâm-ı Cedîd,” *TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi* (2007), https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/nizam-i-cedid. Military matters are grounded in the published scholarship of Virginia Aksan rather than invented archival requisitions. The **1808 *Sened-i İttifak*** is marked outside the main chronology, *not* described as a modern constitution.

**Historiographical framing.** The prose rejects unqualified 'decline', but does not replace it with an equally simplistic progress or resilience thesis. It addresses violence, taxpayer burdens, uneven service delivery, factional conflict, and exclusion. Imperial instructions are differentiated from local enforcement; regional variation and actual social intermediaries receive sustained attention. It preserves boundaries with Chapters 87–90 and the later Mahmud II reforms of Volume VI.

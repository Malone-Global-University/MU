## Historical-critical corrections and scope

1. Corrected **11 June 1908** attempted assassination of Ömer Nazım Bey (survived) and distinguished it from **7 July 1908** assassination of Şemsi Pasha (killed). These were wrongly conflated in the original abbreviated draft.
2. Preserved Niyazi Bey's **3 July 1908** mobilization and distinguished Macedonian proclamation on **23 July** from Istanbul publicity of the sultan's restoration on **24 July**.
3. Preserved the parliamentary opening **17 December 1908**, while explaining indirect elections, limited franchise, ethnic classification problems, and the CUP's influential but not formally comprehensive control of cabinet administration.
4. Explained **31 March 1325 Rumi = 13 April 1909 Gregorian**, avoiding the appearance of two separate rebellions.
5. Distinguished initial Action Army organization involving Hüseyin Hüsnü Pasha from Mahmud Şevket Pasha's major operational leadership; retained **27 April 1909** deposition.
6. Expanded the Adana massacres into a documented two-wave crisis **14–16 and around 25 April 1909**, with differentiated responsibility, imprecise casualty estimates, and attention to Armenian civilian victims, relief, and inquiries.
7. Explained August **1909** constitutional amendments without claiming all parliamentary supremacy was effective in practice. The TBMM institutional history lists adoption on August 8, while the State Department's translated constitutional document identifies the revision by an August 5/18 date; these distinct dating conventions are disclosed in the source window.
8. Restricted primary narrative to the revolution and the unsettled constitutional order by **1909**. Later CUP consolidation, Balkan Wars, and World War I remain in later assigned chapters.

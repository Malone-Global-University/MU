## 3. Historical scope and plan compliance

All approved section assignments are preserved in order:

- **Division I (§§51.1–51.4):** inherited Afro-Eurasian connections; geographic constraints; commercial partnerships and credit; markets, customs, and state authority. Contract terminology is distinguished from anachronistic modern banking.
- **Division II (§§51.5–51.8):** eastern Mediterranean; Fustat and Nile trade; Red Sea shipping and coastal communities; Hijaz pilgrimage and material provisioning. The earlier system is separated from later Fatimid-era documentation.
- **Division III (§§51.9–51.12):** Basra and al-Ubulla; Siraf and Omani connections; monsoon navigation and shipbuilding; India, Southeast Asia, and China. This division integrates archaeology and route-specific technological limitations.
- **Division IV (§§51.13–51.16):** East African participation, Central Asian caravan routes, commodities and production, enslavement and forced movement. African societies are treated as participants in their own right; enslaved people are not reduced to mere inventory.
- **Division V (§§51.17–51.20):** diverse merchant communities, historiography and source criticism, decentralized political conditions, and the final historical synthesis.

Chapter boundaries are maintained: Chapter 45 covers general Abbasid fiscal institutions; Chapter 48 examines slavery and social life in greater detail; Chapter 49 covers the Persianate dynasties; Chapter 50 supplies political fragmentation; Chapter 52 addresses northern frontiers. The chapter ends before the principal period of the later Geniza India-trade archive and the later Fatimid/Ayyubid commercial world.

### Disclosed edition limitations

- Source-window paraphrases are **not** original-manuscript-collated quotations or critical text editions.
- No embedded cartographic plates are supplied; the three geography exercises require an external map/atlas.
- The historical text is an educational synthesis based on verified publications and source registers, not a line-by-line documentary apparatus for every narrative sentence.
- Some early maritime evidence is incomplete or contested, and later Geniza records are deliberately used as a **later comparison**, not direct Abbasid-era transaction records.

**Editorial decision:** No additional general expansion or micro-revision cycle is required for this edition. Any future work should be treated as a separately scoped documentary or illustrated edition, not a blocker for the present publication lock.

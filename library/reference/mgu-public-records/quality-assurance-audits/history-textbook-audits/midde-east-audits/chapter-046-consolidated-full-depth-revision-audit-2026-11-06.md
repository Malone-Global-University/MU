## Core historical improvements & verified corrections
1. **Bayt al-Ḥikma:** distinguished an attested court library from the modern claim of a unified academy directing all Greek-Arabic translation; see Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Greek Sources in Arabic and Islamic Philosophy*.
2. **Papermaking:** avoided the inaccurate Talas-prisoner origin account and qualified blanket claims about the price of paper relative to papyrus or parchment; see Encyclopaedia Iranica, *Paper in the Iranian World Prior to Printing*.
3. **Ḥunayn:** added a traceable translation case: the <em>Risāla</em> report on Galen’s *On Sects*, Arabic ed. Bergsträßer 4.19–5.9, no. 3, in the accessible *Why Translate Science?* source presentation. Emphasized that a translator’s own report is evidence with bias, not a transcript of all ninth-century practice.
4. **Mathematics:** demonstrated the quadratic \(x^2 + 10x=39\), deriving positive root 3 from completing the square; discussed \(x^2+21=10x\) with the two positive solutions 3 and 7; both methods checked against mathematics-history explanations by MacTutor and the Mathematical Association of America. Arabic rhetorical form distinguished from modern notation.
5. **Medical history:** examined the layered Galenic tradition, al-Rāzī’s differential accounts of illnesses, historical medical uncertainty, and the changing institutional history of bīmāristāns; avoided retrospective claims of modern licensing and clinical trials.
6. **Philosophical texts:** qualified the authorship of *Theology of Aristotle* as Neoplatonic material adapted within the Arabic tradition; strengthened treatments of al-Kindī, al-Fārābī, and Ibn Sīnā with arguments rather than name lists.
7. **Alchemy:** corrected a categorical statement that the Jābirian corpus was certainly assembled by multiple ninth/tenth-century authors. The historiography is contested: see *Isis*, “Byzantine Engagement with Islamicate Alchemy” (2022).
8. **Dates:** rendered al-Bīrūnī’s death as disputed around 1048–1050 rather than unconditionally after 1048; al-Fārābī’s circa 950/951 death noted in reference material.
9. **Material science:** supplied concrete examples from the Banū Mūsā’s mechanical devices and Ibn al-Haytham’s optical investigations, distinguishing designs, reconstructed mechanisms, and repeatable observations.
10. **Regional networks:** expanded documented activity in Harrān, Rayy, Khwārazm, Egypt, Córdoba, and manuscript cross-community exchange, without equating common scholarly language with Abbasid political control.

## 3. Historical accuracy and editorial decisions

### Corrections directly made

1. **Nur al-Din's hospital (§61.8):** replaced the vague “mid-1150s onward” foundation date with **1154 CE**. Museum With No Frontiers dates the monument 549 AH / 1154 CE; Archnet lists 1154 with a neighboring Hijri conversion. The Common Era year is therefore preserved without asserting an exact Islamic-calendar conversion beyond the source evidence. Later building phases are distinguished from the initial foundation.
2. **Edessa (§61.5):** distinguished the capture of 1144 from renewed violence and military change in **1146**, and explained why claims about breach, crowd casualties, killings, and enslavement must not be combined into an invented total.
3. **Saladin's Jerusalem surrender (§61.15):** included the **reported** ransom scale of ten dinars for an adult man, five for a woman, and two for a child; explicitly described these as chroniclers' figures, not complete audited disbursement totals. The consequences of ransom inequality, enslavement, negotiated release and communal charity are preserved.
4. **Aleppo–Sinjar (§61.13):** situated the acquisition of Aleppo in 1183 within the preceding Zengid succession and reciprocal negotiations rather than treating it as a simple conquest; the **1186 Mosul settlement** remains a suzerainty agreement, not territorial annexation.
5. **Maimonides (§61.18):** identified al-Qadi al-Fadil as Saladin's chief secretary/adviser rather than simply calling him his vizier. Noted a letter of around 1191 as evidence of Maimonides' medical reputation among Egyptian elites, and avoided assigning an unquestioned appointment date or formal communal office.
6. **Pisa and trade (§61.18):** retained the documented **1173** commercial agreement while emphasizing the distinction between pragmatic market relationships and absence of military conflict.
7. **Ibn Jubayr and pilgrimage (§61.18):** identified the specific exaction associated with the Red Sea port of **Aydhab** and a replacement arrangement rather than broadly claiming that Saladin eliminated all pilgrim levies.
8. **Ayyubid administration (§§61.9, 61.11, 61.17, 61.19):** strengthened treatment of fiscal-office continuity, military revenue assignments, regional princely autonomy, and the administrative significance of dynastic succession.

### Major claims retained after checking

- Zengi's bases at Mosul (1127) and Aleppo (1128), and Edessa's capture in December 1144.
- Nur al-Din's control of Damascus in 1154 and the relationship between religious patronage and political power.
- Shirkuh's short vizierate before Saladin's appointment in March 1169.
- The ordering in 1171: Abbasid caliphal recognition in Egypt's sermon followed by the death of al-Adid.
- Saladin's entry into Damascus after Nur al-Din's death in 1174; separate acquisition of Aleppo in 1183 and limited suzerainty at Mosul in 1186.
- Hattin on 4 July 1187 and negotiated surrender of Jerusalem on 2 October 1187, with civilian impacts not reduced to a tale of universal mercy.
- Third Crusade settlement in September 1192, Saladin's death on 4 March 1193, and al-Adil's Egyptian accession in 1200.

### Source criticism applied

The historical narrative distinguishes religious legitimacy from exclusively religious motives, imperial language from direct territorial control, and retrospective praise of Saladin and Nur al-Din from verifiable administrative and military actions. Citations to translated chronicles must be checked in their specified editions before representing any lines as verbatim source quotations. The finished source-study boxes correctly present **labeled editorial paraphrases**, not pretended original translations or page-verified transcript excerpts.

## 6. Limitations and disposition

1. The five source-study passages are attributed **editorial paraphrases of identified episodes**, not verbatim, original-language manuscript-collated translations. A facsimile/critical-edition project would require additional primary collation and exact edition-specific page checks.
2. Three geography studies are **text-only atlas exercises**; no dated historical map plates have been embedded or rights-cleared.
3. Historical estimates of Edessa casualties and 1187 Jerusalem captivity/ransom totals remain disputed; the chapter gives attributed arrangements and qualitative consequences rather than unsupported totals.
4. Some reference works in the source register are later synthetic or selective editions; bibliographic identification is not a claim of inspection of every original-language manuscript.

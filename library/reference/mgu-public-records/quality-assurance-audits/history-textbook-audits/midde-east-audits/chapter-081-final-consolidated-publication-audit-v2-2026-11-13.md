## 1. What was revised in this final pass

The final file is a direct revision of the earlier reviewed and approved HTML, not a new draft. Eleven targeted changes were made:

1. **§81.7:** Removed the unsupported absolute contrast between wasteful commercial hunting and Indigenous hunters supposedly always using every part of an animal. The revised prose explains differences of markets, purposes, practices, and scale without romantic generalization.
2. **§81.10:** Specified that 1874 H.R. 921 was directed at **non-Indigenous hunters** in U.S. territories; it prohibited killing female bison and restricted male killing beyond food/use and market provisions. It did **not** prohibit Indigenous hunting or all commercial uses. Congressional Record, 43rd Congress, 1st session, March 10, 1874, pp. 2105–2109.
3. **§81.10, source window:** Aligned the H.R. 921 editorial paraphrase with the bill's provisions, Indigenous exception, and pocket-veto outcome. No exact statutory quotations were fabricated.
4. **§81.18:** Replaced the loosely stated Hornaday count with its actual components: **1,091 in 1889 = 635 wild/unprotected (including 550 Athabasca estimate), 200 under Yellowstone government protection, and 256 captive**. It warns that this is not a modern census or an exact count of surviving plains bison.
5. **§81.19:** Qualified the familiar story that Samuel Walking Coyote personally captured the calves from which the Pablo–Allard herd grew. Parks Canada describes additional Indigenous oral-history accounts crediting **Atatitsa (Peregrine Falcon Robe)** and **Latatitsa (Little Peregrine Falcon Robe)**. The chapter now preserves the competing attributions.
6. **§81.19:** Corrected the 1908 National Bison Range land description: the range was established **inside the Flathead Indian Reservation**, using land taken for conservation without the Tribes' consent, not merely on land that had once belonged to the reservation.
7. **§81.19:** Added a restrained forward reference to the **2021 transfer into federal trust for the Confederated Salish and Kootenai Tribes**, with the full modern restoration story reserved for Chapter 135.
8. **§81.8 documentary photograph:** Described the creator as **not identified in the cited online National Archives listing**, avoiding an unsupported attribution while retaining verified ID **520093**.
9. **Source Register:** Added **Congressional Record** page range for H.R. 921 and the actual legislative provisions.
10. **Source Register:** Added targeted Parks Canada, National Park Service, and U.S. Department of the Interior citations for the bison-rescue oral traditions, Yellowstone poaching response, and Bison Range land history.
11. **Source Register:** Added the published scholarly exchange between David D. Smits and William A. Dobak (1995), rather than leaving the frontier-army historiography at a single prominent article.

## 5. Publication limits and control

1. The five documentary windows include labeled editorial paraphrases/descriptions. They are not asserted to be facsimile-collated direct quotations. Exact quotations for future scholarly editions require collation with original pages and catalog items.
2. Verification was **targeted**, including high-risk claims and source identities, not a comprehensive line-by-line audit of every historical claim and citation against all monographs and archives.
3. No original Chapter 1 HTML source was available for byte-for-byte CSS/JS template comparison.
4. Geography exercises are text-guided. Historical map plates would require separate licensed assets or a companion atlas.
5. Local Chromium rendering was tested, but **live-site deployment, navigation in the MGU website, and network availability of external links were not tested end-to-end**.

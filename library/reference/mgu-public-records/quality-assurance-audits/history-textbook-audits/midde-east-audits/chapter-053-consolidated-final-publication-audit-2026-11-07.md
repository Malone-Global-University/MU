## 3. Historical compliance and coverage

The authoritative plan's exactly-20-section / five-division requirement is met:

1. **Division I, §§53.1–53.4:** western Iran and Baghdad before 945; the three sons of Buya; Baghdad settlement; amir al-umara, titles, investiture, sermon, and coinage.
2. **Division II, §§53.5–53.8:** the position of Abbasid caliphs under Buyid supervision; the institutional role of ceremonial; viziers and provincial administration; variation in the reigns of al-Muti, al-Ta'i, and al-Qadir.
3. **Division III, §§53.9–53.12:** Daylamite and Turkic armies, military payment, iqta/revenue assignments, Baghdad's urban populations, provincial geography and regional economy.
4. **Division IV, §§53.13–53.16:** Shiʿi Buyid government alongside the Sunni Abbasid caliphate; Ashura and Ghadir Khumm; scholars, religious institutions, and shrines; Persianate symbolism and multilingual intellectual patronage.
5. **Division V, §§53.17–53.20:** Adud al-Dawla's consolidation; rivalry after 983; al-Qadir and al-Qa'im; Seljuk entry into Baghdad in 1055; continuation in Fars until 1062; synthesis and transition to the Fatimids.

The chapter differentiates the Buyid system from the prior political fragmentation examined in Chapter 50, the commercial framework of Chapter 51, and the Fatimid, Ghaznavid, and later Seljuk subjects of subsequent Volume IV chapters. The heading "Post-Abbasid" is expressly defined as a post-centralized-Abbasid political order, **not** the disappearance of the Abbasid caliphate.

## 4. Historical corrections and qualification log

### Verified corrections made in the finished edition

- **Rukn al-Dawla's death:** Corrected from 977 in Claude's combined death entry to **16 September 976**. The date is supported by the specialist Encyclopaedia Iranica biography of Adud al-Dawla: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/azod-al-dawla-abu-soja/ .
- **Chronology order:** Split and reordered the deaths of Muizz al-Dawla (967) and Rukn al-Dawla (976), with al-Ta'i's accession in 974 appropriately between them. The preview now lists 16 distinct dated events.
- **Adud al-Dawla's entry and consolidation:** Distinguishes campaigns and entry in **977** from consolidation of effective control over Iraq in **978**; his death in **983** remains the end of this exceptional interval. See the same Iranica study and the family chronology in https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/buyids/ .
- **Baghdad/Buyid end dates:** Maintains December **1055** as the termination of Buyid political control of Baghdad, while identifying the continuing Fars branch until **1062**. See https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/buyids/ .
- **352 AH / 963 CE public rites:** Ashura and Ghadir Khumm are distinguished as separate religious occasions; public policy and later chronicle evidence are discussed without depicting them as identical observances. See https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/gadir-komm/gadir-komm-ii-gadir-festival-id-e-gadir/ and https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/iraq-x-shiites-of-iraq/ .
- **Titles and caliphal recognition:** Corrected categorical suggestions that the caliph possessed exclusive and uniformly enforceable appointment authority. The revised narrative distinguishes ceremonial recognition from military and provincial enforcement.
- **Iqta and military service:** Clarifies that Buyid revenue assignments did not necessarily create hereditary ownership of land and cannot be equated simply with European feudalism; Daylamite/Turkic military constituencies are not treated as immutable ethnic parties. See Donohue (2003), Mottahedeh (1980), and the Iranica Buyids entry.
- **Later evidence:** The Ibn al-Athir ritual window is labeled a *retrospective* chronicle account written centuries after the 963 events, not contemporaneous eyewitness testimony.

### Nuances deliberately retained

- Caliphal authority varied with the reign and regional political conditions rather than remaining static from 946 to 1055.
- The Buyids were a family of interrelated branches with distinct territorial and fiscal bases; Iranian symbolism, Daylamite background, and Shiʿi religious affiliations are not conflated.
- Baghdad's unrest involved soldier pay, neighborhood organization, material pressures, and religious mobilization, rather than an exclusive Sunni–Shiʿi explanation.
- Persianate cultural patronage is distinguished from the ongoing role of Arabic scholarship and administration.
- The extraordinary concentration of authority under Adud al-Dawla is not projected across the whole century.

## 5. Source-window verification and limits

All five studies use **editorial paraphrase**, never fabricated verbatim quotation:

1. **Miskawayh, *Tajarib al-umam*:** The 945–946 change of caliph and Buyid military entry; Amedroz/Margoliouth's 1920–1921 seven-volume Arabic/English collection is specified, distinguishing Arabic vols. 1–3, translation vols. 4–6, and index vol. 7. Catalogue: https://library.hs.ias.edu/ipac20/ipac.jsp?aspect=basic_search&index=AUTHOR&menu=search&profile=hslib&ri=1&session=1E8304K9068L8.6227501&source=~%21horizon&term=Amedroz%2C+H.+F.+%28Henry+Frederick%29%2C+1855-1917.&uri=link%3D3100007~%21518661~%213100001~%213100002 .
2. **Hilal al-Sabi, *Rusum dar al-khilafa*:** A specific account involving Adud al-Dawla's audience and ceremonial demands. English edition: Elie A. Salem (1977), independently catalogued at https://books.google.com/books?id=3UJXAAAAMAAJ . An educational edition/excerpt corroborating the episode appears at https://pressbooks.ulib.csuohio.edu/premodernmiddleeast/wp-content/uploads/sites/153/2023/06/Hilal-al-Sabi.pdf .
3. **Al-Tanukhi, *Nishwar al-muhadara*:** A specifically identified earlier-vizier anecdote about Byzantine prisoners and religious intermediaries, deliberately labeled as **pre-Buyid in setting**, preserved by a Buyid-era author. Used for comparative bureaucratic imagination and literary genre, never falsely presented as a direct Buyid event. Edition: D. S. Margoliouth, *The Table-Talk of a Mesopotamian Judge* (1921–1922). Catalogue: https://openlibrary.org/books/OL24180478M/The_table-talk_of_a_Mesopotamian_judge . Historical discussion of episode: https://jscholarship.library.jhu.edu/bitstream/1774.2/60908/1/GRAYSON-DISSERTATION-2017.pdf .
4. **Al-Muqaddasi, *Ahsan al-taqasim*:** Identifiable regional descriptions of Iraq and Iran, contrasted with court chronicles; the chapter is explicit that the window is a regional-synthesis paraphrase, not a collated paragraph. Collins translation (1994) verified through https://wellcomecollection.org/works/getdvv7t .
5. **Ibn al-Athir, *al-Kamil fi al-tarikh*:** Later report of the public Baghdad rites of 352 AH / 963 CE, clearly labeled retrospective. Tornberg edition / Beirut reprint catalogued at https://wellcomecollection.org/works/tn3x6bdg .

**Critical evidentiary limitation:** This is a source-conscious teaching edition with verified bibliographic foundations and qualified paraphrases. It does **not** claim full original manuscript collation, a complete coin-by-coin numismatic catalogue, or authenticated verbatim translation of passages selected from every witness. The five source studies are accurate to the level supported by checked bibliographic and secondary evidence, with the distinction between contemporary and later accounts made transparent.

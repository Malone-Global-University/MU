## Primary historical corrections and source controls

**The October 1895 reform program:** The May 11 reform proposal was not the final governmental action. A modified Ottoman program was announced in October 1895. U.S. Minister A. W. Terrell's October 24 dispatch (<https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1895p2/d602>) describes an October 21 newspaper announcement and comments critically on the changes. British parliamentary discussion on January 21, 1897 (<https://hansard.parliament.uk/commons/1897-01-21/debates/fcf551b2-1060-4e8d-ac2d-75424e6749a0/TurkishReforms>) acknowledges partial measures but denies general implementation. The manuscript and source study distinguish original proposal, government proclamation, and effective implementation; it does not conflate dates assigned to different administrative acts.

**1863 constitutional terminology:** The Armenian National Constitution is explained as an Ottoman-recognized communal regulation, not a sovereign state constitution. Based on the published English translation in H. F. B. Lynch's *Armenia* and the institutional scholarship of Masayuki Ueno and Aylin Koçunyan. Source window 1 identifies the edition without claiming original-language collation.

**Article 61:** Clearly distinguishes treaty commitments concerning security, reforms, and reporting from any promise of Armenian statehood or effective international enforcement. The Fordham online Berlin text is an excerpt of Balkan provisions and *does not include* Article 61; the source window expressly warns against misusing it as a collation witness.

**1894–1896 violence:** The chapter distinguishes insurgents from civilian populations and identifies the roles and responsibilities of state authorities and participating armed formations. It avoids attributing particular regiments' actions to all Kurds and avoids false equivalence between armed political actions and massacres of civilians. Sasun, Zeytun, Van (1896), the provincial massacres of 1895, and the Ottoman Bank occupation of August 26, 1896 are treated as distinct events within the common crisis.

**Mortality:** The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum's minimum of 80,000 and Bedross Der Matossian's figure above 200,000 are separately attributed, not combined into a confidence interval or given spurious precision. The manuscript discusses differences among direct death counts, population loss, displacement, and unregistered persons. Der Matossian's 2023 *Cambridge World History of Genocide* chapter: <https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-world-history-of-genocide/ottoman-massacres-of-armenians-18941896-and-1909/81A9DF0AD37185DBCB8BB02DDC44D10C>.

**Source window 4:** An actual traceable diplomatic transmission is cited: Terrell to Olney, December 16, 1895, enclosing a compiled report dated December 14, 1895 with an entry about Harput on November 11: <https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1895p2/d683>. The edition distinguishes the minister who forwarded the material from the unidentified compiler and earlier informants. No purported direct observation is fabricated.

**Chronological boundaries:** Hamidian massacres c. 1894–1896 are distinguished from the 1909 Adana massacres and the Armenian genocide during World War I. No claim of inevitable historical progression is advanced.

**Other documentary and scholarship controls:** The source register provides fifteen individually identified sources or scholarly works, with titles, dates, links, and methodological qualifications. Some links are edition witnesses; some point only to catalog/abstract pages and are labeled accordingly. Scholarship supports framing but has not been exhaustively page-collated.

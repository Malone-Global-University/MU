## 3. Substantive historical corrections completed

### A. CPA Order No. 1: party rank versus public management

**Original issue:** Section 156.10 implied that Order 1 removed the four highest ranks and required review of the *next tier* of Ba'ath Party rank. This inaccurately compressed the document's distinct tests.

**Correction incorporated:** The revised section specifies that full party members holding the four highest Ba'ath Party ranks were excluded from public-sector employment. It separately explains interviews and screening of personnel occupying the top three **management layers** of ministries, universities, hospitals, and other government organizations. The screening concerned affiliation and possible criminal or security issues; the document required removal of senior managers found to be full party members. Primary Source Window III was corrected to make the distinction clear.

**Document checked:** Coalition Provisional Authority, [Order No. 1, 16 May 2003, Section 1(2)–(3)](https://govinfo.library.unt.edu/cpa-iraq/regulations/20030516_CPAORD_1_De-Ba_athification_of_Iraqi_Society_.pdf). Compare [Order No. 2, 23 May 2003](https://govinfo.library.unt.edu/cpa-iraq/regulations/20030823_CPAORD_2_Dissolution_of_Entities_with_Annex_A.pdf), on dissolution of named institutions. The latter archive filename bears a later administrative date; the original document itself is dated May 23.

### B. Second Battle of Fallujah: casualty reporting windows

**Original issue:** Section 156.13's phrase “several dozen American and Iraqi soldiers” understated the U.S. toll when applied to the entire November–December campaign and collapsed differing casualty groups.

**Correction incorporated:** The manuscript now gives the U.S. Army's **82 Americans killed and more than 600 wounded** during fighting from November 7 through December 31, 2004. It separately identifies a U.S. Marine Corps history's **51 American deaths and 425 seriously wounded**, reflecting a narrower operational phase and injury definition. It expressly warns against combining these figures or conflating them with Iraqi military, insurgent, and civilian fatalities.

**Sources:** U.S. Army, [*On Point II: Transition to the New Campaign*](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/GOVPUB-D110-PURL-LPS96027); U.S. Marine Corps, [*U.S. Marines in Iraq, 2004–2008: Anthology and Annotated Bibliography*](https://www.marines.mil/Portals/1/Publications/U.S.%20Marines%20In%20Iraq%2C%202004-2008%20Anthology%20and%20Annotated%20Bibliography%20%20PCN%2010600001900_3.pdf). These records are linked together in Reference [19].

### C. Invasion-force estimates

**Original issue:** Section 156.8 risked presenting roughly 150,000 Americans and 45,000 British troops as totals for all troops deployed to the 2003 theater.

**Correction incorporated:** The figures are now identified as approximate personnel associated with the principal invasion grouping, not a count of every U.S. or British service member in the wider Gulf theater. The revised paragraph cautions that comparisons with 1991 require matching definitions and deployment categories.

**Evidence basis:** Congressional Research Service military summaries and historical accounts distinguishing forces conducting the ground campaign from broader theater deployment. The numerical distinction is a clarification, not a change to the chapter's interpretation of the invasion.

### D. The 2007 U.S. troop surge

**Original issue:** Section 156.17 described the announced troop increase as approximately 20,000 without distinguishing that public announcement from the later implemented buildup.

**Correction incorporated:** The section now differentiates the initially planned approximately **21,500** combat personnel from the larger implementation, which brought U.S. force levels in Iraq to approximately **169,000** at the 2007 peak, compared with approximately **132,000** in December 2006. Supporting categories and dates are identified as relevant to the precise change figure.

**Evidence:** U.S. Government Accountability Office, [*Iraq: Key Issues for Congressional Oversight*, GAO-09-294SP](https://www.gao.gov/assets/a203616.html), and Congressional Research Service, [*Troop Levels in the Afghan and Iraq Wars*](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R40682.html).

### E. Civilian-death categories and Iraq Body Count

**Original issue:** The source's assertion that Iraq Body Count documented more than 100,000 violent civilian deaths was sound but lacked a precise, period-specific illustration of its methodology.

**Revision incorporated:** Section 156.16 now describes the retrospective IBC figure of **114,212 documented violent civilian deaths in 2003–2011** and distinguishes it from a separate running-database total incorporating later entered records. It contrasts incident-based documentation with household mortality surveys and warns against adding or equating incompatible populations, time windows, and causal categories.

**Evidence:** [Iraq Body Count, *Civilian Deaths from Violence in 2003–2011*](https://www.iraqbodycount.org/analysis/numbers/2011/); Burnham et al., [*The Lancet* 368 (2006), 1421–1428](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17055943/); Iraq Family Health Survey Study Group, [*New England Journal of Medicine* 358 (2008), 484–493](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18184950/).

## 4. Additional source verification and interpretive review

- **Al-Qaeda and September 11:** The narrative correctly distinguishes the Afghan anti-Soviet resistance, the foreign volunteer milieu, al-Qaeda, and the Taliban. The September 11 victims figure of **2,977 excluding the hijackers** is sound. The [9/11 Commission Report](https://www.9-11commission.gov/report/) does not establish Iraqi operational collaboration with al-Qaeda in the September 11 attacks. The chapter preserves that crucial distinction.
- **Prewar Iraq WMD claims:** The text identifies what U.S. and British officials argued before the war, acknowledges the inspection dispute, and contrasts the claims with the [Iraq Survey Group's Duelfer Report](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/GPO-DUELFERREPORT). It does not turn prewar speculation into historical proof.
- **Iraqi self-government and constitution:** The formal transfer of governmental authority in June 2004 is distinguished from the presence and powers of foreign forces. The [Transitional Administrative Law](https://www.refworld.org/legal/legislation/natlegbod/2004/39004), [Security Council Resolution 1546](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/523025), and [2005 Iraqi constitution](https://peacemaker.un.org/en/documents/constitution-iraq-current) support the institutional chronology.
- **Elections:** Inter-Parliamentary Union archives support the January 2005 figures (United Iraqi Alliance 140 seats, Kurdistan Alliance 75, Iraqi List 40), December 2005 main bloc figures (128, 53, 44 respectively for the United Iraqi Alliance, Kurdistan Alliance, and Iraqi Accord Front), and March 2010 results (Iraqiya 91, State of Law 89, Iraqi National Alliance 70, Kurdistan Alliance 43). [January 2005](https://data.ipu.org/election-summary/HTML/2151_05.htm); [December 2005](https://data.ipu.org/election-summary/HTML/2151bis_05.htm); [March 2010](https://data.ipu.org/election-summary/HTML/2151_10.htm).
- **Abu Ghraib:** The manuscript identifies established detainee abuses, the limited scope of Taguba's investigation, and broader institutional-accountability questions. The [Taguba Report](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB127/Taguba%20Report.pdf) is linked in the source window and bibliography. Abuse is not reduced to a public-relations controversy.
- **AQI and sectarianization:** The narrative distinguishes the Zarqawi-led organization from other Iraqi insurgent groups, explains the intentional targeting of Shiite civilians, and treats the post-Samarra escalation as a product of political and military decisions rather than timeless religious antagonism. The 2006 Islamic State of Iraq is clearly distinguished from the later territorial organization whose history belongs to Chapter 158.
- **2007–2008 decline in violence:** The chapter preserves a genuinely plural historical explanation, considering the U.S. surge, Sunni Awakening, Mahdi Army ceasefire, changes in neighborhoods, and Iraqi decisions. The [Biddle–Friedman–Shapiro study](https://esoc.princeton.edu/publications/testing-surge-why-did-violence-decline-iraq-2007) is identified as retrospective scholarship rather than evidence available to actors in 2007.
- **Security agreement and withdrawal:** The [2008 U.S.-Iraq agreement](https://2009-2017.state.gov/documents/organization/122074.pdf) required U.S. combat-force withdrawal from Iraqi localities by June 30, 2009, and all U.S. forces from Iraqi territory by December 31, 2011. The final major convoy departed December 18, 2011. The narrative does not treat troop departure as the end of diplomatic ties or Iraqi political problems.

This was a targeted scholarly verification of the major consequential claims, documentary foundations, and previously flagged inaccuracies. It does not claim that every sentence was independently re-established against an exhaustive global scholarly literature.

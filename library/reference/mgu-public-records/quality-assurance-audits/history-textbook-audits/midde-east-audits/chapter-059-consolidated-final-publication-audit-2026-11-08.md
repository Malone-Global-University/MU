## 4. Historical claims: resolved publication-critical issues

### A. Byzantine and Armenian frontier chronology

- **Ani:** recognized the end of its independent Bagratid monarchy with Byzantine incorporation in **1045**, distinguishing that event from Alp Arslan’s capture of the **Byzantine-held city in 1064**.
- **Earlier warfare:** treated Kapetron (1048), Manzikert expedition (1054), and raids in the 1060s as context, not a substitute for Chapter 56.
- **Military organization:** did not assert a simple, universal replacement of Byzantine local defense with mercenaries; emphasized regional variations and historical debate.

### B. Manzikert and Romanos IV

- Placed the battle on **26 August 1071**, with geographical context but without unsupported exact tactical coordinates or claimed verified troop totals.
- Distinguished the loss of the emperor's immediate command from destruction of the entire Byzantine army; significant other contingents survived, consistent with **Georgios Theotokis, *The Campaign and Battle of Manzikert, 1071* (2024)**.
- Clearly qualified the motives of **Andronikos Doukas** and the absence of **Joseph Tarchaneiotes**; neither is presented as conclusively guilty of intentional betrayal.
- Treated Alp Arslan's negotiated release of Romanos and the reported treaty terms as a documented diplomatic episode with uncertain exact provisions.
- Separated Romanos's **political displacement in 1071** from his **blinding and death in 1072**.

### C. Provincial collapse and Turkish state formation

- Examined post-battle civil conflicts and the actions of **Michael VII, Roussel de Bailleul, Nikephoros III, and Alexios I** as historical causes distinct from the battle.
- Explained **Süleyman ibn Qutalmish's presence at Nicaea by 1078**, with **1081** as a conventional rather than precisely documented founding date for Rum, following the specialist *Encyclopaedia Iranica* account.
- Distinguished the uneven emergence of **Danishmendid, Saltuqid, and Mengujekid** authorities from their later twelfth-century political territories; did not present later epics as contemporary state records.
- Explained **Philaretos Brachamios** and the development of Armenian Cilician authority without calling these simply national states.
- Located the loss of Antioch to Süleyman in **1084–1085** and his death in **1086**; deferred the 1097 recapture of Nicaea to Chapter 60.

### D. Social history and historical interpretation

- Distinguished Turkmen raids, service, pastoral migration, seasonal grazing, and settled rule.
- Distinguished political change, **Turkification**, and **Islamization** as processes with different timescales. No unsupported population totals were introduced.
- Recognized Greek, Armenian, Syriac and other communities as continuing political, religious, and economic actors; religious and linguistic labels do not determine a single political allegiance.
- Integrated women, peasants, traders, herders, clergy, local officials, ransom, and households while marking reconstructed examples as analytical illustrations.
- Distinguished the value of contemporary Byzantine writing from later Armenian and Arabic historical memory. Avoided treating national commemorations as direct evidence of eleventh-century conditions.

Geography exercises are text-only and require scholarly atlas consultation. Some precision of troop movements, military totals, and political territorial boundaries remains historically unrecoverable; the chapter flags these limits rather than inventing detail.

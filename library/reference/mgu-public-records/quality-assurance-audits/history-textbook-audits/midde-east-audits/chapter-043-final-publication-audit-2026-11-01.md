## Historical/source-critical audit and targeted interventions

**Corrections incorporated in the publication master**:

1. **§43.3:** relabeled the chapter sidebar and subsection to distinguish Yazīd's pre-680 *designation* from his *accession in 680*. The prose had already made this distinction; the label no longer implies the designation occurred only in 680.
2. **§43.23:** differentiated the entry of Balj ibn Bishr's Syrian soldiers into al-Andalus in 742 from systematic allocation to Andalusian provincial military districts under Abū al-Khaṭṭār approximately 743–745. Cf. Roger Collins, *Muslim Spain and Portugal: A Political History of al-Andalus* (1995); Taha, *The Muslim Conquest and Settlement of North Africa and Spain*.
3. **§43.25:** qualified the attribution of Qasr al-Mshatta to al-Walīd II and removed the inference that construction alone demonstrates the caliph's preferred habits or attitudes toward Damascus. Cf. Staatliche Museen zu Berlin, Museum für Islamische Kunst, Qasr al-Mschatta project.
4. **Five Primary Source Windows:** added explicit **annal/chronology locators** and preserved the unambiguous label **source-based synthesis**, without inventing page numbers or treating synthesis as verbatim quotations. §43.43's already supplied precise pp. 169–172 for al-Ṭabarī XXVII were checked against a searchable copy of the translated text; al-Ṭabarī reports the factional killing of al-Walīd and the entry into Damascus on Wednesday, 20 Ramadan 132 / May 2, 750.

**Specific claims verified/qualified**:

- **§43.10:** al-Mutawakkil's reported destruction of al-Ḥusayn's tomb in **236 AH (850–851)** is documented in later traditions, notably al-Masʿūdī. As the manuscript makes clear, this is later historical memory, not an event within the chapter's principal 680–750 period.
- **§43.11:** ʿĀʾisha's leading opposition to ʿAlī at the Battle of the Camel (656) is well established; the exact battlefield narratives vary by transmission.
- **§43.15:** al-Akhṭal was a Christian, Taghlibī Umayyad panegyrist particularly associated with ʿAbd al-Malik; contemporary poetry still requires contextual source criticism.
- **§43.19:** Ibn al-Zubayr remodeled the Kaʿba; al-Ḥajjāj, acting under ʿAbd al-Malik, subsequently reversed the additions according to later historical accounts.
- **§43.23:** distinction between the **Battle of the Nobles (740)**, the **Battle of Bagdoura (741)**, and Ḥanẓala ibn Ṣafwān's **defense of Qayrawān (742)** is preserved in the master.
- **§43.25:** attribution of Mshatta to al-Walīd II is plausible and often accepted but not an independent record of his behavior.
- **§43.32:** death of Judayʿ al-Kirmānī dated to **747**, with narrated circumstances treated cautiously.
- **§43.33:** Abū Hāshim poisoning at Umayyad instigation is explicitly a contested account associated with later Abbasid succession legitimation, not established fact.
- **§43.38:** Abū Muslim's promises of fairness, pay and reform are correctly framed as claims transmitted by later sources, not surviving original decrees.
- **§43.43:** al-Walīd ibn Muʿāwiya ibn Marwān's role and death in factional fighting at Damascus, and May 2, 750, follow al-Ṭabarī XXVII, pp. 169–172.

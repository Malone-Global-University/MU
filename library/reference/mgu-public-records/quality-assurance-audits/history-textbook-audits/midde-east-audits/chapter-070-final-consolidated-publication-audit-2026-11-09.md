## 3. Final historical corrections applied

### 3.1 Siege of Constantinople, §70.3
The earlier text made entry via the *Kerkoporta* or another small unattended gate appear to be the operative mechanism of the city's fall. The corrected text places the Ottoman breakthrough in the broader failure of land-wall defenses amid the final assault, particularly the Lycus/Mesoteichion sector and the wounding and withdrawal of Giustiniani. It identifies the Kerkoporta narrative as a **disputed episode**, not an authenticated indispensable explanation. The original text's cautious treatment of death/captive totals and Constantine XI's death is preserved.

**Verification:** Marios Philippides and Walter K. Hanak, *The Siege and the Fall of Constantinople in 1453* (Ashgate, 2011), specialized examination of the Kerkoporta tradition. Publisher: https://www.routledge.com/The-Siege-and-the-Fall-of-Constantinople-in-1453-Historiography-Topography/Philippides-Hanak/p/book/9781409410645 .

### 3.2 Şahkulu and Hadım Ali Pasha, §70.13
Replaced the erroneous location “near Kayseri or Kütahya” with **Çubuk near Sivas on 2 July 1511**, as supported by a report of Prince Ahmed in Ottoman archival records. The death of Şahkulu in the same encounter is no longer presented as established, since sources disagree about his fate. The wider social and religious causes of the uprising remain intact.

**Verification:** TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, “Şahkulu Baba Tekeli,” including TSMA E.3062 report: https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/sahkulu-baba-tekeli .

### 3.3 Ottoman battle positions at Chaldiran, §70.15
Corrected the **Anatolian right wing under Hadım Sinan Pasha** and **Rumelian left wing under Hasan Pasha**. The narrative now describes the assault on the Rumelian left and Hasan's death there, while recognizing the role of Sinan's right wing and supporting artillery. Surviving descriptions of artillery dispositions are still qualified rather than presented as an exact, incontrovertible battlefield diagram.

**Verification:** TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, “Çaldıran Savaşı” https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/caldiran-savasi and “Hadım Sinan Paşa” https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/hadim-sinan-pasa .

### 3.4 Disputed captivity of Ismail's household, §70.15
The new paragraph distinguishes **Tajlu Khanum** from **Behruza Khanum**, notes that Ottoman and Persian narratives differ, and expressly rejects a single confirmed reconstruction of captivity or return. This is important because later partisan accounts framed the issue through prestige, shame, and dynastic legitimacy.

**Verification:** *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Esmāʿīl I Ṣafawī, i. Biography,” https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/esmail-i-safawi/esmail-i-safawi-i-biography/ ; Tufan Gündüz, “Şah İsmail'in Eşi Taçlı Begüm,” *Türk Kültürü ve Hacı Bektaş Veli Araştırma Dergisi* 51 (2009): 223–234, https://research.hacettepe.edu.tr/en/publications/%C5%9Fah-ismailin-e%C5%9Fi-ta%C3%A7li-beg%C3%BCm-2/ .

### 3.5 Dulkadir and the upper Tigris, §70.16
Repaired the compression of several distinct campaigns into one annexation. Dulkadir came under Ottoman suzerainty in **1515**, whereas organization as a separate eyalet was later, likely early in Süleyman's reign. **Mardin's town initially surrendered in October 1515, changed hands, and its fortress fell only after further fighting and a later siege (late 1516–early 1517, with source variation)**. This preserves the distinction between municipal submission, fortification capture, and lasting sovereignty.

**Verification:** TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, “Mardin,” https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/mardin ; “Bıyıklı Mehmed Paşa,” https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/biyikli-mehmed-pasa ; “Dulkadir Eyaleti,” https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/dulkadir-eyaleti .

### 3.6 Hijaz and the next-chapter transition, §70.20
Removed the statement that Ottoman armies captured **Mecca and Medina** in the 1516–1517 Mamluk campaigns. The conclusion now describes the Ottoman conquest of Mamluk-controlled **Syria and Egypt**, followed by the entry of the **Hijaz into Ottoman sovereignty through the allegiance of the Sharif of Mecca**. The detailed political and ceremonial process remains appropriately reserved for Chapter 71.

**Verification:** “Ottoman Spatial Organization of the pre-modern City of Medina,” *ABE Journal*, https://doi.org/10.4000/abe.4341 .

### 3.7 Additional editorial changes
- Chronology preview reordered by the **start of each date/range**, preserving all 19 entries, including the early Safaviyya background before 1451.
- Opening's imprecise assertion of a “Persian-speaking” dynastic background replaced with “Persianate Sufi networks,” avoiding an overly rigid linguistic attribution.
- Full historical evidence and nuance retained concerning military logistics, the limits of guns as a single explanatory factor, religious diversity, and the distinction between a battlefield victory and durable territorial control.

## 4. Source windows and documentary limitations

1. **Nicolò Barbaro**: *Diary of the Siege of Constantinople, 1453*. The English 1969 translation by J. R. Jones / J. R. Melville-Jones (Exposition Press) is catalogued; the diary is valuable as Venetian eyewitness testimony but must be read with attention to the author's animosities and partial vantage point. Catalog: https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA78060026 .
2. **Tursun Beg with Kritoboulos**: Ottoman and Greek historical representations of Mehmed II, useful in comparative state legitimation and reconstruction of conquered Constantinople. Editorial study requires recourse to the cited translations for exact passage-level quotations.
3. **Shah Ismail's Khata'i corpus**: The source window now includes full publication metadata for V. Minorsky, “The Poetry of Shāh Ismāʿīl I,” *Bulletin of SOAS* 10, no. 4 (1942): 1006–1029, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0041977X00090182 . Attribution and transmission of poems are expressly flagged.
4. **Selim–Ismail letters**: Presents the pre-Chaldiran rhetoric as conveyed by later Ottoman collections, including Feridun Bey's *Münşeʾātü's-Selāṭīn*. It acknowledges the textual distance between surviving compilation and the alleged original exchange.

**Important first-edition limit:** The four source passages remain *critical editorial paraphrases*, **not primary-language, manuscript-collated or independently verified verbatim translations**. No direct quotation of unattested text was manufactured. The chapter is suitable as a scholarly **textual** HTML first edition; original-language facsimile collation and exact folio-level identification remain outside the scope of this edition. Geography exercises are text-based and rely on an external historical atlas rather than embedded plates.

## 3. Consolidated historical verification

The expansion was checked against an explicitly identified set of scholarship and documentary records. Changes resolved the most important weaknesses of the first draft:

1. **July initiative:** differentiate Said Halim's **July 15, 1914** preliminary approach from Enver's **approximately July 22** overture and subsequent negotiations. Source: Erik-Jan Zürcher, *Young Turk Governance in the Ottoman Empire during the First World War*, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/00263206.2019.1590820 ; T. Martirosyan, https://doi.org/10.1080/19475020.2023.2279947 .
2. **Secret alliance, August 2:** close reading of Articles 1–8, especially initial Austro-Serbian neutrality, conditional Russian-intervention alliance trigger, military mission and German territorial-protection undertaking. Text: https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/turkgerm.asp . A lacuna in the English Article 4 is identified rather than silently repaired.
3. **British battleships:** distinguish the emotional/political force of the Navy League fundraising campaign from actual loan financing. See https://doi.org/10.1080/07075332.2021.1938634 .
4. **Armed neutrality and the Straits:** distinguish August mobilization, August 10 arrival and August 16 nominal transfer of *Goeben* and *Breslau*, September 9 notification / September 10 diplomatic reports on capitulations, the September 27 closure, and October 1 implementation. Primary records: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1914/d1838 ; https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1914/d1839 ; https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1914Supp/d1214 .
5. **German funding:** qualify the distinction between an October pledge/loan, an immediately negotiated gold transfer around two million Ottoman liras, shipment and confirmed arrival. The text deliberately does not invent exact shipment dates or treat larger total loan commitments as gold already delivered.
6. **Naval operation, October 27–29:** distinguish orders to seek offensive action from Souchon's choice to strike coastal targets and compare claims of Russian provocation with operational evidence. Source: https://doi.org/10.1080/19475020.2023.2279947 .
7. **Declarations and October 30 cabinet divisions:** distinguish October 29 naval hostilities, Russia's November 2 declaration, Britain/France November 5 and the concurrent formal annexation of Cyprus; name the resigning ministers **Cavid Bey, Çürüksulu Mahmud Pasha, Süleyman al-Bustani and Oskan Efendi**. Source: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1914Supp/d258 .
8. **Jihad:** distinguish the five fatwas, a November 11 closed ceremony and the **November 14 public reading** at the Fatih Mosque, separate Ottoman domestic and German transimperial purposes, and avoid assuming uniform Muslim reception. Source: Zürcher (ed.), *Jihad and Islam in World War I*, Leiden University Press, 2016, https://www.loc.gov/item/2020426132/ .
9. **Social history:** describe mobilization as already affecting rural households before official entry, differentiate regional and religious experiences, and reserve later military campaigns and mass atrocities for Chapters 110–113. Related scholarship: Mehmet Beşikçi (2012), Yiğit Akın (2018).

### Historical conclusion

The chapter now makes the key analytical distinction among **alliance signature, armed mobilization, diplomatic neutrality, operational attack, formal declarations, and religious proclamation**. It maintains the authorized scope: summer–December 1914 with contextual antecedents, not a survey of all 1914–1918 campaigns.

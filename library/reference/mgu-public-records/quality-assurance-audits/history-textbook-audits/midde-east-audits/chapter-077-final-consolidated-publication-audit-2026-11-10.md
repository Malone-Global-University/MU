## B. Corrected or clarified historical issues

1. **Tahmasp's religious policies, §§77.4 and 77.20:** The first publicly reported repentance around 1533–1534 is distinguished from later shifts and Tahmasp's second court-wide repentance and directives of 1556. The reign is not described as a single instantaneous conversion or an unbroken course of enforcement.
2. **Karaki's decree, §77.6 and Source Window 2:** The dated 9 July 1533 recognition documented by Colin P. Mitchell is noted alongside variant 1532 dating in another standard reference. Karaki's juristic theory, cooperation with the dynasty, and disputes with Ibrahim al-Qatifi about *kharaj* and court payments are explained. The role of 'Deputy of the Hidden Imam' is not equated with unlimited enforceable executive power.
3. **Dashtaki family, §77.5:** Their contribution to older Iranian scholarship is separated from the contested history of their Sunni origins and retrospective claims of Shiite affiliation.
4. **Amili migration, §77.7:** Andrew Newman's radical challenge to the conventional early migration narrative is distinguished from Devin Stewart's case for a limited migration and Rula Jurdi Abisaab's prosopographical evidence for over 158 first- and second-generation émigré scholars and relatives. The problems of counting families, students, short visits and migrant generations are identified.
5. **Office chronology, §§77.8 and 77.17:** The sadr's older pedigree, periodic dual incumbency and division of crown versus state jurisdiction in 1562–1563 are included. The shaykh al-Islam is kept distinct from the sadr; the mulla-bashi is explicitly dated as an office created around 1712, outside the chapter's principal chronology.
6. **Astarabadi, §77.19 and Source Window 4:** *Al-Fawa'id al-Madaniyya* is dated precisely to 1622 while the scholar's disputed death date is kept approximate. The later Usuli ascendancy is not projected backward onto the seventeenth century.
7. **Iranian Jewish experiences, §77.15 and summary:** Babai ben Lotf is identified as a Kashan Jewish writer. His *Ketab-e anusi* and the persecution of 1656–1662 are treated as relevant to multiple Iranian communities, not exclusively Isfahan.

## C. Documentary studies and source integrity

- **Window 1 (§77.4):** Hasan Beg Rumlu, *Ahsan al-Tawarikh*. Named Persian editions and Seddon's 1934 English translation are identified; use remains an editorial account of the chronicle's religious themes, not a line-by-line collated quotation.
- **Window 2 (§77.6):** Tahmasp's royal order concerning Karaki. Distinguishes the historiographical reconstruction of the decree from direct inspection of a facsimile; explicitly discloses variant scholarly dating.
- **Window 3 (§77.8):** *Tadhkirat al-Muluk*, British Library Or. 9496, c.1725, with Minorsky's 1943 facsimile/translation and commentary. Correctly described as retrospective evidence for a late-Safavid system, not proof of arrangements in 1533.
- **Window 4 (§77.19):** Astarabadi's *al-Fawa'id al-Madaniyya* (completed 1622). Distinguishes analytical paraphrase of Akhbari reasoning from an independently verified textual translation.

All four are **clearly labeled editorial source studies/paraphrases**. Their specific primary works, editions or relevant manuscript witness, context, evidentiary limitations, and questions appear in the actual HTML. No invented original-language quotation, transcribed archival entry, translation, or manuscript page reference has been inserted. A 14-entry source and edition register is included in the completed HTML, with external reference links.

## 3. Historical correction register — completed

1. **Baghdad 1733, §88.13:** Corrected an outright reversal: **Topal Osman Pasha's Ottoman relief army defeated Nader near Samarra in July 1733**, causing Nader's forces to lift the siege. Distinguished the immediate relief from later fighting. Grounding: Laurence Lockhart, *Nadir Shah: A Critical Study Based Mainly upon Contemporary Sources* (Luzac, 1938), chapter VII, esp. pp. 68–71; Government of India digitized volume `https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/35134.pdf` (web text checked; PDF screenshot rendering was unavailable, so no assertion of full facsimile collation).
2. **Asʿad Pasha and Damascus 1757–1758, §88.9:** Corrected dismissal chronology. **He was removed in January 1757 before the disastrous 1757 returning Hajj caravan attack**, which occurred under his successor; he was later accused of instigation and executed in 1758. Expressly distinguished accusation from demonstrated complicity. Cross-check with Shimon Shamir, "Asʿad Pasha al-ʿAzm and Ottoman Rule in Damascus (1743–58)," *BSOAS* 26 (1963), and Karl Barbir, *Ottoman Rule in Damascus, 1708–1758* (1980). The Ibn Budayr source window was reconciled to this sequence and no fabricated original chronicle statement was introduced.
3. **Malikâne inheritability, §88.3 vocabulary:** Specified the central Ottoman *malikâne* as **a lifetime revenue entitlement, not automatically heritable real property**; explained the possibility of renewed family contracts/de facto continuity and preserved the distinction with local Egyptian *iltizam* practices.
4. **Cairo's 1754 transition, §88.5:** Added the death of Ibrahim Kahya al-Qazdağlı in **November 1754**, followed by Ridwan Kahya's killing by a mamluk associated with Ibrahim's household; distinguished senior regimental leadership from the rise of competing mamluk beys. Michael Winter, *Egyptian Society under Ottoman Rule* (1992), is the supporting synthesis.
5. **Zahir al-ʿUmar, §88.10:** Replaced a falsely exact "early 1750s" acquisition with **late 1740s or around 1750**, acknowledging variants for taking Acre and consolidating control. Avoided inventing a single unquestionable date and preserved caution about his death in 1775. Thomas Philipp, *Acre* (2002), and Ahmad Joudah, *Revolt in Palestine in the Eighteenth Century*, are pertinent comparators.
6. **Household succession, §88.2 vocabulary:** Corrected the misleading universal statement that succession generally passed to chosen followers, distinguishing kin-based Syrian and Mosuli families, Baghdad's father-to-son transfer, and recruited mamluk-client networks.
7. **Ottoman caliphal legitimacy, §88.19:** Replaced an overly universal claim with specific mechanisms: imperial investiture, the Friday sermon, sultanic sovereignty, administrative instruments, and holy-city responsibilities; identified Ottoman caliphal claims as historically variable and contested.
8. **Basra and the Zand conflict, §88.15:** Reaffirmed and distinguished **1775 siege / 1776 fall / 1779 end of occupation**, rather than treating all four years as a siege.
9. **Shihab emirate, §88.12:** Retained the difference between the family’s Sunni origins and later Christian affiliations among *some branches*, avoiding a retrospective singular confessional identity.
10. **Bibliography — Philipp:** Standardized Thomas Philipp, *Acre: The Rise and Fall of a Palestinian City, 1730–1831*, to the Columbia University Press **2002** edition, verified against publisher record `https://cup.columbia.edu/book/acre/9780231506038/` and bibliographic DOI `10.7312/phil12326`.

## 5. Documentary/source-window disposition

The four windows were **retained and strengthened without fabricating a direct quotation or archival reference**. They are not presented as facsimile-collated critical editions; all four carry a specific editorial source-status note in the publication HTML:

- **al-Jabarti / Cairo (§88.7):** Author-named biographical and annalistic chronicle; contextualized as retrospective in substantial part. No volume, page or untranslated Arabic passage has been independently collated.
- **Ibn Budayr / Damascus (§88.9):** Named everyday chronicle, with its contested transmission and later editorial mediation made explicit. Asʿad Pasha chronology is corrected, and speculative author attributions are removed. Interpretive prompts, not invented primary wording.
- **Nablus Islamic court registers (§88.12):** An archival genre study grounded in Beshara Doumani, *Rediscovering Palestine* (1995), `https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/rediscovering-palestine-merchants-and-peasants-in-jabal-nablus-1700-1900.html`. Explicitly cautions that nineteenth-century court examples must not be relabeled as eighteenth-century cases.
- **Mosul / 1743 (§88.16):** Ottoman orders, local court registers and retrospective narrative traditions compared as separate documentary genres following Dina Rizk Khoury, *State and Provincial Society in the Ottoman Empire* (1997). No specific archive folio is claimed to have been collated.

**Not completed:** original Arabic/Ottoman manuscript transcription or independent folio/page-level comparison. In an edition that requires original-document quotation windows instead of source-guided interpretive studies, these four windows would still need verified specimens.

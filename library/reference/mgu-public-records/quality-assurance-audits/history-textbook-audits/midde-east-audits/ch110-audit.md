## 3. Historical and chronological consolidated verification

- **Dardanelles/Gallipoli:** Distinguishes the 18 March 1915 naval defeat from the 25 April landings, and the May–July 1915 trench stalemate from the **Lone Pine battle beginning 6 August**. Explains *Nusret* mines and Ottoman artillery without claiming one factor uniquely determined the outcome. Differentiates December 1915 ANZAC/Suvla evacuation from the January 1916 withdrawal from Helles.
- **Caucasus:** Sarıkamış in late December 1914–early January 1915; distinguishes combat, weather, disease and uncertainty of casualty totals. Separates military formations and Armenian civilian communities; the battlefield narrative does **not** excuse, rationalize or subsume the Armenian Genocide. Records Erzurum February 1916, Trabzon April 1916, Erzincan July 1916, the 1917 Russian revolutions, Brest-Litovsk March 1918, resistance at Sardarapat and the September 1918 Baku advance. Avoids projecting modern national borders onto these campaigns.
- **Mesopotamia:** Distinguishes initial British-Indian Gulf security aims, extended advance to Ctesiphon, siege of Kut and 29 April 1916 surrender, British logistical reorganization and 11 March 1917 capture of Baghdad. Includes the severe treatment of captured personnel and explicitly recognizes Indian Army service. Places Sharqat in late October 1918 without confusing the later Mosul dispute with the battle.
- **Sinai–Palestine:** Reconstructs 1915 and 1916 attacks on Suez, Romani, the two 1917 Gaza defeats of the Allies, the wider Beersheba combined operation of 31 October (not only the mounted charge), Jerusalem's 9 December surrender versus 11 December ceremony, and the September 1918 Megiddo breakthrough leading to Damascus and Aleppo.
- **War termination:** **Mudros signed 30 October 1918; Article XXV specified that hostilities cease from noon local time, 31 October 1918.** The legal and political consequences are properly deferred to Chapter 113.

Relevant independently located source records include the Australian War Memorial (Gallipoli RC02687; Lone Pine; Beersheba), National Army Museum (Kut; Palestine/Megiddo), University of Oregon newspaper holdings (Erzurum), publisher descriptions of the Erickson/Gardner/Reynolds scholarship, and the English armistice text in German History in Documents and Images. Some operational interpretations remain interpretive and should not be mistaken for uncontested facts.

## 4. Six documentary source windows — provenance and limits

1. **Ottoman Fifth Army dispositions map**, Australian War Memorial **RC02687**, c. April 1915. AWM catalog and curator explanation explicitly identify some later annotations. No claim that all map inscriptions were collated against the original facsimile.
2. **Noel Medway Loutit Gallipoli landing map**, AWM **G7432G1S65V2F**, related to 25 April 1915. Used for an evidence-based discussion of scale, terrain and retrospective markings.
3. **Eugene Daily Guard**, 17 February 1916, p. 1, University of Oregon Libraries. A contemporary newspaper witness to public information about Erzurum; early casualty and captured-gun claims are **not adopted as verified historical totals**. The provided OCR is acknowledged as error-prone.
4. **Lieutenant Henry Gallup diary**, Kut, April 1916, National Army Museum **NAM.1986-11-43-4**. Museum transcription used; diary assumptions about Indian soldiers are explicitly treated as situated testimony, not established facts.
5. **Henry “Harry” Langtip 1917 diary**, AWM **RCDIG0000899 / PR00053**, and **Donald Cameron after-action report** of 31 October 1917; complementary but differently situated sources.
6. **Edmund Allenby despatch**, *London Gazette* supplement **31087**, 27 December 1918, beginning at p. 15183. Catalog landing page verified; passage-by-passage facsimile collation **not performed**. The source window is an explicitly labeled analytical guide, not a fabricated extract.

Every documentary window supplies a verifiable destination, provenance, substantive contextual analysis, limitations, and three student questions. **No unverified source quotation is represented as an original manuscript transcription.** Several analyses rely on authenticated catalog descriptions and contemporary records available online, rather than line-by-line archival or original-language collation. This limitation is acceptable for a clearly labeled first-edition textual study but must remain disclosed.

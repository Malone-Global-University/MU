## 4. Historical corrections and expansions directly applied

1. **Ottoman revenue categories and land rights:** strengthened the difference between legal ownership, recognized rights of cultivation, and entitlement to taxes; clarified the limits of revenue assignments and survey registers as evidence.
2. **Timar and tax farming:** deepened the connection between military financing, cadastral practices, fiscal intermediation, and regional continuity, without describing a sudden uniform empire-wide abolition of timars.
3. **Egyptian `iltizam`:** distinguished the treasury portion (`mal al-kharaj`), designated expenses (`mukhrijat`), the tax farmer's profit (`fa'id`, `fa'iz`), and potentially additional levies (including `barrani`), with caution against assuming one consistent breakdown in all places.
4. **Safavid `khassa` and `mamalek`:** replaced an overly broad implication about Abbas I with a multi-reign explanation; distinguished cash or silk revenue control from the formal province-wide classification of any particular territory, drawing on Willem Floor's scholarship.
5. **Rural Iraqi hydraulic history:** added the documented late-seventeenth-century Euphrates channel shift southwest of Baghdad, unsuccessful 1701 intervention, and consequences for irrigation, taxation, and local authority.
6. **Shaykh Humam:** specified the defeat of Hawwara power in 1769 and Humam's death in retreat on **7 December 1769**, without describing him as executed in battle.
7. **Kurdish transfers to Khorasan:** preserved the verified relocation policy under Abbas I while avoiding unverified household counts or uniform tribal characterization.
8. **Raqqa settlement attempts:** connected recurring central directives to the limits of implementation, resistance, water access, and the outcome of the 1691–1696 program.
9. **Rural voice and crisis:** expanded village negotiation, collective payments, migration, access to courts, and the interacting military, fiscal, and environmental dimensions of the Celali crises.
10. **Regional comparison:** expanded the chapter's concluding distinction among Ottoman, Egyptian, and Iranian fiscal regimes rather than repeating general claims about imperial decline.

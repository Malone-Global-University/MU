## 3. Substantive corrections and expansions

1. Expanded all twenty sections from short single paragraphs to approximately 694–884 words per section, retaining the approved titles and thematic sequence.
2. Explained preexisting Ottoman provincial administrative connections without depicting Iraq as either a naturally predetermined state or a cartographic invention without local history.
3. Reconstructed the 1920 revolt as intersecting religious, rural, nationalist, political, and military mobilizations, distinguishing contemporary sources and later memorialization.
4. Developed the Cairo Conference, Faisal's political coalition, the disputed 1922 treaty/1924 ratification, and the Organic Law of 1925 with distinctions between constitutional text and implementation.
5. Examined Shia political participation, Sunni Arab elite recruitment, Kurdish political differences, the Mosul inquiry and boundary settlement, and the social histories of other Iraqi communities without reducing politics to immutable communal divisions.
6. **Corrected tribal-law chronology:** occupation-era Tribal Civil and Criminal Disputes Regulations originated in **1916**; the **1924 amendment** transferred administrative powers; land settlement legislation followed in **1932**.
7. **Corrected petroleum chronology:** 14 March **1925** TPC concession; October **1927** Baba Gurgur discovery; July **1928** Red Line consortium agreement introducing formal U.S. consortium participation; **1929** change to Iraq Petroleum Company; **1935** Kirkuk–Mediterranean pipeline opening. The 1925 concession explicitly excludes the prewar Basra vilayet and specified transferred territories.
8. Distinguished the June 1930 alliance, May 1932 minority declaration, and October 1932 end of mandate; summarized verifiable rights provisions and their international supervision.
9. Expanded the **August 1933 Simele massacres** while distinguishing armed frontier clashes from attacks on noncombatants, identifying established military involvement, and not asserting unsupported exact casualty totals or resolved higher-level authorization.
10. Developed the 1936 coup, military institutionalization, regency after Ghazi's 1939 death, and preserved the 1941 war for Chapter 123.

## 4. Source governance

- Each of the **nine** primary-source windows includes historical context, a **clearly marked editorial paraphrase**, evidentiary limits, student questions, and a linked documentary source or identified historical reproduction.
- A consolidated register lists **18** archival, legal, parliamentary, and scholarly references. Key verification resources include UN Geneva's League Covenant Article 22; British Library/Qatar Digital Library contemporary 1920 correspondence; QDL treaty documents and the 1924 tribal-law amendment; the UN's reproduced 1925 Organic Law and 1932 declaration; contemporary Hansard on the TPC concession; the U.S. State Department's history of the 1928 Red Line Agreement; and the British Library's Assyrian Crisis collections.
- Document studies use paraphrases **not verbatim archival transcriptions**. Some linked resources are documentary guides, later reproductions, or archival catalogue records, rather than line-by-line transcriptions or facsimile-collated translations. They are identified accordingly. Never quote a paraphrase as an original document.
- Map studies are **text-guided historical atlas exercises** rather than original rendered maps; period-correct atlases must be supplied for cartographic visualization.
- A comprehensive scholarly critical apparatus involving verification against every surviving original language and archival manuscript was **not** undertaken. The publication approval is for scholarly *textual HTML* subject to these explicit limitations.

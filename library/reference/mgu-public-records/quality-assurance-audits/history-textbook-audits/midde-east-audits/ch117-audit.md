## 3. Consolidated historical / scholarly corrections

1. **Mandate chronology:** Correctly distinguish San Remo (April 1920), League mandate approval (July 1922), and effective entry into force (29 September 1923). Discuss actual obligations of the Mandate, notably Article 1, separately from the discretion retained by French military and civilian authorities.
2. **French partition:** Differentiate the 31 August 1920 Greater Lebanon decree from public proclamation on 1 September; distinguish early states, the federation of 1922, subsequent State of Syria reorganization in 1924–1925, and later status changes.
3. **Lebanese constitution:** Address Articles 9, 10, and the original Article 95 as matters of the 1926 constitutional context; do not present later amended Article 95 or the unwritten 1943 National Pact as the original 1926 text.
4. **Great Syrian Revolt:** Rebuild all four core sections with explanations of grievances, alliances, military response, civilian harm, the October 1925 bombardment, and the shift toward organized nationalist negotiations. Avoid unsourced precise casualty figures and falsely homogeneous communal responsibility.
5. **Political economy:** Replace the original erroneous implication of separately administered Syrian and Lebanese customs with historically connected customs, monetary and Common Interests arrangements, while analyzing the economic consequences of political partition.
6. **1932 Lebanese census:** Present 793,396 resident Lebanese citizens as a single explicit denominator (396,946 Christian and 396,450 non-Christian in the published tabulation); distinguish 254,987 registered emigrants and 61,297 foreign residents. Treat categories and citizenship policy as contested political constructions rather than timeless demographic identities.
7. **1936 treaties:** Distinguish preliminary negotiations, Franco-Lebanese signature (13 November), Franco-Syrian signature (22 December), and absence of completed French ratification.
8. **Hatay:** Separate the special territory arrangements, 1938 state, and 1939 incorporation into Turkey; acknowledge the contested territory's demographic diversity and the Syrian nationalist objection.
9. **Independence:** Distinguish political declarations and government formation in 1941–1943, Lebanese constitutional crisis in November 1943, French violence against Damascus in May 1945, and foreign military evacuation in 1946.
10. **Chronological boundary:** Detailed 1941 military campaigns remain assigned to Chapter 123; Chapter 118 concerns Transjordan and Arabian state formation.

## Historical verification and important distinctions preserved

1. **1722 is not 1736**: the effective loss of Isfahan to Mahmud Hotak is distinguished from the formal displacement of the Safavid dynasty by Nader at Muğan.
2. **Afghan political plurality**: Ghilzai and Abdali interests are distinguished, and the narrative does not depict all Afghan communities as one unified force.
3. **Russia and the Ottomans**: Ottoman battlefield conflict is separated from negotiated Russian withdrawal via the **Rasht (1732)** and **Ganja (1735)** agreements, with the unratified 1723 treaty distinguished from real occupation.
4. **India and Central Asia**: Nader's victories, massacre and booty are explained without claiming permanent annexation of Mughal India or comprehensive administration over the Central Asian khanates.
5. **Ja'fari policy**: Nader's proposal is not characterized as successfully abolishing Iranian Shiism; Ottoman refusal to recognize its central element and the 1746 settlement are stated.
6. **Karim Khan's political language**: the evolution from *vakil al-dawla* toward *vakil al-ra'aya* is included without fictional modern democratic implications.
7. **Zand geographic limitations**: Khurasan and northern Qajar territories are not treated as uniformly controlled by Karim Khan; the 1775–1779 Basra campaign has political and pilgrimage as well as commercial causes.
8. **1794 and its human cost**: the devastation of Kerman, including blinding, killings and enslavement, is discussed without presenting disputed casualty numbers as exact. The Qajar coronation and later wars are not imported into this chapter.
9. **Contested exact-day chronology**: the narrative acknowledges March 8/9 dating differences for Gulnabad and distinguishes Sultan Husayn's October 21 capitulation from the city's fall/occupation sometimes dated October 23.
10. **Krusinski's translation chain**: the chapter identifies the role of du Cerceau and edition differences; the Library of Congress 1725 departure date is noted rather than claiming an indisputable 1728 departure.

## 4. Historical verification and changes applied

| Topic | Publication treatment | Evidence base / audit assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Piacenza and Clermont | Byzantine appeal at Piacenza in March 1095; Urban II's call on 27 November 1095; Byzantine military aims distinguished from the larger religious campaign. | Cowdrey and Strack on the sermon tradition; France, Riley-Smith, Tyerman. |
| Clermont testimony | Fulcher and Robert the Monk are treated as later narratives. No alleged word-for-word speech is represented as authenticated. | Strack; the identified Fulcher/Ryan–Fink and Robert/Sweetenham editions. |
| Spiritual incentives and motivations | Penitential obligations described with period-sensitive qualification; religious commitment, kinship, resources, and political interest distinguished without simplistic reduction. | Riley-Smith; Tyerman; surviving charters summarized through specialist scholarship. |
| Rhineland massacres | A standalone section on Speyer, Worms, Mainz, Cologne, coercive baptism, local protection, community agency, and Hebrew memory; no false claims that every crusader participated. | Chazan; Cohen; Mainz Anonymous and Solomon bar Simson in Eidelberg. |
| Civetot | 21 October 1096, distinguished from the later princes' expedition. | France; chronology research. |
| Nicaea and Dorylaeum | Nicaea's 19 June 1097 surrender to Byzantine representatives and 1 July 1097 battle near Dorylaeum; logistics and Greek military support emphasized. | France; Latin and Byzantine traditions. |
| Armenian agency and Edessa | Baldwin's adoption by Thoros and the uprising of March 1098; responsibility for Thoros' death remains uncertain, rather than presumed. | Matthew of Edessa; Anna Komnene; MacEvitt; Asbridge. |
| Antioch | 1097–1098 siege; differentiated capture of most of the city (3–4 June in a modern reconstruction) from Kerbogha's defeat on 28 June. | Gesta Francorum; France; Brosset 2024. |
| Kerbogha | Spring 1098 Edessa campaign and coalition weaknesses analyzed in relation to troop cohesion, logistics, and commanders' interests. | Brosset, Al-Masāq 36(3), 2024, 281–303. |
| Holy Lance | Reported discovery and its effects on morale explained without certifying its supernatural authenticity. | Gesta Francorum; modern campaign historiography. |
| Ma'arrat al-Nuʿman | December 1098 killings included, together with qualified treatment of medieval cannibalism reports; no sensationalized exact totals. | Latin siege narratives and military scholarship. |
| Jerusalem in 1099 | Fatimid recovery of Jerusalem in 1098 is explicit; the 1099 garrison is not mislabeled Great Seljuk. | Arabic/Latin narrative context and modern Crusades scholarship. |
| Jerusalem killings | Mass killing of Muslims and Jews described directly. Hyperbolic images of blood are not converted into casualty figures. | Raymond of Aguilers; Ibn al-Qalanisi; France; Tyerman. |
| Political settlement | Godfrey chosen 22 July 1099; Ascalon victory 12 August; Baldwin I's coronation 25 December 1100 appears only as epilogue. Tripoli's later county is not projected into 1099. | France; Tyerman; selected chronicles. |

**Historiographical posture:** The finished chapter does not portray western or Muslim governments as unitary blocs; avoids invented army sizes and casualty totals; distinguishes political legitimacy from military possession; presents piety and armed violence together; and gives Jews, Greeks, Armenians, Syriac Christians, and Muslim inhabitants substantive historical roles. These controls were checked in the section prose, feature studies, and review questions.

## 5. Primary-source and bibliographic validation

The five **identified, labeled editorial source windows** address (1) Urban II/Clermont through Fulcher and Robert, (2) the Hebrew chronicles of 1096, (3) Anna Komnene and Matthew of Edessa, (4) the anonymous Gesta Francorum at Antioch, and (5) Raymond of Aguilers and Ibn al-Qalanisi on Jerusalem. Each window supplies identifiable episodes, provenance, historiographical cautions, and interpretive questions. The source register below is included directly in the HTML (18 entries). The principal edition and scholarly identification checks were made against catalog/publisher/specialist records:

1. Fulcher of Chartres, *A History of the Expedition to Jerusalem, 1095–1127*, trans. Frances Rita Ryan, ed. Harold S. Fink (Knoxville: University of Tennessee Press, 1969), verified through sourcebook/bibliographic records.
2. Robert the Monk, *Robert the Monk's History of the First Crusade: Historia Iherosolimitana*, trans. Carol Sweetenham, Crusade Texts in Translation 11 (Aldershot: Ashgate, 2005), verified against Routledge and catalog records.
3. *The Jews and the Crusaders: The Hebrew Chronicles of the First and Second Crusades*, trans./ed. Shlomo Eidelberg (Madison: University of Wisconsin Press, 1977); Chazan's source criticism is reflected in the 1096 study.
4. Anna Komnene, *The Alexiad*, trans. E. R. A. Sewter, rev. Peter Frankopan (Penguin Classics, 2009), publisher catalog verified.
5. Matthew of Edessa, *Armenia and the Crusades: Tenth to Twelfth Centuries*, trans. Ara Edmond Dostourian (1993), specialist Armenian bibliography verified.
6. Anonymous, *Gesta Francorum*, ed./trans. Rosalind Hill (1962), verified in a specialist source bibliography.
7. Raymond of Aguilers, *Historia Francorum qui ceperunt Iherusalem*, trans. John Hugh Hill and Laurita L. Hill (American Philosophical Society, 1968), specialist source catalog verified.
8. Ibn al-Qalanisi, *The Damascus Chronicle of the Crusades*, trans. H. A. R. Gibb (London: Luzac, 1932), specialist source catalog verified.
9. Jonathan Riley-Smith (1986), Christopher Tyerman (2006), Thomas Asbridge (2004), and John France (1994), verified against publisher or catalog records.
10. Robert Chazan (1987), Jeremy Cohen (2004), and Christopher MacEvitt (2008), verified against publisher and scholarly catalog records.
11. Thomas Brosset, “The First Crusade and the Failure of Kerbogha's Campaign,” *Al-Masāq* 36.3 (2024): 281–303, DOI 10.1080/09503110.2024.2342205, university and journal records verified.
12. Georg Strack, “The Sermon of Urban II in Clermont and the Tradition of Papal Oratory,” *Medieval Sermon Studies* 56 (2012): 30–45, specialist research record verified.

**Explicit first-edition limitations.** The source studies identify episodes and supply editorial paraphrases; they are **not verbatim passages independently collated against original Latin, Greek, Armenian, Hebrew, or Arabic manuscript facsimiles**. The chapter does not claim manuscript-critical transcription or full edition-by-edition textual collation. The three geography exercises are text-only and require external dated historical atlases; there are no embedded map plates. These disclosed limits do not defeat first-edition scholarly textual publication, but they must remain visible if the project later develops a critical-source or fully illustrated edition.

### 4. Historical accuracy checks and editorial corrections

**A. Mexican War chronology and state-claim distinctions**

The finalized narrative distinguishes Republic of Texas boundary claims from the territory actually governed; annexation in December 1845; fighting in April 1846; congressional declaration on May 13; Santa Fe occupation on August 18, 1846; Californian campaigns in 1846–1847; and the 1847 Taos uprising. It does not imply Mexican recognition of Texas independence in the 1830s. Supporting authority: U.S. State Department historical chronology <https://history.state.gov/countries/mexico> and National Park Service / New Mexico History Museum materials.

**B. The 1846 Diné (Navajo) Bear Springs memorandum**

Primary text independently located: <https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-navajo-1846-22497>. Date **November 22, 1846**, site **Ojo Oso / Bear Springs**, American signatory **Alexander W. Doniphan**. Article 2 explicitly includes New Mexicans and Pueblo peoples within the treaty's defined 'American people'; Article 3 treats travel and trade, Article 4 prisoners, Article 5 property seized after August 18. Corrected the draft's too-general synopsis to give students an article-level understanding without inventing any verbatim Indigenous first-person testimony. Named Diné signatories represent particular participants rather than guaranteed consent of every community.

**C. Kearny occupation and Taos resistance**

Kearny's occupation of Santa Fe on August 18, 1846, the subsequent Kearny Code, and the January–February 1847 uprising are kept separate. Taos source window now identifies **contemporary military correspondence reprinted in the 1900 Senate Document 442**, alongside modern source-critical context, rather than misidentifying a modern NPS webpage as the original 1847 report. The original congressional compilation is at <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/SERIALSET-03878_00_00-016-0442-0000/pdf/SERIALSET-03878_00_00-016-0442-0000.pdf>, with modern NPS account <https://www.nps.gov/people/charles-bent.htm>. The PDF's 27 MB size prevented a separate web screenshot-facsimile collation; **the source window is explicitly an editorial synopsis**, not a direct quotation or a claim of complete archival verification. The narrative avoids assigning one collective motivation to all Pueblo towns and disaggregates Hispano and Taos Pueblo participants.

**D. The treaty's legal stages and ratified form**

National Archives verified transcript: <https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/treaty-of-guadalupe-hidalgo>. The manuscript now maintains: signature **February 2, 1848**; U.S. Senate consent with amendments **March 10**; presidential ratification **March 16**; exchange at Querétaro and effective treaty **May 30**; U.S. proclamation **July 4**. **Article V** boundary, **VIII** property/nationality election, **IX** amended incorporation and interim rights, **original Article X stricken**, and **XI** U.S. obligations relating to incursions, captives, and stolen property. Important: removing original Article X did **not** eliminate all protections for land grants, since Articles VIII–IX remained; the **Protocol of Querétaro** explained the American position on deletion. Additional evidence: U.S. GAO report on New Mexico land grants <https://www.gao.gov/assets/a157551.html>.

**E. Article XI full scope and later abrogation**

The chapter goes beyond the draft's single-sentence description. It explains U.S. commitments to restrain or punish incursions into Mexico, prevent purchases of Mexican captives or stolen goods, secure return of captives when possible, and consider the risk of displaced Indigenous people being driven to incursions when U.S. settlement/removal policy was implemented. It identifies the treaty's derogatory description of Indigenous peoples as the **signatories' language**, not the textbook's voice. **Article II of the Gadsden Purchase Treaty (1853)** expressly abrogated Article XI; corroboration <https://avalon.law.yale.edu/19th_century/mx1853.asp>. The distinction between claimed authority and capacity to enforce was preserved.

**F. Geography and cartography**

Mapped distinctions: state claims, effective military occupation, Indigenous homelands, and the treaty boundary. The chapter directs students to the **December 1848 Ephraim Gilman map** prepared for Polk's congressional message, not a later retrospective map misrepresented as an 1848 artifact: <https://www.archives.gov/legislative/features/nm-az-statehood/us-map.html>. Geography remains a text-guided exercise requiring access to the actual historical map; no atlas plate has been embedded.

**G. Source integrity and secondary scholarship**

The historical account draws on Brian DeLay's *War of a Thousand Deserts* (2008), his verified 2026 Cambridge chapter, David J. Weber's *The Mexican Frontier, 1821–1846* (1982), Andrés Reséndez's *Changing National Identities at the Frontier* (2004), the National Archives treaty transcript, the 1846 Diné memorandum, the Emory 1848 reconnaissance (Library of Congress catalogue), official New Mexico documentation, and land-grant analysis. Bibliographic details are supplied with traceable resource links in the HTML. The 1846–1848 narrative avoids invention of casualty counts, fabricated diary passages, or claims of a universal Indigenous political alignment.

### 6. Disclosed limitations and release decision

1. **Five source-study windows are transparently labeled editorial paraphrases or studies**, not original-manuscript or facsimile-collated quotations. Full critical editions would require line-by-line verification and additional original-language/tribal-source collation. The Taos primary-report PDF was not visually collated due to web screenshot retrieval limits.
2. **Historical maps are not embedded.** The geography exercise provides named, verifiable external archival map references and requires students to consult an atlas/map.
3. **Exact Chapter 1 model parity was not independently tested.** The HTML shell and stylesheet were matched to the previously approved adjacent Chapter 68 file. A direct comparison with the original Chapter 1 source would be necessary to certify pixel-parity.
4. **No live-site deployment/integration test** has been conducted. Local standalone Chromium rendering and link/anchor checks passed; remote external sites may change.
5. **This is a substantive scholarly first edition, not an exhaustive critical source edition.** Local histories and Indigenous oral accounts require future documentary research where original voices were not recoverable in the verified research set.

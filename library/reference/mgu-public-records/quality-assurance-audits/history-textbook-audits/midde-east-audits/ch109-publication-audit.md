## III. Specific historical and documentary corrections made

1. **July 22, 1914:** Replaced approximate July dating for Enver's approach to Wangenheim; attributed the date to Karpat's scholarly reconstruction.
2. **August 2 German–Ottoman treaty:** Distinguished Article 1 neutrality from Article 2 Russian intervention trigger; explained Article 3's military mission provision, Article 4's defensive guarantee and missing cipher group, and Articles 5–8 on duration/ratification/secrecy. The alliance was not a cabinet-wide decision to attack Russian ports in October.
3. **British dreadnought requisition:** Corrected the unsupported idea that public donations alone financed the two ships. The Navy League fundraising myth and genuine public outrage are distinguished from loan financing established by bank/shipbuilder archival research.
4. **August naval transfer:** Distinguished the ships' official Ottoman names from the continuing role of German officers. Included August 11 FRUS documentary evidence.
5. **Capitulations:** Correctly cites Morgenthau September 10 and 11 documents in regular **FRUS 1914**, documents 1838 and 1839—not the wartime Supplement.
6. **October finance:** Distinguishes the proposed five-million-lira loan from two-million-lira immediate gold conditions; identifies the October 16 and October 21 shipment dates and avoids representing a loan authorization as delivered cash.
7. **October 27–29 fleet:** Differentiates government authorization, Enver's military position, Souchon's operational leadership, and contested cabinet awareness; details Sevastopol, Odessa, Feodosia, and Novorossiysk targets without assuming that contemporaneous claims on all vessel damage were conclusive.
8. **October 29–November 5 diplomacy:** Preserves the distinction between hostilities, ambassadorial departure, Russian declaration of November 2, British/French declarations of November 5, and Britain's annexation of Cyprus.
9. **Cabinet resignations:** Morgenthau's November 7 dispatch identifies Cavid, Mahmud, Bustani, and Oskan; does not invent identical resignation dates; separately records Said Halim's consideration of resignation without taking it.
10. **November jihad proclamation:** Distinguishes five fatwas, endorsement by twenty-nine religious authorities, November 11 closed presentation, November 14 public reading by Ali Haydar Efendi, further circulation, and separate German and Ottoman purposes.
11. **Historical boundaries:** Narrates decisions through the transition to belligerency, while reserving later campaigns, genocide and famine, Arab Revolt and partition diplomacy, and peace settlement for Chapters 110–113.
12. **Source apparatus:** Six source windows now identify exact documentary bases or labeled scholarly text transmission, have explicit clickable source URLs, and disclose paraphrase/facsimile limitations. Added a differentiated documentary and scholarly register.

## 3. Historical corrections verified against documents

**Canal legal character, nationality, and jurisdiction (§93.6):** The manuscript's claim of a simply French legal character has been replaced with the distinction between French capital and management, Egyptian company nationality and jurisdiction, and special French-law rules for specified internal corporate questions. **Basis:** February 22, 1866 convention, sanctioned by Ottoman firman March 19, 1866, summarized in the U.S. State Department's documentary note (link below).

**Territorial sovereignty and reversion (§93.8, 1856 Source Window):** Removed the categorical claim that Egypt 'did not own the canal.' Egypt retained territorial sovereignty; the company possessed concessionary rights to operate the canal and receive tolls. The 1856 arrangement afforded the government the right to resume possession at concession expiry subject to relevant compensation, or to extend the agreement. The chapter now distinguishes the territory, the waterway, the company, and the concession.

**1864 arbitral settlement (§93.7):** Replaced vague 'tens of millions' with the documented **84 million francs awarded by Napoleon III on July 6, 1864**, clearly labeled the amount awarded rather than all eventual spending or paid cash.

**1875 shares, coupon assignment, and interest (§93.10 and source study):** Specified **176,602 shares**, the earlier **1869 assignment of dividend coupons through 1894**, and Egypt's **annual five-percent payment on the purchase price to Britain** until the coupons became available. Avoids treating 1875 as the beginning of immediately lost dividend income. The 15-percent concession entitlement is clarified as distinct from share dividends.

**1880 canal-profit entitlement (§93.11):** Added an appropriately limited explanation of the transfer of Egypt's separate **15-percent profit entitlement** to French creditor interests for approximately **22 million francs**. This does not conflate the rights with the stock sold in 1875.

**Mixed Courts chronology:** Separated the **1875 inauguration / 1876 commencement of adjudication** from the distinct financial measures introduced in 1876–1878. Preserves accurate description of the Caisse and Dual Control.

**1881 Abdin scene (§93.15):** Identified **Auckland Colvin as British financial controller acting for the absent consul-general Edward Malet**, rather than describing him as the substantive consul-general.

**1873 firman:** Added the verified **June 9, 1873** date to the original source study's metadata.

Uncertain casualty statistics, the alleged Ismail quotation, the reported Urabi slogan, and accounts of Abbas I's death remain cautiously qualified rather than being made falsely precise. The chapter does not present the invasion and occupation as inevitable or as evidence that Egyptians were incapable of governing themselves.

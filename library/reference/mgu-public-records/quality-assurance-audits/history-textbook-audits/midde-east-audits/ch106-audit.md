## Historical verification and corrections incorporated

1. The **1905 sugar merchants** punishment is distinguished from preceding bank/endowment disputes and the further 1906 sanctuary movements. The 1906 protests evolved from a demand for an *adalatkhana* to a Majles, and were not a prearranged modern parliamentary party.
2. The **August 5, 1906 decree**, September electoral rules, **December 30, 1906 Fundamental Law** (51 articles) and **October 1907 supplement** are separately explained. The six electoral estates, unequal representation, and the exclusion of women and active military personnel are specified. The 1907 law's Article 2 provided for selection of at least five jurists; enactment is not assumed to prove continuous operation of the scheme.
3. The **Anglo-Russian Convention (August 31, 1907)** is described as a treaty between foreign governments delimiting concession priorities and influence, not Iranian annexation or a law abolishing formal sovereignty.
4. The **June 23, 1908 coup** and **1908–1909 Tabriz** sequence are differentiated, including the acute blockade from February 1909, Russian arrival in late April, and the Iranian revolutionary and civilian experiences.
5. The **July 13–16, 1909** Tehran entry and deposition are distinguished, as are constitutional restoration and the unresolved problem of armed political authority.
6. The **Second Majles**, Democrats, Moderates, women, minority and provincial participation, 1910 political violence, financial constraints and Shuster's 1911 mission receive substantive treatment.
7. Russia's **early and late November 1911 demands** are distinguished, including the later explicit demand to dismiss Shuster. **December 24, 1911** is retained as the dissolution date; constitutional ideas and later attempts to restore parliamentary life are not erased.
8. **Date qualifications:** the October 1907 Supplement may appear as October 7 or 8 depending on published edition or dating convention; specialist accounts distinguish the Russian military entry into Tabriz as April 28 or 30. The manuscript notes these divergences, rather than inventing separate events.

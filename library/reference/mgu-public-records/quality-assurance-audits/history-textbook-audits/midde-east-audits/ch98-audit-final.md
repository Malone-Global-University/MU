## 3. Consolidated historical corrections and expansion

1. **Yıldız Palace relocation:** corrected the move from Dolmabahçe to **April 7, 1877**, distinguishing physical relocation from the subsequent growth of palace-centered administration following the 1878 parliamentary suspension. Reference: Berrin Torolsan, “A World of His Own,” *Cornucopia* (2015), https://www.cornucopia.net/magazine/articles/a-world-of-his-own/.
2. **1881/82–1893 census:** clarified that both women and men were included, unlike earlier male-centered enumerations; censuses still excluded or estimated some women, mobile populations and difficult-to-reach districts. Reference: Kemal H. Karpat, “Ottoman Population Records and the Census of 1881/82–1893,” *International Journal of Middle East Studies* 9 (1978), 237–274.
3. **Aşiret Mektebi:** clarified that the institution, opened **October 4, 1892**, initially enrolled sons of Arab tribal leaders; Kurdish tribal students and subsequently Albanian students also enrolled. Expanded boarding, curriculum, patronage, graduate paths and closure in 1907. Reference: Bayram Kodaman, “Aşiret Mekteb-i Hümâyunu,” *TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi* (1991), https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/asiret-mekteb-i-humayunu; Eugene Rogan, *IJMES* 28 (1996), 83–107.
4. **Pilgrimage scale:** removed the generalized assertion of “hundreds of thousands each year”; differentiated contemporary count types and uncertainty. Reference: William Ochsenwald, *Religion, Society and the State in Arabia* (1984).
5. **Hamidian massacres:** retained a serious explicit account of state-involved violence while explaining divergent estimates: Johannes Lepsius roughly 100,000; Raymond Kévorkian nearly 200,000; reported Armenian Patriarchate figure about 300,000. These are not confidence limits or a single reliable interval. References: Deborah Mayersen, *Genocide Studies and Prevention* 16 (2022), 68–88, https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/gsp/vol16/iss2/10/; mortality-review bibliography at https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14623528.2015.1062281.
6. **Selective historical expansion:** deepened §§98.2, 98.4–98.8, 98.10–98.11, 98.13–98.14, 98.16–98.17, and 98.19, with attention to provincial councils, registration, pedagogical finance, schooling, letters and telegrams, finance and assigned revenue, foreign relations, and the limits of Macedonian reforms. Emphasized policy mechanisms and contrasting community experiences rather than padding.
7. **Historical chronology preserved:** February 14, 1878 parliamentary suspension; Treaty of Berlin July 13, 1878; 1881 Muharrem decree; 1900 Hijaz Railway start; completion to Medina, not Mecca, in 1908; July 1908 constitutional restoration distinguished from April 1909 deposition.

## 4. Documentary/source study resolution

**Window 1, Treaty of Berlin, July 13, 1878.** A recoverable full diplomatic English translation is available from the U.S. State Department's *Foreign Relations of the United States* for 1878, https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1878/d523. Explicitly verified the Armenian-provincial reform clause in **Article LXI** and religious equality in **Article LXII**. The Fordham Modern History Sourcebook (https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/mod/1878berlin.asp) contains only selected excerpts, and is labeled accordingly. The chapter's window remains an editorial paraphrase rather than a fabricated quotation.

**Window 2, photographic albums.** Anchored the study to an **individual, catalogued Library of Congress photograph** rather than generic photographs: Abdullah Frères, *Students, Mekteb-i Edeb-i*, date range 1880–1893, **LOT 9511, no. 10**, catalog **2001696031**, https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2001696031/. This belongs to a portrait album of Istanbul schoolgirls in the 1,819-photograph Abdul Hamid II Collection (https://www.loc.gov/collections/abdul-hamid-ii/about-this-collection/). The image has not been independently analyzed as an original negative; the treatment is explicitly catalog-grounded rather than an invented description of visible details.

**Window 3, Hijaz Railway.** Positively identified British Library **IOR/L/PS/10/12, folio 54r**, https://www.qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100063527354.0x000073. It contains a **14 August 1907 Berlin diplomatic covering letter** forwarding an English translation of an **unsigned *Cologne Gazette* article dated August 5, 1907**. The modern QDL transcription is machine-produced and may contain errors. Editorial paraphrase identifies reported c. 850 km of main line and a 161-km Haifa branch, and describes then-current predictions of reaching Medina in 1910 and Mecca in 1913, neither of which occurred as anticipated: the line reached Medina in 1908, not Mecca. Byline of original newspaper article is unknown on the inspected folio, and no spurious personal attribution is claimed.

**Window 4, Meşveret.** Anchored the study to the **inaugural December 1, 1895 issue** and its introductory editorial as described by Azmi Özcan, “Meşveret,” *TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi* (2004), https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/mesveret--gazete. The editorial's emphasis on religiously inclusive Ottoman unity, lawful dynastic loyalty and criticism of arbitrary government is an attributed **secondary summary**, not a facsimile-collated Ottoman Turkish transcription. The French supplement began December 7, 1895.

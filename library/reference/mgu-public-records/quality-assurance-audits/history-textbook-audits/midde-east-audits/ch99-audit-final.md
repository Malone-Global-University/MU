## 3. Historical verification and correction register

**Publication-critical correction made:** *Al-Hoda* was founded in **Philadelphia on February 22, 1898** by Naoum Antoun Mokarzel, and moved to **New York in 1902**. Claude's original §99.15 implied New York was its founding city. The correction is in §99.15, the chronology and source register. Source: NC State University Libraries, *Al-Hoda Newspapers, 1898–1945*, https://www.lib.ncsu.edu/findingaids/ns0010.

**Cross-checked chronology:**

1. *Al-Ahram* first appeared **August 5, 1876** in Alexandria, initially as a weekly; became daily in **1881**; moved headquarters to Cairo **November 1899**. Museum With No Frontiers / Bibliotheca Alexandrina, https://sharinghistory.museumwnf.org/database_item.php?id=object%3BAWE%3Beg%3B110%3Ben.
2. *Akhtar* launched in Istanbul **January 13, 1876**, and was later suppressed in the mid-1890s. Laurence Paul Elwell-Sutton, "Akhtar Newspaper," *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/aktar-2-persian-newspaper/.
3. *Qanun* began in London in **February 1890**; its opening issue page 1 is identified in *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/journalism-i-qajar-period/.
4. Syrian Protestant College opened in Beirut **December 3, 1866**; its medical department began in **1867**, and the 1882 Edwin Lewis/Darwin dispute is documented by AUB's archival exhibition, https://aub.edu/President/Pages/history.aspx and https://online-exhibit.aub.edu.lb/exhibits/show/caps--gowns--and-beginnings---/commencements-through-challeng/commencement-1882--darwin-issu.
5. *Al-Fatat* began in Alexandria **November 20, 1892** (Women and Memory Forum, https://wmf.org.eg/en/publication/al-fatat-magazine/); *Hanımlara Mahsus Gazete* began **August 31, 1895** (TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi, https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/hanimlara-mahsus-gazete).
6. *Kawkab America*'s inaugural issue dated **April 15, 1892** appeared in New York (Library of Congress Near East collection guide, https://wwws.loc.gov/rr/amed/guide/nes-america.html; Center for Research Libraries holding record, https://digitalcollections.crl.edu/record/1197180/).
7. *Al-Muqtataf* began in Beirut in **1876** and moved publishing to Cairo in **1885**; scholarly literature on the American Mission Press and AUB historical material support the general chronology. The 1882 SPC dispute is kept distinct from the journal's later relocation.
8. The Library of Congress's Abdülhamid II collection contains **1,819 photographs in 51 albums**, approximately 1880–1893: https://www.loc.gov/collections/abdul-hamid-ii/about-this-collection/.

**Historical interpretation:** Retains older cities, markets, manuscript and oral communication; presents regional urban variation and the uneven effects of modernization; treats women, migrants and workers as participants; does not equate print networks with universal literacy or political democracy; distinguishes Chapters 96–98's subjects and the separate intellectual/revolutionary scope of Chapters 100–106.

## 4. Four source windows — documentary integrity and limits

1. **First issue of *Al-Ahram*, Alexandria (1876):** catalog-verified first issue and publication history; **not an independently collated transcription** of particular editorials or advertisements. Student exercise explicitly investigates what the holding proves and what requires consulting the issue itself.
2. **Mirza Malkom Khan's *Qanun*, inaugural London issue (1890):** editorial argument about joining dispersed reformist opinions is an **attributed paraphrase of a secondary translation** identified in *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, not a newly authenticated direct Persian quotation. No article number or invented quotation is supplied.
3. ***Kawkab America*, New York (1892):** date, immigrant origin and contextual description grounded in Library of Congress and CRL curatorial/catalog data; first issue's exact articles and advertisements **not collated**.
4. **Hind Nawfal's *Al-Fatat*, Alexandria (1892):** institutionally attested inaugural issue and later reproduction; study states explicitly that no particular Arabic editorial was authenticated or directly quoted.

All four windows include historical context, documentary provenance, attributed or metadata-grounded analysis, explicitly disclosed limits and three student questions. The chapter's source register names recoverable editions/holdings and scholarly contexts; sources are **identified rather than exhaustively facsimile-collated**. Direct original-language primary-source quotation is absent and never fabricated.

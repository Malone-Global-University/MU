## 3. Substantive historical safeguards and corrected coverage

1. **Armenian Genocide:** Correctly identified as a deliberate, CUP-directed campaign of destruction, with analysis of prewar Armenian societies, the 24 April arrests, May 1915 deportation provisions, the June property measures, labor battalions, convoy killing, Aleppo, Ras al-Ayn, Deir ez-Zor, dispossession, forced assimilation, resistance, protection and local administration. Avoids collective-blame arguments and does not treat Armenian civilians as interchangeable with armed formations.
2. **Assyrian/Syriac/Chaldean Sayfo:** Independently developed across Hakkari, Diyarbekir, Mardin, Tur Abdin, Azakh, and Urmia–Salmas, with careful religious terminology and regional differentiation. Urmia and Salmas explicitly identified in **Qajar Iran**, not Ottoman provinces. Does not invent a single exact aggregate mortality figure.
3. **Greater Syrian famine:** Distinguishes Mount Lebanon and Beirut from the wider region, giving interlocking causal attention to Allied blockade, Ottoman military requisitioning, broken commerce, purchasing power, rail and animal transport, the 1915 locust invasion, disease, municipal provisioning and relief. Does not present a multicausal famine as an automatically identical policy to genocide.
4. **Mortality:** Armenian genocide estimate attributed to the US Holocaust Memorial Museum as **at least 664,000 and possibly as many as 1.2 million**. Sayfo and famine estimate limitations explained without numerical invention; population boundaries and dates flagged as critical.
5. **1915 dates:** April 24 commemorative arrests; May 27 deportation law; May 30 implementing arrangements; June 10 property regulation; July 10 Morgenthau telegram (received July 14); October 15 Dayro d-Zafaran note. Extended processes presented as ranges.
6. **Mudros:** Signed October 30, 1918, taking effect October 31. Postwar legal settlement and Arab wartime diplomacy reserved to Chapters 113 and 112.

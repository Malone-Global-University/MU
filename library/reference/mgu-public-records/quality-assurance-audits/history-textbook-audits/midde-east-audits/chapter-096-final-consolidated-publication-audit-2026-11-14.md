## 3. Publication-critical factual corrections

1. **§96.8 (Decree of Muharrem)**: Fixed the false equivalence between “8 December Old Style” and “8 Muharrem.” Correct Islamic date **28 Muharrem 1299**, equal to **8/20 December 1881** in the relevant dual dating. The decree's restructuring is described without treating “half” as a universal haircut. Evidence: British Library, *Turkey No. 1 (1911). Imperial Ottoman Debt. Decree of 28 Muharrem, 1299*; BnF printed decree catalogue; Murat Birdal, *The Political Economy of Ottoman Public Debt*.
2. **§96.11 (Iranian loan bank)**: Replaced false “1891 Russian government-established bank” with **Yakov Polyakov's private 1890 concession**, distinguishing later Russian state influence and acquisition arrangements. *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Banking i” and “Concessions ii” use slightly different milestones for acquisition and formal control, so the final text does not falsely collapse them into one date.
3. **§96.4 (commercial geography)**: Separates maritime ports, Baghdad's river-trade role, and inland Tabriz, correcting the categorical misclassification of all as seaports.
4. **§96.17 (Gilan silk)**: Corrected mechanism and chronology: silkworm pébrine appeared in Europe earlier and **spread into Iranian production districts in the 1860s**, damaging Gilan's own sericulture.
5. **§96.2 (Balta Liman customs)**: Clarified the commonly cited applicable **5% import** (3+2) and **12% export** (3+9) structure, while noting that treaty terms were revised later, including in 1861. Rates are not generalized to every merchandise class or year.
6. **§96.14 (Izmir–Aydın railway)**: Added the contemporary **1857 company prospectus advertising a six-percent dividend guarantee for fifty years**, distinguished from the original signed Ottoman concession and later kilometrical guarantee mechanisms.
7. **§96.7–96.8 (debt values)**: Differentiated face value, proceeds, coupon, outstanding obligation, and restructuring valuation; retained a cautious approach to the approximate £200 million historical headline, which cannot by itself stand for cash received or debt after conversion.

## 4. Source integrity and study windows

- **1838 Convention:** Anglo-Ottoman Convention of Balta Liman, 16 August 1838; contemporary English treaty collections, and Şevket Pamuk's Ottoman political economy research. General effects are editorial paraphrase, not fabricated quotations.
- **Egypt's Caisse:** Khedival decree of **2 May 1876**, reproduced in T. E. Holland, *The European Concert in the Eastern Question* (Oxford, 1885), Chapter IV, Document XII, p. 147 onward. Distinguish from the subsequent unification decree of 7 May and the later Dual Control.
- **Muharrem:** Ottoman decree of **28 Muharrem 1299** (20 December 1881 Gregorian); printed 1881 original catalogued at BnF (`cb457268086`); published English document in British Library (`IOR/L/PS/20/C141/3`). Specific conversion clause language was not reproduced as a quotation.
- **Izmir–Aydın:** Investor-facing company notice in *The Economist*, **30 May 1857**, advertising guarantee and other claimed terms. The scanned periodical is a contemporary investor presentation and **not** a manuscript-collated Ottoman concession text. Its text was accessible in a searchable historical PDF transcription; visual-page verification via the online PDF screenshot facility failed because of source size. The final chapter maintains the document-type distinction.
- **Further source register:** Ottoman Bank institutional chronology; *Encyclopaedia Iranica* for Iranian banking, concessions and Gilan silk; Akram Khater for the Mount Lebanon silk workforce; Murat Birdal, Edhem Eldem, Donald Quataert, Şevket Pamuk, and Daniel Stolz for banking, industries, loan calculations and fiscal administration.

**Methodological caution:** The four windows remain editorial paraphrases or source studies, not direct quotations from manuscript originals. Actual clause-number quotations or unit-specific time series would require edition-level archival collation. Where source interpretations differ, especially stages of Russian acquisition of the Iranian loan bank, the chapter avoids a false degree of precision.

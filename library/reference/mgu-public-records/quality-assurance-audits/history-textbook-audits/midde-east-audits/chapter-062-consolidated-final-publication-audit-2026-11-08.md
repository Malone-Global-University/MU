## 3. Historical and editorial findings

### Primary chronology

The corrected chapter keeps the following independently checked sequence:

- 1123: Pactum Warmundi and Venetian privileges in the Latin kingdom.
- 1147–1149: Second Crusade, including the failed Damascus siege in July 1148.
- 1189–1192: Third Crusade; Acre surrenders in July 1191; Richard I conquers Byzantine-held Cyprus in 1191; September 1192 settlement preserves a coastal Latin kingdom.
- 1202–1204: Fourth Crusade turns to Zara and Constantinople; 1204 sack, Latin Empire, and Byzantine successor states.
- 1217–1221: Fifth Crusade; Damietta captured in 1219 and evacuated after defeat in 1221.
- 1229: Frederick II and al-Kamil's restricted Jerusalem settlement; Muslim authority concerning the Haram al-Sharif is retained.
- 1239–1241: Barons' Crusade and temporary diplomatic gains; 1244: Khwarazmian seizure of Jerusalem and battle of La Forbie. The Khwarazmians are explicitly differentiated from Mongol imperial armies.
- 1249–1250: Louis IX's Egyptian campaign, Damietta, Mansurah, capture; subsequent Latin East efforts through 1254.
- 1261: Constantinople recovered for Michael VIII **by forces under Alexios Strategopoulos**, correcting the imprecise impression that the emperor personally commanded the operation.
- 1268, 1289, 1291: Mamluk conquests of Antioch, Tripoli, and Acre; the last on May 18, 1291.

### Historical safeguards and interpretive balance

- Cyprus is not treated as territory taken from a Muslim government; Greek-speaking Cypriot society under Lusignan rule is discussed independently.
- The Fourth Crusade's diversion is analyzed through debts, choices, Byzantine succession politics, and Venetian interests, not a conspiratorial prearranged destination.
- The Fifth Crusade's rejection of negotiations is not assigned exclusively to Pelagius; leadership disagreement, uncertainty over treaty terms, and military objectives are considered.
- The 1229 settlement does not become a full sovereignty transfer of all Jerusalem's sacred and political jurisdictions.
- Latin rulers' treaties with Muslim rulers, Byzantine political differences, Italian maritime conflicts, Armenian and other local Christian agency, and Muslim and Jewish community histories are given narrative attention.
- Later Mongol and Mamluk political systems are introduced only as they alter the Latin states' choices; the full state histories remain with Chapters 63–65.
- Acre's loss is not presented as the inevitable result of Hattin or a single broken truce. It is placed in a broader sequence of military, fiscal, dynastic, and maritime developments.
- Massacres, enslavement, and civilian suffering are reported without invented numerical totals or sensationalized estimates.
- The draft's nonsensical vocabulary item “Ghayba (none)” was deleted. The final glossary contains 32 relevant definitions.

## 4. Primary-source study scope and editorial limits

Five independently developed windows cover (1) the Pactum Warmundi, (2) the Itinerarium and Ibn al-Athir on Acre and prisoners, (3) Villehardouin and Niketas Choniates on 1204, (4) the 1229 agreement in imperial, patriarchal, and Arabic reporting, and (5) the *Templar of Tyre* on 1291. Each identifies an episode, source provenance, interpretive situation, explicitly designated editorial paraphrase, limitations, and four analytical questions.

**Publication limitation:** The paraphrases are not verbatim original-language transcriptions or freshly collated critical editions, and individual page/line references were not exhaustively resolved. No inference is made that the original manuscript text was personally consulted when a publisher's description or catalog was used for bibliographic confirmation. A future critical-source edition would require passage-by-passage comparison and exact page citations. This does **not** invalidate the present explicitly labeled historical-text first edition.

Three geography studies are complete *as text-based atlas exercises*; they do not embed copyrighted map plates or claim to be a fully illustrated atlas edition.

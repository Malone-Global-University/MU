## Historical content check

- §§75.1–75.5 distinguish *sharia*, juristic *fiqh*, *kanun*, fatwas, court judgments, and documentary registration; the chapter does not assume uniform application of prescriptions.
- §§75.6–75.8 differentiate scholarly institutions, Sufi networks, waqfs, and specific non-Muslim communal arrangements. The *millet* discussion follows the historiographical challenge to the universal classical-era framework, including the distinct 2026 debate about the term's later use.
- §§75.9–75.12 distinguish *devshirme*, unfree *kul* service, Janissary membership, *sipahi* status, and *timar* revenue assignments; the narrative does not treat military transformation as an unqualified decline caused solely by firearms.
- §§75.13–75.16 address trade and craft organization, regulatory ideals versus practice, coercive slavery, legal manumission, and unequal paths to patronage and mobility.
- §§75.17–75.20 explain women's legally recognized capacities and practical barriers, variation across the empire, petitioning, long-term continuities, and uneven transformation.
- Chapter scope respects neighboring Parts: central political architecture in Chapter 72; local/provincial narratives in 73–74; Safavid Iran in 76–80; extended comparative city and communal history in 81–85.

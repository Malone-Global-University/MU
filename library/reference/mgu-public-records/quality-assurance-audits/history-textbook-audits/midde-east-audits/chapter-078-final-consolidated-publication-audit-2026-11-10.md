## 3. Specific historical corrections and substantive expansions

1. **§78.13, Battle of Torches (1583): FIXED.** The 1583 battle is now situated in northern Shirvan near Baştepe/Derbent rather than incorrectly at Tabriz. The Ottoman seizure of Tabriz in **1585** remains separately identified. Expansion distinguishes Ottoman acquisition of Caucasian strongholds, garrison difficulty, and court histories from more marginal soldiers’ narratives. Supporting reference: *Cambridge History of Turkey*, vol. II, and recent Iranian Studies war scholarship.
2. **§78.12, Prince Bayezid: FIXED.** The revised text explicitly places his execution by Ottoman agents **at Qazvin**, while identifying the **1561/1562 dating divergence**. It does not suggest he was first sent back to Ottoman territory. The chronology in the recent scholarly *Memoirs of Shah Tahmasp I* uses 1562.
3. **§78.4, firearms: QUALIFIED.** Later claims of Qizilbash scorn for firearms are attributed to narrative traditions rather than used as a general causal explanation; artillery, battlefield configuration, command, reserves, and logistics all receive attention. Exact troop and casualty totals remain explicitly uncertain.
4. **§78.12, Amasya: STRENGTHENED.** Negotiation chronology, 29 May 1555 settlement, Sunni-Shiite public ritual restraint, and pilgrimage guarantees are distinguished from later transmission of correspondence; no fabricated verbatim treaty extracts.
5. **§§78.6–78.8, 78.18–78.19: DEVELOPED.** Kurdish hereditary jurisdictions, the variation within Ottoman treatment of Anatolian Qizilbash communities, Caucasian local actors, state classifications, livelihood disruption, diplomacy, and social recovery were substantially expanded. Later ethnic-national labels are not retroactively substituted for period-specific terms.
6. **§§78.9–78.16: COMPREHENSIVELY EXPANDED.** The 1533–1535 Iraq campaign; Ottoman and Safavid strategic constraints; 1548 and 1553–1554 itineraries; the 1555 settlement; 1578–1590 Caucasian campaigns; Abbas’s 1603–1618 recovery, including 1604 mass deportations; the 1623–1639 Iraq war and Baghdad sieges; and limits of Zuhab as a border agreement now receive full, historically differentiated explanation.
7. **§§78.17–78.20: DEVELOPED.** Military recruitment, cash finance, garrison logistics, civilian costs, cross-frontier trade, diplomatic institutions, and subsequent border-demarcation historiography receive expanded synthesis.
8. **Primary-source integrity: IMPROVED.** The four original windows remain *editorial source studies and paraphrases*. Specific manuscripts, editions, or scholarly publication references were supplied in an adjacent documentation note, together with a separate 13-entry source register. No passage is represented as a newly checked original-language quotation.

- **Primary-source collation:** The four windows are attributed editorial analyses and paraphrases, **not manuscript- or edition-page-collated quotations**. Bibliographic verification is selective, not exhaustive, and broad reference entries should not be mistaken for source-by-source proof of every statement.

- **Deployment:** No live website, navigation path, CDN, or external hosting was tested. Breadcrumb and adjacent chapter reading-order text do not imply verified links.
- **Scope:** First-edition scholarly instructional HTML; not a critical diplomatic, Ottoman Turkish, Persian, or Kurdish manuscript edition. New archival discoveries may warrant future editorial revision, but no remaining issue identified here blocks first-edition publication under these limitations.

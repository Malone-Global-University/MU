## 3. Material factual corrections and source verification

1. **1813 versus 1818:** clarified that Egyptian-Ottoman forces recovered the Hijaz by 1813 while destruction of the first Saudi state at Diriyah occurred in 1818; eliminated misleading implication of continuous Saudi rule of the holy cities until 1818.
2. **1904 Saudi–Rashidi fighting:** corrected unqualified “Saudi victory” at al-Bukayriyya to an inconclusive, costly engagement, followed by improved Saudi fortunes in fighting near al-Shinanah; retained 1906 Rawdat Muhanna and Rashidi succession issues.
3. **1902 Riyadh raid:** retained January 1902 and qualified the conflicting participant counts; no unsupported exact troop roster.
4. **Ikhwan:** used approximate 1912–1913 settlement onset rather than attributing the movement's later mature organization to the prewar years.
5. **Da'an 1911:** distinguished negotiated limited Ottoman devolution in Yemeni highlands from a full independence treaty; verified M. Şükrü Hanioğlu, DOI 10.1080/21534764.2018.1529223, *Journal of Arabian Studies* 8(1) (2018), pp. 25–46.
6. **Ibn Saud–Ottoman treaty:** clarified **May 15, 1914**, the vassal/governor formula and reported articles concerning hereditary appointment and administrative/military obligations; referenced British Library India Office record IOR/R/15/1/745, fol. 22r. The 1934 British memorandum is a later documentary intermediary, not mistaken for a contemporaneous treaty transcript.
7. **1913 Gulf convention versus 1914 Aden settlement:** preserved **July 29, 1913 signed/unratified** versus **March 9, 1914 ratified**; cited catalogued India Office collection IOR/L/PS/18/B381/1 and India Office memorandum. Qatar's Ottoman garrison remained until 1915; Britain's treaty with Qatar followed in November 1916.
8. **Oman 1913:** retained May 1913 Salim bin Rashid al-Kharusi's election, October 1913 succession of Taimur bin Faisal; differentiated the later 1920 Seeb agreement.
9. **Gulf commercial evidence:** upgraded the generic trade-record window to dated Lorimer *Gazetteer* (1908) records, including Qatar p. 1533 (817 estimated pearl vessels, approximately 13,000 crew) and Bahrain p. 245; documented limitations and OCR uncertainty.
10. **Iranian petroleum:** retained 1908 discovery, 1909 formation of Anglo-Persian, refinery operations by 1912 in BP's corporate chronology and Britain's 51% stake in 1914; distinguished corporate retrospectives from primary concession text and acknowledged published differences in refinery completion terminology.
11. **Egypt:** retained the essential separation between British military occupation before 1914 and the declaration of a protectorate on **December 18, 1914**, after Ottoman participation in the war.
12. **Bibliography:** corrected Stuart A. Cohen's *British Policy in Mesopotamia, 1903–1914* to Ithaca Press, 1976 (reissued later); distinguished it from Cohen's separate Cambridge journal article.

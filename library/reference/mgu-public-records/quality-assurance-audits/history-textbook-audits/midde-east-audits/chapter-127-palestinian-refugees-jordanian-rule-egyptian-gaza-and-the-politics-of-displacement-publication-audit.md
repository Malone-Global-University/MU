## Historical corrections and expansion decisions

- The 1949 UN Economic Survey Mission figure of approximately 726,000 is differentiated from later UNRWA registration and service figures; its additional borderline category is not assumed to be interchangeable with the administrative data.
- Jordan’s 24 April 1950 parliamentary union is distinguished from the military armistice boundaries, from Palestinian political consent, and from Britain’s qualified recognition concerning Jerusalem.
- Jordan’s 1954 nationality law is presented as a set of legal eligibility provisions rather than a universal and instantaneous grant of identical documents to every displaced person.
- Egypt’s 1955 Basic Law and 1962 Constitutional Order are discussed without inferring independent Palestinian sovereignty or Egyptian annexation.
- The 28 February 1955 Gaza raid is identified through the UNTSO report, including 38 deaths on the Egyptian-controlled side (36 military and two civilian); no all-sides toll is inferred.
- Syria’s Law No. 260 of 1956 is distinguished from full Syrian citizenship, with attention to the rights and limits of its statutory scope.
- Resolution 194 paragraph 11 accurately distinguishes compensation for persons not returning from the separate provision relating to loss of or damage to property.
- The 1953 Sinai development scheme and mid-1950s refugee protests are situated within material needs, political consent, and the fear that resettlement might substitute for return.
- The founding Council of 28 May–2 June 1964 is separated from the later political and military trajectory of the PLO, reserved for Chapter 136.
- The chapter gives independent treatment to women’s roles, school formation, migrant remittances, legal status, camp life, and refugee professional networks in four main host settings.

## Source integrity and limitations

- All twelve Primary Source Windows label their evidence as paraphrase, not direct quotation. Each includes a traceable document link or an openly described later documentary collection.
- UNRWA 1950, 1960 and 1965 numbers are administrative registration series; they cannot be read as an exhaustive census of the displaced population. The pre-1967 Jordan reporting field includes the West Bank.
- The 1950 resolution’s $33.7 million budget (including $20.2 million for direct relief and $13.5 million for works) is an estimate of program requirements, not expenditure verified as paid.
- Contemporary UN and government materials are cross-referenced with archival and later research. Official reporting and institutional self-descriptions remain sources with interests and omissions.
- Some third-party or institutional URLs can change, redirect, or impose access restrictions; the manuscript links to identified document pages, but no claim is made that every external URL was automatically fetched successfully during the final build.
- Interpretive debates are described without placing assertions of equal evidentiary weight on incompatible readings. Historical outcomes and contested claims should remain open to expert editorial evaluation.

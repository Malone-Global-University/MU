## III. Historical verification and targeted corrections

The following checks were independently grounded in publisher, catalog, and scholarly article descriptions, and the final HTML now employs the stated safeguards:

1. **Conquest versus administration:** The text distinguishes the Mamluk defeat in 1516–1517 from the 1525 Egyptian provincial code and from the later legal controversies of the 1550s. The 2024 article by Atçıl and Markiewicz is used for the **waqf controversy**, explicitly not as a verbatim citation of a 1525 Qanunname clause. Reference: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-middle-east-studies/article/sharia-and-governance-in-ottoman-egypt-the-waqf-controversy-in-the-midsixteenth-century/532AD3FC1E9CA69C982EC15AA3AC2303
2. **Fiscal terminology:** *Salyane* is defined as an annual allowance linked to distinctive provincial fiscal arrangements, not the whole financial system reduced to a flat provincial payment. *Timar* remains a right to assigned revenues, not private landed ownership. Comparative provincial background: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-history-of-turkey/semiautonomous-forces-in-the-arab-provinces/4C7C8EFF31CCC15AD687D3C828350FEF
3. **Syrian imperial orders:** Linda T. Darling’s analysis of the *mühimme* orders from 1544–1572 explicitly identifies concerns with money, military personnel, and administration. The chapter accurately characterizes the registers as sources for orders and problems, not proof of implementation. Reference: https://openbooks.ffzg.unizg.hr/index.php/FFpress/catalog/download/131/216/9753%3Finline%3D1
4. **Kurdish agreements:** The text describes specific negotiations by Idris-i Bidlisi, avoids a fictitious uniform grant to all Kurdish rulers, and treats *hükümet* and *yurtluk-ocaklık* in an evolving documentary context. Nilay Özok-Gündoğan’s chapter and book verify the importance of differentiated *temessüknâme* arrangements. Reference: https://academic.oup.com/edinburgh-scholarship-online/book/51557/chapter-abstract/418151357
5. **Sharafnama:** The text dates composition c. 1596–1599, identifies its author as the ruler of Bitlis, and avoids presenting it as nationalist writing in a modern sense. The Austrian Academy of Sciences describes the 1860–1862 Veliaminov-Zernov reference edition and cautions about the lack of a complete critical edition. Reference: https://www.oeaw.ac.at/isa/forschung/forschungsgebiete-des-isa/naher-osten/sharaf-nama-amir-sharaf-khan-bidlisi-a-persian-source-for-the-history-of-kurds
6. **Hijaz:** Ottoman sovereignty over Mecca and Medina in 1517 is characterized as recognition and political accommodation; the text **does not** claim armed Ottoman conquest of the holy cities or a documented ceremonial transfer of Abbasid caliphal office in 1517. Nir Shafir confirms the importance of Damascus and the pilgrimage road. Reference: https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/709169
7. **Yemen:** The final chapter distinguishes the 1538 Red Sea intervention, Sana’a 1547, al-Mutahhar’s resistance, the 1569–1571 campaigns and the end of the first Ottoman administration in **1635–1636**, acknowledging conventional use of 1635 and different descriptions of the final withdrawal. A modern study of the formation of Ottoman Yemen: https://www.academia.edu/94653528/The_Integration_of_Yemen_into_the_Ottoman_Bureaucratic_and_Central_Judicial_System_1872_1918_
8. **Seventeenth-century Iraq:** Safavid capture of Baghdad in 1623, Ottoman reconquest in 1638, and settlement in 1639 are distinguished; the text does not equate these events with uniform collapse across all Arab provinces. Comparative framework: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/arabs-of-the-ottoman-empire-15161918/establishment-and-survival-of-ottoman-rule-in-the-arab-lands-15161798/143A29ABC0352B40E526ED1CBC721ECE
9. **Political-household chronology:** The household-based military elite is treated as a developing Ottoman-Egyptian phenomenon. Later Qazdağlı preeminence is **not** projected into the sixteenth-century Egyptian code. Reference: https://nes.princeton.edu/publications/politics-households-ottoman-egypt-rise-qazdaglis
10. **No teleological decline thesis:** The chapter acknowledges major regional setbacks and institutional adaptations without presenting the entire empire as following one predetermined seventeenth-century collapse. See Jane Hathaway, *The Arab Lands under Ottoman Rule*, 2nd ed. (2020): https://www.routledge.com/The-Arab-Lands-under-Ottoman-Rule-1516-1800/Hathaway/p/book/9781138643444

**Substantive verdict:** The full text presents no identified historical contradiction requiring a further general rewrite after this consolidated pass. Publication-critical factual dates and administrative claims received targeted verification. This is not a claim of exhaustive verification of every contextual statement against primary sources.

It would be misleading to claim that original pages, archival signatures, readings, or quotations have been verified. The four windows openly distinguish this limit from the historical argument they support.

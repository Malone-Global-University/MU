### 2. Historically substantive changes directly applied

1. **Suez and British East India Company, 1768–1786.** §82.9 and both chronology exercises now distinguish locally invited Egypt–India Company-connected commerce from **1768**, the British commercial and navigation negotiations of the **1770s**, explicit **Ottoman prohibition of British navigation to Suez reported in 1777**, and renewed consular arrangements in **1786**. They no longer suggest that the negotiations permanently opened the northern Red Sea. The Red Sea paragraph also distinguishes formal Ottoman restrictions from the intermittent admission of European vessels at Jeddah and the earlier negotiated access at Mocha. Key evidence: British Library IOR/G/17 catalogue and Abdulmennan M. Altıntaş, *Journal of Global History* (2026).
2. **French capitulations.** §82.8 identifies a source-dependent **1594/1597** variation, instead of treating one specific unqualified list as exclusively correct. The BnF gives 1594; alternate diplomatic chronologies include 1597; 1569, 1581, 1604, 1673, and 1740 remain as clearly identifiable renewal or negotiation landmarks. The disputed traditional 1536 text remains explicitly qualified.
3. **Bandar Abbas English customs.** §82.12 and the Hormuz source window now state that **half** of customs revenue was a **promised English Company entitlement** following the 1622 operation, not the amount paid. Safavid officials seldom paid the promised half in full; the document does not fabricate exact receipts.
4. **1757 Damascus Hajj caravan attack.** Retained; the event is corroborated in the specialist literature. No unsupported figures, perpetrator identifications, or casualty estimates introduced.
5. **Surat and Gujarati merchants.** Broad source-supported descriptions retained without introducing page-unverified individual merchant biographies.

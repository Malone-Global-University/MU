## III. Substantive factual corrections and source verification

### 1. Yemeni unification and southern grievances

**Corrected.** The first draft asserted that unification was not demanded by a mobilized public. The revision distinguishes genuine popular enthusiasm for unity in 1990 from elite-negotiated arrangements and the consequences of the 1994 civil war. It also explains the distinct former southern state institutions and later development of al-Hirak. The revised discussion does not equate all southerners with the STC.

### 2. The 2015 intervention and Security Council Resolution 2216

**Corrected.** The coalition campaign began **March 26, 2015**; Resolution **2216** was adopted **April 14, 2015**. The revision distinguishes the resolution's demands, sanctions and arms restrictions from legal arguments advanced for the earlier intervention. It avoids claiming that the resolution retrospectively or prospectively authorized the original use of force.

- Source: [UN Security Council, Resolution 2216 (2015)](https://main.un.org/securitycouncil/en/s/res/2216-%282015%29-0).

### 3. Yemen's 2022 truce

**Verified and developed.** The truce came into force **April 2, 2022**, initially for two months, and included a pause in offensive operations, arrangements for fuel shipments, flights from Sanaa and discussion of roads around Taiz. Formal expiration in October 2022 is distinguished from the period of lower violence that followed; the chapter does not call the truce a final peace settlement.

- Source: [UN Special Envoy, original truce statement, April 1, 2022](https://osesgy.unmissions.org/en/speeches-and-statements/press-statement-un-special-envoy-yemen-hans-grundberg-two-month).
- Source: [UN Special Envoy, truce implementation chronology](https://osesgy.unmissions.org/en/node/15891).

### 4. The Al-Ula declaration and 2023 Saudi-Iranian statement

**Verified and developed.** The January 5, 2021 declaration is treated as an intra-GCC political commitment and reconciliation framework, not a complete resolution of every dispute. The March 10, 2023 China-hosted statement is treated as restoration of diplomatic relations and a declaration of noninterference, not a Yemen peace treaty.

- Source: [GCC Secretariat, Al-Ula Declaration, January 5, 2021](https://gcc41.org/press-releases/the-al-ula-declaration).
- Source: [Chinese Foreign Ministry, joint Saudi-Iranian-Chinese statement, March 10, 2023](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/eng/gjhdq_665435/2675_665437/2878_663746/2879_663748/202303/t20230311_11039241.html).

### 5. Humanitarian statistics

**Verified with qualifications.** The revision uses the UN's **2026 planning estimates** of **22.3 million people requiring humanitarian aid/protection** and **18.3 million people facing acute food insecurity**. The categories are not presented as war casualties or as exact numbers receiving aid. The underlying indicators, coverage dates, funding gaps and limits of the data are acknowledged.

- Source: [UN Yemen, 2026 Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan](https://yemen.un.org/ar/node/312711).
- Source: [UNICEF Yemen, Economic and Social Monitor, August 2026](https://www.unicef.org/yemen/documents/yemen-economic-and-social-monitor-issue-2-august-2026).

### 6. STC territorial reversal and political reorganization in 2026

**Corrected and developed.** The revision describes the **December 2025** southern expansion and **January 2026** PLC counteroffensive; the removal of STC figures from the PLC; the UAE withdrawal announcement; the disputed dissolution declaration; and political activity under the later name **Transitional Council of South Arabia**. It distinguishes military defeat, disputed organizational dissolution and continued southern separatist demands.

- Source: [UK House of Commons Library, Yemen 2026, October 5, 2026](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/CBP-10427/).

### 7. Mid-2026 escalation and military attribution

**Corrected and expanded.** The account distinguishes a June 2026 Houthi announcement, a claimed July attack, September intensification in Taiz and Hudaydah, and October–November attacks on Saudi infrastructure. It does not claim that all renewed conflict began in September. Particular attacks are identified as claimed or attributed where the public evidence does not independently settle launch origin or responsibility. The 2019 Abqaiq attack is likewise presented as a contested attribution rather than proof of a Houthi launch from Yemen.

- Source: [UN Special Envoy, September 10, 2026 Security Council briefing](https://osesgy.unmissions.org/en/news/briefing-by-the-un-special-envoy-for-yemen-hans-grundberg-to-the-security-council-2).
- Source: [UK House of Commons Library, 2026 conflict timeline](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/CBP-10427/).

### 8. UN Security Council documents in autumn 2026

**Corrected.** The source window now distinguishes the Council's **October 15 presidential statement S/PRST/2026/4** from **November 12 Resolution 2837**, which renewed targeted Yemen sanctions and the expert-panel mandate. The revised source window does not use a report about a resolution as a substitute for identifying the resolution itself.

- Source: [UN Mandate Registry, S/PRST/2026/4](https://mandates.un.org/mandate/S/PRST/2026/4).
- Source: [UN Digital Library, Resolution 2837 (2026)](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4132761).

### 9. Provisional casualty figures

**Corrected by removing false precision.** The first draft used a precise November 2026 casualty aggregate with imprecise institutional attribution. The revised narrative does **not** treat that aggregate as a settled toll and explicitly instructs readers to distinguish the source, geographic scope and methodology of emergency-health counts. This is safer for a historical chapter written during an ongoing escalation.

## 3. Factual corrections incorporated into the chapter

### 3.1 B'Tselem fatalities and minors (Section 153.7)

**Original issue:** The original sentence suggested that all **1,317 Palestinian minors** belonged to the **2,996 fatalities classified as not participating in hostilities**. These are different categories.

**Correction made:** The revised narrative states that B'Tselem counted **6,371 Palestinians killed by Israeli security forces from September 2000 to September 2010**, **including 1,317 minors**, and **separately** classified **at least 2,996 people** as not participating in hostilities at the time of death. The difference between age classification and participation status is explicit. It also states that the 2000–2010 series includes events beyond the chapter's 2007 cutoff.

**Verification:** B'Tselem, [“10 Years to the Second Intifada — Summary of Data,” 27 September 2010](https://www.btselem.org/press_releases/20100927).

### 3.2 Palestinian election figures (Section 153.17 and source window)

**Original issue:** National-list percentages were approximate and uncited.

**Correction made:** The revised manuscript uses **74 seats for Change and Reform (Hamas), 45 for Fatah, and 13 for other lists and independents**. The list-vote percentages are now **44.45%** and **41.43%**, respectively. Added prose explains the mixed electoral system: 66 nationally elected proportional seats and 66 district seats. Parliamentary seat shares are not substituted for popular-vote shares.

**Verification:** [Palestinian Central Elections Commission final results, 29 January 2006](https://www.elections.ps/tabid/146/ArtMID/9115/ArticleID/889/language/en-US/Default.aspx); [IFES Election Guide](https://electionguide.org/elections/id/1433/); and the [NDI/Carter Center final report](https://www.ndi.org/sites/default/files/sites/default/files/2068_ps_elect_012506_5_1.pdf).

### 3.3 Gaza settlements and settlement land (Section 153.14)

**Original issue:** The chapter called all **21 evacuated settlements** Gaza settlements and attributed approximately **one-third of Gaza** to the Gush Katif settlement bloc, without separating settlement land from other restricted territory.

**Correction made:** The text now distinguishes **17 settlements in Gaza** from **four in the northern West Bank** and attributes an estimated **15–20% of Gaza's territory** to the 17 Gaza settlements in the World Bank's pre-withdrawal assessment. It notes separately that additional military and restricted areas existed and were not identical to settlement land.

**Verification:** World Bank, [*Disengagement, the Palestinian Economy and the Settlements*](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/998471468176641197/pdf/404600ENGLISH0GZ0Disengagement01PUBLIC1.pdf), section IV, particularly paragraphs 57–58. This distinction is central to the chapter's territorial analysis and was an additional factual defect discovered during the final pass.

### 3.4 Area B's overlapping security responsibilities (Historical Context and Section 153.10)

**Original issue:** The initial wording treated Area B as Palestinian civil administration with undifferentiated Israeli security control.

**Correction made:** The historical context and Section 153.10 now distinguish Palestinian responsibility for civil administration and public order among Palestinians from Israel's retained overriding security responsibility regarding Israelis and threats of terrorism. Area A and Area C are retained as contextually approximate territorial categories, not timeless or mutually exclusive descriptions of actual access after 2000.

**Verification basis:** Oslo II's differentiated civil, public-order, and security responsibilities. The chapter's Chapter 152 cross-reference remains appropriate.

### 3.5 Road Map chronology (Source Window I)

**Original issue:** The source study said that the Road Map was issued after the Iraq War had ended.

**Correction made:** The text now explains that the Road Map was presented on **30 April 2003**, after the U.S.-led invasion of Iraq and the fall of Baghdad, **while the occupation and subsequent armed conflict continued**.

**Verification:** United Nations Peacemaker, [*A Performance-Based Road Map to a Permanent Two-State Solution*](https://peacemaker.un.org/en/node/8984), 30 April 2003; UN Security Council document S/2003/529.

### 3.6 Beit Hanoun shelling (Section 153.19)

**Original issue:** The figure of “about nineteen” civilian deaths was given from memory.

**Correction made:** The revised section states that **19 Palestinian civilians** were killed in the **8 November 2006** shelling and attributes the figure to UN OCHA.

**Verification:** [UN OCHA Humanitarian Update, November 2006](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-200593/).

### 3.7 June 2007 factional fatalities (Section 153.20)

**Original issue:** “Well over a hundred” deaths was vague and unattributed.

**Correction made:** The revised section attributes **161 deaths**, including **41 civilians**, during the decisive June fighting to Palestinian Centre for Human Rights figures cited by Human Rights Watch. The text distinguishes that fighting episode from broader monthly counts of Palestinian factional violence.

**Verification:** Human Rights Watch, [*Internal Fight: Palestinian Abuses in Gaza and the West Bank*](https://www.hrw.org/report/2008/07/29/internal-fight/palestinian-abuses-gaza-and-west-bank), July 2008.

### 3.8 World Bank, OCHA, and barrier context

The manuscript now links its humanitarian-economic and spatial analyses to specifically identifiable sources. The World Bank's 2003 economic study documents sharp reductions in Palestinian income and elevated unemployment during the early Intifada; OCHA's 2007 barrier map reports a total approved barrier route of approximately **721 kilometers**. The manuscript avoids implying that the entire approved route had been constructed at the observation date.

Documentary foundations: [World Bank, *Twenty-Seven Months*](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-205621/); [OCHA, 2007 Barrier Route Map](https://www.ochaopt.org/content/map-west-bank-barrier-route-june-2007); [OCHA, *Israeli-Palestinian Fatalities Since 2000*](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-208380/).

## 5. Interpretation and evidentiary safeguards

**Uprising and violence:** The chapter separates structural causes from immediate triggering events and distinguishes demonstrations, attacks on civilians, and armed hostilities. It identifies and analyzes competing claims about the uprising's planning rather than resolving them through retrospective assumption. Accounts of Jenin acknowledge documented harms without reproducing unsubstantiated early massacre estimates as proven events.

**Civilian history:** Israeli and Palestinian civilian experiences are presented in parallel but not collapsed into a false equivalence of legal status, geography, or sources of danger. Individual casualty totals are described by source, interval, and classification. The updated B'Tselem paragraph particularly avoids conflating minors with the status “not participating in hostilities.”

**Law and the barrier:** The chapter distinguishes the ICJ's advisory opinion from the Israeli High Court's binding judicial review of particular barrier segments. Neither legal framework is presented as an identical form of judgment to the other. The geography study is retained as instructional material; actual map artwork is intentionally reserved for the user's later insertion and is **not** a deficiency or blocker.

**Gaza disengagement:** The chapter explains that the evacuation of settlements and permanent ground troops did not automatically resolve disputes about external control, sovereignty, or the application of occupation law. Its economic discussion now accounts for crossings, infrastructure, and productive assets rather than treating land handover as a complete economic transition.

**2006 election and 2007 split:** The chapter preserves the difference between electoral victory, formation of a government, international financial conditionality, factional conflict, and armed territorial takeover. The closing sections distinguish Palestinian constitutional disputes from the coercive control each rival faction exercised in its territory.

## 7. Remaining editorial limitations

No publication-critical factual discrepancy was left knowingly unresolved in the checked passages. As with any substantial historical textbook, exhaustive independent confirmation of every interpretive sentence is not possible from a targeted scholarly audit alone; the chapter's source links and bibliography support future checking and expansion. Some sources are retrospective or use different definitions, and the chapter identifies important examples. The completed chapter does not yet contain graphical maps, by the publisher's standing production arrangement. The functioning of live shared site components and deployment-specific metadata requires a later website integration check, not another historical-content drafting pass.

The existing chapter's CSS, sidebar, header, feature-box classes, and scroll-highlighting script were retained, with small additions for documentary references and the shared navigation/footer integration. Exact byte-level correspondence with the original Chapter 2 model was not claimed.

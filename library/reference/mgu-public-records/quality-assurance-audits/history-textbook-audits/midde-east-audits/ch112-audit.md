## 3. Historical and documentary audit

**General conclusion:** The main event sequence is consistent with the checked primary documents and major scholarly literature. No major claim in the final chapter is presented as an authenticated quotation unless its transcription was directly established. Identifiable original records are linked in each documentary window, and the text differentiates contemporary statements from interpretations developed later.

1. **Hussein–McMahon:** The 24 October 1915 reservation concerning Mersin, Alexandretta and western portions of the districts of Damascus, Homs, Hama and Aleppo is explicitly distinguished from later British and Arab interpretations. Jerusalem's Ottoman administrative status and the translation/meaning of “districts” are explained. The chapter does not assert that Palestine was indisputably included or excluded.
2. **Arab Revolt:** Initial operations near Medina on 5 June 1916, public Mecca proclamation on 10 June, and the later printed proclamation (26/27 June depending on the document) are distinguished. The original Arabic pamphlet is identified through the Library of Congress, not misrepresented as a personally collated facsimile quotation. Arab command, tribal coalitions, Ottoman Arab loyalists, and British military support are differentiated.
3. **Sykes–Picot:** The 16 May 1916 Anglo–French exchange is analyzed by blue/red direct-control zones, A/B Arab-state or confederation zones with French/British priority, and proposed international administration in the brown zone, with Haifa/Acre, railway, water, and customs provisions. The chapter explicitly denies that this map itself established modern international boundaries.
4. **Secret-treaty disclosure:** Bolshevik publication in late November 1917 and the *Manchester Guardian* report of 26 November are separated from the 2 November Balfour Declaration; the earlier draft's impression of immediate consecutive-day disclosure has been corrected.
5. **Balfour Declaration:** The official 2 November 1917 Cabinet-approved policy letter is treated as a policy declaration and not an international treaty conferring sovereignty. Its national-home pledge and differentiated protections concerning non-Jewish civil/religious rights versus Jews' rights and political status elsewhere are analyzed. No explicit promise of immediate Jewish statehood is attributed to the text.
6. **1918 reassurances:** The Hogarth Message, Declaration to the Seven, and Anglo–French Declaration are differentiated by author/audience, political scope, and legal form. The 7/8/9 November date variants in archival issue and subsequent citation are openly qualified.
7. **Damascus:** The October 1918 provisional political authority is separated from internationally recognized independence; the later conflict with France is left to the postwar narrative.

## Historical verification and factual controls

Historical claims were checked selectively against primary-document transcriptions and specialist scholarship. This is not a claim to a newly collated archival critical edition.

1. **Treaty of London (May 30, 1913):** Distinct provisions have been reconstructed in §107.8 and Source Window I: Article II cession west of the Enos–Midia line subject to the Albanian exception, Article III Albania and Great Power decisions, Article IV Crete, and Article V most Aegean islands and Mount Athos. The Enos–Midia line is expressly *not* treated as the final Ottoman–Bulgarian frontier. Basis: published treaty text, https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Treaty_of_London_(1913) and Greek Ministry https://www.mfa.gr/en/international-conventions/.
2. **Separate 1913 treaties:** The Ottoman Empire is correctly excluded from the signatories of the Treaty of Bucharest (August 10), and included in its separate treaties of Constantinople (September 29) and Athens (November 14). Basis: FRUS 1913 https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1913/ch109 and Greek MFA treaty inventory.
3. **Salonica:** Greek military entry on November 8 with surrender formalities extending into November 9 is distinguished in Chapter Opening and §107.7, rather than presented as an entirely simultaneous process. Comparison: documented contemporary surrender-related studies.
4. **Balkan War military critique:** Staff Major Asım (later Asım Gündüz) is identified through Edward J. Erickson and Aksakal bibliographic research, not passed off as a newly checked Ottoman Turkish original. Military weaknesses are treated jointly with Balkan enemy capabilities and tactical decisions. Erickson, *Ottoman Army Effectiveness in World War I*, preview https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781135984571_A24666064/preview-9781135984571_A24666064.pdf.
5. **Civilian violence:** §107.11 discusses crimes and coerced displacement affecting multiple communities, separates identifiable perpetrators and places in principle, notes consequences of Ottoman reconquest as well as Balkan occupation, and explicitly cautions that the Carnegie Commission's material requires corroboration. Public 1914 report index: https://promacedonia.org/en/carnegie/index.html.
6. **Refugee figures:** §107.12 uses UNHCR's broad estimate of 0.5–1 million displaced persons with categorical limitations, not an exact total presented as a complete Ottoman census. Source: *People Forced to Flee*, https://data.unhcr.org/en/documents/details/91169; scholarly treatment by Üngör https://doi.org/10.7228/manchester/9781784994419.003.0015.
7. **Ottomanism and Turkism:** §107.15 and Debate II explicitly distinguish postwar changes from a supposed automatic or total replacement of Ottomanism by Turkism, following Ebru Boyar, https://doi.org/10.1080/19436149.2014.905081.
8. **Arab Congress:** §§107.18–107.19 distinguish the Paris meeting (18–24 June in Kayalı's convention; some other sources give the substantive meetings as 18–23 June), its reformist rather than uniformly separatist demands, the Midhat Şükrü negotiating agreement, and later reduced or disputed implementation. Kayalı, https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/arabs-and-young-turks-ottomanism-arabism-and-islamism-in-the-ottoman-empire-1908-1918.html.
9. **Türk Yurdu:** Source Window V identifies the 12 June 1913 volume 4 issue 17 in the Turkish National Library's catalog; it expressly does not imply that an article's Ottoman text was freshly translated or its contents read in full. Catalog https://dijital-kutuphane.mkutup.gov.tr/tr/periodicals/catalog/details/650.

## Disclosed limitations and publication scope

1. **Template comparison:** The designated Chapter 2 source HTML was not attached for this turn. The available Chapter 106 HTML was used as a close MGU structural and CSS reference; exact Chapter 2 byte-for-byte CSS/structural identity cannot be certified.
2. **Primary-source collation:** Several studies are source-specific but mediated by modern scholarship; they are labeled as such. Neither original-language manuscript collation nor original newspaper facsimile translation has been claimed.
3. **Bibliography:** Key references and landmark sources received selective web verification. The twenty external links were not exhaustively clicked from a deployed MGU host, and third-party websites may change their availability.
4. **Maps:** Historical geography work is text-guided; it does not include a newly authenticated atlas plate or scaled GIS map.
5. **Deployment:** The final HTML was tested in local Chromium, but actual deployment to maloneuniversity.org, domain-specific routing, shared site components, and future browser behavior were not tested.
6. **Narrative:** The 12,000–14,000 direct narrative target is fulfilled. Remaining opportunities for a future expanded critical edition (collated translations, annotated cartography) are not publication-critical for the explicitly scoped first-edition scholarly textual HTML.

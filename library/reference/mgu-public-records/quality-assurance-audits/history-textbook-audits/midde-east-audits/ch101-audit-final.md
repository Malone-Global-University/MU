## Scholarly argument and chapter-plan compliance

**Division I (§§101.1–101.4)** develops the Nahda as an intellectual renewal grounded in earlier Arabic scholarship, Egyptian translation, Beirut institutions, and scholarly networks beyond Cairo/Beirut. The movement is not presented as a passive import or the sudden beginning of Arabic learning.

**Division II (§§101.5–101.8)** examines dictionaries, grammar, rhetoric, classical manuscript resources, al-Bustani, the Yazijis, al-Shidyaq, theater, and Zaydan's historical novels. Historical novels are treated as evidence for their publication era, not as eyewitness evidence for their fictional settings.

**Division III (§§101.9–101.12)** explains periodical argument, scientific translation, Al-Muqtataf and Al-Hilal, women as intellectual authors, and the specific institutional and professional consequences of the 1882 Syrian Protestant College controversy.

**Division IV (§§101.13–101.16)** differentiates al-Afghani, Abduh, and Rida, distinguishes Al-Urwa al-Wuthqa (1884) from Al-Manar (1898), reconstructs the Antun–Abduh dispute, and treats women and social reform beyond male reformist advocacy alone.

**Division V (§§101.17–101.20)** distinguishes al-Bustani's Syrian patriotism, Ottomanism, cultural Arabism, Islamic solidarity, and administrative decentralization. Al-Kawakibi's critique of despotism is not recast as straightforward separatism. The chapter ends at the 1908 constitutional transition, reserving events after 1908 for Chapters 105 and 107.

**Boundary controls:** Chapter 99 retains material publishing and public-sphere institutions; Chapter 101 analyzes the intellectual *contents*. Chapter 100 concerns Armenian politics and massacres; 102 turns to Zionism and late Ottoman Palestine; 105 and 107 retain revolutionary and post-1908 nationalist developments. Cultural Arabism is not equated with sovereign separatism, and post-1918 political arrangements are not projected backward.

## Chronological and documentary audit

1. **Al-Tahtawi and Egypt:** 1826 Paris mission and 1834 publication retained. School of Languages treated as a staged institution-building development in **1835–1836**: accounts differ by establishment, formal opening, directorship, and naming. The revised manuscript does not falsely claim that every stage had a single universally accepted date. Supporting context: <https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23311983.2023.2188778> and <https://doi.org/10.1080/00309230.2016.1229346>.
2. **Al-Muqtataf:** founded in Beirut **1876**. Clarifies scholarly dating of editors' relocation around **1884** versus journal production in Cairo from **1885**, drawing on the printing-history study by Hala Auji. The relevant original draft phrase is narrowly clarified.
3. **Al-Bustani:** *Nafir Suriyya* pamphlets **September 1860–April 1861**, verified by University of California Press 2019 text edition and Andrew Arsan's 2021 historical interpretation. Civic/territorial Syrian patriotism is not treated as a demand for an independent modern Syrian state.
4. **Darwin controversy:** Syrian Protestant College 1882, Edwin Lewis's address, institutional departures, **thirteen students expelled**, petition, substitute teaching and credential difficulties verified in the AUB Libraries online exhibition: <https://online-exhibit.aub.edu.lb/exhibits/show/caps--gowns--and-beginnings---/commencements-through-challeng/commencement-1882--darwin-issu>.
5. **Al-Fatat:** Hind Nawfal, Alexandria, **November 20, 1892**, documented through Women and Memory Forum's republication: <https://wmf.org.eg/en/publication/al-fatat-magazine/>. The source study does not fabricate a transcription of the inaugural Arabic editorial.
6. **Antun–Abduh:** 1902–1903 exchange over Ibn Rushd, religious and civil authority. The source window identifies Antun's *Al-Jamiʿa* vol. 3, no. 8 (1902), pp. 517–540 and Mohammad Magout's 2024 annotated selection/translation, pp. 203–215: <https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783111254067-036/html>.
7. **Al-Kawakibi:** Distinguishes disputed/approximate early circulation around 1899 from *Umm al-Qura*'s demonstrable **1902–1903** serialization in *Al-Manar*. Does not represent the fictional Mecca congress as an actual meeting or collapse composition, circulation, serialization, and later editions into one date.
8. **Works and genres:** Dates for *Al-Hilal* (1892), *Al-Manar* (1898), *Al-Urwa al-Wuthqa* (1884), *Al-Jawaib* (1861), and *Al-Jinan* (1870) remain historically situated. Writings on women's education are not made dependent on the appearance of Qasim Amin's 1899 book.

## Four developed source windows: degree of collation

- **Window 1 — *Nafir Suriyya*:** identifies the eleven-pamphlet series, 2019 Hanssen/Safieddine translation, editorial setting, civic argument, limitations, and four questions. The description is explicitly an attributed editorial paraphrase, not a supposed original-language quotation.
- **Window 2 — SPC 1882 petition:** identifies the petition and relevant AUB archive exhibition; explains the event, institutional positions, credential consequences, and what a petition does not prove. The petition text is **not** presented as individually collated/transcribed.
- **Window 3 — *Al-Fatat*:** dates the first issue, identifies Nawfal and Women and Memory Forum's series republication, and distinguishes a reliable collection description from an unverified transcription of the first editorial. Source reception and circulation are not assumed.
- **Window 4 — Antun–Abduh:** identifies a contemporary article by journal volume and pages and a recoverable modern annotated translation; provides differentiated analytical positions and limitations without inventing a quote.

**Method:** The four windows are documentary studies and attributed editorial paraphrases. Their stated source identifications are recoverable, but the chapter makes no claim to comprehensive collation of all Arabic original issues, page-by-page facsimile comparison, or permissions to reproduce facsimiles.

## Disclosed limits to publication approval

1. Source-window paraphrases and documentary descriptions have identifiable editions or holdings but are **not** fully collated original-language quotations. Further philological publication could revisit exact passages; no original quotations were invented.
2. Both geography studies are **text-guided**, requiring historical atlas plates for a cartographic edition. The document does not claim to include embedded maps.
3. The sixteen-entry source register includes modern source-edition witnesses, online archives, scholarly interpretations, and some bibliographic-only works. A register link is not proof of exhaustive page-by-page verification for every statement.
4. The locked Chapter 100 MGU HTML shell and script were used as the donor. Byte-exact comparison with the original Chapter 2 template was not independently performed.
5. Live website deployment, permissions for archival image/facsimile reproduction, and server/CDN behavior have not been tested.

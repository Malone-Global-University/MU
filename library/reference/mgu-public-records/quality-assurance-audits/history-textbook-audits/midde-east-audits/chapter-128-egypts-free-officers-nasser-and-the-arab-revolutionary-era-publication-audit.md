## Significant historical corrections and decisions

- Distinguishes the July 1952 military seizure, Farouk's abdication on 26 July, and the 18 June 1953 proclamation of the republic.
- Differentiates Muhammad Naguib's public presidential role, the RCC's effective authority and Nasser's formal presidency beginning in 1956.
- Corrects Muslim Brotherhood chronology: formal dissolution in **January 1954**, before the **October 1954** assassination attempt and subsequent expanded crackdown.
- Explains the 1952 landownership ceiling of **200 feddans per individual** with family-related allowances, the 1961 reduction to 100, and later 1969 legislation as explicitly outside the chapter's principal period. Land acquired, land actually redistributed and beneficiaries are not treated as interchangeable.
- Names the Kafr al-Dawwar executions of **Mustafa Khamis and Muhammad al-Baqari**; retains uncertainty concerning contested accusations and trial process. The contemporary Le Monde report is distinguished from full Egyptian trial records.
- Separates Aswan High Dam planning, **1960 construction**, Nubian displacement, and eventual environmental impacts (some documented after 1965). Links UNESCO's monuments campaign to a distinct critique of the treatment of living Nubian communities.
- Distinguishes expanded Egyptian schooling from pre-1952 free-education reforms; documents women's 1956 electoral eligibility and first women parliamentarians in 1957 while noting administrative and political constraints.
- Explains retained maintenance/emergency-access provisions of the **19 October 1954 Anglo-Egyptian Canal Zone Agreement** instead of claiming that evacuation immediately ended all treaty-based strategic provisions.
- Identifies **Operation Black Arrow** on 28 February 1955 and the contemporaneous UNTSO count of **38 fatalities on the Egyptian-controlled side (36 military, two civilian)**, without presenting it as an all-side total.
- Distinguishes **nationalization of the Suez Canal Company** (26 July 1956) from sovereignty over the physical canal, obligations concerning navigation, and the separate 1956 invasion in Chapter 132.
- Distinguishes Bandung (1955), Eastern-bloc arms procurement, and the later institutionalized Non-Aligned Movement; nonalignment is not represented as absolute neutrality.
- Reserves detailed history of the UAR, Yemen and the 1967 war for Chapters 133, 134 and 135.

## Sources, verification and limitations

- Twelve source windows identify contemporary records, legal texts, institutional documents or separately identified contemporary reporting. **Every source narrative is explicitly labeled as a paraphrase, not a purported verbatim quotation.** One National Charter source window links to a bibliographic catalogue rather than publicly reproducing the entire edition; that limitation is disclosed in the manuscript.
- The agrarian-reform retrospective numerical table summarizes longer-term cumulative distribution; it is not falsely attributed in full to 1952–1965. The other quantitative tables distinguish dated legal ceilings, earlier schooling access, and 1956–1957 women's political inclusion rather than producing synthetic unsupported indexes.
- The 24 reference links include contemporary diplomatic records (FRUS), a treaty text (UNTS), Egyptian legal materials, contemporary news reporting, official institutional sources, and later scholarly research. Not all external URLs can be guaranteed continuously accessible and individual URLs are not a substitute for archival edition-level authentication.
- The manuscript is evidence-backed and factually revised but remains open to expert peer review of contested interpretations, historiographic framing, and source translations. The absence of fabricated quotations is not proof of exhaustive primary-source collation.

## Map provenance

The three cartographic studies use **original generated Basemap coastlines and selected approximate geographic points**. They are locator/teaching schematics, not a depiction of surveyed land reform parcels, 1950s troop dispositions, exact Nubian village boundaries, or quantified diplomatic flows. The map graphics are embedded as base64 PNG images, so the HTML renders without accompanying image files. Source-type and geographic limitations appear in each map's caption. External documentary reference links still require connectivity.

## Completed editorial revisions

1. **Fixed authoritative assignment:** incorrect Part XI changed to Part XII throughout displayed chapter metadata. Chapter title, chapter sequence, Volume III, and Chapter 48 numbering retained.
2. **Rebuilt all 20 main sections:** sustained explanatory prose developed for sources, four-city comparison, housing, street life, kinship, marriage, women's property, gender, childhood, slavery, concubinage, qiyan, Zanj rebellion, free labor, material culture, entertainment, religious communities, care, war, and synthesis.
3. **Historical source criticism strengthened:** explicitly distinguishes law from practice, satire from documentary evidence, elite representation from popular life, later Geniza evidence from earlier centuries, and slavery's coercion from exceptional mobility narratives.
4. **Corrected chronology:** early foundations of Basra and Fustat precede Baghdad; introduced key Abbasid and Samarra events in chronological order; distinguished the 871 Basra disaster from the 883 defeat of the rebels.
5. **Developed the Zanj narrative:** represented coerced labor, coalition complexity, waterways and military logistics, civilian consequences, and perspectives of modern historians. Avoided conflating all rebels with an undifferentiated ethnoracial or legal group.
6. **Expanded institutional and social analysis:** differentiated marriage and mahr, umm walad doctrinal nuance, women's property claims and practical constraints, changing religious tax positions, markets and hisba chronology, and uncertain hospital provision.
7. **Improved back matter:** 12-paragraph summary, 5-paragraph reflection, larger analytical exercises, 4 writing prompts, a 12-entry scholarly register and explanatory primary-source limitations.
8. **Navigation and presentation:** inserted missing Writing Prompts anchor in sidebar, inserted Sources & Further Reading link, retained the existing CSS / sidebar / layout, and tested responsive browser behavior.

## Historical verification — selected externally checked sources

- Adam Ali, “Zanj Revolt in the Abbasid Caliphate (Iraq)” (Oxford Research Encyclopedia of African History, 2022), DOI `10.1093/acrefore/9780190277734.013.933` — supports coercive labor, revolt framework.
- Philip Grant, “Infrastructures of Contention: The Zanj Rebellion (255–69/869–83)” — additional environmental and logistical historiography; only publicly surfaced extract considered, not a complete critical source collation.
- Pernilla Myrne, “A Jariya’s Prospects in Abbasid Baghdad” (2017), DOI `10.1093/oso/9780190622183.003.0004` — supports caution about literary accounts of Inan and enslaved women.
- Matthew S. Gordon, “Abbasid Courtesans and the Question of Social Mobility” (2017), DOI `10.1093/oso/9780190622183.003.0003` — supports wrongful-enslavement motifs and social mobility critique.
- Nadia Maria El Cheikh, *Women, Islam, and Abbasid Identity* (2015) — sources the argument that gendered literary constructions served Abbasid identity.
- UNESCO World Heritage Centre, *Samarra Archaeological City* — verifies 836–892 capital period and significance of surviving urban plan.
- Tariq al-Janabi, “Islamic Archaeology in Iraq: Recent Excavations at Samarra” (*World Archaeology* 14, 1983), DOI `10.1080/00438243.1983.9979871` — excavation and artisan residential evidence.
- Hugh Kennedy, *When Baghdad Ruled the Muslim World* (2005/2006) — publication record and contextual coverage verified.
- Nawal Nasrallah, trans., *Annals of the Caliphs’ Kitchens* (Brill, 2007) — publication record verified; elite culinary-source caution retained.
- Yedida Stillman, “The Importance of the Cairo Geniza Manuscripts for the History of Medieval Female Attire” (1976), DOI `10.1017/S0020743800024715` — chronology cautions for medieval documentary evidence.

The chapter's register contains further entries; publication metadata and abstracts are the basis of verification in several instances, not claims to full-text review. Broad, plausible historical syntheses are editorial reconstructions and should not be mistaken for newly discovered archival facts.

The supplied manuscript shell was retained and improved, but exact model fidelity (favicon, breadcrumb, global bottom-nav placeholders, hosting assets) cannot be certified. This is a publication-integration check, not a missing historical chapter.

Three explicitly labeled paraphrase windows are suitable as teaching discussions but are not primary-source facsimiles. For a later scholarly documentary edition, add manuscript identifiers, volume/page, and validated edition-specific translations.

4. Much evidence for nonelite Abbasid households is fragmentary. Claims are framed cautiously; one cannot infer representative demographic percentages or average wages from the cited sources.
5. Links in the reference register identify source records and known publishers. Automated checking of every external destination was not performed.

## 3. Scholarly corrections applied directly

1. **§47.12 — Caliphal religious authority.** Replaced the inaccurate absolute assertion that caliphs never again sought to impose a creed after the miḥna. The new paragraph distinguishes failure of the ninth-century created-Qurʾan inquisition from the Qādirī creed issued under al-Qādir in 1017–1019 and 1029, and its reaffirmation under al-Qāʾim in 1040–1041. Relevant research: Livnat Holtzman, “The Caliph al-Qādir bi-llāh and the Qādirī Creed,” 2024, DOI 10.1163/9789004690615_008; Tayeb El-Hibri, *The Abbasid Caliphate: A History*, Cambridge, 2021.
2. **§47.16 — Early Zaydi divergence.** Corrected the unqualified description of early Zaydi acceptance of the first two caliphs. Batrī and Jārūdī tendencies differed, although the heresiographical names are themselves historically debated. Research: Scott Lucas and Egbal Almahatwary, “Zaydī Shīʿism,” *St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology* (2025), https://www.saet.ac.uk/Islam/ZaydiShiism.
3. **§47.17, chronology, summary and people entry — Fatimid dates.** Distinguished the overthrow of the Aghlabids and rise of the Fatimid state in 909 from the formal proclamation of ʿUbayd Allāh al-Mahdī as caliph at Qayrawān in January 910. Research: Farhad Daftary, *A History of Shiʿi Islam* (Fatimid section, January 910), and general Fatimid chronology.
4. **§47.8 — Ibadi classification.** Clarified that Ibadi legal tradition persisted independently outside the Sunni legal-school framework; other Sunni schools, including the Ẓāhirī, were the schools that waned.
5. **§47.16 — Cross-references.** Explained that Chapters 87 and 134 concern substantially later Yemeni periods and should not be presented as standalone accounts of ninth-century Zaydi state formation.
6. **§47.12 — Civil-war cross-reference.** Directed the reader to Chapter 45, not Chapter 44, for the 809–813 war. The Karbala and Alid references were also made chronologically explicit.

### Findings retained without change

- The four Sunni legal schools and the major hadith collections are treated as institutions whose authority consolidated over time, not as a completed eighth-century system.
- The hadith debate appropriately distinguishes traditional hadith criticism from modern source dating and avoids sweeping assumptions of universal authenticity or fabrication.
- The miḥna is dated 833 through approximately 849–851, consistent with evidence of a phased termination under al-Mutawakkil.
- The Zaydi, Ismaili and Twelver traditions are given differentiated historical treatment. The 874 crisis and traditional 874–941 Lesser Occultation dates are identified as developments interpreted through later Imami sources.
- The three source windows use labeled paraphrases and acknowledge genre, transmission, bias, and interpretive limits; they do not present invented verbatim quotations.

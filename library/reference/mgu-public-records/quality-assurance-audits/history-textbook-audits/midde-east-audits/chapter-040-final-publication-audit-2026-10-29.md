## Historical source cross-checks

- 18 Dhū l-Ḥijja 35 AH / approximately 17 June 656 for ʿUthmān's killing is well attested in a standard dating convention; other transmitted dates exist.
- Shawwāl 35 AH for the Egyptian delegation is recorded in al-Ṭabarī's year 35 reports; convert to approximately April 656 rather than an exact Gregorian date.
- Rajab 36 AH / January 657 for the move to Kūfa is a reported chronology; no need for unsupported day-level precision.
- Ṣafar 37 AH / summer 657 for the Ṣiffīn arbitration agreement is appropriate; the exact document date is debated and later reconstructions differ.
- First Fitna military and provincial episodes, including Syrian raids by Sufyān al-Ghāmidī and Busr ibn Abī Arṭāt, are attributed to transmitted narratives rather than stated as independently witnessed accounts.
- Failed Constantinople expedition around 654 is retained as a *debated hypothesis* linked to Sebeos, not a settled event (Shaun O'Sullivan, *Byzantine and Modern Greek Studies*, 28/1, 2004, 67–88).
- Martin Hinds: “The Murder of the Caliph ‘Uthmān,” *International Journal of Middle East Studies* 3/4 (1972), 450–469; “The Siffin Arbitration Agreement,” *Journal of Semitic Studies* 17/1 (1972), 93–129.
- Competing Arabic and Maronite Chronicle chronology remains clearly distinguished; the draft avoids projecting later doctrinal divisions directly into the events of 656–661.

## Limitations

The bibliographic pinpointing in Source Window II now refers to al-Ṭabarī's translated reports, not individually verified Arabic isnād chains or a collation of every counterpart in al-Balādhurī. This scope is declared in the text. As with all this series' source-critical chapters, reconstructed battlefield dialogue, troop numbers, and uncorroborated responsibility claims are not treated as certain facts. No publication-significant issue remains requiring another chapter-wide drafting pass.

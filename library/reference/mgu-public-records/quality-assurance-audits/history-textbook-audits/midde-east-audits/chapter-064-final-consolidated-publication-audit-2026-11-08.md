### Historical consistency and targeted source verification
**1. Dynasty and succession.** Hülegü's western line is distinguished from the original centrally directed Mongol expedition; the fragmentation after Möngke and the successive Ilkhanid reigns are analyzed. The death of Abu Sa'id in 1335 is identified as ending the united political state, without claiming that all Ilkhanid-era art or later Chinggisid claims ceased that year. Reference: Encyclopaedia Iranica, *Il-Khanids I: Dynastic History* (https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/il-khanids-i-dynastic-history/), and Peter Jackson, *Abu Sa'id Bahador Khan* (https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/abu-said-bahador-khan/).

**2. Ghazan's conversion.** The revised narrative dates conversion to **17 June 1295, during the contest against Baydu and before securing the throne**. It describes initial persecution of Buddhists and attacks affecting some Christian and Jewish institutions, subsequent changes in treatment, continued Chinggisid legitimacy, and ongoing warfare against the Muslim Mamluks. Reference: Reuven Amitai, *Ghazan Khan, Mahmud*, Encyclopaedia Iranica (https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/gazan-khan-mahmud/).

**3. Fiscal policy and paper money.** The experiment with *chao/chav* is placed in 1294 under Gaykhatu, with the failure interpreted in institutional and monetary terms. Ghazan's reforms are differentiated from their implementation; the main detailed witness, Rashid al-Din, is explicitly described as a participating minister. References: Encyclopaedia Iranica, *Gaykhatu Khan* (https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/gaykatu-khan/), *Il-Khanids I*, and the publication history of *Jami' al-Tawarikh* (https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/jame%CA%BF-al-tawari%E1%B8%B5/).

**4. Religious chronology under Öljeitü.** The chapter treats movement toward Twelver Shia ideas as a debated phase in the ruler's political and devotional history, not as an empire-wide conversion or an inevitable prefiguration of later Safavid Iran. Abu Sa'id's succession is carefully distinguished from variant accounts of the formal enthronement in 1317. Reference: Encyclopaedia Iranica, *Il-Khanids I* and *Abu Sa'id Bahador Khan*.

**5. Maragha.** Construction of the observatory is placed in 1259, with early operation around 1261–1262. Institutional resources, al-Tusi, al-'Urdi, theoretical work, *zij* tables, and the Tusi couple are differentiated. Reference: George Saliba, *Tusi, Nasir al-Din II: As Mathematician and Astronomer*, Encyclopaedia Iranica (https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/tusi-nasir-al-din-mathematician-astronomer/).

**6. Sultaniyya.** The dossier distinguishes earlier royal interest in the site, Öljeitü's expansion of the city, and the early-fourteenth-century mausoleum. Its dating is stated **approximately 1302–1313 in major references**, acknowledging that UNESCO gives 1302–1312 and Metropolitan Museum of Art documentation often uses 1307–1313. References: UNESCO World Heritage Centre, *Soltaniyeh* (https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1188); Metropolitan Museum of Art, *The Art of the Ilkhanid Period (1256–1353)* (https://www.metmuseum.org/ja/essays/the-art-of-the-ilkhanid-period-1256-1353).

**7. Art and historical scholarship.** Rashid al-Din's historical project, Tabriz manuscript production, East Asian artistic influences, and major Ilkhanid structures are presented without inferring unsupported mechanisms of transmission. References: Sheila Blair, *A Compendium of Chronicles* (1995); Smithsonian catalog (https://siris-libraries.si.edu/ipac20/ipac.jsp?index=BC&menu=search&term=39088016160848); Metropolitan Museum of Art, *The Legacy of Genghis Khan* (https://www.metmuseum.org/met-publications/the-legacy-of-genghis-khan-courtly-art-and-culture-in-western-asia-1256-1353).

**8. Armenian and Syriac testimony.** Kirakos Gandzaketsi is used for the Armenian regional perspective, with the translator's statement about his work ending around events of 1266–1267. Bar Hebraeus and the continuation of his chronicle are not conflated. All four source windows explicitly state **editorial paraphrase, not quotation**. Reference: Robert Bedrosian translation of Kirakos (https://www.attalus.org/armenian/kgtoc.html); E. A. Wallis Budge translation of Bar Hebraeus (1932).

**9. Diplomacy and warfare.** The manuscript does not present Ayn Jalut in 1260 as a final, fixed frontier. It includes Ilkhanid operations at Homs and the 1299 victory/1303 defeat, and places the Mamluk settlement in 1323 while preserving Chapter 65's separate analytical responsibility. Reference: Reuven Amitai, *Ghazan Khan, Mahmud*, and Peter Jackson, *The Mongols and the Islamic World* (2017).

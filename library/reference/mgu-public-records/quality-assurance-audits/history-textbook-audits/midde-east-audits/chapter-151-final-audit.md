## 4. Substantive factual changes made from the submitted first build

The revision did more than lengthen prose:

1. **1991 chronology clarified.** The Kurdish uprising is described as beginning in early March, with major cities seized later that month; the southern uprising is contextualized as involving diverse military, social, and religious participants. The narrative avoids presenting the rebel coalition as a unitary Shia movement.
2. **Coalition and uprising analysis expanded.** The chapter distinguishes U.S. political encouragement of opposition from any actual military guarantee to sustain the uprisings, and examines surviving Iraqi elite units, helicopters, and nonintervention debates.
3. **Resolution 688's legal scope clarified.** The chapter distinguishes condemnation of repression and demands for humanitarian access from an explicit Security Council authorization for foreign-enforced no-fly zones, which the resolution did not provide.
4. **Northern political history completed.** The Kurdish elections, internal blockade, KDP–PUK rivalry, the 1996 Erbil intervention, the 1998 Washington Agreement, and the limits of autonomy receive sustained analytical treatment. Kurdistan is not retrospectively labeled an independent state.
5. **Kirkuk and minority displacement integrated.** Forced population change affecting Kurdish, Turkmen, and Assyrian residents is included, together with the distinction between documented estimates and precise census counts.
6. **The marshlands are treated as social and environmental history.** Drainage, forced displacement, village repression, livelihood change, and the distinction between engineering evidence and human-rights testimony are developed.
7. **Sanctions periods distinguished.** The original embargo is separated from the Oil-for-Food phase and the 2002 Goods Review List reform under Resolution 1409. The chapter no longer implies uninterrupted prohibition of all oil exports through 2002.
8. **Mortality claims qualified.** The 1999 UNICEF-supported survey findings and the 2017 Dyson–Cetorelli reassessment are treated as a historiographical evidence problem. Documented hardship is not made dependent on one disputed child-death statistic.
9. **Oil-for-Food chronology strengthened.** Resolution 986 (1995), the memorandum of understanding (May 1996), first exports (December 1996), and first food deliveries (March 1997) are distinguished. The unusual split between UN administration in the three northern governorates and Iraqi government administration elsewhere is addressed.
10. **Inspections differentiated technically.** UNSCOM, IAEA, and UNMOVIC roles; the 150-kilometer missile threshold; Hussein Kamel's 1995 defection; Iraqi concealment; incomplete destruction records; and the limits of subsequent WMD conclusions are explained.
11. **No-fly zone chronology refined.** Northern operations, Southern Watch (1992), the September 1996 southern-zone extension, French policy differences, and the distinction between control of airspace and control of territory are included.
12. **Late Baathist political history broadened.** The Faith Campaign, tribal patronage, Uday and Qusay Hussein, opposition factionalism, and the 1998 Iraq Liberation Act appear in their institutional contexts.
13. **2002 endpoint clarified.** Resolution 1441, the renewed inspections beginning 27 November, and post-2003 Iraq Survey Group findings are distinguished chronologically. The chapter closes in 2002 rather than narrating the invasion.

## 5. Source verification and historical method

A targeted web-supported desk check compared crucial factual claims with primary UN Security Council records and several institutional and scholarly sources. The completed chapter's own 24-item bibliography contains the exact URLs. Particularly important checkpoints include:

- **UN Security Council Resolutions 661, 687, 688, 1284, 1409, and 1441:** dates, scope, ceasefire/disarmament clauses, humanitarian access, institutional transitions, and 2002 revised procedures.
- **FAO/WFP and UK parliamentary humanitarian evidence:** Oil-for-Food implementation chronology, regional administration, and the distribution and procurement challenges.
- **Human Rights Watch reporting:** forced displacement around Kirkuk, marshland repression, and repression after Muhammad Sadiq al-Sadr's death.
- **UNEP reporting:** large-scale wetland loss and the environmental evidence for the drainage crisis.
- **Congressional Research Service:** Kurdish politics, 1991 uprisings, sanctions-era political and economic changes, and weapons-inspection developments.
- **Tim Dyson and Valeria Cetorelli (BMJ Global Health, 2017):** the evidentiary dispute over Iraqi under-five mortality estimates.
- **Samuel Helfont (Oxford University Press, 2018):** the 1993 Faith Campaign and the state-directed administration of religious activity.
- **Postwar Iraq Survey Group findings, summarized through Congressional Research Service:** distinctions between past programs, gaps in accounting, residual expertise, and active alleged 2002 stockpiles.

The chapter's Primary Source Windows use *explicitly labeled paraphrases*, not invented quotations. Numerical statements about displacement and ecological loss are marked as estimates where appropriate. Later investigative evidence is used retrospectively but is not falsely attributed to observers in 1991 or 2002.

These checks establish a reasonable research basis for an educational textbook. They are not an exhaustive primary-archive audit or independent verification of every statistic and testimony in the available literature.

## Consolidated historical-accuracy corrections
1. **Military slavery clarified:** Recruitment depended on enslavement and human trafficking; manumission, trained military careers, and household cohesion are distinguished from ordinary slavery and from freeborn descendants (*awlad al-nas*). No claim is made that the system erased coercion or guaranteed advancement.
2. **Ain Jalut correctly bounded:** The victory of 3 September 1260 is explained as a pivotal defense, not a permanent elimination of the Mongol threat. The discussion distinguishes Hülegü’s withdrawal and Kitbuqa’s reduced army without assigning every decision to one deterministic cause.
3. **Continued Ilkhanid campaigning addressed:** The account distinguishes the Mamluk victory at Homs (1281), Ghazan’s field victory at Wadi al-Khaznadar (December 1299), the Mongol occupation of Damascus into early 1300, the Mamluk victory at Shaqhab (1303), and peace in 1323.
4. **Post-1291 Crusader geography corrected:** Acre’s capture ended the principal mainland Latin Christian state system, not all Christian communities or Latin influence in the eastern Mediterranean. The Hospitaller Rhodian establishment belongs to the early fourteenth century (c. 1310), not immediately to 1291.
5. **Cilician Armenia treated at its proper endpoint:** The Mamluk capture of Sis and termination of Armenian royal sovereignty in 1375 are included, with Armenian communal continuity explicitly distinguished from a kingdom’s fall.
6. **Fiscal chronology checked:** The Egyptian cadastral redistribution of al-Nasir Muhammad is associated with 1315; the narrative also recognizes a broader program of regional surveys rather than implying that a single assessment ended fiscal problems.
7. **Black Death chronology qualified:** 1347–1349 and subsequent recurrences are separated. Al-Maqrizi is dated 1364–1442 and explicitly identified as a later compiler of the first epidemic’s events, not an eyewitness to 1348.
8. **Burji transition qualified:** Barquq’s accession in 1382 and later return in 1390 are distinguished; the designation Burji is treated as a conventional periodization, not the creation of an unrelated state or a precise ethnic category for all soldiers.
9. **Cyprus and later trade:** Barsbay’s 1426 campaign is described as producing tributary status, not identical provincial government. Pepper controls and Portuguese maritime competition are discussed without the unsupported claim that Indian Ocean trade ceased immediately or that Portuguese activity alone caused the 1517 conquest.
10. **Architecture differentiated:** The Qalawun hospital/madrasa/mausoleum complex (1284–1285) is separated from the Sultan Hasan mosque-madrasa (begun 1356), and the function of later Qaytbay foundations is integrated with urban patronage, social labor, and waqf finance.
11. **Ottoman transition contained:** Battles of Marj Dabiq (1516) and al-Raydaniyya (1517) receive sufficient endpoint context while reserving a detailed invasion account for Chapter 71. Gunpowder is not presented as a self-sufficient monocausal explanation.

## Sources and citation integrity
All four source windows include source genre, author/document identification, research context, a clearly marked **editorial paraphrase**, limitations, and four specific student questions each. No fabricated direct quotation or unverified textual excerpt is presented as verbatim.

1. **Al-Maqrizi, *al-Khitat*, on barracks and mamluk education.** A primary narrative about the institution written retrospectively, with a specific modern translation and edition identified. Reference: https://medievalslavery.org/middle-east-and-north-africa/source-the-training-of-mamluks/ . The work’s idealization of training and exclusion of recruits’ own voices is disclosed.
2. **Ibn Abd al-Zahir, *al-Rawd al-zahir*, on Baybars.** Royal biographical/panegyrical evidence contextualized as chancery-based, politically interested narrative. Translation edition identification: https://mamluk.lib.uchicago.edu/fullview1.php?id=37497 .
3. **Al-Maqrizi, *al-Suluk*, on the Black Death.** First epidemic recorded retrospectively; explicit qualification about chronology, high death totals, and modern epidemiological interpretation. Scholarly comparison: https://nes.princeton.edu/publications/black-death-middle-east and https://scholarworks.wmich.edu/tmg/vol1/iss1/7/ .
4. **Qalawun complex foundation inscription (1284–1285).** A surviving material/epigraphic source associated with the hospital–madrasa–mausoleum foundation. Photographic/textual documentation: https://archives.arce.org/catalog/%2F21198%2Fz10m32nk ; historical context: https://qalawun.aa-ken.jp/en/facility/history/ and https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/record/747/files/MamlukStudiesReview_V_2001.pdf . Differing scholarly accounts of property acquisition and displacement are disclosed rather than resolved by the inscription alone.

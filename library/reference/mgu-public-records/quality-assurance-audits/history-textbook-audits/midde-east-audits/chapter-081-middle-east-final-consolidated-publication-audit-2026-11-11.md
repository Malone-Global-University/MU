## 4. Consolidated historical checks and corrections

**Corrected major shortcomings from Claude’s draft:** New Julfa is explicitly contextualized as a product of Shah Abbas I’s forced deportations in 1604 and the 1605 settlement process; subsequent restrictions and relocations during Abbas II’s reign are described without idealizing privilege. The chapter distinguishes Isfahan’s inherited city from gradual Safavid building; the 1722 siege is treated through the urban-human consequence and cross-referred to Chapter 80. Baghdad’s 1508, 1534, 1623, and 1638 sovereignty changes remain precise, while the Ottoman Arab cities retain local institutional histories rather than being portrayed as passive Ottoman replicas.

**Source-grounded historical spot checks:**

1. **Baghdad:** *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Iraq iv. Relations in the Safavid Period,” supports the 1508, 1534, 1623, and 1638 chronology: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/iraq-iv-safavid-period/ .
2. **Isfahan’s royal square:** UNESCO describes the Ali Qapu, Royal Mosque, Sheikh Lotfollah Mosque, Qaysariya portal, arcades, commercial uses, and staged Safavid monumental setting: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/115 .
3. **New Julfa:** Vazken S. Ghougassian’s *Encyclopaedia Iranica* article records the 1604 coercive deportations, the 1605 foundation, merchant distinctions, and relocations in 1655–59: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/julfa-i-safavid-period/ .
4. **Istanbul hydraulic conflict:** Nathaniel Moses’s Harvard Mellon Urban Initiative project identifies the 1608 decree as printed by Ahmed Refik, explains *katma*, and records a 1748 mapping initiative: https://mellonurbanism.harvard.edu/mapping-and-contesting-early-modern-hydropolis-water-shortage-istanbul-0 .
5. **Aleppo:** the Stony Brook University catalog identifies Jean-Claude David’s study (with Bruno Chauffert-Yvart) of the 1653 İpşir Pasha waqf: https://commons.library.stonybrook.edu/amar/224/ .
6. **Chardin:** *Encyclopaedia Iranica* documents the expanded 1711 edition and its relationship to earlier Isfahan observations: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/chardin-sir-john/ .
7. **Cairo:** the American University in Cairo describes Nelly Hanna’s combined court-record and architectural research on 17th–18th-century residential housing: https://www.aucegypt.edu/fac/nellyhanna .
8. **Urban comparative frame:** André Raymond’s *Arab Cities in the Ottoman Period* (2002), as described by Routledge, and Eldem/Goffman/Masters (1999) provide the basis for the critical “Islamic city” historiographical framing: https://www.routledge.com/Arab-Cities-in-the-Ottoman-Period-Cairo-Syria-and-the-Maghreb/Raymond/p/book/9780860788744 .

**Evidence calibration:** No fabricated population totals, court case identifiers, rent amounts, primary-source speeches, or numerical mortality totals were inserted. Official assertions, especially the Istanbul water-decree charges, are described as assertions rather than independently proven facts. Comparison does not imply universal historical sameness across cities, faiths, or regimes.

## 5. Document and source-study status

The four windows are (1) Istanbul’s 1608 water decree, (2) Jean Chardin’s descriptions of Safavid Isfahan, (3) the 1653 İpşir Pasha waqf complex in Aleppo, and (4) Cairo’s judicial and property archive as studied by Nelly Hanna. They are pedagogically substantial attributed studies with provenance, historical questions, interpretive limits, and scholarly links. **They are not facsimile/page-collated primary transcriptions.** Window 1 identifies the specific 1931 printed pages through Harvard scholarship; Windows 3–4 deliberately avoid inventing deed folios or individual court entries. For actual line-by-line text analysis, students must obtain the cited edition or an authenticated archive copy.

The bibliography holds **16 selectively verified entries** rather than an exhaustive multilingual archival catalogue. The geography studies are text-based and explicitly require historically dated atlases or scholarly plans; the HTML does not embed a misdated map or third-party image without clear reuse authorization.

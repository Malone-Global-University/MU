## 1. Authorized scope and plan compliance

The chapter follows the authoritative Part XXXIII assignment: investigate the political, social and economic origins of the Arab uprisings; analyze Tunisia, Egypt, Libya, Bahrain, Syria and Yemen; include youth, labor, professional networks, women and media; compare monarchies and republics; evaluate military behavior, outside intervention and counterrevolution; and explain why analogous protest slogans produced divergent outcomes.

The temporal frame is December 2010–2014, with concise background from the preceding decades. Section 157.13 and 157.14 treat Syria's initial uprising and escalation without attempting to narrate the extended war assigned to Chapter 158. Sections 157.15–157.16 address Yemen's 2011–2014 transition rather than appropriating the later conflict assigned to Chapter 160. The conclusion supplies a disciplined handoff to Chapters 158–162 without projecting subsequent events backward into the expectations of 2011 participants.

### Interpretive argument

The finished manuscript's controlling distinction is between **leadership removal**, **transformation of governing institutions**, and **durable constitutional authority**. It uses that distinction to explain the disparate experiences of Tunisia, Egypt, Libya, Bahrain, Syria, Yemen and the surviving monarchies. It avoids treating protest as a homogeneous regional organization or presenting social media as a solitary cause. Civil-society organizations, security institutions, economic resources and foreign intervention are treated as interacting variables, not exclusive explanations.

## 2. Independent historical and documentary verification

The following verification concentrated on exact dates, vote figures, legally significant statements, allegations of coercion, and the scope of primary documents. Online primary-source texts, institutional historical records and representative scholarly catalogs were consulted. Not all 39 bibliography entries underwent a fresh page-by-page or catalog-by-catalog review.

### Tunisia

- Mohamed Bouazizi's December 17, 2010 self-immolation and Ben Ali's January 14, 2011 departure are correctly sequenced. The narrative avoids inventing dialogue or claiming that every disputed detail of Bouazizi's confrontation with officials is known.
- The October 23, 2011 Constituent Assembly election is correctly described. Ennahda's **89 of 217 seats** is supported by the Inter-Parliamentary Union's election record: https://data.ipu.org/election-summary/HTML/2392_11.htm .
- The National Dialogue Quartet is correctly constituted by the UGTT, UTICA, Human Rights League and bar association. Its mediating role is not confused with the legislative authority of the elected assembly. The Council of Europe history supports the description: https://www.assembly.coe.int/nw/xml/XRef/Xref-XML2HTML-en.asp?fileid=23737&lang=en .
- The National Constituent Assembly adopted the constitution on **January 26, 2014**, by **200 votes in favor, 12 against and four abstentions**. The English constitutional translation and parliamentary retrospective support this dating. The 2014 election totals given in the narrative (**Nidaa Tounes 86 seats; Ennahda 69**) are supported by the IPU: https://data.ipu.org/parliament/TN/TN-LC01/election/TN-LC01-E20141026/ .
- Political compromises are described as historically important, not as proof of irreversible constitutional success. Regional inequalities and graduate unemployment remain visible, consonant with the World Bank's *Unfinished Revolution* study: https://www.worldbank.org/en/country/tunisia/publication/unfinished-revolution .

### Egypt

- January 25 demonstrations, Mubarak's resignation on **February 11, 2011**, the SCAF transition and Mohamed Morsi's **June 2012** election are correctly placed. The approximate **51.7 percent** runoff result is appropriate.
- The July 3, 2013 military statement is identified as the military's account of its own actions, not independent confirmation that those actions enjoyed universal legitimacy. The documentary comparison includes the SCAF's 2011 interim constitutional announcement and the Egyptian Armed Forces' 2013 statement: https://sis.gov.eg/en/presidency/speeches-telegrams-posts/statement-by-the-armed-forces-on-thursday-3-july-2013/ .
- The **August 14, 2013** Rabaa massacre is treated as a documented event. Human Rights Watch's **at least 817 persons killed at Rabaa alone**, with a higher probable total, is attributed specifically to its investigation rather than generalized to every protest killing in Egypt. Its original report is *All According to Plan* (2014): https://www.refworld.org/reference/countryrep/hrw/2014/101220 .
- The manuscript differentiates opposition to Morsi from support for the military intervention, and describes the SCAF, Brotherhood, revolutionary activists and rights advocates as heterogeneous political actors rather than uniform blocs.

### Libya

- Security Council Resolution **1973**, adopted on **March 17, 2011**, authorized specified civilian-protection measures and a no-fly zone while excluding a foreign occupation force. The chapter separates these legal terms from NATO's later operational choices. Official resolution text: https://mandates.un.org/mandate/S/RES/1973%282011%29 .
- The military intervention, fall of Tripoli in August, and Qaddafi's death on **October 20, 2011** are chronologically correct. The narrative neither assumes all opposition allegations were independently verified nor treats government and rebel abuses as legally indistinguishable.
- The account of postwar fragmentation recognizes both the personalized nature of Qaddafi's rule and the independent decisions of local brigades, political actors and international forces. The 2012 General National Congress election and the renewed fragmentation visible by 2014 remain within assigned scope.

### Bahrain

- The February 14, 2011 protest opening and **March 14** entry of GCC forces are correctly dated.
- The chapter distinguishes regional forces' deployment and government support from specific abuses attributable to Bahraini security institutions. It does not assert that every reported act by a Gulf soldier was verified.
- The Bahrain Independent Commission of Inquiry's **November 23, 2011** report documents serious violations, including excessive force and torture. It did **not** find substantiating evidence, in material submitted to the commission, for a claim that Iran directed the uprising. This is framed as a limit of the available evidence, not proof that every conceivable foreign contact was impossible. Official report: https://www.bici.org.bh/BICIreportEN.pdf .
- The report's cover and the official Resolution 1973 cover were visually checked as PDF page images in addition to examining the associated document text. The Yemen mechanism source was accessible in extracted form, but its PDF screenshot retrieval failed at the web host, a limitation recorded here.

### Syria

- Initial Daraa demonstrations and the arrest of schoolboys are treated without invented dialogue. The lifting of emergency law and elimination of the Supreme State Security Court are dated to **April 21, 2011**.
- The November 23, 2011 UN Commission of Inquiry report, **A/HRC/S-17/2/Add.1**, supplies the early documented allegations and findings. The section distinguishes evidence from this initial reporting period from the later Syrian war's history. Document record: https://www.refworld.org/reference/countryrep/unhrc/2011/83896 .
- Peaceful coordination, armed defections, the fragmented Free Syrian Army designation, international disagreement and regional differences are explained without treating Syrian religious communities as fixed political blocs.

### Yemen

- Saleh's signature of the GCC Initiative and accompanying implementation mechanism on **November 23, 2011**, Hadi's **February 21, 2012** election as sole consensus candidate, and the 2013–2014 National Dialogue are correctly distinguished. Institutional and UN chronologies support those dates: https://dppa.un.org/en/node/103448 .
- The transition mechanism is described as a **two-phase** framework with early election, shared government, reform and dialogue obligations—not as proof that armed factions surrendered control or that constitutional change had been completed.
- The end-of-2014 destabilization is included solely to explain transition limits; extended later Houthi and regional wars are reserved for Chapter 160.

### Morocco, Jordan, Algeria and the wider region

- Morocco's February 20 Movement, July 1, 2011 constitutional referendum and November 2011 election are correctly situated. Continued royal authority is differentiated from the expansion of elected institutions. Relevant CRS account: https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RS21579.html .
- Jordan's government reshuffles, constitutional review, security-sector continuity and fiscal context are treated with appropriate qualifications. Relevant CRS record: https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/20111121_RL33546_b1c0f5ec149011adefa727a39c52bc751bfd7e18.html .
- Algeria's civil-conflict memory, Oman's employment protests, Saudi reform and Eastern Province mobilization, and Kuwait's parliamentary contention are represented as distinct cases. No one claim is made to stand for all Gulf monarchies.

## 3. Substantive corrections made

1. **Narrative reconstruction:** Replaced twenty one-paragraph national summaries with 129 substantive historical paragraphs (12,226 principal words), developing chronology, causal mechanisms, evidence, and social history without changing numbered section assignments.
2. **Tunisia:** Expanded the 2013 assassinations, political impasse, Quartet roadmap, 2014 constitutional vote, Ennahda seat share and competitive parliamentary transition. Distinguished the Quartet's mediation from the assembly's authority.
3. **Egypt:** Expanded the 2011 military transition, the 2012 presidential and constitutional disputes, Morsi's removal, the documentary military rationale and the separate evidence of Rabaa killings. Differentiated political opposition from endorsement of coercion.
4. **Libya:** Set Resolution 1973's legal terms alongside actual military operations, and developed militia organization, oil, displacement and political fragmentation. Avoided treating early allegations or later outcomes as established at the first protests.
5. **Bahrain:** Added the inquiry's documented findings, separate descriptions of GCC and Bahraini forces, and evidentiary treatment of claims concerning Iran.
6. **Syria:** Clarified April 21, 2011 decrees and differentiated early demonstrations, security-force response, local coordination networks, fragmented armed opposition and later war histories.
7. **Yemen:** Distinguished the GCC settlement's promised procedures from actual control over armed institutions; clarified Hadi's consensus ballot and the National Dialogue's limits.
8. **Comparative conclusion:** Developed explicit mechanisms involving regime institutions, military decisions, social organization, political economy, and external intervention. Rejected arguments resting on fixed national or sectarian character.
9. **Documentary windows:** Restored all six assigned sources in their planned narrative positions with historical context, labeled paraphrases, source limitations, targeted student questions and references.
10. **Bibliographic refinement:** Identified Fawaz A. Gerges as **editor**, not sole author, of *The New Middle East* (Cambridge University Press, 2014). Added a comparative 2011 Egyptian constitutional source for the Egyptian window.

The revised chapter preserves the relevant claims and intellectual purpose of the submitted draft, but its manuscript text is substantively rewritten to fulfill the master production standard.

## 6. Remaining limitations and editorial judgment

**External links:** Primary documentary links were researched against relevant institutional sources; however, not all 39 reference destinations were opened and checked for continued accessibility, and some archival sites may require a reader to follow an alternate mirror. This is a website maintenance matter rather than evidence that the manuscript lacks a bibliography.

**Contested historical evidence:** Some early protest incidents and responsibility for particular acts cannot be reconstructed with complete certainty. The manuscript explicitly identifies disputed claims, source constraints, and the methodological limits of official texts, rights inquiries and online testimony. It does not invent quotations or claim comprehensive verification of every casualty.

**Research published after the chapter endpoint:** Later scholarly works are cited as retrospective analysis and are not projected into actors' 2011 knowledge. The chapter ends analytically by 2014, while later developments are assigned to Chapters 158–162.

**Interactive website components:** Shared footer and navigation files are requested from `/component/...`; they must be checked after deployment in the actual MGU hosting environment. Browser testing of the live site has not been performed.

**Maps:** The chapter provides a geography study and five closing map questions; physical map images will be inserted by the publisher in the separate map workflow.

**Scope of independent research:** This audit verified critical documentary events and representative publication records. It did not constitute a full academic peer review of every sentence, a complete archival investigation, or a live website acceptance test. These limitations are recorded rather than hidden.

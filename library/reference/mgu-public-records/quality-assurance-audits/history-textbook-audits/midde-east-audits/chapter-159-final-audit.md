## 2. Substantive corrections made

1. **Iraqi constitutional federalism:** Corrected the source commentary's inaccurate reference to a “unitary Iraqi state.” Articles 1 and 117 of the 2005 Iraqi Constitution establish a **federal** republic and recognize the Kurdistan Region as a federal region. The constitutional discussion distinguishes autonomy from a unilateral constitutional right to secede.
2. **The 2017 independence referendum:** Removed the categorical assertion that Kurdish independence had “never” become an operational KDP program. The September 2017 referendum was a concrete, ultimately unsuccessful statehood initiative. Refined the official reported “yes” result to **92.73%** of participating voters and characterized the UN position more carefully as serious institutional concern and a call for constitutional dialogue.
3. **The 2014–2015 Kobani chronology:** Corrected the instructional timeline to distinguish the U.S. partnership with YPG-led forces at Kobani from subsequent U.S.–SDF cooperation. The SDF was formed in October 2015.
4. **The PKK's military history:** Removed wording suggesting that Turkey had definitively defeated the PKK before the later negotiated initiatives. The text now distinguishes military pressure and containment from final conflict settlement.
5. **Turkish cross-border operations:** Clarified the original description of airstrikes and domestic security operations after the 2015 collapse of the peace process, so that activity in northern Iraq is not conflated with fighting in southeastern Turkish towns.
6. **Syrian Kurdish legal recognition:** Added **Syrian Presidential Decree No. 13 of January 16, 2026** to the narrative, chronology, and institutional analysis. The decree recognized Kurdish as a national language and allowed its teaching in qualifying areas, addressed nationality deprivation associated with the 1962 Hasakah census, and recognized Newroz. The analysis distinguishes these enacted provisions from broader claims to constitutional autonomy and from their administrative implementation.
7. **Kurdish education in Syria:** Incorporated the **August 27, 2026** education instructions and ongoing issues involving teachers, curricula, school certificates, and the difference between national-language recognition and full official-language equality.
8. **Syrian integration agreements:** Distinguished the published January 18 ceasefire and full-integration terms from the separately publicized **January 29–30** implementation framework. Revised the primary-source commentary so that it no longer wrongly implies the January documents were unavailable.
9. **SDF dissolution:** Preserved the verified **August 25, 2026** public dissolution announcement while clarifying that some administrative, personnel, property, educational, and women's-security-force questions remained unsettled as of the historical cutoff.
10. **Turkey's 2026 legislation:** Corrected the name to **Law No. 7595, Law on Strengthening National Solidarity and Social Integration**, adopted **August 10** and published **August 18, 2026**. Added the recorded **468 in favor, 88 against, six abstentions** and maintained the critical distinction between conditional legal provisions and a general amnesty.
11. **Political geography:** Qualified the claim that all relevant Kurdish borders were “drawn” during 1920–1926; the post-Ottoman settlements interacted with an older Ottoman–Iranian frontier.
12. **Instructional vocabulary:** Replaced an undifferentiated opening list with twenty-four historically specific definitions, preserving more detailed terms in the closing reference material.

## 3. Narrative and instructional development

The first draft had the correct architectural structure but several underdeveloped portions. The completed manuscript now develops the following substantive material:

- **§§159.2–159.4:** civilian consequences of urban fighting; legal and democratic stakes of municipal trusteeships; Kurdish electoral diversity; the Turkish government's changing opposition, refugee, and border policy; and the conflict between Turkish and American security priorities.
- **§§159.5–159.8:** competing Syrian Kurdish parties; the tension between governing ideals and concentrated security power; the transition from military coalition to territorial administration; differentiated experiences of women, Arab and Syriac communities; the fiscal realities of oil and agriculture; and the continuing consequences of Turkish operations.
- **§§159.9–159.12:** the constitutional and multiethnic significance of Kirkuk; concrete limitations of the 2017 independence initiative; the influence of KDP and PUK patronage on public wages and services; the continuing Sinjar and Yazidi reconstruction problem; and differences among Kurdish movements in four states.
- **§§159.13–159.16:** ISIS detention and repatriation, water and infrastructure disruption, external security guarantees and their limits, Turkish national and municipal electoral dynamics, the distinct stages of PKK demobilization, and the operational ambiguities of the March 2025 Damascus–SDF pact.
- **§§159.17–159.20:** different legal and security obligations under the January 2026 agreements; the January Kurdish-rights decree; the August educational measures; integration of former combatants, police, and YPJ personnel; citizenship administration, land and property restitution, Turkish legislation, and continuing Iraqi Kurdish parliamentary disputes.

The revised manuscript contains approximately **18,000–18,500 words across narrative and instructional apparatus**. Important sections previously near 200–300 words now provide sustained multi-paragraph analysis. Length is not treated as a substitute for evidence; the expanded discussions are linked to the chapter's stated purpose.

## 6. Limitations and outstanding matters

1. **Historical proximity:** Outcomes of the 2025–2026 peace, disarmament, legal reform, and integration processes are not historically settled. Later scholarship may correct contemporary figures, political interpretations, or implementation assessments.
2. **Different kinds of sources:** Some 2026 evidence derives from official statements and contemporary journalism. Not every assertion can yet be checked against independent archival or judicial records. The manuscript maintains explicit distinctions between enacted law, announced policy, implementation, and interpretive forecasts.
3. **Source accessibility:** Relevant reports and official web pages were consulted or located as described; the audit does not certify perpetual public availability of every external URL. A requested image screenshot of a UN-hosted PDF could not be obtained because that host denied the screenshot fetch, although the PDF's extracted text on the relevant passage was accessible through the document viewer. The chapter does not rely on unverified visual content from that PDF.
4. **Maps and images:** Geography Study questions and spatial interpretation are included. Visual maps remain for later editorial insertion, according to the series' production workflow; their absence is **not** a substantive deficiency in the current manuscript.
5. **Publication environment:** The file was browser-rendered locally but was not published to the live MGU website. Server routing, deployed asset paths, accessibility audits with assistive devices, and live-site behavior remain outside this audit's claim.

## 7. Final decision

**APPROVED FOR EDITORIAL HANDOFF / PUBLICATION PREPARATION.** The revised manuscript fulfills the approved Chapter 159 assignment and the required twenty-section organization, integrates its relevant peoples and institutions, corrects the identified constitutional and chronological errors, incorporates major legal developments of 2026, and distinguishes formal political changes from incomplete implementation. The remaining limitations are inherent chiefly to recent history and deployment, not unfinished drafting. Do not reopen the chapter for cosmetic micro-passes absent new substantive evidence.

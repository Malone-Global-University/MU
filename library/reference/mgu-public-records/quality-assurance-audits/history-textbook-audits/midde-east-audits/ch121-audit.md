## 3. Major historical corrections and editorial refinements

**The Peel Commission's hearings, 1936–1937.** The revised §121.8 expressly distinguishes the Arab Higher Committee's initial boycott from its subsequent decision in January 1937 to give evidence. The commission did not hear every party continuously and equally from the beginning of its hearings.

**End of the 1936 strike.** The revision distinguishes the manifesto reported as issued 10 October, the commonly cited 11 October call to end the strike, and the general return to work on 12 October. These dates describe different points in a closely connected sequence, rather than necessarily conflicting claims. See the 1936 British report and its reproduction of the proclamation.

**1937 Peel, 1938 Woodhead, and later partition.** The revised §§121.9–121.10 and 121.18 distinguish the specific Peel proposal from Zionist authorization to negotiate on the basis of partition, Palestinian Arab rejection, and the Woodhead report's conditional and contested consideration of alternative maps. The Woodhead technical difficulties were not represented as proof that every imaginable territorial compromise was impossible.

**Britain's 1938 policing system.** The 1938 northern border barrier is distinguished from the larger number of fortified police facilities later associated with Charles Tegart. The account avoids projecting completion of the later network into the first months of the revolt.

**September 1937 repression.** The assassination of Lewis Andrews and the subsequent dissolution of Palestinian nationalist political organizations, exiles, deportations, and emergency powers are set out as a sequence of specific official measures, not treated as a single instantaneous event.

**Counterinsurgency and civilian abuse.** §121.13 separates dated legal instruments and official rationales from scholarly documentation of reprisals, abuse, property destruction, and evidentiary gaps. Al-Bassa and Halhul are discussed cautiously, without attaching invented exact casualty numbers. The account also distinguishes attacks on Jewish civilians and the responsibilities of armed groups from the much larger legal coercive power of the colonial administration.

**The 1939 White Paper.** §121.19 distinguishes the 50,000 places envisaged as five annual 10,000-person quotas from the additional provision for 25,000 refugees, subject to capacity and other conditions. The policy's conditional ten-year independence proposal is not described as an enacted constitution. The Land Transfers Regulations are correctly dated to **1940**, not to the publication of the 1939 White Paper.

**Casualty methodology.** The quant study distinguishes the mandatory government's 1937 and 1938 incident-based series, the separate 1938 estimate of insurgents killed during military/police operations, and later four-year overview totals. These are **not additive series**; counting rules and reporting periods vary. Explicit methodological questions invite students to compare rather than average incompatible data.

**Social and institutional context.** Expanded §§121.15–121.16 show the activities of women, workers, village communities, and groups affected by rebel coercion without depicting any community as politically homogeneous. The everyday-life feature consists of documented-case assignment instructions rather than invented quotations or purported biographies.

## 4. Core documentary evidence and source status

Documentary references are hyperlinked within the HTML. Priority texts checked for historical claims include:

1. **British annual report for 1936** (UN archive): 1935 Arab-party memorandum; National Committee formation; emergency powers; Arab rulers' appeal and the October strike-ending proclamation. <https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-208860/>
2. **Palestine Royal Commission summary** (League/UN archive): inquiry's purpose and July 1937 partition framework. <https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-197740/>
3. **British annual report for 1937** (UN archive): repression of the Arab Higher Committee and military-court powers. <https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-208273/>
4. **British annual report for 1938** (UN archive): militarized security administration, official annual casualty reporting, and political developments. <https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-198684/>
5. **Woodhead Commission report** (Palquest original-document reproduction) and **11 November 1938 government statement**: technical and political reasons for retreat from immediate partition. <https://www.palquest.org/en/historictext/6721/palestine-partition-commission-woodhead-commission-report> and <https://www.palquest.org/en/historictext/21811/british-government-statement-partition-woodhead-commissions-report>
6. **British 1939 White Paper** (Yale Avalon reproduction): conditional independence, immigration, and land policy. <https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/brwh1939.asp>
7. **Matthew Hughes**, "From Law and Order to Pacification" (scholarly article), on the documentation and practice of British counterinsurgency. <https://www.palquest.org/sites/default/files/From_Law_and_Order_to_Pacification_Britains_Suppression_of_the_Arab_Revolt_in_Palestine_1936-39-Mathew_Hughes.pdf>
8. **Ori Yehudai**, Ohio State Origins, provides a later synthetic account and broad historical casualty estimates; these are never presented as identical to mandatory annual incident tables. <https://origins.osu.edu/read/1936-1939-arab-revolt-palestine>

Each source window identifies author/issuing authority, date, documentary form, an **explicit editorial paraphrase (not a quotation)**, interpretation, limitations, and questions. Several source windows access an official *reproduction* of an earlier memorandum, or a near-contemporary report about a document, rather than the archival original. That provenance is disclosed in the window. Exact verbatim transcription of twelve original manuscripts **is not claimed**.

## 6. Edition limitations and publication disposition

- This is a **scholarly textual HTML edition**, with primary-source windows using carefully labeled editorial paraphrases and sources of varying proximity to the original manuscript. A future documentary-critical edition may add certified transcriptions.
- Map exercises are **atlas-based text studies** with links to documentary reports; no bespoke map image is embedded. Students must consult appropriate historical cartography.
- The browser test validates the generated local file and its functioning controls. It does not establish live-site hosting or the identity of a separate, unavailable Chapter 2 CSS model in every detail.
- The comparative narrative was checked against the principal available official and scholarly historical sources noted above, but it should not be represented as a critical edition in which every interpretative sentence has a separate footnote or every archival original has been inspected.
- Editorial lock is for this first-edition **textual** publication, not for the end of possible future research refinements.

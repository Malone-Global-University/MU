## 3. Substantive historical corrections and expansions

### A. Accession and wartime status

- Distinguished Reza Shah's **16 September 1941 abdication/succession** from Mohammad Reza Shah's constitutional oath, usually dated **17 September 1941**. The latter day is supported by a documented photograph and by historical accounts, while some works give 16 September; the narrative avoids suggesting that the date is entirely uncontested.
- Corrected the statement that Iran remained neutral for the whole of 1941–1945. Iran declared war on Germany **9 September 1943**, after entering the Allied wartime arrangements while still under occupation.

### B. Azerbaijan and Mahabad (§§85.5–85.8)

- Kept the **3 September 1945** Azerbaijan Democratic Party twelve-point program distinct from the November National Congress and the establishment of the People's Government in December.
- Developed the assembly's origins, restricted election conditions, local administrative reach, coercion and participation, ministries, armed formations, and constitutional claims.
- Expanded analysis of Azerbaijani-language education, school and fiscal capacity, landholding, local responses, and non-Azerbaijani populations. These policies are evaluated as short-lived programs rather than uniformly realized reforms.
- Developed Mahabad's **limited territorial reach**, local tribal differences, the role of Mustafa Barzani's forces, Soviet support, and the varying outcomes for Kurdish leaders. Claims of universal national support and precise repression figures are avoided.

### C. United Nations and Qavam's diplomacy (§§85.9–85.11)

- Replaced the unresolved editorial note with exact Security Council resolutions: **2 (30 January 1946), 3 (4 April), and 5 (8 May)**. Clarified the distinction between the Council's deferrals and a binding order to withdraw.
- Expanded the **4 April 1946 Qavam–Sadchikov correspondence** using the translated documentary text in *Foreign Relations of the United States*, 1946, vol. VII, doc. 306: proposed fifty-year joint company; **51% Soviet/49% Iranian ownership for the first twenty-five years, then 50%/50%**; territorial qualifications; Iranian responsibility for installation security; and the requirement for a later agreement to go before the Majles. Explained why these were specific executive commitments, **not** an already ratified concession or operating company.
- Distinguished the Soviet government's declared nonconditional troop withdrawal from the politically simultaneous petroleum bargain. The manuscript does not assert an unsupported formal Truman ultimatum.
- Made the withdrawal completion date more precise: Soviet announcement **9 May 1946**, with diplomatic observation and Iranian verification continuing into May.
- Reworked the **13 June 1946 Tehran–Azerbaijan fifteen-point agreement**: recognition of the existing assembly as a provincial council; governor-general selected by Tehran **from provincial nominees**; integration of regular and volunteer forces under arrangements to be settled; **75% of locally collected government receipts** for local spending; and additional minority and educational provisions. Identified implementation disputes rather than treating the agreement as either nonexistent or a durable settlement.

### D. Restoration of central authority and organized labor (§§85.12–85.16)

- Expanded December 1946 operations along the Zanjan–Tabriz approach, political changes within Tabriz, local opponents of the ADP, reprisals, and the distinct Mahabad outcome. The narrative does not conflate Soviet withdrawal in May with the army's advance in December.
- Expanded the **14 July 1946** Abadan/Khuzestan strike: Friday rest pay, labor legislation, worker demands and different constituencies, the AIOC, British officials, the Iranian government, and local Arab–left-wing conflict. Reported **17 killed and 150 wounded** only as *provisional figures stated in the British House of Commons on 17 July*, not definitive totals.
- Corrected the simplified account of the **2 December 1944** petroleum law: specified officials were prohibited from undertaking discussions or agreements concerning foreign oil concessions, while petroleum sales and Iranian state exploitation were treated differently and subject to parliamentary reporting provisions; the law also contained penal sanctions.
- Preserved the conventional **102–2** description of the **22 October 1947** vote but specified that the two ballots consisted of **one blank and one abstention**, not two affirmative votes for a Soviet petroleum company.
- Expanded Qavam's coalition and party-building, the limits of parliamentary openness, and social differences among labor, rural, religious, and urban political constituencies.

### E. Royal powers, constitutional revision, and nationalist opposition (§§85.17–85.20)

- Preserved caution around the **4 February 1949** assassination attempt and Nasser Fakhrarai: the government's subsequent repression of the Tudeh Party does not itself establish organizational responsibility for the attack.
- Corrected the original error that the constitutional amendments created the Senate. The **1906 constitutional settlement** had already provided for a sixty-member Senate; enabling legislation was enacted in **1948**, and the Senate first convened in **1950**.
- Distinguished the **8 May 1949 amended Article 48**, authorizing royal dissolution of the two chambers under stated conditions, from the **separate additional constitutional article** prescribing a future amendment procedure. The third Primary Source Window now accurately separates these developments.
- Added analysis of contested election administration, the **October 1949** National Front formation, and the constitutional logic linking elections, ministerial responsibility, and petroleum control.
- Clarified the First Seven-Year Plan and the emergence of the Plan Organization in **1949**, while limiting the chapter's discussion of the subsequent oil-nationalization struggle to a transition to Chapter 86.

## 7. Remaining limitations and editorial control

1. **Model parity:** The draft supplied an established HTML shell, but the external Chapter 84 implementation of shared MGU bottom navigation and footer loaders was not supplied. Accordingly, **byte-for-byte parity and integration with those shared components cannot be certified**. The existing static footer remains in place. Before deployment, the publisher should substitute the already verified project-wide loaders if required.
2. **Publication URLs:** Adjacent-chapter titles are printed as orientation text, not presumed live destination links. Canonical routing, external-link availability, and project-wide header/footer resources were not tested on `maloneuniversity.org`.
3. **Scope of verification:** Selected consequential claims and documentary provisions were checked against authoritative records. This was not a line-by-line collation of every historical detail or a search of all Soviet, Iranian, or Azerbaijani archives. Claims based on diplomatic observation retain the contextual limits of those sources.
4. **Contested numbers and agency:** Reprisal casualty totals, the extent of popular support, some motives attributed to political actors, and the shooter's organizational connections remain historiographically uncertain; the chapter does not present these as settled facts. The July 1946 strike figure is expressly provisional.
5. **Source window limits:** Azerbaijani program statements are paraphrased from historical treatments rather than presented as verified original-language quotations; all three primary-source boxes disclose that their explanatory language is a paraphrase.
6. **Deployment:** Tests were performed on the generated standalone HTML markup, **not** on a deployed web page. No live URL, server-side include, user authentication, or production navigation test was performed.

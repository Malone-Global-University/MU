## 3. Material historical and source corrections

### a. Firuzkuh and the wrongly identified British Library narrative (§57.6)

Corrected British Library **Or. 3248** from an erroneously described local contemporary source into the **retrospective history of Shah Ismail by Bijan**, titled *Tarikh-i Jahangusha-yi Khaqan-i Sahibqiran*, composed in the **1680s** and formerly styled the “Ross Anonymous.” It is not eyewitness testimony or an account written for Amir Husayn Kiya's regional court. Corrected the study's source type, authorship, perspective, limits, questions, and comparison instructions. The narrative now identifies the fortress of **Usta**, interruption of the water supply, and **12 May 1504** capitulation, as given by Roger M. Savory; accounts of extreme punishment remain attributed to chroniclers rather than asserted as newly authenticated eyewitness fact.

### b. Merv and the late retrospective witness (§57.8)

Identified the **same** British Library **Or. 3248**, fols. **187b–189a** (via Savory), for its narrative of Merv as a **late-Safavid memory of the 1510 battle**, not a contemporary battlefield account. The well-supported battle outcome and date remain distinct from later heroic details and unverified precise language. The student questions now explicitly address source date and retrospective perspective.

### c. Early Safavid coinage (§57.4)

Verified Ashmolean Heberden Coin Room **HCR18188**, a silver *shahi* from **Tabriz**, catalogued broadly to **907–924 AH / approximately 1502–1519 CE**. Corrected the previous 1501–1518 range, relabeled the box **“Early Safavid Coinage”**, and clarified the obverse ruler/mint inscription and reverse Shi'i-affiliated formula. The coin is not claimed to be a securely dated accession issue from 1501. Link included to the actual museum record.

### d. Italian observations (§57.19)

Replaced a generic synthesis of unnamed Italian observers with an identifiable source: **the anonymous Venetian merchant**, *The Travels of a Merchant in Persia*, **chapter XX, pages 201–202**, in Charles Grey's **1873** Hakluyt Society translation. The source study now concerns the merchant's description of Ismail's **courtly archery and public display at Tabriz**. It does **not** present the visit as an eyewitness description specifically from 1514–1524. The merchant's estimates of crowds and nineteenth-century editorial notes are not treated as verified demographic or chronological facts. Source edition link supplied.

### e. Hasan Rumlu and the Khata'i manuscript (§§57.3, 57.15)

Specified the relevant 907 AH / 1501 annal of Hasan Rumlu's *Ahsan al-Tawarikh*, identified **C. N. Seddon's 1931 text/1934 translation** and **Abd al-Husayn Nava'i's 1978 edition**, and retained the author's later retrospective perspective. For **Smithsonian S1986.60**, specified the **Tabriz c. 1520** manuscript, **50 folios, three illustrated pages, and folio 23 verso's ruler-praising inscription**, without inventing a translated poem or asserting independent textual collation. Material and literary analysis was expanded, retaining attribution uncertainties.

### f. Dynasty, government, frontier, and society

Clarified the accepted variants of Ismail's mother’s name (*Halima Begum/Alamshah Begum*) while retaining her documented descent from Uzun Hasan. Expanded early provincial administration, the institutional struggle among the *wakil*, Qizilbash commanders, Persian civil officials, the court, and provincial elites; examined revenue and territorial control, the military costs of the eastern and western frontiers, and the difference between proclamations and local practice. Developed the central/southern, Caspian, Diyarbakir, Baghdad, and Khurasan campaigns through the distinction of conquest and actual government. Expanded the social-history narrative, including court women, agrarian provisioning, urban occupations, and uneven evidence. Clarified Chaldiran's political and military implications without duplicating Chapter 59, and expanded Ismail's final decade and the unresolved succession question. Tempered the original implication that Chaldiran eliminated all future Safavid ambitions in eastern Anatolia.

## 4. Primary-source methodology and boundaries

**All six instructional studies remain explicitly editorial paraphrases or catalogue-guided descriptions.** They are **not facsimile-collated Persian passages, independently authenticated English translations of manuscript leaves, or reproductions of original coin/manuscript images**. Identified catalogue records and scholarly citations can direct students to primary witnesses; they do not establish that the present editor has personally read all original folios or editions. Historical maps remain text-guided and need a historical atlas. Exact Chapter 1 template byte-parity and live website deployment were not tested. These disclosed limitations do not prevent approval of a first-edition scholarly **textual** HTML chapter.

## Historical corrections and safeguards

1. **Abbas I's reign:** distinguished political struggle from 1587 from his formal enthronement on **1 October 1588**, consistent with *Encyclopaedia Iranica* (`https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/abbas-i/`).
2. **Imam Quli Khan:** standardized the principal date as **1633**, identifying **1042 AH** and openly noting that some regional historical accounts give 1632. The specialist biographical article dates the execution to 1042/1633, while *Iranica*'s article on Fars renders 1042/1632; disagreement is not silently erased (`https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/emamqoli-khan/`; `https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/fars-iii/`).
3. **Fath Ali Khan Daghestani:** corrected vizierate inception to **about 1715**, and removal/blinding to **December 1720**, consistently in narrative and biographical reference. Cross-checked against Roger M. Savory's *Encyclopaedia Iranica* article (`https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/dagestani-fath-ali-khan-b/`).
4. **Madrasa-ye Sultani:** distinguished construction of principal buildings in **1704–1707**, inauguration around **1710**, the **1711** endowment arrangements and continued work on the larger complex through approximately **1714**, to avoid assigning one completion year to all phases. Cross-checked against *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, Isfahan madrasa entry (`https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/isfahan-x4-madrasas/`) and scholarly notes.
5. **Safi II / Shah Suleiman:** original dates and regnal-name distinction remain intact; Hasht Behesht dating at approximately 1669 retained.
6. **Abbas-nama:** corrected bibliographic edition to Ebrahim Dehgan, Arak **1329 Solar Hijri / 1950–1951**, following the *Cambridge History of Iran* bibliography. The source study points to the 1648–1649 Kandahar campaign but does not falsely claim an independently verified folio.
7. **Krusiński source:** corrected the English 1728 title to the singular *The History of the Revolution of Persia*, London: J. Pemberton. Disclosed mediation through Father du Cerceau's French narrative and the retrospective character of the account. Wellcome Collection catalog: `https://wellcomecollection.org/works/r8yqacxv`.

## Repaired instructional primary evidence

**Source Window 2 and Quantitative Study:** real, item-level museum evidence replaces invented example figures. The Fitzwilliam Museum records two Tabriz silver abbasis of **Abbas II**: CM.2403-2003, **AH 1053**, **7.63 g** (`https://data.fitzmuseum.cam.ac.uk/id/object/256321`); CM.2395-2003, **AH 1054**, **7.33 g** (`https://data.fitzmuseum.cam.ac.uk/id/object/256313`). Measured difference **0.30 g**, equal to **3.93%** of the earlier specimen's mass. Critically, this **does not** prove a 3.93% debasement: there are only two surviving specimens, fineness is not established by those weights, and purchasing power and official policy require additional evidence. Source Window 2 now identifies the objects and limitations directly; the study includes the mathematical calculation and research extension.

**Source Window 3:** the 1728 London edition of Krusiński's retrospective narrative is identified, with its mediated editorial transmission and the lack of verified passage-level folio or an authenticated Sultan Husayn decree explicitly stated. No historical quotation has been invented.

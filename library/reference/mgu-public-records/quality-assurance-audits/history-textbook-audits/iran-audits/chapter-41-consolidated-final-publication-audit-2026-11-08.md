## 3. Publication-critical corrections made in the delivered HTML

1. **Chronology: Nasir-i Khusraw (§ opening chronology).** Corrected journey row from approximate **1046–1052** to **1045–1052**, consistent with the chapter's own narrative and accounts of his journey.
2. **Masyaf (§41.15 and chronology).** Replaced vague “by about 1140” with **around 1141**, matching modern scholarship on the Syrian Nizari acquisition of the fortress. The source supports 1141; original wording was approximate rather than a major factual error.
3. **Attempts on Saladin (§41.15).** Corrected **1174 and 1176** to the generally attested **1175 and 1176**. Retained caution about actors' motives and responsibility.
4. **Attributions of killings (§41.8).** Qualified blanket assertions identifying particular early deaths as confirmed Nizari acts. Distinguished evidence that attacks occurred from varying degrees of certainty over responsibility, planners and motives, particularly for Nizam al-Mulk and the Abbasid caliphs.
5. **Qiyama (§41.14).** Changed the statement that Hasan II merely “had a proclamation read” to his historically recorded delivery of a sermon to assembled representatives at Alamut on **8 August 1164 / 17 Ramadan 559 AH**.
6. **Lamasar after 1256 (§41.19).** Corrected the suggestion that it fell soon after Alamut: **Lamasar resisted until 1257**, while Girdkuh continued until **December 1270**. The latter was made more precise in the main narrative and chronology.
7. **Khurshah's end (§41.19).** Replaced vague “died on the way or soon after” with the well-supported report of his **killing by Mongol guards in 1257** during his unsuccessful approach to the Great Khan's court; retained caution about the finer sequence.
8. **Synthesis (§41.20).** Replaced overly conclusive personal blame toward Khurshah with a balanced explanation including Mongol material superiority, siege capabilities, negotiation, and commanders' independent decisions.
9. **Glossary wording.** Changed a categorical description that fidaʾi motives were undocumented to the more accurate assertion that individual motivations and organizational details remain incompletely known.
10. **Responsive chronology tables.** Added narrowly scoped scrollable wrappers around the two wide tables, preserving their headings, rows, values, and anchors.

## 5. Primary-source windows and interpretation

Three labeled source windows were inspected:

- **Nasir-i Khusraw, *Safarnama***: thematic paraphrase of the pilgrimage and observations of Fatimid Cairo. The itinerary predates the Nizari state; the window acknowledges this limit and does not invent a verbatim excerpt.
- **Qiyama of 1164**: comparative thematic synthesis from later hostile chroniclers and Nizari doctrinal materials. The text should **not** be treated as a line-by-line original text of the 1164 proclamation; the surviving transmission differs by source and era.
- **Ata-Malik Juvayni, *Tarikh-i Jahangushay***: appropriately marked paraphrase of library selection/destruction and historical evidence; Juvayni's role as a Mongol administrator and the limitations of his hostile account are disclosed.

No source window is presented as a verified verbatim quotation. Comprehensive manuscript-facsimile collation was outside the audit; a later critical-source edition may add pinpoint book/chapter/page references and explicit manuscript witnesses.

## 7. Limitations, judgment and lock

This is a **consolidated targeted source audit**, not an exhaustive verification of every claim against manuscripts or critical editions. Particular assassination attributions, recollections of early Ismaili leaders, religious details of the Qiyama, and everyday-life reconstructions are necessarily source-limited; the chapter generally distinguishes uncertain tradition from established history. Text-only geography/map exercises require an external atlas, not an embedded map plate. No evidence-based need for further expansion or section proliferation was identified.

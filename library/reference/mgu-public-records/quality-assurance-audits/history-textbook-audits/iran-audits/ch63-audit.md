## Historical and bibliographic verification

1. **Gilan:** Distinguished the 1592 conquest from ensuing fiscal administrative restructuring; did not force a single disputed date for all crown-land arrangements. Check *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Julfa i. Safavid Period” (calls Gilan crown-domain in 1592) and broader scholarship on Gilan's transition.
2. **Royal silk monopoly:** Described the developing restrictions c. 1617–1629 and especially c. 1619, with incomplete enforcement and discontinuation as a formal royal export monopoly after Abbas I; corroborated by *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Commerce vi,” and “Julfa i.”
3. **1593 merchant privilege:** Source Window 1 now identifies the dated royal protection for Khwaja Nazar (Jomada I 1001 AH), reported in Tēr-Yovhaneancʽ, vol. I, pp. 158–159, as summarized in *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Julfa i.” This is not a collated transcription.
4. **Russian–Julfan trade:** Correctly described the 1667 concessions, restricted immediate effect, later renegotiation, and local tolls, drawing on Rudi Matthee, “Anti-Ottoman Politics and Transit Rights,” *Cahiers du Monde russe* 35 (1994), pp. 739 ff. Source Window 2 juxtaposes seventeenth-century treaty provisions with post-Safavid Prize Papers letters instead of misdating those letters.
5. **Armenian–EIC agreement:** Corrected from Madras to **London, 22 June 1688**. The agreement affected trading privileges in India, including Madras. Corroborated in *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Julfa v. Armenians in India,” and “East India Company (British) i.”
6. **EIC correspondence:** Source Window 3 identifies dated English Company instructions to Persia: 20 May 1681 (India Office Records E/3/89) and 6 September 1682 (E/3/90), as cited by Ronald W. Ferrier in *Encyclopaedia Iranica*. The source study explicitly does not treat these as 1622 Hormuz agreements.
7. **Hormuz and Kandahar:** Retained Imam Quli Khan's Safavid command and English naval aid at Hormuz (1622), and Kandahar changes in 1622, 1638, and 1649; precise economic implications are distinguished from military narratives.
8. **Dutch trade:** Replaced the unsupported categorical claim that Bengal silk straightforwardly displaced Iranian exports with a qualified discussion of changing purchases, prices and destinations.
9. **Monetary units:** Retained the Safavid toman accounting value of 10,000 dinars and qualified the abbasi/coin equivalences as historically contingent; *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Coins and Coinage,” and “Abbasi.”
10. **Bibliography:** Retained the 19 principal references; corrected the title of Ina Baghdiantz McCabe's *The Shah's Silk for Europe's Silver: The Eurasian Trade of the Julfa Armenians...*, and the singular title of Matthee's “Merchants in Safavid Iran: Participants and Perception.” Added six targeted links to specialist reference articles in the bibliography. Core titles selectively verified through publisher or library records; a full external library-catalogue audit of every edition and page was **not** performed.

## Disclosed limits

**Original model parity:** The authoritative original `chapter-001-the-iranian-plateau.html` was not present among conversation-mounted files and was not located in the accessible Library search. The revision used the previously completed Chapter 62 shell as the strongest available local template. The chapter therefore is *not* certified byte-for-byte identical to Chapter 1, and any original breadcrumb/header/bottom-navigation placeholders not present in that neighboring shell could not be independently compared. Do not assert original-template exactness.

**Source windows:** All three present editorial document summaries (paraphrases), not quotations from independently collated original-language documents. The 1593 decree and 1667 treaty are supported by published secondary discussion; the later Prize Papers letters are after the period and are explicitly distinguished. A true documentary edition would require examination of original texts and page-level source collation.

**Quantitative exercise:** The sample purchase, shipment and profit numbers are invented and labeled teaching figures. The 1667 rate comparison is historically based, but its example cargo value is hypothetical. No unwarranted empire-wide trade totals are asserted.

**Maps and deployment:** The map study is a text-guided route exercise, not a historical cartographic plate. Live-site components, deployment, external links and browser loading under actual production conditions were not independently checked.

## 3. Principal factual corrections and verification

### 3.1 The Trans-Iranian Railway

The final chapter dates the main building project **1927–1938**, identifies **Kampsax's coordinating appointment in 1933**, describes the **Veresk Bridge completed in 1936**, and gives the **26 August 1938** inauguration of the Caspian-to-Gulf trunk at approximately **1,394 kilometers**. It explains that different foreign contractors participated across construction phases rather than attributing every work to Kampsax. It distinguishes early trunk service from later railway-network extensions and evaluates cost, freight, routing, labor and geography separately. UNESCO's World Heritage account and historical nomination materials underpin these corrections.

**References:** [UNESCO Trans-Iranian Railway](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1585); [UNESCO nomination dossier](https://whc.unesco.org/document/172293); Mikiya Koyagi, *Iran in Motion* (2021).

### 3.2 Railway financing and indirect taxation

The chapter now identifies the tea-and-sugar finance measures as measures enacted **from 1925**, explains the link between earmarked consumer taxes and construction, and distinguishes domestic fiscal independence from reliance on foreign engineering and imported materials. The new source window is labeled as a **bounded paraphrase of a documented legislative program**, not a fabricated literal statutory quotation. The specific primary law's complete original-language wording was **not transcribed or collated article by article**, and the window says so.

**References:** [Encyclopaedia Iranica, Fiscal System v](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/fiscal-system-v-pahlavi-period/); [UNESCO nomination dossier](https://whc.unesco.org/document/172293).

### 3.3 Bank Melli and monetary reform

The revision separates the dates often collapsed into an erroneous single banking event:

- **4 May 1927:** parliamentary authorization of Bank Melli;
- **8 September 1928:** beginning of operations;
- **30 May 1930:** agreement regarding the Imperial Bank's note-issue privilege;
- **1931:** legislation establishing the rial as the basic currency unit;
- **1932:** Bank Melli's own paper currency enters circulation.

The chapter identifies the role of early German banking specialists, describes the limits of public-sector-centered credit allocation, and distinguishes the new monetary institutions from the later Iranian central bank, established after this chapter's period.

**References:** [Encyclopaedia Iranica, Banking i](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/banking-in-iran/banking-i-history-of-banking-in-iran/); [Encyclopaedia Iranica, Eskenās](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/eskenas/); [Encyclopaedia Iranica, Bank-e Markazi](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bank-e-markazi-e-iran-central-bank-of-iran-a-bank-established-under-the-iranian-banking-and-monetary-act-of-7-kordad/).

### 3.4 A necessary correction to the earlier prepublication audit: the 1933 bank

The earlier review identified an **Industrial and Mining Bank in the early 1930s** as an anachronism and rightly warned against conflating it with the later **Industrial and Mining Development Bank** (1959). Further primary-source-oriented research established an important distinction omitted from that initial judgment: **an Agricultural and Industrial Bank was indeed authorized in June 1933** as an institution supporting agricultural and selected industrial credit. The final chapter preserves this authentic institution and explicitly distinguishes it from the postwar industrial-and-mining bank.

This refinement supersedes the earlier review's implication that no early-Pahlavi bank bearing both agricultural and industrial functions existed. The final text also discusses the Agricultural Bank and the later mortgage institution without suggesting that credit was equally available to all peasants.

**References:** [Encyclopaedia Iranica, Banking i](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/banking-in-iran/banking-i-history-of-banking-in-iran/); [Encyclopaedia Iranica, Commerce vii](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/commerce-vii/); scholarly research on the 11 June 1933 bank authorization; [Bank Keshavarzi history](https://www.apraca.org/members-list-iran-bank-keshavarzi/).

### 3.5 Oil concession of 1933

The manuscript traces the 1901 D'Arcy concession, 1908 discovery, 1909 APOC formation, 1912 Abadan refinery, British participation from 1914, the 1932 cancellation, League of Nations involvement, and Sir John Cadman's direct negotiations. It distinguishes the following stages:

- **24 April 1933:** Cadman's meeting with Reza Shah;
- **29 April 1933:** signature of the revised agreement;
- **28 May 1933:** approval by the Majles;
- **29 May 1933:** royal assent.

The factual core of the documentary analysis includes: geographic contraction from about **480,000 to no more than 100,000 square miles by 1938**; concession duration to **31 December 1993**; **four gold shillings per metric ton** sold domestically or exported; the **20% specified-distribution calculation above the £671,250 threshold** (not a flat 20% share of worldwide net profits); **£750,000 guaranteed minimum annual payment**; **£1 million settlement** of outstanding claims; plus tax, training, petroleum-supply, information and arbitration provisions. The narrative examines immediate improvements alongside constraints on future Iranian bargaining power.

The source window names the agreement, dates, and access paths to a published copy. It paraphrases rather than reproducing long verbatim text.

**References:** [Encyclopaedia Iranica, Oil Agreements in Iran](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/oil-agreements-in-iran/); [British Library / Qatar Digital Library, 1933 concession in archival dossier](https://www.qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100055779980.0x000048); R. W. Ferrier (1982); J. H. Bamberg (1994).

### 3.6 Abadan and labor politics

The 1929 oil-workers' strike is described as a concrete episode of collective organization and suppression, with attention to the involvement of workers' families and to the distinction between technical modernization and independent labor rights. The chapter compares the lived conditions of oil employees, railway workers, factory workers and their households rather than treating industrialization as an employer-centered process.

**Reference:** Stephanie Cronin, “Popular Politics, the New State and the Birth of the Iranian Working Class: The 1929 Abadan Oil Refinery Strike,” *Middle Eastern Studies* 46, no. 5 (2010), 699–732, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/00263206.2010.504555).

### 3.7 Education, overseas scholarships, and foreign schools

The chapter preserves the distinction between university foundation by statute in **1934** and its inauguration on **4 February 1935**. It identifies the **1928** overseas scholarship law's commitment to **at least one hundred students annually**, and the scholarly figure of **640 students sent overseas in 1928–1933**. These are not presented as interchangeable measurements. The history of foreign schools now includes the **1939 government decisions** and transfers during **1940**, rather than describing all restrictions as occurring simultaneously.

**References:** [Encyclopaedia Iranica, Higher Education](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/education-xvii-higher-education/); [Education Abroad](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/education-xxii--education-abroad-1/); [Isfahan, Education and Cultural Affairs](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/isfahan-xv-education-and-cultural-affairs/).

### 3.8 Chronological boundary with later national planning

The chapter distinguishes Reza Shah's project-based and ministry-directed development spending from Iran's **first formal Seven-Year Development Plan of 1949**. It does not project that formal postwar planning apparatus back into the 1930s or treat the White Revolution period as the origin of the 1949 plan. Wartime supply operations, Allied occupation and Reza Shah's abdication remain with Chapter 84. Later land-reform, industrial and oil-policy developments are signposted without being narrated prematurely.

## 4. Interpretation and historical method

The final text uses 'developmental state' as an analytical term, not a contemporary official description or an automatic endorsement of success. It explicitly evaluates four intersecting questions:

1. **State capacity:** whether governments gained the ability to tax, finance, construct, administer and maintain institutions;
2. **Economic effect:** the use, output and limitations of a railway, factory, bank, university, or concession;
3. **Distribution:** who paid, worked, benefited, lost access to markets, or experienced coercion;
4. **Dependence:** whether national ownership and finance reduced, displaced or merely altered relations with international markets and specialists.

The two debates represent different, defensible historical interpretations of railway investment and the 1933 oil agreement. They avoid false claims that either subject is settled by an isolated statistic. The chapter's discussions of mortality, injuries, fiscal proportions and estate ownership avoid unsupported precise totals. Pahlavi achievements and authoritarian constraints are both treated as historical evidence requiring explanation, not as mutually exclusive ideological labels.

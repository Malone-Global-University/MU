## 1. Scope and editorial method

The submitted Claude manuscript was the correct Chapter 97 HTML, supplied as `Pasted text(20261127-015911).txt`. The editorial revision was conducted by ChatGPT independently of Claude. The authoritative chapter assignment was checked against `iran-outline-revised(4).txt`; the production and evidence requirements were checked against `iran-master(3).md`. The chapter-specific twenty-section plan and the preceding structural and scholarly audit governed this revision.

The authoritative outline requires military campaigns, volunteer mobilization, conscription, industrial and agricultural disruption, chemical warfare, civilian losses, family experiences, propaganda, and the enduring social memory of the eight-year war. The final chapter retains all of these responsibilities. Chapter 96 provides the origins and outbreak of war; Chapter 98 provides the detailed peace and succession history; Chapter 99 provides postwar reconstruction. Chapter 97 uses later demographic, clinical, and humanitarian evidence only to elucidate the war's continuing consequences.

The original chapter's narrative and established design were preserved rather than replaced. Substantive paragraphs were added to all twenty sections, selected original passages were corrected, economic and humanitarian tables were rebuilt around specific sources, and documentary links and mobile navigation were added. No intermediate Claude revision was required. The present audit independently reviews the original draft and documents ChatGPT's consolidated editorial revision; it is not a claim of independent third-party validation of the final manuscript.

## 3. Consolidated historical correction register

1. **Khorramshahr:** Replaced the imprecise late-October/early-November occupation chronology with October 24, 1980, corroborated by Encyclopaedia Iranica's general war entry. May 24, 1982 remains the date of liberation. Reconciled opening and timeline terminology.
2. **January 1981 counteroffensive:** Replaced the draft's misleading Dehlavieh label with Operation Nasr and the Hoveyzeh context. Avoided a monocausal attribution of the failure to organizational rivalry. The exact operational history was not independently reconstructed from military archives in this revision.
3. **Continuation in 1982:** Corrected Resolution 514's date to July 12, 1982. Distinguished May liberation, June withdrawal announcement, July diplomatic appeal, and the continuation of the war. Added discussion of expellees, reparations, attribution of aggression, security concerns, and competing interpretations. No hypothetical settlement is presented as certain. The identified Cambridge chapter summary on the Iranian decision is a research path, not a claim of full-text examination.
4. **Basij:** Distinguished the November 1979 call, July 10, 1980 legal establishment, and January 1981 incorporation. The Iran Data Portal translation explicitly refers to the July 1980 legal bill. Preserved the institutional chronology of Chapter 95.
5. **Child recruitment and law:** Distinguished the 1977 Additional Protocol I's child-protection norm from treaty status during the war, Iraq's 2010 accession, Iran's signature without ratification, the relevance of customary law, and the 1989 Convention on the Rights of the Child. No unsupported national recruitment total is asserted. Modern treaty and customary-law compilations are not automatically treated as proof that every rule had the identical legal status in the 1980s.
6. **Guard institutional development:** Used the September 7, 1982 statute to establish ministerial and institutional arrangements, distinguished the conventional Ministry of Defense from the Guard Ministry, and elaborated separate command, political, and administrative responsibilities. Exact appointment dates and complete organizational records are not claimed to have been collated.
7. **Petroleum:** Replaced remembered rounded estimates with the EIA annual 1980–1988 series for both Iran and Iraq. Correctly identifies crude oil excluding condensate, annual thousand barrels/day, and separates production from exports and revenue.
8. **Fiscal trends:** Replaced the assertion of continuously widening deficits with Mazarei's period averages and selected-year figures. Explained the partial fiscal retrenchment before the 1986 shock. Distinguished central-government accounts, extra-budgetary organizations, and currency constraints.
9. **Inflation and petroleum earnings:** Added the WDI/FRED annual consumer-price inflation series, plus the Iranica reported Iranian oil revenue observation for 1986. Distinguished national price indexes, rationed prices, and household-specific expenditure. The series is not treated as an informal-market price index.
10. **Agriculture:** Added the published 1979–1987 agricultural growth estimate of 4.1% and discussion of crop incentives, land reform context, subsidies, rural services, and household rationing. Distinguished aggregate output from food security and local damage.
11. **Iraqi financial comparison:** Added the IMF-published selected-year Iraqi oil revenue series. Distinguished nominal dollar revenue, foreign credit, liabilities, public spending, and national production. No precise total of external aid is constructed from incomparable estimates.
12. **Displacement and population:** Replaced the untraceable nationwide displacement range with an explicit limitation and a documented Khorramshahr census series. Confirmed rounded national census values against the Iranica census entry. A missing 1986 municipal observation is not entered as zero. Postwar residents are not interpreted as counts of wartime survivors or displaced persons.
13. **Women's history:** Incorporated the identifiable English-language memoir of Seyyedeh Zahra Hoseyni and Farzaneh's published study. The source-specific claims remain bounded by accessible catalog and publisher descriptions. No invented quotation or claimed reading of unavailable passages.
14. **Urban warfare:** Expanded civilian protection and the distinction between direct attacks on civilians, indiscriminate attacks, proportionality, and precautions. Included UN mediation and Resolution 582; distinguished condemnation from incident-level evidence. Recognized suffering in both countries without treating populations as responsible for government decisions.
15. **1984 UN chemical inquiry:** Corrected the false implication that nerve agents were identified only in later investigations. Rewrote Feature 16 as an explicitly identified editorial paraphrase. The preceding scholarly audit inspected the original report; this revision retrieved the official catalogue and related records, but direct retrieval of the original English document returned HTTP 403. Consequently it relies on the preceding audit for the detailed findings and does not claim a fresh page-by-page reading. No verbatim quotation is presented.
16. **Sardasht and Halabja:** Distinguished the June 1987 Iranian civilian attack from the March 1988 attack in Iraqi Kurdistan within the broader persecution of Iraqi Kurds. Replaced untraceable incident death estimates with bounded descriptions. Introduced the documented 64,190-record medical cohort, not a national count of everyone exposed. The OPCW anniversary record is used for commemoration, not as an epidemiological census.
17. **Human losses and humanitarian obligations:** Rebuilt Feature 18 using the published mortality-review range and registered clinical cohort. Added POW protection, 2003 final exchange, 2008 tripartite mechanism, missing persons, disability, and family care. No mixed Iranian/Iraqi casualty aggregate, military-only death estimate, or exact national wounded count is asserted without a comparable source.
18. **Internationalization:** Retained broad Tanker War history, economic disruption, Gulf financing, the Iran–Contra contradiction, and international diplomacy. Removed unnecessary detailed incident descriptions and unverified aggregate shipping-attack totals. Distinguished outside support from the belligerents' political decisions.
19. **Cultural memory:** Expanded the source-based study of Avini's documentary work, published memoir, institutional historiography, and the Bajoghli–Moosavi research collection. Maintained distinctions between contemporary representation, personal memory, and subsequent official commemoration.
20. **Synthesis:** Expanded the conclusion across military, institutional, economic, social, humanitarian, and cultural transformation. Preserved the distinction between recovering territory, institutional survival, and achieving all declared aims. Resolution 598, acceptance, and the August 20, 1988 ceasefire are distinguished, with detailed termination and succession reserved for Chapter 98.

The two annual series were checked directly against accessible web table text; EIA numbers are reproduced in their published units, and CPI percentages are rounded consistently. The fiscal study and Iraq revenue source were checked against accessible text. The medical review range and cohort population were checked against accessible abstract/indexed text. No attempt was made to derive a single war-cost or mortality total by combining inconsistent categories. Directly comparable nationwide annual military expenditures, Iranian oil export revenues for every year, industrial output by industry, crop-by-crop production, national civilian deaths, national displacement, total wounds, and POW or missing-person totals remain incomplete. The chapter appropriately identifies this evidentiary limitation rather than filling tables with remembered values. These are desirable future research extensions, not prerequisites to an accurate first-edition historical synthesis.

## 8. Remaining limitations and lock decision

The chapter exceeds both the minimum narrative target and the original total-length estimate. The longer manuscript is retained for its historical and instructional value. Quantitative gaps are explicitly acknowledged. The source evidence supports the central chronology, institutional analysis, economic comparison, and humanitarian discussion, but does not represent exhaustive verification of every minor campaign, legal claim, statistic, oral history, or secondary interpretation. The original 1984 UN report could not be reopened during the final pass; its paraphrase is grounded in the prior audit and official document metadata rather than a fresh direct collation. Exact external link reachability and the final site navigation require deployment-specific checks. None of these limits warrants further discretionary expansion of the chapter at this stage.

**Final disposition:** Chapter 97 is substantively complete and approved for first-edition scholarly publication. Lock the chapter after deployment-specific navigation integration. No further discretionary narrative expansion is recommended. Preserve the final HTML and this audit as the chapter's publication record.

## 2. Historical and interpretive corrections

1. **§70.1, Shaqaqi identification:** Ṣādeq Khan Shaqaqi is correctly described as a Shaqaqi leader who previously served the Qajar founder, not erroneously as a member of the Qajar tribal body. Context checked against [*Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Fatḥ-ʿAlī Shah Qājār”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/fath-ali-shah-qajar-2/).
2. **§70.15, Echmiadzin:** The monastery's Armenian ecclesiastical identity and the contested military use of its ostensibly neutral buildings are now explained. It is not simply treated as a Russian-captured fortress in spring 1827. Source: [*Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Aštarak”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/astarak/).
3. **§70.15, Ashtarak/Oshakan:** The August 1827 encounter is characterized as an **Iranian tactical success**, with the Russian relief operation and later strategic outcomes distinguished. Its date is given as 29 August Gregorian / 17 August Old Style in the scholarly account. Source: [*Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Aštarak”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/astarak/).
4. **§70.15, Ardabil and Urmia:** The advance beyond Tabriz and occupation of these towns are correctly located in **early 1828**; the seizure of manuscripts from Shaykh Safi al-Din's shrine in Ardabil is discussed as part of the 1828 operation. Sources: [*Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Ardabīl ii. Modern Ardabīl”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ardabil/ardabil-ii-modern-ardabil/) and [Çaycıoğlu, Turkish Journal of History](https://iupress.istanbul.edu.tr/tr/journal/tjh/article/1826-1829da-erdebil-ahiska-bayezid-ve-erzurumda-bulunan-bazi-yazma-eserlerin-st-petersburga-nakli).
5. **§70.19, succession designation:** Abbas Mirza's 1833 death is separated from the formal assembly of **20 June 1834** confirming Mohammad Mirza's position. The chronology of court deliberation and foreign diplomatic involvement is developed. Source: [*Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Moḥammad Shah Qājār”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/mohammad-shah/).
6. **§70.19, accession and coronation:** Mohammad Mirza's recognition in **November 1834** is distinguished from his ceremonial coronation in Tehran on **14 January 1835**. Source: same *Iranica* article.
7. **Campaign depth:** Additional analysis examines Russian garrisons and local rulers, the territorial challenge after Ganja, cavalry and infantry integration, military finance, fortress logistics, the 1807 Franco-Russian realignment, the 1812–13 defeats, the unresolved 1813 border, the 1826 crisis, and the distinct stages of the 1827–28 campaign. Additional civilian and institutional history addresses tax intermediaries, requisitions, religious endowments, displacement, and the political consequences of defeat.

## 3. Primary-source and bibliography corrections

- **Portrait window (§70.2):** A precise reference to Museum of Islamic Art Doha object **PA.18.2010** is now linked. Its catalog describes the shah **seated on a floor covering with a pearl-embroidered bolster, ceremonial mace and sword**, not enthroned. [Qatar Museums object record](https://collections.qm.org.qa/en/objects/portrait-of-fath-ali-shah-qajar-pa182010).
- **Finkenstein window (§70.8):** Document explicitly dated **4 May 1807**, described as an attributed paraphrase, with the published documentary tradition identified through Hurewitz and the reference discussion in [*Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “France iii”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/france-iii-relations-with-persia-1789-1918/). No unverified verbatim treaty language added.
- **1826 declaration window (§70.13):** The Russian declaration of **October 1826** is identified via the translated textual reference in Saʿid Nafisi, *Tarikh-e ejtemaʿi va siyasi-ye Iran dar dowran-e moʿaser*, vol. 2, pp. 124–127, as documented in the references to Maziar Behrooz, “Revisiting the Second Russo-Iranian War (1826–28): Causes and Perceptions,” *Iranian Studies* (2013), [DOI 10.1080/00210862.2012.758502](https://doi.org/10.1080/00210862.2012.758502). Explanatory comparison is explicitly an analytical paraphrase rather than a verified line-by-line transcription.
- **Further Reading:** Replaced the provisional list with identified publications and relevant source and museum links; removed editor-facing instructions to verify titles before publishing. Major sources include Muriel Atkin, *Russia and Iran, 1780–1828* (Minnesota, 1980); Gavin R. G. Hambly in *The Cambridge History of Iran*, vol. 7 (1991); Hamid Algar, *Religion and State in Iran, 1785–1906* (1969); and Abbas Amanat, *Iran: A Modern History* (2017).

**Source limitations:** The three teaching windows are documentary analysis / clearly labeled paraphrase, not verified quotations from manuscript or complete original-language treaty collation. The 1826 declaration's independent text and the individual Iranian documents used for comparison were not personally collated from archival originals. A future critical edition should document exact archival folios and translations. These limitations are disclosed, not represented as completed verification.

## 1. Scope and editorial decisions

The authoritative outline assigns this chapter the embassy seizure, hostage diplomacy, factional competition, the weakening of provisional and liberal forces, and the domestic and international consequences of the crisis. The revision retains the original Iranian-centered interpretation, five division banners, twenty numbered main sections, twenty integrated instructional features, original chapter shell and diplomatic process illustration. The narrative concentrates on the period from the revolutionary transition of 1979 to the January 1981 settlement, with a short legal epilogue for the May 1981 discontinuance of the ICJ proceedings and a retrospective historiographical discussion.

The intervention was a consolidated expansion and correction, not a new chapter constructed from scratch. The chapter does not reproduce the earlier history of the 1953 coup (Chapter 87), the revolutionary mobilization and collapse of monarchy (Chapters 91–92), or the original constitutional framework (Chapter 93) at length. The June 1981 removal of Bani-Sadr and detailed subsequent repression remain for Chapter 95; the origins and campaigns of the Iran–Iraq War remain principally for Chapters 96–97. Later sanctions regimes receive only contextual references. The treatment of the failed military mission emphasizes diplomatic, institutional and human consequences rather than operational reconstruction.

## 3. Consolidated factual and interpretive review

| Finding from initial audit | Completed intervention | Documentary basis / limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Khomeini's message incorrectly placed several weeks before the seizure | Corrected to November 1, 1979, and separated the general message from claims of advance authorization and the subsequent endorsement. | ICJ Case 64, merits judgment, particularly its discussion of attribution; FRUS and Iranica context. No definitive determination of advance authorization is asserted. |
| Bazargan's prior complaints and resignation dates | Distinguished November 5 resignation from November 6 acceptance; attributed his earlier knife analogy to contemporary September 1 reporting and explained institutional weaknesses. | Contemporary Washington Post reporting; Iranica biography of Khomeini. The analogy is paraphrased. |
| Approximate hostage accounting | Replaced uncertainty with the Carter Library accounting: 66 initially held, 13 released November 19–20, 1979, Richard Queen released July 11, 1980, and 52 released January 20, 1981. Six Americans who avoided capture remain outside that count. | Carter Library hostage accounting and NARA chronology. |
| UN commission said to have achieved neither objective | Explained testimony from Iranian witnesses and meetings with officials, while distinguishing failure to obtain acceptable access to the hostages and failure to settle the crisis. Added March 11 departure. | FRUS XI/1, documents 189 and 203. |
| April custody-transfer arrangement vague | Added March 31–April 4 negotiations, the conditional proposal, reported failure to implement it, and April 7 rupture. Distinguished transfer of custody from final release. | FRUS XI/1, documents 233, 238, 240. American diplomatic reports are evidence of negotiation, not omniscient accounts of Iranian internal deliberations. |
| ICJ discontinuance and legal nuance | Specified application November 29, 1979; provisional measures December 15; merits judgment May 24, 1980; discontinuance May 12, 1981. Distinguished initial protection failure from official endorsement and continued detention; added the Court's concern over the American military operation. | ICJ Case 64, including judgment paragraphs 56–74, 83–87 and 93 and the later order. Discontinuance is not a reversal of the judgment. |
| Frozen assets and sanctions imprecise | Identified Executive Orders 12170, 12205, 12211 and 12282; clarified jurisdiction and freezing versus confiscation. Separated contemporary estimates of total blocked property and liquid assets from the later escrow package. | NARA executive-order texts; FRUS XI/1, documents 30 and 48. Contemporary valuations were estimates and are not added together. |
| Bani-Sadr's election and military role hedged | Added official 10,709,330 votes (75.6%) and the February 19, 1980 delegation of command. Distinguished delegated military authority from the powers of the presidency under the original constitution. | Iran Data Portal; translated appointment decree; FRUS contemporary context. Electoral results remain officially reported results. |
| Embassy-document discussion abstract; carpet-weaver anecdote unsupported | Removed the anecdote, added the documented December 1979 arrest of Abbas Amir-Entezam, and distinguished contacts and accusations from established evidence of espionage. | FRUS XI/1, document 58; retrospective U.S. district-court memorandum, Amirentezam v. Islamic Republic of Iran (2023). Detailed subsequent imprisonment reserved for Chapter 95. |
| Rescue failure framed as eliminating military action altogether | Replaced categorical claim with reduced confidence in military rescue; expanded discussion of Vance's resignation, Iranian political interpretations and diplomatic implications. Removed unnecessary tactical reconstruction. | NARA documentary chronology and ICJ judgment. |
| November parliamentary resolution imprecise | Identified November 2, 1980, and linked the legislative mandate to the formal text of the settlement; distinguished September 12 conditions from the later Iraqi invasion. | Opening of the General Declaration; Iranica context. |
| Algiers financial arrangements underdeveloped | Explained initial $7.955 billion escrow package, $3.667 billion loan allocation, $1.418 billion retained for specified banking matters, and $2.870 billion arithmetic residual directed to Bank Markazi; distinguished these from the separate $1 billion award-security mechanism and total recovered assets. | General Declaration paragraphs 6–7 and related January 19 Undertakings, paragraphs 1–2; the $2.870 billion is derived from the stated figures, not a separate valuation of total recoveries. |
| Claims Tribunal insufficiently specified | Identified nine arbitrators, three appointed by each government and three neutral members; clarified qualifying claims, exclusions, binding awards, and institutional independence from the ICJ. | Claims Settlement Declaration Articles II–III; Tribunal institutional materials. |
| Election-timing debate lacked later evidence | Added the March 2023 Barnes account of Connally's activities, retaining the earlier congressional findings and the absence of demonstrated Iranian acceptance of an agreement. | Contemporary report of Barnes interview and reporting on congressional inquiries, plus 1993 Joint Report bibliographic record. No exhaustive reassessment of all congressional exhibits claimed. |

The final narrative maintains a distinction between explanation of historical grievances and legal justification, and does not confuse the students' initiative, Khomeini's endorsement, Iranian state responsibility, or the later transfer of political responsibility to the Majles. It avoids describing the revolutionary coalition as a single political actor or attributing all later repression and factional consolidation to one diplomatic episode.

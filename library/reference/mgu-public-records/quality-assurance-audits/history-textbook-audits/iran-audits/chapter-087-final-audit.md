## 3. Consequential historical corrections

### A. Article 48 and the Senate — §§87.1 and 87.18; review terminology

The draft incorrectly claimed that the 1949 amendments gave the Shah authority to dissolve parliament **with the consent of the Senate**. The revised narrative correctly states that the amendment of 8 May 1949 authorized the Shah to dissolve the Majles, the Senate, or both, subject to requirements concerning reasons and new elections; it did not make Senate consent a prerequisite. The constitutional dispute over dismissal of a prime minister remains correctly distinguished from the formal dissolution provision.

The draft also said that the Senate was *created* by the 1949 amendments. The revised narrative and glossary now distinguish the original 1906 constitutional provision for a sixty-member upper chamber from the Senate's first actual convening in 1950, and from the separate 1949 enlargement of royal powers. This correction maintains continuity with the Chapter 85 audit.

**Verification:** [Iran Data Portal, English translation of the Constitution including amended Article 48](https://irandataportal.syr.edu/wp-content/uploads/iran1949.pdf); [*Encyclopaedia Iranica*, Constitutional Revolution III](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/constitutional-revolution-iii/).

### B. The referendum — §87.1 and Chronology Preview

The original draft described 3 August 1953 as though the referendum was held on a single day. The revised chapter specifies the staged voting: Tehran on **3 August** and other parts of the country on **10 August**. It retains the important discussion of separate voting arrangements and the contested constitutional authority of the referendum, without asserting a legal conclusion that the evidence cannot establish unambiguously.

**Verification:** [*Foreign Relations of the United States* (FRUS), contemporaneous report of the planned 3/10 August schedule](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1951-54Iran/d257).

### C. Colonel Nasiri and the first attempt — Opening and §87.5

The original opening implied that Nasiri was arrested **before the royal decree reached the residence**. Contemporary accounts describe a copy being delivered to an officer responsible for the residence's defense, followed by Nasiri's arrest. The opening has been corrected to avoid a definitive claim that no document was presented. The operative point remains the failure of the planned seizure on the night of 15–16 August. The constitutional authenticity and legal effect of the farmans are distinguished from the military operation attempting to enforce them.

**Verification:** [Contemporary British political review, September 1953, National Security Archive](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/media/16421/ocr); [FRUS, 16 August 1953 report](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1951-54IranEd2/d266). Descriptions of the exact point of delivery still vary among sources.

### D. Shaban Jafari and the mobilizations of 19 August — §87.7

The original draft incorrectly placed Shaban Jafari among the leaders of the **initial morning** pro-Shah mobilization. The revised chapter distinguishes those early organizers from Jafari, who was still imprisoned when the events began, was released **in the afternoon of 19 August**, and joined the demonstrations thereafter. The crowd narrative also separates organized or paid mobilizers, local royalists and clerical networks, people joining during the day, and defections by armed personnel.

**Verification:** [Houchang E. Chehabi, “Jaʿfari, Šaʿbān,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/jafari-saban/); [National Security Archive, British political review](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/media/16421/ocr).

### E. Military success, street clearing, and historical causation — §§87.6–87.8, 87.12

The revised reconstruction supplies fuller analysis of the initial conspiracy's failure, the Shah's flight, 17–18 August political mobilization, Mossadegh's dispersal orders, the mixed character of the 19 August crowds, changes in military allegiance, the seizure of Radio Tehran, and fighting at Mossadegh's residence. It treats the claim that the 18 August orders *caused* defeat as an interpretation rather than a conclusively documented single cause. It also distinguishes propagandists' expectations from orders actually followed and avoids invented precise casualty totals.

**Verification and interpretive controls:** [FRUS retrospective CIA operational summary](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1951-54IranEd2/d363); [*Encyclopaedia Iranica*, 1953 coup](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/coup-detat-1953/); [National Security Archive, updated historical documentation](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/iran/2017-06-15/iran-1953-state-department-finally-releases-updated-official-history).

### F. Trials, repression, and the Tudeh organization — §§87.10–87.11

Mossadegh's trial and **21 December 1953** sentence have been explained in their institutional setting. The source window now points to a declassified CIA historical compilation whose appendix reproduces a translated military court decision. That document is treated as a state judicial record requiring contextual scrutiny, not as neutral proof that the state prosecution was substantively justified. The discussion of Hossein Fatemi distinguishes his later arrest, trial, and **November 1954** execution from Mossadegh's sentence. The 1954 uncovering of the Tudeh military organization is separated from the immediate post-coup arrests; the commonly cited figure of **548 arrested network members** is explicitly attributed rather than presented as a measurement of Tudeh combat power. The killing of three students at Tehran University on **7 December 1953** is appropriately identified.

**Verification:** [CIA internal history, Appendix F: judicial proceedings](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/sites/default/files/documents/4404303/Document-3-Central-Intelligence-Agency-History.pdf); [*Encyclopaedia Iranica*, Communism II](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/communism-ii/); [*Encyclopaedia Iranica*, chronology](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/chronology-of-iranian-history-part-3/); [*Encyclopaedia Iranica*, Hossein Fatemi](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/fatemi/).

### G. The 1954 oil agreement — §§87.13–87.16

The former division's principal weakness was the avoidance of verifiable terms. It now explicitly distinguishes **formal national ownership**, **operational authority**, **marketing**, **consortium equity participation**, and **Iran's profit share**. The original consortium equity percentages—**AIOC/BP 40%, five American majors 40%, Royal Dutch Shell 14%, CFP 6%**—remain intact and are correctly distinguished from the fifty-fifty profit framework.

The revised narrative specifies:

- **Legislative approval:** Majles **21 October 1954**, Senate **28 October 1954**, royal assent **29 October 1954**; a contemporary official appendix separately labels a signed instrument **30 October**, so the stages are not collapsed into one date.
- **Duration:** Initial **25 years**, with up to **three five-year extensions** conditional upon specified area reductions.
- **Operating structure:** Consortium-led production and refining arrangements, NIOC's legal title, and facilities retained under NIOC's independent operational responsibilities.
- **Compensation:** **£25 million** from the Iranian government, paid in ten interest-free annual installments beginning in 1957; approximately **£214 million** from other consortium participants as consideration for their participating interests. These are distinct obligations, not percentages of oil profits and not simultaneous direct costs to the Iranian treasury.
- **Government income:** Estimated receipts in a contemporary intelligence forecast distinguished from retrospectively reported approximately **$90 million in 1955** and **$285 million in 1960**. Historical figures are not treated as interchangeable merely because they are expressed in dollars.

The final account evaluates the settlement as financially consequential while falling short of the full operational control sought by the 1951 nationalization movement. It avoids anachronistically describing the 1954 agreement as either a complete reinstatement of the 1933 concession or the fulfillment of unrestricted national management.

**Verification:** [FRUS, official summary of the 1954 agreement](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1951-54IranEd2/d375); [FRUS, 21 October parliamentary vote](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v10/d501); [FRUS, Senate approval](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v10/d502); [UK House of Commons, 1 November 1954](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1954/nov/01/persian-oil-agreement); [Parviz Mina, “Oil Agreements in Iran,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/oil-agreements-in-iran/).

### H. Zahedi and Western alignment — §§87.17–87.19

The date of Zahedi's replacement by Hossein Ala is identified as **5 April 1955**. The narrative separates this turning point in domestic power from the later fully developed authoritarian system. Iranian entry into the Baghdad Pact is dated to **October 1955**, and the chapter makes clear that the United States supported the organization without being an original full member.

**Verification:** [*Encyclopaedia Iranica*, Hossein Ala](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ala-hosayn/); [FRUS, April 1955 assessment](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v12/d304).

## 6. Remaining historical and evidentiary limitations

1. **Coup causation and personal intentions:** No single source can isolate the independent contribution of each crowd organizer, officer, cleric, intelligence official, or civilian demonstrator. The revised text explains what is documented and marks causal inferences rather than manufacturing certainty.
2. **Casualties and repression figures:** Contemporary estimates differ by time period, geographical extent, source, and categories counted. The chapter therefore avoids unsupported aggregate mortality and execution totals. The cited count for the 1954 Tudeh military arrests is identified by source and scope.
3. **Primary-source status:** Retrospective intelligence histories and translated trial materials were compiled or selected by interested institutions. Their evidentiary value is substantial but they should not be equated automatically with full archival records of every contemporaneous decision.
4. **Contract nomenclature:** Some source descriptions differ in how they date signature or characterize the incorporation/registration of operating companies. The chapter prioritizes the well-supported division of legal title and operational control and explicitly distinguishes ratification and assent from separate documentary dating.
5. **Geography illustrations and live publication:** Maps are intended for later addition by the project editor. Domain-specific navigation and final website deployment remain external editorial tasks.

These limitations do not prevent a scholarly content lock because the chapter does not use unresolved numerical or procedural points as foundations for unsupported conclusions.

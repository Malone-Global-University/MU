## III. Independently checked historical and documentary anchors

1. **Career and political setting.** Rashid al-Din (c. 1247–1318), an Ilkhanid physician and minister, held high office during Ghazan and Öljeitü's reigns. Co-vizierial appointment around 1298, the patronage of Ghazan and Öljeitü, and dynastic perspective are consistent with the scholarly synthesis and *Encyclopaedia Iranica* ([Il-Khanid dynastic history](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/il-khanids-i-dynastic-history/); [Melville's Jāmeʿ al-tawāriḵ entry](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/jame-al-tawarik/)). His earlier conversion to Islam is not assigned an artificially precise date.
2. **Composition chronology.** The Ghazan-centered dynastic history is situated principally around 1302–1304, while the expansion under Öljeitü is distinguished from a reported presentation on 14 April 1307. The latter date is attributed to Qashani rather than presented as proof that all illustrations and later copies were finished. See Stefan Kamola, [*Making Mongol History* (2019)](https://academic.oup.com/edinburgh-scholarship-online/book/34045), especially the chapter on projects under Öljeitü and the publication on the Salghurid history.
3. **Mongol source access.** Bolad's important role, references to privileged records associated with the *Altan Debter*, and differences between oral information and written records are presented in terms of scholarly reconstruction. No independently surviving complete Golden Register is claimed. [Melville, *Encyclopaedia Iranica*](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/jame-al-tawarik/).
4. **World-historical materials.** The accounts of Iranian/Islamic tradition, Chinese dynasties, India/Buddhism (including al-Biruni and Kamalashri), and Franks/Jews are separated by source families and mediation. The book is not called the uncontested first universal history. [Melville](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/jame-al-tawarik/); Peter Jackson, [*The Mongols and the Islamic World* (2017)](https://academic.oup.com/yale-scholarship-online/book/35772).
5. **Endowment.** The dated waqf deed (1 Rabiʿ I 709 AH / 9 August 1309) and subsequent copying-program changes are described as institutional prescriptions whose complete implementation cannot be assumed. [Sheila Blair on illustrations and copying](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/jame-tawarikh-ii/); Nourane Ben Azzouna, “Manuscript Production Project ... Reconsidered” (2014), cited in the chapter source guide.
6. **Manuscript provenance.** Edinburgh Or Ms 20 is itself an undated early Arabic fragment; its companion Khalili MSS 727 is associated with 714 AH / 1314–1315 CE. The chapter does not assign that precise date independently to every Edinburgh folio. [University of Edinburgh manuscript record](https://archives.collections.ed.ac.uk/repositories/2/archival_objects/145535); [Khalili Collections manuscript record](https://khalilicollections.org/collections/islamic-art/khalili-collection-islamic-art-the-jami-al-tawarikh-of-rashid-al-din-mss727/).
7. **Timurid reuse and additions.** Later manuscript transmission, including manuscript Hazine 1653 and Hafiz-i Abru's interventions, is identified as reception and not silently attributed to Rashid al-Din's original writing. [Sheila Blair, *Encyclopaedia Iranica*](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/jame-tawarikh-ii/); [Kamola's study of reception](https://academic.oup.com/edinburgh-scholarship-online/book/34045/chapter-abstract/288966934).
8. **Executions under separate rulers.** Saʿd al-Din Savaji was executed in 1312 under Öljeitü; Rashid al-Din was executed in July 1318 under Abu Saʿid, amid rivalry with Taj al-Din ʿAli Shah. This avoids conflating the two executions. [*Encyclopaedia Iranica*, ʿAlišah](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/alisah-taj-al-din-vizier/).

The narrative avoids invented day-level dates for uncertain stages, does not treat modern national borders as timeless, and distinguishes what the manuscripts prove from what patronial statements or court histories claim.

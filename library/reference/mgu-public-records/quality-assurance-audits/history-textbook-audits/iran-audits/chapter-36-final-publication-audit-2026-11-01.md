## 3. Historical correction register — incorporated in final HTML

1. **§36.29: Buyid succession after 983.** Removed the claim that ʿAḍud al-Dawla designated Samsam al-Dawla successor. Specialist historical scholarship states that he **had not designated a successor**. Samsam happened to be in Baghdad and was proclaimed there; Sharaf contested from Kirman. Corresponding analytical reference to “ʿAḍud al-Dawla's arrangements” updated.
   - Source: Tilman Nagel, “BUYIDS,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/buyids/ .
2. **§36.32, opening chronology, and chapter summary: Fars's end in 1062.** Replaced the false attribution of Shiraz's capture in 1062 to **Alp Arslan** (who did not become sultan until 1063). Shabankara leader **Fażlūya** took Shiraz about **1056–57** and overthrew/killed the last Buyid claimant **Fulad Sutun** in **1062**. Seljuk prince **Qavurt** then took Shiraz in 1062; significant Alp Arslan campaigns occurred later in the 1060s. Explained the difference between the 1055 loss of Baghdad and the last Buyid dynasty's 1062 extinction; avoided claiming seven years of stable independent rule in Fars.
   - Sources: “FĀRS iii. History in the Islamic Period,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/fars-iii/ ; “ALP ARSLĀN,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/alp-arslan-saljuq-sultan/ .
3. **§36.29: Samsam al-Dawla.** Specified assassination by a Buyid prince near Shiraz late in **998** rather than a contextless death.
   - Source: “BUYIDS,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*.
4. **Opening and §36.3: Buya's background.** Removed an unsupported categorical description of Abu Shuja Buya as a “chieftain”; later sources describe the family as of modest background, with a fisherman story and doubtful claims of Sasanian lineage.
   - Sources: “BUYIDS” and “ʿEMĀD-AL-DAWLA,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/emad-al-dawla/ .
5. **§36.17: Band-e Amir.** Included the specialist work's approximate construction year **975**, retaining appropriate caveats about original extent and later renovations.
   - Source: Judith A. Lerner, “BAND-E AMĪR,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/band-e-amir-the-amirs-dike-or-band-e-azodi-a-dam-or-weir-constructed-across-the-kor-river-at-the-southeast-end-of-the-m/ .
6. **§36.25: Ghadir Khumm.** Clarified that the chroniclers place public celebration of both Ashura and Ghadir Khumm in **352 AH / 963 CE**, rather than an unspecified period within a year.
   - Source: Ahmad Kazemi-Moussavi, “ḠADĪR ḴOMM ii. Ḡadīr Festival,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/gadir-komm/gadir-komm-ii-gadir-festival-id-e-gadir/ .
7. **§36.23, al-Tanukhi Primary Source Window.** Explicitly identified the prose as a **thematic synthesis of the collection**, not as a quotation or paraphrase of one identifiable anecdote. Clarified that particular anecdotes require consultation of editions or translations before being used as discrete primary evidence.
   - Background source: Hugh Kennedy, *The Prophet and the Age of the Caliphates*, description of al-Tanukhi's *Nishwār al-muḥāḍara* and *al-Faraj baʿd al-shidda*; https://erenow.org/ancient/prophet-and-the-age-of-the-caliphates/17.php .

## 4. Other historical checkpoints reviewed

- **19 December 945 = 11 Jumada I 334 AH:** supported by the specialist Buyid entry in *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, as are the timing of the titles and appointment as *amir al-umara*.
- **Muʿizz al-Dawla's 963 public Shiʿi rituals:** supported by *Encyclopaedia Iranica*'s Ghadir article.
- **981 Gurgan campaign / Qabus's displacement:** the general chronology matches the established episode of the flight of Fakhr al-Dawla and Qabus; avoid spurious precision in attribution to ʿAḍud's own troops versus his brother's.
- **Buyid recovery and loss of Basra:** broad, hedged dating in the manuscript is suitable because possession changed amid wider regional conflict.
- **ʿAḍudi hospital:** the text cautiously says founded around 981–982 and roughly two dozen physicians as reported in later accounts, rather than a documented exact initial staffing census; this is appropriate.
- **al-Muqaddasi on Shiraz's library:** the details about vaulted halls, shelves, catalogues and library officials are consistent with literature citing *Aḥsan al-taqāsīm* (e.g., al-Muqaddasi, p. 449, as quoted in the SOAS scholarship). This window is explicitly a paraphrase; no invented direct quotations.
- **Ibn Miskawayh on the 945 takeover:** the primary-source window draws on the chronicle narrative and correctly states its perspective and limitations, but should not be mistaken for a line-for-line verified translation.

**Audit scope:** A targeted specialist-source verification of historical pressure points and narrative consistency, **not** claim-by-claim primary-source collation of the entire chapter. The al-Tanukhi feature remains an explicitly labeled thematic synthesis rather than a line-specific passage; it should not be quoted as if it were a transcript of an individual anecdote.

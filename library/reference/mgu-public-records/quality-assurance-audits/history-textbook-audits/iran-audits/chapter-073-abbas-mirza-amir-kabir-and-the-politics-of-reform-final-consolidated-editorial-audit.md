## 3. Historical corrections and substantive additions

- **§73.3:** Added the material requirements of regular infantry and artillery, the continuing tactical role of cavalry, and cautiously contextualized observer-based force-strength estimates. Source: Heribert Busse, *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Abbas Mirza Qajar.”
- **§73.6:** Identified the 1811 students Hajji Baba Afshar and Mohammad Kazem and noted Kazem’s death in 1813, as well as the careers of Hajji Baba, Mirza Saleh Shirazi, Mirza Ja’far Khan Tabrizi, and Mirza Reza Mohandes-bashi. The two-student (1811) and five-student (1815) counts are retained. Sources: *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Education Abroad,” “Haji Baba Afshar,” and “Dar al-Fonun.”
- **§73.7:** Clarified Abbas Mirza’s movable-type printing patronage around 1816–1817; distinguished preexisting Armenian printing from Tabriz typography and documented lithographic operation by 1832–1833. Sources: *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Chap” and “Lithography i.”
- **§73.9:** Treated Haji Mirza Aqasi’s imperfect but real military and educational initiatives rather than relying only on hostile retrospective characterizations.
- **§73.10:** Corrected the birthplace to **Hazava** (Farahan); clarified administrative training, the 1829–1830 mission to Russia, and the significance of long-duration negotiations at Erzurum. Source: Hamid Algar, *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Amir Kabir.”
- **§73.11:** Distinguished Amir-e Nezam from the additional Amir-e Kabir and Atabak honors in **October 1848**; added his **17 February 1849** marriage to Ezzat al-Dowleh and the **March 1849** military mutiny against him. Source: Algar, “Amir Kabir.”
- **§§73.13–73.15:** Deepened the mechanisms of fiscal supervision, payroll verification, provincial tax assessment, military recruitment, industrial sponsorship, vaccination, minority policies, and coercive centralization. §73.14 identifies Soltan Morad Mirza as commander and the execution of Hasan Khan Salar and relatives in **1850**.
- **§73.16:** Corrected the misleading suggestion that the earlier 1837 newspaper was irregular: Mirza Saleh began a regularly issued newspaper in May **1837**. The 1851 gazette is distinguished as the continuing **official government** paper. Source: *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Chap.”
- **§§73.17–73.18:** Added the 1850 commissioning, building plan, August 1851 staff contracts, November arrival of the European teachers, their specialties, Iranian translators, the **28 November 1851** age/class admissions criteria, first-year enrollment of approximately 105, and financial support. Dar al-Funun’s opening remains **29 December 1851**, between dismissal and execution. Source: John D. Gurney and Negin Nabavi, “Dar al-Fonun.”
- **§§73.19–73.20:** Expanded causal analysis of ministerial vulnerability and clarified the different durability of technical education, trained personnel, fiscal policy, royal patronage, and administrative accountability. Avoided teleological claims equating reform with constitutionalism.

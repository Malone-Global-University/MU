## 3. Publication-relevant historical and source-attribution corrections made

1. **§37.17: Hasanak's execution.** Replaced the unsupported narrow dating **1032–1033** with **1031**, acknowledging that some secondary references use **1032**. TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi provides 28 Ṣafar 422 / 24 February 1031 with reference to Bayhaqi; *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, Āl-e Mīkāl, also dates him to 1031, while its Fatimids entry uses 1032. Sources: [TDV, Mīkālīler](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/mikaliler), [Iranica, Āl-e Mīkāl](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/al-e-mikal/), [Iranica, Fatimids](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/fatimids/).
2. **§37.30 and Chronology Preview: Dandanqan.** Changed broad “late May/May 1040” to **23 May 1040**, the date given by *Encyclopaedia Iranica* from 8 Ramażān 431 AH. Source: [Iranica, Dandānqān](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/dandanqan-a-small-town-of-medieval-khorasan-in-the-qara-qum-or-sandy-desert-between-marv-and-saraks-10-farsaks-from/).
3. **§37.34: Khusrau Shah, the Oghuz, and Lahore.** Removed the categorical assertion that **Khusrau Shah personally lost Ghazna to the Oghuz and withdrew to Lahore**. Specialist scholarship finds that he appears to have retained Ghazna for at least part of c.1157–1160, that the Oghuz takeover was around 1160 or soon afterward, and that the exact timing of the relocation to Lahore is uncertain. The text now attributes the later consolidation at Lahore to Khusrau Malik without false precision. The Chapter Opening's too-early “from the 1150s ... centered on Lahore” was also qualified. Sources: [Iranica, Ḵosrowšāh](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/kosrowsah-b-bahramsah/); [Iranica, Ghaznavids](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ghaznavids/).
4. **Historical Context: Abbasid caliph.** Replaced the statement that the caliph was literally a **“political prisoner of the Buyids”** with a more accurate distinction between Buyid military domination and the caliph's continuing religious and ceremonial position. Compare Chapter 36's established treatment.
5. **§37.6, al-ʿUtbi source window.** Clarified that the description is a **thematic synopsis of the chronicle**, not a translation or paraphrase of one individually identified passage. The text cannot be mistaken for source-specific quotation or close collation.
6. **Responsive stylesheet.** Added a narrow-screen `table.chron-table` overflow rule so wide chronology tables scroll inside the column without horizontal page overflow.

No sections or teaching features were removed or summarized to achieve these corrections.

## 5. Primary-source treatment and critical limits

- **Al-ʿUtbi:** Appropriately characterized as rhetorical court-connected historiography; now unmistakably marked thematic rather than an identifiable translated passage. An exact primary text should be supplied before making passage-specific linguistic claims.
- **Somnath:** The multi-source window compares near-contemporary Persian court poetry, al-Biruni, later Persian and Arabic chronicles, and the later Indian epigraphic record. It accurately warns about the radically different time horizons, contextual agendas, and unreliable numbers. Academic discussion of Farrukhi's Manat analogy supports that framing: [*Philological Encounters* (2023)](https://melc.washington.edu/sites/melc/files/documents/research/24519197_bja10039_text.pdf). Do not treat any retrospective synthesis as a directly translated unified primary source.
- **Al-Biruni:** Thematically consistent with his *India* introduction and the historiography of Mahmud's campaign, including his concern about obstacles to understanding Indian learning. The window is explicitly paraphrased, not a quotation; his methodological independence does not imply he was unconnected to Ghaznavid patronage.
- **Bayhaqi:** Refers specifically to the Hasanak execution, a documented narrative in the surviving chronicle; source perspective and temporal distance are acknowledged. See [Iranica, Bayhaqi](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bayhaqi-abul-fazl-mohammad-b/).

No fabricated direct quotation was found in the four window bodies. The audit does not claim complete collation against Arabic/Persian editions or comprehensive validation of every historical assertion.

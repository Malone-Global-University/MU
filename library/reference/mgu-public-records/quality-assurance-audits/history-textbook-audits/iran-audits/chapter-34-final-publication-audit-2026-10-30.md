## Repairs made directly in the final HTML

1. **Abdallah ibn Tahir's death:** changed narrative and chronological references from 845 to **December 844**, consistent with the usual specialist dating (Rabīʿ I 230 AH). All affected timeline, people and review references harmonized.
2. **Tahir's political independence:** included the material numismatic evidence that *some* issues from Tahir's governorship omitted al-Maʾmun's name; qualified its significance and aligned the Historical Debate, rather than relying solely on the later khutba account.
3. **Al-Bukhari and the governor of Bukhara:** removed the unsupported assertion that the governor who allegedly expelled al-Bukhari was specifically a **Tahirid-appointed** official; the incident remains explicitly a later biographical report.
4. **Muhammad ibn Tahir's later career:** corrected the claim that the deposed governor disappeared from effective politics: specialist sources place him subsequently in Baghdad security office into the 880s, with conflicting accounts of his immediate captivity.
5. **Rafiʿ ibn Harthama:** avoided pinning down an unverified specific alliance with the caliph's brother, while preserving his well-supported opposition to Amr and defeat by 896.
6. **Battle of Balkh campaign:** corrected the reversed Oxus crossing. **Ismaʿil ibn Ahmad** crossed from Transoxiana into Khurasan; Saffarid forward forces suffered defeat in 899, followed by the decisive Samanid victory near Balkh in 900. Removed an unsupported precise comparative army-size claim.
7. **Aftermath of 900:** clarified that Khurasan passed to the Samanids immediately but Saffarid successors retained or contested Kirman, Fars and Khuzestan for a period, rather than losing all western provinces immediately.
8. **Chronology table:** placed the 861 rise of Yaʿqub before the 864/865 establishment of the Zaydi state in Tabaristan.
9. **Responsive layout:** added a narrow-screen scrolling rule for chronology/comparison tables; no broader visual redesign.

## 5. Evidence and interpretive limits

The chapter includes 32 numbered linked reference entries, with one intentionally repeated Iranica URL for different historical applications. This is a focused documentary review, not a line-by-line verification of every sentence. Accessible treaty text, UN catalog records, official diplomatic records, scholarship abstracts and selected historical reference entries support the principal dates and legal propositions. The full original French treaty, the complete 1937 treaty file, Iranian and Iraqi archival collections, regional demographic/economic statistics, and the listed monographs were not comprehensively collated. The 1991 PDF page 2 was visually inspected; the Resolution 479 text was retrieved but its screenshot was blocked. The full Brands green-light article was not retrieved (403); the research question and standard of evidence are discussed without claiming the article was read in full. The September 17 speech is not represented as a verbatim transcript. No fabricated quotations or exact unverified casualty totals are used. Incomplete figures are omitted rather than invented. The links in the source catalog identify the underlying resources; every external link was not independently live-tested.

The chapter maintains the distinctions between Iranian and Iraqi society, governments, opposition movements and ideological rhetoric; between initiating a full-scale interstate war and responsibility for later conduct; and between actual decision evidence and inferred motives. Several broader contextual claims about individual regional governments rely on standard historical synthesis rather than extensive local archival research. They remain qualified and are not presented as evidence of secret prewar commitments.

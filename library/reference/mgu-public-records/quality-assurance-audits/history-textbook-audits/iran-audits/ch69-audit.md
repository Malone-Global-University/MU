## 3. Consolidated substantive revisions

**§§69.1–69.4 — Qajar origins and the north:** Deepened the Zand succession's institutional consequences, Qajar factional politics, the northern tax and military base, Caspian geographical constraints, the 1781 Voinovich episode, and the uncertainty of foreign travel testimony. **Corrected the founder's lineage:** Agha Mohammad Khan and Mohammad Hasan Khan were **Qovanlu/Qoyunlu (Ashaqabash)**, whereas **Develu (Yukharibash)** was a rival branch. Corrected the people preview, main text, feature-box explanations, chapter summary and review key terms.

**§§69.5–69.8 — Central Iran:** Expanded the wars with Ali Morad and Jafar Khan, the strategic rationale for Tehran and the distinction between a court center, enthronement and coronation. Developed the relationship between revenues, garrisons, local offices, appointments and real provincial compliance. Restored Lotf Ali Khan's independent military and political agency.

**§§69.9–69.12 — Shiraz, Kerman, and provincial administration:** Reconstructed the 1791 Shiraz coup and 1792 Qajar entry, with the roles of ward networks, militias, hostages and provincial elites. **Corrected Mirza Mohammad Kalantar's relationship to Haji Ebrahim:** he was a **mentor**; Haji Ebrahim's father was **Mohammad Hashem**, not Mirza Mohammad. Clarified Haji Ebrahim's advancement to Qajar governor and, in **1794**, grand vizier under Agha Mohammad Khan. Expanded the Kerman siege, coercive punishment and source uncertainty about quantities; included displacement, agricultural disruption, captivity and the limits of administrative continuity.

**§§69.13–69.16 — Caucasus:** Expanded Kartli-Kakheti's political agency, the 1783 Georgievsk protection arrangement (not annexation), the strategic differences among Karabakh, Yerevan, Ganja and Georgia, and the logistical limits of multi-column campaigns. Distinguished **Shusha's resistance in 1795** from **Qajar entry in 1797**. Attributed the often-repeated approximate **15,000 deportees** from Tbilisi to the later court-chronicle tradition cited by *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, not to an independently verified count. Developed the separate 1796 Russian campaign under Valerian Zubov and the policy reversal after Catherine II's death, without moving the nineteenth-century wars into Chapter 69.

**§§69.17–69.20 — Kingship and succession:** Distinguished effective rule, enthronement, ceremonial kingship, and continued provincial negotiation. Presented the competing identifications of **Moghan** (in John R. Perry's *Encyclopaedia Iranica* reconstruction) and **Tehran** (in Nobuaki Kondo's documentary reconstruction) for the 1796 coronation; explained how coins, seals and edicts help test retrospective ceremonial accounts. Deepened the Khurasan takeover and the limits of its reach. **Corrected the 1797 Shusha campaign:** Agha Mohammad Khan **entered Shusha before being assassinated there**. Developed Baba Khan's succession and Haji Ebrahim's administrative role while reserving the following reign for Chapter 70.

## 4. Primary-source and reference audit

All three source windows now identify one source and its relevant period of evidence; none invents an original-language quotation:

1. **§69.4 — George Forster**, *A Journey from Bengal to England*, vol. I, p. 198 (as cited by the *Encyclopaedia Iranica* biography of Agha Mohammad Khan). The window uses a clearly marked, source-based paraphrase of Forster's assessment of Qajar administrative attention at Sari. Forster's 1790 initial volume and 1798 two-volume edition are distinguished.
2. **§69.9 — Hasan-e Fasa'i**, *Farsnama-ye Naseri*, in Heribert Busse's 1972 English translation, as a retrospective chronicle suitable for **1791–1792**. The earlier *Ruznama* of **Mirza Mohammad Kalantar** ends around **1785** and is explicitly **not** treated as firsthand evidence for the 1791–92 rupture. Fasa'i was not an eyewitness to those events; the window acknowledges its retrospective character.
3. **§69.15 — Reza Qoli Khan Hedayat**, *Rawzat al-Safa-ye Naseri*, vol. IX, as cited in the *Encyclopaedia Iranica* entry on Iranian–Georgian relations. The window treats the later Qajar narrative as one perspective on 1795, emphasizes the absence of a verified captive census, and directs comparison with Georgian evidence.

**Important evidence limitation:** These are documented paraphrase/source-analysis windows grounded in identifiable works and specialist references. They are *not* critical editions: the original Persian manuscripts, exact text locations and translation variants have not all been collated line by line. The windows explicitly disclose those limits, and do not claim verbatim excerpts.

The finished Further Reading list supplies nine identified works or specialist entries and removes the draft's instruction to verify references in a future editorial phase. Selected foundational references: Gavin R. G. Hambly, “Agha Muhammad Khan and the Establishment of the Qajar Dynasty,” *The Cambridge History of Iran*, vol. 7 (Cambridge University Press, 1991), 104–143; John R. Perry, “Āḡā Moḥammad Khan Qājār,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*; Abbas Amanat, “Ebrāhīm Kalāntar Šīrāzī,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*; Hasan-e Fasa'i, *History of Persia under Qajar Rule*, trans. The actual HTML was rendered with Chromium using the same source markup injected into a browser document. Browser viewport widths tested: **320, 360, 390, 600, 900 and 1440 pixels**. All displayed a document width equal to the viewport, and **no JavaScript errors** were reported. The two chronology tables now scroll within bounded containers on narrow screens rather than widening the page. At 320 px the first table scroll container is 280 px wide with 610 px scrollable contents; document width remains 320 px. The two tables remain full-width or container-width at larger breakpoints.

The final file has **zero duplicate IDs, zero broken in-page anchors**, and balanced counted opening/closing tags for `section`, `div`, `table`, `tr`, `td`, `ul`, `ol`, `script`, and `style`. A total of **20** section identifiers (`s69-1` to `s69-20`) and **five** independent division banners are present. The chapter's original shell, CSS style scheme, active-link script and footer were retained, with small additions for responsive tables and long metadata.

**Unverified:** The original **Chapter 1 model file** was not available in this workspace for byte-for-byte comparison. Consequently, the existing Chapter 69 shell was retained but exact CSS/script identity with Chapter 1 cannot be independently certified.

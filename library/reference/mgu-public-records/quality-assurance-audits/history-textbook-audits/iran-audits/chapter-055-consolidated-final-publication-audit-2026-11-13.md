## III. Historical corrections incorporated

1. **§55.5 — Baghdad 1412 and the correct son of Qara Yusuf.** Replaced the incorrect identification of Pir Budaq with **Shah Muhammad (d. 1434)**, Qara Yusuf's son and principal early Baghdad governor. Explicitly distinguishes him from **Pir Budaq (d. 1466)**, the later son of Jahan Shah. The preexisting context of Jalayirid survival after Baghdad remains. Verification: Peter Jackson, “Jalayerids,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/jalayerids/; “Baghdad ii,” https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/baghdad/baghdad-iranian-connection-ii/.

2. **§55.8 — Pir Budaq's fate and agency.** Dates his death to **1466** and identifies his brother **Muhammad** as the immediate killer in one scholarly reconstruction, dispatched by Jahan Shah after the long Baghdad struggle. Does not mistake the brother's action for an independently verified personal execution by Jahan Shah. Study: Malika Dekkiche, “The Letter and Its Response,” *Arabica* 63 (2016), 579–626, https://www.academia.edu/34420931/The_Letter_and_Its_Response_The_Exchanges_between_the_Qara_Qoyunlu_and_the_Mamluk_Sultan_MS_Arabe_4440_BnF_Paris.

3. **§55.17 — Death of Qazi Isa Savaji and failed reform.** Added Yaʿqub's precise death date, **24 December 1490**, and Qazi Isa's execution by rebellious amirs in **January 1491**. Clarifies the opposition to soyurghal measures and the tamgha. Verification: Rosemarie Quiring-Zoche, “Aq Qoyunlu,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/aq-qoyunlu-confederation/.

4. **§55.18 — Aq Qoyunlu succession and genealogy.** Distinguishes **Baysunghur (son of Yaʿqub), Rustam (son of Maqsud; grandson of Uzun Hasan), Gövde Ahmad (son of Ughurlu Muhammad; grandson of Uzun Hasan), Alvand (son of Yusuf; grandson of Uzun Hasan), Muhammadi (Alvand's brother), Murad (son of Yaʿqub), and Qasem (son of Jahangir)**. Dates their respective late-fifteenth-century struggles with appropriate historical granularity. Verification: Quiring-Zoche, *Iranica*, as above.

5. **§55.19 and chronology — Safavid military sequence.** Differentiates **1501 Sharur and Tabriz**, the **battle near Hamadan against Sultan Murad (1502 or 1503 in different specialist accounts)**, **Fars by 1503**, **Diyarbakir in 1507–1508**, and **Baghdad/Iraq in 1508**. Avoids treating an isolated battle as the single event that ended every Aq Qoyunlu line. Verification: “Iraq iii,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/iraq-iii-mongols-to-safavids/; “Safavid Dynasty,” https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/safavids/; Quiring-Zoche, cited above.

6. **§55.20 — Limits of Ottoman victory in 1473.** Replaced the claim of immediate Ottoman securing of all eastern Anatolia after Otlukbeli with a historically defensible account of Ottoman strategic advantage and a frontier that remained disputed.

7. **Chronology and people reference:** Added dated anchors for Qazi Isa and Safavid campaigning and corrected the entries for Pir Budaq and Yaʿqub's government, so the introductory apparatus does not contradict the corrected narrative.

The main established chronology—1408 Qara Yusuf/Miran Shah; Shah Rukh's three western campaigns; Jahan Shah's 1458 Herat occupation; Uzun Hasan's 1467 and 1469 victories; Otlukbeli in 1473; and Turkmen state formation under Persianate fiscal and urban institutions—remains substantively sound.

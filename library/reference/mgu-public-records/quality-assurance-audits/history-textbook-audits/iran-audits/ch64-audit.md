## 4. Source windows: precise scope and verification limits

1. **`source-fiscal` — *Tadhkirat al-Muluk*.** Corrected dating to **approximately 1725**, after Isfahan's fall and during Afghan government. The document focus is financial administration (**chapter IV**) and the concluding provincial-revenue material; manuscript **British Library Or. 9496**; edition Minorsky, 1943. This is **not** a contemporary sixteenth-century source. Verified through *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, `https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/tadkerat-al-moluk/` and Gibb Memorial Trust.
2. **`source-women` — 1661 Isfahan waqf charter no. 111.** Replaced the previous generalized court-chronicle account of Pari Khan Khanum. The new window names **Guhar Shah Begum**, a non-royal founder, and identifies the Jiyan and Anduwan farm holdings and pilgrimage-related beneficiaries. Primary charter reference: Isfahan waqf corpus, Ashkiwari (2009), vol. 7, pp. 214–245, as reported in Mohammadreza Neyestani, *Hawwa* 15 (2017), **pp. 59–60**, DOI `10.1163/15692086-12341324`. **The underlying Persian deed has not been independently facsimile-collated**; the window transparently summarizes the scholarly published abstract, not a fabricated transcript. Pub: `https://doi.org/10.1163/15692086-12341324`.
3. **`source-baba` — Baba'i ben Lotf's Jewish chronicle.** Identifies the particular sequence of forced conversions beginning in **1656** and the experience through **1662**, with Moreen (1987), pp. 62–79 and appendixes C–D as cited by *Encyclopaedia Iranica* (`https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/judeo-persian-communities-iv-medieval-to-late-18th-century/`). The source window makes no unsupported word-for-word or line-level claim. A fully collated line-numbered passage would require direct access to the translated edition.

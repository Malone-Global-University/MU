## 1. Scope and determination

The initial manuscript was compared against the approved twenty-section production plan and governing Persian and Iranian History master instructions. The consolidated revision retains the original chapter's historical argument, twenty sections, five divisions, thirteen instructional features, and opening and review apparatus. The revision addresses the specific factual, chronological, documentary, and narrative deficiencies identified in the initial editorial audit. It does not restructure the chapter or transfer substantive material assigned to adjacent chapters.

This final audit records substantive scholarly changes and completion checks. Targeted source verification was undertaken for the principal disputed claims; this is not a claim that every sentence of the historical narrative has been independently verified against original archival materials. The manuscript is suitable for textual publication. Live browser rendering and website deployment have not been independently tested.

## 5. Remaining historical qualifications

- The 1907 Supplement is dated 29 Sha'ban 1325; published translations and specialist accounts give 7 or 8 October 1907. The variant is disclosed rather than silently harmonized.
- The 1911 electoral law is dated 28 Shawwal 1329 in two specialist accounts, but their Gregorian conversions differ: 22 October in the electoral history and 21 November in the constitutional history. The revised manuscript explicitly records the discrepancy and does not rely on the less secure Gregorian date for its argument. The substantive provisions (136 deputies, 82 districts, direct male voting with earlier property and educational qualifications removed) are well supported.
- Estimates of military casualties and the detailed attribution of political assassinations remain contested. The revised narrative does not assert uniform casualty figures or identify organizers beyond available evidence.
- Accounts of women's demonstrations and contemporary diplomacy are partisan and sometimes retrospective; qualification is retained. Constitutional texts are distinguished from enforcement, and military victories from durable institutional consolidation.

These qualifications constitute normal scholarly transparency rather than unresolved publication-blocking defects.

### 3. Independent verification of substantive historical claims

**Otrar oasis.** Willem H. J. Toonen et al., “A hydromorphic reevaluation of the forgotten river civilizations of Central Asia,” *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 117, no. 52 (2020): 32982–32988, DOI **10.1073/pnas.2009553117** ([full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7776828/)). The study uses archaeological and hydrological evidence to argue that the observed irrigation abandonment in the studied oasis often reflects river entrenchment and drought rather than a single 1219–20 war episode. It **does not** establish that Mongol invasion caused no irrigation damage elsewhere. The chapter now cites the actual publication and corrects the separate Otrar merchant incident to **1218**.

**Herat.** Shivan Mahendrarajah, *A History of Herat: From Chingiz Khan to Tamerlane*, chapter 7, “Early Efforts to Revive Agriculture and Commerce” (2026), pp. 185–211; DOI **10.3366/edinburgh/9781474499347.003.0012** ([publisher summary](https://academic.oup.com/edinburgh-scholarship-online/book/62150/chapter-abstract/549487409)). Supports early hydrological restoration **around 1236** and the reported **1240–41 enumeration of c. 6,900 persons**. *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, [Herat iii](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/herat-iii/), further associates the early canal repairs with **returning weavers** permitted back under Ögedei. These are place-specific counts, not a population denominator for all of Herat or Iran. The final HTML makes these limitations explicit.

**Tarabi uprising.** *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, [Tarabi, Mahmud](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/tarabi-ma%E1%B8%A5mud/) places the rising in **635–636 AH / 1237–38 CE**, whereas [Bukhara iii](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bukhara-iii/) gives **636 AH / 1238–39**; both support **c. 1238** but not a certain single year. The final HTML reflects this.

**Balkh and Ibn Battuta.** *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, [Ibn Battuta](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ebn-battuta/), dates his Balkh visit to **1333** and reports his description of extensive ruins; [Balkh iii](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bal%E1%B8%B5-iii-from-the-mongols-to-modern-times/) emphasizes both severe destruction and later recovery. The chapter correctly presents the report as a later traveler’s observation, not a measurement of losses in 1221.

**Sayf al-Harawi.** *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, [Herat v: Local Histories](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/herat-v/) identifies his *Tarikhnama-yi Herat* as completed **by 1321**, refining Claude's looser c. 1318–22 dating. The chapter identifies its retrospective and Kartid-centered perspective; the passage is an editorial paraphrase.

**Rumi family migration.** *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, [Baha al-Din Muhammad Walad](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/baha-al-din-mohammad-walad-b/), finds that the departure date and motives remain disputed; a scholarly reconstruction prefers **c. 1214**, possibly well before Mongol threat. The final HTML no longer treats the family as a securely documented refugee flight from the 1219 invasion.

**Yaqut al-Hamawi.** A historically contextualized account of Yaqut's Merv and Khwarazm travels and his letter from Mosul dated 617 AH / 1220–21 is preserved in E. G. Browne, [*A Literary History of Persia*](https://persian.packhum.org/text/090001012/5). His departure from Merv is placed around **1219**, preceding his subsequent flight from Khwarazm. The opening's chronology is corrected accordingly.

**Gurganj / Urgench.** [UNESCO, Kunya-Urgench](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1199) confirms the historical urban site's importance through later centuries. The manuscript formerly contradicted itself by describing the historical capital as both revived and non-recovered. It now identifies Gurganj / historical Urgench and separates its trajectory from Samarkand's Afrasiab site.

**Merv, Nishapur and violent reprisals.** *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, [Mongols](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/mongols-2/) and Peter Jackson, *The Mongols and the Islamic World* (2017), support severe massacres, especially where high-ranking Mongols were killed, while emphasizing that chronicle casualty totals cannot be accepted literally. The chapter makes no unverified population-scale numerical claims.

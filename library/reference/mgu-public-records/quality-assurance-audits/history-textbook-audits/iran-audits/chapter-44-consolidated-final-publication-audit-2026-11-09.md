## 5. Historical audit findings and publication cautions
- The **1097 succession** was the clearest factual omission. Bosworth records the short tenure of Ekinchi ibn Qochqar between the Anushtegin and Qutb al-Din Muhammad appointments. Corrected.
- The treatment of **Atsiz, Qatwan (1141), Sanjar’s death (1157), Tekish’s defeat of Tughril III (1194), Muhammad II’s reign (1200–1220), Otrar (1218), and Mongol campaigns (1219–1221)** is broadly consistent with specialist chronology.
- The geographic and environmental foundation of the lower Amu Darya, Persianate chancery, Turkic/Qangli army, and **Terken Khatun’s independent administration** are substantively treated. The chapter correctly warns that the internal financial machinery cannot be reconstructed exhaustively.
- The **Otrar** account distinguishes broadly corroborated diplomatic breakdown from disputed motives, numbers, and attribution. It contains no speculative massacre statistics presented as verified totals.
- The **Jalal al-Din** narrative covers his disputed succession, Parwan, the Indus (1221), India, return to Iran, campaigns in the Caucasus and Anatolia, defeat in 1230, and death in 1231. The strongest primary witness, al-Nasawi, is treated as sympathetic and retrospective.
- The **transition to Volume IV** is appropriately limited: Chapter 44 tells the invasion from the Khwarazmian side, without attempting a complete history of Mongol administration, demographic loss, or the Ilkhanate.
- The **three Primary Source Windows** consist of explicitly labeled topical paraphrases and comparative accounts, not verified translated primary-source passages. Especially Source Window I is a **genre-level synthesis**, not an excerpt from a specific identified chancery letter. It is acceptable as a transparent pedagogical study but must not be advertised as a collated quotation.

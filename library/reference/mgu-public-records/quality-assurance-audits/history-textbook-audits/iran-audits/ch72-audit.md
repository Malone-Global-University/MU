## 3. Historical accuracy and corrective findings

1. **Treaty of Paris, 4 March 1857:** Corrected the attribution of the clauses. Article V concerns the withdrawal of Iranian forces from Herat/Afghan territory; Article VI concerns renunciation of claims, noninterference, independence and the British good-offices process. Additional frontier obligations are distinguished. The revised Primary Source Window now identifies published text and specialist scholarly analysis. Source: [Encyclopaedia Iranica, Herat vi](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/herat-vi/).
2. **March–April 1857 operations:** Corrected misleading chronology. The peace treaty was signed on **4 March** before the British attack at Mohammerah (**26 March**) and advance toward Ahvaz (**1 April**). Distant military operations continued before news of the settlement arrived. Sources: [Encyclopaedia Iranica, Anglo-Persian War](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/anglo-persian-war-1856-57/) and [Herat vi](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/herat-vi/).
3. **1856 military and diplomatic sequence:** Distinguishes October Iranian capture of Herat, November British declaration of war, December occupations of Kharg and Bushehr, February 1857 Khushab battle, March signing, and subsequent southern fighting. Avoids explaining the **signing** by operations that happened afterward.
4. **Gulf treaty obligations:** Differentiates Britain's 1820 maritime treaty, the 1853 perpetual maritime truce, and the later protectorate-type system; does not misrepresent the first treaties as cessions of Gulf sovereignty. Reference: [UAE National Library and Archives, British Era](https://www.nla.ae/en/our-history/historical-periods/british-era/).
5. **Sistan and Makran:** Distinguishes Goldsmid's early 1870s Makran work, the **1872** Sistan award, subsequent acceptance/demarcation disputes, and later unresolved Helmand water questions. Sources: [Iranica, Boundaries iii](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/boundaries-iii/), [Helmand River iv](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/helmand-river-iv/), and [FAO legal background](https://www.fao.org/4/w9549e/w9549e07.htm).
6. **Central Asian states:** Differentiates Russia's annexation of Kokand in 1876 from dependent/protectorate arrangements with Bukhara and Khiva, rather than treating all three as identical annexations. Source: [Iranica, Central Asia vii](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/central-asia-vii/).
7. **Turkmen frontier:** Recognizes the Qajar defeat near Merv in 1861, the roles of Qowshut Khan and Hamza Mirza, community differences among Turkmen groups, the harms of raids and enslavement, and the violence of Russian conquest. Avoids ungrounded Geok Tepe death totals. Sources: [Iranica, Central Asia vii](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/central-asia-vii/) and [Cambridge, The Russian Conquest of Central Asia](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/russian-conquest-of-central-asia/60AD9B4DF95196BEA2F8EF67AF0CB8BB/listing).
8. **Akhal frontier:** Identifies the **21 December 1881** convention, participants and boundary logic, distinguishes public convention, confidential additions, ratification and demarcation, and situates Merv (1884) and Panjdeh (1885) as distinct developments. Sources: [Iranica, Boundaries ii](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/boundaries-ii/) and [UNESCO History of Civilizations of Central Asia](https://apnaorg.com/books/english/history-of-civilizations-of-central-asia-vol-6/history-of-civilizations-of-central-asia-vol-6.pdf).
9. **Telegraph engagement:** Documents a December 1862 Anglo-Iranian engagement for the Khanaqin–Bushehr line, connected implementation in 1863, and later distinct instruments; distinguishes IETD, the separate Indo-European Telegraph Company and Iran's own services. Sources: [British Library IOR/L/PS/18/C119, QDL](https://www.qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100025775647.0x000005) and [Iranica, Indo-European Telegraph Department](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/indo-european-telegraph-department/).
10. **Interpretive balance:** Explains Iranian agency without denying imperial constraints, gives voice to local/regional interests without inventing quotations, and flags analytical composites in the Everyday Life Study.

## 2. Historically necessary corrections completed

### 2.1 Gulistan's Article IX already contained a five-percent tariff

Sections 71.3 and 71.12 now distinguish the original five-percent customs framework in **Gulistan Article IX** from the enlarged trade, consular, and jurisdictional safeguards of the **1828 commercial convention**. The comparative summary and chronology were updated accordingly. Do not attribute the introduction of the number five percent to the second treaty.

Documentary and contextual sources: [Gulistan treaty text, British Library/Qatar Digital Library](https://www.qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100023947391.0x00008b); [Encyclopaedia Iranica, Golestan Treaty](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/golestan-treaty/); [published account of the five-percent provision](https://preslib.az/en/historical/page/zxurayo).

### 2.2 Gulistan's boundary and territorial articles separated

Section 71.2 and Primary Source Window 1 distinguish **Article II's frontier description** from **Article III's list of territories recognized as Russian**. The primary text is an attributed documentary paraphrase and links to the digitized British treaty collection. The original Persian and Russian versions have not been collated directly.

### 2.3 Turkmenchay's Article XII and XV property deadlines separated

Section 71.13 and Primary Source Window 3 now distinguish:

- **Article XII:** three years for qualifying property owners on both sides of the Aras to sell/exchange immovable property, with specified exceptions.
- **Article XIII:** exchange of prisoners and captives.
- **Article XIV:** refugee and politically sensitive settlement restrictions.
- **Article XV:** amnesty **for Iranian Azerbaijan's inhabitants and officials**, together with **one year** for certain movements of persons and movable property and **five years** for disposal of immovable property.

The former draft's erroneous blanket amnesty for inhabitants of ceded khanates was removed, and the Key Terms definition of *amnesty* was corrected.

Documentary source: [Treaty of Turkmenchay, Articles XII–XV, published English translation](https://westernazerbaijan.preslib.az/en/page/xdbsBvP). The published translation is identified as an English edition rather than an original-language diplomatic witness.

### 2.4 Indemnity and its separate payment agreement corrected

Section 71.11 and Primary Source Window 2 distinguish **main treaty Article VI** (ten kurur of tumans / twenty million silver rubles) from the **special attached payment agreement** (installment dates and military guarantees). The original four-installment schedule is identified as **3 + 2 + 3 + 2 kurur**. The 1829 reduction following the Khosrow Mirza mission reduced the **last two-kurur installment to one kurur**, rather than cancelling it entirely. The manuscript does not claim to have reconciled every coin received.

Sources: [Treaty of Turkmenchay, Article VI](https://westernazerbaijan.preslib.az/en/page/xdbsBvP); [*Iranian Studies*, “ʿAbbās Mīrzā’s Will,” note 7](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/iranian-studies/article/abs/cabbas-mirzas-will/ABF2EF2C6C961F8847F8BB00F4EDDFFD); [British Library archive of March 1828 treaty summary](https://www.qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100127000065.0x000004).

### 2.5 Armenian migration estimate given with scope and qualification

Section 71.14 identifies a source-defined estimate of **approximately 45,000 Armenian emigrants from Iran in 1828–1829**, drawn from *Encyclopaedia Iranica*'s Armeno-Iranian relations article. This is not conflated with Ottoman-origin migration, households versus individuals, or the total Armenian population of the Russian Caucasus. The text retains the range of reasons for migration and the documented continued Armenian presence within Iran.

Source: [*Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Armenia and Iran vi”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/armenia-vi/).

### 2.6 Local administration given differentiated chronology

Section 71.6 identifies abolition of Shaki (1819), Shirvan (1820), and Karabakh (1822) at **different** times, not as a uniform 1819–1822 event. It describes subsequent Russian surveys as evidence of fiscal and demographic administration with associated limits rather than treating the records as neutral modern ethnic censuses. Relevant [Bournoutian, *The 1820 Russian Survey of the Khanate of Shirvan*](https://www.gibbtrust.org/the-1820-russian-survey-of-the-khanat-of-shirvan/) and [*The 1823 Russian Survey of the Karabagh Province*](https://www.mazdapublishers.com/book/the-1823-russian-survey-of-the-karabagh-province).

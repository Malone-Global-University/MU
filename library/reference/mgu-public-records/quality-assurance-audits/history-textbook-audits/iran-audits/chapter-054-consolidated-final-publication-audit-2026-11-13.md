## III. Historical corrections made in the final HTML

**1. §54.2 — Gawhar Shad's 1426 deed.** Corrected the suggestion that the 829/1426 endowment deed documents all Herat Musalla arrangements. The surviving deed covers Gawhar Shad's Mashhad mosque and Imam Reza shrine complex and the shrine of Ahmad-i Jam, and includes irrigation, fiscal, and public-private benefit provisions. It is now separated explicitly from the Herat architectural foundations. Scholarly anchor: Shivan Mahendrarajah, 'The Gawhar Shād Waqf Deed: Public Works and the Commonweal', *Journal of the American Oriental Society* 138.4 (2018), pp. 821–857, DOI https://doi.org/10.7817/jameroriesoci.138.4.0821.

**2. §54.8 — British Library Nizami Khamsa.** The precise item is **Or 6810**. The library dates the manuscript generally to the 1490s, while **folio 284r** contains a dated painting, AH 900 / 1494–95. Other paintings are **possibly** by Behzad. Names beneath paintings were added later. The revised text distinguishes these three facts and links the catalog record: https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/032-002748654.

**3. §§54.3 and source study — Baysunghur Shahnameh.** Identifies the actual manuscript as **Golestan Palace Library, Tehran, MS 716**, colophon 30 January 1430. Thomas W. Lentz counts **21 paintings**, while the UNESCO documentary-nomination form says **22 miniature paintings**; divergent counting conventions are acknowledged rather than falsely presented as independent agreement. Sources: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/baysongori-sah-nama/baysongori-sahnama-paintings/ and https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/prince_bayasanghors_book_of_the_kings.pdf. No individual painters' names are invented.

**4. §§54.8 and source study — Cairo Bustan.** Identifies **Cairo, Dar al-Kutub, MS Adab Farisi 22**, text copied by **Sultan Ali al-Katib**, with the **Yusuf–Zulaykha painting on folio 52b**. The revised study points to Lamia Balafrej's article and its figure 4: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-royal-asiatic-society/article/three-ways-of-reading-a-frontispiece-the-example-of-the-cairo-bustan/911CA93848A5AC692E8316A402B93CC9. Painting inscriptions are evidence for association and attribution, not conclusive proof of one person's sole hand in a collaborative production.

**5. §§54.10 and 54.19 — Jami's travel.** Added the chronology of departure in **1472**, **hajj in May 1473**, and return to Herat in **January 1474**, as reported in *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, 'Jāmi i. Life and Works': https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/jami-i/.

**6. §54.12 — Khwandamir.** Replaces 'in the 1520s' with **1524** for completion of *Habib al-Siyar*, after the end of Timurid rule in Herat: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/historiography-vi/.

**7. Bibliography / Jami and Nava'i studies.** Corrected the scholar's name to **Robert Devereux** (not Devereaux) in narrative, source metadata, and research extension. His edited translation *Muhakamat al-Lughatain* (Leiden: E. J. Brill, 1966) is a real published book. The source exercise now specifies the passage type on Turkic verb distinctions, without inventing a page number not independently collated. Catalogue: https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA49483401.

**8. The Jami study now uses an identified edition and locus.** The designated exercise is the **First Garden** (pp. 22–36) of Jami's *Baharistan* in Sorabji Fardunji Mulla, trans., *The Beharistan-i-Jami* (Bombay: Meher Printing Works, 1899), available at https://persian.packhum.org/text/011301030. This is an **historical, incomplete translation**, not a critical Persian-text edition: Mulla omitted the sixth garden and some Arabic material. Those limits are openly disclosed. No pseudo-original quotation is fabricated. See additional discussion of translation history: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/iranian-studies/article/persian-studies-in-india-and-the-colonial-universities-18571947/7492A41816FF0D.

**9. Musalla study traceability.** The study now directs students to the mausoleum's cruciform layout and squinch-net vault as described by Archnet (site 5415), and to the named *Timurid Architecture Research Archive* **AKDC.2015.0005, box 2, folder 69** (Herat Musalla photos and drawings). It also identifies Aube–Lorain–Bendezu-Sarmiento (2020), pp. 62–83, as a modern site study. This provides a traceable architecture register without claiming first-hand examination of an unviewed original photograph or measured plan. Sources: https://www.archnet.org/sites/5415 and https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/05786967.2019.1571769.

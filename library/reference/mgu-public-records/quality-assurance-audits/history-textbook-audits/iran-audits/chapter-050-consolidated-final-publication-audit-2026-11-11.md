### 3. Historical and source-verification decisions

1. **Hormuz (§50.16):** The old mainland port near Minab transferred its commercial center to Jarun Island around 1300, under Baha al-Din Ayaz (who came to power in 1296), in part under pressure from military insecurity. Distinguishes mainland settlement, island town, and later name. Verified against *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Hormuz ii: Islamic Period,” https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/hormuz-ii/ , and “Bandar-e ʿAbbas,” https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bandar-e-abbasi/ . The exact event chronology is approximate.
2. **Juvayni (§50.6):** ʿAta-Malik Juvayni correctly identified as the historian and governor of Baghdad, not the vizier. His canal project is stated as the reported construction of a canal from the Anbar/Euphrates area toward Kufa–Najaf; exaggerated cost figures are not presented as established data. See George E. Lane, “Jovayni, ʿAlaʾ-al-Din,” https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/jovayni-ala-al-din/ ; “Jovayni Family,” https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/jovayni-family/ .
3. **Qays (§50.16):** Jamal al-Din Ibrahim al-Tibi (d. 1306) named as identifiable merchant-administrator associated with Qays and Fars. Avoids presenting reported fleet or revenue figures as audited statistics. See “Kish,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/kish/ .
4. **Sultaniyya (§50.10 and preview):** Distinguishes development begun under Arghun around 1290 from Öljeitü’s monumental capital-building, principally 1305–1313; avoids the claim that Öljeitü originally founded the settlement ex nihilo. See UNESCO nomination statement https://whc.unesco.org/document/5857 and https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1188 .
5. **Reclamation and documentation (§§50.7–50.8, 50.19):** Distinguishes administration’s policies and agricultural intentions from demonstrated sector-wide output; notes that records do not establish the scale of actually cultivated land. See Ann K. S. Lambton, “Economy V,” part 3, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/economy-5-part2/ (on the <em>divan-e khalesa</em> and <em>Tarikh-e Ghazani</em> evidence).
6. **Merchant partnerships and women (§50.14):** Elaborates economic mechanisms without treating all merchants as <em>ortoq</em> or extending elite <em>khatun</em> autonomy to all women. See Bruno De Nicola, *Women in Mongol Iran* (2017), chapter 4, https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.3366/j.ctt1g09twn.11 .
7. **Fiscal sources (source-fiscal):** Identifies the actual late-thirteenth-century manual <em>al-Murshid fi al-Hisab</em>, by al-Hasan b. ʿAli, and limits claims to Watabe’s published analysis of poll/census assessment and exceptional military levies; no manuscript facsimile collation is claimed. See Ryoko Watabe (2015), https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/studies-on-the-iranian-world/new-insights-on-the-fiscal-system-of-the-early-ilkhanids-through-the-analysis-of-almurshid-fi-alhisab-an-unknown-accounting-manual/6792E91436ED72EDC6882CF82F4BB944 .
8. **Source windows:** The Rashid al-Din and Mustawfi/al-Umari windows now identify appropriate primary texts and editions/secondary pointers. All three explicitly remain **source-guided editorial paraphrases**, not invented original-language quotations. Mustawfi bibliography checked against the Le Strange 1919 translation record: https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Geographical_Part_of_the_Nuzhat_al_q.html?id=N0QDAAAAMAAJ .

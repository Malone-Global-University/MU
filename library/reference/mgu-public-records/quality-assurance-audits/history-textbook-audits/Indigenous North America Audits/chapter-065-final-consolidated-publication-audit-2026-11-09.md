### Verified historical issues and corrections applied

1. **Indian Removal Act roll calls:** Senate **28–19** on April 24, 1830; House **102–97** on May 26; signed May 28. Draft numbers were correct. Opening adjusted so the Senate margin is not characterized as equally narrow as the House margin. The bill's legislation, $500,000 appropriation, land exchanges, and treaty-protection proviso are correctly explained. Sources: Library of Congress, U.S. House History, and statute (4 Stat. 411–412).
2. **Treaty of Fort Jackson (1814):** reputable National Park Service histories report **nearly 22 million** or **23 million** acres. Changed two passages to **roughly twenty-two to twenty-three million acres**, preserving the scale without false precision.
3. **Treaty of Dancing Rabbit Creek (1830):** **more than 10 million**, commonly about **10.4 million acres**. Corrected the geographic description from Mississippi alone to **Mississippi and Alabama** in §65.17.
4. **Treaty of Cusseta (March 24, 1832):** date, Washington setting, and Opothleyahola/Opothleholo as signatory are confirmed by the Smithsonian's treaty transcript and Oklahoma State treaty collection. No material correction needed.
5. **Treaty of New Echota opposition:** a National Archives–based educational record describes an **earlier memorial with 3,352 Cherokee signatures**, while John Ross's delegation later stated that **upwards of 15,000** had protested the treaty. The draft conflated these into one 15,000-signature petition. Replaced with an explicit distinction between documentary counts.
6. **Treaty of New Echota ratification:** confirmed Senate approval by **31–15 in May 1836**, one vote above the necessary two-thirds threshold; clarified the tally in §65.17. Source: U.S. Senate voting research and National Park Service's one-vote account.
7. **George W. Harkins's farewell letter:** substantively sound paraphrase but wrongly dated **early 1832**. The Choctaw Nation identifies first publication on **December 2, 1831** in the *Natchez Weekly Courier*, with a *Cherokee Phoenix* reprint dated **January 28, 1832**. Corrected heading, context, and linked both sources.
8. **Worcester v. Georgia (1832):** Marshall's authentic phrase is **“a distinct community occupying its own territory”**, **without the comma** inserted in the draft's purported exact quotation. Corrected the phrase and linked the opinion. The chapter correctly treats Jackson's alleged “let him enforce it” remark as unverified.
9. **Earlier legal and presidential chronology:** Georgia Compact (1802), Monroe's January 27, 1825 message, Georgia's 1827 tenants-at-will position, Jackson's December 8, 1829 first annual message, and the 1830 act chronology were checked against digitized institutional sources and match the chapter. The draft's unsubstantiated claim that the removal bill provoked the “first great national debate” was moderated to a defensible formulation.
10. **Selected Further Reading:** all eight entries correspond to verifiable publications; added full titles/publisher data to distinguish works and editions. The 1984 Prucha reference is the original two-volume work, not the abridged 1986 edition. The Kappler reference specifies the treaty volume (1904).
11. **Source traceability:** added directly clickable source references to each of the four documentary windows, without converting paraphrases into invented verbatim quotations.

### Stated limitations and approval boundary

This is an **independent first-edition historical-text audit**, not a complete line-by-line collation against the original treaty facsimiles, full Congress journals, every original state legislative act, and all relevant Indigenous-language documents. Four primary-source windows present properly identified critical paraphrases, except for the brief and corrected *Worcester* quotation. The geography study is **text-guided** and has **no embedded historical atlas plate**. These limits are disclosed and do not invalidate publication as a scholarly textual edition; they prevent claims of complete facsimile/source reproduction and verified Chapter 1 model parity.

No substantive reorganization, compression of 20 historical sections, or migration of Chapter 66–67 material was undertaken. Revisions were targeted to historically checkable facts, exact-source fidelity, references, bibliography, and a minor mobile CSS issue.

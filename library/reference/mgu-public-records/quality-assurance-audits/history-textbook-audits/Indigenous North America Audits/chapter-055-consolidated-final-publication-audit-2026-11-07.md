## 3. Documentary and historical accuracy checks

The following historically important claims were compared against identified documentary records and specialist historical sources; this is a targeted audit, not a claim of exhaustive independent archival collation.

| Issue | Corrected treatment | Verification basis |
|---|---|---|
| Great Peace of Montréal, 1701 | Multinational Indigenous–French settlement, with distinct Indigenous peace provisions, mediation, and Haudenosaunee neutrality in Anglo-French wars. Kondiaronk's crucial role identified. | Pointe-à-Callière museum treaty transcription; Parks Canada Kondiaronk record; Dictionary of Canadian Biography topic page. |
| Kiotseaeton, 1645–1646 | Mohawk envoy and speaker at Trois-Rivières; not represented as a final confederacy-wide and durable general peace. | Dictionary of Canadian Biography: Kiotseaeton. |
| Two Row Wampum and Dutch relations | Tradition and political meaning are identified; disputes about 1613 chronology and provenance are disclosed without treating absence of documentary certainty as disproof of all early contacts. | Paul Otto, 'Wampum, Tawagonshi, and the Two Row Belt'. |
| Lancaster, 1744 | Names Canassatego and Conrad Weiser; does not presume every affected Indigenous community was represented; identifies interpretation and land-title problems. | Oklahoma State treaty collection, reproduced conference proceedings. |
| Wabanaki and Mi'kmaq treaties, 1725–1726 | Distinguishes agreement from subsequent ratifications and avoids declaring a general Indigenous land surrender. Does not force disputed day-of-month ratification details. | Cape Breton University text; Nova Scotia Archives; Government of Canada Peace and Friendship history. |
| Yamasee War | Captive trading, debt, abuse, settlement and varied Indigenous decisions given causal weight; approximate 1715–1718 range. | William L. Ramsey, *The Yamasee War*; relevant specialist studies. |
| Choctaw internal war | Periodized broadly in later 1740s–1750, with trade, kinship, and personal grievances integrated; Red Shoes identified. | Mississippi Encyclopedia; Auburn University dissertation by Matthew Sparacio. |
| Cherokee embassy, 1730 | Attakullakulla and London delegation presented without assuming a universal Cherokee cession of authority. | Oxford Dictionary of National Biography; South Carolina Encyclopedia. |
| Pickawillany, 1752 | Memeskia and Langlade identified; Indigenous participation and commercial strategy emphasized; sensational details not adopted without qualification. | American Battlefield Trust; specialist Ohio Valley research. |
| The 'middle ground' | White's framework clearly identified as an interpretation particularly associated with the Great Lakes; limits are explained. | Richard White, *The Middle Ground* (1991). |

**Interpretive control:** The narrative distinguishes Indigenous sovereignty from centralized European-style government, does not portray all Indigenous nations as one political unit, treats trading alliances as contingent rather than automatic subordination, identifies where European maps overstated effective rule, and separates colonial transcription from contemporary Indigenous consent. Numerical precision and quotation claims were minimized when not defensible.

## 6. Publication qualification and remaining limitations

**Approved and locked for the historical textual HTML first edition.** The following delimit the scope of the approval, not known blockers in this edition:

1. Linked archival and treaty transcriptions were evaluated for identifiable events and documentary context, but original manuscripts, every translation variant, and all historical editions were not exhaustively collated. Historical source passages inside the chapter are labeled as paraphrase, not claimed as verified direct quotation.
2. Geography and map exercises are text-based and require students to consult historical atlases; no embedded map plates or original archival images are supplied.
3. The styling and chapter shell were inherited from the Claude HTML and preserved with targeted improvements; absolute byte-for-byte identity to a separately held Chapter 1 model cannot be independently certified because that model was not among the governing files attached in this thread.
4. The source register is a selective first-edition scholarly guide, not an exhaustive bibliography covering every nation's archives or modern community treaty interpretations. The book's limitations and source perspective are explained in the chapter itself.

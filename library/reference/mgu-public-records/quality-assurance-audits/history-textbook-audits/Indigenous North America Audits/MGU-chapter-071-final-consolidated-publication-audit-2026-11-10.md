## 3. Historical, documentary, and editorial corrections applied

1. **Opening timing:** Replaced the ambiguous phrase saying families were walking home “within two years of the treaty” with “within weeks”; the Treaty of June 1, 1868 was followed by departure in late June.
2. **1849 treaty:** Added Senate advice-and-consent **September 9, 1850**, and presidential proclamation **September 24, 1850**, distinguishing these from field signature on **September 9, 1849**. Verified against the original text and federal treaty metadata.
3. **Fort Sumner chronology:** Refined “established in late 1862” to **developed in 1862–1863**, differentiating initial planning from construction and reservation operations.
4. **June 1863 Carleton order:** Removed the imprecise sex-specific phrasing “Diné men” and used “Diné people ... treated as hostile by the army,” without asserting every person was killed or captured.
5. **Bosque Redondo disease evidence:** Qualified the earlier claim inferring individual sexual assaults directly from recorded venereal illness. Those records demand interpretation alongside survivor testimony; diagnosis alone cannot establish particular acts.
6. **1868 Treaty Article II:** Replaced the modern **Arizona–New Mexico state line** assertion with the treaty's actual latitudinal, longitudinal, and landmark-defined reservation, including **Canyon de Chelly**. Boundaries are recorded in Article II.
7. **1868 Treaty Article XI:** Distinguished federally supervised return and subsistence/transport requirements from the material appropriation under Article XII.
8. **1868 Treaty Article XII:** Specified the planned **$150,000** appropriation, including approximately **$50,000** for return transportation, up to **$30,000** for **15,000 sheep and goats**, and **500 beef cattle plus one million pounds of corn**, as set out in the article. These are contractual provisions, not evidence that every item was delivered as planned.
9. **1868 Treaty Article X:** Specified the threshold of **three-fourths of interested/occupying adult male Diné** for valid future treaty cessions of communal reservation lands.
10. **1868 Treaty Article XIII:** Removed the claim that Article XIII, on its own, constituted an unconditional U.S. guarantee against future removal. It imposed permanent-home and settlement restrictions on Diné signatories; read together with Article II it arose from the negotiated homeward settlement, but the article's actual direction is important.
11. **Source-window practice:** Added direct authoritative document/archive/teaching-collection links to each of the five documentary windows; paraphrases remain explicitly identified, without invented quotations.
12. **Bibliography:** Clarified the 1973 *Navajo Stories of the Long Walk Period* bibliographic record, crediting **Broderick H. Johnson as editor** and noting original Navajo-language materials were collected under Ruth Roessel's supervision (publisher/catalog metadata differs between records).
13. **Source-method caveat:** Replaced uncompleted instruction to verify every article and book citation with an accurate first-edition disclosure of what is and is not collated.
14. **Narrative depth:** Expanded substantive explanatory paragraphs about policy design, dispersed Indigenous resistance, military logistics, group-by-group marches, family separation, Bosque Redondo's scale, ecological constraints, the independent Mescalero strategy, mortality classifications, cultural continuity, 1868 negotiations, exact treaty provisions, and rebuilding after return. The original five-division structure was preserved.
15. **Accessible mobile chronology:** Added independent horizontal scrolling for large chronology tables, overflow-safe documentary links, and clearer keyboard focus outlines, preserving the established visual design.

## 5. Quantitative and interpretive safeguards

- **Multiple journeys:** Do not replace the 1863–1866 march sequence with a single route or distance. Smithsonian and New Mexico Historic Sites present differing distance ranges because departure points and route criteria differ.
- **Mescalero history:** The Mescalero Apache were moved under a separate campaign, and many departed Bosque Redondo in 1865. Their counts, deaths, and subsequent homeland policy must not be merged indiscriminately with Diné statistics.
- **Mortality estimates:** Distinguish deaths on marches, during collection, during Bosque Redondo incarceration, and persons who never returned. Smithsonian material commonly estimates about two thousand Diné deaths at Bosque Redondo; New Mexico sites may use broader totals up to approximately three thousand for the wider removal-confinement period. The chapter avoids claiming these numbers can be added or independently verified from complete rosters.
- **Survivor testimony:** Testimony recorded decades later is historical evidence; record its collector and timing and do not confuse it with verbatim contemporaneous campaign documentation.
- **Treaty interpretation:** Land rights, school provisions, farming and subsistence assistance, retained hunting permissions, cession-vote threshold, and permanent-home restrictions differ across treaty articles and are now separately specified.
- **Land and nation:** The Diné homeland and sacred-geographical concept are broader than the 1868 federal reservation. A treaty recognized a nation and established federal relations; it did not create the Diné as a people.

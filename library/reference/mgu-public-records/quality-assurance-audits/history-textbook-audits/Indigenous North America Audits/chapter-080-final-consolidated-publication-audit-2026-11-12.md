### 3. Historical corrections and research verification

1. **Naiche:** Corrected erroneous 1919 death date to **1921**, corroborated by Fort Sill Apache Tribe, *Our Ancestors*.
2. **Crawford:** Distinguished the **January 10, 1886** camp attack, **January 11** fire by Mexican troops, and **January 18** death using *Foreign Relations of the United States*, 1886, no. 301. Historical “Haros” is acknowledged as a variant of Aros.
3. **Crook conference and March return:** Identified **March 25–27** conference, **March 28** beginning of escorted march, and departure during the night of **March 29**, as reported in Howard's September 1886 account; preserved uncertainty around motives.
4. **March versus September 1886 terms:** Corrected the potentially material conflation of Crook's March proposal for temporary eastern exile with the September claims of **reunion with family in Florida**. Stanley's **October 22** telegram, printed in Barrett (1906), pp. 156–161, is the identified witness for those latter claims. The chapter explains that no jointly signed documentary treaty was verified.
5. **September 4 surrender:** Kept the Skeleton Canyon date and treated Native intermediaries, Gatewood, Lawton and Miles as distinct participants.
6. **Geronimo's Janos memory:** Qualified scholarly **1851** dating against the **1858** date in his edited 1906 autobiographical account.
7. **Carlisle:** Grounded the initial November 1886 group of **37 young people (29 boys, 8 girls)** in Schofield's November 2 notice and the Carlisle descriptive statement. Pratt's consent-seeking discussion is acknowledged without interpreting it as proof of uncoerced parental consent. The first cohort is distinguished from later arrivals and from the wider boarding-school system.
8. **Exile and mortality:** Retained defensible nonprecision where complete cohort denominators or death registers were not collated; added the April 27, 1887 Fort Marion final report as a starting point for further research.
9. **Fort Sill and emancipation:** Corrected Hugh Lenox Scott's supervision to 1894–1897. The 1913 Mescalero relocation (approximately 200, according to the Mescalero Apache Tribe) is distinguished from the Fort Sill Apache Tribe's 1914 release and Oklahoma allotment of approximately 81 people in twenty families.
10. **Longer memory:** Added Mildred Imach Cleghorn, born as a prisoner at Fort Sill in December 1910 and later tribal chairperson (1976–1995), with a Fort Sill Apache tribal biographical source.

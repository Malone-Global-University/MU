## 3. Consolidated historical and documentary findings

**Early republic, §§60.1–60.4.** The Northwest Ordinance's Article III language is separated from American land policy and actual occupation. Fort McIntosh (1785) and Fort Harmar (1789) are treated as contested settlements; the Northwestern Confederacy's collective-consent position and its diverse leadership are explained. Harmar's 1790 campaign and St. Clair's defeat on November 4, 1791 are correctly sequenced. No unsupported claim gives one leader exclusive direction of the multinational force. Principal checks: [National Archives, Northwest Ordinance](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/northwest-ordinance); [Fort McIntosh treaty](https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-wyandot-etc-1785-0006); [Fort Harmar treaty](https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-wyandot-etc-1789-0018).

**Northwestern conflict and northeastern diplomacy, §§60.5–60.8.** Fallen Timbers is dated August 20, 1794. The Greenville study now distinguishes the August 3, 1795 treaty's peace, hostages and prisoner restoration (Article II), land boundary/reserved parcels (Article III), and continuing territorial and payment provisions (Article IV), rather than paraphrasing only a cession. Canandaigua is dated November 11, 1794 and analyzed separately from Greenville, including the named land reservations and federal commitments. Jay Treaty Article III is presented as specific cross-border provisions with exceptions, not as a blanket modern legal entitlement. Sources: [Greenville text](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/greenvil.asp); [Onondaga Nation, Canandaigua](https://www.onondaganation.org/government/the-canandaigua-treaty-of-1794/); [Jay Treaty](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/jay.asp).

**Southeast, §§60.9–60.12.** Cherokee settlement geography distinguishes **Lower, Middle, Valley, and Overhill** communities. Hopewell (1785) and Holston (1791) are separately identified; Holston's provisions regarding protection, boundaries, and agricultural assistance are discussed without repeating American civilizational rhetoric as fact. Treaty of New York (August 7, 1790) receives developed Muscogee town-level analysis; Choctaw, Chickasaw, and Catawba are not assigned uniform pro-American alignments. Social history encompasses matrilineal agriculture, trade, women, captivity, African American enslavement, and changing federal policies without retrojecting nineteenth-century scale. Sources: [Hopewell](https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-cherokee-1785-0008), [Holston](https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-cherokee-1791-0029), [New York with Creek](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/cre1790.asp), and Tiya Miles's *Ties That Bind* (2005).

**Comanche, Ute, Southwest, Plains, North, §§60.13–60.16.** The earlier first-pass **erroneous attribution of an anti-Apache military alliance to the Ute–Comanche agreement** has been removed. Documented Spanish–Comanche anti-Apache diplomacy is distinguished from **incompletely preserved Ute–Comanche talks**, characterized cautiously as likely involving peace and trade. Texas and New Mexico Comanche agreements are not flattened into universal cessation of warfare. The northern Plains smallpox episode is dated **1781–1782** to reflect different institutional chronologies; it is explicitly distinguished from Fort Pitt's documented 1763 attempted intentional transmission. Distinct agricultural Missouri villages, mobile Plains communities, and Indigenous women's roles in fur exchange receive substantive coverage. Sources: [NPS Ute–Comanche history](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/indigenous-diplomacy-the-ute-comanche-treaty-of-1786.htm), [Nebraska Plains encyclopedia](https://plainshumanities.unl.edu/encyclopedia/doc/egp.ha.042.html), [NPS Mandan](https://www.nps.gov/knri/learn/historyculture/mandan.htm), [North Dakota Studies](https://www.ndstudies.gov/mandan), [McGill NWC history](https://digital.library.mcgill.ca/nwc/history/06.htm).

**Pacific and Alaska, §§60.17–60.20.** California's mission system is explained through labor compulsion, movement restrictions, disease, and organized resistance; Toypurina and Nicolás José's 1785 resistance is treated through mediated colonial judicial records. Nuu-chah-nulth and other Indigenous maritime powers are distinguished from Anglo-Spanish claims in the 1789–1794 Nootka crisis. Russian settlement is correctly identified as **Three Saints Bay on Kodiak Island in August 1784**, separate from the later headquarters near modern Kodiak. The Russian-American Company charter is dated **1799** and does not imply control of every Alaska Native nation. Sources: [Smithsonian NK360 California missions](https://americanindian.si.edu/nk360/california-missions/source-b/), [Nootka Sound scholarly study](https://academic.oup.com/past/article/242/Supplement_14/50/5637701), [NPS Three Saints Bay](https://www.nps.gov/places/three-saints-bay-site.htm), [LOC Russian-American Company collection](https://www.loc.gov/collections/yudin-collection-of-russian-american-company-records/about-this-collection/).

## 6. Scholarly scope and disclosed limitations

1. This is an **independent, selectively web-verified scholarly historical-text edition**, not a claim that every historical statement has been collated against unpublished archival originals. Identified government, Indigenous-nation, National Park Service, museum, university, published treaty, and scholarly sources support the checked chronology and key contentious facts.
2. Documentary study windows contain **identified editorial paraphrases and source summaries**, not fabricated quotations or source-page-collated original-language translations. Students requiring verbatim material should follow the linked documents, inspect edition and context, and compare with tribal and regional histories.
3. The historical geography exercises are **text-only**, designed for use with a historical atlas; the HTML does not supply embedded cartographic plates or make claims about maps not provided.
4. The 30 linked source-register entries were checked for identity against searchable institutional/publisher pages and primary-document transcriptions on a targeted basis, **not live-click-tested individually from the offline Chromium environment**. The bibliography is a traceable source register rather than a comprehensive page-level footnote apparatus.
5. Historiographical and community-level variation remains important for casualty estimates, epidemic chronology, treaty consent, Indigenous political titles, and the meanings of colonial records; the chapter avoids unjustified exact figures where evidence is inconsistent.
6. The core Chapter 1 CSS shell supplied in the Claude first pass was retained, but the Chapter 1 master file was not independently supplied for a **byte-for-byte CSS comparison**. The rendered standalone chapter was tested directly.

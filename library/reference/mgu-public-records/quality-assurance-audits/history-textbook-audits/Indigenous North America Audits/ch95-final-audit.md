## 2. Historical and legal corrections made

1. **Section 95.14, Canadian fundraising restriction:** Inspected the March 31, 1927 amending statute, **S.C. 1926–27, c. 32, s. 6**, which initially inserted **s. 149A**; the 1927 consolidated Indian Act presents it as **s. 141**. Fixed the passage that incorrectly placed the committee's final report before royal assent, and clarified that March 31 preceded the committee's final report. Confirmed the narrow funding language, written-consent prerequisite, and $50–$200 fine or imprisonment up to two months. Distinguished statutory text from subsequent expansive departmental interpretation and uncommon criminal prosecutions. Original reproduction: https://epe.lac-bac.gc.ca/100/205/301/ic/cdc/aboriginaldocs/stat/pdf/1927mr31.PDF?nodisclaimer=1. Scholarly corroboration: https://activehistory.ca/blog/2026/06/11/an-unwarranted-restraint-shining-light-on-section-141-of-the-indian-act-1927-1951/.
2. **Sections 95.11/Bryce window:** In Bryce's own 1922 retrospective account the 1907 survey covered 35 schools; about 24% of historical pupils included in the reported returns were known dead over the period covered. This is **not** an annual mortality rate or a nationwide school-system rate. He said the 1907 report was published but not its recommendations; distinguished publication from action. Source: https://www.fadedpage.com/books/20230315/html.php.
3. **Section 95.8:** Identified Gertrude Bonnin's General Federation of Women's Clubs/Indian Welfare Committee connection, Charles H. Fabens's American Indian Defense Association affiliation, and Matthew K. Sniffen's Indian Rights Association role, with the latter organization publishing *Oklahoma's Poor Rich Indians*. Source: https://digitalprairie.ok.gov/digital/api/collection/culture/id/6516/download.
4. **Sections 95.10/95.13:** Identified the Roe Indian Institute's approximately 1920–1921 renaming and transfer negotiations beginning in 1927; dated the establishment of the Allied Indian Tribes of British Columbia to the June 1916 Squamish-reserve meeting and named chairman Peter Kelly, secretary James Teit, and organizer Andrew Paull. Sources: https://pcusa.org/historical-society/collections/research-tools/guides-archival-collections/rg-555 and https://read.aupress.ca/read/liberalism-surveillance-and-resistance-indigenous-communities-in-western-canada-1877-1927/section/246135d9-5ac3-414a-96a9-e1998c265591.
5. **Section 95.12:** Dated the major Village Island potlatch associated with Dan Cranmer to December 1921, with prosecutions and ceremonial-object seizures in 1922. Retained distinction between Indigenous cultural authority and official framing of confiscations. Scholarly case study: https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/bitstream/handle/1828/8814/Cullon_Deidre_Sanders_PhD_2017.pdf.
6. **Sections 95.7/95.18/95.19:** Clarified the 1924 Pueblo Lands Act's partial protection and compensatory scheme; 1933 additional compensation did not restore every land or water right. Corrected the *Meriam Report* from a falsely categorical recommendation to 'halt further allotment' to **extreme conservatism before additional allotment with adequate support** (original report chapter I, printed pp. 41–42). Distinguished a partial allowance for the continuation of Indigenous culture from political recognition of Indigenous sovereign government (chapter IV, printed pp. 86–87). https://narf.org/nill/resources/meriam.html.
7. **Section 95.6:** Identified the November 5, 1922 Pueblo council and appeal and added an evidence-method paragraph without fabricating quotation. Historic transcript and context remain linked as an identified published scholarly study rather than an independently collated signed original.

## 5. Residual limitations and editorial lock

- Pueblo appeal remains grounded in published transcriptions and historical research, not a full manuscript/facsimile collation of every signed copy; the window is a documentary study and does not claim to quote an uninspected original.
- The 1927 Indian Act reproduction is viewed as digitized legislation and linked; the broad operational consequences draw on published archival scholarship, not independent examination of all associated department files.
- Some original source publications are linked as large PDFs. Source accessibility may vary with the hosting repository.
- The three geography studies are text-guided exercises rather than separately produced historical atlas plates, consistent with the series' textual HTML standard.
- Desktop and mobile behavior was tested locally only. Live-site deployment, remote file paths, and exact parity with a separately hosted Chapter 1 stylesheet have **not** been independently confirmed.
- Narrative is historical through approximately 1933, with identified references to later policy only for continuity. The chapter does not prematurely narrate the 1934 Indian Reorganization Act, assigned to Chapter 97.

## 3. Corrective historical work performed

1. **§86.10 Five Tribes:** Replaced misleading unitary acreages with statute-specific distinctions. Creek basic 160-acre allotment is identified separately; Cherokee 1902 Act §§11–13 distributed land **equal in appraised value to 110 average allotable acres**, not necessarily 110 actual acres, and included a homestead valued at 40 average acres; Seminole 1897/1898 agreement distributed land according to appraisal and included a distinct **40-acre homestead**, not a universal 120-acre entitlement. Choctaw–Chickasaw formulas are separately distinguished. Sources: [Cherokee Agreement (OSU)](https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/agreement-with-the-cherokee-1902-22444), [Seminole Agreement (OSU)](https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/agreement-with-the-seminole-1897-22396), [Oklahoma Historical Society](https://www.okhistory.org/publications/enc/entry.php?entryname=ALLOTMENT).
2. **§86.16 Everyday Life:** Replaced the unverified hypothetical family with the **documented Carter allotments on the Nez Perce Reservation**. The primary case is *United States v. Nez Perce County, Idaho*, **95 F.2d 232 (9th Cir., Jan. 27, 1938)**, reporting 1895 allotments to John, William and Mary Carter; patents dated 1895/1897; a 1919 heirship-partition request; Caleb Carter's 1921 fee patent and 1922–1932 tax dispute; the 1932 restoration order; and the appellate remand on the unresolved question of Caleb's **consent**. Crucially, the narrative **does not assert** that the Ninth Circuit found consent absent or determined the ultimate result on remand, nor invent kinship relationships, work routines, crops or wages. Source: [published opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/95/232/1563070/). Direct narrative now incorporates a bounded explanatory paragraph and the feature has five questions.
3. **Biographical precision:** Corrected the reference and §86.17 distinction between **Guipago (Lone Wolf the Elder; died 1879)** and **Mamadayte (Lone Wolf the Younger, plaintiff in 1903 *Lone Wolf v. Hitchcock*)**. Source: [Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/lone-wolf).
4. **Source Register and source windows:** Added 34 live HTML source/catalogue hyperlinks across 26 entries, including the original Dawes Act, 1891 amendment, Great Sioux Act/proclamation, Curtis Act context, Burke Act text, 1902 Cherokee and 1897/1898 Seminole agreements, 1928 Meriam Report, tribal history, 1882 Omaha petition, 1938 court opinion, and the GAO map. All five source windows now contain direct document/context reading links. Links to scholarly books are clearly marked **catalogue searches**, not open full-text claims. GAO map is correctly described as **1890 historical territorial geography**, not an ownership map of the completed allotment era.
5. **Statutory distinctions retained:** Surplus cessions versus later individual sales; original 1887 acreage formulas versus amendments; separate 1893 Dawes Commission; 1906 Burke fee patent changes; 1924 citizenship versus land title; 1934 reorganization versus retroactive land restoration. Earlier consolidated audit supported the principal Sioux acreage, Nez Perce cession, 1902 heirship-sale statute, 1917 competency policy and Gates/Roosevelt attribution; those claims were retained.

## 4. Source methodology and limits

The five Primary Source Windows are **editorial explanatory paraphrases and selective excerpts**, not full facsimile-collated transcriptions of the original statutes and report. This revision verified selected primary provisions and case details but did not exhaustively check every printed paragraph of every historical source or scholarly book against a particular edition. Documentary windows now link students directly to identifiable institutional copies or source guides; the 1890 GAO illustration is a reference to an actual report, not a separately embedded historical map. Geography studies remain text-guided and should be used with a historical atlas or archive map.

## 3. Historical accuracy corrections made in the final HTML

1. **§84.3: Treaty 5 acreage.** Corrected the erroneous blanket grouping of Treaties 3–7. Treaties 1 and 2: generally 160 acres per family of five; Treaties 3, 4, 6, 7: generally 640 acres per family of five; Treaty 5: generally 160 acres per family of five, with a 100-acre family-of-five provision for specific Norway House families relocating to Fisher River. Checked against the official Treaty 5 text: https://rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1100100028699/1581292696320 .
2. **§84.3: Treaty 8 tenure.** Corrected mischaracterization of 128 acres per person as a fundamentally distinct band-reserve formula. The Treaty 8 band-reserve equivalent was one square mile per family of five; 160 acres per person in severalty was a **separate individual option**, not a replacement band formula. Official treaty: https://www.rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1100100028813/1581293624572 .
3. **§84.5: 1868 Fort Laramie Article VI.** Corrected 160 acres to **up to 320 acres for a farming head of family inside the reservation**; distinguished separate 160-acre homesteading provision and later 1887 Dawes Act allotment figures. Source Window 1 now describes Article VI. National Archives: https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/fort-laramie-treaty .
4. **§84.18, Primary Source Window 5.** Corrected attribution of the 1870 Red Cloud statement: this document is the <em>Cooper Union address in New York City, June 16, 1870</em>, reported by the <em>New York Times</em> June 17, not a Washington council transcript. Rewrote the paraphrase, limitations and source questions to address the actual newspaper audience without importing separate Washington treaty negotiations. https://teachingamericanhistory.org/document/address-at-cooper-union-2/ .
5. **§84.17 and supporting chronology.** Corrected sequencing of the Great Sioux Reservation division: **March 2, 1889 statute → 1889 Crook Commission consent campaign → February 10, 1890 presidential proclamation**. Checked against Benjamin Harrison's official message and proclamation: https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/message-congress-reporting-the-great-sioux-reservation and https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/proclamation-295-sioux-nation-indians . All directly associated brief references were harmonized.
6. **§84.16: land-area estimates.** Retained qualified, approximate 1887–1934 Indigenous-held acreage figures and made explicit that definitions vary and that not every loss is attributable to a single statute. Chapter 86 remains responsible for an extensive quantitative treatment.

## 7. Interpretive/adjacent-chapter control

The chapter retains separate U.S./Canada legal analyses; explains reservations/reserves as both territorial promises and instruments of control; does not equate Canada's departmental peasant-farming program to the U.S. Dawes statute; does not portray the pass system as a statute; preserves examples of Indigenous agriculture, petitions, kinship and political agency; and maintains the planned transition to Canadian statutory administration in Chapter 85 and U.S. allotment detail in Chapter 86.

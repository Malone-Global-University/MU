## Final historical review and narrowly scoped corrections

The submitted additions were compared paragraph by paragraph with the prior audited manuscript. The 17 additions preserve the teaching features and improve the particular historical topics for which development was requested. The additional source cross-check found three publication-worthwhile refinements:

1. **§45.10 (Onontio):** specify that the title originated with Charles Huault de Montmagny, governor 1636–1648, and came to designate successors. It must not be back-projected into the original 1603–1625 Champlain alliance formation. Source: *Dictionary of Canadian Biography*, Montmagny, https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/342.
2. **§45.23 (Allouez and Illinois visitors):** Illinois delegation reached Chequamegon in **1666**, while Allouez's report is commonly dated **1667**. The submitted prose had collapsed the date of the report into the visit's date. Source: Illinois State Museum historical overview, https://museum.state.il.us/muslink/nat_amer/post/htmls/hi_contact.html.
3. **§45.26 (Carheil):** distinguish Carheil's presence and earlier complaints in the 1690s from his **August 30, 1702** comprehensive letter criticizing liquor trafficking and colonial personnel. This later source is legitimate retrospective evidence for late-seventeenth-century conditions, not a 1690s-dated document. Source: *Dictionary of Canadian Biography*, Carheil, https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/687.

### Additional verified newly added historical material

- Gabriel Sagard lived among the Wendat in 1623–1624 and published *Le grand voyage* and the Wendat word list/dictionary in 1632. *Dictionary of Canadian Biography* https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/sagard_gabriel_1E.html; American Philosophical Society https://as.amphilsoc.org/repositories/2/resources/352.
- The Huron-Wendat refuge on Île d'Orléans lasted approximately 1651–1656; a Haudenosaunee attack in 1656 contributed to leaving the island. Quebec official cultural-heritage register https://www.patrimoine-culturel.gouv.qc.ca/detail.do?id=93521&methode=consulter&type=bien.
- Sainte-Marie II on Gahoendoe/Christian Island was a fortified mission built in 1649; Wendat refugees suffered major hunger and disease in 1649–1650. The exact number of residents is debated and appropriately not stated as an unqualified precision figure. Parks Canada https://www.pc.gc.ca/apps/dfhd/page_nhs_eng.aspx?i=59322&id=361.
- The Sainte-Marie mission (1639–1649) had residential, hospital and workshop facilities with European workers and Wendat visitors; food and cultural relations depended upon surrounding Wendat settlements. Parks Canada https://www.pc.gc.ca/apps/dfhd/page_nhs_eng.aspx?id=834 and Huronia Historical Parks https://hhp.on.ca/digital-tour-of-sainte-marie/.
- Marquette and Wendat travelers established Saint-Ignace in 1671 near Odawa communities; the mission expanded upon already established Indigenous settlement at the Straits. *Dictionary of Canadian Biography* https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/marquette_jacques_1E.html and Mackinac State Historic Parks https://www.mackinacparks.com/explore/history/colonial-michilimackinac-history/.
- The earlier audited claims on Savignon (1610–1611), Wendake (1697), Saint-Lusson's contested date and 14/17 participant reports (1671), *Edge of Empire* Document 1 (May 16, 1671, pp. 1–2), Fort Pontchartrain (July 24, 1701), Kondiaronk (1688 and 1701), and the Great Peace (August 4, 1701) have been preserved.
- The Michilimackinac Evidence Study remains correctly labeled an **Evidence Study**, not a fabricated primary-source quotation. All three actual source windows remain labeled paraphrases. No unverified source excerpts introduced by the expansion.

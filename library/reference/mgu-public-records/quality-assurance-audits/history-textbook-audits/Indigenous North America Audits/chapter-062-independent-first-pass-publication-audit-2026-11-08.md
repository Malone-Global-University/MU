## Historical source checks — source text versus external verification

1. **§62.3 — Tenskwatawa's emergence:** The year **1805** is supportable in standard biographical treatments, but accounts differ about the precise timing and the traditional narrative of a single near-death episode. Douglas L. Winiarski's 2024 reassessment identifies major evidentiary problems with supposedly known details of the Prophet's early life. **Action:** distinguish reported visions, outsider descriptions, and firmly dated records; avoid asserting retrospective moral biographies as established fact. Source: https://scholarship.richmond.edu/religiousstudies-faculty-publications/61/ ; https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/tenskwatawa_7F.html .

2. **§62.4 — White River accusations:** The narrative correctly refuses to generalize the episode to all Indigenous religion. The documented violent Lenape witch-hunt is **especially associated with March 1806**, when four people were killed at or near Woapikamunk on the White River. The preview's 1805–1806 span may confuse students by implying a 1805 killing without source-specific support. **Action:** make 1806 primary, identify the place and people involved, distinguish Tenskwatawa's influence from responsibility established by records. Source: https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/tenskwatawa_7F.html ; https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119583707.ch9 .

3. **§62.5 — Eclipse and Harrison:** **June 16, 1806 is correct**, and a contemporary challenge from Harrison asking the Lenape to test the Prophet does have an evidentiary basis. The draft's phrase that “Harrison's role [is] better attested in later retellings than in contemporary evidence” is **overly dismissive**: an early-1806 Harrison letter is cited in scholarship. The precise claim that Tenskwatawa knowingly arranged his prediction *in direct response* to the challenge, and the details of an encounter, are less certain. **Action:** acknowledge the contemporaneous challenge and the confirmed eclipse, distinguish them from later narrative embellishments and disputed foreknowledge. Sources: https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/tenskwatawa_7F.html ; https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10123/chapter/9 ; https://news.utexas.edu/2024/04/02/the-power-in-prediction-eclipses-and-native-americans/ .

4. **§62.8 — Prophetstown:** **1808 and the confluence of Tippecanoe/Wabash rivers are accurate.** The source draft is far too brief to explain the religious, agricultural, demographic, and diplomatic dimensions of a multinational community. **Action:** develop documented peoples, kinship networks, settlement supply, motives for relocation, and range of loyalties. Sources: https://www.in.gov/history/about-indiana-history-and-trivia/explore-indiana-history-by-topic/timelines/indiana-territory-timeline/ ; https://www.in.gov/dnr/state-parks/parks-lakes/prophetstown-state-park/ .

5. **§62.10 — collective territorial consent:** The connection to the **1786 confederacy address** is appropriate. The draft correctly recognizes that collective ownership was a contested political program, not a doctrine uniformly held by every Indigenous nation. **Action:** substantiate the political argument through separately identified contemporaneous councils, treaties, and Tecumseh's speeches; do not conflate the 1786 and 1810 movements. Source: https://www.in.gov/history/state-historical-markers/find-a-marker/tecumseh-and-harrison/ .

6. **§62.12 — Treaty of Fort Wayne:** **September 30, 1809** and named parties (Delaware, Potawatomi, Miami, Eel River Miami) are correct. Article 9 conditioned a specified Wabash cession on Kickapoo consent. A **separate Wea agreement on October 26** and **Kickapoo agreement on December 9, 1809** followed. **Action:** name the precise dates, articles, compensation and affected lands rather than vaguely referring to “supplementary agreements”; distinguish signatories from later objectors. Sources: https://docsteach.org/document/treaty-tribes-fort-wayne/ ; https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-wea-1809-0103 ; https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-kickapoo-1809-0104 .

7. **§62.13 — Vincennes:** The **August 1810** meeting between Tecumseh and William Henry Harrison is historically correct; so is a **second meeting in July 1811** (§62.17). **Action:** reconstruct the words recorded in Harrison's reports and distinguish his hostile framing from Tecumseh's reported argument; avoid invented dialogue. Source: https://www.in.gov/history/state-historical-markers/find-a-marker/tecumseh-and-harrison/ .

8. **§62.16 — southern tour:** The 1811 mission and reported opposition of Choctaw leader Pushmataha are broadly supported, but actual talks, extent of Cherokee engagement, and retrospective anecdotes require individual source identification. **Action:** avoid attributing a single unified response to Creek, Choctaw, Chickasaw, or Cherokee peoples, and do not back-project the later Creek War into 1811.

9. **§§62.17–62.19 — Tippecanoe:** The **November 7, 1811** predawn battle, Tecumseh's absence, and the American destruction of Prophetstown are accurate. Harrison's troops burned the town on **November 8**, after taking provisions. **Action:** reconstruct the preceding diplomatic exchanges, Harrison's advance, both forces' roles, casualties with sourced uncertainty, destructive aftermath, civilian displacement, and the fact that the religious movement did not immediately disappear. An important caution: some accessible institutional pages contain internal errors about Nov. 6 or Nov. 11; use the securely supported Nov. 7 chronology. Sources: https://www.in.gov/history/state-historical-markers/find-a-marker/tecumseh-and-harrison/ ; https://www.in.gov/dnr/state-parks/parks-lakes/prophetstown-state-park/ ; U.S. Army Center of Military History, *Defending a New Nation, 1783–1811*.

10. **§62.20 — New Madrid earthquakes:** The draft is correct that the first principal earthquake occurred **December 16, 1811**, *after* Tippecanoe. The chapter should distinguish contemporaneous reports, Tecumseh's reported remarks, and later associations of the earthquakes with prophecy. Source: https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/science/summary-1811-1812-new-madrid-earthquakes-sequence .

11. **Source-register bibliographic weakness:** Winiarski's article can be fully identified as Douglas L. Winiarski, “Revisioning the Shawnee Prophet: Revitalization Movements, Religious Studies, and the Ontological Turn,” *Early American Studies* **22**, no. **2** (2024): **305–350**, DOI **10.1353/eam.2024.a927907**. The four final surname-only entries need author, title, publication year, edition/publisher, and relevant section. See https://scholarship.richmond.edu/religiousstudies-faculty-publications/61/ .

**Historical verdict:** The overall framing and most dates in the sparse outline are reasonable; the major issue is absence of developed historical content, not a multitude of demonstrably false statements. An especially important *wording correction* is Harrison's documented eclipse challenge, and the 1806 White River sequence should be more precise. Even where a one-sentence assertion appears correct, it is not sufficient basis to certify a 600–850-word textbook section.

## 3. Substantive historical verification and corrections

1. **Cayuse Five and contested trial:** Reconstructed disease and mission conflict, legal uncertainty and Cayuse interpretations. Set execution of the five Cayuse men to **June 3, 1850**, following National Park Service and Oregon scholarship. The trial is not treated as conclusive proof of each defendant's individual guilt; dates and legal status are distinguished.
2. **Oregon territory and land legislation:** Distinguished the 1846 U.S.–British Oregon Treaty from Indigenous consent, the 1848 federal territorial organization, the 1853 establishment of Washington Territory, and the **September 1850 Donation Land Act**. Federal land allocation did not establish pre-existing Indigenous consent.
3. **Western Oregon treaty inventory:** Clearly distinguishes the **1851 unratified agreements**, various **1853–1855 ratified treaties** (including the January 22, 1855 Willamette Valley Treaty), and the **1855 Coast Treaty that was never ratified**. This prevents a broad, incorrect assertion that all Oregon treaties failed or all were approved.
4. **Coast Reservation vs unratified treaty:** Explicitly distinguishes the August 1855 negotiated Coast Treaty from the executive reservation created **November 9, 1855**, verified in *United States v. Alcea Band of Tillamooks*, 329 U.S. 40. Reservation creation by executive authority did not itself validate the unratified cession.
5. **Puget Sound treaty dates:** Medicine Creek signed **December 26, 1854**, Senate-approved **March 3, 1855**, and proclaimed **April 10, 1855**. Point Elliott signed **January 22, 1855**, Senate-approved **March 8, 1859**, proclaimed **April 11, 1859**. The draft's correct basics are retained, and signing versus proclamation is now explicit.
6. **Walla Walla treaty distinctions:** Yakama and Walla Walla/Cayuse/Umatilla treaties signed **June 9, 1855**; Nez Perce treaty **June 11, 1855**. All three were Senate-approved **March 8, 1859**, but the presidential proclamations occurred on differing April dates. The current chapter does not confuse these with ratification immediately in 1855.
7. **Fox Island revision and Leschi chronology:** The **August 5, 1856** Fox Island council revised southern Puget Sound reservations. The account no longer falsely implies that Leschi had entered territorial custody by that council; a detailed historical account places the delivery of Leschi into custody on **November 13, 1856**. His execution date of **February 19, 1858** and the **December 2004 historical exoneration** are retained with the express limitation that the special historical court was not a conventional appellate reversal.
8. **Rogue River War:** The final phase opened after the **October 8, 1855 Lupton attacks**, with the formal conflict ending by **June 1856** and most surviving communities removed. Differentiates Takelma, Shasta-related, and Athabaskan-speaking peoples and the roles of vigilantes, territorial volunteers and federal regulars. No ungrounded exact casualty totals are asserted.
9. **Inland campaigns of 1858:** Steptoe's force defeated **May 17, 1858**; Wright's engagements at **Four Lakes September 1** and **Spokane Plains September 5**; destruction of a reported approximately **800 horses September 8**. The chapter identifies official reporting and numerical uncertainty, and distinguishes military fighting from attacks on economic resources.
10. **Grand Ronde removal versus reservation creation:** The March 1856 Ambrose removal journey is distinguished from the **June 30, 1857** executive order formally establishing Grand Ronde. Distinct west Oregon peoples and separate routes are not collapsed into one uniform march.
11. **Unresolved Ambrose departure count:** **Source discrepancy explicitly disclosed.** The Confederated Tribes of Grand Ronde's educational summary gives **325** people starting one February–March 1856 removal march; a published transcription of Ambrose's closing report says **395**. Both describe a roughly **263-mile, 33-day** journey, with **eight deaths and eight births** in the diary. Neither number is presented as securely reconciled or as a statewide removal total. The original manuscript has not been facsimile-collated for this audit.
12. **Reserved rights and sovereignty:** Clarifies that treaty fishing provisions concern retained pre-existing rights, not new privileges spontaneously conferred by the United States. Specific treaty terms vary, including **Makah whaling and sealing**. Later litigation is referenced only briefly; Chapter 113 owns the modern Fish Wars, and Chapter 78 owns the Nez Perce War of 1877.
13. **Sohon source:** Identifies the 1855 images as drawings by an expedition participant and states clearly that art showing a treaty council does not itself establish informed political consent. Descriptions are tied to the Smithsonian and Washington State University collections without reproducing restricted images.

- The bibliography is selective, contains verifiable documentary collection links and identified scholarly books, and is not a claim of exhaustive catalog or archival verification.

- No production-site publication, CDN deployment, live hosting, remote link-health test, accessibility certification, or complete archive-facsimile collation was performed.
- Excluding major later campaigns is intentional: Chapter 73 treats the Dakota War, Chapter 78 the Nez Perce War, and Chapter 113 the modern treaty-rights struggles.

The uploaded Claude HTML was a skeletal first build despite correctly named sections. It had an accurate broad outline but lacked a full narrative, visible main-column division headings, four documentary windows, most teaching features and a complete scholarly review. The final version retains all assigned sections and the chapter's boundaries while developing the history at the agreed first-edition depth. Its principal corrections distinguish disputed source counts, treaty status, executive reservation action, legal processes, and political sovereignty.

The canonical publication HTML named in Section 1 is the authoritative file. No broad rewrite is recommended.

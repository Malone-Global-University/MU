## Consolidated historical and documentary corrections

1. **Winnemucca publication and wrong URL:** Removed `gutenberg.org/ebooks/9862` (which resolves to an unrelated book). Corrected bibliographic original: Sarah Winnemucca Hopkins, *Life Among the Piutes* (1883), printed for the author, available in Boston through Cupples, Upham & Co. and in New York through G. P. Putnam's Sons. Linked the University of Pennsylvania Online Books bibliography and digitized editions: https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupid?key=olbp36960 .
2. **Zitkala-Ša:** Kept the 1921 digital edition and the first publication of her essays in *The Atlantic Monthly* in 1900; a direct source link is now in the window: https://digital.library.upenn.edu/women/zitkala-sa/stories/stories.html .
3. **Francis La Flesche:** Added the actual full-text *The Middle Five* (1900), https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/62094 , in both the window and the Source Register; retained the Library of Congress biography as supplemental rather than substituting it for the work itself.
4. **Dated *Wassaja* issue completed:** Source 4 now specifically identifies **volume 1, number 1 (April 1916), Chicago**. Historical scholarship in Rosalyn R. LaPier and David R. M. Beck, “Crossroads for a Culture,” *Chicago History* 38, no. 1 (Spring 2012), pp. 28–29, reproduces and dates the issue's masthead and reports the inaugural editorial's abolition-of-the-Bureau purpose. The Museum of Indigenous People also reproduces the first cover; the ASU Carlos Montezuma Collection identifies the periodical holdings. The lesson now gives a source-based paraphrase of the first issue's image and documented editorial position, rather than an account of the whole run with an unfinished editorial-status warning. The **complete physical/digitized original April issue was not collated leaf by leaf**, and no verbatim archival transcription is asserted. https://www.museumofindigenouspeople.org/post/wassaja-long-fight-for-native-rights ; https://empdb.apps.umt.edu/storage/facultyfiles/674/Chicago%20Hist.pdf ; https://aspace.lib.asu.edu/repositories/2/resources/994/collection_organization .
5. **Laurier Memorial:** The primary-source catalog dates presentation to **August 25, 1910**, at Kamloops. Linked the exact four-page digitized item and noted James Teit's catalogued secretary role: https://www.canadiana.ca/view/oocihm.65247 .
6. **Alaska Native Brotherhood:** Clarified **1912 organizing meetings in Juneau** and **1913 elected grand presidency of Peter Simpson**, rather than implying Simpson was elected in 1912 or treating Sitka as the only founding setting. The ANB/ANS ceremonial manual identifies no grand president in its 1912 row and lists Simpson beginning in 1913; the State of Alaska's chronology and the State Library historical collection also support 1912 organizing. https://www.alaska.gov/kids/learn/chronology.htm .
7. **Alaska Native Sisterhood:** Disclosed differences in sources: Josephine Ukas's retrospective account locates early organizing at Wrangell in autumn **1914** (Sealaska Heritage Institute), the National Library of Medicine gives establishment at Haines in **1915**, and the State of Alaska's chronology records a 1915 Sitka convention. These reflect differing reports and potentially differing organizational milestones; no single definitive founders' list was invented. https://shispecialcollections.blogspot.com/2012/10/a-history-of-leadership-alaska-native.html ; https://www.nlm.nih.gov/nativevoices/timeline/419.html .
8. **ANB newspaper:** Identified *The Alaska Fisherman* (**1923–1932**) rather than a generic unnamed newspaper; distinguished the later *Voice of Brotherhood*. https://www.camp14.com/history .
9. **Posey:** Specified his Dawes Commission work as translator and fieldworker beginning in **1904**, avoiding the inference that employment equaled endorsement of allotment. https://academic.oup.com/reference/62335/reference-article-abstract/554026636 .
10. **Louis Shotridge:** Distinguished museum association and collecting in the early 1910s from the **1922 assistant-curator appointment** recorded in Penn Museum's curatorial finding aid. https://collections.penn.museum/collections/archives/findingaid/552766 .
11. **Greenlandic literature:** Identified Storch's original novel **1914**, common spellings *Singnagtugaq* and *Sinnattugaq*, and **1915** Danish translation by Knud Rasmussen. His work remains contextualized with Henrik Lund's 1912 anthem poem and 1911 administrative changes. https://inuit.uqam.ca/iu/documents/singnagtugaq ; https://lex.dk/Mathias_Storch .

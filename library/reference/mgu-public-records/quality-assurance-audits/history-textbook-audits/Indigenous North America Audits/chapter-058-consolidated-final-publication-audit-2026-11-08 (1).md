### Specific quotations and high-risk factual cases retained

- **Fort Pitt, June 24, 1763:** William Trent's “I hope it will have the desired effect” matches the published transcription. The chapter distinguishes the historical attempt, separately documented Amherst–Bouquet correspondence, and the unresolved question of actual infection caused by this attempt. Elizabeth A. Fenn, “Biological Warfare in Eighteenth-Century North America: Beyond Jeffery Amherst,” *Journal of American History* 86.4 (2000): 1552–1580; [published text](https://historycooperative.org/journal/smallpox-blankets/).
- **Royal Proclamation, October 7, 1763:** “as their Hunting Grounds” appears in the [Government of Canada reproduced text](https://www.cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1370355181092/1607905122267). Its distinction between restriction of private/colonial purchases and the Crown's own asserted dominion is retained.
- **Michilimackinac, June 2, 1763:** Keeps the cautious description of the ball near the open gates rather than an unverified trajectory over the walls. [Michigan DNR historical note](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/MIDNR/bulletins/7f4206).
- **Paxton Boys and Conestoga, December 1763:** Retains the identified six deaths at Conestoga Town and fourteen at Lancaster, and identifies the people killed as nonparticipants in Pontiac's War. [Digital Paxton](https://digitalpaxton.org/works/digital-paxton/historical-overview.4).
- **Bushy Run, Bloody Run and Devil's Hole:** Numeric casualty estimates remain source-contingent, not population-level certainties. See [U.S. Army Bushy Run account](https://www.army.mil/article/4153/training_ground_the_battle_of_bushy_run_august_5_6_1763) and [Detroit Historical Society](https://www.detroithistorical.org/learn/online-research/blog/battle-bloody-run-and-pontiacs-tree).

The chapter's larger interpretive claims remain historically defensible: the broad resistance was decentralized; British diplomatic and supply policies contributed to unrest but were not the exclusive cause; religious revitalization mattered; European land cessions did not amount to negotiated Indigenous cessions; and the negotiated end of hostilities varied by region.

## Substantive historical corrections

### H1 — Treaty of New Echota, 1835, Article 12 (publication-critical)

**Problem:** The source draft twice implied that Article 12 remained an operative guarantee under which Cherokee households could stay on eastern individual land reservations. Article 12 of the 1835 treaty did propose conditional pre-emption rights, but **Article 1 of the March 1, 1836 supplementary agreement, ratified with the treaty, expressly relinquished and declared void the reservation/pre-emption rights of Articles 12 and 13**.

**Correction:** Both the in-text vocabulary discussion in §66.3 and the eastern-remainer explanation in §66.17 now distinguish the original provision from the operative treaty as amended and explain other circumstances of Cherokee continuity.

**Source:** Oklahoma State University, *Treaty with the Cherokee, 1835*, March 1, 1836 supplementary articles: https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-cherokee-1835-0439 ; see also 7 Stat. 488 and the National Archives/Indigenous Digital Archive treaty catalogue https://digitreaties.org/treaties/treaty/183393855/ .

### H2 — Tishomingo's death in 1838

**Problem:** Summary, narrative, and back matter unconditionally declared the Chickasaw leader died on the removal route. Chickasaw Nation historical materials identify newer research placing his death *after arrival in western Choctaw territory*, while acknowledging older tellings that locate his death en route.

**Correction:** All three locations now identify the dating and revised location with appropriate historiographical qualification rather than asserting a death on the road.

**Source:** Chickasaw Nation, *Removal* (July 4, 1837): https://www.chickasaw.tv/events/removal .

### H3 — Cherokee 1838 protest memorial

**Problem:** Draft gave an unanchored 'more than fifteen thousand' number.

**Correction:** §66.13 specifies the 1838 memorial submitted as House Document No. 316, reporting **15,665 signatures**. The number is properly attributed to the petition's submission, not represented as an independently verified population census or conflated with earlier memorials.

**Source:** U.S. Congress, 25th Cong., 2nd sess., *Memorial of the Cherokee Delegation*, House Doc. 316, laid before House April 9, 1838. First page visually inspected; https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/SERIALSET-00329_00_00-021-0316-0000/pdf/SERIALSET-00329_00_00-021-0316-0000.pdf . The Digital Library of Georgia separately preserves the April 13, 1838 Cherokee delegation letter reporting the same number: https://dlg.usg.edu/record/dlg_zlna_ch025 .

### H4 — Steamboat *Monmouth*, 1837

**Problem:** Draft correctly located the disaster in October 1837 and reported hundreds of deaths, but speculated too firmly that financial incentives, defective vessel condition, and inadequate regulation *caused* the collision.

**Correction:** §66.8 gives October 31, 1837; *Monmouth*; *Trenton* towed by *Warren*; Mississippi River near present-day Profit Island Bend, Louisiana. States the historical disagreement: **311 commonly reported; other sources roughly 300–400**, while withholding disputed causal judgments.

**Sources:** National Park Service, https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/new-madrid-and-the-trail-of-tears.htm ; Smithsonian National Museum of the American Indian, https://americanindian.si.edu/nk360/removal-muscogee/during.html ; Muscogee Nation's Mvskoke Media, https://www.mvskokemedia.com/more-than-300-mvskoke-killed-in-steamboat-wreck-honored-in-louisiana/ ; University of Arkansas at Little Rock interpretive source, https://ualrexhibits.org/steamboats/explore-the-exhibit/steamboats-and-their-role-in-indian-removal/ .

### H5 — Muscogee who remained in Alabama

**Problem:** The draft strongly documented western reconstruction but did not name the continuing Poarch Creek people in Alabama, an important omission for a five-nation chapter that emphasizes surviving homelands.

**Correction:** Added evidence-based explanation in §66.8 and cross-national reference in §66.20; added Poarch tribal historical resources in further reading. Does **not** retroject modern federal recognition to the 1830s.

**Sources:** Poarch Creek Indians, https://pci-nsn.gov/our-story/history/ ; Encyclopedia of Alabama, https://encyclopediaofalabama.org/article/poarch-creek-indians/ .

### H6 — Harkins letter date and edition identification

**Problem:** Draft's general December 1831/1832 dating was broadly defensible but unspecific enough to obscure the identifiable first printing.

**Correction:** Identifies *Natchez Weekly Courier*, December 2, 1831, p. 6, and the *Cherokee Phoenix* republication of January 28, 1832. The editorial paraphrase remains clearly marked, not a quotation.

**Sources:** Choctaw Nation https://www.choctawnation.com/news/iti-fabvssa/george-w-harkins-letter-to-the-american-people/ ; Western Carolina University *Cherokee Phoenix* digital collection https://www.wcu.edu/library/DigitalCollections/CherokeePhoenix/Vol4/no28/indians-page-1-column-1a.html .

### H7 — 1837 Treaty of Doaksville and provenance

**Correction:** Source window now directly identifies January 17, 1837, treaty source (11 Stat. 573; Kappler II:486–488) and Article 3's **$530,000** payment: $30,000 initially, $500,000 invested under federal direction. This supplements rather than replaces the comparative Indigenous diplomacy narrative.

**Sources:** Oklahoma State University Indian treaties database https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-choctaw-and-chickasaw-1837-0486 ; Chickasaw Nation https://www.chickasaw.tv/events/treaty-of-doaksville .

### H8 — Source-window traceability, bibliography, and scope

**Problem:** The four 'primary-source windows' contained labeled paraphrases or generic document descriptions but little direct archival identification; the bibliography had an incorrect Thornton book title and sparse edition data.

**Correction:** Added verified source links for all four source windows and anchored the Muscogee study to John G. Reynolds's Sept. 2, 1837 letter to C. A. Harris and J. T. Sprague's Apr. 1, 1837 journal as reproduced/excerpted by the Smithsonian. Cherokee window now identifies National Archives material (M1475) and John Ross's financial account of a steamboat detachment. Fixed Thornton's title to *The Cherokees: A Population History*, added edition/publisher details for 9 titles, and explicitly disclosed that the source passages are *not* full original-manuscript collations.

**Sources:** National Archives, https://www.archives.gov/research/native-americans/cherokee-removal ; DocsTeach, https://docsteach.org/document/report-john-ross/ ; Smithsonian https://americanindian.si.edu/nk360/removal-muscogee/during.html ; University of Nebraska Press, https://www.nebraskapress.unl.edu/nebraska-paperback/9780803294103/the-cherokees/ . Individual publishers and Smithsonian catalogue entries were used for other titles.

### H9 — Chickasaw migration duration

**Correction:** §66.10 now notes Chickasaw records of continued removals through **1851** and later family arrivals. It retains the approved 1837–1838 principal movement window without implying that the entire migration ended then.

**Source:** Chickasaw Nation, https://chickasaw.net/Our-Nation/History/Removal.aspx .

## Explicitly retained uncertainty

- **Mortality:** Approximately 4,000 Cherokee deaths is a conventional estimate associated with missionary physician Elizur Butler, not a complete measured count. Similarly, Choctaw, Muscogee, Chickasaw, and Seminole death estimates rely on incomplete rolls and changing observation windows. The chapter appropriately separates deaths in detention, during journeys, and after arrival. Smithsonian, NPS and tribal sources support this cautious approach; NPS: https://www.nps.gov/trte/learn/historyculture/index.htm .
- **Monmouth:** The incident is secure; number of deaths and blame for the collision remain disputed.
- **Seminole:** Emphasizes coercion and Black Seminole perspectives but reserves detailed armed campaigns for Chapter 67.
- **Harkins:** Precise first print and reprint identified; the historical source window is still an editorial paraphrase.
- **Chickasaw Tishomingo:** The corrected account signals newer Chickasaw Nation research while acknowledging older narratives.
- **Geography:** The map feature is text-guided and relies on a separately provided historical atlas/map; no embedded map plate is present.

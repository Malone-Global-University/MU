## 3. Conformity to approved 20-section plan

All approved topics occur in their intended sections:

1. **Division I, §§59.1–59.4:** Indigenous and imperial meanings of the Revolution; contested land transfers, Dunmore's War and prewar land pressure; British/American diplomatic recruitment; governing councils, kinship and women's political roles.
2. **Division II, §§59.5–59.8:** Six Nations neutrality and differentiation; Oriskany/Fort Stanwix (1777); Joseph and Molly Brant, Loyalist networks and Stockbridge-Munsee service; Sullivan–Clinton destruction of Haudenosaunee agricultural and residential infrastructure (1779).
3. **Division III, §§59.9–59.12:** Wabanaki/Mi'kmaq/Wolastoqiyik diplomacy and Watertown (1776); Cherokee regions, 1776 invasions and 1777 treaties; Dragging Canoe and Tennessee River resistance; Creek, Choctaw and Chickasaw town-level diplomacy, including documentary anchors for George Galphin and John Stuart.
4. **Division IV, §§59.13–59.16:** Ohio Country and Cornstalk's murder; first U.S.–Indigenous treaty at Fort Pitt (1778) with conditional state proposal; Ohio/Wabash operations; Gnadenhutten, Crawford and evidence of retaliatory violence in 1782.
5. **Division V, §§59.17–59.20:** household/refugee reconstruction; Treaty of Paris (1783); Treaty of Fort Stanwix and Haldimand Grand River Proclamation (1784); synthesis of continuing Indigenous sovereignty and the emerging United States.

Chapter 58's 1763–1766 resistance is referenced only as antecedent; later Northwest Indian War and detailed treaties of the 1790s are deferred to Chapter 60. The chapter's end remains 1784, with the start of negotiations in 1785 mentioned only as forward transition.

## 7. Limitations and closure decision

**Supported first-edition scope:** A complete, substantial, standalone **textual HTML historical chapter**, with identified documentary windows and scholarly bibliography, suitable for publication within the series at the standard verified here.

**Not claimed:** Exhaustive original-language or manuscript-facsimile collation of Indigenous speeches or every historical quotation; original scholarly archival research; full book-by-book review of the entire academic historiography; independent cartographic plate design or embedded historical atlas images; pixel-by-pixel comparison to the unavailable original Chapter 1 reference shell. Text-based map exercises require access to a historical atlas. Linked scholarly works may be catalog or publisher records rather than open full texts.

**Publication disposition:** **APPROVED FOR PUBLICATION — LOCKED** (November 8, 2026, Pacific). The historical topics, word-count requirement, source identification, internal navigation and five-viewpoint responsive checks are satisfied. No further broad revision cycle is warranted absent a demonstrated factual, editorial or technical defect. The canonical HTML identified in §1 is the edition to deploy; the unedited original manuscript and the earlier HOLD audit are retained only as provenance and audit history.

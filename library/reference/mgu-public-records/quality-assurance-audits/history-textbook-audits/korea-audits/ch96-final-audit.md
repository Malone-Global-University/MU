## Historical and documentary verification

### Corrected or sharpened historical details

1. **Neutral Nations Supervisory Commission:** the original manuscript's description of the NNSC as largely inactive was corrected. Sweden and Switzerland maintain permanent Panmunjom participation and carry out observations, inspections, and training; Poland participates periodically. The commission has sharply reduced access to the northern side and no current direct contact there. Source: [Swedish Armed Forces](https://www.forsvarsmakten.se/en/about-the-swedish-armed-forces/defending-sweden/tasks/operations/south-korea-nnsc/).
2. **North Korea's amended 2026 constitution:** the original account based on May South Korean analysis was updated to acknowledge the November public posting through North Korea's Naenara portal. The constitutional text adds a territorial clause naming South Korea to the south and removes unification references; the specific hostile-state rhetoric in Kim Jong Un's speeches is not falsely attributed as a literal constitutional phrase. [Yonhap, November 11](https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20261111007300315); [SBS coverage of Article 2](https://news.sbs.co.kr/english/article.do?news_id=N1008841757).
3. **Republic of Korea constitutional claims:** Article 3's territorial statement and Article 4's peaceful-unification obligation are separated and sourced to the [National Law Information Center's English reference translation](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsInfoP.do?lsiSeq=61603&urlMode=en&viewCls=engLsInfoR).
4. **Demography:** 2025 final birth count (approximately 254,300) and total fertility rate (0.80) are distinguished from the preliminary death data. [Ministry of Data and Statistics, August 26, 2026](https://mods.go.kr/board.es?act=view&bid=11773&list_no=447264&mid=a20108100000).
5. **Public opinion:** KINU's 48.2% unification necessity and 66.9% peaceful-coexistence findings (1,003 interviews in April) are not confused with the separate SNU 39% necessity finding (1,200 adults in July). [KINU original](https://www.kinu.or.kr/main/board/view.do?code=XDXh8TgkVFNJ&idx=26090&nav_code=mai1674793705), [SNU contemporary report](https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20260928009000315). Duplicate discussion from the initial expansion was removed in the final editorial consolidation.
6. **Russia–DPRK treaty:** the documentary study distinguishes Article 3's consultation provision from Article 4's military-assistance language. The linked English text is explicitly a published translation, not an authenticated official original-language facsimile.
7. **Separated families:** the newly added humanitarian analysis grounds its 2025 year-end registry figures (134,516 registered, 101,148 deceased, 34,368 living) in [Ministry of Unification figures reported contemporaneously](https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20260115005400315); the last major official reunions occurred in August 2018.
8. **Korean Wave:** the 2026 Overseas Hallyu Survey is specifically identified as a 2026 report on 2025 fieldwork among people already exposed to Korean cultural products, not a representative global vote. [Ministry of Culture](https://www.mcst.go.kr/english/policy/pressView.jsp?pSeq=622).
9. **Armistice:** the historical study links the U.S. National Archives original and distinguishes ceasefire supervision from the unachieved comprehensive peace settlement. [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/armistice-agreement-restoration-south-korean-state).
10. **Contemporary legal and military claims:** 2026 Yoon rulings, the Ninth Party Congress, June Xi visit, nuclear estimates and proposed external military deployments continue to be attributed or qualified where evidence is non-final. The text does not assume events beyond November 15, 2026 have occurred.

The factual audit is **targeted, not exhaustive**. The documents represent a mix of government pages, survey sources, historical agreements, translations and contemporaneous reporting. In the North Korean case, source access remains especially incomplete. The window on the November 2026 constitution explicitly distinguishes coverage of a publicly posted primary text from independent source collation.

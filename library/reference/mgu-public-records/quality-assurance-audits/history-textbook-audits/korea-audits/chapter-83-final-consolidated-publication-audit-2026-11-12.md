The revised chapter preserves **exactly 20 sequential historical sections (§§83.1–83.20)** in the four approved groups:

1. **§§83.1–83.5 — The collapse of economic foundations**: the late Cold War economic structure, changing Soviet trade terms, industrial decline, agricultural fragility, and the 1994 succession.

A key analytical principle runs through the revision: **state rations, actually received food, production, purchasing power, and market exchange are related but different mechanisms**. The expansion does not equate market participation with sufficient nutrition, economic diversification with political liberalization, or one refugee's recollection with a nationally representative statistic. It rejects environmental monocausality and explains the famine through the intersection of institutional fragility, changing external trade conditions, production and distribution problems, and natural shocks.

## 5. Source and factual verification retained

1. **1998 nutrition survey.** Contemporary UN and FAO/WFP materials support **62.3% stunted**, **15.6% wasted**, and **60.6% underweight** in the population of children surveyed. These are *sample findings with geographic limitations*, not exact percentages for every North Korean child. The 2002 follow-up comparison reports **39.2% stunting**, not a rate still above 40%. Sources: FAO/WFP (1998, 1999 and 2003); UN DPRK 2012 National Nutrition Survey retrospective table.
2. **Famine mortality.** Goodkind and West (2001) estimated **approximately 600,000–1 million excess deaths in 1995–2000** using demographic methods. Other estimates differ and should be evaluated by sampling, projection, migration, birth-rate effects, and the limitations of census records. The chapter does not select a falsely exact national death toll.
3. **1995 rice transfers.** The chapter distinguishes **South Korea's 150,000 metric tons in rice grants** from **Japan's 300,000 tons arranged in June 1995** (150,000 grant plus 150,000 deferred payment) and **200,000 additional tons arranged in October on deferred-payment terms**. Agreements, grants and actual shipment delivery are not conflated. Source: Japan MOFA Diplomatic Bluebook 1996.
4. **2002 prices.** Wonhyuk Lim's Brookings comparison supports the official rice retail selling-price change from **0.08 to 44 won per kilogram (550-fold)**. It is distinguished from the separate procurement-price multiplier, market prices, and nominal wage changes. No false direct equivalence between relative purchasing-power loss and inflation remains.
5. **Songun chronology.** North Korean official retrospectives identify **January 1, 1995** with an inspection of a military post; the association of the **105th Tank Division** with **August 25, 1960** is a different official retrospective event. The chapter does not collapse these accounts into a purported January 1995 tank-division visit.
6. **1996 editorial.** The New Year joint editorial is used as evidence of the state's official invocation of the “Arduous March,” but the window explicitly labels the passage an editorial paraphrase rather than claiming a verified verbatim Korean translation.
7. **Refugee interview.** The third source window identifies a specific account in Human Rights Watch's **2002** *The Invisible Exodus*, published from a small group of interviews, and explains selection, editorial mediation, and nonrepresentativeness.
8. **Regional food differences.** A 1999 FAO/WFP report differentiates agricultural districts from public-distribution-dependent industrial regions, supporting the chapter's qualified discussion of North Hamgyong and the northeastern industrial belt. It does not establish that all residents of Pyongyang were protected or all provincial farmers secure.
9. **Women and informal markets.** The explanation of women's market roles is supported as an institutional and historical interpretation, with explicit differences among marital status, employment arrangements, household responsibilities, trading capital, and region. No invented national proportion of women traders is offered.
10. **Aid monitoring.** Restrictions, access disputes, and different NGO choices are explained as documentary problems. The chapter neither assumes no diversion nor asserts that every contested shipment was diverted without evidence.

**Reference register in the HTML:** the FAO/WFP crop and nutritional assessments, UN nutrition data, Daniel Goodkind and Loraine West, the Japanese MOFA record, Wonhyuk Lim, the 1996 editorial scholarship, North Korean official Songun retrospectives (classified as official claims), Human Rights Watch's 2002 and 2006 reporting, National Research Council 2001, Stephan Haggard and Marcus Noland 2007, and later gender/migration research.

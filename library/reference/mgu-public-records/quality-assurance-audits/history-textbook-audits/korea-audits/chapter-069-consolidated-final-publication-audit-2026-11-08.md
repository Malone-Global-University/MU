## 3. Historical correction register

1. **Korean Marines at Incheon (September 15).** Corrected the impression that ROK Marines only joined later. They participated with Regimental Combat Team 5 at the Red Beach assault. Confirmed against the Naval History and Heritage Command's *Inchon Assault Landings*.
2. **Marine September 15 casualty categories.** Replaced the ambiguous “roughly two hundred killed and wounded” with the documented Marine Corps tabulation: 20 killed in action, 1 died of wounds, 1 missing, 174 wounded; **196 total in these categories**. This is not a civilian or combined-force casualty estimate.
3. **187th Airborne command and strength.** Corrected Frank S. Bowen's contemporary rank to **colonel**. Distinguished **approximately 2,800 personnel in the initial October 20 flight** from about **4,000 across subsequent drops**. Source registers: Appleman's account and the UN Command contemporaneous reporting; the initial-drop figure must not be interpreted as the complete force's eventual strength.
4. **ROK Chosan / Yalu photograph.** Reframed the Yalu water-bottle story. The National Archives of Korea photograph independently documents the bottle being filled on October 26, 1950; **it does not prove that a bottle subsequently reached President Syngman Rhee**.
5. **“Home by Christmas.”** Clarified that the phrase was associated with contemporary reports of MacArthur's optimistic expectations, not a word-for-word documentary operational order. The November 24 campaign launch remains the chapter's endpoint.
6. **Korea Institute of Military History citation.** Corrected the three-volume English-language publication history: Seoul original 1997; Bison Books / University of Nebraska Press edition 2000–2001, introduction by Allan R. Millett. Confirmed against library catalogs.
7. **Internal cross-references.** Corrected the statement that Chapter 67 narrated the June 25 invasion to **Chapter 68**; removed the misplaced Chapter 2 tide reference and pointed to §69.1.
8. **Documentary interpretation of 1950 political decisions.** Expanded differentiation between the September 27 U.S. directive, September 29 Marshall communication, October 7 UN General Assembly Resolution 376 (V), Rhee's political claims, and the military command's responsibility.
9. **Chinese engagement chronology.** Preserved October 19–25 entry and first contact, November 1–2 Unsan fighting, subsequent withdrawal, and the November 24 offensive opening. The chapter does **not** portray Chinese intervention as having begun only in late November.
10. **Combined-force and Korean agency.** Added substantive ROK Marine, KATUSA, ROK I Corps and ROK 1st/6th Division roles, alongside U.S. Army, Marine, Navy and international participants.

### Core chronology and evidence checks

- **September 15:** Wolmi-do and Incheon landing, including ROK Marine participation; Marine-specific D-day casualty total qualified.
- **September 16–29:** Eighth Army breakout, Kimpo, Seoul battle, September 26 declaration, September 28 securing, September 29 ceremony.
- **October 1:** ROK forces cross the 38th parallel; **October 7:** Resolution 376 (V); **October 10:** ROK forces take Wonsan.
- **October 19–20:** Pyongyang falls; 187th drops at Sukchon/Sunchon; **October 25–26:** Chinese engagements and ROK Chosan border arrival.
- **October 25–29:** Naval mine clearance and Marine landings at already occupied Wonsan, followed by Army landing at Iwon; distinction between intended assault and administrative landing maintained.
- **November 21:** U.S. elements reach Hyesanjin; **November 24:** UN offensive and chapter endpoint.

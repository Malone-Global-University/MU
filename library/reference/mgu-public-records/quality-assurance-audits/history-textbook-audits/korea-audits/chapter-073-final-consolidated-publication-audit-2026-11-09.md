## Historical/documentary corrections, verified and retained from prior audit

1. **United States aid:** Specific historical accounting rather than vague “several billion”; approximately $1.745 billion in bilateral economic assistance in 1953–1960, ~$2.083 billion including the separately counted CRIK and UNKRA economic-relief categories. Military assistance is NOT included; scope and period are identified. NBER historical table.
2. **Defense fiscal burden:** Approximately 54% of South Korean government expenditure in 1953 versus approximately 32% in 1957 in the cited National Archives of Korea description. Distinguishes direct outside military assistance and inconsistent denominators.
3. **1960 income:** Explicitly labels the older Bank of Korea $79 nominal GNP-per-person series and the newer World Bank approximately $159 nominal GDP-per-capita series as different measures/revisions.
4. **Seoul population:** Historical museum series, approximately 1.57 million in 1955 and 2.44 million in 1960.
5. **Primary-school enrollment (ROK):** Official historical series 72.9% in 1953 and 96.4% in 1959; these are enrollment measures, not direct proof of consistent attendance, completion, or quality.
6. **DPRK compulsory schooling:** 1956 primary and 1958 secondary as retrospective DPRK self-report to UN; independent implementation is not presumed.
7. **Northern aid:** Soviet one-billion-ruble 1953 commitment, Chinese eight-trillion **old yuan** commitment for 1954–1957 and cancellation of wartime supplies; commitments not equated to disbursement or final allocation.
8. **East German Hamhung:** Documented town-planning/engineering involvement, without unsupported country-by-country assignments of other plants.
9. **Periodization:** Two political-economic systems began diverging in 1945–1948, not for the first time after the July 1953 armistice.
10. **Primary sources:** U.S. window now identifies the June 15, 1953 Tasca Mission Report in FRUS 1952–1954, XV/2, doc. 632; its date correctly **precedes** the armistice. Chinese window identifies the November 24, 1953 *People’s Daily* account of the November 23 Sino–Korean cooperation agreement. Both are explicitly **editorial paraphrases**, not quotations and not original-language critical editions.

## Final direct revision — thirteen improvements

1. The chronology now distinguishes the **31 May 1955 U.S.–ROK surplus agricultural commodity agreement** from **P.L. 480 shipments beginning in 1956**. This follows the Republic of Korea’s official development-cooperation history.
2. The chronology no longer suggests UNKRA completed its life in 1958.
3. The chronology records the United Nations Korean Reconstruction Agency’s **formal closure on 31 August 1960**.
4. Section 73.6 distinguishes UNKRA’s late-1950s wind-down from its formal 1960 closure, citing UNGA A/4517.
5. Section 73.6 adds the 1955 agreement / 1956 delivery distinction with direct source identification.
6. Section 73.19 corrects the assertion that the National Assembly continuously reserved seats for northern constituencies. The specific **1948 constitutional-assembly electoral design** envisioned 200 southern and 100 northern seats; the North could not participate, and no claim is made of regularly elected northern deputies in the 1950s.
7. The cause-and-effect box now describes 1953 reconstruction as **reinforcing** economic systems already forming before the Korean War, not creating those institutions from nothing.
8. Section 73.15 adds analytical distinctions among the South’s national budget, external military aid, mixed-use infrastructure, and the opportunity costs of military mobilization.
9. Section 73.17 develops the South’s 1954–1959 Six-Year Compulsory Education Plan, shortage of schoolrooms and teachers, household contributions, and the limitations of enrollment statistics.
10. Section 73.17 adds comparative analysis of locality, family resources, attendance, and educational quality in both states, distinguishing formal compulsory education from educational experience.
11. Section 73.19 expands the historical difference between a claim to govern all Korea and effective administration within each state's postarmistice territory.
12. Section 73.20 expands the different experiences of farmers, industrial workers, women, refugees, and other groups behind macroeconomic reconstruction statistics.
13. Section 73.20 adds methodological guidance on aid pledges, official production indices, cross-system comparisons, and historical contingency rather than declaring a retrospective economic winner.

## 3. Completed historical and interpretive corrections

**79.1–79.5: Collapse and military takeover.** Expanded the Bu-Ma student-manifesto context; explained what leaflets establish versus what they cannot, the military intelligence investigation as an instrument of power, the gap between civilian legal succession and military command, and the later legal assessment of December 12. Inserted precisely identified May 17 proclamation No. 10, dated and signed by martial law commander Lee Hui-seong, distinguishing the order from post-1987 retrospective judicial findings.

**79.6–79.9: Gwangju, May 1980.** Expanded the sequence of urban protest, attacks by special-forces troops, the participation of drivers and other civilians, May 21 shootings, road blockade violence, citizen relief and negotiated/armed factions. A dated 2024 investigation is attributed for 166 confirmed civilian deaths in the defined May 18–27 period, 179 missing, and 2,617 injured, kept as separate populations and not inflated into an invented comprehensive toll. The 1997 Court ruling is identified by docket and does not falsely purport to resolve every individual firing order. The early citizen bulletin <em>Tusa Hoebo</em>, May 21, is now named and contextualized.

**79.10–79.12: Fifth Republic and U.S. alliance.** Expanded the Emergency Policy Committee’s cross-ministry influence, constitutional procedures, party and personnel restrictions. Differentiated a retrospectively reported 567 restricted political figures from a contemporaneous broader 811-person screening list; figures are not summed. Distinguished the 1980 referendum of October 22 from Constitution No. 9 promulgated/effective October 27. Rewrote the U.S. explanation to distinguish Korean sovereign command, CFC operational control, removal/notification/consultation of units, airborne units outside CFC control, U.S. reluctance/acceptance concerning regular army deployment, later declassifications, and Korean commanders’ responsibility. U.S. military/diplomatic involvement is neither categorically denied nor exaggerated into an undocumented direct command to shoot civilians.

**79.13–79.16: Repression and economic/social context.** Confirmed Samcheong arrest versus military reeducation categories (60,755 arrests, 39,742 reeducation assignments). Clarified the chronological distinction between 1980 dismissals and November restructuring versus promulgation of the Basic Press Act on December 31. Added concrete organizational distinctions among student, labor, religious, family and legal opposition networks. Preserved the historically labeled IMF 1980 real-GNP fall of 5.2 percent, with recognition of revised statistical-series differences.

**79.17–79.20: Memory and democracy movement.** Expanded how Gwangju eyewitness records and later commemoration were preserved; distinguished historical events from memory politics. Explained why the 1985 NKDP popular-vote figure (29.26%) and ruling DJP figure (35.25%) must be separated from the distribution of seats. Added the February 1986 ten-million-signature drive, its regional public campaign, dated March 8, 1986 opposition memorandum (as reprinted in the May 8 U.S. Congressional Record), and the October 1986 Konkuk University operation (1,447 taken into custody, 1,288 formally arrested). Concluded with the political impasse at the end of 1986; no account of the June 1987 transition was imported from Chapter 82.

## 4. The five documentary windows — corrected locators and limits

1. **Bu-Ma student declarations, October 15, 1979.** NIKH historical-source edition of two Pusan National University student texts, preceding October 16 demonstrations. Government police records remain a separate source category, not a falsely identified transcription.
2. **Martial Law Proclamation No. 10, May 17, 1980.** Seoul National University archival reproduction and the separately identified Supreme Court case 96Do3376 (April 17, 1997), with the High Court 96No1892 (December 16, 1996). Later judicial reasoning is not conflated with the original emergency proclamation.
3. **Gwangju citizens’ bulletins.** <em>Tusa Hoebo</em>, issue 1, distributed May 21, 1980. The existing window remains a comparative editorial synthesis of bulletin genres, not a purported verbatim translation or issue-specific quotation.
4. **Original 1980 constitution.** Constitution No. 9, referendum October 22 and promulgation October 27, 1980. Linked official 1980 law, plus a dated U.S. Department of State analytical memorandum on that version; no substitution of the post-1987 text.
5. **1986 constitutional petition and political statement.** Identifies a March 8, 1986 opposition memorandum associated with Kim Dae-jung, as contemporaneously reprinted in the U.S. Congressional Record of May 8, 1986; contrasted with contemporary diplomatic/human-rights reporting. The original petition signature sheet was **not** separately collated, and the editorial paraphrase does not claim to reproduce it.

**Documentary limit:** All five windows are explicitly labeled attributed *editorial paraphrases or contextual syntheses*, not archival-facsimile-verified verbatim primary-source excerpts. Some links lead to catalog descriptions, edited transcriptions, scholarly summaries, contemporary reprints, or later reports; these source types are identified rather than represented as interchangeable primary documents. Original-language facsimile collation is incomplete. This is a scholarly textual HTML first edition, not a source-critical diplomatic edition.

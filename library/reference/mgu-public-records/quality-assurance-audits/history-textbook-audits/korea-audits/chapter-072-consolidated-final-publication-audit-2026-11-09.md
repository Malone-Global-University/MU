## Publication corrections applied

### 1. Source-window fidelity — §72.9

The manuscript falsely presented the opening of Armistice Paragraph 62 as a verbatim quotation beginning **“The Armistice Agreement shall…”**. The corrected source window reproduces a **short, accurately excerpted opening**, **“The Articles and Paragraphs of this Armistice Agreement shall remain in effect until expressly superseded”**, followed by an expressly labeled editorial summary of the remainder. The National Archives transcription confirms the wording. The other quoted armistice provisions and the treaty preamble/Articles III–IV were selectively compared against documentary transcriptions; no materially contradictory quotation was identified.

### 2. Original Military Armistice Commission schedule — §72.11

Corrected the assertion that the Commission was designed to meet only on request. **Armistice Paragraph 31 originally prescribed daily meetings**, with recesses up to seven days agreed by both senior members and twenty-four-hour termination notice. The chapter distinguishes this original contractual schedule from later institutional practice.

### 3. South Korean non-signature and obligations — §72.9

Removed the overbroad categorical claim that the Republic of Korea was legally bound simply as a part of the UN Command. The chapter now distinguishes (a) signatory military commanders, (b) the military armistice's application to forces under their operational control, (c) the ROK Army's operational subordination to UNC and ceasefire compliance, (d) Rhee's governmental refusal to sign and the diplomatic assurances permitting implementation, and (e) unsettled questions concerning the separate ROK governmental position. This formulation accords with the armistice text and later UNC explanations submitted to the United Nations.

### 4. Preliminary versus final POW screening statistics — §72.3

The approximate **70,000 willing to return out of 170,000** figure is now explicitly identified as **preliminary**. The subsequent UNC screening report counted **169,944 screened**: **83,722** classified as willing to return and **86,222** as unwilling, covering mixed POW and civilian-internee categories. The manuscript emphasizes that source totals depend on screening date, prisoner classification and the conditions under which choices were made; neither preliminary nor final aggregate is presented as a timeless measure of freely expressed views.

### 5. NNRC prisoner destinations and American non-repatriates — §72.14

Distinguishes **89 prisoners expressing wishes for neutral-country resettlement** in UN document **A/2809** from the final NNRC disposition of **86 prisoners (12 Chinese and 74 Koreans) going to India**, as tabulated in Hermes's U.S. Army official history. These numbers reflect different decision and disposition dates, not contradictory complete totals. Corrected the false retrospective assertion that most of the **21 American non-repatriates** permanently remained in China: a 1980 contemporary retrospective counted 15 back in the United States, three in Europe, one deceased and two still in China. Their original 1954 choices must be kept distinct from subsequent life trajectories. The later-outcomes figure is explicitly dated and attributed, not treated as an exhaustive ultimate biography.

### 6. Geneva Conference participant count — §72.17

Corrected an impossible 19-versus-20 enumeration. The 1954 Korean discussions comprised **19 delegations**: **16 allied** (the Republic of Korea and 15 UN-force contributors, including the United States) and **three Communist** (the DPRK, PRC and USSR). South Africa was a UN military contributor but not one of the Geneva participants. This matches the State Department's contemporary conference documentation and British parliamentary statements.

### 7. Geneva Conference outcome — §72.17

Replaced wording incorrectly suggesting that the political conference never convened. The **Geneva Conference met April 26–June 15, 1954 and substantively considered the Korean question, but produced no political settlement**; the allied delegations subsequently treated it as the conference contemplated in the armistice. It occurred beyond the Article IV, Paragraph 60 recommended three-month horizon. Article V, Paragraph 62 left the armistice in effect in the absence of a superseding settlement.

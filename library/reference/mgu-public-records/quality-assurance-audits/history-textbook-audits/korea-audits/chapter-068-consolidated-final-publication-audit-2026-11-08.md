## 4. Consolidated historical corrections

**A. First American ground formation and attached artillery (§68.8).** Corrected the original assertion that infantry and artillery both flew to Pusan on July 1. The infantry portion of Task Force Smith arrived by air; attached **A Battery, 52nd Field Artillery Battalion** crossed from Japan by landing ship and joined the force near Pyongtaek on approximately July 3–4. The correction distinguishes the battalion's infantry movement from the artillery movement and avoids calling the artillery element B Battery. Contemporary U.S. Army medical interviews support the separation; Roy E. Appleman's operational study gives additional unit detail. See <https://achh.army.mil/history/book-korea-taskforcesmith-adams/> and Appleman, *South to the Naktong*, chapter on American ground-force entry.

**B. ROK antitank armament (opening and §68.2).** Replaced the claim of no antitank weapons with **no effective antitank capability against T-34/85 armor**: 2.36-inch bazookas and a small number of antitank guns existed, but were inadequate against the tanks encountered. This distinction is consistent with the official U.S. Army campaign narratives.

**C. Han River bridges, casualty estimates and force returns (§68.4).** Retained source-dependent approximate bridge-death counts and prohibited treating any number as a verified tally. Changed the **54,000 returning ROK soldiers** from a firm July 1 count to a report accumulating during the first days of July. Clarified the uneven sources on Choi Chang-sik's posthumous retrial: the Academy of Korean Studies describes a 1962 retrial, while the National Archives discusses a later appeal/acquittal reported in 1964. See <https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0060951> and <https://www.archives.go.kr/next/newsearch/listSubjectDescription.do?id=006336&pageFlag=&sitePage=>.

**D. Walker source window III (§68.13).** Replaced the generic reconstructed-provenance description with the documentable **July 29, 1950 address to the 25th Infantry Division staff at Sangju**. Appleman, chapter XII, reproduces a paraphrase from **contemporary notes**. The displayed teaching passage remains explicitly labeled as an editorial paraphrase rather than misidentified as a Walker verbatim transcript. See <https://www.koreanwaronline.com/history/Appleman/Chapter12.htm>.

**E. Pusan Perimeter geography (§68.13).** Corrected the assertion that about 140 miles measured the circuit: it is used as an approximate length of the irregular defended front, while the defended area is described roughly as 100 miles north–south and 50 miles east–west. These are not exact, immutable geometric boundaries.

**F. No Gun Ri documentary comparison (§68.12).** Corrected the first message's unit and date to the **8th Cavalry Regiment message log, July 24, 1950**; its transmission to the 7th Cavalry at No Gun Ri was **not established** by the 2001 American review. Independently distinguished Ambassador **John J. Muccio's July 26 letter to Dean Rusk** reporting refugee-control policy; this letter received public attention in 2006, after the original bilateral investigation. The two items cannot be conflated into a single direct order to the troops involved. The Korean statutory recognition of **218 victims** is broken down as **150 dead, 13 missing and 55 with lasting injury/disability**, rather than falsely representing all 218 as deaths. Sources: U.S. Army No Gun Ri review, <https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_the_Army_No_Gun_Ri_Review_Report/Executive_Summary>; Korean Yeongdong County / No Gun Ri Peace Park, <https://mayor.yd21.go.kr/nogunri/html/sub04/040304.html>.

**G. Korean agency and campaign history.** Expanded §§68.5, 68.11, 68.14–68.16 and §68.18 with additional discussion of ROK unit cohesion and reorganization, the central/eastern sectors, Tabu-dong, Pohang, Yeongcheon, the southern Masan corridor, combined-arms coordination and mobile reserves. Added Korean official-history verification entry points: the Academy of Korean Studies' <https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0011567> and <https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0013458>; the Ministry of Patriots and Veterans Affairs, <https://unarchives.mpva.go.kr/contents/about625>.

**H. Narrative and synthesis.** Added historically substantive development to the first American engagements, the July delaying defense, civilian displacement, Naktong bridgeheads, southwest and eastern operations, supply-line logistics, the September offensive, and the comparative explanation of why the perimeter held. Did not narrate the Incheon landing.

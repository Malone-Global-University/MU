## 3. Documentary source register — 16 individually identified source studies

**Provenance principle.** “Direct NIKH transcription” means the original-language text or official modern-Korean transcription was available in the National Institute of Korean History’s publication; it does **not** mean that the physical archival artifact was inspected. “Mediated” means a named historical record was encountered through a later scholarly quotation or discussion, not that the original folio was authenticated. The chapter paraphrases rather than inventing archival quotations; all source-study boxes state their evidence limitations.

1. **February 20, 1923: Declaration of the National Representatives Conference.** https://contents.history.go.kr/front/hm/view.do?levelId=hm_123_0115  
   - HTML anchor: `#source-conference`. Provenance: Direct inspection of the NIKH published transcription; a translated interpretive paraphrase, not a verbatim English translation. The delegates did not represent every Korean community.

2. **March 1925: Provisional Assembly resolution and charges against Rhee Syng-man.** https://contents.history.go.kr/front/hm/view.do?levelId=hm_123_0130  
   - HTML anchor: `#source-impeachment`. Provenance: Direct inspection of the published NIKH text and editorial analysis; paraphrase. These are charges by opponents, not judicially neutral proofs of motive.

3. **1926: Korean Communist Party Declaration in Bulkkot.** https://contents.history.go.kr/front/nh/view.do?levelId=nh_049_0020_0020_0030_0010  
   - HTML anchor: `#source-kcp`. Provenance: Mediated original: the chapter cites a named contemporary declaration as analyzed and partly reproduced by NIKH; the complete periodical original was not independently collated. Source notes vary between September 1 and September 10 for the issue: verify the issue date before quoting it.

4. **January 1923: Shin Chae-ho’s Declaration of the Korean Revolution.** https://contents.history.go.kr/front/hm/print.do?levelId=hm_126_0020&tabId=01&treeId=010701&whereStr=  
   - HTML anchor: `#source-declaration`. Provenance: Direct NIKH transcription consulted; nonliteral English summary. Its polemical assertions are political argument, not measured socioeconomic data.

5. **April 26, 1932: Yun Bong-gil’s Korean Patriotic Corps oath.** https://contents.history.go.kr/front/hm/view.do?levelId=hm_126_0040

6. **June 1926: Student leaflets for Sunjong’s funeral demonstrations.** https://contents.history.go.kr/front/hm/print.do?levelId=hm_124_0030&tabId=03&treeId=010701&whereStr=

7. **January 20, 1927: Announcement of Shinganhoe principles.** https://contents.history.go.kr/front/hm/view.do?levelId=hm_124_0060

## 4. Historical findings and corrections

- **1923 National Representatives Conference:** restored the conference’s multiple phases, February 20 declaration, and distinction between attendance and accredited representation. The assembly’s conclusions remain contested.
- **1925 impeachment:** placed Rhee Syng-man’s removal within an extended constitutional dispute and did not convert formal charges into independently proven misconduct.
- **1925–1928 Korean communism:** distinguished party formation, successive arrests, the party’s 1926 declaration and the 1928 Comintern intervention. Avoided depicting all communist activity as one stable party.
- **1926 June 10 and 1929 Gwangju movements:** used dated student leaflets and separated local demonstrations from cumulative national movement figures.
- **1927–1931 Shinganhoe and Kunuhoe:** compared printed programs and actual branches, and established the 1927/1929 differences in the women’s organization’s declarations.
- **Bongodong and Cheongsanri:** avoided manufacturing casualty totals, acknowledged incompatible Korean and Japanese claims, and evaluated combat outcome separately from strategic and civilian consequences.
- **Gando reprisals:** documented violence against civilians without claiming an independently established aggregate death toll.
- **Free City 1921:** explained rival Korean communist and Russian/Soviet-linked commands, and labeled the later witness account and remembered-date discrepancy.
- **Korean Independence Corps and Three Federations:** dated the corps’ organization to Mishan, Manchuria, in December 1920, and noted the 1924 documentary basis for Chamui-bu.
- **1930s Manchurian organizations:** differentiated northern/southern nationalist armies, Chinese partners and communist guerrillas, and treated Minsaengdan accusations separately from proven actions.
- **1935 parties:** distinguished the July 1935 National Revolutionary Party at Nanjing from the late-November Korean National Party at Hangzhou; interpreted the surviving party program as aspirations, not implemented policy.

## 7. Publication limitations and editorial safeguards

1. The scholarly HTML edition **does not claim** that all sixteen original historical folios or facsimiles were personally collated. The location and mediated status of each record appear in the manuscript and this register.
2. No disputed military death figure is presented as established when the original reporting series has not been compared item by item. Such a future facsimile audit would be a separate critical-edition project, not a prerequisite for publishing this source-transparent textbook narrative.
3. Three of the map studies use guided geographic interpretation and archive-grounded toponymy without supplied historical cartographic plates. Captions and questions make this clear; they must not be advertised as original period maps.
4. Two Everyday Life features use expressly hypothetical illustrations rather than fictitious eyewitness biographies or diaries.
5. This audit is for standalone HTML and scholarly textual content, not proof of deployment on an external website or of licensed reproduction rights for any visual archival materials.

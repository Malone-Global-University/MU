## 3. Essential documentary and historical corrections made

### 3.1 No Gun Ri — victims, investigations and Muccio letter (§71.7)

The earlier draft's **218** recognized victims (150 deceased, 13 missing, 55 wounded) was replaced with the official **226** total maintained by the No Gun Ri Peace Park: **150 deceased, 13 missing, 63 people with lasting injury/disability**. This is an administrative recognition total, **not** a census of everyone killed at No Gun Ri. In particular, it is wrong to read 226 as a death count. Official listing: https://www.yd21.go.kr/nogunri/html/sub04/040301.html.

The 2001 U.S. Army review is now described as acknowledging that American soldiers killed or wounded an undetermined number of civilians, while **not** establishing a definitive count or proving transmission of a specific order to the 7th Cavalry at the scene. Its treatment of reported aircraft involvement is distinguished from Korean survivors' evidence and later scholarship. For contemporary debate, see Sahr Conway-Lanz, *Diplomatic History* 29, no. 1 (2005): 49–81, https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-7709.2005.00459.x.

The July 26, 1950 **Muccio letter** was **not** described as first discovered/publicized during the late 1990s. It had been declassified earlier, appeared in later historical research, and became a prominent public issue after reporting in **May 2006**, subsequent to the 2001 inquiry. The letter reports a refugee-control policy involving warning shots and lethal fire if refugees persisted in advancing. Its existence is not independent proof that the particular soldiers involved at No Gun Ri received it. Reporting and documentary context: https://www.post-gazette.com/news/nation/2006/05/30/u-s-policy-condoned-shootings/stories/200605300135.

### 3.2 TRC Source Window I — identified case decision (§71.5)

The earlier generic description of “Busan and Sacheon investigations” is replaced with the **specific September 22, 2009 Truth and Reconciliation Commission decision** concerning the Busan–Sacheon National Guidance Alliance incident. The official account identifies South Korean police, counterintelligence and military-police personnel and specifies killings between **July and September 1950**. The investigation does not claim to identify the full local victim count. The editorial paraphrase now remains properly limited to this identified case: https://www.jinsil.go.kr/KoreanWar/27.do.

### 3.3 UNCOK Source Window III — exact archival symbol (§71.10)

The report is confirmed as **UN General Assembly A/1434, October 7, 1950** (note transmitting UNCOK's preliminary reporting on atrocities in recaptured Daejeon and Seoul). The section now links to https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/862758/files/A_1434-EN.pdf. The report contains preliminary observations and testimony; it should not be represented as an exhaustive independently verified casualty register. **Text and metadata were located; a direct web facsimile screenshot request returned an access error.** No manuscript-collated quotation from the PDF is claimed.

### 3.4 Sinchon evidence and responsibility (§71.11)

The chapter's cautious distinction between documented mass violence and contested **perpetrator attribution** is preserved and strengthened with named Korean scholarship:

- Monica Hahn, “The Gap between 'Uprising' and 'Massacre': A Review of the Sinchon Incident in Hwanghae Province,” *Ewha Historical Research* 46 (2013): 97–137, https://doi.org/10.37091/ewhist.2013..46.003.
- Daeyeol Yea, “The Invention of Anti-American Sentiment — Why North Korea Shifted the Blame for the Sinchon Massacre to the United States,” *International Journal of Korean History* 29, no. 1 (2024): 137–178, https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART003053787.

The official DPRK figure of **35,383** remains explicitly an **official claim**, not an independently established count. Research indicating major involvement by local right-wing security groups does not imply that killings were invented, and does not automatically resolve every disputed incident or question of military oversight.

### 3.5 Wartime biological-warfare allegations (§71.14)

An overly general appeal to what “most historians” believe was replaced with source-specific discussion of former Soviet documentary disclosures and the interpretations of **Milton Leitenberg** and **Jeon Ye-mok (2021)**. Their work provides strong evidence that prominent 1952 biological-warfare accusations were orchestrated/fabricated, while Jeon cautions that not every detail of the larger allegation history has been resolved. References: https://gotech.spp.umd.edu/research-impact/publications/new-russian-evidence-korean-war-biological-warfare-allegations and https://journal.kci.go.kr/imhc/archive/articleView?artiId=ART002754561.

### 3.6 ROK special comfort units (§71.15)

The section retains the historically documented subject while improving provenance: work by **Kim Gui-ok**, interviews, and the ROK Army's 1956 *Rear-Area War History (Personnel)* record army-operated special comfort facilities. The text does not infer that every woman's circumstances are known, that all were conscripted in an identical manner, or that a complete national tally has been established. Documentary reporting: https://www.khan.co.kr/article/200202241912531.

### 3.7 Abductee lists (§71.16)

The approximately 83,000-person estimate is tied to the **1952 Ministry of Public Information list of 82,959 reported abductees**, separately from later lists and any claim about a comprehensive total. National Memorial inventory: https://www.abductions625.go.kr/home/en/sub/dta01_02.html.

### 3.8 Geoje POW camp (§71.18)

The June 10, 1952 Compound 76 operation now distinguishes prisoner from American casualties: the contemporary UN Command account **S/2774**, summarized in the *UN Yearbook 1952*, gives **31 POWs killed, 139 POWs wounded; one U.S. enlisted man killed and 14 U.S. personnel wounded**. These are source-attributed contemporary reports, not later forensic reconciliation. Source: https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/860247.

### 3.9 Rhee's June 18 prisoner release (§71.19)

Source Window IV now identifies and differentiates the contemporaneous **letter to Mark W. Clark** and Rhee's **enclosed public statement**, both in *FRUS 1952–1954*, vol. XV, part 2, **Document 607**. The independent historical account of the operation is **Document 606**. The initial June 18 escape is reported as approximately **25,000**; a Joint Chiefs history gives **25,131** by that afternoon; retrospective accounts sometimes say approximately **27,000** when discussing the broader release. The chapter does not collapse these into a single exact headcount. Sources:
- https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v15p2/d606
- https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v15p2/d607
- https://www.jcs.mil/Portals/36/Documents/History/Policy/Policy_V003_P002.pdf

## 4. Other audited claims retained with qualifications

- **Geochang, February 1951:** commonly documented **719** victims; this number refers to a specific incident and should not be amalgamated with national totals. Academy of Korean Studies: https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0001927.
- **Hungnam:** the chapter retains the source-qualified naval evacuation figures, approximately 105,000 military personnel and 91,000 civilians; numbers vary across source scopes.
- **SS *Meredith Victory*:** about 14,000 civilian passengers, an approximate documented vessel-specific estimate, not a total for Hungnam.
- **Population and massacre figures:** no unqualified aggregate Korean civilian death toll, Bodo League death toll or North Korean bombing death toll is presented as conclusively known.
- **Postwar truth-seeking:** the February 2026 Truth and Reconciliation Commission relaunch is confined to retrospective context and reflection, not inserted into the chronology of fighting.

## 6. Publication qualifications

This is approval of a **first-edition scholarly textual HTML** chapter. It does not imply exhaustive examination of every original Korean-, Chinese-, Russian- or English-language file, witness statement, court record, forensic report, or archival facsimile. The four classroom source windows are **explicit editorial paraphrases**, not direct quotations based on page-by-page manuscript collation. The two map-study assignments are **textual exercises** requiring students to consult a suitable historical atlas; they do not contain embedded, independently licensed cartographic plates. Estimates of deaths, displacement, and prisoner totals remain expressly qualified where sources disagree or use different definitions.

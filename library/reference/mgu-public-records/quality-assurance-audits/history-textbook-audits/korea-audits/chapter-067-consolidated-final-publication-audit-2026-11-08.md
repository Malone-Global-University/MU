## 3. Historical and documentary corrections

1. **KPA/ROK comparison:** Corrected the initial draft's inaccurate literal claim that the ROK lacked antitank weapons. U.S. Army sources list approximately 140 antitank guns and 1,900 2.36-inch bazookas, which were inadequate against the KPA's T-34/85s. Distinguishes the absence of tanks and effective antitank defense from absence of all antitank weapons. Figures are classified by their source-defined categories.
2. **Military strengths:** Discusses the differing source estimates of KPA/constabulary and ROK divisional/support strength; differentiates personnel totals from combat units, reserves, service troops, armor, air power and logistics. Does not pretend that slightly different official counts are mutually exclusive.
3. **1949 Kaesong/Ongjin:** Names Ambassador Muccio's June 11, 1949 **airgram A–196** and describes its limited evidentiary scope. Explains the Ongjin southern-controlled sector's effective land isolation and differentiates localized frontier action from an organized national offensive.
4. **U.S. troop withdrawal:** Distinguishes June 29, 1949 combat withdrawal, July 1 creation of KMAG, ongoing economic/military advisory aid and the absence of an advance defense treaty. Corrects the front-matter chronology so NSC 8/2 is dated March 22 and presidentially approved March 23, 1949.
5. **Land reform:** Distinguishes southern Law No. 31, June 21, 1949, Law No. 108 amendment of March 10, 1950, implementing orders, earlier sales of former Japanese lands and later ownership completion; avoids equating the original law with fully implemented redistribution.
6. **Soviet decision:** Separates Kim's 1949 initiative, Stalin's reluctance, the January 30, 1950 communication about preparation, April 1950 discussions and conditional Soviet authorization; avoids unsupported exact dates for all private discussions.
7. **Chinese consultations:** Identifies Mao's request via Zhou Enlai on May 13 and Stalin's response via Soviet channels on May 14. Contrasts the earlier transfer of ethnic Korean PLA veterans with Chinese army intervention later in the Korean War.
8. **Acheson:** Describes the January 12, 1950 defense perimeter statement in context, with collective security and continuing American support. Does not treat the speech as proven sole cause of Soviet authorization.
9. **June 1950 operational endpoint:** Explains planning and warnings through June 24 without narrating the June 25 attack, the fall of Seoul or UN intervention; those belong in Chapter 68.
10. **Civilian history:** Develops guerrilla coercion, police and army suppression, movement restriction, political classification and the prewar origins of the National Guidance Alliance. Separates its prewar registration role from later wartime atrocities (Chapter 71).

## 4. Primary-source and scholarly register

The 9 original bibliography references were retained and supplemented with 15 linked documentary/historical entries (24 total). The chapter gives source-window links to:

- ROK land reform legislation and National Institute of Korean History chronology;
- U.S. Ambassador Muccio, June 11, 1949, airgram A–196, **FRUS 1949**;
- National Security Council **NSC 8/2**, March 22, 1949, **FRUS 1949**;
- Wilson Center/CWIHP publication guide to the Stalin–Mao May 13–14 diplomatic telegrams.

Additional documented source families include Korean History Database legal records, U.S. Army Center of Military History, Joint Chiefs history, official American diplomatic materials, Wilson Center Soviet archival transcriptions and major scholarly monographs. Verbatim archival quotations were not introduced; source-window passages are clearly labeled editorial paraphrases.

**Edition limitations:** Not all original Korean, Russian or Chinese archival facsimiles, unit war diaries or source translations were exhaustively collated page-by-page. Some quantitative military estimates vary between reputable official histories owing to their counting categories. The map studies are scholarly text-based exercises, requiring students to consult a dated historical atlas; there are no embedded plates. Original bibliographic monographs are identified without exhaustive chapter/page-level collation. Accordingly approval is for a **scholarly textual HTML first edition**, not a facsimile-critical documentary edition or a cartographic edition.

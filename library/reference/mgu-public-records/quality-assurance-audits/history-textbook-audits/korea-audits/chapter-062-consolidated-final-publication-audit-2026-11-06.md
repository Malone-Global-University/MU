## Substantive accuracy interventions
- Reconciled the three labor mobilization phases (1939 recruitment / 1942 official placement / September 1944 broad legal conscription) and distinguished each from the separate military enlistment sequence.
- Identified the 1938 army volunteer and 1943 naval/student programs and the April/September 1944 examinations/inductions for conscription. Defined the different denominators behind Palmer and Korean institutional figures rather than aggregating them.
- Corrected the false implication that wartime compulsion began de novo after 1937; earlier colonial coercion is recognized.
- Corrected anachronistic statements suggesting Koreans in Sakhalin lost Japanese nationality immediately in August 1945; policies, ethnicity-based exclusion, and later nationality settlement are distinguished.
- Correctly distinguished the Women’s Volunteer Labor Corps from military sexual slavery, including the overlaps of deception and vulnerability without incorrectly equating the institutions.
- Identified U.S. OWI Interrogation Report No. 49 by date, author, interrogations and location; expanded explanation of its translation, interrogators’ bias and intelligence purpose. No invented direct quotations were added.
- Replaced Primary Source Window II’s secondary-only characterization with a real identified workplace primary source: the 1944–1946 Korean tobacco-distribution rosters from Sado Mine, as analyzed by Hye-kyung Jung (2022). No invented roster entries.
- Verified Duró/Palmer 2024 article and the wording/protection-of-source corrigendum.
- Treated estimates for Korean atomic-bomb victims, the *Ukishima Maru* dead, and those left in Sakhalin as estimates referring to different record sets.
- Added differentiated post-surrender outcomes, wages, savings, missing persons, and source methodology; preserved chapter boundaries with Chapters 61, 63, 64–66 and later memory debates.

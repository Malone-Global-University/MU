## 3. Consolidated historical verification

1. **Agricultural policy chronology:** The 1920 initial rice-increase plan and the 1926–1934 revision are distinguished; the 1940 wartime effort is treated separately. The 1926 government outline explains the policy's imperial rice-supply objective and also asserts benefits to Korean cultivators; the assertion is interpreted critically.
2. **Rice outcomes:** An NIKH-reproduced historical reconstruction (Tomita and colleagues, 1984) supplies rice production, outbound shipments, shipment share, and estimated per-capita consumption for 1924, 1928, 1932, and 1936. The rows are copied with their units and attribution, not treated as village-level statistics. No source-free assertion is made that every household experienced identical consumption.
3. **Tenant legislation:** The 1932 Tenancy Conciliation Ordinance was promulgated December 10, 1932, and effective February 1, 1933. The Agricultural Land Ordinance was promulgated April 11, 1934, and operative October 20, 1934; key provisions include differentiated contract periods (generally three years, seven for some special cultivation) and no uniform maximum rent. Both the existence and limits of protective rules are explained.
4. **Industrial finance:** The 1918 bank is correctly named Chōsen Industrial Bank (Chōsen Shokusan Ginkō), not Chōsen Colonial Bank. Enterprise ownership, control, financing, and returns are distinguished.
5. **Hungnam:** The Chōsen Nitrogen Fertilizer company was established in 1927; plant commissioning and industrial operations are distinguished from corporate incorporation, as is company investment from Korean-controlled industrial ownership.
6. **Labor evidence:** The Wonsan Federation's identifiable January 1929 instructions and a dated July 2, 1936 report about women employed at Busan Chōsen Spinning are integrated. The 15–16 jeon and 30–40 jeon wage observations are explicitly workplace-specific, *not* a generalized Korean/Japanese differential.
7. **Migrations and conscription:** Korean residents in Japan in the 1930 and 1940 enumerations, the 1937 Soviet deportation (~172,000), Japanese settlers in Korea, and the 1939/1942/1944 phases of labor mobilization are described with category and estimate caveats; cumulative migration is not confused with simultaneous stock or single-time conscription counts.
8. **Chapter scope:** Chapter 58 covers economic organization and labor practice. Political/ideological organizing is reserved principally for Chapter 59; the assimilation regime for Chapter 60; fuller wartime forced-labor and total-war experience for Chapter 62. The supplementary 1921 Busan docks strike, 1923 Kantō violence, and 1931 Wanbaoshan incident are retained where economically or migrationally relevant.
9. **Historical method:** Economic growth is distinguished from welfare; aggregate output from household income; rice production from exports and retained food; nominal from real wages; owner, tenant, and laborer categories from one another. The existing illustrative model households remain visibly hypothetical.

| Year | Production (10,000 seok) | Outbound shipments (10,000 seok) | Share shipped | Estimated consumption (seok/person/year) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1924 | 1,517 | 475 | 31.3% | 0.60 |
| 1928 | 1,729 | 742 | 42.9% | 0.54 |
| 1932 | 1,587 | 758 | 47.8% | 0.41 |
| 1936 | 1,788 | 951 | 53.2% | 0.38 |

Source: NIKH, *Korean Cultural History* vol. 26, reproducing a 1984 reconstruction by Tomita Akiko and collaborators (`https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/km/view.do?levelId=km_026_0060_0020_0030_0010`). The term **outbound shipments** is more precise than imputing an individual farm's export decision, and this series should not be extended to every unreported year by interpolation. NIKH's complete historical table displays an apparent 1940 ratio typo; the present manuscript avoids that unrelated row.

### Documented example of female textile employment

Kim Sang-gi's *Chosun Joongang Ilbo* report, July 2, 1936, examined at NIKH (`https://contents.history.go.kr/front/hm/view.do?levelId=hm_132_0010`), reports women newly employed at a Busan textile concern earning about 15–16 jeon daily, versus 30–40 jeon for some experienced women. The manuscript distinguishes these specific observations from a population-wide wage comparison or a price-index-adjusted real-wage calculation.

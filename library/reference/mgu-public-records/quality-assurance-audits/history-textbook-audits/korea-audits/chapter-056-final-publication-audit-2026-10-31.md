## Consolidated historical verification and corrections

1. **Muo Declaration (§56.4, chronology):** Replaced a misleading conventional pre-March 1919 dating with a clear statement of disagreement. The National Institute of Korean History draws on Jeong Won-taek’s diary to place drafting/printing after March 1, while some archival catalogues retain February dating. Principal authorship associated with Jo So-ang. Do not infer certain pre-March circulation from the traditional *Muo* name.
2. **Taehwagwan (§56.10):** Named the four absent signatories: Gil Seon-ju, Kim Byeong-jo, Yu Yeo-dae, Jeong Chun-su. Explained that absence from restaurant was not withdrawal from movement.
3. **Aunae (§56.18):** Historical commemorative and scholarly accounts report nineteen dead and roughly thirty severely wounded; treated as source-specific data, not a universally uncontested count. April 1 event not conflated with March 1.
4. **Longjing (§56.26):** Distinguished Chinese troops' shooting from Japan's consular influence; Korean scholarly reference reports seventeen total deaths (thirteen at the scene), with some variant figures.
5. **Trials (§56.24):** Corrected assertion that all 33 national representatives were arrested/tried; Kim Byeong-jo escaped, Yang Han-muk died in custody; the State Archives holds court proceedings covering 31 of 33 signatories and other organizers; main appellate decision reached October 30, 1920.
6. **Korean National Council (§56.32):** Identified chair Mun Chang-beom, deputy Kim Cheol-hun, secretary O Chang-hwan and organizational inheritance from All-Russian Korean Central Association.
7. **April 11 cabinet (§56.35):** Listed Syngman Rhee as premier; An Chang-ho, interior; Kim Kyu-sik, foreign affairs; Yi Si-yeong, justice; Choe Jae-hyeong, finance; Yi Dong-hwi, military affairs; Mun Chang-beom, communications; Yi Dong-nyeong as assembly chair. Emphasized appointees did not necessarily take office immediately.
8. **September constitutional reorganization (§56.40):** Distinguished Yi Dong-hwi’s designation as premier from his actual assumption of office in November.
9. **September 2, 1919 (§56.41):** Specified Kang U-gyu’s bombing attempt near Saitō Makoto at Namdaemun Station.
10. **April/September constitutional texts (§56.37):** Added the verified difference in provisions on equality and abolition of capital punishment, corporal punishment and licensed prostitution. The April charter and September constitution are treated as distinct documents.
11. **Rural repression (§56.23):** Added source-grounded differentiation of twenty-three deaths at Jeam-ri and six at Goju-ri, while retaining testimony cautions.

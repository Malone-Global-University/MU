## Historical and editorial revisions

1. **Planning/debt and production:** Substantially developed the difference between production capacity, productivity, export earnings, and foreign-exchange availability; the time lag from imported equipment purchase to productive operation; and how creditor rules affect later imports. Added clearly attributed analysis of two **contemporary** U.S. intelligence studies: CIA, *North Korea: A New Direction in Economic Policy*, **EA 83-10010, January 1983**; and *North Korea: Turnaround in Foreign Debt Management and Trade Patterns*, **EA 85-10024, February 1985**. In the latter, the CIA estimated **$1.3 billion hard-currency debt at mid-1984 exchange rates**, separate from about **$1.1 billion socialist-bloc obligations**. These are bounded foreign intelligence estimates, not audited DPRK accounts. The January 1983 production target comparisons are similarly attributed to an outside assessment; the precise electricity target range is not asserted where the reproduced table is unclear.
2. **Economic mechanisms and household consequences:** Added non-invented explanatory models for the coal–power–rail feedback loop, agricultural-input allocation, food procurement, housing and consumer shortages, administrative access to goods, and the distinction between social provision and reliable supply. No constructed illustration is presented as a verified incident at a particular named factory or household; the text repeatedly cautions against transferring post-1990 famine evidence to earlier decades.
3. **Joint Venture Law:** Confirmed historical adoption **September 8, 1984**, WIPO Lex record of **Decision No. 10**. Crucially, the English WIPO consolidated text includes **1994–2011 amendments**, so the chapter no longer states uncollated original-1984 sector, tax, or remittance wording as if it came verbatim from the enactment. Added explanation of legal credibility, investor risks, and limited implementation.
4. **Militarization, labor and social institutions:** Expanded production opportunity costs and uncertainty in classified military expenditure, described 1973 Three Revolutions Team assignments as an institutional mechanism rather than an invented factory vignette, and distinguished neighborhood social-service administration from party/security surveillance. The 2012 Gause retrospective study is cited by exact verifiable title and not misrepresented as a contemporaneous 1980s document.
5. **Cultural propaganda, monuments and succession:** Added the Choi Eun-hee/Shin Sang-ok film case in material-institutional terms, the 1982 state cult with the 1982 Juche treatise, the Mount Paektu origin narrative in contrast to independent biographical accounts, and post-1980 party elite-management and generational succession structures. Kim Il Sung remains the supreme political authority through 1989; Kim Jong Il's accession is not backdated to his public elevation.
6. **Festival metrics:** The 1989 festival organizer figures of about **22,000 delegates from 177 countries** are explicitly attributed to the North Korean organizers as summarized by the Academy of Korean Studies, not described as an independently verified count. The stadium's **up-to-150,000** historical capacity claim is explicitly qualified because other assessments provide lower numbers. No disputed multibillion-dollar spending estimate is presented as audited expenditure.
7. **Security and aviation:** The KAL 858 operative is called **Kim Sung-il**, as in contemporary U.S. intelligence and diplomatic records (the Korean name 김승일 is also transliterated **Kim Seung-il** in some Korean-language reference works). Another independently detected error was corrected: KAL Flight 858's scheduled route was **Baghdad–Abu Dhabi–Bangkok–Seoul**; **Bahrain** was the place to which the operatives traveled separately after disembarking at Abu Dhabi, *not* a scheduled stop of the aircraft. The treatment of the December 1983 Burmese death sentences for the Rangoon bombing explicitly avoids conflating sentencing with execution.
8. **Humanitarian contact:** Added a contemporary reporter's description of the **September 1985** exchange and separated the **151-person delegations** from the **35** visiting southerners and **30** visiting northerners who reunited with family members. The contemporary report documents a dispute over press cameras and privacy; it is not presented as an original Red Cross protocol.
9. **Bibliography integrity:** Removed Charles K. Armstrong's *Tyranny of the Weak* as ordinary supporting scholarship in light of Columbia University's 2019 research-misconduct finding. Replaced the original unidentifiable Gause placeholder with **Ken E. Gause, *Coercion, Control, Surveillance, and Punishment: An Examination of the North Korean Police State* (HRNK, 2012)**, appropriately identified as retrospective. The source register expanded to **31** grouped entries to provide document-specific evidence, beyond the approximate 20–25 pedagogical target; the excess reflects documentary provenance and is not a missing feature or historical defect.

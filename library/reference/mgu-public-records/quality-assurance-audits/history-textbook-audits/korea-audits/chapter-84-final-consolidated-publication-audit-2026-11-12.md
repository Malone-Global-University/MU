## 4. Verified essential factual corrections

**1998 GDP and subsequent recovery.** The initial draft's older-vintage −5.8% estimate was replaced consistently with **−6.7% in 1998** from the revised IMF 2001 historical series, paired with **+10.9% in 1999** and **+8.8% in 2000**. A chapter paragraph explicitly explains that earlier IMF announcements had different estimates rather than treating the earlier number as fabricated. The original December 1997 IMF program's anticipated **positive growth around +3%** remains distinguished from realized outcomes.

- IMF monitoring, August 2001: <https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2015/09/28/04/53/pn0179>
- Korean Ministry of Finance data: <https://english.mofe.go.kr/pc/selectTbPressCenterDtl.do?boardCd=N0001&seq=827>
- Contemporary IMF 1999, older data estimate: <https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2015/09/28/04/53/pn99115>

**Foreign-bank debt exchange timing.** The January 28, 1998 creditor deal is accurately described as an **agreement in principle**. The definitive signed restructuring occurred **March 31, 1998**, with about **US$21.8 billion** of tendered obligations from **134 creditor banks in 32 countries**. The chapter distinguishes rollover, negotiation, contract execution, and government-guaranteed maturity extension. It does not claim the debts were forgiven.

- IMF chronology: <https://www.elibrary.imf.org/display/book/9781557757838/ch04.xml>
- IMF retrospective: <https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/001/2001/154/article-A001-en.xml>

**Daewoo liability scope.** The submitted unscoped approximately US$80 billion figure was replaced by the more precise **₩89.0 trillion in total liabilities across twelve principal workout affiliates, as established in an end-1999 due-diligence assessment**. The text explicitly distinguishes that subset from all affiliates, bank loans, workout claims and dollar-converted sums. Confirmed against printed page 33 / PDF image page 37, Table III-6, and its footnotes (which clarify that some affiliate accounting cutoffs were before December 1999).

- Kim, Kim and Kim, *Restructuring of the Chaebols and Financial Sector in Korea*, Table III-6: <https://www.agi.or.jp/media/publications/workingpaper/WP2000-23.pdf>
- Contemporary Korean ministry description: <https://english.mofe.go.kr/pc/selectTbPressCenterDtl.do?boardCd=N0001&seq=1119>

**Exchange-rate-band chronology.** Corrected to **November 20, 1997** (daily range ±10%) and **December 16, 1997** (daily band removed). Distinguished late-December rates near ₩2,000/US$ from annual-average won rates.

- Bank of Korea, *Exchange Rate System*: <https://www.bok.or.kr/eng/main/contents.do?menuNo=400186>

**Gold collection.** Preserved a well-supported **approximately 3.51 million participants** and **around 227 metric tonnes**. Expanded explanation that participation included sales, delayed remuneration, and donations; not every item was an uncompensated gift. The campaign is not substituted for international debt management in explaining recovery.

- Academy of Korean Studies: <https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0080908>

**IMF program distinction.** Maintained the separate ~**US$21 billion** IMF stand-by arrangement and the announced **~US$58.4 billion** multilateral/bilateral potential support package. Not all commitments were drawn; it was not a US$58.4bn cash handover. References distinguish the historical government's documentary claims from implementation.

- IMF Korea program briefing: <https://www.imf.org/external/np/exr/ib/2000/062300.htm>
- 1997 Letter of Intent, archival locator: <https://www.imf.org/external/np/loi/120397.htm>

**Labor politics.** Correctly represents the Tripartite Commission and the February 6, 1998 social pact, while qualifying political disagreement and incomplete social-insurance coverage. Does not treat all unions as united or imply mass layoffs were universally voluntary.

- Economic, Social and Labor Council record: <https://english.eslc.go.kr/agreements.do?menuNo=1>
- Brookings social safety net analysis: <https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-labor-market-policy-and-social-safety-net-in-korea-after-the-1997-crisis/>

## 5. Source method

Two primary-source windows are analytical paraphrases of the **December 3, 1997 Letter of Intent** and a **February 1998 revised program**. They are conspicuously labeled as paraphrases, not presented as fabricated direct quotations. The text distinguishes what policy memoranda can establish (government commitments and expectations) from matters requiring separate evidence (realized outcomes, workers' experiences, implementation). A selected **15-entry source register** provides links, document categories and section references. IMF and governmental sources are not treated as omniscient; historiographical debates and alternative stakeholder perspectives remain visible.

The archive page URLs for the two IMF letters are document locators and may be access-restricted for automated clients; the edited text makes no claim of facsimile collation of the documents or independent verification of every source line.

## Historical quality / factual controls

1. **Education:** 1911/1922 education ordinances provide institutional background; the Third Ordinance was promulgated March 4, 1938 and made Korean optional within the formal curriculum; the Fourth Ordinance, March 8, 1943, removed it. The separate 1941 national-school restructuring is maintained. Planned compulsory elementary schooling from 1946 was not realized under colonial rule. Evidence: NIKH `<https://contents.history.go.kr/front/hm/view.do?levelId=hm_141_0010>`.
2. **Names:** November 10, 1939 Ordinances Nos. 19 and 20 and the Feb 11–Aug 10, 1940 registration period; distinguish legally required household *shi*, default use of an existing surname when unreported, and separately permitted personal-name change. Also explain coercive practical enforcement. Evidence: NIKH `<https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/kc/view.do?code=kc_age_40&levelId=kc_i403280>`.
3. **State oath:** two authorized age-specific versions circulated from Oct 4, 1937, as reported in *Dong-A Ilbo*, Oct 5. Source: NIKH `<https://contents.history.go.kr/front/hm/view.do?levelId=hm_125_0080>`.
4. **Shrines:** distinguish Chōsen Jingū’s 1925 establishment from later coercion; the 1935 Pyongyang school disputes and September 1938 Presbyterian assembly are tied to specifically identified contemporaneous accounts, including directly inspected *Presbyterian Guardian*, December 1938, p. 229 and continuation.
5. **Press and film:** privately owned Korean dailies ceased August 10, 1940 amid pressure; government-affiliated *Maeil Sinbo* continued. The Chōsen Film Ordinance was promulgated Jan 4, 1940 and applied from Aug 1, 1940, explicitly linked to Japan's Film Law. Evidence: NIKH and official Korean law portal `<https://www.law.go.kr/lsInfoP.do?lsiSeq=67401&viewCls=lsRvsDocInfoR>`.
6. **Organizations:** National Spiritual Mobilization League formed July 7, 1938; its September 22, 1938 platform is identified; National Total Power League formed Oct 16, 1940 and is distinguished structurally from its predecessor. Evidence: NIKH and Academy of Korean Studies.
7. **Collaboration:** decisions evaluated by dated acts, authority and constraints; specific December 1942 *Sin Sidae* article by Kim Hwal-lan and November 1941 *Kokumin Bungaku* essay of Choe Chae-seo identified. Full original issue inspection for both remains incomplete and is stated honestly. Historical-period examples contrasted with coerced schoolchild and family compliance.
8. **Language Society:** distinguish the 1933 orthography resolution and the 1942–1945 criminal case, with Yi Yun-jae and Han Jing dying in custody and the dictionary materials recovered in 1945. Police allegations are not presented as independent proof of members' motives. Evidence: NIKH documentary database and historical studies.
9. **Political rights:** distinguish no Korean colonial constituency in an elected Japanese Diet from the eligible Korean residents in Japan proper after 1925, and individually appointed House of Peers from automatically enfranchised nobility. April 1945 allocation of proposed 23 Korea seats was never put to an election.
10. **Postliberation:** Chosun Ilbo resumed Nov 23, 1945; Dong-A Ilbo resumed Dec 1, 1945. Southern zone Ordinance No. 122 (Oct 23, 1946) addressed name restoration. A 2009 *Dictionary of Pro-Japanese Collaborators* includes 4,389 entries, a scholarly compilation rather than a judicial verdict. Scope of postwar political history kept appropriately limited.

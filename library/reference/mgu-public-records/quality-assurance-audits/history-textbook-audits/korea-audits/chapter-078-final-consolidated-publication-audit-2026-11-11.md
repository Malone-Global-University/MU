## 3. Historical corrections applied and source checks

### A. DMZ incidents — §78.5

**Correction:** The manuscript previously reported *more than five hundred* incidents in 1967 and *more than seven hundred* in 1968 while referring to DMZ-only incidents. These corresponded neither to a consistently defined DMZ-only series nor to a properly distinguished comprehensive series.

The corrected account identifies the declassified U.S. military study's **Table 2-1, “Incidents in Korea”** and keeps the geography of the categories separate:

| Year | DMZ incidents | Rear-area incidents | Combined incidents |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1966 | 37 | 13 | 50 |
| 1967 | 452 | 277 | 729 |
| 1968 | 542 | 219 | 761 |

This is an **American retrospective incident classification**, not an omniscient or universally accepted record. A UN contemporaneous series and other military sources count differently, as do casualty denominators and categories. The historical narrative now discloses this. Original table (declassified U.S. study, p. 323): https://www.archives.gov/files/declassification/iscap/pdf/2011-102-doc-1-part-15.pdf . The PDF's original table was independently examined, including a visual screenshot of the scanned page.

### B. North Korean military purge — §78.11

**Correction:** Ho Bong-hak was improperly called the leader of a Workers' Party department responsible for the South. The revised chapter calls him a former director of the **Korean People's Army General Political Bureau**, emphasizing the military political apparatus. The chapter does not assert that surviving outside documentation permits complete reconstruction of the decision-making and motivations of the 1968–1969 purges. Background: Ken E. Gause, *Coercion, Control, Surveillance, and Punishment*, Committee for Human Rights in North Korea, and specialist Korean military-history research, catalogued in the preceding independent audit.

### C. Tunnel-investigation casualties — §78.18

**Correction:** Replaced an indefinite claim that an explosion killed or wounded several people with the UNC's dated account: on **November 20, 1974**, an explosive device during examination of the first tunnel killed **U.S. Navy Commander Robert M. Ballinger** and **ROK Marine Major Kim Hah-chul**, and wounded **five Americans and one South Korean**. The wording attributes the casualty details to UNC records without implying that all forensic questions have been independently settled. Source: https://www.unc.mil/History/Post-1953-Evolution-of-UNC/ .

### D. Lee Seung-bok incident — §78.8

**Refinement:** Distinguished the December 1968 killings attributed to infiltrators, the newspaper's contemporary report about the boy's statement, subsequent anti-communist memorial use, and later litigation about journalistic authenticity. The edited account does not assert that the exact reported words are an independently verified transcript; nor does it assert that the story was proven fabricated. Relevant journalism and court history are described in the independent audit, including the 2009 Supreme Court outcome and earlier judicial findings.

### E. Carter-era troop reductions — §78.20

**Refinement:** Replaced the potentially misleading statement that “roughly three thousand troops” were withdrawn during 1978 without distinction. The revised prose states that the combat reduction was scaled back to **approximately 800 troops (one infantry battalion)**, while plans for the initial reductions concerned **around 3,400 personnel positions including support personnel**. This avoids confusing combat-unit strength with broader personnel numbers or planned with achieved withdrawals. Compare contemporaneous April 22, 1978 reporting and U.S. Army official historical summaries: https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1978/04/22/president-slows-us-pullout-from-korea/e42d67a3-c8ba-4ea0-8d1c-553d8f8b1/ .

### F. Competing reunification proposals — §78.16

**Refinement:** The June 1973 North Korean proposal is identified as the **Confederal Republic of Koryo**, not the later **Democratic Confederal Republic of Koryo** of 1980. The prose no longer assumes that Kim Il Sung's related public statements and Park Chung-hee's June 23 declaration were necessarily released on precisely the same date in every documentary chronology.

### G. Previously verified facts left intact

- **Armistice:** July 27, 1953; MDL and DMZ arrangements; no South Korean signature; ceasefire not a peace treaty (U.S. National Archives original agreement).
- **Blue House Raid:** January 21, 1968; approximately 31 infiltrators; documented assassination objective (FRUS vol. XXIX, pt. 1, editorial note 144).
- **USS *Pueblo*:** January 23, 1968; 83 crew, one death, 82 surviving crew returned December 23, 1968 (FRUS vol. XXIX, pt. 1, editorial note 331 and U.S. Navy).
- **ROKS *Dangpo*:** January 19, 1967; commonly recorded as 39 fatalities among 79 crew; **EC-121:** April 15, 1969, 31 fatalities.
- **1972 Joint Communiqué:** July 4; independence, peaceful unification, national unity; text available as a published official translation.
- **1974 assassination attempt:** Mun Se-gwang, First Lady Yuk Young-soo and student Jang Bong-hwa; North Korean direction is treated as a disputed attribution rather than an independently established command chain.
- **Panmunjom:** August 18, 1976 killings of Capt. Arthur Bonifas and First Lt. Mark Barrett; August 21 Operation Paul Bunyan; uncertainty as to authorization persists.

The review was **targeted**. It does not constitute exhaustive archival fact verification for every sentence or casualty number across 13,000+ words.

## 6. Limits disclosed; editorial lock

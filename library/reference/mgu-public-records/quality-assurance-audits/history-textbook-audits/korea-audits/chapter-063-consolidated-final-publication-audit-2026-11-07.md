## Factual repairs completed

1. **Free City Incident (§63.7 and review material):** Reversed the erroneous factional assignment. The **Sakhalin Volunteer Army**, associated with **Park Il-ya** and the Shanghai-oriented network, resisted the authority of the **Irkutsk-aligned Korean Revolutionary Military Council**, associated with **O Ha-muk**. On 28 June 1921 Red Army/Far Eastern Republic forces attacked or disarmed the resisting formation near Svobodny. Distinguished the initial gathering at **Mishan (Chinese Manchuria)** from **Svobodny (on the Zeya River, Amur basin)**. Kept casualty figures and political interpretation qualified. Corrected both primary narrative and conflicting review-summary wording. Cross-checked against Academy of Korean Studies and National Institute of Korean History sources.
2. **Nine Rules and army command (§§63.11–63.12):** Replaced the false statement that the 1941 Nine Rules remained operative through 1945. Documents establish a **23 August 1944 notification**, recorded in a **28 August 1944 Chinese military communication**, cancellation of the Nine Rules, and subsequent instructions confirming Provisional Government command; an additional aid arrangement was concluded in April 1945. Explained that formal command rights did not end Chinese resource dependence.
3. **Army geography (§63.12):** Clarified the establishment ceremony on **17 September 1940** at Chongqing, early headquarters relocation to **Xi'an**, and the **October 1942 headquarters return to Chongqing**, distinguished from geographically dispersed detachments.
4. **Yang Se-bong (§63.8):** Replaced the misleading phrase *captured and killed* with a Japanese-directed deception and ambush leading to a battle in which he was killed, noting the limits of surviving accounts.
5. **Hujiazhuang (§63.10):** Added a chronological warning that the **1941 engagement** belongs to Korean Volunteer Corps-associated forces before the later formal organization of the Yan'an-oriented Korean Volunteer Army.
6. **India–Burma mission (§63.18):** Distinguished the documented **nine-member detachment led by Han Ji-seong** and its propaganda/intelligence work from exaggerated claims of independent large-scale field operations.
7. **Allied strategy and recognition (§63.19):** Distinguished the public 1943 Cairo promise from private American proposals for trusteeship and wartime administration; continued to avoid treating proposals as settled, uniformly Allied decisions.

## Explicit edition limitations

1. **No exhaustive archival manuscript collation:** The corrections and documentary register are source-backed, but Korean, Russian, Chinese, British and American archival holdings were not systematically collated page by page. The documents' exact wording in each of the four source windows remains editorially paraphrased except for the short Cairo phrase.
2. **No illustrated map plates:** Both map studies are text-based spatial exercises and require a separate atlas or future authored map plates.
3. **Disputed quantitative claims:** Combat deaths, incidents of repression, and organizational strength remain qualified, not asserted as definitively reconciled totals.
4. **Model equivalence boundary:** CSS and script were compared byte-for-byte with the uploaded draft, not the unavailable separately named Chapter 1 model file.
5. **Not a universal factual warranty:** The consolidated review checked the identified publication-sensitive errors, source anchors, internal consistency and representative passage accuracy; it does not certify every minor claim through original archives.

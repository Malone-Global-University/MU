## Source-supported historical findings

1. **Education Rescript:** The contemporary Korean gazette gives the second day of the second lunar month, 1895. The corresponding Gregorian date is **February 26, 1895**, confirmed independently by Korean historical lunisolar conversion. This is not a conflict with the source date. Both §52.16 and Source Window III now label the calendar explicitly. Primary: https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/hm/view.do?levelId=hm_140_0040 ; calendar context: https://db.history.go.kr/diachronic/level.do?levelId=kn_103_0020_0020 .
2. **Chunsaengmun participant:** The intended captain is **Yi Min-goeng (李敏宏)**, whose romanization also appears as Yi Min-gweng. The Academy of Korean Studies identifies him alongside Major Yi Do-cheol. A contemporary French report reproducing a *Hanseong Sinbo* account describes Nam Man-ri and Yi Gyu-hong as coerced into leading units, an important nuance. Sources: https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0057971 ; https://db.history.go.kr/id/hk_017r_0010_0400 .
3. **Eulmi righteous-army commanders:** Yi So-eung is associated with Chuncheon, Yu In-seok with Jecheon. The existing guarded wording was retained. Sources: https://db.history.go.kr/modern/level.do?levelId=hdsr_001_0020_0050_0010 ; https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0042949 .
4. **Daewongun's marginalization:** The documentary account supports Japanese Minister Inoue's effort to exclude the Daewongun from central politics after the latter pursued his own anti-Japanese intrigues. The draft's account is substantively sound, although specific motivations require source-aware phrasing. Source: https://contents.history.go.kr/front/nh/print.do?levelId=nh_040_0040_0020_0010_0010 .
5. **Japanese civilian participation in the assassination:** The *Hanseong Sinbo*'s Japanese staff and Adachi Kenzō were involved; later reporting through the newspaper sought to attribute the crime to Korean rivals. Sources: https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0061738 ; https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART002269238 ; https://www.jacar.go.jp/english/exhibition/nichiro/18951008.htm . The specific officer name Umayabara Muhon is also attested in Korean secondary commentary, but is retained with 'reported' attribution rather than presented as uniquely corroborated.
6. **Hiroshima proceedings:** The Japanese civilian case was a preliminary inquiry, decision of January 20, 1896, rather than a full trial establishing innocence. A second set of military proceedings did not result in convictions. The main text already reflected this; two forgotten student questions still used 'acquittals' and were repaired.
7. **Palace history:** The assertion that 1894 was the first time a foreign army had controlled a royal palace in Joseon's history was overstated in view of the Imjin War and earlier wartime occupations. The replacement distinguishes the novel direct pressure on Gojong from claims of an unprecedented foreign occupation.
8. **The First Gabo Reform institutions:** The eight *amun* under Uijeongbu are supported by Academy of Korean Studies and NIKH institutional history; the seven-*bu* system followed in 1895, and the draft distinguishes the phases. Sources: https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0012439 ; https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0000925 .
9. **Legal-status reform:** The 1886 curtailment of hereditary nobi status before the 1894 formal abolition is corroborated. Source: https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0012757 .
10. **Calendar-date checks:** Korean historical lunar calculations confirm 1894 lunar 6/11 → July 13, 1894; lunar 6/25 → July 27, 1894; 1895 lunar 8/20 → October 8, 1895; lunar 10/12 → November 28, 1895; lunar 12/28 → February 11, 1896. The **Triple Intervention on April 23, 1895** is precisely documented, so an unnecessary approximate qualifier was removed from the chronology.

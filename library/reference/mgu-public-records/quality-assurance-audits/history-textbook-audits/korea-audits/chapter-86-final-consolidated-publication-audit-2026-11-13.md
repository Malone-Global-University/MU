## 4. Historical verification and corrective findings

### Treaty and 1992 declaration

North Korea's December 1985 NPT accession, safeguards agreement in force April 10, 1992, and the inter-Korean January 20, 1992 declaration (effective February 19) are distinguished. The declaration's substantive nuclear prohibitions are explained alongside its unsuccessful bilateral verification proposal. Sources: IAEA report [1], official declaration [2].

### 1993–94 inspection crisis

IAEA material-accounting discrepancies, the request for special inspections, the March 1993 NPT withdrawal notice, suspension on June 11, 1993, and the 1994 discharge of fuel without full inspection procedures are traced in their actual sequence. The legal status of the 2003 announced resumption of withdrawal is not presented as universally settled. Sources: IAEA reports [1] and [7].

### 1994 Agreed Framework

The agreement is described as a **monitored freeze of specified graphite-moderated facilities**, including larger unfinished reactors, not as total denuclearization. The light-water-reactor and heavy-fuel-oil commitments, staged safeguards, unresolved prior inventory and the subsequent role of KEDO are separated. Sources: official Agreed Framework [3], contemporary GAO oversight [4].

### October 2002 uranium dispute

The text distinguishes the **U.S. State Department's statement** that North Korean officials acknowledged enrichment from later North Korean denial and from subsequent direct observations in 2010. The 2010 facility does not retroactively validate every claim about the words spoken in October 2002. Sources: contemporaneous State Department statement [6], Hecker [16].

### Six-Party Talks and financial measures

The 2005 political commitments are not misrepresented as an implemented disarmament agreement. The 2005 U.S. Treasury action against Banco Delta Asia is identified as a **separate American regulatory action**, not a Security Council sanction. The approximately $25 million funds controversy in 2007 is distinguished from continuation of the bank rule. Sources: official joint statement [8], Treasury documents [9–10].

### 2006 nuclear test

The chapter reports monitoring of the October 9 event and avoids asserting that a small estimated yield proves technical failure or a reliable deliverable stockpile. Observed seismic/radionuclide evidence is distinguished from estimates and military inferences. Sources: CTBTO [11], CRS [17].

### UN sanctions

Resolution 1718 is described in its **October 2006 scope**, including specified trade prohibitions, targeted asset freezes, travel restrictions, and international cooperation measures; it is not falsely described as a universal embargo. Resolution 1874 distinguishes inspection within territorial jurisdiction from **high-seas inspection requiring flag-state consent** and related port-direction/reporting mechanisms. Sources: official UN resolutions [12, 15].

### 2007–10 developments

The IAEA July 17, 2007 verification of Yongbyon shutdown is correctly distinguished from a national comprehensive safeguards return. The 2008 declaration and tower demolition are identified as partial, observable progress without complete material accounting. The April 2009 expulsion of inspectors precedes the May second test. The November 2010 observation of approximately 2,000 centrifuges is attributed to Hecker and framed as a controlled visit rather than an IAEA inspection. Sources: IAEA [7,14], CTBTO [18], Hecker [16].

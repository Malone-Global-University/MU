## I. Scope, outline, and textbook coverage

Chapter 114 preserves all **20 planned main sections** in the approved **five four-section divisions**. Its governing historical argument remains the distinction between inherent Indigenous sovereignty and federal self-determination policy. The main chronological treatment runs from the failures of termination in the 1960s through the 1994 permanent Interior self-governance framework, with limited later legal context clearly identified. Its coverage remains distinct from Chapter 104 (termination), Chapter 113 (Fish Wars), Chapter 115 (Alaska Native land claims), Chapter 118 (tribal courts), Chapter 124 (later educational developments), and Chapter 131 (child welfare).

The final text includes the chapter opening, 12 learning objectives, 9 guiding questions, historical context, chronology preview, major people and institutions, core vocabulary, chapter summary, full review apparatus, and an integrated source list. Three historical vocabulary features and three primary-source windows are retained along with causation, continuity/change, timeline, geography, everyday life, multiple perspectives, and a historical debate. The chapter contains **9,642 direct main-section narrative words** (excluding insets) and approximately **19,708 total rendered words**, including teaching and review material. Depth is appropriate for the assigned material; additions were restricted to historically relevant discussion of tribal administrative accountability, natural-resource governance, and the chapter's synthesis.

## II. Essential scholarly corrections completed

1. **§114.5 — Original 1975 law:** Corrected the declaration-of-policy source window to a verified short excerpt of the **original section 3(b) of Public Law 93-638**. The source now explains that Congress rewrote this subsection in **1988**, so language from the present-day codification must not be attributed verbatim to 1975. Original: 88 Stat. 2203; amendment notes: [Office of Law Revision Counsel, 25 U.S.C. §5302](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-136/t25/s5302/).
2. **§§114.5, 114.8, 114.18 — Contract support costs:** Correctly distinguishes administrative overhead disputes that arose under the original 1975 contracting regime from the **express statutory contract-support-cost requirement added in the 1988 amendments**. Later Supreme Court litigation remains correctly marked as retrospective context. [CRS, *Tribal Self-Determination Authorities*, R48256](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R48256.html).
3. **§§114.11 and 114.18 — Indian Health Service chronology:** Distinguishes Title I IHS contracting after 1975, **IHS self-governance demonstration authority in 1992 (Public Law 102-573)**, and the **permanent IHS program in 2000 (Public Law 106-260)**. The 1994 statute permanently established the Interior program, not the IHS program. [Indian Health Service, Office of Tribal Self-Governance](https://www.ihs.gov/SelfGovernance/aboutus/).
4. **Chronology, §114.18, timeline study, and summary — First compacts:** Replaced the unsupported claim that the first compacts were negotiated in 1991 with the **Quinault agreement dated June 27, 1990**, while distinguishing agreement from later funding and implementation in 1991. [Quinault Indian Nation compact](https://tribalselfgov.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Quinault-COmpact.pdf); [Department of the Interior history](https://www.doi.gov/ocl/tribal-self-governance).

## 2. Historical corrections integrated into the chapter

1. **Agreement-in-principle (§116.11, chronology and summary):** Explicitly states the **April 30, 1990 signing at Igloolik**, rather than describing an agreement concluded in 1989 and formalized sometime in 1990. Negotiation during 1989 remains part of the historical setting; formal signature is a separate event.
2. **Settlement compensation (§116.13, summary):** Clearly separates the **C$580 million principal stated in first-quarter 1989 dollars** from later interest-adjusted payments totaling **approximately C$1.148 billion over fourteen years**, and from royalty-sharing and implementation arrangements.
3. **Nunavut Act commencement (§116.17, original statute source window, summary and references):** Corrects the mistaken assertion that the **Nunavut Act** took effect July 9, 1993. Its provisions commenced in stages, with Part III on **December 10, 1993** and the main territorial establishment on **April 1, 1999**. **July 9, 1993** properly refers to the coming into force of the **Nunavut Land Claims Agreement**.
4. **Constitutionally protected rights versus public government (§§116.11, 116.17):** Adds the explicit distinction in **Nunavut Agreement Article 4.1.3**: neither the political accord nor the related territorial legislation was intended to constitute land-claims agreement or treaty rights within **section 35**. Treaty rights and the separate territorial government's delegated public powers are not interchangeable.
5. **Original 1976 proposal (§116.6):** Notes that its initial geographical conception extended into western Arctic areas connected with the Inuvialuit/Beaufort Sea, and that subsequent regional strategies and boundary negotiations changed the proposed territory before 1999.
6. **Historical geography (§116.13):** Elaborates the distinction among the Nunavut Settlement Area, Inuit-owned parcels and relevant marine rights, Crown lands, and the public territory. Retains the transparently rounded figure **approximately 356,000 km²** and the approximate **38,000 km²** subsurface entitlement, rather than artificially treating differently rounded official figures as identical.

## 3. Scholarly narrative expansion

A single comprehensive content pass deepened fifteen sections, particularly those in which the historical mechanisms had previously been compressed:

- **§§116.5–116.6:** Inuit mapping as political evidence; translation and limitations of maps; changing territorial footprint and the two-track strategy of treaty plus public government.
- **§§116.8–116.12:** Regional representation and negotiating mandates; the distinct meaning of plebiscites; Inuit, Dene and Métis boundary interests; the 1990 agreement-in-principle; treaty drafting and ratification; the different 1993 commencement dates.
- **§§116.13–116.16:** Parcel-specific land title and minerals; financial transfers; conservation and wildlife-management board powers; separate environmental institutions; Inuit employment and procurement obligations.
- **§§116.17–116.20:** The legal layering of section 35 treaty rights, the political accord and delegated territorial government; six years of administrative preparation; decentralized services; implications of the 1999 election and Nunavut's creation.

Expansions advance historical explanation and student comprehension; no new numbered sections, unrelated contemporary material, or repetitive padding were added.

## 4. Primary source and instructional apparatus

The three original source windows were retained and improved:

- **1976 Inuit Land Use and Occupancy Project:** named **Volume One: Land Use and Occupancy**, **Volume Two: Supporting Studies**, and **Volume Three: Land Use Atlas**, with a direct government publication record.
- **1993 Nunavut Agreement:** identifies **Articles 4, 5, 19, 23, and 24**, and specifically explains 4.1.3.
- **1993 Nunavut Act:** identifies **sections 3, 13 and 79**, corrects commencement, and warns against retrojecting later amendments to the 1993 enactment.

These windows present **attributed analytical paraphrases**, not purported verbatim quotations. A new **Documentary Sources and Further Reading** section contains twelve authentic links to governmental and Inuit institutional sources. The opening, objectives, guiding questions, chronology, vocabulary, historical debate, perspectives, cause-and-effect, continuity/change, summary, and review questions remain in place.

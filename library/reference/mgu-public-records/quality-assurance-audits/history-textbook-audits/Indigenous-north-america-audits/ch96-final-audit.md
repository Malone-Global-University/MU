## Historical and documentary verification and corrections

1. **1930 Census:** *The Indian Population of the United States and Alaska*, 1937 report of the 1930 census. Chapter 1, Table 2, printed p. 3: **332,397** U.S. continental American Indians, not including Alaska; Table 6, printed p. 8: **32,816 urban (9.9%), 188,946 rural-farm (56.8%), 110,635 rural-nonfarm (33.3%)**. These describe historical census classifications rather than Indigenous citizenship or full continental Indigenous population. Primary PDF: https://www2.census.gov/library/publications/decennial/1930/population-indians/1930sr-indians-ch01.pdf
2. **Canadian population:** 122,911 is a published *1931 census classification* in an official Dominion Bureau of Statistics retrospective. It is not identical to the Indian Affairs departmental population or necessarily inclusive of Métis and Inuit, and is not improperly merged with U.S. totals. Retrospective: https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2017/statcan/11-203/CS11-203-1939-eng.pdf
3. **Land base comparison:** approximately 138 million acres in 1887 and 48 million acres in **1934**, BIA historical endpoints, not a direct 1930 measurement. https://www.bia.gov/bia/history/IndianLawPolicy
4. **Canadian schooling:** Corrected 1920 Indian Act context to compulsory **school attendance**, with day, industrial, and boarding institutions distinguished, rather than a claim that every First Nations child had to attend residential school. Statutory historical review: https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2017/aanc-inac/R32-313-1978-eng.pdf (historical discussion, printed p. 115).
5. **1932 Canadian administration:** Harold W. McGill’s October 13 succession to Duncan Campbell Scott is specified. Earlier claims concerning 1923 Navajo council, 1928 *Porter v. Hall*, Alaska Native Brotherhood/Sisterhood (1912/1915), and Métis organizing were retained only within verified historical scope.
6. **United States 1932 reporting:** The independently inspected *Board of Indian Commissioners* report, printed pp. 1–3 and 9–11, documents Depression relief and administrative interpretations of schooling and work requirements. Explicitly distinguish the **Board** from the Commissioner’s separate 1932 annual report. Facsimile: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-I20_5_932-67e5c4c3b74d9f831d9bc1180bb5cbf1/pdf/GOVPUB-I20_5_932-67e5c4c3b74d9f831d9bc1180bb5cbf1.pdf
7. **Meriam 1928 report:** Chapter VIII, printed pp. 189–192 on staffing, prevention, records, overcrowded boarding schools and nutrition; Chapter XI, printed pp. 548–549 on family/community relations and women’s activities. Both now analyzed with the investigators’ limitations explicitly stated. https://narf.org/nill/documents/merriam/k_meriam_chapter8_part1_health.pdf ; https://narf.org/nill/documents/merriam/r_meriam_chapter11_part1_family.pdf
8. **Canada 1931 departmental report:** Corrected precise date to fiscal year ended **March 31, 1931**, report published in Ottawa in 1932. Its opening report acknowledges Depression-era unemployment and prices but asserts a comparatively moderated impact in reserve communities; the chapter identifies that as an **official claim**, not established fact across all Canada. https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2017/aanc-inac/R1-90-1931-eng.pdf
9. **William L. Paul letter:** Grand Camp of Alaska Native Brotherhood, **October 1, 1929**, printed *Congressional Record*, House, **January 7, 1930, p. 1199**. Reviewed from the printed congressional passage; he contests fisheries administration, loss of customary access, and commercial fishing inequality. Allegations and magnitudes remain attributed to Paul rather than adopted as independently verified fact. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1930-pt2-v72/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1930-pt2-v72-2.pdf
10. **1933 administration:** Differentiates federal emergency conservation authorization, the **May 22, 1933** Indian Emergency Conservation Work administrative division, and the later 1934 Indian Reorganization Act. https://www.archives.gov/research/native-americans/bia/civilian-conservation-corps-indian-division

## Limits honestly disclosed

- Full page-by-page collation of the 1932 **Commissioner** annual report was not performed; the case-specific 1932 analysis uses the separately identified and inspected **Board** publication. Institutional authorship is explicit.
- The five source windows are **editorial documentary analyses and paraphrases**, not facsimile transcriptions of all original archival material. Descriptions are linked to identifiable documentary passages with print-page, table, or named-section anchors.
- Local-level and individual-level evidence cannot be inferred from national government statistics. This volume does not independently establish modern or historic membership in a particular nation for every enumerated person.
- Geography studies are guided text/map exercises, not commissioned cartographic plates; the everyday-life composite is not a named documentary family.
- Live MGU domain deployment, remote URL availability from a user browser, absolute byte-exact parity with the original Chapter 1 template, and complete archival manuscript collation were **not independently verified**.

## Historical verification and editorial corrections

1. **Choctaw, 1919:** Corrected an erroneous claim that “little gun shoot fast” and “grains of corn” were only later commemorative expressions. They are recorded in the 23 January 1919 Army memorandum, linked to National Archives DocsTeach.
2. **Original Navajo twenty-nine:** Clarified Carl Gorman's documented participation among the initial twenty-nine. The chapter recognizes the Diné recruits' intellectual role in designing the working vocabulary.
3. **Hopi:** Distinguished ten U.S. Army-documented code talkers (eight associated with the 81st Infantry Division and two with Army Air Forces service) from accounts listing approximately eleven; did not conflate roster categories.
4. **2001 honors:** Distinguished five then-living original Navajo recruits from the four men who attended the ceremony; named the four and Joe Palmer, represented by his son.
5. **Reagan's proclamation:** Distinguished the July 28, 1982, issuance of Proclamation 4954 from its August 14 observance.
6. **Charles Chibitty:** Specified the November 30, 1999, Knowlton Award recognition.
7. **Navajo Code Talkers Association:** Replaced approximate “early 1970s” founding language with **1971**.
8. **Iwo Jima evidence:** Recast the Howard Connor assessment as an attributed account found in subsequent institutional narratives, not an identified stand-alone contemporaneous memorandum. Linked separate National Archives operational-record research. No individual transmitted-message log or verified verbatim Connor report is claimed.
9. **Narrative expansion:** Strengthened Guadalcanal chronology, the comparative conditions of Pacific operations, Comanche Normandy service, Meskwaki history and archival limits, Hopi recognition counts, differences among language programs, postwar classification, and the gap between commemoration and community-directed language revitalization.

Principal source families: National Archives, National Museum of the American Indian, Library of Congress, U.S. Army, Government Publishing Office, and Oklahoma Historical Society. Citations and archive access are supplied in the HTML; the audit does not claim exhaustive source-by-source collation of every narrative assertion.

## 1. Governing materials and scope

The authoritative chapter outline assigns Chapter 135 the management of salmon, bison, caribou, fisheries, wildlife, and habitat. The Universal Master Instructions govern historical method, instructional completeness, source accuracy, and preservation of the supplied HTML design. The approved chapter plan specifies five divisions, twenty main historical sections, an integrated feature for each section, three principal comparative species, continental case studies, and full introductory and concluding apparatus. The preceding independent audit identified inadequate narrative depth and specific factual, legal, bibliographical, and navigation corrections.

The consolidated revision was made directly to the uploaded manuscript. Existing section headings, section IDs, instructional features, core design, and review apparatus were preserved. New paragraphs expand the original historical argument rather than replace the work with a new chapter. The revision uses Chapter 134's mobile contents approach as the immediate design precedent. The original Chapter 1 HTML model was not supplied for independent comparison.

## 4. Verified substantive corrections and evidence

### Pacific Northwest fisheries and Klamath

- CRITFC's fisheries timeline documents the 1968 litigation and Judge Belloni's 1969 decision. The text now distinguishes this Columbia River history from the Boldt framework in western Washington. See [CRITFC, Fisheries Timeline](https://critfc.org/about-us/fisheries-timeline/).
- CRITFC's 2024 fishery closure FAQ provides a specific example of the United States v. Oregon Technical Advisory Committee estimating the run using counts and downstream impacts. It distinguishes the commission's technical/coordination function from the regulatory authority of the participating governments. See [CRITFC, 2024 Spring Fishery Closure FAQ](https://critfc.org/fishery-announcement/2024-spring-fisher-closure-faq/).
- The project operator confirms removal work began in 2023 and ended in 2024. Copco No. 2 preceded the other three removals. The text now distinguishes 2023 physical work, January 2024 drawdown, 2024 completion, and continuing ecological recovery. The precise day of physical removal of Copco No. 2 is not asserted because project summaries use differing milestones. See [KRRC completion announcement](https://klamathrenewal.org/klamath-dam-removal-comes-to-a-close/) and [KRRC project overview](https://klamathrenewal.org/the-project/).
- The existing discussion of the 1999 Marshall decisions is retained, with explanatory distinctions concerning collective rights, geography, justified regulation, ordinary commercial licences, and treaty implementation. The chapter includes the DFO overview as a further reference. No generalized claim of a present unrestricted entitlement is made.

### Buffalo restoration

- ITBC's own institutional history identifies the February 1991 organizing meeting with nineteen tribes and the 2009 federal charter approval. The text distinguishes that meeting from establishment in 1992. The ITBC homepage displayed 86 member tribes when consulted; the figure is explicitly dated, not treated as permanent. See [ITBC history](https://www.itbcbuffalonation.org/itbchistory) and [ITBC homepage](https://www.itbcbuffalonation.org/).
- The Buffalo Treaty's history identifies the September 24, 2014 inaugural signing at the Blackfeet Reservation and the eight founding signatory entities. The text identifies them and distinguishes the treaty relationship from ITBC's operating organization. See [Buffalo Treaty history](https://www.buffalotreaty.com/buffalo-treaty-history-and-origins).
- Stroupe et al., *Scientific Reports* (2022), detected cattle introgression in all sampled bison genomes. The text distinguishes earlier marker-based tests from whole-genome results, and restricts the claim to the sampled genomes rather than asserting that every individual animal has been tested. See [DOI 10.1038/s41598-022-09828-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-09828-z).

### Caribou and transboundary management

- The Vuntut Gwitchin First Nation's release reports 218,000 Porcupine caribou in 2017 and approximately 143,000 in the July 2025 survey, announced in January 2026. See [Vuntut Gwitchin First Nation news releases](https://www.vgfn.ca/news).
- The Government of Northwest Territories reports 3,609 adult Bathurst caribou in 2025 compared with 6,851 adults in 2022. See [2025 survey results](https://www.gov.nt.ca/en/newsroom/2025-population-survey-results-released-bathurst-and-bluenose-east-barren-ground-caribou) and [survey methodology](https://www.gov.nt.ca/ecc/en/services/barren-ground-caribou/population-surveys).
- Nunatsiavut Government reports an estimated 8,600 George River caribou in July 2024, some recent adult population growth, and continued vulnerability and hunting prohibition at the time of its report. See [George River Caribou Herd remains vulnerable](https://nunatsiavut.com/george-river-caribou-herd-remains-vulnerable/).
- The three estimates are not interchangeable counts; dates, age classes, population definitions, and uncertainty are explained. No claim is made that these estimates will remain current indefinitely or that one causal factor explains every decline.
- The authentic bilateral Porcupine agreement (Ottawa, July 17, 1987; CTS 1987 No. 31) remains the primary-source exercise. The official text confirms the domestic 1985 agreement reference, additional eligible Canadian users under E2(e), international advisory arrangements, and the nonbinding nature of the board's recommendations. The original short quotation and six concluding source questions remain appropriate. See [Government of Canada treaty text](https://www.treaty-accord.gc.ca/text-texte.aspx?id=100687).

### Nunavut and Alaska

- Article 5.2.33 identifies NWMB as the principal wildlife management institution and regulator of wildlife access in the settlement area, while government retains ultimate responsibility. Article 5.3 provides decision-specific ministerial procedures and limitations. The text no longer describes the board as a simple delegated agency or equates all decisions with advisory recommendations. See [Nunavut Agreement, Article 5](https://tunngavik.com/nunavut-agreement/article-5) and [NWMB responsibilities](https://nwmb.com/en/about-nwmb/responsibilities).
- KRITFC confirms establishment in 2015, with its federal memorandum adopted in 2016. The erroneous 2016 founding date is corrected. See [KRITFC mission and history](https://www.kuskosalmon.org/mission-history).

### Great Lakes and Red Lake

- GLIFWC confirms its 1984 formation and eleven member tribes. See [GLIFWC institutional history](https://glifwc.org/index.php/about-us).
- Michigan's consent decree archive and CORA's own regulatory history establish the distinction between the predecessor authority's 1982 rules, the 1985 consent decree, and the 2000 successor charter and decree. The erroneous 1985 CORA creation date was corrected in both the narrative and the regional feature. The 2023 decree is noted as a subsequent development. See [Michigan decree archive](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/managing-resources/fisheries/tribal/2000), [CORA regulations](https://www.1836cora.org/Fishing-Regulations.php), and [U.S. Department of Justice account](https://www.justice.gov/opa/blog/treaty-fishing-rights-tribal-members-michigan-affirmed).
- Red Lake's 1997 voluntary commercial suspension, April 1999 recovery agreement, broader closures, stocking and monitoring, and 2006 regulated reopening are distinguished. The example develops Indigenous-initiated conservation and cooperation between jurisdictions. See [Red Lake Nation DNR fisheries](https://www.redlakednr.org/fisheries/) and [2019 renewed management agreement account](https://www.redlakenationnews.com/story/2019/12/06/news/upperlower-red-lake-fishery-management-agreement-renewed/85245.html).

### Mexico and the Colorado River Delta

- Mexico's National Human Rights Commission Recommendation 8/2002 was inspected in the Spanish original, including its legal background, findings, and recommendations; a page image of the legal-background portion was also inspected. The document identifies publication of the biosphere reserve decree on June 10, 1993, and notice of completion of the management program on July 2, 1996. Recommendation 1 calls for revising the management program to account for Cucapá needs, and Recommendation 4 calls for permits within the core zone subject to fisheries science and revised management provisions. These are paraphrased and expressly distinguished from a judgment, new statute, or unrestricted permission. The chapter does not generalize the 2002 permit conditions to current fisheries. See [CNDH Recommendation 8/2002](https://www.cndh.org.mx/sites/default/files/doc/recomendaciones/2002/rec_2002_008.pdf).
- Minute 319's 2014 environmental pulse flow and Minute 323's cooperative term ending December 31, 2026 are distinguished. Environmental flow provisions are not equated with an unrestricted local fishing right. See [IBWC minutes by project](https://www.ibwc.gov/minutes-by-project/).

## 7. Remaining limitations and editorial decisions

1. The revision targeted the preceding audit's identified errors and weak sections. It was not an exhaustive independent verification of every inherited historical or ethnographic sentence. The listed primary and institutional sources substantiate the principal corrected claims, and the remaining general narrative should be understood in that context.
2. Several source URLs inherited from the approved source foundation were converted to links but were not independently re-retrieved during this specific revision. The new reference list separates documentary and institutional evidence from further reading through descriptive labels and section references.
3. Caribou figures and ITBC membership are dated snapshots. The chapter does not claim to establish the latest available estimate for every herd, current permit conditions in Mexico, or future ecological outcomes. Legal implementation is jurisdiction-specific and may evolve after publication.
4. Exact Chapter 1 shell equivalence could not be certified because that model was unavailable. The uploaded Chapter 135 shell was preserved, with bounded CSS and navigation improvements based on finalized Chapter 134. The live MGU environment was not tested.
5. Narrative expansion was prioritized in §§135.5–11 and §§135.13–20, particularly regional cases and contemporary institutions; the original early sections and authentic treaty exercise were preserved with targeted changes. The final total word count is slightly beyond the recommendation, not a substantive failure under the master instructions.

## 8. Final editorial disposition

The chapter meets its assigned purpose and preserves the approved twenty-section architecture. It now reaches the intended historical narrative depth, includes substantially improved traceable documentation, resolves the principal identified factual and legal deficiencies, and has passed the stated structural and browser checks. The editorial recommendation is **publication-ready and recommended for lock**, with the explicit limitations above. No further discretionary expansion or repeated micro-revision is recommended.

## 4. Substantive factual and legal corrections

### 4.1 United States mineral administration

- The correct Supreme Court caption is *United States v. Navajo Nation*, 537 U.S. 488 (2003), rather than the reversed case name in the draft. The decision is presented as addressing the legal foundation of a damages claim, not abolishing the general trust relationship.
- The revised account distinguishes ownership, contractual arrangements, taxes, environmental authority and receipt of benefits.
- The Northern Cheyenne discussion now differentiates the leases and permits of 1969–1971, the 1974 challenge, and the October 9, 1980 congressional authorization of cancellation arrangements. It does not assert independently verified dates for the completion of every cancellation.

### 4.2 Uranium extraction, exposure, and remediation

- Retained the EPA figures of nearly 30 million tons of ore extracted from Navajo lands in 1944–1986 and more than 500 identified abandoned uranium mines. These are agency estimates and inventories, not measurements of exposure for each household.
- Clarified the AEC's domestic procurement monopoly through 1966.
- Distinguished the July 16, 1979 Church Rock tailings breach from mine operation and reactor accidents. The approximately 94-million-gallon estimate is retained as an approximate figure; the account now distinguishes the liquid entering the river from the approximately 1,100 tons of released solids, much of which was intercepted.
- Differentiated cleanup financing from compensation, site assessments, completed remediation and individual health causation. The 2014 Tronox settlement and EPA's 2015 recovery of approximately $1 billion for work at about 50 mines are distinguished; the 2017 settlement for approximately $600 million and 94 mines is also included. These figures do not imply that all mines have been remediated.
- Updated RECA: original legislation in 1990, expansion in 2000, expiration in 2024, reauthorization and expansion under Public Law 119-21 on July 4, 2025. The Department of Justice confirms the covered uranium-work period of January 1, 1942–December 31, 1990 and the December 31, 2027 claim-filing deadline. Eligibility is conditional on all statutory requirements. The filing deadline is not represented as the end date of every administrative or payment activity.
- Expanded the methodological distinction between exposure pathways, population-level epidemiology, statutory compensation eligibility and individual medical causation.

### 4.3 Coal and Osage mineral ownership

- Distinguished Black Mesa and Kayenta production cessation (2005 and 2019 respectively) from the closure of generating stations, the termination of leases, reclamation and monitoring.
- Clarified that Osage headrights are entitlements to distributions from the collective estate, not individually titled physical mineral deposits. Payments to headright holders are also distinct from revenue available to an Indigenous government for public expenditure.

### 4.4 Canadian resource development and health evidence

- Specified the East Tank Farm transaction as a C$503 million combined 49 percent investment by Fort McKay and Mikisew Cree First Nations, with 51 percent retained by Suncor. The investment is not represented as a transfer of petroleum ownership or regulatory authority.
- Distinguished the 2009 Alberta Cancer Board investigation from the 2014 Alberta Health Services follow-up regarding Fort Chipewyan. The text avoids inferring industrial causation from observed disease incidence and explains uncertainty in small populations.
- Corrected Crandon: Forest County Potawatomi Community and Sokaogon Chippewa Community jointly acquired the proposed mine property in 2003. The draft's 1998 date and single-nation attribution were incorrect.
- Distinguished the Innu Nation and Labrador Inuit Association's 2002 agreements, environmental management arrangements, government development agreements and later land-claims governance. The historical Labrador Inuit Association is not retroactively renamed the Nunatsiavut Government.
- Added the December 6, 2024 direct indictment of Imperial Metals Corporation, Mount Polley Mining Corporation and Wood Canada Limited involving 15 Fisheries Act charges. The charges are allegations, not findings of guilt. Added the November 2026 company-reported procedural status concerning vacated January 2027 trial dates and Crown disclosure. Corporate reporting is identified as such. Provincial regulatory history, environmental remediation, engineering findings and federal prosecution remain distinct.

### 4.5 Mexico and Greenland

- Replaced dependence on secondary reporting for the central Tecoltemi case with links to the official Mexican Supreme Court announcement and signed judgment in Amparo en Revisión 134/2021. The February 16, 2022 First Chamber decision set aside the challenged concession titles and recognized prior consultation and free and informed consent in the applicable framework. It did not invalidate the whole Mining Law or establish an unrestricted nationwide veto.
- Clarified the distinction between ejido membership and Indigenous collective rights.
- Distinguished Greenland's 2009 Self-Government Act from the assumption of mineral-resource responsibilities on January 1, 2010.
- Qualified the December 2021 uranium legislation: other minerals may be explored or exploited subject to applicable rules where the average uranium content of the total resource falls below the specified 100 ppm threshold by weight.
- Updated the Kuannersuit/Kvanefjeld account to reflect the developer's November 2026 reporting of halted development and ongoing litigation, preserving related claims. No arbitration award or mine authorization is asserted.

### 4.6 Critical-mineral cases

- Thacker Pass is under construction, not an operating mine. The November 2026 developer update is identified as projecting mechanical completion in late 2027 rather than assuring that outcome.
- Oak Flat: the account distinguishes the March 2026 conveyance from the March 16 Forest Service Record of Decision, the September 29 rehearing denial and continued litigation. It does not represent a land exchange or environmental decision as commercial production. The exact day of conveyance was not independently established in this final pass; the month is retained rather than supplying an unverified day.
- The historical comparison does not equate low-carbon technology with harmless extraction or presume that all Indigenous nations have the same position on proposed projects.

## 5. Primary-source integrity

Both original exercises are retained, clearly identified as paraphrases rather than invented verbatim quotations. Direct document links were inserted inside each feature, not merely in the bibliography.

1. Navajo Nation Council Resolution CAP-18-05 (April 19, 2005; presidential approval April 29). The preceding independent audit verified the recorded vote of 63–19 and the operative prohibition and findings. The conflicting press count was not substituted for the legislative record. The exercise distinguishes the authentic law's stated authority from jurisdictional and implementation disputes.
2. United Nations General Assembly Resolution A/RES/61/295, Article 32 (2007). The exercise distinguishes development priorities, consultation and cooperation directed toward obtaining consent before approval, and redress and mitigation. It does not present the declaration as a treaty or as establishing identical domestic law in every jurisdiction.

## 9. Remaining limitations and editorial disposition

- The original Chapter 1 model was unavailable for exact comparison.
- This revision incorporated the preceding independent audit's verification findings and performed targeted further checks. It was not an exhaustive sentence-by-sentence independent source verification of every inherited or newly expanded claim.
- The 2014 Fort Chipewyan follow-up and November 2026 Mount Polley corporate procedural report were addressed using the preceding audit's findings; the bibliography includes the 2009 health release and a corporate reporting landing page, rather than claiming a fresh retrieval of each original report in this pass.
- The precise day of the Oak Flat conveyance was not independently established. The month is retained and the separately reported Record of Decision date identified. Legal proceedings, project schedules, uranium cleanup and RECA eligibility remain time-sensitive.
- Corporate disclosures concerning construction and litigation are attributed to their issuers. They are not independent judicial or regulatory findings.
- Not every inherited or added external URL was individually tested for current accessibility. No live MGU deployment was performed.
- Actual maps are to be supplied by the user in the established textbook workflow.

The principal audit corrections have been applied; the historical narrative has been expanded substantially; and the complete twenty-section, twenty-two-feature instructional manuscript is preserved. The final chapter is suitable for editorial publication and recommended for lock, with ordinary future maintenance of dated contemporary developments and links. No additional discretionary expansion or repeated micro-revision cycle is recommended.

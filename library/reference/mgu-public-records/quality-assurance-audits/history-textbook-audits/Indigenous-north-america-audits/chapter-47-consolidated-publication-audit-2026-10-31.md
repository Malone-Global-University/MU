## Historical source verification

**Confirmed or acceptably qualified:**

- **1609:** Champlain encountered his opponents on July 29 and fought on July 30; this chronology is compatible with his own 1613 narrative, not proof that his actions alone caused the ensuing century of conflict. Primary source: https://minnstate.pressbooks.pub/ushistory1/chapter/lake-champlain-1609/ .
- **1642–1646:** Isaac Jogues was captured in August 1642; escaped via Dutch help in 1643; returned to the French colony in 1644; was killed October 18, 1646. Dictionary of Canadian Biography: https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/jogues_isaac_1E.html .
- **1648–1649:** Antoine Daniel was killed in the July 1648 attack on Saint-Joseph; Saint-Ignace/Taenhatentaron and Saint-Louis fell March 16, 1649, with Brébeuf and Lalemant seized at Saint-Louis and taken to Saint-Ignace. Parks Canada: https://www.pc.gc.ca/apps/dfhd/page_nhs_eng.aspx?id=562 ; archival/archaeological qualifications retained.
- **Long Sault, 1660:** Approximately 17 French and 40 Wendat, plus Algonquin participants, are supported in the contemporary record; military objective and impact on Montreal are historically contested. DCB https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/dollard_des_ormeaux_adam_1F.html .
- **La Barre–Otreouti, September 1684:** Meeting at La Famine on September 5; Otreouti an Onondaga orator, named Grangula in Lahontan; attributed oratory must not be treated as verbatim transcription. DCB https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio.php?id_nbr=495 .
- **1687 and 1688:** Denonville's summer 1687 Seneca campaign and the 1688 Kondiaronk ambassador-ambush story are broadly supported, but the latter depends on retrospective French storytelling and must remain qualified. DCB https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/kondiaronk_2F.html .
- **1689:** The August 5 Lachine attack is securely dated; battlefield and casualty numbers in colonial narratives remain disputed and rightly hedged.
- **1693 and 1696:** French attacks on Mohawk settlements in 1693 and Frontenac's Onondaga/Oneida campaign in 1696 are supported; there was no decisive annihilation of the Confederacy. Canadian government survey https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/services/military-history/history-heritage/popular-books/aboriginal-people-canadian-military/arrival-europeans-17th-century-wars.html .
- **1701 Great Peace:** August 4 is firm; sources give differing totals (approximately 38 or 39 Indigenous nations and roughly 1,300 attendees, depending on counting approach). Manuscript wisely uses a non-exact qualified count. Pointe-à-Callière https://pacmusee.qc.ca/en/press-room/press-releases/press-release-august-4/ and https://pacmusee.qc.ca/en/stories-of-montreal/article/the-great-peace-of-montreal/ .
- **1701 Albany Deed:** Full text https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/nanfan-treaty-1701 ; the instrument is a purported conveyance/quitclaim of an extensive claimed hunting territory, preserving explicit **free hunting** with expected royal protection; it does **not separately specify fishing rights** in its transcribed reservation. Non-signatory Indigenous territorial rights cannot be inferred to be extinguished.

**Scholarly interpretation:** Broad attribution to Daniel Richter, José António Brandão, Jon Parmenter, and Bruce Trigger is consistent with the debate over mourning-war, commercial, territorial and diplomatic interpretations. Claude should ground particular interpretive claims in named works and avoid treating one author's argument as consensus. **Numbers of combatants or captives:** keep colonially reported force sizes identified as source claims, never established headcounts.

## Narrow factual/source corrections applied to the working HTML

1. **§47.38 and 1701 deed feature** — eliminated unsupported claim that the deed expressly retained *both hunting and fishing*; the text reserves free hunting for the Five Nations and descendants while seeking English protection. The host catalog's tags are not a substitute for the deed's words.
2. **§47.20 feature** — changed an undated composite 'Primary Source Window: French accounts of 1666' to **Evidence Study**. It now acknowledges that the synthesis spans multiple French military and Jesuit documents and directs students to identify a specific dated document before citing.
3. **§47.36 feature** — changed the generic Covenant Chain council-record paraphrase into **Evidence Study** with transparent research instructions. No individual speech, council or transcript had been identified, so the chapter must not present the generalized summary as a direct primary-source excerpt.

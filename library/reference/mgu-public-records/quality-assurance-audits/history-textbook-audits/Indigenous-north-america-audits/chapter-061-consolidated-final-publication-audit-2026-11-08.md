## 4. Historical corrections and independent verification

### A. December 1786 confederated council

- The original address was made at a Huron/Wyandot village near the mouth of the Detroit River and dates to December 18, 1786. The publication edition interprets its demand for a **united voice in land cessions** and a general council, and correctly distinguishes the request to stop surveyors crossing the Ohio **until talks occurred** from the more explicit later permanent-boundary demand.
- Checked against National Archives Foundation DocsTeach: https://docsteach.org/document/united-indian-nations/ and Papers of the War Department: https://wardepartmentpapers.org/document/39863/. Documentary English was translated and copied, and is not represented as an original-language transcription.

### B. Treaty diplomacy, 1784–1789

- **Fort Stanwix (October 22, 1784):** Six Nations instrument, distinguished from western peoples’ own authority; see https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-six-nations-1784-0005.
- **Fort McIntosh (January 21, 1785):** Wyandot/Delaware/Chippewa/Ottawa parties; Article I required **three chiefs as hostages**, with boundary and U.S. reservations in later articles. See https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-wyandot-etc-1785-0006.
- **Fort Finney (January 31, 1786):** Shawnee treaty and contested consent; see https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-shawnee-1786-0016.
- **Fort Harmar (January 9, 1789):** repeated disputed territorial provisions; Article I held two Wyandot hostages. See https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-wyandot-etc-1789-0018.
- **Northwest Ordinance:** Article III’s land-protection statement is interpreted together with its authorized-war exception, checked against https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/northwest-ordinance.

### C. Harmar and St. Clair

- Harmar’s expedition is described as **American tactical reverses combined with destructive raids on Indigenous settlements**, not as an inconclusive draw. The chapter preserves the distinction between battlefield outcome and civilian/agricultural losses.
- St. Clair’s defeat is dated **November 4, 1791**. The chapter differentiates combined killed-and-wounded figures from any unproven exact count of soldiers killed. The cited U.S. Army account reports over 900 soldiers and accompanying women/children killed or wounded: https://www.army.mil/article/65594/st_clairs_campaign_of_1791_a_defeat_in_the_wilderness_that_helped_forge_todays_us_army.
- The chapter rejects unfounded claims that Little Turtle held permanent centralized command over all the coalition’s member peoples.

### D. Sandusky diplomacy, 1793 — publication-critical issue resolved

- The earlier Claude draft incorrectly said American commissioners **met the general Indigenous council at the Maumee Rapids**. The corrected §61.14 differentiates commissioners Benjamin Lincoln, Beverley Randolph, and Timothy Pickering **waiting near the mouth of the Detroit River** from Indigenous delegates **counciling at the Maumee Rapids**. The planned full treaty meeting at Sandusky did not convene.
- Checked using the July 21 record https://wardepartmentpapers.org/document/45096/, August correspondence https://wardepartmentpapers.org/document/45234/, and August 16 termination https://wardepartmentpapers.org/document/78922/. American commissioners rejected recognition of the Ohio as boundary; the confederacy insisted on it. These records express the commissioners’ perspective and do not independently establish Indigenous responsibility for failure.

### E. Fallen Timbers and Greenville

- Fallen Timbers is correctly dated August 20, 1794. The military sequence and refusal of Fort Miamis to admit retreating warriors were checked against NPS: https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/historical-overview-of-fallen-timbers-battlefield-and-fort-miamis.htm. The chapter does not assert all warriors reached the fort or that British refusal was the sole cause of peace.
- Greenville is dated **August 3, 1795**; twelve named Indigenous groupings are recorded. **Article II** provides ten Indigenous hostages for prisoners’ return; **Articles III–V** concern territorial settlement, continuing rights, and future land purchases. Checked against https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-wyandot-etc-1795-0039 and https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/greenvil.asp.
- The narrative distinguishes U.S. territorial expansion from the continued existence of Indigenous governments and avoids teleological assertions that defeat was inevitable.

## 6. Disclosed first-edition limits

1. **Source collation:** Original-language council speeches, British Indian Department manuscript series, and all archival document facsimiles were not exhaustively collated. The source windows are explicitly identified editorial paraphrases, not verified diplomatic transcriptions. The *Papers of the War Department* may contain machine transcriptions requiring facsimile checking for precise quotation. No invented verbatim source quotations are presented.
2. **Cartographic plates:** Historical geography and military-map tasks are text-based exercises; there are no embedded original historical atlas plates. An external reliable atlas is necessary to complete them.
3. **Casualty precision:** Uncertainty in St. Clair and other campaign counts is retained rather than replaced by false exactitude.
4. **Historiographical coverage:** First-edition explanatory synthesis is substantial but is not a substitute for a comprehensive archival monograph; specialized scholars may refine leadership attribution, village locations, or particular council records.
5. **External links:** Hyperlinks identify sources as of publication preparation; some institutional pages may subsequently move or require institutional access.

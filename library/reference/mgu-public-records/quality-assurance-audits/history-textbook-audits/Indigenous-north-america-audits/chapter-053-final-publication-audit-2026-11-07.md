## 3. Historical corrections performed

**Southeastern trade / Gallay.** Corrected §53.8 to attribute the approximately 24,000–51,000 estimate to the broader southeastern captive trade of c. 1670–1715 rather than falsely treating that estimate as the number shipped out of Carolina. The section now distinguishes people traded/captured, locally held, relocated inside the mainland, and shipped overseas. The §53.19 synthesis was updated to match.

**New France population counts.** Corrected §53.6 to distinguish a Trudel-based compilation of approximately 3,604 individual enslaved people of African and Indigenous ancestry through 1759, including approximately 1,132 Black people, from the Canadian Museum of History’s narrower time-specific summary of about 2,000 enslaved people of **both** origins between 1700 and 1760. The figures are not current population censuses, and source methodology/coverage varies.

**Muscogee coalition history.** Replaced §53.8’s compressed “creation of the Creek Confederacy” assertion with the longer-term process of Muscogee town-coalition reorganization, consolidation, and incorporation of displaced peoples.

**Abiquiú chronology.** Added §§53.10 discussion distinguishing early Santa Rosa de Abiquiú settlement and abandonment from Governor Tomás Vélez Cachupín’s **May 10, 1754** resettlement and land grant. Expanded the interpretation of property, defense obligations, and community agency.

**Comanche captivity.** Added context on varying routes into households, kinship incorporation, the effect of age and gender, diplomacy and repatriation, and the danger of projecting Rivaya-Martínez’s 1820–1875 material backward as an eighteenth-century frequency.

**Northwest Coast slavery.** Expanded §53.14 on maritime geography, labor and hereditary status, transfer mechanisms, ransom limits, and the differences between eighteenth-century evidence and better-documented nineteenth-century examples.

**California mission chronology and evidence.** Corrected the statement about mission foundation to **20 by 1821; the 21st, San Francisco Solano, was established in 1823**. Clarified that approximately twenty thousand residents is a point-in-time aggregate near peak, not the cumulative sum of all baptisms or deaths. Distinguished mission questionnaires, administrative discipline records, mission registers, later demographic interpretations and Native trial testimony.

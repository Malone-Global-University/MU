## Historical accuracy audit and targeted repairs

1. **§43.5 — Bonilla–Humaña:** Corrected an added claim that dated the unauthorized Leyva de Bonilla/Gutiérrez de Humaña expedition to 1593. Specialist accounts generally associate its departure with 1594 and Plains movements with 1595. Added the Indigenous participant Jusepe Gutiérrez as the source of the reported killing of Bonilla, while acknowledging limited knowledge of the party's fate. The Castaño de Sosa expedition of 1590–1591 remains correctly dated.
2. **§43.15 — Encomienda tribute:** Removed an overly categorical statement that Pueblo tribute was collected annually after the harvest. The annual *assessment* and collection frequency are not identical: one documented 1638 account specifies an annual household assessment; studies of seventeenth-century practice identify sometimes twice-yearly collections (and local variation). The revised prose distinguishes those facts.
3. **§43.23 — Zuni and Dowa Yalanne:** Removed anachronistic claims that the Zuni communities' large-scale refuge at Dowa Yalanne followed the 1632 deaths of missionaries. The well-established mass relocation to that mesa belongs to the 1680 era (Chapter 44). The 1632 attacks and disruption of the Hawikuh mission remain described. The related later-section sentence and closing review glossary entry were repaired.
4. **§43.23 and chronology/review — Taos:** Changed the hard-edged 1639 date to 1639–1640, reflecting differences among specialist/NPS chronologies for the Taos uprising and killing of the missionary.
5. **§43.16 — Agricultural seasons:** Retained the documented structural conflict between agricultural work and colonial labor demands, but removed an added claim about negotiated labor scheduling that was not directly established by the identified sources. The revised text explicitly indicates uncertainty.

The formerly audited corrections remain: Oñate's 1614 sentence; Bernardo López de Mendizábal's inquisitorial prosecution, death and later exoneration; 1650 conspiracy community list; differences between Holy Office and provincial missionary jurisdictions; 1675 Pueblo religious-leader deaths; differentiated Salinas abandonments (Las Humanas by 1672, Abó 1673, Quarai around/by 1678). The two Primary Source Windows remain expressly labeled paraphrases rather than fabricated literal quotations.

## Verification of Claude's flagged new claims

- **Mission supply caravan approximately every three years — supported.** John L. Kessell reproduces the 1631 mission supply contract and explicitly identifies triennial supply convoys. Avoid assuming every arrival was punctual.
- **Famine-era consumption of hides and cart straps — supported as a reported extreme.** Fray Francisco de Ayeta's account describes Spanish inhabitants and Indigenous people alike resorting to hides and cart straps in the famine around 1670. The chapter does not present a fabricated direct quotation.
- **Spring ditch work / late summer and autumn harvest — ecologically and agronomically well supported.** However, specific bargaining by Pueblo officials over draft scheduling is not established by the sources examined; prose now makes that distinction.
- **Castaño de Sosa and Bonilla–Humaña — verified with corrected chronology and source qualifications.**
- **Mission at Las Humanas began major new church works in the 1660s — confirmed by NPS architectural research.**
- **Pecos and other colonial Pueblo mission burial practices — independently supportable as a broad archaeological theme; individual belief cannot be inferred from graves.**

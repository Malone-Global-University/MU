## Historical checkpoints and deliberate corrections

1. **Treaties One and Two / 1875:** Describes the 30 April 1875 Order in Council, outside-promises memorandum, and annuity adjustment; explicitly notes that the original printed articles of Treaties One and Two lacked the express continuing hunting/fishing provision later treaties included. [Federal treaty overview](https://www.rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1360948213124/1544620003549) and [research report](https://www.rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1100100028660/1564413031536).
2. **Treaty Three:** Identifies the Paypom document in relation to Joseph Nolin’s notes and states that the transmission and provenance need critical examination. [Federal Treaty Three research](https://www.rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1100100028671/1564413174418).
3. **Treaty Four:** Explains 1874 Crown commission instructions, boundary surveying, projected telegraph works and North-West Mounted Police movement; does not mistake an Hudson’s Bay Company transfer for an Indigenous land treaty. [Treaty Four primary text and Order in Council](https://www.rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1100100028689/1581293019940).
4. **Treaty Six:** Separates the 1876 medicine chest at an Indian Agent’s house and the conditional general-famine/pestilence provisions from broader later interpretations. [Official Treaty Six text](https://www.rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1100100028710/1581292569426).
5. **Treaty Seven:** Uses 22 September 1877; identifies the five treaty peoples without merging them, David Laird and James F. Macleod; distinguishes the treaty text and the Treaty Seven Elders and Tribal Council's later recorded traditions. [Canadian Museum of History](https://www.historymuseum.ca/digital-on-demand/history-hall-article-negotiating-treaty-7).
6. **Adhesions:** Identifies Big Bear’s August 1883 Treaty Six adhesion as a distinct historical decision. [Federal Numbered Treaties](https://www.rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1360948213124/1544620003549).
7. **Treaty Eight:** Identifies the 1899 commissioners and northern livelihood assurances without treating the report as a universal verbatim transcript. [Treaty Eight record](https://www.rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1100100028813/1581293624572).
8. **Treaty Nine:** Identifies MacMartin’s 1905 diary and New Post context separately from the 1905–1906 official treaty clauses; distinguishes written exceptions from reported oral assurances. [Nishnawbe Aski Nation documents](https://nantreaties.ca/resources/documents/).
9. **Treaties Ten and Eleven:** Distinguishes Métis scrip from First Nations treaty-making, northern resource interests and the 1921 Treaty Eleven / additional 1922 signings. [Treaty Ten](https://www.rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1100100028874/1581292941464) and [Treaty Eleven research](https://www.cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1100100028912/1564415459067).
10. **Continuing rights:** Describes Constitution Act 1982, section 35 as recognizing and affirming *existing* rights rather than creating rights in 1982.

## Disclosed limitations

- Source windows contain explicit **editorial paraphrase and analysis**, not original-language/facsimile-collated direct quotations. Their linked sources establish documented pathways for further verification.
- Historical verification was targeted to key documentary claims and bibliographic identities; it was **not** an exhaustive fact check of every sentence against every archival original. Some Indigenous histories remain insufficiently recorded in surviving published documentation.
- Geography activities require a separate reliable historical atlas or community map; the chapter provides a text-guided geographical study, not a map plate.
- The **Chapter 81 style and behavior** were retained as the directly available MGU model. The original Chapter 1 HTML was not supplied, so byte-exact style and script equivalence cannot be claimed.
- Browser testing was done on generated HTML locally; live-site publishing, file-server link integrity, and production content-security policies remain untested.

**Lock policy:** Treat Chapter 82 as complete for the first-edition textual HTML release; reopen only for verified material factual errors or publication-system integration requirements.

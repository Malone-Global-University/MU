## Executive judgment

This audit examines the final revised HTML, **not merely Claude's completion statement or the original prepublication draft**. The manuscript retains the agreed five-division, twenty-section architecture and the complete MGU history-textbook instructional apparatus. Its direct numbered-section historical narrative now totals **10,768 whitespace-delimited words**, within the approved **10,000–12,000-word** range. The previously identified 2026 Canadian progress-report figures have been corrected throughout the numbered narrative and the chapter summary; the Manitoba landfill-search outcome has been brought into the 2025 historical record; the additional September 2026 federal audit is distinguished from the July Justice Department audit; and the primary-source features now connect directly to documents. No major historical or publication-critical discrepancy was found in the final targeted review.

The chapter's core historical interpretation remains appropriately qualified: Indigenous families, survivors, researchers, and governments had different roles; missing persons, homicide, and unknown-status cases are not interchangeable; datasets with different definitions cannot be summed; and awareness, legal reform, operational activity, and demonstrated safety outcomes are not the same. The chapter distinguishes colonial and institutional structures from responsibility for any particular act of violence. It also uses respectful, non-sensational descriptions of survivors and families.

## II. Final revision history and factual verification

### 1. Sisters in Spirit — §129.5

The final manuscript explicitly preserves the 2010 Native Women's Association of Canada report's four case classifications: **393 deaths attributed to homicide or negligence, 115 missing women and girls, 21 suspicious deaths, and 53 cases of unknown nature**, totalling 582. It does not incorrectly represent all 393 as adjudicated homicide cases. The text distinguishes research documentation from a population incidence estimate and explains why police records and Indigenous research cannot be combined mechanically.

**Original documentary basis:** Native Women's Association of Canada, *What Their Stories Tell Us* (2010):  
https://nwac.ca/assets-knowledge-centre/2010_What_Their_Stories_Tell_Us_Research_Findings_SIS_Initiative-1.pdf

### 2. 2014 RCMP overview and calls for inquiry — §129.7

The chapter retains the **1,181-case** total with distinct components: **1,017 homicides over 1980–2012** and **164 unresolved missing cases at the end of 2013**. The expansion clarifies differences in time frames, racial identification in police records, the limits of clearance/solve-rate comparisons, and why families and advocates called for inquiry beyond police statistical compilation. It does not claim the RCMP count corrected the Sisters in Spirit count or that comparable clearance rates establish quality of response in every case.

**Original documentary basis:** RCMP, *Missing and Murdered Aboriginal Women: A National Operational Overview* (2014):  
https://rcmp.ca/en/corporate-information/publications-and-manuals/missing-and-murdered-aboriginal-women-national-operational-overview

### 3. Canadian National Inquiry — §129.8

The final chapter specifies **2,386 participants in the truth-gathering process** and separately identifies the **1,484 family members and survivors who provided testimony**. It retains the report's **231 Calls for Justice**. The genocide finding remains explicitly attributed to the National Inquiry; the manuscript distinguishes the inquiry's historical and legal interpretation from an individual criminal conviction. The primary-source feature links to the report itself rather than an institutional homepage.

**Original documentary basis:** National Inquiry, *Reclaiming Power and Place* (2019):  
https://www.mmiwg-ffada.ca/final-report/

### 4. Urban Indian Health Institute — §129.10

The 2018 UIHI study is correctly presented as **506 cases in 71 selected cities: 128 missing persons, 280 murdered persons, and 98 cases with unknown status**. Additional prose explains city-selection limits, variable agency responsiveness, the role of media and family information, and why these results cannot be generalized into an American national rate. The manuscript continues to distinguish the 5,712 reports and 116 NamUs entries as data from different systems; their difference is not claimed to be an exact count of uninvestigated disappearances.

**Original documentary basis:** UIHI, *Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women & Girls* (2018):  
https://www.uihi.org/missing-and-murdered-indigenous-women-girls/2/

### 5. U.S. law, the Not Invisible Act Commission, and 2026 audits — §§129.11–129.12

The text preserves the October 2020 enactment of **Savanna's Act** and the **Not Invisible Act**, the 2023 Not Invisible Act Commission report, and the distinction between legislation and operational compliance. The **July 29, 2026 DOJ OIG audit (26-076)** is reported as concerning U.S. Attorney districts' guidelines and the MMIP Regional Outreach Program, with 10 of 49 districts requiring guideline correction and additional findings regarding staffing and performance measures. The material explicitly notes that overlapping categories of deficiency must not be added as unique districts.

The final manuscript now also describes the **September 1, 2026 Department of the Interior OIG audit (2024-CR-019)**, which identified inefficient handling of referrals by the Bureau of Indian Affairs' Missing and Murdered Unit and inconsistent provision of required victim services. It notes that this separate audit made **eight recommendations**. This is distinguished from DOJ OIG's prosecutorial-guidelines and outreach review, not treated as a second account of the same audit.

**Documentary bases:**  
DOI, Not Invisible Act Commission transmission (November 1, 2023):  
https://www.doi.gov/pressreleases/not-invisible-act-commission-transmits-recommendations-federal-government-address

U.S. Government Publishing Office, *Not One More* (2023):  
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-J-PURL-gpo220266/pdf/GOVPUB-J-PURL-gpo220266.pdf

DOJ OIG, Audit 26-076 (July 29, 2026):  
https://oig.justice.gov/reports/audit-united-states-attorneys-offices-compliance-savannas-act-2020-and-their-efforts

### 6. Two-Spirit terminology and institutional access — §129.15

The expanded discussion distinguishes the modern organizing history of the term *Two-Spirit* from historically specific gender roles and terms within individual Indigenous nations. It identifies problems involving intake forms, records, chosen names, gender classifications, shelters, and cultural safety, while emphasizing consent, confidentiality, and Indigenous leadership. It avoids treating Two-Spirit as a universal synonym for every Indigenous sexual or gender-diverse identity.

### 7. Canadian 2025–2026 Federal Pathway Annual Progress Report — §129.18 and Chapter Summary

**Publication-critical correction completed throughout the chapter.** The federal report's **July 30, 2026 correction** replaced the earlier numbers of 171 initiatives and 28 departments/agencies. The final manuscript and the closing summary consistently use **174 initiatives across 29 federal departments and agencies**, actively advancing **163 Calls for Justice**. It expressly distinguishes the government's account of *advancing* calls from full implementation, and proposes independent measures of actual safety and service access.

**Original documentary basis:** Government of Canada, *2025–2026 Federal Pathway Annual Progress Report*, corrected July 30, 2026:  
https://www.cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1777993275079/1778001918612

The report's own footnotes document the amendment. Some introductory government-language versions continued to display superseded figures; the final chapter adopts the corrected main-report figures.

### 8. Winnipeg Prairie Green Landfill search — §129.19

The draft's assertion that the search outcomes were beyond the account has been removed. The revised text records that, after sustained advocacy by their families and Manitoba's supported humanitarian search, the remains of **Morgan Harris and Marcedes Myran**, both of **Long Plain First Nation**, were recovered. The Prairie Green search **concluded July 9, 2025**; private ceremonies followed **July 14–15**. It distinguishes that completed recovery from other pending or subsequent search efforts and from the criminal proceedings.

**Original documentary basis:** Province of Manitoba, *Prairie Green Landfill Search Bulletin #6* (July 17, 2025):  
https://news.gov.mb.ca/news/?item=69998

### 9. Comparative synthesis — §129.20

The conclusion now draws a sharper distinction between Canada's national inquiry and progress-report framework and the United States' legislative, administrative, and inspector-general accountability mechanisms. It emphasizes that a government program's existence or reported activity cannot alone demonstrate reductions in violence. It avoids prescribing a uniform solution for distinct regions, nations, legal systems, and identities, and ends on evidence, effective protection, and responsibility rather than a claim that the crisis is solved.

## III. Instructional completeness and editorial safeguards

The finalized chapter retains **six case studies, three primary-source windows, three contextual vocabulary boxes, and all seven required historical-analysis features**. Its source windows present their account as **paraphrase rather than fabricated direct quotation**, identify their institutions and documentary purpose, and now offer original-document links. The separate source register supports library research and cross-checking. The Geography Study presents the intended instructional questions without an inserted map; actual map creation remains a later editorial activity managed by the publisher.

The final text continues to avoid sensational descriptions of violence and unnecessary details of individual deaths. It names persons only within documented public historical contexts. It does not infer common perpetrators in all cases, collapse missing-person reports into homicide totals, treat the National Inquiry's genocide finding as a criminal verdict, or confuse North American statistics with the distinct Mexican legal and statistical framework. It also reserves the broader jurisdictional history for Chapter 130 and modern child welfare for Chapter 131.

## V. Remaining limitations and conditions

1. **Current and future reporting:** This chapter contains 2026 governmental and inspector-general findings. Their status can change after the audit date. Any new government report, changed audit-recommendation status, later correction, or new investigation should be incorporated as a dated scholarly update rather than silently replacing the historical record.
2. **No claim of exhaustive verification:** The substantive revisions were checked against authoritative material for the key issues identified in the independent prepublication audit. This was not an independent review of every sentence, court record, or research citation in the approximately 10,768-word narrative.
3. **No live site deployment test:** Local HTML parsing and browser rendering do not establish successful publication on `maloneuniversity.org`; public routing, site-level scripts, remote accessibility and HTTP status remain untested.
4. **No inserted map image:** The geography instructional exercise is complete; insertion of finished maps remains the publisher's separate workflow and is **not a publication blocker**.
5. **Sourcing scope:** The linked source register provides major documentary references, not a footnote for every factual clause. Source links to historical reports and administrative audits should continue to be checked and supplemented as the series matures.

## 3. Material substantive corrections completed

### 3.1 Ancient One chronology — §125.11

The original text incorrectly described a Department of the Interior determination in **2015** as establishing Native American status and cultural affiliation after a DNA study. The final text separates **five different stages**:

1. **2000:** Interior Secretary Bruce Babbitt made a government determination concerning Native American status and cultural affiliation; that position was challenged in litigation.
2. **2004:** The Ninth Circuit in *Bonnichsen v. United States* upheld the judicial rejection of the earlier application of the law to the remains on the available record.
3. **2015:** Ancient-DNA research associated the remains with present-day Native Americans, particularly relevant to the Colville among sampled populations; the genetic study was scientific evidence, not a final administrative transfer order.
4. **April 2016:** The **U.S. Army Corps of Engineers**, not the Department of the Interior in 2015, determined that the remains were Native American for NAGPRA purposes, informed by the genomic research and agency review. **December 2016:** Congress enacted the relevant WIIN Act transfer provision.
5. **February 17–18, 2017:** The Corps transferred the remains for return to the claimant nations on February 17, and the nations held their reburial on February 18.

**Verification:** *Bonnichsen* Ninth Circuit ruling; U.S. Army Corps 2016 determination and February 2017 transfer statements; Burke Museum historical chronology. Direct links now appear in §125.11. The revised historical explanation distinguishes archaeological, scientific, judicial, administrative, legislative, and Indigenous forms of authority.

### 3.2 House of Ni'isjoohl memorial pole — §§125.14, 125.19

Corrected the two **2024** return-date errors to **2023**. The institutional decision to return the pole was made in **2022**; its return from Scotland and raising in the Nisg̱a'a Museum occurred in **2023**, with the raising in October. The expanded paragraph distinguishes decision, physical journey, ceremonial reception, and Indigenous-house responsibilities. Verified against Museums Galleries Scotland and National Museums Scotland historical reporting.

### 3.3 NAGPRA 2010 and 2024 regulatory descriptions — §§125.7, 125.9, 125.10, 125.12, 125.18

Clarified that the **2010 disposition regulation** for culturally unidentifiable Native American remains established procedural arrangements involving consultation, requests, and priorities. It did **not** mandate indiscriminate or automatic transfers to any claimant.

The **2024 rules**, effective **January 12, 2024**, are tied to the original **December 13, 2023 Federal Register notice** and to the current law in **43 CFR Part 10**, including §10.1(d)(3). The chapter now explains that covered museums and federal agencies need appropriate **free, prior, and informed consent** before exhibition, access or research involving covered remains or cultural items. The text expressly does not universalize this rule to all private owners or foreign institutions. Historical inventory requirements are differentiated from revised affiliation, consultation and notice procedures. The later inventory compliance date of **January 10, 2029**, where applicable, is grounded in National Park Service guidance.

### 3.4 Further accuracy and interpretive refinements

- **§125.3:** Removed the overly absolute statement that Indigenous nations literally had *no legal standing* before NAGPRA. The revised text describes limited effective remedies and severely constrained decision-making authority. Distinguished antiquities and archaeology permitting statutes from comprehensive repatriation rights.
- **§125.4:** Anchored the documented **1987 Smithsonian return of two Zuni Ahayu:da** without inventing an aggregate number of all returns.
- **§125.7:** Differentiated historical summaries, inventories, consultations, reasonable cultural-affiliation connections, and contemporary priority rules.
- **§125.10:** Expanded the treatment of multiple claimants, intertribal cooperation, displacement, and legal priority without presuming that one institution should determine Indigenous cultural responsibility unilaterally.
- **§125.16:** Clarified the interpretation of the Greenland *Utimut* process: approximately **35,000 objects** were transferred from the National Museum of Denmark to Greenland's national museum during **1982–2001**, not indiscriminately to each descendant community. Qilakitsoq is treated as collection-specific. The **1993** Inughuit repatriation is distinguished from ordinary NAGPRA processes.
- **§125.18:** Expanded archival governance, digital access, restricted ceremonial information, sampling, continuing consent, and disagreements about research access.
- **§§125.19–125.20:** Clarified differences among national patrimony, international restitution and direct Indigenous community authority. Strengthened the final synthesis across the United States, Canada, Mexico, and Greenland.
- **Chronology preview:** Repositioned the **1982–2001 Utimut** period before the later 1988–1992 and 1989–1990 rows by the period's starting year. Overlapping periods retain their explicit spans.

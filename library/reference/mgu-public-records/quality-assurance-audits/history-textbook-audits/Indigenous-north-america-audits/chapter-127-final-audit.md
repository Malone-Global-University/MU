## 3. Verified historical corrections made in the final HTML

### A. The 1998 Statement of Reconciliation — §127.4, with a consequential correction in §127.7

**Previous problem:** The source draft said the federal government expressed regret instead of apologizing and that some observers found the statement “fell short of an apology” in the sense of containing no apologetic language.

**Correction completed:** The new narrative acknowledges that Jane Stewart's January 7, 1998 statement both expressed the government's profound regret for historical policies and directly told survivors of physical and sexual abuse, “we are deeply sorry.” The historical contrast with June 11, 2008 is now defined in terms of **scope, institution, forum, and explicit repudiation of the assimilation policy**: a ministerial statement launched with a federal action plan in 1998 versus an address by the prime minister to Parliament in 2008. This correction preserves the genuine criticisms of the 1998 statement without falsifying its language. The primary document is linked in §127.4; the §127.7 comparison and source-window analysis are aligned.

**Verified text:** [Jane Stewart's January 7, 1998 address, Government of Canada](https://rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1100100015725/1571590271585); [Harper apology in parliamentary proceedings, June 11, 2008](https://cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1100100015677/1571589339246).

### B. National Council for Reconciliation chronology — §127.20

**Previous problem:** The source manuscript assigned the Council's inaugural report to August 2026, conflating a report date with later public reporting about it; the institutional board sequence was also underexplained.

**Correction completed:** The narrative now distinguishes the Council's statutory creation (Royal Assent **April 30, 2024**, in force **July 2, 2024**), its 2025 inaugural governance phase, the announced **September 2026** first permanent board, and its inaugural report. The Council's homepage lists **June 30, 2026** for the inaugural report, whereas its online report page presently bears a later **October 20, 2026** webpage date. The chapter explicitly alerts readers to this documentation difference and does not assert that an August newspaper story establishes the report's date.

**Verified records:** [National Council for Reconciliation homepage and publication timeline](https://reconciliation.ca/), [2026 inaugural report landing page](https://reconciliation.ca/read-the-inaugural-report/), [first permanent board announcement](https://reconciliation.ca/national-council-for-reconciliation-announces-its-first-permanent-board-of-directors/).

### C. Compensation and IAP accuracy — §127.6

The revised paragraph gives the actual **Common Experience Payment schedule**: **$10,000** for the first eligible year or part of a year and **$3,000** for each subsequent eligible year or part of a year. It cites the government's dated **March 31, 2016** implementation snapshot: **79,309 paid applications**, totaling approximately **$1.62 billion**. For the Independent Assessment Process, it identifies the **March 31, 2019** government snapshot: **38,257 applications**, **38,178 resolved**, and about **$3.18 billion in approved payments**. These figures are explicitly labeled historical administrative snapshots, not current annual spending or necessarily final program-closeout figures.

**Verified record:** [Government of Canada, implementation statistics for CEP and IAP](https://www.cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1315320539682/1571590489978). The legal distinctions between the CEP and the IAP are also supported by [the official settlement overview](https://www.cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1100100015576/1571581687074).

### D. Catholic church obligations and post-2021 initiative — §127.18

The draft's vague reference to a fundraising shortfall is now concrete. Catholic entities' settlement-related **$25 million best-efforts fundraising target** yielded approximately **$3.7 million** by church-published accounts. The **$30 million pledge by Canadian Catholic bishops in 2021** was a later, separate initiative advanced through the Indigenous Reconciliation Fund; it is **not** treated as an installment of or automatic satisfaction of the earlier undertaking. The revised chapter preserves differences between Catholic legal entities, religious orders, and the bishops' conference.

**Verified record:** [Archdiocese of Toronto, financial and reconciliation background](https://www.archtoronto.org/en/outreach/home/background/).

### E. Independent Special Interlocutor — §127.16

The chapter now gives the **full title** *Upholding Sacred Obligations: Reparations for Missing and Disappeared Indigenous Children and Unmarked Burials in Canada*, correctly states the **October 29, 2024** release, and attributes the proposed **42 obligations** to the Interlocutor's office. It continues to distinguish recommendations from enacted and enforced protections.

**Verified record:** [Office of the Independent Special Interlocutor, official final-report announcement](https://osi-bis.ca/news-post/independent-special-interlocutor-final-report/).

### F. Missing children and Kamloops investigation — §§127.13–127.15

The manuscript preserves essential evidentiary distinctions: a documented historic death, a missing student whose fate is unknown, a burial record, a geophysical indication consistent with a burial, a confirmed burial, and a person's identification are not interchangeable. It does not claim 215 excavated bodies or an established number of identified burials at Kamloops. The 2021 preliminary findings are described in their historic context, and the **February 17, 2026** Tk'emlúps te Secwépemc update is linked directly. The community's multi-method process, narrowed areas of inquiry, ruled-out locations, and unresolved investigative work remain accurately characterized.

The NCTR's 2025 update about **1,143 newly verified names** is treated as additional identification from historical records, not as new deaths. The discussion of Cowessess, Sagkeeng, and Penelakut retains separate community and methodological contexts and does not aggregate non-comparable findings into a supposed national forensic total.

**Verified records:** [Tk'emlúps te Secwépemc, February 2026 investigation update](https://tkemlups.ca/news/update-on-ongoing-investigation-at-former-kamloops-indian-residential-school/); [NCTR, National Student Memorial Register research update](https://nctr.ca/statements-and-news-releases/honouring-a-sacred-commitment-more-names-verified-for-the-national-student-memorial-register/).

### G. Legal exclusions and historical settlement context — §127.17

The legal account distinguishes former students at institutions covered by the national agreement from those whose cases proceeded through other lawsuits and settlements. A new documentary reference clarifies the separation between the **2021 Day Scholars settlement** and the **2023 Gottfriedson Band Class settlement**, under which the federal government committed **$2.8 billion** to a collective fund. This collective remedy is not represented as a new individual claim under the 2006 settlement.

**Verified record:** [Government of Canada, Gottfriedson Band Class settlement backgrounder](https://www.canada.ca/en/crown-indigenous-relations-northern-affairs/news/2023/01/settlement-agreement-reached-in-gottfriedson-band-class-litigation.html).

### H. Progress comparisons and commemoration — §§127.19–127.20

The final text preserves the key statistical distinction between the federal government's claim of **more than 85% of 76 calls completed or well underway** and the Assembly of First Nations' **approximately 13 of the total 94 calls completed** in its September 2025 evaluation. The denominators and meanings of “underway” versus “complete” are different; these are attributed institutional assessments, not comparable numerical verdicts. Orange Shirt Day's Indigenous origins and the federal 2021 statutory commemoration remain separate historical developments.

**Documents:** [Federal Calls to Action tracker](https://www.rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1524494530110/1557511412801); [AFN September 2025 report](https://afn.bynder.com/asset/eb649a4b-f0ce-4afd-8f93-9ca9410e641d/Progress-on-Realizing-the-Truth-and-Reconciliation-Commission-s-Calls-to-Action-September-2025.pdf); [Orange Shirt Society history](https://orangeshirtday.org/about/orange-shirt-day/).

## 7. Residual limitations and publication considerations

1. **Historical evidence limitations:** Not every statement in this long chapter was independently corroborated at the sentence level; this audit targeted the consequential historical claims, disputed facts, principal quantitative measures, and official primary records. The language around burial investigations is intentionally attributed and evidentially qualified; continuing investigations can supersede present information.
2. **2026 source chronology:** The National Council's homepage lists its inaugural report as June 30, 2026, while its online report landing page currently presents an October 20, 2026 page date. The chapter explicitly notes the distinction instead of silently resolving potentially different document and publication dates.
3. **Statistical dating:** IAP and CEP figures are historical snapshots, as stated in the chapter. Annual or more recent updates must be separately verified if future editions claim current totals.
4. **Record and privacy constraints:** The chapter does not purport to authenticate confidential survivor testimony, examine protected school records, or decide which Indigenous communities should authorize further investigation or excavation.
5. **Public deployment:** Publishing to maloneuniversity.org, testing server-side integration, confirming canonical URLs, and adding later maps remain outside this local editorial process.

These limitations are explicitly bounded and do not warrant a further general rewrite of the chapter.

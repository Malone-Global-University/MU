## III. Consolidated historical revision record

1. **Skeleton Cave, December 28, 1872 (§79.10).** Replaced an unqualified “Captain William H. Brown” with officer's name and unit, because Sharlot Hall Museum identifies him as major while some published accounts say captain. His contemporaneous rank remains an unresolved source discrepancy, not an essential narrative assertion. Clarified that the National Park Service estimates approximately 50–75 dead, while Sharlot Hall Museum reports 76, and explicitly identified the Yavapai victims.
2. **Camp Verde / Verde Valley Exodus, February–March 1875 (§79.12).** Replaced vague accounts of a long winter march with mutually attributed sources: NPS approximately 150 miles and around 1,450 people; Yavapai-Apache Nation's 2024 U.S. Senate testimony approximately 180 miles and 1,476 people. NPS records at least 100 deaths, and the Nation reports more than 100. These differences reflect source perspectives and measurement conventions, not a chosen false exact total.
3. **Chiricahua Reservation in 1876 (§79.12 and chronology).** Distinguished the **summer 1876 forced removal** from **President Ulysses S. Grant's legal revocation on October 30, 1876**, of the reserve originally set aside on December 14, 1872. These dates were confirmed against the published executive orders. Avoided retroactively dating legal extinction before all removals.
4. **Camp Grant Massacre, April 30, 1871 (§79.8).** Added William S. Oury's incompatible later statements: 140 assailants in 1879 (92 Tohono O'odham, 42 Mexican participants, six Anglo-Americans) and 146 in 1885. Distinguished Whitman's first reports (63 bodies located, more than 100 dead or missing in the contemporary published account) from later estimates approaching 140–144 deaths. Identified retrospective and mediated sources, including incomplete enumeration of kidnapped children. Removed an overcategorical claim that Army officials possessed no authority to restrain civilians; instead described failed protection and accountability.
5. **Tres Castillos, October 14–15, 1880 (§79.16).** Disaggregated the *reported* Terrazas total of 78 killed into 62 fighters plus 16 women and children, and 68 captured. Original Mexican military manuscript has **not** been collated; figures explicitly remain attributions to reconstructions drawing on Terrazas, rather than independently confirmed counts.
6. **Cibecue Creek, August 30, 1881 (§79.18).** Clarified trial in November 1881 and March 3, 1882 executions of Dead Shot, Dandy Jim and Skippy at Fort Grant. Published extracts of court-martial evidence have been identified; full original proceedings were not collated.
7. **Reciprocal pursuit (§79.19).** Retained the July 29, 1882 U.S.–Mexico agreement and added the **September 21, 1882 protocol** revising its duration term, distinguishing diplomatic instruments from Crook's 1883 implementation.
8. **Mangas Coloradas, January 1863 (§79.7).** Maintained the documented killing in Army custody and qualification of contested escape/execution narratives; removed unnecessary uncertain local military-command description.
9. **Bibliography.** Corrected **Karl Jacoby**, *Shadows at Dawn: A Borderlands Massacre and the Violence of History* (2008), and **Grenville Goodwin**, *Western Apache Raiding and Warfare*, edited by **Keith H. Basso** (1971). Publisher and catalog records establish the title and contributor roles.
10. **Primary-source apparatus.** Added explicit documentary/source locators to each of the five windows and a ten-entry linked documentary locator register. Distinguishes treaty text and original executive orders from newspaper reprints, edited oral history, modern NPS explanations, Congressional tribal testimony, and bibliographical catalogues.

**Source limitation:** the five studies remain **attributed editorial paraphrases and composite source-comparison exercises, not original document/page-collated verbatim quotations**. Source records were selectively verified online; there was no exhaustive U.S./Mexican archive, full court-martial, or exact original Terrazas dispatch collation. The chapter's source apparatus distinguishes these statuses rather than suggesting otherwise. Historically contested casualty figures, troop ranks, and dates are appropriately attributed or qualified. The textual geography feature requires external historical maps.

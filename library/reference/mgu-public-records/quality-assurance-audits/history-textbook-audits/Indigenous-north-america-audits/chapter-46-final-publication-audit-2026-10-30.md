## III. Historical verification of Claude's flagged additions

**Confirmed and retained:**

1. **Rensselaerswijck:** 1629 patroonship framework; Kiliaen van Rensselaer's land transaction and settlement arrangements around Fort Orange date principally to **1630**. The manuscript's “After 1630” is safe. Sources: New York State Library Van Rensselaer papers and Albany Institute: https://nyslibrary.libguides.com/vrpapers/vrm-manor-history ; https://www.albanyinstitute.org/about/where-art-and-history-connect .
2. **Compagnie du Nord:** New France merchants' enterprise known as the Compagnie du Nord by **1682**; competition with HBC in the James Bay region. Source: *Dictionary of Canadian Biography*, Pierre-Esprit Radisson: https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/1052 .
3. **Harmen Meyndertsz van den Bogaert:** 1634–35 visit to Mohawk and Oneida communities is historically consistent; the narrative avoids invented direct quotations. New Netherland Institute records supplement the context: https://www.newnetherlandinstitute.org/ .
4. **Albany Dongan Charter:** **July 22, 1686**, with city authorities granted broad local control of Indigenous trade, attested in charter records and contemporary regulations. Source: Library of Congress https://www.loc.gov/item/mm78003407 ; New York State Library https://nysl.ptfs.com/data/Library1/106685.pdf .
5. **Henry Kelsey:** inland journey **1690–1692**, dependent on Indigenous guidance, documented in Canadian reference works. Sources: https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio.php?id_nbr=869 ; https://esask.uregina.ca/entry/kelsey_henry_1667-1724.html .
6. **New France brandy controversy:** Laval's 1660 excommunication action and the crown-ordered **1678** inquiry/consultative assembly are verified, including October 26, 1678 council meeting. Sources: https://nouvelle-france.org/eng/item/details?id=6996 and https://sfdl.omeka.net/items/show/18731 .
7. **French congés:** licensing in **1681** and royal declaration of **May 21, 1696** suppressing the 25 congés are supported by archival documentation. Source: https://nouvelle-france.org/eng/item/details?id=30417&page=1 .
8. **The Leacock/Speck debate:** genuine scholarship, now properly identifies both sides and cautions against an all-region generalization. Sources: https://ehrafworldcultures.yale.edu/cultures/nh06/documents/001 ; https://www.jstor.org/stable/44876731 .
9. **Calvin Martin/Shepard Krech III:** *Keepers of the Game* (1978 original), critiques edited by Krech as *Indians, Animals, and the Fur Trade: A Critique of Keepers of the Game* (**1981**), accurately represented. Source: https://iucat.iu.edu/iupuc/4019657 .
10. **Wendat family trading privileges:** specifically attested in Jean de Brébeuf's **1636** discussion of rights held by families and inherited by children; does not imply a universal monopoly. Source: Canadian Museum of History, reproducing the Jesuit Relations 10:223–225: https://www.historymuseum.ca/virtual-museum-of-new-france/economic-activities/fur-trade/ .
11. **1701 Albany/Nanfan Deed:** identifiable document of **July 19, 1701** describing a purported quitclaim to English Crown and an expectation of reserved hunting rights and Crown protection. Not equivalent to proof of undisputed legal transfer of all described territories. Source: Oklahoma State University Indigenous Treaties portal: https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/nanfan-treaty-1701 .

**Already properly qualified:** disappearance of St. Lawrence Iroquoian settlements, Indigenous restrictions on French travel toward additional markets, different patterns of trapping pressure, control of Pequot wampum supply, and retrospective evidence used to reconstruct early hunting territories. Claude's flagged “several thousand settlers over fifteen years” does not occur in the final uploaded narrative; no unsupported numerical assertion of that kind required correction.

1. **§46.2:** Replaced a generalized unattributed “historians following Trigger” claim about inherited trading relationships with an explicit historical reference to **Brébeuf's 1636 description** of family-held trade privileges, with the limitation that it was not a universal Wendat monopoly.

## 3. Historical-source verification and substantive findings

### A. Opening and regional military history
- June 18, 1812 U.S. declaration and the relationship between Anglo-American grievances and prior Indigenous territorial conflict: **PASS**.
- July 17, 1812 Fort Mackinac **on Mackinac Island**, distinct from the earlier mainland Fort Michilimackinac; upper-lakes trading and Indigenous alliance: **PASS**. Crosschecked against [National Park Service Mackinac Island NHL documentation](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/99c4e6c6-d262-44b5-a1d6-0e3550d23109/).
- Fort Dearborn, August 15, 1812 and Fort Wayne in September: dates and basic developments preserved; criticism of later commemorative 'massacre' language is integrated. Individual participant motivations, casualty totals and competing eyewitness accounts are not asserted beyond supported consensus.
- August 16, 1812 Detroit capitulation, October 13, 1812 Queenston Heights, and subsequent western operations: **PASS** against [Government of Canada historical chronology](https://cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1348771334472/1607906032957).
- Frenchtown includes the two January 18 and January 22, 1813 engagements, the postbattle killing of prisoners, and differences between British and Indigenous responsibility: **PASS** against [NPS River Raisin historical account](https://www.nps.gov/rira/learn/historyculture/index.htm) and [NPS discussion of the prisoner killings](https://www.nps.gov/rira/faqs.htm).
- Beaver Dams, Châteauguay, September 10 Lake Erie, October 5 Thames and July 5, 1814 Chippawa: **PASS at selected date/event level** based on Canadian historical chronology and NPS regional resources. No assertion of Tecumseh's definitively identified killer or fabricated final speech.

### B. Creek War and Treaty of Fort Jackson
- Muscogee internal politics, Red Stick reform, the July 27, 1813 Burnt Corn engagement, and August 30 Fort Mims: **PASS** against [NPS Creek War account](https://www.nps.gov/articles/creek-war-in-the-southeast-1.htm) and [NPS Red Stick movement study](https://www.nps.gov/articles/creek-organize-to-resist-white-expansion.htm).
- November 1813 campaigns and the Cherokee/Choctaw/anti-Red-Stick Muscogee alliances are treated as distinct Indigenous political choices. The term *friendly Creeks* is explicitly recognized as a historical partisan classification.
- March 27, 1814 Battle of Horseshoe Bend and approximate 22–23 million acre cession: **PASS** against [NPS Horseshoe Bend](https://www.nps.gov/hobe/). The different approximations are not claimed to be an exact surveyed figure.
- Original **August 9, 1814 Treaty of Fort Jackson** examined [full transcription](https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-creeks-1814-0107): **Article 1** cession as compensation for war costs, narrow conditional individual square-mile reservations for qualifying U.S.-allied chiefs/warriors; **Article 2** guarantee of remaining territory; **Article 3** restrictions on British/Spanish intercourse and licensed trade; **Article 4** military posts, trading houses, roads and navigation; **Article 7** recognition of food scarcity and relief. These details are correctly distinguished in §§63.16, 63.18 and the documentary study.

### C. Upper Mississippi and peace settlements
- July 17–20, 1814 Prairie du Chien campaign and capture of Fort Shelby: **PASS at event level** against [American Battlefield Trust battle account](https://www.battlefields.org/learn/war-1812/battles/prairie-du-chien) and broader war chronology. No single Indigenous leader is invented as representing all upper Mississippi peoples.
- **Treaty of Ghent signed December 24, 1814**, **ratifications exchanged and treaty in force February 17, 1815**; **Battle of New Orleans January 8, 1815**, between the two: **PASS** against the [National Archives original transcription](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/treaty-of-ghent) and [Library of Congress diplomatic history](https://guides.loc.gov/treaty-of-ghent).
- **Ghent Article IX** accurately describes the United States' conditional restoration commitment concerning 1811 rights and possessions, and **reciprocal** commitments by Britain toward Indigenous nations with whom it was at war. No fictitious ratified Indigenous buffer-state provision is attributed to the treaty.
- **Spring Wells September 8, 1815 (7 Stat. 131)** and **different Portage des Sioux treaties** are not collapsed into one settlement. Treaty chronology and signatory names checked against the [National Archives ratified treaty catalog](https://www.archives.gov/research/native-americans/treaties/catalog-links) and the [official published text at 7 Stat. 131](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-7/pdf/STATUTE-7-Pg131.pdf).

## 4. Apparatus quality, thematic and publication checks
- Indigenous political aims are treated as distinct from British or U.S. state aims; no single pan-Indigenous army or single Haudenosaunee position is incorrectly asserted.
- The Muscogee conflict is reconstructed as an internal political and religious struggle entangled with U.S. military intervention, not as a subsidiary theater automatically controlled by Tecumseh.
- Indigenous households, women, agriculture, trade, captives, refugees and destroyed resources receive sustained attention in §§63.4 and 63.18 and throughout regional narratives.
- The historiography of Fort Dearborn, River Raisin, Fort Mims, and the Thames is source-critical: eyewitness access, later commemorative embellishment, and politicized labels are flagged.
- The Treaty of Fort Jackson / Ghent comparison is the culminating analytical device; postwar government continuity is not confused with security against dispossession.
- Map exercises are intentionally text-only, requiring a separate historical atlas; no nonexistent embedded atlas plates are claimed.

## 6. Disclosed scholarly and technical limitations
1. This is a **first-edition scholarly textual HTML** publication. Treaty articles and selected historical facts were checked against identifiable primary texts and institutional historical interpretations; **not every campaign statement has undergone exhaustive manuscript-level, original-language, or multilingual archival facsimile collation**.
2. Editorial source windows provide **labeled contextual paraphrases**, not verbatim quotations attributed to Indigenous speakers. Students are directed to identified documents for independent source reading.
3. Historical source literature includes national commemorative accounts and modern synthesis, which are read critically; the bibliography is an identified source register rather than a claim that every work was collated page by page for this edition.
4. Map exercises remain **text-only with no embedded historical atlas plates**.
5. The responsive design is browser-tested, but accessibility compliance was not independently certified under an exhaustive WCAG audit.

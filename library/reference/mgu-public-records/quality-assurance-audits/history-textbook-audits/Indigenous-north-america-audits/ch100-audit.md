## Verified historical changes

1. **§100.11 — High Arctic:** replaced the erroneous attribution of 87 as the Royal Commission's figure. The 1994 Commission (printed p. 7) counts ten families comprising 54 people in the 1953 transfer, plus six additional families in 1955, for a total of approximately 92 people by 1955. The figure excludes special constables and their families at Craig Harbour and has an uncertainty of one to two people. Differing public totals refer to different populations or counting conventions.
2. **§100.12 — Ahiarmiut:** dates and distinguishes Nueltin Lake (1950); North Henik and Oftedal Lakes (1957, with seven deaths); airlift to Arviat (1958); and subsequent movements to Rankin Inlet and Whale Cove (1959–1960). Removed the misleading description of the 1953–1955 High Arctic program as a single operation.
3. **§100.19 — Greenland:** distinguishes the internal Greenlandic Home Rule Committee formed in 1973 (proposals submitted in 1975) from the joint Greenlandic-Danish commission formed in 1975 (report in 1978). Corrects November 1978 legislation, January 1979 referendum, and May 1979 commencement.
4. **§100.20 — Circumpolar movement:** corrects Eben Hopson Sr.'s first office to **founding chair, 1977–1980**. Records the conference at Barrow on 13–18 June 1977, preceding the International Whaling Commission's 29th annual meeting in Canberra on **20–24 June 1977**. The bowhead restriction is treated as a following political test, not the cause of the earlier conference.
5. **§100.1 and §100.17:** refines Greenland's population around 1901 to approximately 12,000 and specifies Okpik's 1969 Project Surname leadership appointment. §100.2 avoids equating the absence of colonial-style institutions with an absence of Inuit governance.

## Consolidated substantive corrections

### 1. Claims Commission and the distinction between restitution and compensation

Expanded §119.4 to explain the commission's 1946 establishment, litigation processes, monetary remedial limits, and archival significance. Added the third required vocabulary box distinguishing **restitution**, **compensation**, and **recognition**. The chapter contrasts 1970 Blue Lake restitution with the 1980 Sioux Nation monetary judgment instead of using the words interchangeably.

**Supporting record:** National Archives, [Records of the Indian Claims Commission](https://www.archives.gov/research/native-americans/indian-claims-commission); [United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/448/371).

### 2. Maine settlement participants

Expanded §119.7 to name the **Houlton Band of Maliseet Indians**, alongside the Passamaquoddy Tribe and Penobscot Nation, and explained that the settlement treated these governments through different arrangements. The major people/nations reference entry was amended correspondingly.

**Supporting record:** [Maine Indian Claims Settlement Act of 1980, Pub. L. 96-420](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/COMPS-16904); [Maine legislative documentary history](https://www.maine.gov/legis/lawlib/lldl/indianclaims/index.html).

### 3. Ownership, jurisdiction, access, and consultation

Revised the categorical claim that only ownership conveys control. §119.3 and related narrative now distinguish fee title, federal trust title, Canadian Aboriginal title, governing authority, treaty access, consultation, and cooperative stewardship. Canadian Crown consultation is described as a potentially enforceable constitutional duty, rather than reduced to general stakeholder input; U.S. historic-preservation consultation is identified as a separate regime.

**Supporting record:** [British Columbia's Haida title agreement description](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/natural-resource-stewardship/consulting-with-first-nations/first-nations-negotiations/first-nations-a-z-listing/haida-nation-council-of/haida-title-agreement); [Tsilhqot'in National Government](https://tsilhqotin.ca/governance/tsilhqotin-rights-title/).

### 4. Sacred-site judicial decisions

Expanded §119.10 to distinguish the **1988 Supreme Court's legal holding in *Lyng*** from the separate historical question whether the proposed road was constructed. Added the lower-court and wilderness-designation context, without claiming that a judicial victory or defeat itself dictated the physical outcome. §119.11 distinguishes *Lyng* from the later **RFRA** litigation concerning the San Francisco Peaks and reclaimed wastewater for snowmaking.

**Supporting record:** [*Lyng*, 485 U.S. 439](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/485/439); [Ninth Circuit's *Navajo Nation* decision](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2008/08/07/0615371.pdf).

### 5. Oak Flat, March–September 2026

Expanded §119.12 with the 2014 land-exchange legislation, the separate forms of legal challenge, the **March 13, 2026** Ninth Circuit ruling, the **March 16, 2026** transfer of approximately **2,422 acres** to Resolution Copper, and subsequent September appellate developments. The chapter does not treat transfer of title as completion of mine development or as the end of all Indigenous objections.

**Supporting record:** [Ninth Circuit opinion, March 13, 2026](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2026/03/13/25-5185.pdf); [Resolution Copper land-exchange timeline](https://resolutioncopper.com/land-exchange/).

### 6. Canadian Aboriginal title, 2014–2025

Expanded §119.13–119.15 with distinct treatment of <em>Delgamuukw</em>, <em>Haida Nation</em>, <em>Tsilhqot'in</em>, the 2024 **Rising Tide** and **Big Tide (Low Water)** agreements, and the **September 5, 2025** Haida consent declaration. The chapter explicitly distinguishes recognition of collective title from automatic cancellation of private fee-simple interests and explains the negotiated protection of existing interests and staged implementation.

**Supporting record:** [Supreme Court of Canada, *Tsilhqot'in*, 2014 SCC 44](https://decisions.scc-csc.ca/scc-csc/scc-csc/en/item/14246/index.do); [Council of the Haida Nation, September 2025](https://www.haidanation.ca/public-notices/press-release-from-the-council-of-the-haida-nation-court-declaration-of-haida-titlenbsp); [British Columbia's title agreement materials](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/natural-resource-stewardship/consulting-with-first-nations/first-nations-negotiations/first-nations-a-z-listing/haida-nation-council-of/haida-title-agreement).

### 7. Bears Ears and the limits of executive-dependent stewardship

Expanded §119.16 on the **five founding coalition nations**, the advisory and planning function of the Bears Ears Commission, the 2016/2017/2021 monument changes, and **Proclamation 11043 of July 13, 2026**. That proclamation reduced the monument to approximately **121,096 acres** and disbanded the Commission. The text distinguishes the action taken by the executive from the final resolution of judicial or political challenges and does not mislabel the Commission as unrestricted tribal co-government.

**Supporting record:** [Official proclamation](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/DCPD-202600459); [Bears Ears Inter-Tribal Coalition](https://www.bearsearscoalition.org/the-bears-ears-commission/).

### 8. Wiyot land return and Indigenous agency

Expanded §119.17 to connect the 1860 violence, the **2000** Wiyot purchase, the **2004** initial municipal transfer, and the **2019** final city transfer to the long-term restoration of ceremonial and community responsibilities. The chapter also examines organizing, local governance, and the limits of treating land return as instantaneous repair.

**Supporting record:** [Wiyot Tribe, Landback](https://wiyot.us/466/Landback).

## Source criticism and historical method

The three source windows continue to label paraphrases as **paraphrases**, not manufactured direct quotations. They now link to the identified legal documents, and major case studies link to original decisions, relevant government records, and Indigenous nation or intertribal organizational materials. The chapter explains when a federal statute or judicial opinion documents a legal act but cannot supply an unmediated account of Indigenous relationships to territory. It does not disclose confidential ceremonial knowledge.

Contemporary facts through November 21, 2026 were checked against available dated government, judicial, community-authored, and documentary records. The Oak Flat and Bears Ears passages deliberately avoid describing unsettled downstream legal or operational consequences as final. Complete line-by-line independent verification of every prose sentence is not claimed; the principal contentious dates, holdings, and distinctions were separately checked.

## 1. Independent editorial conclusion

The completed chapter is a substantive, historically grounded textbook treatment of Indigenous language revitalization across North America. The draft's five-division, twenty-section architecture has been retained. Corrections were made in one consolidated editorial pass, not through a general rewrite or compression. The revised text distinguishes community-controlled school founding from adoption of full immersion; resolves the 1992/1996 Breath of Life dates; distinguishes organization founding from preschool opening in Hawaiʻi; anchors the Cherokee speaker estimate to the exact source category; corrects several regional policy and orthography details; and adds identifiable documentary access to the three primary-source windows and relevant case studies.

The chapter's principal claim is sound and appropriately qualified: language revitalization is a long-term effort to renew intergenerational transmission, public uses, community institutions, and decision-making authority, rather than simply preserve word lists. The chapter addresses surviving language communities as active political and cultural agents, treats no single method as universally successful, and differentiates documentation, instruction, home use, law, and digital governance. It does not collapse distinct peoples or linguistic traditions into a single national story.

The manuscript is **approved for publication** on the basis of the specific historical and local HTML checks documented below. This assessment does not claim line-by-line verification of every historical statement, nor does it establish the functionality of the live website.

## 3. Consolidated substantive corrections actually made

### 3.1 Kahnawà:ke and Akwesasne school chronology

**Former problem:** The draft described 1978 Kahnawà:ke and 1979 Akwesasne school foundings as though they were immediately full immersion schools.

**Final correction:** Section 123.4 and Section 123.10 now distinguish the **1978 founding of Kahnawake Survival School**, a community-controlled secondary school, from later language-focused elementary initiatives. They distinguish the **1979 founding of Akwesasne Freedom School** from its **1985 decision to adopt a total Mohawk-immersion curriculum**. The chronology preview, in-chapter timeline and review reference were aligned with this sequence. 

**Verification:** Kahnawake Survival School's institutional history and Akwesasne Freedom School's own history:  
https://kahnawakesurvivalschool.kecedu.ca/about  
https://akwesasnefreedomschool.org/history-of-afs/

### 3.2 California Breath of Life chronology

**Former problem:** Section 123.6 and a review definition incorrectly attributed the first *Breath of Life* workshop to 1992.

**Final correction:** The text now distinguishes **1992 California organizing and Master–Apprentice development** from the **first California Breath of Life workshop in 1996**. The chronology preview, relevant teaching discussion and Key Terms definition were reconciled without implying that each related initiative had the same start date. The 1996 workshop date follows the National Breath of Life/Myaamia Center's account; descriptions of the precursor program may use a mid-1990s starting period.

**Verification:** https://mc.miamioh.edu/nbol/about/history

### 3.3 Hawaiian language nest chronology

**Former problem:** The draft placed the beginning of Hawaiian-language Pūnana Leo preschools in 1983 without distinguishing organizational foundation.

**Final correction:** Section 123.9 now states that **ʻAha Pūnana Leo was established in 1983** and that its **first Hawaiian-language immersion preschool opened on Kauaʻi in 1984**. An entry in the chronology and timeline clarifies the distinction. The Maori kōhanga reo model beginning in 1982 is retained as contextual comparison.

**Verification:** https://www.ahapunanaleo.org/history-hl-1

### 3.4 Cherokee immersion and language-speaker categories

**Former problems:** The Eastern Band's early immersion was prematurely described as an operating academy in the late 1990s, and a claim of “fewer than two thousand fluent speakers” lacked a matching source category.

**Final correction:** Section 123.10 distinguishes earlier Eastern Band language planning from the **first New Kituwah immersion classroom opened April 19, 2004**. It distinguishes the Cherokee Nation of Oklahoma's **2001 preservation-school program start** from its subsequent immersion-school expansion. Section 123.5 now reports that the **2019 Cherokee Nation legislative finding estimated approximately 2,000 first-language Cherokee speakers**, not an exact count of all fluent speakers. The chapter retains its qualified treatment of variation across Cherokee communities.

## 6. Scholarly boundaries and residual limitations

- The chapter describes Indigenous languages as parts of living communities and avoids making language proficiency a test of Indigeneity. Claims concerning language loss, school effectiveness and technological progress remain qualified where causation and generalization are difficult.
- The generalized Lakota teaching-materials dispute in the historical-debate feature remains a **qualified example**, not a statement of final adjudicated ownership rights or a finding applicable to every community. It is not necessary to expand it into a separate legal-history subchapter.
- The 2019 Canadian statute, Mexico's 2003 statute, U.S. policy, and Greenlandic/Nunavut rules are compared in their proper jurisdictions; legal recognition is not conflated with actual service availability or speaker restoration.
- Numerical descriptions belong to specified source years and denominators (notably 2019 Cherokee legislative estimate and 2020 Mexican census). They should not be mistaken for real-time 2026 counts.
- Actual geographic map graphics are intentionally left for later insertion. The planned Geography Study remains fully present, and no map omission blocks approval.
- Live publication on maloneuniversity.org, server-side canonical URL behavior, website-wide navigation, and the availability of every external URL in a future browser session were **not** tested. These are deployment-side checks, not unresolved manuscript corrections.

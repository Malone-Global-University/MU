## Independent historical checks

Verified against sources outside the uploaded audit:

1. **13 November 1890:** President Benjamin Harrison ordered a sufficiently large military force to the reservation area. Nebraska State Historical Society, <https://history.nebraska.gov/the-military-buildup-to-wounded-knee/>.
2. **28 versus 29 December 1890:** Spotted Elk's group was intercepted by the Seventh Cavalry on December 28; the Wounded Knee Massacre occurred December 29. U.S. Army Center of Military History, <https://history.army.mil/Research/Reference-Topics/Army-Campaigns/Brief-Summaries/Indian-Wars/>.
3. **1891 photographic reference:** Smithsonian National Anthropological Archives record NAA INV.00357300 identifies a Mooney Ghost Dance photograph, catalogued as Arapaho, Oklahoma, 1891; participants are not individually named. <https://www.si.edu/object/archives/components/sova-naa-photolot-24-ref697>.
4. **1892 church organization:** The Indian Shaker Church's organizing meeting took place June 7, 1892, at Mud Bay; incorporation was in 1910, not 1892. Washington State Historical Society archival description reproduced by SNAC, <https://snaccooperative.org/view/35568497>.
5. **1894 musical recordings:** The Library of Congress identifies James Mooney's July 5–11, 1894 recordings, and its Folklife blog cautions that the voice was likely Mooney's rather than a recording of 1890 Indigenous ceremonial performers. <https://www.loc.gov/item/2014655251/> and <https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2017/11/james-mooney-recordings-ghost-dance-songs/>.
6. **Interpretive scope:** The 1870 and 1890 Ghost Dance movements are distinct but related Paiute religious movements, a framing consistent with Gloria Young's *Oxford Research Encyclopedia* treatment, DOI 10.1093/acrefore/9780199329175.013.955.

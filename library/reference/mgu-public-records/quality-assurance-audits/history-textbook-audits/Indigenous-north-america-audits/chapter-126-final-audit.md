## 3. Scholarly accuracy and correction register

The following prominent claims and dates were independently cross-checked against university, museum, government, specialist, and primary-institution records. This is a targeted historical audit, **not a claim that every sentence has been independently corroborated**.

1. **Santa Fe Indian Market (§126.3):** 1922 was the predecessor Southwest Indian Art Fair and Industrial Arts and Crafts Exposition, organized by the Museum of New Mexico and School of American Research. The chapter distinguishes that from current SWAIA organization rather than projecting present governance backward. [SWAIA history](https://www.swaia.org/history).
2. **Institute of American Indian Arts (§126.4):** establishment on October 1, 1962, supported by IAIA and Bureau of Indian Affairs institutional histories. [IAIA](https://iaia.edu/about/mission/); [BIA contemporaneous notice](https://www.bia.gov/node/49601/printable/pdf).
3. **Maria and Julian Martinez (§126.5):** differentiated vessel formation and polishing, painted designs, family collaboration, black-on-black technique and continuation by later relatives; avoided the mistaken implication that no earlier Pueblo black pottery existed. [Smithsonian Maria](https://americanart.si.edu/artist/maria-martinez-3142); [Smithsonian Julian](https://americanart.si.edu/artist/julian-martinez-3141).
4. **Professional Native Indian Artists Inc. (§126.6):** separated early-1970s organizing/1973 collective work from **formal incorporation April 1, 1975**. [Canada's History](https://www.canadashistory.ca/explore/first-nations-inuit-metis/headwaters-of-their-own-stream); [MacKenzie Art Gallery](https://mackenzie.art/exhibition/7-professional-native-indian-artists-inc/).
5. **Kinngait printmaking (§126.7):** experimentation began before the 1959 first annual print collection; account recognizes Inuit artists, trained printers, co-operative governance and the changing involvement of outside advisers. [Inuit Art Foundation](https://www.inuitartfoundation.org/inuit-art-quarterly/iaq-online/how-has-printmaking-evolved-at-kinngait-studios-in-the-last-sixty-years); [Kinngait Studios](https://www.dorsetfinearts.com/kinngait-studios).
6. **Momaday (§126.10):** *House Made of Dawn* appeared in 1968 and won the **1969 Pulitzer Prize for Fiction**. The chapter explicitly differentiates mainstream recognition from the existence of Indigenous literary traditions. [Pulitzer record](https://www.pulitzer.org/winners/n-scott-momaday).
7. **Maria Campbell (§126.11):** *Halfbreed* was originally published in **1973**, with appropriate separation of Métis autobiographical writing from other national literary traditions. [Bibliographic account](https://gladue.drc.usask.ca/node/6087).
8. **Theytus Books (§126.11):** the 1980 founding is supported by the University of British Columbia library’s archival guide. [UBC guide](https://library.ok.ubc.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/116/2023/02/Theytus-Books-Descriptive-Cards_WebVersion.pdf).
9. **Greenland (§126.12):** Mathias Storch's *Sinnattugaq* (catalogue spelling *Singnagtugaq*) was published **1914**; Niviaq Korneliussen's *HOMO sapienne* first appeared in **2014**. [Inuit Literatures: Storch](https://inuit.uqam.ca/en/documents/singnagtugaq-greenlanders-dream); [Korneliussen](https://inuit.uqam.ca/en/documents/homo-sapienne-0).
10. **U.S. film (§126.14):** *Smoke Signals* (1998), Chris Eyre and Sherman Alexie are correctly identified; the chapter does not falsely call it the first Indigenous-produced film. [Yale Film Archive](https://web.library.yale.edu/film/notes/fn00125).
11. **Inuit cinema (§126.15):** Isuma began in **1990**; <em>Atanarjuat</em> won the **2001 Caméra d’Or**. The chapter distinguishes the Inuit-led production history from outside festival recognition. [IsumaTV](https://www.isuma.tv/atanarjuat/atanarjuat-fast-runner); [Cannes](https://www.festival-cannes.com/en/f/atanarjuat-the-fast-runner/).
12. **Documentary (§126.16):** Alanis Obomsawin’s <em>Kanehsatake: 270 Years of Resistance</em> was released in **1993** and documents the 1990 conflict. [National Film Board of Canada](https://www.nfb.ca/film/kanehsatake_270_years_of_resistance/).
13. **Broadcasting (§126.17):** Inuit Broadcasting Corporation developed in **1981**; CRTC **Decision 99-42 was issued February 22, 1999**; APTN launched **September 1, 1999**. [IBC](https://inuitbroadcasting.ca/); [CRTC](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/archive/1999/db99-42.htm); [APTN](https://www.aptn.org/about/our-story/).
14. **United States arts law (§126.19):** the Indian Arts and Crafts Act of **1990** is accurately characterized as a truth-in-advertising law covering deceptive origin representations, not a universal transfer of Indigenous cultural copyrights. The text distinguishes tribal membership/certification from a gallery’s stylistic judgment. [Interior](https://www.doi.gov/iacb/act); [25 U.S.C. §305e](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-138/t25/s305e/).
15. **Mexico (§126.19):** the federal law addressing Indigenous and Afro-Mexican collective cultural heritage was originally published **January 17, 2022**; the chapter does not conflate it with the U.S. advertising statute and flags the relevance of later amendments. [Diario Oficial](https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/notas/5640770).

**Interpretive safeguards retained:** Indigenous creativity long precedes mainstream recognition; Indigenous artists need not be homogeneous or representative of all Indigenous peoples; community protocols and personal artistic freedom may differ; individual authorship is distinct from institutional ownership and control; films and documentaries are edited arguments as well as cultural artifacts; external finance and regulation can both enable and constrain Indigenous-led production.

## 2. Binding outline and historical scope

The authoritative outline assigns Chapter 122, **“Gaming and the New Tribal Economy,”** to Volume X, Part XXI, between Chapter 121 (*Indigenous Economic Development*) and Chapter 123 (*Language Revitalization*). Its assignment is to explain the rise of tribal gaming, federal law, state compacts, revenue, political controversy, and economic diversification. The revised chapter addresses these subjects without converting itself into a general history of Indigenous enterprise, assigning language revitalization to this chapter, or treating United States law as the sole continental model. Saskatchewan and Ontario are used as substantive Canadian comparisons; Mexico receives limited comparative attention without a false analogy to IGRA.

The five retained historical divisions are:

1. **Origins (§§122.1–122.4):** Indigenous games, reservation economic conditions, Seminole high-stakes bingo, and *Cabazon*.
2. **Federal law (§§122.5–122.8):** IGRA, Classes I–III, regulators, and eligible Indian lands.
3. **Compacts and expansion (§§122.9–122.12):** interstate and tribal-state negotiations, the 1996 *Seminole Tribe* holding, major resorts, regional divergence, and Navajo decision-making.
4. **Revenue and controversy (§§122.13–122.16):** industry accounting, government investments, per capita policy, empirical studies, and social and distributional risks.
5. **Diversification and future (§§122.17–122.20):** investment beyond gaming, Canadian gaming arrangements, mobile and prediction-market law, and final synthesis.

No sections were added, deleted, merged, or renumbered. All 20 section anchors `s122-1` through `s122-20` are present.

## 6. Remaining qualifications and editorial boundary

1. **Not live-site verification.** Tests concern the exact delivered HTML bytes, not publication to maloneuniversity.org. Live-server asset loading, CSP, template integration, font availability, and external target uptime must be checked in the deployment environment. External source URLs were selected from retrieved/verified references, but no continuous link-monitoring is represented.
2. **Time cutoff.** The 2026 event-contract litigation is reported through November 22, 2026. Orders after that date, appeals, stays, final judgments, geofencing compliance, new regulations, and statutory amendments require subsequent updating.
3. **Contested evidence.** Navajo referendum/council history is differentiated by source type, including a contemporary report of a proposed 2004 vote's removal. The final chapter does not pretend internal political disagreements disappeared or that a single secondary retrospective interpretation is unanimous.
4. **Study limitations.** Census working papers and the Great Smoky Mountains Study are not treated as proof of universal, homogeneous gains for all Indigenous nations or households; regional gaming receipts are not equated with profits, tribal distributions, or local poverty rates.
5. **Maps and images.** Actual maps may be inserted separately. The geography-study material is preserved, and the absence of embedded map artwork is **not** counted as a problem or a reason to withhold publication.
6. **Stable-source caveat.** Full documentary references are linked. Source links and any legal/statistical data should be periodically rechecked because web addresses and current case status can change.

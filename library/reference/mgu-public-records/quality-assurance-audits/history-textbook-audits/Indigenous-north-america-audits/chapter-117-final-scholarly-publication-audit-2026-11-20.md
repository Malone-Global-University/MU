## Consolidated historical and legal corrections

1. **§117.5 — inaugural AFN national chief:** Replaced the inaccurate assertion that Delbert (Del) Riley was the first National Chief of the Assembly of First Nations. **David Ahenakew (1982–1985)** was the inaugural AFN National Chief; **Delbert Riley** led the preceding National Indian Brotherhood **1980–1982**. Ahenakew and Riley are now correctly identified in the narrative and the reference apparatus. Primary institutional history: [Assembly of First Nations, Our History](https://afn.ca/about-us/our-history/).
2. **§117.5 — Native Council of Canada:** Distinguishes **preparatory organizing in 1970** from the organization's **formal establishment in 1971**. The main narrative and people reference list agree. Source: [Congress of Aboriginal Peoples, About Us](https://abo-peoples.org/homepage/about-congress-of-aboriginal-peoples/).
3. **§117.2 — enfranchisement versus citizenship:** Removed the formulation that people gave up status in exchange for “full citizenship rights.” The revised text distinguishes loss of federal Indian status and linked rights from the history of political enfranchisement and states that the **1960 federal franchise was obtained without status surrender**. Source: [Elections Canada, A History of the Vote in Canada](https://www.elections.ca/content.aspx/content.aspx?dir=his%2Fchap3&document=index&lang=e&section=res).
4. **§117.13 — Section 35.1 precision:** Replaced the categorical assertion that the constitutional commitment was “not legally enforceable” with precise language describing **the principle of Indigenous participation and the absence of an express Indigenous veto**. The statute is not characterized as a grant of consent authority. Source: [Department of Justice, Constitution Acts](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/const/FullText.html).
5. **§117.4 — White Paper withdrawal:** Retains the established political sequence but uses “by 1971 the Trudeau government had abandoned the proposal,” recognizing retrospective differences over formal withdrawal year without manufacturing an exact administrative event.

## Final scholarly assessment

The chapter's central thesis is retained: recognition under Section 35 was won through Indigenous political mobilization, and neither created pre-existing rights nor removed the unresolved questions of nationhood, treaty implementation, representation, jurisdiction, or self-government. Indigenous women, treaty nations, unceded-territory First Nations, Inuit, and Métis are differentiated without implying uniform political representation. All five divisions follow the plan, and the direct historical narrative is sufficiently developed for first-edition textbook publication.

## 3. Authoritative verification of chronology and statistics

**Confirmed or substantially supported:**

1. The Seminole Tribe of Florida opened the modern high-stakes Hollywood bingo hall in **December 1979**; *Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Butterworth* was decided in 1981. Indigenous and historical sources independently support the timeline.
2. *California v. Cabazon Band of Mission Indians*, 480 U.S. 202 (**February 25, 1987**), correctly anchors the criminal/prohibitory versus civil/regulatory distinction and federal/tribal preemption analysis.
3. IGRA was signed **October 17, 1988**. The draft correctly emphasizes that gaming existed before IGRA and that legislation both recognized and constrained its development.
4. *Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida*, 517 U.S. 44 (**March 27, 1996**), correctly addresses state sovereign immunity and loss of the IGRA good-faith lawsuit against nonconsenting states; it did not repeal the statutory good-faith obligation.
5. *Rincon Band of Luiseno Mission Indians v. Schwarzenegger*, 602 F.3d 1019 (**2010**), supports the account that revenue-sharing demands require meaningful concessions and may evidence bad-faith bargaining.
6. *Texas v. United States*, 497 F.3d 491 (**Fifth Circuit 2007**), supports the discussion that regulations designed to supply secretarial procedures after *Seminole Tribe* faced a major circuit-level limitation. Do not describe this Fifth Circuit holding as nationwide invalidation of every secretarial procedure.
7. Hard Rock International purchase: **approximately $965 million**, announced in December 2006 and completed in **March 2007**; the quoted approximate price is well supported.
8. The National Indian Gaming Commission's **July 21, 2026** release reports **$46.2 billion FY 2025 gross gaming revenue**, **5.3%** increase, **545 establishments**, **246 tribes**, **29 states**, with **seven of eight regions** increasing. This is **gross gaming revenue, not profit or distributions**.
9. NIGC regional figures reported for FY 2025—Sacramento roughly **$12.6 billion**, Washington, D.C. region roughly **$11.2 billion**, Rapid City roughly **$440 million**, down about **0.9%**—are consistent with published region summaries. When publishing, link directly to the NIGC region report for the exact counts and the draft's concentration claim (**46 establishments / ~56% of total**); those latter detailed breakdowns were not fully recomputed independently here.
10. Saskatchewan's **August 31, 2026** amendment extended SIGA's online-gaming exclusivity through **September 2029**, authorized a Flying Dust First Nation pilot of **up to 49 slots**, and increased the provincial slot allocation to **2,619**. Direct provincial confirmation.
11. SIGA casino net profits are distributed **50% First Nations Trust; 25% community development corporations; 25% Saskatchewan General Revenue Fund**. Online profits follow a **50% First Nations / 50% provincial** formula. The draft identifies the three casino beneficiaries but omits the readily verifiable exact distribution; add it.
12. *West Flagler Associates v. Haaland* and the Interior's **February 2024** Class III compact regulations are accurately distinguished at a high level: IGRA does not by itself authorize off-Indian-lands wagers merely because compact language deems them made at a server; state law is separately relevant. Link the actual rule and appellate opinion.
13. The draft's Laurel Wheeler **2023** and Akee/Jones/Simeonova **2025** Census research references match genuine Census working papers. Do not represent working-paper associations as conclusive nationwide causal effects outside their research designs.
14. The Great Smoky Mountains Study began before Harrah's Cherokee opened and has been used to study effects of income changes on the Eastern Band of Cherokee families; the draft generally presents its findings with suitable qualification.
15. Casino Rama opened in **1996** and its proceeds became the subject of disputes over sharing among Ontario First Nations and the host community. Source in the Ontario Court of Appeal and the Supreme Court of Canada; expand nuance if presenting host-community entitlement.

## 4. Required publication-critical corrections

### A. §122.12 — Navajo referendums (**material factual error**)

**Current wording:** "The Navajo Nation ... approved casinos only after a series of referendums."

**Correction:** Navajo voters **rejected** gaming referendums in **1994** and **1997**. The **Navajo Nation Council** adopted a gaming ordinance in **2001**; a proposed **2004** referendum was removed from the ballot; federal regulatory documents report **Fire Rock Navajo Casino opened November 19, 2008**. The chapter should use this case to illustrate political disagreements and the different legal significance of voter referendums and council enactments. Do **not** imply a sequence of successful referendums.

### B. §122.6 — Class III statutory alternatives (**legal precision**)

Current section says three conditions include that Class III gaming "must be conducted according to a tribal-state compact." Explain that a **tribal-state compact is the ordinary route**, and IGRA additionally contemplates **Secretarial Procedures** in the specified statutory circumstances, with subsequent procedural disputes after *Seminole Tribe* and *Texas v. United States*. Cross-reference §122.10. Avoid misrepresenting a state as having absolute veto power in all conceivable cases.

### C. §122.19 — Distinguish prediction-market case holdings and update through November 2026 (**publication-critical currency**)

- **May 11, 2026, Ho-Chunk Nation v. Kalshi:** The Western District of Wisconsin found the IGRA claim likely to succeed but **denied a preliminary injunction for insufficient evidence of irreparable harm**. September 11, 2026, certification for interlocutory appeal is separately relevant. Likelihood of success is not a final merits decision.
- **August 28, 2026, Ninth Circuit Nevada proceeding:** State regulatory/preemption question, not a blanket judicial holding affirming tribal jurisdiction. Distinguish it clearly from the California tribal-land case.
- **September 16, 2026, Blue Lake Rancheria v. Kalshi:** Ninth Circuit ruling in favor of two tribes' preliminary-injunction claims (Blue Lake Rancheria and Chicken Ranch Rancheria of Me-Wuk Indians); do not present as final judgment or as an order immediately imposing all requested relief.
- **November 4, 2026:** On remand, the district court granted a **preliminary injunction barring specified Kalshi sports event contracts on the plaintiff tribes' Indian lands**. This is a significant outcome before the chapter's November 2026 cutoff and must be added. The initial litigation included a third tribe that left the appeal; identify the current plaintiffs accurately. If mentioning later compliance/geofencing reports, verify their precise status as of the cutoff.
- Record the **Commodity Exchange Act vs IGRA preemption** dispute and the possibility of different outcomes in different circuits without declaring the overall question finally resolved.

### D. §§122.4, 122.5, 122.10 — Primary-source text and direct documentary linking

The three windows currently give accurate paraphrases but no clickable source documents. Each must link to its *exact judicial opinion or statutory text*; add a short, verified excerpt from the actual public-domain decision/statute where instructional value warrants it, distinguished from the editor's paraphrase. The draft need not introduce long quotations. The same rule applies to the NIGC industry data, revenue-allocation law, Census research, 2024 Interior rule, and 2026 Saskatchewan amendment. The manuscript currently has **zero external links**.

### E. §122.18 — SIGA financial distribution precision

The draft's recipient list is correct but incomplete. For gaming proceeds under the Saskatchewan framework, state the **50/25/25 casino-profit allocation**, and distinguish it from **50/50 online-gaming profits**. Identify the distribution as **net profits/income**, not gross wagering receipts or aggregate revenue.

### F. §122.20 — Expand concluding synthesis moderately

The section has **296 words of direct history**, far below most other sections. Extend by about two substantive paragraphs that compare the cases and demonstrate the relationship among fiscal capacity, self-government, dependency, market geography, and responsible governmental investment. Do not duplicate Chapter 121's general enterprise history or inflate the chapter elsewhere.

## 5. Additional precisions (non-blocking but suitable for consolidated pass)

- **§122.9:** A tribal-state gaming compact has contractual and intergovernmental characteristics; calling it a "treaty" should be framed explicitly as an analogy, not the same legal category as historical federal Indian treaties.
- **§122.7:** Clarify that the **NIGC chair** approves ordinances and management contracts according to statutory allocation of powers; the Secretary of the Interior reviews tribal-state compacts. These are distinct institutions. Attribute regulatory roles precisely.
- **§122.12:** The claim that about 46 large properties generated roughly 56% of GGR is plausible from reported NIGC category tables, but independently anchor both numerator and denominator in the **FY 2025 report**, not unattributed press summaries.
- **§122.15:** Distinguish the source and timing of direct per capita payments in the Great Smoky Mountains Study from casino opening, and avoid generalizing the study's findings to all tribes or households.
- **§122.16:** Tie poverty rates to their statistical reference years and denominators; note that a nationwide American Indian poverty percentage is not the poverty rate for citizens of gaming tribes.
- **§122.18:** Casino Rama's proceeds and the host Nation's claims were contested; do not imply its original revenue formula was always uncontroversial. The present short summary is fair but can be historically richer.

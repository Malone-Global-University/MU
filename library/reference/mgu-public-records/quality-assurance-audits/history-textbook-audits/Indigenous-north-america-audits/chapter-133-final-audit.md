## 4. Source verification and historical corrections

The following were independently researched against accessible official documents, reliable historical publications, or peer-reviewed research and integrated into the revised narrative. This is a **targeted scholarly verification**, not a claim that every sentence of a 24,000-word instructional file was externally verified.

### 4.1 Si'ahl / “Chief Seattle” speech

The source window distinguishes Henry A. Smith's version published in the *Seattle Sunday Star* on **October 29, 1887**, from the substantially rewritten ecological version associated with screenwriter **Ted Perry around 1970**. The exact original wording and chain of translation are not recoverable with confidence. The source window therefore uses labeled paraphrase and explicitly discusses attribution and evidentiary limitations.

Primary checks: [University of Washington, Center for the Study of the Pacific Northwest](https://sites.uw.edu/cspn/resources/reading-the-region/texts-by-and-about-natives/texts-by-and-about-natives-commentary/5-two-versions-of-chief-seattles-speech/); [U.S. National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/publications/prologue/1985/spring/chief-seattle.html); [HistoryLink's publication history](https://www.historylink.org/File/1427).

### 4.2 Bowhead whale estimates and the Alaska Eskimo Whaling Commission

The manuscript characterizes the **600–2,000** bowhead estimate as a **historical late-1970s assessment**, not a contemporary census. It separates the commission's origins in **1977** from its **1981** incorporation and cooperative management agreement, and it explains why hunter observations were relevant to subsequent counting methods without implying that all disputes were resolved by one epistemology defeating another.

Checks: [International Whaling Commission, Alaska subsistence whaling](https://iwc.int/management-and-conservation/whaling/aboriginal/usa/alaska); [Cultural Survival, Iñupiat subsistence hunting](https://www.culturalsurvival.org/publications/cultural-survival-quarterly/subsistence-hunting-activities-and-inupiat-eskimo).

### 4.3 Menominee termination, restoration, and forest governance

The revision retains **1954** as the year the Menominee Termination Act was enacted, **April 30, 1961** as termination's effective date, and **December 22, 1973** as the date the Restoration Act was signed. It links the dates to governance and resource management, rather than presenting them as disconnected chronology.

Checks: [Milwaukee Public Museum](https://www.mpm.edu/node/28079); [Menominee Indian Tribe historical timeline](https://menominee-nsn.gov/CulturePages/HistoricalInfo/TimeLine).

### 4.4 Northwest Coast clam-garden chronology

The chapter uses the studied **northern Quadra Island** sites as the specific basis for the finding that **some** clam gardens are over **3,500 years** old. It distinguishes archaeological dating of the studied features from a continent-wide claim and explains why radiocarbon evidence, sea-level change, and depositional context matter.

Check: [Smith et al., “3500 years of shellfish mariculture on the Northwest Coast of North America,” *PLOS ONE* (2019)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0211194).

### 4.5 Bears Ears and the durability of Indigenous participation

The revised governance narrative covers the **2016** monument designation, the **June 2022** cooperative agreement, **Presidential Proclamation 11043 of July 13, 2026**, and the **November 18, 2026** litigation filed by tribal nations. It states that the proclamation reduced the reserved monument area to approximately **121,096 acres** and purported to end the Bears Ears Commission, while noting that the legal dispute remains unresolved at the chapter's cutoff. It does not represent litigation as a completed court judgment.

Checks: [U.S. Department of the Interior, 2022 agreement](https://www.doi.gov/pressreleases/blm-forest-service-and-five-tribes-bears-ears-commission-commit-historic-co-management); [Government Publishing Office, Proclamation 11043](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/DCPD-202600459); [Native American Rights Fund, Bears Ears litigation](https://narf.org/cases/bears-ears/).

### 4.6 Federal recognition of Indigenous knowledge

The manuscript identifies the **November 30, 2022** OSTP–CEQ guidance and **2024** NOAA guidance as historically documented publications, while distinguishing agency guidance from statutes and declining to assume identical implementation across agencies or subsequent administrations.

Checks: [Department of the Interior background on Indigenous Knowledge guidance](https://www.bia.gov/service/tribal-consultations/departmental-policy-indigenous-knowledge); [NOAA 2024 guidance](https://www.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/2024-05/NOAA_Indigenous%20Knowledge_Guidance_with_Appendix_FINAL_e-signed%205-17-24%20%282%29.pdf).

### 4.7 Additional refinements

- The original manuscript's generalized statement about Inuit, Yupik, Iñupiat, and Unangan political representation was qualified to distinguish the Inuit Circumpolar Council from separate Unangan/Aleut institutions.
- The chronology distinguishes **Melvin Gilmore's 1914 dissertation** from the **1919 publication** of *Uses of Plants by the Indians of the Missouri River Region* and preserves **1917** for Gilbert Wilson's work with Maxi'diwiac.
- The regional discussion distinguishes documented Indigenous environmental traditions from modern extrapolations about alleged uniform ecological harmony.
- The chapter separates Indigenous environmental authority from consultation, advisory participation, and the use of information in an agency assessment.

The closing summary and review materials were retained, reconciled with the expanded chapter, and supplemented where the reorganization introduced significant new events. **Actual maps remain a later production task under the user's established MGU workflow; their absence is not treated as a manuscript deficiency.**

## 7. Remaining limits and deployment-stage verification

This audit does **not** claim to have examined every linked historical work in full, independently verified every interpretive assertion, tested every browser and accessibility technology, or confirmed the chapter after uploading it to the live MGU website. Published government guidance, administrative arrangements, and litigation can change after the November 25, 2026 cutoff; those passages should be reviewed if the chapter is substantially updated later.

The source register provides a route for future detailed verification, but selected older ethnographies should be consulted in their complete original editions if a later revision introduces new direct quotations or detailed claims about restricted cultural practices. Sensitive cultural knowledge was not reproduced from unverified private or restricted sources.

Live-site deployment checks should confirm the file path, canonical/public URL according to MGU policy, any hosting-specific restrictions on inline JavaScript, accessibility and responsive behavior in the production CSS environment, and the intended links to adjacent chapters. No live publication or deployment action has been taken as part of this editorial revision.

## 4. Historical and documentary revisions

1. **Minneapolis founding and AIM Patrol:** Expanded details of neighborhood networks, founding participants, women organizers, patrol financing and practice, and evidentiary limits of self-reported outcomes.
2. **Independent institutions:** Established the March 1970 Legal Rights Center founders and clarified AIM's collaboration with other communities; distinguished the Indian Health Board and Little Earth from programs owned and operated by AIM.
3. **Survival schools:** Expanded the January 1972 founding, community control, parental participation, school finance, staffing, and child welfare concerns; treated school and curricular outcomes as historical questions, not unqualified movement accomplishments.
4. **Sovereignty and organizing:** Distinguished tribal governments' legal powers from AIM's intertribal advocacy; expanded chapter autonomy, organizational accountability, women activists, WARN, and the International Indian Treaty Council's June 1974 origins.
5. **Land actions and regional networks:** Documented the August 14, 1971 McKinley Coast Guard station occupation and May 17, 1971 Naval Air Station action; distinguished AIM from the independently governed Indians of All Tribes occupation at Alcatraz.
6. **1972–1973:** Placed Raymond Yellow Thunder, the Trail of Broken Treaties, and Wounded Knee in AIM's institutional development while reserving detailed event histories for Chapters 111–112.
7. **Judicial findings:** Corrected the 1976 joint trial and acquittal of Robert Robideau and Darrell Butler, distinguishing Peltier's separate 1977 conviction. Clarified September 1974 Banks–Means disposition and the individual Aquash murder convictions; did not assert unproven organizational guilt.
8. **Contemporary legal update:** Precisely described the January 19, 2025 commutation of Peltier's remaining imprisonment to home confinement beginning February 18, 2025. A commutation is **not** a pardon or reversal of conviction.
9. **Longest Walk:** Explained the February 11–July 15, 1978 chronology and its coalition and logistical significance without attributing every subsequent reform to AIM.

## 5. Primary-source audit

- The first-board and AIM Patrol images are by **Roger Woo**; MNopedia credits the **American Indian Movement Interpretative Center**, not Minnesota Historical Society as holder of those two photographs.
- The May 17, 1971 Naval Air Station image is documented in the Minnesota Historical Society's newspaper-negative holdings; a photographer is **not established** by the consulted description.
- The survival-school pamphlet is explicitly identified as an **undated item in a composite digitized scan**; its original edition and individual author remain unverified. The chapter does not invent them or manufacture quotations.
- Vernon Bellecourt's March 15, 1973 interview is identified with interviewer **Lincoln Bergman** and its Pacifica archive record. The prose does not pretend that the entire broadcast was transcribed and analyzed word-for-word.
- The 1978 Smithsonian Longest Walk poster is identified as **NMAAHC 2014.183.3**, with creator **unidentified**.
- Each of the six windows includes a direct archival or catalog URL; the bibliography includes verified publication years for major books, legal decisions, institutions, and additional source links.

**Research limit:** A reliable source catalog, excerpt, or historical summary is not a substitute for inspection of every original manuscript, audio minute, or archival photograph. The chapter uses transparent descriptions and labeled interpretations where complete originals were not analyzed. A full scholarly peer review and exhaustive third-party URL availability check were not performed.

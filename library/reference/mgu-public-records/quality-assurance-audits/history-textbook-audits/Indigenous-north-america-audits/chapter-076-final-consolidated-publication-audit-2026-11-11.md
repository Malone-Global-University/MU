## 4. Historical corrections incorporated directly

### 4.1. Article VI — citizenship and 160-acre patent category (major)

Claude correctly treated the 320-acre family-head and 80-acre non-head selections but omitted an additional operative clause. The revised §76.11 explains the **distinct** process for certain adult male treaty participants who established qualifying homesteads outside the designated reservation, with required improvements, a three-year occupancy period, evidence, 160-acre patent eligibility, and citizenship expressly consequent on the issuance of a patent. This does not confer citizenship on every treaty participant or transform the reservation selections automatically into private patent grants. Source Window III now includes this clause.

The phrase “head of a family” is no longer presented as explicitly restricting 320-acre selections to men: that was an administrative/gendered inference, not a limitation stated in Article VI itself.

### 4.2. Separate 1868 treaties: Crow and Northern Cheyenne–Northern Arapaho

- **Crow treaty:** May 7, 1868; ratified July 25; proclaimed August 12. [OSU text](https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-crows-1868-1008).
- **Northern Cheyenne/Northern Arapaho treaty:** May 10, 1868; ratified July 25; proclaimed August 25. [OSU text](https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-northern-cheyenne-and-northern-arapaho-1868-1012).
- The latter treaty's Article II presents **two named reservation destinations** but also **three agency choices** (Medicine Lodge Creek agency, Missouri River agency near Fort Randall, Crow agency near Otter Creek). Its hunting right was explicitly conditional. §76.8 now distinguishes residence options from agency affiliations rather than compressing all choices into two destinations.
- Neither separate treaty is to be treated as an annex to the principal Lakota instrument. Their 1868 ratification dates differ from the principal agreement's February 16, 1869 ratification and February 24 proclamation.

### 4.3. Whetstone, Red Cloud, and Spotted Tail agency chronology

Checked against the [South Dakota State Archives Indian agency register](https://history.sd.gov/archives/data/archives/project.aspx) and [National Archives agency collection](https://www.archives.gov/kansas-city/holdings/rg-050-099.html):

- The existing **Upper Platte Agency** moved to Whetstone Creek on the Missouri in **December 1868** and was renamed the **Whetstone Agency in 1869**; its earlier administrative history should not be erased by calling it newly established.
- The **Red Cloud Agency** was established in **1871** near Fort Laramie and moved to northwestern Nebraska in **1873**.
- The Brulé-associated Whetstone administration moved in **1871**, adopted the name **Spotted Tail Agency in 1874**, underwent further moves, and later became the **Rosebud Agency in 1878**.
- Specific relocations and administrative jurisdictions overlapped; agencies were not synonymous with tribal membership or reservation boundaries.

### 4.4. 1870 Red Cloud speech

The [Smithsonian National Museum of the American Indian case study](https://americanindian.si.edu/nk360/plains-treaties-fort-laramie/) identifies its source as *New York Times*, **June 17, 1870**, reporting a speech at Cooper Union. The revised manuscript retains cautious attribution as translated, edited contemporary reporting, **not** a verbatim Lakota-language transcript. The speech's message regarding treaty explanation and the Missouri agency is supported.

### 4.5. 1876 agreement, 1877 act and 1980 Supreme Court

Verified against [United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/448/371), 448 U.S. 371 (June 30, 1980): the Court reports that **only approximately ten percent of adult male Sioux** signed the 1876 agreement, below Article XII's three-fourths requirement. Congress's **February 28, 1877 act** implemented the disputed agreement. The Court sustained a Fifth Amendment just-compensation conclusion rather than ordering restoration of the Black Hills.

The phrase beginning **“A more ripe and rank case of dishonorable dealings”** originates in a **Court of Claims** judgment, subsequently quoted by the Supreme Court; the revised §76.19 states that attribution precisely. The discussion of *Lone Wolf v. Hitchcock* (1903) now explicitly identifies the **distinct Kiowa–Comanche 1867 Medicine Lodge Treaty** context, rather than implying the case adjudicated the Black Hills itself. [Lone Wolf opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/187/553/).

### 4.6. 1889–1890 reservation division

The **Act of March 2, 1889** required subsequent consent satisfying Article XII and presidential proclamation before it became effective. Signatures were collected in **1889**; President Benjamin Harrison accepted the commission's evidence of sufficient consent and issued the operative proclamation on **February 10, 1890**. This is different from treating the 1889 law as instantly implementing division. The manuscript and chronology now distinguish the conditional act, signatures, contested circumstances, and proclamation. [Presidential proclamation](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/proclamation-295-sioux-nation-indians); [contemporaneous presidential report](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/message-congress-reporting-the-great-sioux-reservation).

## 5. Other audit findings and editorial decisions

- Principal treaty signing **April 29, 1868**, Red Cloud's signing **November 6, 1868**, principal Senate ratification **February 16, 1869**, and proclamation **February 24, 1869** are correct. The article does not falsely represent every signatory as participating on April 29. [Standing Rock Sioux Tribe's treaty text](https://www.standingrock.org/about/history/fort-laramie-treaty/).
- Red Cloud and Spotted Tail did not exercise sole authority over all Lakota, Dakota, Northern Cheyenne, and Northern Arapaho communities. The chapter preserves band and nation distinctions.
- Article II reservation, Article XVI unceded country, and Article XI off-reservation hunting remain three different legal-geographic categories throughout the narrative and mapping exercises.
- The Black Hills are correctly treated as within Article II's protected reservation area; the Powder River country is not casually relabeled reservation land.
- The 1871 prohibition on entering new Indian treaties is not falsely described as invalidating treaties previously ratified.
- The five source windows remain accurately labeled **paraphrases/source studies**, not fabricated verbatim quotations. Documentary edition hyperlinks were added to each window; this improves reproducibility without falsely claiming facsimile collation.
- The bibliography's three principal modern monographs are identifiable: Jeffrey Ostler (2004), Pekka Hämäläinen (2019), Jeffrey Ostler (2010). Verified documentary edition links now supplement the reading register. Page-level cross-indexing of secondary claims and exhaustive comparison to archival original manuscripts remain separate from this first-edition audit.
- Legal analysis is historically explanatory; it should not be treated as modern legal advice about a pending matter.

## 7. Limitations and source integrity disclosures

1. **Primary-source status:** The five source windows are documented, attributed editorial paraphrases and methodological comparisons. They are not authenticated facsimile-collated original-language transcripts and should not be represented as such.
2. **Source verification:** Verification concentrated on the controlling treaty articles, key signatory dates, agency chronology, leading museum and archives sources, the 1877/1980 legal sequence, and cited judicial language. It does not certify every inferential sentence against every possible specialized monograph or original administrative manuscript.
3. **Historical mapping:** The geography activity is text-guided and requires a separate historical atlas/map. No embedded authoritative map plate is claimed.
4. **Template parity:** The original Chapter 1 baseline file was not independently supplied alongside the uploaded chapter. Visual and structural continuity with the MGU shell was confirmed, but a byte-exact original CSS/JavaScript diff cannot be certified.
5. **Deployment:** The standalone HTML passes browser checks; installation and behavior on the live MGU domain were not tested.
6. **Later political/legal debates:** The 1980 court judgment and the reported 1889 acceptance threshold are distinguished from broader tribal evaluations and disputes over consent, land restitution, and treaty obligations.

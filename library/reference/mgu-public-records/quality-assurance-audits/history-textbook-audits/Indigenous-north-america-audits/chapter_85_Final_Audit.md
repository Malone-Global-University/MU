### 4. Publication-critical historical corrections applied

**1881 agricultural statute (§85.11, Source Window 3, chronology):** The Act assented to **March 21, 1881**, *An Act to amend “The Indian Act, 1880”*, **S.C. 1881, c. 17 (44 Vict.), ss. 1–3**, authorizes the Governor in Council to make and publish regulations restricting specified transfers of reserve-grown produce in Manitoba, the North-West Territories and Keewatin. It did **not** itself impose an across-the-board Indian-agent written-permission rule. Section 2 creates penalties relating to transactions contrary to such regulations; section 3 concerns seizure under them. Narrative, source paraphrase, metadata, student questions and chronology were corrected. Verified text: https://www.sac-isc.gc.ca/eng/1100100010280/1618939069442

**1884 potlatch amendment (§85.13, Source Window 4, chronology):** **S.C. 1884, c. 27 (47 Vict.), s. 3** received assent **April 19, 1884** but began operating **January 1, 1885**. The narrative and metadata now distinguish enactment and commencement. Sources: https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2018/aanc-inac/R32-384-1981-eng.pdf and historical analysis at https://ojs.library.ubc.ca/index.php/bcstudies/article/download/185830/185137

**Cranmer Potlatch (§85.13):** Corrected to **45 people charged**, **22 suspended sentences**, **three remanded on appeal**, **20 imprisoned for two or three months**, and approximately **750 confiscated objects**, as reported by the Canadian Museum for Human Rights and U'mista Cultural Centre. Distinguishes actual sentences from the original statute's 2–6-month penalty range. Sources: https://humanrights.ca/story/bringing-potlatch-home and https://www.umista.ca/pages/collection-history

**Mary Two-Axe Earley (§85.16 Everyday Life):** Corrected her marriage to Edward Earley to **1938**; documents his death in **1969**, her difficulty claiming the Kahnawà:ke home inherited from her grandparents, her public organizing beginning in 1966, and the later 1985 legislative reform. Park Canada's biographical record: https://parks.canada.ca/culture/designation/personnage-person/mary-two-axe-earley

**Compulsory enfranchisement (§85.7):** Retains the documented **1920 → 1922 → 1933** legislative sequence, with specific identification of **S.C. 1932–33, c. 42, s. 7** and a clear separation between what law allowed and what officials can be shown to have done. It remains distinct from marriage-based loss of status. Cross-checked against Canadian legal historical overviews; extensive enforcement and numerical claims are not invented.

**1927 land-claims funding (§85.18):** Retains the accurate distinction between prohibiting fundraising for claims without approval and banning legal representation generally; clarified assent in source metadata as **March 31, 1927**.

5. **Review process:** Verification was targeted to identified material risks rather than exhaustive review of every assertion, translation or provincial statute in the manuscript.

## 3. Targeted historical verification

- **Taylor et al. (2023), Science:** radiocarbon/genetic/isotope/osteological findings support pre-1680 Indigenous presence and management of horses in the central Plains and northern Rockies. Specific sites, including Paa’ko, are distinguished from automatic dates of adoption by whole nations. [Original study](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adc9691) and [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36996225/).
- **1581–1591 Spanish expeditions:** Chamuscado–Rodríguez (1581), Espejo (1582–1583), and Castaño de Sosa (1590–1591) are documented expeditions in the Pueblo region. These are examples of exposure, not demonstrated origins of sustained Indigenous horse breeding. [Galisteo Basin archaeological history](https://galisteo.nmarchaeology.org/history/spanish-colonial-period.html); [Texas State Historical Association](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/rodriguez-sanchez-expedition).
- **St. Denis, Natchitoches:** trade and diplomatic activity after the 1713–1714 establishment is supported. [Dictionary of Canadian Biography](https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/juchereau_de_saint_denis_louis_3E.html?print=1); [Texas State Historical Association](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/st-denis-louis-juchereau-de).
- **Bourgmont and Padouca (1724):** expedition is documented; *Padouca* identity is not stable. Early descriptions may refer to Plains Apache, other uses to Comanche, and attributions remain debated. [University Press of Colorado](https://read.upcolorado.com/read/new-mexico-and-the-pimeria-alta-the-colonial-period-in-the-american-southwest/section/76adc0a8-9e82-4dda-810c-ac36b7268da8); [Smithsonian historical study](https://repository.si.edu/bitstream/handle/10088/15468/bulletin1741959smit.pdf). The final file replaces one overly confident assignment.
- **Ute and Shoshone horse histories:** evidence supports substantial horse acquisition and networks during the later seventeenth and eighteenth centuries; dates remain regionally variable. [Utah History to Go](https://historytogo.utah.gov/post/ute-indians/); [Southern Ute tribal chronology](https://www.southernute-nsn.gov/history/chronology/); [National Museum of the American Indian horse trading](https://americanindian.si.edu/exhibitions/horsenation/trading.html).
- **Nez Perce equestrian societies:** horse use, breeding and 1805 Lewis–Clark encounter documented; 1805 evidence cannot establish exact first adoption. [USDA Forest Service trail plan](https://www.fs.usda.gov/Internet/FSE_DOCUMENTS/fseprd1236469.pdf); [NPS Nez Perce](https://www.nps.gov/nepe/learn/historyculture/lewis-and-clark.htm).
- **Crow/Hidatsa and Missouri River villages:** Crow–Hidatsa historical relationships and equestrian expansion supported while precise split chronology remains uncertain. Mandan smallpox in **1781** and Hidatsa village continuities supported. [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/knri/learn/historyculture/kniferiverpeople.htm); [Yale eHRAF](https://ehrafworldcultures.yale.edu/cultures/nq10/summary).
- **Buffalo Bird Woman / Gilbert L. Wilson:** source is dated 1917 and records a Hidatsa woman’s memory of nineteenth-century labor and horse management; chapter treats it as later testimony rather than direct seventeenth-century evidence. [Original text](https://digital.library.upenn.edu/women/buffalo/garden/garden.html); [North Dakota Studies horse lessons](https://www.ndstudies.gov/gr8/node/246).
- **Ecological history:** Hämäläinen’s 2003 critique of horse-centered economic/ecological vulnerability and Dan Flores’s southern Plains bison interpretation are distinct but compatible research emphases; chapter acknowledges evidence limits and does not invent herd sizes. [Hämäläinen 2003](https://academic.oup.com/jah/article-abstract/90/3/833/688119); [Hämäläinen 2001 historiographic discussion](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/greatplainsquarterly/2227/).
- **Villasur, Henday, Saukamappee, Lakota winter counts:** prior independently corrected source descriptions remain intact: Villasur 1720, Henday 1754–1755 later copied journals, Thompson-mediated Saukamappee account, Long Soldier dated winter-count example. None are converted into spurious eyewitness quotations.

## 4. Final three narrowly scoped corrections

1. **Historical context:** clarify the nature of Spanish restrictions as unauthorized Indigenous **riding** and regulated horse access, rather than assert an identical general ban on all Indigenous ownership.
2. **§49.5 (Quivira):** replace an unsupported absolute “first Plains encounter” with the defensible “documented early Plains encounter”.
3. **§49.10 (Padouca):** explicitly state the contested Apache/Comanche referents instead of a confident uniform Apache identification.

All original additions otherwise remain. Feature markup, CSS, JavaScript, headings, navigation, IDs, and review are preserved.

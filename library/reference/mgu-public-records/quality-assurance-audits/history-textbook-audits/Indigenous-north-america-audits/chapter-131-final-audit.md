## 1. Governing assignment and boundaries

The authoritative outline assigns Chapter 131 to **adoption, foster care, the Indian Child Welfare Act, Canadian child-removal policies, and Indigenous control over family services**. The completed manuscript follows that scope in five divisions and all twenty planned historical sections (§§131.1–131.20). It substantially examines Indigenous kinship and customary care; state and provincial child welfare systems; the Indian Adoption Project and Sixties Scoop; Indigenous resistance; ICWA's enactment and legal operation; Canadian human-rights and jurisdictional reforms; and the operation of Indigenous-led child and family services.

The chapter stays within its assigned division of historical labor. It briefly uses residential and boarding schools as background but does not reproduce Chapters 127–128. It does not displace Chapter 130 on criminal justice or Chapter 132 on historical memory, monuments, and decolonization. References to Chapters 114, 117, 118, 140, 142–144, and other established chapters are compatible with the supplied 150-chapter outline. No changes were made to the book-level architecture.

### Additional historical claims checked and retained

- **Indian Adoption Project:** a contemporaneous evaluation reported **395 placements** through December 31, 1967, for the 1958–1967 project. The manuscript contextualizes this as one documented program rather than the total number of Indigenous adoptions. [Historical evaluation transcribed by University of Oregon](https://pages.uoregon.edu/adoption/archive/LysloIAP.htm).
- **Historical 25–35% removal estimate:** the 1969 and 1974 Association on American Indian Affairs surveys were cited by the U.S. Supreme Court in *Mississippi Band of Choctaw Indians v. Holyfield*. The manuscript appropriately flags the estimate's historical and methodological scope instead of treating it as a current statistic. [*Holyfield*, 490 U.S. 30 (1989)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/490/30).
- **ICWA statutory quotation:** the Primary Source Window's short phrase “alarmingly high percentage” appears in the 1978 congressional findings. The surrounding interpretation is labeled as a paraphrase. [Public Law 95-608 / 25 U.S.C. §1901](https://www.ssa.gov/OP_Home/comp2/F095-608.html).
- **Spallumcheen Band child-welfare bylaw:** the **1980** enactment is documented, and the chapter uses it as an early assertion of Indigenous authority rather than proof of unconstrained implementation. [Splatsin child and family services](https://splatsin.ca/programs-services/children-families).
- **Dakota Ojibway Child and Family Services:** the agency reports a **July 1, 1981** mandate to deliver services. [Agency history](https://www.docfs.org/about).
- **Alaska Tribal Child Welfare Compact:** signed in **October 2017**, effective **December 15, 2017**. The manuscript's year is correct. [Alaska legislative year-one briefing](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/get_documents.asp?docid=34829&session=31).
- **U.S. Supreme Court in *Haaland v. Brackeen*:** the manuscript correctly distinguishes the Court's disposition of congressional-power and anticommandeering arguments from the equal-protection question left undecided for lack of standing. [U.S. Supreme Court opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/22pdf/21-376_7l48.pdf).

These checks focused on material facts, legal doctrines, contested dates, figures, and named institutional milestones. They **do not constitute line-by-line external corroboration of every historical observation** in the 20 sections.

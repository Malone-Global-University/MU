## Executive determination

The submitted chapter was substantively aligned with the binding chapter outline. A single consolidated revision preserved all twenty numbered sections in five divisions, retained the instructional apparatus, and corrected the documentary and statutory issues identified in the prepublication audit. Selective expansion strengthened the negotiation, land-conveyance, shareholder, revenue-sharing, subsistence and 1988–1992 reform sections. The first primary-source window now identifies an actual 1966 conference document and quotes only a short excerpt verified through an archival guide; the window explicitly distinguishes that evidence from the unseen complete original report.

A further historical error, **not listed in the preceding audit**, was caught and corrected: the original historical context incorrectly claimed that the Indian Claims Commission did not cover Alaska. The Indian Claims Commission Act of 1946 expressly included Alaska, and ANCSA itself contemplated extinguishing certain claims pending before that body. The final wording distinguishes the availability of monetary claims proceedings from a comprehensive land-and-corporate settlement.

### Documentary and interpretive cautions retained

- The 1966 primary-source window quotes an archival guide's excerpt of a **named 1966 policy statement**. This identifies a genuine primary record, but **does not amount to an independent examination of the full archived original**. The window states that limitation explicitly.
- Corporate title to land, membership as a corporate shareholder, and political authority as a tribal government remain **three separate concepts** throughout.
- The chapter evaluates ANCSA as a historically consequential achievement with structural limits—not simply an oil-industry agreement, a generic reservation treaty, or a complete resolution of Indigenous sovereignty and subsistence.
- Where claims are based on the original 1971 Act, the chapter distinguishes original text from later amendments; the linked U.S. Code version contains subsequent changes and its amendment notes must be consulted accordingly.

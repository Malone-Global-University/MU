## 4. Documentary historical verification

The following items were checked against retrievable historical documentary resources and recognized institutional or scholarly discussions:

1. **1677:** April/spring Albany diplomatic dealings are distinct from the July negotiations with Maryland envoy Henry Coursey; the Onondaga Nation's published discussion identifies the July 21 chain metaphor associated with Carachkondie. [Onondaga Nation](https://www.onondaganation.org/history/2010/polishing-the-silver-covenant-chain-a-brief-history-of-some-of-the-symbols-and-metaphors-in-haudenosaunee-treaty-negotiations/); [Maryland Archives](https://msa.maryland.gov/megafile/msa/speccol/sc2900/sc2908/000001/000005/html/am5--244.html).
2. **1701:** The Great Peace of Montreal and the **July 19 Nanfan deed** are separate arrangements. The latter expressly advances territorial claims in legal language and reserves hunting protection; it is not treated as universally accepted title. [Nanfan deed transcription](https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/nanfan-treaty-1701).
3. **1722:** The Great Treaty records identify the Five Nations, Mahicans, and participating colonies. The related published proceedings preserve an answer to Virginia governor Alexander Spotswood on travel boundaries and peace. The National Archives–linked treaty file and the edited proceedings have distinct documentary histories. [Published proceedings](https://treatiesportal.unl.edu/earlytreaties/treaty.00001.html); [National Archives–linked treaty](https://digitreaties.org/treaties/treaty/81147473/).
4. **1742:** The Philadelphia proceedings document Canassatego's intervention in Pennsylvania–Lenape land disputes; the text contextualizes these claims rather than assuming they were accepted by every affected Lenape community. [Treaty proceedings transcription](https://www.cyberlibrary.org/en/books/the-treaty-held-with-the-indians-of-the-six-nations-at-philadel-50e6ccd6/chapters/3/).
5. **1744:** The Lancaster proceedings record June–July sessions, Canassatego, Gachradodow, Conrad Weiser, distinctions among Virginia and Maryland claims, wampum and compensation, and Indigenous challenges to claims of British conquest. [Lancaster text](https://treatiesportal.unl.edu/earlytreaties/treaty.00003.html).
6. **1753–1754:** Mohawk grievances are treated as an attempt to press for redress, not proof that the Covenant Chain definitively ended. The 1754 Albany Congress proceedings distinguish Indigenous complaints and proposed colonial union. [Albany Congress proceedings](https://treatiesportal.unl.edu/earlytreaties/treaty.00005.html).
7. **July 1754 land dealings:** The Pennsylvania proprietary transaction dated **July 6** and the separate **July 11 Susquehanna Company** transaction have been distinguished and are not folded into one treaty. See Pennsylvania land-deed chronology and historical scholarship; the land claims remain contested by other Indigenous and colonial parties.

**Critical interpretive safeguards:** The text does not treat the Haudenosaunee Confederacy as a unitary monarchic empire; does not equate confederal decisions with every town's consent; distinguishes evidence of a diplomatic claim from proof of valid land title; and distinguishes oral tradition from specific colonial documentary dates. Wampum is not represented as a literal written transcript.

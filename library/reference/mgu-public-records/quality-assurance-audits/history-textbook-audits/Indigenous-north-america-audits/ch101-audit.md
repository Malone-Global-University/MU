## Historical verification and editorial decisions
- Smithsonian/NMAI basis for approximately 44,000 U.S. American Indian service members and more than 6,300 Alaska Native ATG volunteers from 107 communities; figures identified as attributed estimates, not comprehensive censuses.
- Veterans Affairs Canada minimum: at least 3,000 First Nations service members; Métis and Inuit data incomplete, as explicitly stated.
- Canadian National Resources Mobilization Act: distinction between home defense and later overseas conscription; treaty-based questions and 1944–45 administrative qualifications retained rather than alleging universal exemption.
- Attu: Japanese capture and removal of 42 villagers to Japan; distinguished from U.S. evacuation of 881 Unangax̂ from nine other communities to Southeast Alaska.
- Unangax̂ confinement: camp conditions, separate origin of approximately 82-death estimate and specific community/agency attribution; material placed in section 101.11 and documented through NPS and Aleutian Pribilof Islands Association.
- Greenland: Henrik Kauffmann's April 9, 1941 agreement and occupied-Copenhagen protests accurately distinguished, referencing Department of State records and govinfo original.
- Military biographies: Childers (September 22, 1943, Oliveto, Italy), Montgomery (February 22, 1944, near Padiglione), and published museum/service descriptions of Greyeyes-Reid, Rexroat, Spotted Wolf and Tommy Prince.
- NCAI: nearly eighty delegates from approximately fifty tribes/associations, Denver 1944; founding constitution treated as contemporaneous organizational evidence, not proof of realized goals.
- All six source windows contain **clearly labeled source-based paraphrase**, not invented quotations; a documentary source list links to official or Indigenous institution pages.
- Preserved Chapter 102’s code-talker responsibilities and Chapter 103’s substantive postwar veterans/relocation narrative. Explicitly avoided equating wartime economic migration with postwar federal relocation.

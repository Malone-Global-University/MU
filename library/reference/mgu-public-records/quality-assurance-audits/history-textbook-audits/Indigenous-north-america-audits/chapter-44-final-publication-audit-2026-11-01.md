## 3. Historical and documentary audit

| Question | Determination |
|---|---|
| First invasion (1257–1258), Bình Lệ Nguyên and Quy Hóa | Broad chronology and geographical interpretation defensible; explicit attention to non-uniform northern upland alliances. The Quy Hóa engagement is corroborated by James A. Anderson, *The Dong World and Imperial China's Southwest Silk Road* (UW Press, 2024), chapter 5. |
| 1285 operations (Hàm Tử, Chương Dương, Tây Kết) | Earlier Tây Kết actions distinguished from fighting in which Sogetu died; source limitations qualified. |
| Trần Khánh Dư and Zhang Wenhu | Corrected in this Claude revision: *Yuan shi*, juan 209 records Zhang jettisoning grain and withdrawing toward Qiongzhou rather than delivering to the field army. Vietnamese and Chinese records offer different accounts of the fighting. |
| 1288 Bạch Đằng battle | Tides, ambush and naval defeat carefully distinguished from exact tactical reconstructions; Nguyễn Khoái's involvement appears in Vietnamese military-historical accounts. The usual April 9 date is historically standard but the revised chapter does not require false precision. |
| Stake archaeology | Yên Giang, Đồng Vạn Muối, and Đồng Má Ngựa correctly identified. Excavated stake fields and radiocarbon ranges do not by themselves prove all installations belong to the single battle of 1288. |
| Ô Mã Nhi | Capture well supported; 1289 drowning presented only as a later, disputed account, not as an independently verified fact. |
| Hồ Quý Ly reform chronology | Paper currency (1396), land limits and Tây Đô (1397) distinguished from limits on household dependents introduced under Hồ rule (1401). |
| Trần Khát Chân | Failed assassination plot and execution in 1399 corroborated in Vietnamese historical sources. |
| 1377 Champa campaign | **Independent selective correction applied:** the draft conflated the rear guard's failure to reinforce Duệ Tông with Hồ Quý Ly's responsibilities. Final assigns rear-guard failure principally to Đỗ Tử Bình and identifies Hồ Quý Ly with provisioning and reported withdrawal. |
| Lý Chiêu Hoàng abdication | Traditional date 1225 properly qualified against late-December 1225/early-January 1226 Gregorian conversions. |

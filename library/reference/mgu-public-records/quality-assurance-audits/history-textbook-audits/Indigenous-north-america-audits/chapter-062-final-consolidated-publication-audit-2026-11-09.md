## Historical and documentary spot checks

1. **Post-Greenville framework, §§62.1–62.2:** Presents Indigenous government, settlement, and distinct political strategies rather than a single homogeneous Native position. Cross-check: Sami Lakomäki, *Gathering Together* (Yale University Press, 2014), especially the post-1795 study: https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300180619/gathering-together/.
2. **Tenskwatawa, §§62.3–62.5:** Separates a documented movement emerging in 1805 from retrospective and hostile stereotypes; treats the March 1806 White River killings and the June 16 eclipse with different evidence standards. Cross-check: R. David Edmunds, Dictionary of Canadian Biography, https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/tenskwatawa_7F.html; Douglas L. Winiarski (2024), https://scholarship.richmond.edu/religiousstudies-faculty-publications/61/.
3. **Prophetstown, §§62.7–62.8:** Develops agriculture, households, visitors, diplomacy, supply constraints and civilian institutions; uses 1808 for the relocation. Individual family experiences are not invented.
4. **Collective territorial consent and Fort Wayne, §§62.10–62.13:** Distinguishes the 1786 confederacy principle from Tecumseh’s later initiative. Treats the September 30 principal treaty, same-day separate article, October 26 Wea convention and December 9 Kickapoo agreement as distinct instruments; summarizes Article 9’s conditional consent and four-hundred-dollar annuity. Cross-check: https://docsteach.org/document/treaty-tribes-fort-wayne/; https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/supplementary-treaty-with-the-miami-etc-1809-0103; https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-wea-1809-0103; https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-kickapoo-1809-0104.
5. **Vincennes, §§62.13 and 62.17:** Maintains separate August 1810 and July 1811 meetings and avoids allegedly verbatim modern speeches. Cross-check: Indiana Historical Bureau, https://www.in.gov/history/state-historical-markers/find-a-marker/tecumseh-and-harrison/.
6. **British and southern diplomacy, §§62.15–62.16:** Treats Indigenous leaders as autonomous agents; recognizes Pushmataha’s opposition and varying southern responses without back-projecting the Creek War into 1811. Cross-check: Choctaw Nation, https://www.choctawnation.com/news/iti-fabvssa/choctaws-and-the-war-of-1812-part-1/.
7. **Tippecanoe and Prophetstown, §§62.17–62.19:** Separates Harrison’s army advance from the November 7 predawn battle and the November 8 burning. American casualties are presented approximately and distinctly from less certain Indigenous losses; Harrison’s contemporary report is identified as interested testimony. Cross-check: https://history.hanover.edu/texts/1811/Web/Articles/1812-0201sun-harrison.html and https://home.nps.gov/articles/tecumseh.htm.
8. **Earthquake chronology, §62.20:** Dates the first major New Madrid shock to December 16, 1811, after Tippecanoe; later prophetic stories are not represented as contemporary transcript. Cross-check: https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/science/summary-1811-1812-new-madrid-earthquakes-sequence.

Original treaty and dispatch links are included where directly identified; extensive private-source and multilingual archival research is outside this first-edition editorial pass.

A few historical details remain necessarily qualified due to contradictory or retrospective documentation.

- **Deployment:** All tested layouts and functions passed Chromium tests with in-memory HTML. No live maloneuniversity.org upload, automated accessibility certification, or production-server test occurred.

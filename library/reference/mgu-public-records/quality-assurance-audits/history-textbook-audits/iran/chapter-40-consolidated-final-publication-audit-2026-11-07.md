### 3. Consolidated correction register

**C1 — Opening: al-Qushayri's death month (historically important).** The manuscript incorrectly says al-Qushayri died in Ramadan 1072. Specialist material places his death late in 465 AH, reported as **16 Rabiʿ II 465 / 30 December 1072**; some medieval accounts differ in the specific Islamic month. The corrected opening says **late in 1072** without fixing an erroneous Ramadan date.

**C2 — Opening: geographical distance.** The opening asserted a **700-kilometer** separation between Nishapur and Najaf. This was geographically incorrect, given their positions in eastern Iran and southern Iraq. Removed the unsupported number; retained the analytical comparison of al-Qushayri and al-Tusi.

**C3 — Chronology and §40.11: al-Tusi's move to Najaf.** Specialist accounts place the flight in the **mid-1050s**, variously around **1055 or 1057**, amid assaults and disorder. The chronology and text now qualify the range rather than imply a uniquely settled late-1050s date. This does not imply that Najaf's existing shrine/pilgrimage history began with al-Tusi.

**C4 — Qadiri Creed: publication phases and transmission.** The original source-window metadata blurred al-Qadir's **1017–1019** statements, **1029** books, and al-Qaʾim's **1040–1041** reaffirmation. Livnat Holtzman's specialist study identifies distinct original documents, **none of which survives**, and a concise transmitted text preserved in **Ibn al-Jawzi's later chronicle**. Revised the opening chronology, source-window metadata, excerpt label, and limitations to distinguish the nonextant caliphal originals from the later surviving recension.

**C5 — §40.15: false claim of a revised Qadiri Creed.** The draft said the caliph issued a *revised form* of the creed in 1041 under his successor. It now states that a **concise form was publicly reaffirmed** under al-Qaʾim in **1040–1041**. The change avoids a claim of distinct textual revision not established by the cited evidence.

**C6 — Primary Source Window II: identify al-Mufid's text.** Added the title ***Tashih iʿtiqadat al-Imamiyya*** (*Correction of Imami Beliefs*) and made its relationship to Ibn Babawayh's doctrinal account explicit. Its passage is still labeled a **paraphrase**, not a verified direct quotation.

**C7 — Primary Source Window III: al-Ghazali's composition date.** Replaced the unnecessarily fixed *c. 1108* with a qualified **c. 1106–1109** range for ***al-Munqidh min al-Dalal***, the date of which is debated. The four intellectual categories listed in the manuscript agree with the underlying work (theologians, esoteric instructionalists, philosophers, Sufis).

**C8 — §40.20: Islamization overstatement.** The original synthesis asserted that the Iranian world was *already thoroughly Muslim by 900*, contradicting its own recognition of ongoing conversion and persistent Zoroastrian, Christian, Jewish, and other communities. Revised to say Islam was **well established across much of the Iranian world**, while conversion remained uneven and minority communities survived. This is important to avoid erasing regional diversity in a chapter devoted to it.

**C9 — Sidebar accuracy.** The original sidebar described the unnumbered review containers as “within §40.20,” although those HTML sections actually follow the closing numbered section. Relabeled the sidebar to “Review — following §40.20,” without altering the review layout or section count.

**C10 — Responsive CSS.** The original chronology table caused horizontal overflow at a **320px viewport**. Added a narrow-screen CSS rule making wide tables individually horizontally scrollable instead of widening the document. No table rows or historical content were removed.

### 4. Focused verification results

- **Buyid Ashura and Ghadir observances:** The draft wisely says *early 960s*, rather than imposing a single unqualified year. For the Ghadir public celebration, Encyclopaedia Iranica identifies **352 AH / 963 CE**; some sources place the inauguration of Ashura mourning in **351 AH / 962 CE**, while others connect the two observances in 352/963. The draft's broader timing is acceptable.
- **Al-Kunduri's anti-Ashʿari campaign:** Supported in the narrative tradition and specialist scholarship, with a commonly reported Nishapur episode in **445 AH / 1053 CE**; the text appropriately acknowledges disputes over scope and severity, and the later policy reversal.
- **Al-Mawardi:** Death in **1058** and association with juristic treatment of governance are supported; the draft properly distinguishes normative juristic theory from political practice.
- **Baghdad Nizamiyya:** Inauguration in **459 AH / 1067 CE** is verified. The original wording is aligned with the Chapter 39 chronology; no correction needed.
- **Twelver Occultation:** **874** for al-Askari's death and **941** for the end of the Minor Occultation are standard reference dates; the original qualified references are adequate.
- **Ismaili chronology:** **Alamut 1090** precedes the **Nizari–Mustaʿli division of 1094**. The manuscript preserves the distinction and reserves the Nizari state's full development for Chapter 41.
- **Al-Juwayni and al-Ghazali:** The **1091** appointment of al-Ghazali to the Baghdad Nizamiyya, his departure in **1095**, and his death in **1111** are consistent with specialist references; his *al-Munqidh* source-window context is appropriately retrospective.
- **Shia scholarship:** The careers and dates of al-Mufid (d. 1022), al-Murtada (d. 1044), and al-Tusi (d. 1067), together with the significance of Qom, Baghdad, Rayy, and Najaf, are appropriately differentiated.
- **Gender and everyday life:** §40.16 does discuss women's involvement in hadith transmission, prayer and ritual, and documents the limits of elite-written evidence; no missing required subsection.

This is a **targeted specialist verification**, not a claim to have collated all dates, biographies, or original Arabic/Persian manuscripts. Some dates, interpretations, and details remain uncertain in the historical literature and are appropriately presented as such.

### 5. Source-window assessment

1. **Qadiri Creed:** The most sensitive window. The corrected edition explicitly says the surviving compact text is a **later chronicler's recension**, not an autograph or complete preservation of the original 1017–1029 documents. Paraphrase and limitations correctly labeled.
2. **Al-Mufid, *Tashih iʿtiqadat al-Imamiyya*:** Identifiable work, correct educational purpose (disputes over transmitted reports and rational theology), and explicit paraphrase rather than a verbatim quotation. No line-by-line collation with an edition was performed.
3. **Al-Ghazali, *al-Munqidh min al-Dalal*:** The four seeker categories and retrospective argument are grounded in the text and its scholarship. Precise dating remains qualified. No verbatim quote is claimed.

**The source features require an external published edition or translation for students seeking exact wording**. All three are suitable as interpretive first-edition source activities with that clear disclosure. None should be described as a critical edition or facsimile reproduction.

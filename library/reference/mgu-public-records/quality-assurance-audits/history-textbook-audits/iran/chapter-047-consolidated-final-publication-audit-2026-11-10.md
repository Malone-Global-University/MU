## C. Publication-critical historical edits made directly

1. **§47.1 — Chormaqan appointment and succession.** The source said he was simply “left in the region” after the first invasion and then used the unclear phrase “After his incapacity.” Corrected to show that Great Khan **Ögedei dispatched Chormaqan after 1229**, that Chormaqan developed a paralytic illness, and that his deputy **Baiju succeeded around 1242**. The Köse Dağ battle date was specified as **26 June 1243**. Specialist confirmation: Peter Jackson, *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “ČORMĀGŪN” and “BĀYJŪ.”
2. **§47.1 — Körgüz's reputation reversed.** The original inaccurately attributed “rapacity” to Körgüz as a reputation among Persian historians. Juvayni portrays the Uighur official as a restorer of tax order and an opponent of abuses by certain military officers, although that favorable portrayal itself requires source criticism. Corrected directly. This was the clearest substantive error overlooked in Claude's own flagged list. Specialist confirmation: Peter Jackson, *The Mongols and the Islamic World*; *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “ČORMĀGŪN”; Juvayni's administrative descriptions.
3. **§47.13 — Maria Palaiologina.** Clarified that Maria was an **illegitimate daughter of Michael VIII**, originally intended as a spouse for Hülegü, and married **Abaqa after Hülegü's death**. Specialist confirmation: recent Byzantine prosopography and studies of the Palaiologan court; the principal chronology is well supported.
4. **§47.13 — Bukhara raid.** Replaced an imprecise phrase that the raid in “1273 punished” a whole realm with the narrower, attested statement that **Abaqa's forces raided Bukhara in 1272–1273**. **Direction of the raid is Ilkhanid into Chaghatayid territory**. The original did not get the side wrong; it simply overspecified motive. Specialist confirmation: Reuven Amitai, *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “IL-KHANIDS i. Dynastic History”; Michal Biran, “JOVAYNI, ṢĀḤEB DIVĀN.”
5. **§47.19 — Origin of the paper-money project.** Clarified roles: the initial proposal is attributed to **ʿIzz al-Din Muzaffar**, adviser to vizier **Sadr al-Din Zanjani**; **Bolad/Pulad**, the Great Khan's representative familiar with Yuan practice, supported the plan; Gaykhatu sanctioned it in **1294**. Original text did not properly distinguish proposal from technical advice. Specialist confirmation: Peter Jackson, *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “ČĀV”; “ECONOMY v.”
6. **§47.13 — Doquz Khatun chronology.** Replaced “died within weeks” with her death **16 June 1265**, **four months after Hülegü (8 February 1265)**. Specialist confirmation: Charles Melville, *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “DOKUZ ḴĀTŪN.”
7. **Document metadata and responsive layout.** Added a chapter-specific meta description, three keyboard-reachable horizontal scroll containers for the existing historical tables, and minimal CSS to preserve the page width on small screens. No visual redesign or modification to the chapter's historical section structure.

**Facts specifically flagged by Claude which were verified and did not require correction:** Hülegü's February 1265 death; the 1272–1273 Bukhara raid; Sadr al-Din Zanjani's role; Bolad/Pulad's connection to the experiment; Abaqa's Byzantine marriage; Tegüder's reported earlier Christian affiliation/baptism. ## D. Focused historical accuracy review

- **Formation and imperial allegiance:** Hülegü was sent west under Möngke's authority. The 1256 foundation is conventional; autonomous hereditary rule developed across the 1259–1265 imperial succession crisis. The manuscript correctly avoids treating “Iranian state” as ethnic assimilation.
- **Campaign boundaries:** 1256 Nizari fortress campaign, 1258 Baghdad, 1260 Ain Jalut, 1261–1262 open Berke–Hülegü conflict and 1243 Köse Dağ are properly distinguished. The chapter does not unnecessarily recreate the campaigns in Chapter 45.
- **Administration:** The Juvayni brothers are correctly distinguished; Shams al-Din is the chief fiscal officer and Ata-Malik the Baghdad governor and historian. The Juvayni political decline and October 1284 execution are chronologically sound. The chapter appropriately separates early levies from Ghazan's subsequent reforms.
- **Abaqa and international politics:** The 1270 Baraq defeat near Herat, 1272–1273 Ilkhanid Bukhara raid, 1277 Elbistan, 1281 Homs, and Rabban Sauma's 1287–1288 embassy are properly situated.
- **Religious plurality:** The chapter does not classify the early Ilkhanate as uniformly Buddhist, Christian or Muslim. It treats Doquz Khatun's court influence and Tegüder's conversion without making religion a monocausal explanation of politics.
- **Late succession:** Tegüder (1282–84), Arghun (1284–91), Gaykhatu (1291–95), Baydu (1295), and Ghazan's accession in 1295 are correctly ordered. The account of paper money identifies political-financial context rather than claiming novelty in China.
- **Source dependence:** The strongest historical findings were spot-checked against specialist scholarship. This was a targeted source/accuracy audit, not line-by-line verification of every original-language sentence or every reference in the 21,580-word chapter.

## E. Primary-source windows and documentary caveats

1. **Ata-Malik Juvayni, *Tarikh-i Jahan-gushay* (History of the World Conqueror)** — accurately characterized as a Persian work written under Mongol rule and emphasizing legitimation, suffering, and administrative experience; *the displayed segment is an editorial paraphrase, not a quotation*.
2. **Bar Hebraeus, *Chronography*** — Syriac Christian viewpoint, primarily late-thirteenth-century context; *editorial paraphrase, not a manuscript-collated quotation*.
3. **Rashid al-Din, *Jamiʿ al-Tawarikh*** — retrospective early-fourteenth-century compilation, with recognition of the interests of Ghazan's and his successors' dynastic house; *editorial paraphrase, not an exact quotation*.

The coinage/document study is **methodological** rather than a presentation of a particular photographed coin or a directly collated inscription. Students need a catalogued specimen to conduct that exercise. Historical maps are text-guided and need a suitable historical atlas. No facsimile-level or exhaustive original-language primary-source verification is claimed.

## 4. Applied consolidated historical corrections

1. **§38.8 — Fars and Shiraz.** Original erroneously stated that Alp Arslan took Shiraz in 1062 and ended the Fars Buyids. Corrected to the 1062 overthrow and death of Fulad Sutun at Fażlūya's hands, Qavurd's Kirman-based intervention around 1063, and Alp Arslan's later Fars campaigns in 1067–69. Source: Encyclopaedia Iranica, *Fārs iii. History in the Islamic Period*.
2. **§38.11 — Repeated Fars error.** Removed a second assertion that Alp Arslan took Fars in 1062. The Baghdad narrative now distinguishes the Buyids' Iraqi fall in 1055 from their Fars branch's demise in 1062.
3. **§38.14 — Qutalmish's death.** Replaced the vague/incorrect 1063–64 battle sequence with the defeat in November–December 1063; according to the reported tradition, Qutalmish subsequently died after falling from his horse. Source: Encyclopaedia Iranica, *Alp Arslān*.
4. **§38.17 — Sulayman's Nicaea chronology.** Replaced definite conquest in 1081 with the cautious statement that Sulayman is securely attested at Nicaea by 1078, while 1081 is a conventional capital date. Source: Encyclopaedia Iranica, *Saljuqs iii. Saljuqs of Rum* (Andrew Peacock).
5. **§38.17 — Status of Seljuks of Rum.** Removed the inaccurate representation of Sulayman as Malik Shah's straightforward subordinate. Corrected to emphasize the branch's independent and often hostile relationships with the Great Seljuk court and its documented early Fatimid connection. Source: same Iranica article.
6. **§38.36 — Caliph al-Mustarshid.** Clarified that he was defeated and captured by Sultan Masʿud in 1135 and subsequently assassinated in captivity by attackers identified in chronicles as Ismailis. Responsibility for arranging the murder is disputed. Source: Encyclopaedia Iranica, *Sanjar*.
7. **§38.39 — Eldigüzid succession.** Corrected mistaken attribution of control under Arslan Shah to Jahan Pahlavan. Shams al-Din Eldigüz himself exercised predominant power until 1175; Muhammad Jahan Pahlavan succeeded as atabeg (1175–86), followed by his brother Qizil Arslan (1186 onward). Source: Encyclopaedia Iranica, *Atābakān-e Āḏarbāyjān*.
8. **Source Window III — Nishapuri's manuscript.** Removed the statement that Zahir al-Din Nishapuri's *Saljuqnama* survives only through reworking or quotation. A manuscript is extant, and A. H. Morton published a critical edition in 2004. Sources: Gibb Memorial Trust and Cambridge scholarship on the text.
9. **Source Window III — Ravandi's patronage.** Revised an overconfident statement that Ravandi served specifically at the court of the Seljuks of Rum. He was connected with the late Iranian Seljuk court and dedicated the work to the Rum sultan Kaykhusraw; whether he actually visited Konya to present the work is uncertain. Sources: Iranica historiography; Islamisation of Anatolia academic project.
10. **Source Windows I, III, IV — attribution.** Reclassified composite historical summaries as *thematic source-based summaries*, not quotations or paraphrases of one identifiable passage. The Baghdad event window remains an explicitly labeled episode-specific paraphrase and does not present invented verbatim source text.
11. **Responsive CSS.** Added table-local horizontal scrolling at phone widths, without altering chapter structure, features, established palette, sidebar, or JavaScript.

## 5. Historical claims independently spot-checked

- **Dandanqan (23 May 1040)** and its role in Ghaznavid–Seljuk transition: consistent with the preceding Chapter 37 and established specialist chronology.
- **Baghdad (1055)** and the **al-Basasiri interruption (1058–1059)**: coherent and consistent with the Buyid and Seljuk institutional transition.
- **Tughril's marriage negotiations with the Abbasid family (1062–63)**: supported by the Iranica article on al-Kunduri; later marriage between al-Muqtadi and Malik Shah's daughter is documented in the Iranica *Malekšāh* entry.
- **Manzikert (26 August 1071)**: correctly distinguished from the slow and disputed process of Anatolian political transformation.
- **Isfahan Friday Mosque**: south dome c. 1086–87 and northern Taj al-Mulk dome 1088 are supported; the chapter appropriately qualifies the chronology of the four-iwan courtyard. Sources: Encyclopaedia Iranica *Domes*, *Isfahan x. Monuments*, UNESCO.
- **Qatwan (September 1141)**: loss of Transoxiana, but not instantaneous end of Sanjar's rule in Khurasan. Source: Encyclopaedia Iranica *Sanjar* and *Qarā Ḵeṭāy*.
- **Oghuz revolt (1153), escape (1156), death (1157)**: supported by Encyclopaedia Iranica *Khorasan vii* and *Sanjar*. The manuscript does not invent precise casualty counts.
- **Defeat of Tughril III by Tekish in 1194**: accepted chronological endpoint for the western Iranian Seljuk line; the Rum dynasty continued beyond it.

## 6. Primary-source and evidence limitations

The four source features cover early Seljuk origin traditions, Ibn al-Athir's treatment of Baghdad, Ravandi's political representation, and accounts of Sanjar and the Oghuz revolt (including Anvari-attributed laments). They distinguish later historical compilations from near-contemporary events and do not purport to quote verbatim lines not checked against editions. Three windows now explicitly warn that they are *thematic syntheses*, not passage-specific paraphrases. Their educational usefulness is accepted, but their texts have not been collated line-by-line against complete critical editions or translations. Future critical editions should add stable passage references when available; this limitation does not warrant another general drafting pass.

This was a targeted specialist historical and technical audit of the actual complete HTML. It cannot certify every sentence of the approximately 34,711-word manuscript as independently verified. Specific high-impact chronological, succession, attribution, and architectural claims were verified and corrected where necessary.

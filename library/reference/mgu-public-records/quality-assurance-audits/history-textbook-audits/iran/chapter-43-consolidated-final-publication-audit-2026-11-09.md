## 3. Historical, scientific and bibliographic verification

This was a targeted independent audit of the central scientific claims, the issues Claude expressly left unchecked, the mathematical example, the three source windows and the main historical periodization. It was **not** line-by-line original Arabic/Persian manuscript collation.

### Verified historical claims, retained

- The **Ibn Sina–al-Biruni exchange** concerning Aristotelian physics and cosmology is real and survives in correspondence including replies. Specialist research differentiates the exchanges and responses by participants. The chapter describes the exchange without falsely attributing every reply to Ibn Sina himself.
- **Al-Karaji** (flourished circa 1000) wrote important algebraic treatises including *al-Fakhri* and *al-Badiʿ* and applied arithmetic operations to powers and polynomials. The chapter also correctly connects him to the technical treatise *Inbat al-miyah al-khafiya* on groundwater and qanats. His precise life dates remain uncertain.
- **Al-Sufi's Book of Fixed Stars**, dated to approximately **964**, updated Ptolemaic star data and incorporated observations and constellation imagery.
- **Al-Biruni's mountain-and-horizon method** for estimating Earth's radius is a documented historical method. The chapter rightly avoids repeating a highly precise modern kilometer accuracy claim because the results depend on units, interpretations and observational uncertainties.
- **Al-Jurjani's *Dhakhira-yi Khwarazmshahi*** is an important systematic Persian medical encyclopedia begun after his move to Khwarazm around **1110**; it is strongly indebted to but not simply a copy of Ibn Sina's *Canon*.
- **Gerard of Cremona's** twelfth-century Toledo translation of Ibn Sina's *Canon* into Latin is documented. The work's subsequent educational adoption varied by institution and time; the chapter's broad reception summary is sound.
- **Al-Khazini's** association with Merv and Sanjar, the *Sanjari Zij*, his formerly enslaved Byzantine background, and his *Book of the Balance of Wisdom* are supported by specialist references. His mechanical treatise dates to **1121**; the *Sanjari Zij* is generally placed in **1118–1131**.
- **The ʿAdudi hospital** was founded in Baghdad by ʿAdud al-Dawla in **981**. A first-edition overview should avoid extrapolating the scale of recordkeeping and formal teaching at all hospitals from the evidence about major institutions.
- **Ibn Sina's anti-astrology treatise** is transmitted under his name and cataloged in a British Library manuscript; its presence does not mean that he rejected every premise concerning celestial effects.
- **Suhrawardi** completed his *Philosophy of Illumination* in 1186 and died under politically and religiously contested circumstances in 1191, sometimes dated late 1191/early 1192 in accounts. The chapter appropriately leaves method and causation of execution qualified.
- **Nasir al-Din Tusi's *Rawda-yi taslim*** was completed in **1243**, and his Avicennan *Sharh al-Isharat* belongs to the pre-1256 period, commonly dated to **around 1246**. The degree of Tusi's personal religious identification during Ismaili patronage remains debated.

### Mathematical content: PASS

The feature in §43.6 demonstrates a modern-symbol restatement of a historically appropriate circle-and-parabola method for solving a cubic. Its example uses `x³ + p²x = p²q`, `x² = py`, and `y² = x(q − x)`. Eliminating `y` yields the stated cubic for a nonzero intersection, and the worked example `p=2`, `q=4`, `x=2`, `y=2` checks numerically (`8+8=16`). The text distinguishes the modern coordinate representation from Khayyam's rhetorical geometry. This is mathematically sound and consistent with historical discussions of his geometric cubics, though it is an adapted teaching presentation, not a manuscript-transcribed original construction.

### Corrected items incorporated directly

1. **§43.8 — Al-Biruni's *Masʿudic Canon*.** Replaced the imprecise classification as merely another *zij* with an accurate characterization as a broader astronomical encyclopedia with theoretical derivations **and** tables. *Encyclopaedia Iranica* specifically distinguishes it from largely table-focused *zij* manuals.
2. **§43.8 — Al-Khazini.** Reworded the awkward “Greek-slave origin” formulation to a qualified account of Byzantine origin and former enslavement, as attested by biographical sources.
3. **§43.8 — Astronomical instrumentation.** Replaced a potentially overgeneralized list of observatory equipment with more historically cautious language about sighting instruments and variation by period.
4. **§43.12 — Balance of Wisdom.** Set the date to **1121** in accordance with the specialist entry instead of an imprecise “early 1120s.”
5. **§43.3 — Al-Jurjani.** Identified the beginning of the *Dhakhira* after his arrival in Khwarazm around **1110** while retaining its significance for Persian scientific prose.
6. **§43.3 — Manuscript copying.** Replaced an unsupported inference that Arabic script inherently enabled “rapid copying” with the grounded explanation of a large book-production and scholarly correspondence network.
7. **§43.11 — Baghdad hospital.** Included the founding date **981** and qualified the claim that hospitals *typically* provided teaching and systematic records; the institution-level evidence varies.
8. **§43.18 — Ibn Sina and astrology.** Distinguished an attested manuscript treatise transmitted under his name from claims that all branches of astrology or medieval celestial physics were rejected.
9. **§43.19 — Tusi.** Replaced “dating disputed” for the *Rawda-yi taslim* and indeterminate dating of the *Isharat* commentary with **1243** and **approximately 1246**, respectively; retained uncertainty about personal allegiance and other texts.
10. **Responsive CSS.** Added narrow-screen containment for tables and mathematical blocks. This fixes full-page horizontal overflow without removing data, altering the shell or changing the main text's structure.

The other specifically flagged issues did **not** justify substantive rewriting once compared with specialist evidence. In particular, the earth measurement is already appropriately qualified, and the Suhrawardi and al-Razi date ambiguities are treated as uncertainties.

## 4. Three source windows — PASS with qualifications

**Window I, al-Biruni's *On India* (c. 1030):** A thematic paraphrase of its discussion of language, doctrinal differences, informants and effects of political conflict on cross-cultural study. The text identifies the older E. C. Sachau translation and warns against treating the paraphrase as a quotation. It does not independently reproduce precise original wording.

**Window II, Ibn Sina's *Canon of Medicine*:** A thematic paraphrase describing medicine and the five-book organization. Distinguishes the Latin transmission and later manuscript copies from the eleventh-century original. Does not claim a verified primary quotation or modern clinical efficacy.

**Window III, al-Ghazali's *Incoherence of the Philosophers* (c. 1095):** An analytical paraphrase of its twenty targets of criticism, the three issues he considered tantamount to unbelief, and the critique of necessary causation. It avoids the inaccurate claim that he destroyed natural science or philosophical logic.

**Limit:** None of these windows is a philologically collated direct translation of a specified manuscript passage. They are suitable as explicitly labeled first-edition source-study summaries. If a future edition requires exact page-by-page direct quotations, that would be a distinct original-language research project, not an outstanding first-edition correction.

### Substantive revisions by area

- Division I: Explained military coalition incentives, the operational importance of political geography, steppe-sedentary logistics, court ideology, and the distinction between Chinggisid legitimacy and actual command. Clarified nominal khans Suyurghatmish and Sultan Mahmud (commonly identified as Ögedeid descendants in specialist scholarship), with cautious dating for the latter's death.
- Division II: Expanded Herat's 1381 submission with elite divisions, Bushanj, *mal-i aman*, gate transfers, fortification removal and Miran Shah's deputyship; the 1383 rebellion and coerced transfers; the Sarbadar settlement; Sistan's irrigation vulnerability. Distinguished the 1383 Jaʾun-i Qurbani Mongol deportation from skilled-urban transfers.
- Division III: Expanded Mazandaran's 1392–93 alliances with Eskandar Shaykhi and Saʿd al-Dawla Tus, captured and deported Marʿashi leaders; Tabriz's exposure and skilled-worker removal; the Isfahan uprising and civilian massacre; the two-phase Muzaffarid confrontation and the historically documented execution of most princes on **23 May 1393**.
- Division IV: Distinguished Baghdad's 1393 capture from its 1401 devastation, placed northern wars and 1391/1395 victories in geopolitical context, introduced Ibn ʿArabshah's own forced displacement from Damascus, and evaluated overstretch versus expansion without predicting the unrealized China invasion.
- Division V: Expanded the mechanics and dissemination of intimidation, chronology and categories of deportation, costs to home communities, social and ecological consequences in multiple Iranian regions, and the political fragility of a conquest empire dependent on its founder.

## 4. Historical/source verification — selective high-risk checks

Specialist research and the cited source register support the following targeted factual decisions:

1. **Herat:** submission in Muharram 783 / April 1381 after initial fighting; heavy protection tribute, dismantling fortifications, transfer of gates and treasures to Transoxiana, and appointment of Miran Shah as deputy. 1383 punitive intervention followed rebellion; Pir ʿAli was eliminated in **1389**. See *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Herat iii.”
2. **Isfahan:** occupation, rebellion, massacre in 1387. The figure of **70,000** is consistently identified as a historical chroniclers' report, not a verified demographic count. Shami, vol. I, p. 105 and Hafiz-i Abru, vol. II, pp. 666–67 are bibliographic locators provided by the *Iranica* Isfahan entry; not independently manuscript-page collated in this audit.
3. **Muzaffarids:** Shah Mansur killed after fighting Timurid forces near Shiraz in 1393; execution of most surviving princes **23 May 1393**, with exceptions Zayn al-ʿAbidin and Sultan Shibli; appointment of Edigu Barlas in Kerman and ʿUmar Shaykh in Fars and Iraq-i Ajam. See *Iranica*, “Mozaffarids.”
4. **Mazandaran:** *Iranica* “Baduspanids” and “Āl-e Afrāsīāb” identify Eskandar Shaykhi's cooperation, Saʿd al-Dawla Tus's changing alliances, conquest in 1393, and deportation of captured Marʿashi sayyids rather than their universal execution.
5. **Forced transfers:** *Iranica*, “Deportations iii” distinguishes **Jaʾun-i Qurbani Mongols** transplanted from Tus and Kalat in 1383 from **scholars, artisans, and craftsmen** moved from Tabriz in 1386. Roemer, *Cambridge History of Iran*, vol. 6, pp. 49 and 58; Manz, *The Rise and Rule of Tamerlane*, p. 102, cited there.
6. **Chronicle testimony:** Shami wrote at Timur's command around 1404, edited by Felix Tauer (Prague, 1937–56). Ibn ʿArabshah was deported from Damascus with his family in 1400–01 and wrote a later polemical biography; his personal trajectory is instructive but not a neutral lens. See *Iranica* “Historiography v” and “Ebn ʿArabšāh.”

No line-by-line original-language chronicle collation or exhaustive archaeological validation was performed. This is a selective historically grounded first-edition audit, not a claim of archival-final textual criticism.

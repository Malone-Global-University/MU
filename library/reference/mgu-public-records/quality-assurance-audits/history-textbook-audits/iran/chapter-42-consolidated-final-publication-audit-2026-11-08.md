## 3. Publication-critical historical and bibliographical corrections made directly in HTML

1. **§42.3, first primary-source window: Abu Mansur's prose *Shahnameh* preface (957).** The draft called its creator the unspecified “scholars of Abu Mansur's project.” Specialist source material identifies **Abu Mansur Maʿmari**, the minister of patron **Abu Mansur Muhammad ibn ʿAbd al-Razzaq**, as the writer of the surviving introduction. Distinguished the patron from the minister who directed the compilation and corrected the source-window attribution. Source: Djalal Khaleghi-Motlagh, “Abū Manṣūr Maʿmarī,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/abu-mansur-mamari-minister-dastur-of-abu-mansur-b/ .

2. **§42.5: calendrical terminology.** The phrase “25 Isfand of the year 400 of the Islamic calendar” mistakenly assigned a **Persian month** to the Islamic lunar calendar. Now distinguishes the Persian Esfand reckoning from the transmitted reference to AH 400 and retains the conventional scholarly conversion to **8 March 1010**. The more detailed calendar reconstruction is a specialized, textually contested question; the chapter does not claim all date-formulas independently verified. Source: *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Ferdowsi i. Life,” https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ferdowsi-i/ .

3. **§42.12: reconnaissance vs. battlefield identity.** The draft conflated Rostam's nocturnal reconnaissance in the Turanian camp with the exchange in which Sohrab asks his battlefield opponent whether he is Rostam. Corrected the order: Rostam observes Sohrab at a feast and kills **Zhendeh-Razm**, who could identify him; **Hojir** withholds identification afterward; Sohrab questions Rostam on the battlefield, where his father refuses to identify himself. Source: academic epic analysis and the narrative's documented scene sequence; https://www-current.chs.harvard.edu/olga-davidson-womens-lamentations-and-the-ethics-of-war/ ; narrative comparator https://shahnameh.fitzmuseum.cam.ac.uk/explore/objects/no-36-sohrab-slain-by-rostam .

4. **§42.12 and Source Window II: three encounters, not two.** The draft compressed the sequence into two days, including Sohrab's release of Rostam on the first and the fatal injury on the second. Now reflects **an initial indecisive encounter, the next encounter in which Sohrab spares the defeated Rostam after a misleading claim about combat custom, and the fatal encounter on the third day**. The commentary and source-window context were aligned to the correction. Source: Olga M. Davidson, Center for Hellenic Studies, https://www-current.chs.harvard.edu/olga-davidson-womens-lamentations-and-the-ethics-of-war/ ; cross-checked with manuscript-based outline of episode order.

5. **Primary Source Windows II and III: Dick Davis translation.** Twice the draft described Dick Davis's 2006 Penguin *Shahnameh* as a **complete verse translation**. It is an accessible **abridged prose-and-verse translation/retelling**, with some sections summarized or omitted. Changed the labels and identified Arthur and Edmond Warner (1905–25) as the complete verse translation for readers needing extended text. Source: *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Šāh-nāma Translations iii. Into English,” https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/sah-nama-translations-iii-english/ ; Smithsonian catalog https://www.si.edu/object/siris_sil_1089690 .

6. **§42.18: earliest manuscripts.** The Florence manuscript is dated **9 May 1217** and preserves the **first part** of the epic (approximately 22,000 couplets) rather than losing the opening portion as a whole; its initial sheet is missing. The London witness's conventional date **1276** is stated with a source-critical qualification (dating and transmission have been questioned). The **1333 St Petersburg** witness is identified more precisely. Sources: *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Italy viii. Persian Manuscripts,” https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/italy-viii-persian-manuscripts-2/ ; Alessandro Sidoti and Mario Vitalone's codicological study in *The Seljuqs and Their Successors*; Fitzwilliam Shahnameh project, https://shahnameh.fitzmuseum.cam.ac.uk/princely-patronage .

7. **§42.18: al-Bundari's Arabic version.** The draft placed preparation around 1223 without distinguishing commission and work. Corrected to his translation work in **Damascus, 1223–1224**. Source: scholarly biographical study, https://isamveri.org/pdfdkm/02/DKM021181.pdf .

8. **Responsive HTML:** Added a narrow-screen rule enabling local horizontal scrolling in chronology tables while preventing document-wide horizontal overflow. No content or headings were removed.

All corrections are targeted and substantive; neither the existing forty-two chapter navigation convention nor the twenty-section structure was altered.

## 4. Other checked historical details that do not require editorial change

- **957 prose *Shahnameh* commission** by Abu Mansur, with its introduction surviving and most of the main compilation lost: supported by *Encyclopaedia Iranica* “Abu Mansur Maʿmari” and the 1956 Minorsky study of the old preface.
- **Daqiqi and roughly one thousand verses** taken over by Firdawsi: consistent with the mainstream literary history.
- **Composition through 1010, with possible multiple recensions:** supported. The wording correctly leaves earlier versions and later revisions as historical issues rather than fixed events.
- **Rostam–Sohrab episode:** after the corrected encounter sequence, the account of Tahmina, Afrasiyab's attempt to prevent recognition, the token, Kay Kavus's refusal to send healing medicine, and mourning is consistent with the standard narrative.
- **Rostam–Esfandiyar:** the prince's demand to put Rostam in chains, the Simorgh's intervention, the tamarisk arrow directed at Esfandiyar's eyes, the warning of misfortune, and the dying request concerning Bahman are supported by *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/esfandiar-1-son-gostasp/ .
- **Mahmud's patronage and reward legends:** correctly marked as problematic late testimony; the *Chahar Maqala* was written c. **1155–1157**, not a contemporary record. Sources: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cahar-maqala/ ; https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ferdowsi-i/ .
- **Minorsky's Abu Mansur preface study:** identified as Vladimir Minorsky, “The Older Preface to the Shāh-nāma,” **1956**: supported in *Encyclopaedia Iranica* references.
- **Post-Firdawsi reception and illustrated copies:** Great Mongol *Shahnameh* (1330s), Timurid Baysunghur manuscript, and Safavid Tahmasp manuscript appropriately characterized as later witnesses and patronage rather than evidence for the poet's own decade.

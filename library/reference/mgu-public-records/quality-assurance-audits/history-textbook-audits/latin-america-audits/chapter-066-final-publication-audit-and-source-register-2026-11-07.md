## 2. Corrective work incorporated directly into the canonical HTML

1. **§66.3 — Wartime emancipation:** Corrected Decree 3,725-A (6 November 1866) to its actual scope: free-of-charge emancipation of persons enslaved by the **state** and designated for army service, extended to wives where married; clearly distinguished privately owned substitutes, owners' compensation, and other recruitment pathways.
2. **§66.7 — Ceará's collective antislavery labor actions:** Corrected the chronology to recognize two separate dockworker stoppages on **27–31 January** and **30 August 1881**, as well as the contributions of **José Luís Napoleão**, **Francisco José do Nascimento**, and their fellow Black workers; avoided a single-hero reduction.
3. **§66.8 — Sexagenarian Law:** Distinguished Article 3, paragraphs 10–11 (three years of service and termination at 65), paragraph 13 (qualified former-owner maintenance/care duty), paragraph 14 (five-year domicile requirement for people freed *through the emancipation fund*), and broader police/employment controls on freed people. Eliminated the unqualified claim of legal abandonment without any duty of support.
4. **§66.8 — Punishment law:** Identified Law 3,310 of 15 October 1886 as repeal of the legal **judicial sentence of flogging**, not a ban on all coercive violence by slaveholders.
5. **§66.12 — Speech from the throne:** Replaced the unsupported claim that Isabel's 3 May 1888 speech was 'read for her' with the documented statement that the regent herself read it.
6. **§66.13 — Suffrage and citizenship:** Corrected the 1881 reform to its requirement of documented **net annual income ≥200 mil réis** rather than ownership of land, alongside literacy qualifications; clarified that Article 6 of the 1824 Constitution explicitly recognized **Brazilian-born** freedpeople, while African-born freedpeople did not automatically gain that citizenship.
7. **1871 source window:** Corrected age-eight compensation/service choice to **Article 1 §1**, with the separate emancipation fund and savings/self-purchase provisions identified under Articles 3 and 4.
8. **1885 source window:** Corrected the overbroad reference to residence restrictions and added the statute's differentiated maintenance and policing provisions.
9. **All six source windows:** Added clickable, traceable documentary or bibliographic references; clearly marked paraphrase versus quotation.
10. **Documentary register:** Added 15 referenced editions and scholarly sources in **unnumbered back matter**, reachable from the sidebar; specified evidence limitations and atlas-dependent map exercises.
11. **Mobile fix:** Added a keyboard-focusable, horizontal-scroll region around the chronology table to remove the original 320-pixel overflow without altering the established chapter theme or sidebar script.

## 5. Remaining documentary/editorial limits, disclosed rather than hidden

- The chapter is approved as a **scholarly historical-text HTML first edition**, not as an exhaustive archive/critical transcription: manuscript variants, every nineteenth-century newspaper issue, and all bibliographic page numbers were **not** independently collated.
- The three Map Studies are **text-based exercises** requiring an external atlas; no separately drawn map plates are embedded.
- Estimates of the total enslaved population, migration, military enlistment, and post-abolition demographic changes remain dependent on imperfect historical records; the chapter characterizes material uncertainties rather than manufacturing exactness.
- Verbatim phrases are deliberately short, with some normalized spellings, and non-verbatim selections are labeled as paraphrase or editorial summary.
- A later, genuinely new publication-critical error can justify an erratum, but **no additional iterative content passes** are recommended on the current record.

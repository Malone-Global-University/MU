## 4. Fact-check — key records consulted

- **Brazil:** Federal Senate 2016 impeachment vote **61–20**; STF annulment of Lula Curitiba convictions in 2021; TSE final Lula 2022 result **50.90%**; Supreme Court's Bolsonaro attempted-coup conviction September 2025 and January 2026 order; TSE 2026 runoff **Lula 51.79% to Flávio Bolsonaro 48.21%**, with 64,256,232 and 59,826,041 valid votes. See documentary bibliography entries **1, 4–10**.
- **Argentina:** congressional and electoral records of the 2023 Milei presidential election, official **provisional** 2025 legislative returns, and INDEC December 2025 year-on-year CPI **31.5%**; poverty measure is from a dated urban survey sample. See **12–17**.
- **Mexico:** INE 2018 returns, TEPJF 2024 Sheinbaum count **35,924,519 (59.76% as reported by electoral tribunal)**, the September 15, 2024 judicial constitutional decree, and the INE 2025 judicial electoral report. See **18–22**.
- **Chile:** Servel 2025 runoff data **Kast approximately 58.17%**, TRICEL January 2026 official proclamation for March 11 inauguration. See **23–25, 47**.
- **Colombia:** electoral reporting and August 7, 2026 Senate inauguration record for Abelardo de la Espriella. See **26–29**. The narrative avoids asserting an unverified exact national runoff share.
- **Peru:** JNE's 2026 proclamation **Keiko Fujimori 9,223,396 valid votes, 50.135%** and Congress's July 28 inauguration statement; the reported margin of **49,641 votes** is from the 100% count. See **30–32, 48**.
- **Bolivia, Ecuador, Uruguay, Costa Rica:** official or official-source reports documenting 2025–2026 presidential results and inaugurations. See **33–40**.
- **Democracy methodology:** Latinobarómetro's 2024 report showing **52% regional support for democracy** (48% in 2023 comparator), and separate V-Dem/IDEA report links. Do not treat surveys and expert-coded indices as interchangeable measures. See **41–46**.

Important interpretation controls: the chapter distinguishes contested political explanations from judicial findings; treats impeachment as a constitutional procedure whose political legitimacy remains debated; distinguishes reelection from inauguration; and does not forecast events after the historical cutoff.

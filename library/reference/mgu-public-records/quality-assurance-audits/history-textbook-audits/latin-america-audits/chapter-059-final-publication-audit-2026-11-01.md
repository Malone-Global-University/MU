## Publication-critical corrections applied to the submitted HTML

1. **§59.17 / Barradas and Guerrero:** On September 15, 1829 the Spanish landing force had **already capitulated on September 11**, not 'approaching.' Corrected the abolition chronology. Evidence: Mexico SEGOB, `https://sitios.segob.gob.mx/es/FomentoCivico/VictoriaTampico`; contemporary surrender agreement, `https://www.memoriapoliticademexico.org/Textos/2ImpDictadura/1829DIB.html`.
2. **§59.8 / slave-trade law:** Corrected an anachronistic reference to the first Congress elected under the October 1824 constitution passing a July law: the *constituent* Congress enacted the prohibition on **July 13, 1824**, before promulgation of the constitution. `https://www.memoriapoliticademexico.org/Textos/2ImpDictadura/1824-PCE-TM.html`.
3. **§59.23 / Plan of Cuernavaca:** Removed the document's overstatement as explicitly ordering the 'expulsion' of reform officials and speaking directly of army privileges; it denounced reform/proscription measures, called on Santa Anna and asked officials to leave office. Distinguished wider movement slogans from the five articles. `https://bond.st-andrews.ac.uk/pronunciamientos/dates.php?f=y&m=5&pid=1004&y=1834`.
4. **§59.23 / Gómez Farías:** Clarified his removal from the exercise of executive power, avoiding a premature claim that his vice-presidential office had itself been formally eliminated in May 1834.
5. **§59.25 / Seven Laws:** Corrected the presidency's qualification from a property 'requirement of four thousand pesos' to an **annual income qualification**. `https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/leyes-constitucionales-de-1836/html/d396303c-ac34-4be3-baa0-06164c882def_2.html`.
6. **§59.34 / Yucatán economy:** Reframed henequen as a developing rather than already dominant sector in 1839–1841, and restored agriculture, cattle, sugar, logwood and Gulf trade. Universidad Autónoma de Yucatán: `https://www.mayas.uady.mx/historia/cont_02.html`.
7. **§59.24 / Zacatecas:** Distinguished Francisco García Salinas's **1829–1834 governorship** from his leadership of the militia in 1835. Instituto Nacional de Estudios Históricos de las Revoluciones de México chronology and archival/academic research: `https://bibliotecadigital.ilce.edu.mx/sites/estados/libros/zacateca/html/sec_119.html`.
8. **Casa Mata source window:** Replaced an inaccurate attribution of Catholic-religion assurances to the plan itself with an article-specific paraphrase of sovereignty/Congress (Art. 1), provincial deputy selection (Art. 3), interim provincial deputation role (Art. 9), and the emperor's personal security (Art. 10). St Andrews critical documentary edition: `https://bond.st-andrews.ac.uk/pronunciamientos/dates.php?f=y&m=2&pid=747&y=1823`.
9. **Chronology preview:** Removed implication that Guerrero acceded to the presidency in September–December 1828; he took office April 1, 1829.
10. **All source windows:** Added explicit links to published documentary text editions for the eight windows, including both Texas declarations, without altering paraphrases into unverified direct quotations.
11. **320/390-pixel overflow:** Added one mobile-only CSS rule to scroll chronology tables horizontally **inside their own boxes**, without page-level scrolling.

## Checked historical and documentary claims

- Mexico 1824 federation, distinction between states and territories and unsettled Tlaxcala status — UT Tarlton Law Library original text, `https://tarlton.law.utexas.edu/constitutions/federal-estados-unidos-mexicanos-1824-sp/table-contents`.
- Mexico 1828 state legislative vote reported as Gómez Pedraza 11 and Guerrero 9 — biographical electoral reconstruction, `https://www.archontology.org/nations/mex/mex_1824_55/gomez_pedraza.php`. Note additional votes went to other candidates; do not interpret as a two-candidate-only 20-vote universe.
- Guerrero's 1829 abolition decree and Texas's December administrative exception — Texas State Historical Association, `https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/guerrero-decree`.
- 1830 colonization law, including the immigration restrictions in Article 11 and limitations on importing enslaved people — original statute at `https://www.memoriapoliticademexico.org/Textos/2ImpDictadura/1830-colo.html`, contextual TSHA `https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/law-of-april-6-1830`.
- The Seven Laws of 1836, qualifying income and domestic-service exclusion — Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes original text, `https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/leyes-constitucionales-de-1836/html/d396303c-ac34-4be3-baa0-06164c882def_2.html`.
- Texas declaration 1835 (federal restoration) versus 1836 (independence) — UT Tarlton and Texas State Library original texts, `https://www.tsl.texas.gov/treasures/republic/declare-01.html`.
- Alamo and Goliad approximate casualty figures, retaining uncertainty in the manuscript — U.S. Census historical review and TSHA Goliad account, `https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/goliad-massacre`.
- 1841 Bases de Tacubaya versus 1843 Bases Orgánicas — Mexican Archivo General de la Nación and 1843 constitutional text at `https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/bases-organicas-de-la-republica-mexicana-de-1843/html/4b38b9fc-06b1-4b0f-8793-925d787aa722_2.html`.
- Aguascalientes's federal-territory separation from Zacatecas in May 1835 — statute `https://es.wikisource.org/wiki/Ley_de_23_de_mayo_de_1835_%28N%C3%BAmero_1571%29` and UNAM scholarly article `https://moderna.historicas.unam.mx/index.php/ehm/article/view/67483`.

## 3. Historical corrections directly applied

### A. National Front constitutional sequence — primary publication correction

The original conflated an elite-negotiated political agreement, the *plebiscito* of **1 December 1957**, and later constitutionalized presidential alternation.

The final text now distinguishes:

1. The **Benidorm (1956) and Sitges (1957)** negotiations, which outlined the two-party political settlement.
2. **Decreto Legislativo 0247 of 4 October 1957**, as amended by **Decreto 0251 of 9 October 1957**, submitting the constitutional package, including **party parity**, to the **1 December 1957** plebiscite.
3. **Acto Legislativo 1 of 15 September 1959**, which expressly mandated alternating Liberal and Conservative presidents for the three terms from **7 August 1962 to 7 August 1974** and continued the constitutional framework of parity. Lleras Camargo's 1958–1962 presidency preceded this newly formalized succession provision.

The correction was applied throughout **§83.8, §83.9, Source Window 4, the chronology preview, the vocabulary/terminology module, and chapter summary**, not only to one paragraph.

**Verified sources:**
- [Constitutional Court's discussion of the 1959 amendment](https://jurinfo.jep.gov.co/Normograma/compilacion/docs/c-141_2010.htm).
- [Full text of Acto Legislativo 1/1959](https://legalize.dev/co/law/ACTO-LEGISLATIVO-1-1959).
- [Legal-historical article identifying 1957 Decrees 0247, 0251 and Acto Legislativo 1/1959](https://www.scielo.org.mx/scielo.php?pid=S1870-21472019000100161&script=sci_arttext).

### B. *La violencia en Colombia* book provenance

The opening and documentary study previously treated the **1962 book** as a government-commissioned investigation. The final text now distinguishes the official **1958 investigative commission**—of which Germán Guzmán Campos was a participant and whose evidence he retained—from the **independently initiated university scholarly book** coauthored by Guzmán, Orlando Fals Borda, and Eduardo Umaña Luna. The register also distinguishes **Editorial Iqueima's 1962 first printing** from later Tercer Mundo editions; Volume II appeared subsequently.

**Verified sources:** [Universidad del Valle archival history of the commission and book](https://germanguzman.univalle.edu.co/la-comision/memoria-y-violencia); [scholarly research on the 1958 commission and 1962 book](https://online.ucpress.edu/gp/article/4/1/74935/196269/Making-Sense-of-Violence-in-Latin-America-Social).

### C. The 1964 Southern Bloc and 1966 FARC sequence

The original conflated the **20 July 1964 agrarian program** and the timing of an organizational conference. The final §83.14 and Source Window 6 distinguish the **May–June 1964 Marquetalia campaign**, the **20 July 1964 agrarian program**, subsequent coordinating conferences described with variant dates (**late 1964 or 1965** in different histories), and the **April–May 1966** formal adoption of the name **FARC**. The later title **FARC-EP** is not projected backward onto the original organization.

**Verified sources:** [Colombian Truth Commission discussion of the 1964 agrarian program and early organization](https://www.comisiondelaverdad.co/sites/default/files/2022-07/10.%20ORINOQUIA%20VERSION%20FINAL.pdf); [historical study recording an alternative 1965 first-conference chronology](https://repositorio.uniandes.edu.co/bitstream/handle/1992/7881/dcede2000-08.pdf).

### D. Plan Lazo, U.S. assistance, and 1965 defense legislation

The final §83.17 now primarily uses **Plan Lazo**, associated with Colombian/U.S. counterinsurgency planning following the **1962 U.S. Army advisory survey**. It notes that **LASO/LASSO** occurs in other histories but does not certify the disputed expansion “Latin American Security Operation” as the project's indisputably original designation.

The final text also distinguishes the relevant parts of **Decree 3398 of 1965**: **Article 25** concerned assigning civilians to defense-related tasks; **Article 33 paragraph 3** concerned administrative authorization of certain arms. It notes that aspects of the decree were given legislative permanence by **Law 48 of 1968**, without equating the 1960s legislation with the later paramilitary architecture.

**Verified sources:** [U.S. Army Center of Military History, *The Advisory Experience, 1955–1975*](https://history.army.mil/Portals/143/Images/Publications/Publication%20By%20Title%20Images/U%20Pdf/us-army-counterinsurgency-2.pdf); [Colombia government legal text, Decree 3398 of 1965](https://www1.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?i=66354).

### E. Mortality estimates, 1970 election and remaining dates

The final §83.4 preserves the distinction between older estimates and the method-specific reconstruction by **Julio Romero-Prieto and Adolfo Meisel-Roca (2019)**, giving both reported bounds for each period: **1949–1958: conservative 39,142, maximum 57,737; 1949–1966: conservative 80,498, maximum 113,032**. These are attributed estimates, **not** conclusive enumerations of politically motivated deaths.

The final §83.18 and Source Window 9 identify the certified 1970 tally—**Misael Pastrana Borrero: 1,625,025; Gustavo Rojas Pinilla: 1,561,468; margin: 63,557**—and distinguish legal certification from ANAPO's allegations and the independently documented election-night controversy. The dispute is not falsely represented as either proven fraudulent or proven free of irregularities.

Other verified events were sharpened without expanding chronology: the **Pact of Chicoral, 9 January 1972**, is explicitly inside the chapter's period; the **Consejo Regional Indígena del Cauca**, founded **24 February 1971 in Toribío**, is described by date and place. Guadalupe Salcedo's **1957 death** and women's **1954 suffrage recognition / 1957 first national vote** were already correctly presented and retained.

**Verified sources:** [Published historical electoral totals, Universidad del Rosario](https://editorial.urosario.edu.co/pageflip/anexos/HistoriaOrganizacionElectoral_anexo.pdf); [Colombian National Registrar, women's suffrage chronology](https://www.registraduria.gov.co/Cronologia-del-voto-en-Colombia-636.html); [CRIC organizational history](https://www.cric-colombia.org/portal/estructura-organizativa/origen-del-cric/); [the public archive's 2019 mortality study discussion](https://www.banrep.gov.co/es/node/49251/printable/print).

## 6. Remaining first-edition limitations (disclosed, not blockers)

1. **Original-language collation:** The nine source windows are identified editorial paraphrases and source-guided studies. Most original manuscript pages, historical newspaper printings, and printed editions have not been page-by-page collated against an archival facsimile. Window 2 is specifically catalog-description-grounded rather than transcribed testimony.
2. **Scholarly bibliography:** Thirty entries remain in the register; selected core records have been checked, but exhaustive edition, DOI, page, and archive-box verification for every scholarly citation has not been completed. The manuscript does not assert otherwise.
3. **Maps and archival photographs:** Three maps and two documentary image exercises are deliberately text-guided. No unlicensed photographs, historical map plates, or archive facsimiles were embedded. In particular, the Museo Nacional de Colombia's registration 5663 for the 1957 women's-voting photo identifies the record but does **not** automatically grant reproduction rights.
4. **Design and deployment:** Chapter 31 shell parity is stylistic rather than proven byte-for-byte. The local HTML passes the above Chromium tests; production deployment to the university website and server-side link routing have not been tested.
5. **Historical interpretation:** The chapter preserves careful distinctions among civilian experiences, insurgent claims, partisan narratives, state responsibilities, and debated demographic estimates. It should not be read as establishing the truth of every allegation quoted or paraphrased in historical documents.

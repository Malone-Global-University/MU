## 5. Consolidated historical corrections and verification findings

- **Argentina:** Corrected the chronological relationship between the Federal Pact signed **January 4, 1831** and Paz’s later capture in May; the Representative Commission did not persist until the mid-1830s, but was dissolved in **July 1832**. Corrected the account of Ramírez’s death to acknowledge involvement of Córdoba and Santa Fe forces. Preserved the February/December sequence of Rivadavia’s presidency and the 1826 constitution, plus the distinction between Rosas’s 1829 and 1835 grants of authority.
- **Chile:** Corrected O’Higgins’s accession as Supreme Director to **February 1817**, the 1826 federal experiment to the governments of Ramón Freire/Manuel Blanco Encalada rather than Francisco Antonio Pinto, and distinguished Pinto’s subsequent constitutional leadership in 1828. The 1833 constitution’s date and structure are supported by Chilean parliamentary records.
- **Uruguay:** Distinguished the 1842 Montevideo Defense emancipation measure from Oribe’s separate 1846 Cerrito measure, their wartime context and their unequal implementation. Preserved the 1830 constitutional citizenship qualifications and the sovereignty of the new Eastern State.
- **Paraguay:** Differentiated modernization under Carlos Antonio López from later telegraph development under Francisco Solano López. Expanded the **October 7, 1848** decree to cover citizenship and nationalization of communal property belonging to twenty-one Indigenous towns.
- **Central America:** Corrected the 1824 constitution’s differentiated Congress/Senate system; separated the Salvadoran-Honduran forces at La Arada from wider regional union projects; distinguished the April 1856 Costa Rican campaign from later multi-state resistance to Walker; grounded Walker’s September 22, 1856 decree in the annulment of earlier federal laws. Guatemala’s March 21, 1847 declaration is treated as compatible with possible future Central American reunion.
- **Remaining qualification:** These targeted checks and document comparisons do not establish line-by-line archival verification of all 56 sections. Qualitative generalizations are framed as interpretations, especially where early source records are incomplete or political testimony is partisan.

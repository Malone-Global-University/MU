## 3. Direct historical and documentary corrections applied

### A. Decree 900: communal property and legal categories (§79.10)

The former description of municipal and Indigenous communal property as alike available for redistribution was corrected. Article **9(f)** addressed certain eligible **municipal** lands; Article **10(c)** expressly exempted preexisting **Indigenous and campesino agrarian community lands**. Article **33** provided for claims in certain community–municipality disputes. The revised prose now distinguishes the categories.

**Controlling reference:** `Decreto Número 900`, Law of Agrarian Reform, 17 June 1952, arts. 9–10 and 33, congressional facsimile: https://geo.cepal.org/kbtx/auxwim84jdna7n95PoDqhG/Guatemala-900-1952.pdf . Congressional document catalogue: https://www.congreso.gob.gt/detalle_pdf/decretos/5726 .

### B. Decree 900: usufruct, rental, private title and succession (§79.10)

The false exclusivity of lifetime usufruct was removed. **Article 4** permitted lifetime usufruct and rent for nationalized parcels, and also restricted **private-title awards to eligible beneficiaries of parcels up to 18 hectares**; **Article 32** contemplated title or usufruct depending on eligible beneficiaries' choices. **Article 38** restricted sale/attachment of awarded ownership interests. **Article 39** provided that lifetime usufruct ended on the recipient's death while surviving dependants obtained **priority to apply for another usufruct**—not automatic inheritance of the same right. The vocabulary box and Source Window 5 now match this legal distinction.

**Reference:** Decree 900, arts. 4, 32, 38 and 39 (same facsimile).

### C. Decree 900: the six-caballería threshold and printed inconsistency (§79.10)

The revised narrative uses the statute's **two-caballería** and **six-caballería** thresholds (conventionally about 90 and 270 hectares). The surviving facsimile has an internally inconsistent spelled-out metric figure for six caballerías. This is explicitly identified; the draft's rough 272-hectare conversion is **not** falsely described as a demonstrated substantive error. Eligibility and cultivated-area conditions are stated with appropriate limits.

**Reference:** Decree 900, arts. 10 and 32; U.S. Department of State, March 5, 1953 assessment in *FRUS 1952–1954, Guatemala*, document 35: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54Guat/d35 .

### D. Source Window 6: an identifiable agrarian petition replaces the invented composite

The former editorial composite describing a generic petition was fully removed. Its replacement examines the **Unión Campesina de San Vicente Pacaya** petition concerning **Finca El Barretal** (Escuintla), **identified archival case file** at the Instituto Nacional de Transformación Agraria (INTA), **folio 0026**, reproduced and discussed by Guatemala's **Comisión para el Esclarecimiento Histórico**, *Guatemala: Memoria del silencio*, vol. VI, **illustrative case 99, p. 292, note 906** (1999).

The revised study does **not** fabricate a signature, precise day, original text, or new quotation. It is explicitly an **editorial paraphrase grounded in the CEH's published presentation of a specific archive record**. The CEH identifies the community's claim that it cultivated the land as tenants and asserted an older community title, and documents subsequent attempts to reverse the agrarian award. All four analytical questions refer to this particular case and to the limits of retrospective reproduction. A targeted cross-reference was added to §79.11 and the CEH register entry.

**Reference:** Judiciary-hosted CEH volume VI: https://biblioteca.oj.gob.gt/digitales/1134.pdf , printed pp. 292–293, n. 906; the original INTA folio itself was not independently collated in this edition.

### E. Land measures (§79.11)

The account now distinguishes applications, expropriation notices, property affected, awarded land and durable possession. Broadly reported areas and the oft-cited approximately 100,000 affected families are expressly described as **estimates with differing definitions**, not verified counts of recipients who actually held land continuously. The El Barretal case supplies a documented example of awards later contested and reversed.

### F. United Fruit and compensation (§79.12)

The former comparison of an **aggregate** roughly $1 million Guatemalan offer against a U.S. demand for a **single property** was repaired. The new wording matches the two amounts for the same **Tiquisate** expropriation: approximately **$594,572 in proposed bonds** versus a claim of approximately **$15.85 million** on roughly **234,000 acres**, and separately identifies the February 1954 **Bananera** taking. This is grounded in contemporaneous reporting and the FRUS editorial record.

**References:** *FRUS 1952–1954, The American Republics*, vol. IV, doc. 420: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v04/d420 ; *TIME*, May 3, 1954, “Guatemala: Square Deal Wanted”: https://time.com/archive/6825949/guatemala-square-deal-wanted/ . These are historically situated reports and diplomatic positions, not an independently established market appraisal.

### G. British freighter *Springfjord* (§79.17)

The categorical assertion that the ship “was sunk in port” was replaced with the attested wording that it was **bombed and set ablaze off Puerto San José on June 27, 1954**. This is supported by the contemporaneous British parliamentary account, while retrospective descriptions of its ultimate loss vary.

**Reference:** House of Commons, Hansard, June 28, 1954: https://hansard.parliament.uk/commons/1954-06-28/debates/4d489fe2-97f0-4d6e-a2f2-1cada68b676d/Situation .

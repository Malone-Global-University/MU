### Five divisions and subject boundaries

1. **I — Sandino, Sovereignty, and the Formation of Somocismo**: U.S. intervention and military organization; Sandino, his movement and assassination; the family's coercive, economic and party institutions.
2. **II — The FSLN, Religious Opposition, and the Deepening Crisis**: FSLN organization; Catholic social activism; 1972 earthquake; 1974 hostage seizure; guerrilla strategy divisions; Chamorro's 1978 assassination.
3. **III — Nicaragua's National Uprising, 1978**: Monimbó and civic opposition; the August palace seizure; September urban insurrections and civilian costs; mediation and regional diplomacy.
4. **IV — The Fall of Somoza and the July 1979 Revolution**: reunification of FSLN tendencies; final offensive; OAS and foreign relations; Somoza's departure, Urcuyo's failed succession and the July 19 victory.
5. **V — The First Revolutionary Government and Its Contradictions**: junta versus FSLN Directorate; state security; confiscation and the mixed economy; literacy and health; pluralism, Church divisions, coastal politics and emerging U.S. conflict.

The chapter reserves the main Contra War, the 1982 Miskitu relocations, the 1984 electoral contest and Iran-Contra for Chapter 90; it does not preempt the Salvadoran and Guatemalan civil-war narratives of Chapter 91.

## 4. Principal historical corrections made

**a. The initial December 1974 hostage operation.** Section 89.7 now distinguishes the party held in honor of U.S. ambassador Turner B. Shelton from his actual location during the FSLN takeover: Shelton and his wife had already left. This detail is established by FRUS telegram 4972 (28 December 1974): https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76ve11p1/d248 .

**b. The 1980 election timetable.** The original §89.12 contained a contradiction describing elections as scheduled in 1984 and later brought forward to 1984 from 1985. Revised §89.20 and AI-9 explain correctly that the September 1980 Decree 513 anticipated the *electoral process beginning January 1984, with voting in 1985*. Elections were later moved to November 1984. Evidence: https://hrlibrary.umn.edu/iachr/country-reports/nicaragua1981-ch7.html .

**c. FSLN's 1969 Atlantic Coast pledge.** AI-3 now accurately describes promises of economic integration/development, cultural recognition and an end to ethnic discrimination—not a fully articulated commitment to territorial autonomy. Source: https://cedema.org/digital_items/3400 .

**d. The Caribbean Coast chronology.** The old §89.12's detailed forward narrative about 1982 mass relocations was replaced with a bounded treatment of 1979–1981 tensions and a cross-reference to Chapter 90. This avoids conflating the early coalition dispute with later military operations.

**e. The role of U.S. and other regional governments.** The revision distinguishes diplomatic mediation, regional political support, material aid and the internal Nicaraguan campaign, and does not imply U.S. command over Somoza's decisions or a single foreign-directed revolution. See https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v15/d218 and https://history.state.gov/milestones/1977-1980/central-america-carter .

**f. The 1978–1979 loss of life.** A contemporary ECLAC/CEPAL assessment estimated approximately 35,000 deaths during the intensive conflict, many civilian; the number remains a reconstruction, not an exhaustive victim register. This chapter characterizes the figure as estimated. Source: https://repositorio.cepal.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/837df766-0f72-48e1-8fe5-8715cdb9eedc/content ; IACHR 1981 report https://hrlibrary.umn.edu/iachr/country-reports/nicaragua1981-ch9.html .

**g. FSLN's 1978 coordination versus March 1979 reunification.** AI-6 uses the documented 9 December 1978 *joint statement* as evidence of public convergence while distinguishing it from the formation of the integrated nine-member directorate in March 1979. Source: https://cedema.org/digital_items/10303 .

**h. The 1979 transition and institutional ambiguity.** The reconstruction distinguishes Somoza's 17 July departure, Urcuyo's failed interim claim, 19 July Sandinista entrance to Managua, and the subsequent Government of National Reconstruction, and separately distinguishes the junta from the FSLN's Directorate. Sources: https://history.state.gov/milestones/1977-1980/central-america-carter and https://www.enriquebolanos.org/media/publicacion/Leyes_de_Nicaragua-1979_Vol_I.pdf .

**i. The 1980 Literacy Crusade.** The account uses UNESCO's archival coverage and widely reported reduction in illiteracy from approximately 50% to 12%, explicitly explaining that the figures depend on campaign assessment and do not establish uniform language access or long-term retention. https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/national-literacy-crusade .

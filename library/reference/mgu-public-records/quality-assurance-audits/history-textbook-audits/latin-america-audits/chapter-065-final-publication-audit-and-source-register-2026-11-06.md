## Consolidated revisions completed

1. **§§65.1/65.3 and chronology — nitrate chronology:** distinguished Peru's **18 January 1873** state nitrate sales *estanco* from the **1875** expropriation/acquisition program. An academic study of Tarapacá's response to these policies provides independent corroboration.
2. **§65.2 + 1874 source window — treaty geography:** separated the 24th-parallel frontier from the **23°–24°** mineral-duty context; maintained guano-sharing arrangements and clarified the **21 July 1875** complementary protocol extending guano-sharing interpretation to **23°–25°** and providing arbitration. Removed the inaccurate suggestion that Bolivia was still negotiating the already-signed 1873 Peru–Bolivia alliance.
3. **§65.4 and chronology — Peru's formal act:** distinguished Chile's **5 April 1879** declaration of war from Peru's **6 April** activation of the defensive alliance, documented in the United States diplomatic record.
4. **§65.5 — Bolivian logistics:** removed the inaccurate claim that Bolivia had no terrestrial access to its own prewar coast; explained difficult Bolivian routes and the combined Allied front through Tacna/Arica.
5. **§65.6 — Grau letter:** corrected the date to **2 June 1879**, marked the primary text as a paraphrase, and linked an identifiable Spanish transcription.
6. **§65.9 — Dolores and Tarapacá:** corrected attackers/defenders at San Francisco/Dolores, where Buendía's forces attacked Chilean positions; corrected the subsequent Chilean initiative under Luis Arteaga at Tarapacá; expanded geographic explanation of water and supply.
7. **§65.12 — Arica:** expanded the command structure, objectives, Morro topography, the contested evidentiary status of casualties and reported post-assault violence, and the distinction between capture of a port and ending the war.
8. **§65.13 — Lackawanna:** specified the seven demands in the **22 October 1880** protocols and described them explicitly as **unaccepted Chilean proposals**, not final peace terms; added documentary link and analysis.
9. **§65.15 — occupation and library:** expanded labor, minority, women, and family experience; identified Palma's **20 November 1883** letter to Marcelino Menéndez Pelayo, distinguished advocacy from inventory, and connected it to Peruvian National Library scholarship.
10. **§65.17 — Ancón / Lima:** added omitted Peruvian signatory **José Antonio de Lavalle**, explained plebiscite administration and prolonged dispute, and identified the **1929** treaty's **$6 million** payment (Article VI) and Arica port/railway facilities (Article V).
11. **§65.18 — Bolivia:** stated accurately that the **1884** truce expressly terminated the state of war without definitive territorial cession, and specified **1904 Articles II–VII**, including **£300,000**, rail transport, debt undertakings, and transit rights, while distinguishing commerce from sovereignty.
12. **§65.19 — Santa María:** distinguished the **126 deaths and 135 wounded** in a later official investigation from higher estimates and **3,600** in commemorative memory, as documented by Chile's National Archives; expanded structural labor analysis.
13. **All five source windows:** retained their analytical design while adding identifiable linked editions, clear metadata, and paraphrase disclosures.
14. **Unnumbered Documentary Sources and Further Reading:** added 15 verified documentary or institutional reference links and matching sidebar jump link; not counted as a historical section.

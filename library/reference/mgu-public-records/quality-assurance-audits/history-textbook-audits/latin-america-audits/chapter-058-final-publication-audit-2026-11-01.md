## 3. Historical/source review and corrections incorporated

The review used direct texts of the six constitutions and selected national archives, government institutions, reputable legal editions, and scholarly works. All six primary source windows now provide clickable source-document editions and explicitly characterize their provisions as paraphrases rather than original quotations.

1. **Cúcuta, 1821:** Differentiated the numbered constitution from the accompanying Catholic-religion address and from the separate 19 July free-womb statute. Article 15's 1840 literacy-delay clause and occupational alternative checked. Clarified that Ocaña's 1828 constitutional convention occurred **before** the ten-year review interval envisaged by the charter. Primary legal edition: <https://www.alcaldiabogota.gov.co/sisjur/normas/Norma1.jsp?dt=S&i=13690>.
2. **Mexico, 1824:** Federation, 19 states/four territories, unsettled Tlaxcala status, religion and Article 171 amendment protections supported in historical constitutional text. Reference: <https://archivos.juridicas.unam.mx/www/legislacion/federal/historicos/1824.pdf>.
3. **Brazil, 1824:** Four powers, Article 98–101 Moderating Power, executive relationship to Senate/Chamber and granted constitution checked. Qualified assertions that monarchy **alone** ensured territorial unity, recognizing substantial provincial resistance and later revolts. Primary text: <https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/consti/1824-1899/constituicao-35041-25-marco-1824-532540-publicacaooriginal-14770-pl.html>. Brazilian National Guard's 18 August 1831 enabling law separately checked: <https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/lei_sn/1824-1899/lei-37497-18-agosto-1831-564307-publicacaooriginal-88297-pl.html>.
4. **Bolivia, 1826:** Verified Article 6 Catholic establishment; Articles 9 and 27 four powers/tricameral legislature; Articles 77–85 lifetime presidency and vice-presidential succession with legislative approval. Added Article 11(5)'s important **qualified emancipation**: people previously enslaved were declared free but could not yet depart former masters pending special regulation. Integrated this qualification into the source window and the comparative abolition narrative. Source: <https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/constitucion-del-estado-del-19-de-noviembre-de-1826/html/6f240562-0c16-4f70-81af-3a115470d05c_2.html>.
5. **Haiti, 1816:** Article 1 abolition, Articles 38–39 white-property/citizenship restrictions and narrow historical exceptions, and Article 44 **immediate recognition as Haitian upon arrival with a one-year delay before exercising citizen rights**. Corrected source window and §58.12 to avoid implying nationality was delayed and to acknowledge exceptions. Text: <https://www.haiti-reference.info/pages/plan/histoire-et-societe/documents-historiques/constitutions/1816-text/>.
6. **Chile, 1833:** Verified transitional literacy deferral until after 1840 and corrected the source window's description of voting eligibility to reflect different married/unmarried age thresholds and occupational/property/income alternatives. Clarified Article 82(20)'s distinction between external attack (executive with Council of State) and internal disturbance (Congress or limited interim executive authority with Council). Text: <https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/constitucion-de-la-republica-de-chile-jurada-y-promulgada-el-25-de-mayo-de-1833--0/html/ff2db0b2-82b1-11df-acc7-002185ce6064_2.html>.
7. **Buenos Aires church reforms:** Removed the unsupported categorical claim that the government abolished “four of five” convents. Replaced it with the enacted 1822 law's suppression of Bethlemite houses and smaller houses of other orders and its regulation of the remainder. Scholarship: <https://www.teseopress.com/laconstruccionestatalenelriodelaplata/chapter/del-reformismo-rivadaviano-a-la-hegemonia-rosista/> and <https://estudiossocioterritoriales.fch.unicen.edu.ar/ojs-3.1.0/index.php/anuario-ies/article/download/2432/2295/6672>.
8. **Pius VII:** Correctly identified the 30 January 1816 document `Etsi longissimo terrarum` as a **papal brief**, not an encyclical; its loyalty-to-Ferdinand-VII position verified. Document index: <https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-vii/es.html>.
9. **Selected remaining historical checks:** Puerto Rico's 1815 Royal Decree of Graces and differential land incentives (<https://enciclopediapr.org/content/real-cedula-de-gracia-1815/>); December 1822 Plan of Veracruz / 1 February 1823 Plan of Casa Mata (<https://www.constitucion1917.gob.mx/es/inehrm/De_imperio_a_republica>); 1821 smaller-convent legislation in Gran Colombia; 1823 Chilean abolition (<https://www.archivonacional.gob.cl/galeria/documentos-destacados-de-la-independencia-de-chile>); San Martín's August 1821 abolition of Indigenous tribute (<https://sanmartiniano.cultura.gob.ar/noticia/cronologia-sanmartiniana/>); Cabanagem casualties reported in the tens of thousands (<https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/9781405198073.wbierp0277>); Tristan Platt's scholarship on tribute and communal lands (<https://www.loc.gov/item/hlas-bi84101695/>).

**Scope limitation:** This is a consolidated publication-focused review and targeted verification of documentary, chronological, numerical, and interpretive claims, not a sentence-by-sentence archival corroboration of every detail in approximately 14,766 narrative words. The cited source editions substantiate the important provisions discussed; the narrative itself remains a synthesis and includes scholarly interpretations.

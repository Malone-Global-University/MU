## 3. Consolidated historical corrections

1. **Argentina, November 1902.** Corrected chronology: Law **4,144 (Residence Law)** passed on **22 November**; separate Law **4,145 (state of siege)** on **24 November**. Repaired both opening and §69.18, with more institutional context in §69.19 and Source Window VII. Argentina's official historical statute records support the dates.
2. **El Salvador 1881–1882 land statutes.** Differentiated the 1881 community division law (co-owner shares and recognition of existing possessors) from the 1882 ejido act (compensation based on six annual ground rents, four-year installment possibility with nine-percent legal interest, title applications, six-month procedures and auctions). The enactment/approval stages are separately identifiable in the transmitted statute text.
3. **Argentina Law 947 (1878).** Confirmed **1,600,000 pesos fuertes** in Article 1 and financing by anticipated public-land proceeds in Article 2, public subscription/survey arrangements in Articles 5–10, and the land reservation provision in Article 19. The military campaign itself belongs principally to 1879 and later operations.
4. **Mapuche land grants.** Changed an unqualified estimate of roughly 500,000 hectares to the Chilean National Library's **3,078 titles / 475,194 hectares** for the **longer titling process through 1929**, not a count valid solely to 1914. Other totals/definitions exist and must be identified when used.
5. **Bolivia's law of 5 October 1874.** Clarified that the Article 7 removal of state recognition of communities/ayllus was conditioned on individual titles having been conferred; it was not an automatic immediate extinguishment of all communities.
6. **Putumayo fatalities.** Retained documentation of atrocities but explicitly labels the frequently repeated estimate of about 30,000 fatalities as **unverified**, without incorrectly ascribing a precise death tally to Roger Casement. Separates killing, illness, flight and displacement as unresolved demographic components.
7. **Asian migration.** Specified dates and denominators: approximately 100,000 Chinese indentured workers reaching Peru in 1849–1874, and approximately 125,000 surviving arrival in Cuba from more than 141,000 embarked in 1847–1874, subject to source-series boundaries. Distinguished later Japanese and voluntary Chinese migration.
8. **São Paulo migration.** Removed the denominator-undefined claim that 'over a million Europeans came to the state in the following decades.' Explained that registries, re-entries, time frames and the full long-lived hostelry intake must not be conflated.
9. **Argentina's Law 8871 (1912).** Specified male native-born/naturalized citizens aged 18+, qualifications, exemptions and explicit exclusions. Distinguishes national citizens from foreign residents and women. The official digitized statute lists **13 February** as its sanction date; some retrospective accounts record congressional passage on **10 February** and promulgation on the 13th. The discrepancy is disclosed, not silently reconciled.
10. **Social legislation.** Added institutional contexts for Argentine Sunday-rest Law 4,661 (1905), women/children Law 5,291 (1907), labor administration from 1907, and coverage limitations.

## 4. Documentary integrity and source windows

All eight source windows now identify real documents, laws, tables, or a specifically catalogued register item, and include directly linked editions or repositories. **No purported verbatim historical quotations have been invented.** Excerpts previously described in generalities were replaced with article-specific editorial paraphrases, or clearly labeled source-study descriptions where original documents were not collated. Each source window has four individualized student questions.

**Critical archival limitations:**

- **Source Window III (Chile):** The CONADI AGAI **Título de Merced No. 1, Ancapi Ñancuche**, is an identifiable archival object. A Chilean National Archive publication dates the title to 1884, while a photographed cover bears a marking of 1894. This edition does not assert that the conflict is resolved, or fabricate precise acreage, names or quotations from the facsimile; the PDF is linked for direct examination.
- **Source Window VI (São Paulo):** The *Hospedaria de Imigrantes* **Book 02, page 92**, beginning 28 August 1884, is traceable by its exact book/folio/page number from a 2017 University of Brasília historical study and contemporary archive collections. The original PDF image was not reliably accessible for complete collation. The study accurately identifies the archival record without inventing any individual entry or pretending a specific labor contract was read. This record concerns the earlier reception organization, before the 1887 opening of the Brás building.
- **Source Window V:** The Argentine **29.9% foreign-born** statistic is supported by official retrospective census history; a particular city/occupation matrix from the 1914 census has not been individually reconstructed.
- Official statutory transcriptions and museum inventories were consulted, but systematic checking of every original archival facsimile, provenance annotation, language variant, and statistical series was beyond the scope of this first-edition textual HTML audit. All such limitations are disclosed on-page.

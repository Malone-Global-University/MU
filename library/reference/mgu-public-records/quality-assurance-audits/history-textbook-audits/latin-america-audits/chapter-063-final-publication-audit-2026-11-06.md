## 3. Historical changes made directly to Claude's submission

1. **Veracruz, March 1847 (§63.4):** differentiated the military capitulation agreed on **March 27** from U.S. occupation of the city on **March 29**. The draft had used only March 29 as the surrender date. The quantified initial landing of about 8,600 comes from one U.S. Army institutional history; another official U.S. Army volume reports more than 10,000 going ashore and a larger expedition complement. The distinction is one of counting scope, not proof of exact troop strength.
2. **Treaty citizenship rights (§63.5):** repaired the logically erroneous nationality-choice sentence. Under the treaty's Article VIII, residents had a choice whether to retain Mexican nationality or acquire U.S. citizenship, with a one-year declaration rule after exchanged ratifications.
3. **Land-grant clause (§63.5):** explained the U.S. Senate's deletion of Article X and its revision of Article IX, rather than describing Article X as replaced by a simple equivalent property guarantee.
4. **Cross-border raids (§63.5):** clarified that Article XI required restraints on incursions and aid involving certain captives; Mexican monetary indemnity demands were contested, not expressly an unqualified treaty obligation. The later Gadsden treaty abrogated Article XI.
5. **Constitution of 1857 (§63.10 and source window):** clarified that Article 2 reaffirmed the prior abolition of slavery rather than originating abolition in 1857; identified the institutional development of **amparo** without obscuring earlier Mexican precedents.
6. **Juárez's presidency (front-matter reference):** removed the inaccurate implication that he served separate nonconsecutive presidencies beginning in 1858 and 1861; his constitutional claim and executive tenure continued through wartime, until his death in 1872, subject to contested wartime extension.
7. **Political constituency (§63.20):** removed the sweeping and unqualified claim that all Conservative and imperial support had a uniformly narrow social base; the revised wording acknowledges regionally uneven coalitions.
8. **Chronology:** corrected the assertion that the September 1847 occupation of Mexico City ended all organized national resistance; resistance and treaty negotiations continued.
9. **Source integrity:** added readable links to identifiable documentary texts or institutional editions for all six Primary Source Windows. These remain explicitly labeled **paraphrases**, with no invented original-language quotations. The quotation from Juárez's July 15, 1867 manifesto matches the government's published text.
10. **Historiography:** appended bibliographically identifiable works by Jan Bazant, Florencia E. Mallon, and Guy P. C. Thomson with David G. LaFrance to the Historical Debate, clearly separating scholarly research themes from the chapter's pedagogical syntheses.
11. **Mobile defect:** placed both chronology/timeline tables in keyboard-focusable, horizontally scrollable containers with scoped responsive CSS, without altering the historical 20-section structure or the existing scroll-highlighting JavaScript.

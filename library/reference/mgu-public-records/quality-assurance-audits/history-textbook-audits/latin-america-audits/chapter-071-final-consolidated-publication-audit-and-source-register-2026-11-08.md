### 3. Essential historical corrections applied

1. **Palo Blanco, §71.1 and Source Window I:** Specifies March 21, 1876 conditional interim executive arrangement: Article VI required the Supreme Court's presiding officer to affirm the plan; otherwise military command would take executive authority. Grounded in the University of St Andrews archival transcription of the *Plan reformado*.
2. **Land policy, §71.9 and Source Window III:** Replaces the inaccurate assertion that March 1894 abolished surveying. The statute's Articles 7–8 relaxed requirements for settlement, enclosure, cultivation and some large transfers, whereas Articles 23–30 preserved formal denunciation, surveys, boundary work, publication, neighbors' notice, and adjudicatory review. Distinguishes 1856, 1883 (Manuel González), and 1894 (Díaz).
3. **1906 PLM platform, §71.14 and Source Window V:** Retains the eight-hour day, wage, land and education proposals but explicitly identifies **Article 16's proposed prohibition on Chinese immigration** and analyzes its discriminatory character, avoiding a sanitized description of the program's reformism.
4. **Cananea, §71.15 and Source Window VI:** Distinguishes June 1 miners' strike and initial violence from June 2 crossing of armed personnel from Arizona; uses the INEHRM documentary study's specifically identified **Document No. 3** for the proposed five-peso wage, eight-hour day, 75% Mexican employment and merit-based advancement. No unsupported casualty precision or claims to direct manuscript collation.
5. **Río Blanco, §71.16 and Source Window VII:** Gives the precise **January 4, 1907 presidential arbitration award**, its required **January 7 return to work**, the reported January 6 communication of the result and separate January 7 confrontation. Source window now analyzes original award clauses, including controls on workers' newspapers and strikes; no invented newspaper edition is assigned.
6. **Additional authentic analysis:** §§71.5, 71.7, 71.8, 71.17, 71.19 and 71.20 expand on the social geography of railways, regional industry and agricultural contrasts, urban service inequality, 1907–09 economic shock transmission, Madero's heterogeneous political coalition, and structural conditions for regime breakdown. This material is historical narrative—not filler added to apparatus.

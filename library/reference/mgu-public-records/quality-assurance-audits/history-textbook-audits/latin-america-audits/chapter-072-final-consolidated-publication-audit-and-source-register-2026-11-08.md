## 3. Essential historical corrections applied

**H1 — Díaz and Corral resignations, 25 May 1911 (§72.2).** Removed the erroneous claim that Ramón Corral had already resigned before Díaz. Both resignation letters were dated **25 May 1911**; Corral requested their contemporaneous acceptance. The Mexican congressional *Diario de los Debates*, reproduced by UNAM, supports this correction; an accessible edited transcription of Díaz’s letter appears in the documentary register. Original congressional scan location: https://archivos.juridicas.unam.mx/www/bjv/libros/6/2883/5.pdf. The full facsimile was **not** page-by-page collated in this pass; date and text were independently confirmed through indexed records.

**H2 — May 1911 Battle of Ciudad Juárez (§72.2).** Replaced unwarranted certainty that Orozco and Villa deliberately disobeyed Madero in starting the battle. The prose now distinguishes operational initiative from disputed triggers: deliberate disobedience, confusion on the line, or defensive response to federal fire. The significance of the May 8–10 victory is preserved. INEHRM historical account: https://www.gob.mx/cultura/prensa/conmemorara-el-inehrm-el-110-aniversario-del-triunfo-de-la-revolucion-maderista?idiom=es-MX.

**H3 — Ciudad Juárez Treaty textual distinction (§72.2 and PSW I).** The treaty *recitals* state the intention of Díaz and Corral to resign and anticipate León de la Barra’s interim succession. The operative clause orders cessation of hostilities and progressive demobilization. The revision no longer falsely presents the anticipated resignation as an operative command of the treaty. Edited transcription: https://www.memoriapoliticademexico.org/Textos/6Revolucion/1911TCJ.html.

**H4 — Two Teoloyucan agreements (§72.13 and PSW VI).** Specified the separate **13 August 1914** capital-transfer act (Álvaro Obregón and Eduardo N. Iturbide) and federal army/navy dissolution agreement (Constitutionalist side Obregón and Lucio Blanco; federal side Gustavo A. Salas and Vice Admiral Othón P. Blanco). José Refugio Velasco was senior federal commander but does not replace those signatories. The original printed Spanish text and contemporary FRUS English translation were examined at https://www.memoriapoliticademexico.org/Textos/6Revolucion/1914TDT.html and https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1914/d876.

**H5 — Villa agrarian law (§72.17 and PSW IX).** Dated the measure **24 May 1915, León, Guanajuato** and specified Convention-derived authority, compensation, and the intended subdivision of large estates; distinguished legislative ambition from wartime implementation. Document: https://www.memoriapoliticademexico.org/Textos/6Revolucion/1915LFV.html.

**H6 — Zapatista agrarian law (§72.16).** Explained that on **26 October 1915 in Cuernavaca**, the Executive Council of the Sovereign Revolutionary Convention promulgated a law implementing the Plan of Ayala. Not a decree issued solely by Zapata as an individual. Some versions bear an earlier **22 October** date associated with drafting/adoption. Dated governmental historical notice: https://www.cultura.gob.mx/efemerides-del-dia/?numero=183. Complete original archival facsimile not collated; details beyond that notice should not be attributed to a directly examined original.

**H7 — Veracruz losses (§72.10).** Replaced vaguely phrased “scores” with the distinction that **19 U.S. military personnel died** and Veracruz hospital records listed **126 Mexican dead and 195 wounded**, many civilians; the Navy warns that the Mexican tally likely undercounts. The scope of that source—hospital records—is now explicit. Naval History and Heritage Command: https://www.history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/wars-conflicts-and-operations/early-20th-century-conflicts/veracruz-1914.html.

**H8 — Columbus losses (§72.19).** Replaced “about eighteen Americans” with **18 Americans: ten civilians and eight U.S. soldiers**, the National Park Service count, without implying this covers all casualties. NPS: https://www.nps.gov/places/village-of-columbus-and-camp-furlong-columbus-new-mexico.htm.

**H9 — Plan de la Empacadora documentary versions (PSW III).** The 25 March 1912 37-point manifesto is explicitly distinguished from a preceding 6/9 March declaration and notarial record that some digital publications also associate with the name. The relevant dated documentary transcription includes both components: https://www.memoriapoliticademexico.org/Textos/6Revolucion/1912MPO.html ; official date overview: https://www.gob.mx/defensa/documentos/25-de-marzo-de-1912-plan-de-la-empacadora.

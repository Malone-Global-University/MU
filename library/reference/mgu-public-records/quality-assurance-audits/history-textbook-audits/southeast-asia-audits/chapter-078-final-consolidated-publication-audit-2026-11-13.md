## Historical and documentary corrections completed

1. **Bandanese authority:** Replaced the inaccurate categorical claim that Banda had no kings with the documented distinction between no unified monarchy, local royal titles, the `orang kaya`, assemblies, port officers, and other leadership arrangements. The analysis draws especially on the preconquest discussion by S. J. Miske and the longer literature on Banda.
2. **Trading agreements:** Clarified that agreements of 1602 and 1605 had differing scope, participants, and interpretations. The Dutch claim to exclusive purchase is distinguished from the validity and practical consent of independently organized Bandanese settlements.
3. **1609 aftermath:** Developed the continued VOC action after Verhoeff’s death, including the role of Simon Jansz Hoen and the use of supply restrictions, drawing on the Dutch National Archives guide. Retained caution about disputed details of immediate negotiations.
4. **Ai in 1615–1616:** Connected military conquest with the failed effort to obtain knowledgeable coerced workers, including the Dutch National Archives account of captives taken from Siau.
5. **The 1621 force:** Distinguished reported company soldier/seaman numbers from larger composites that include auxiliaries, mercenaries, and logistical personnel, avoiding an exact unsupported force total.
6. **Executions:** Maintained 47 detained and 44 executed on 8 May 1621, while specifying that the remaining three died before execution. The role of Japanese soldiers is documented in Adam Clulow’s scholarship and not treated as transferring command responsibility away from VOC officials.
7. **Population and deportation:** Separated contested estimates of original population (including Pires, Verken, and the later Gijsels/Valentijn tradition) from more specific recorded deportation and survival counts. Rejected the treatment of 15,000 as a census.
8. **Early landholding:** Removed the erroneous ‘approximately one hectare’ claim and presented early reconstructed *perken* at approximately 1.8–2.8 hectares where the historical discussion supports it; distinguished the gradual process through 1628 from instant reorganization in 1621.
9. **Perkeniers and slavery:** Developed early Mardijker and former-company-employee access, fixed prices, debt, and the compositional diversity of the enslaved workforce, without projecting the fully developed nineteenth-century estate system onto the 1620s.
10. **1622 Run:** Attributed the approximate 160 adult male deaths and the 12 September date to Tristan Mostert and Wim Manuhutu as published secondary research rather than falsely claiming independent manuscript collation.
11. **2026 scholarship:** Identified **S. J. Miske** by name and gave the article’s DOI, exact online publication date (**30 September 2026**), and the distinction between that date and the journal issue’s **June 2025** cover date. The article argues for methodological continuity and stagnant or declining output rather than a proven increase in plantation efficiency.
12. **Source register:** Added documentary and scholarly source register with live URLs and archival reference identifiers; retained explicit editorial-paraphrase labels in all three Primary Source Windows.

## Three primary-source studies

- **Window II — Depopulation and starvation (1621):** Dutch National Archives, VOC archive **1.04.02, inv. 1073, scan 114** (24 April 1621 removal decision) and **inv. 1073, scan 36** (6 May 1621 starvation decision).

## D. Scholarly and historical correction register

### D1. Early Toungoo
- Geographic explanation differentiates Kyaukse / upper Irrawaddy agricultural systems, the Sittang corridor, and the Lower Burma coast. Mingyinyo's early chronology remains qualified where documents are limited.
- Tabinshwehti's capture of Pegu (1538–1539) is distinguished from the consolidation of Lower Burma. Martaban (1540–1541) and Prome (1542) are treated as separate operations rather than one vague '1540s' campaign.
- Pegu's Mon courtly traditions and the dynasty's multiethnic political coalition are distinguished from the modern imposition of ethnic blocs.

### D2. Bayinnaung's reconquest and expansion
- Toungoo (11 January 1551), Prome (30 August 1551), Pegu (12 March 1552) are incorporated in the section and source-window chronology as dated in scholarship on Bayinnaung's bell inscription; Ava (1555) remains distinguished. Contemporary royal testimony is recognized as partisan self-presentation, not a neutral record.
- The Shan states are described as multiple principalities, with variable local political arrangements. Lan Na (1558), Ayutthaya (1563–1564 and 1568–1569), and Lan Xang (1565; 1574 Vientiane/client installation) are treated separately.
- The 1564 and 1569 Ayutthaya conflicts are not merged. Maha Thammaracha's post-1569 position is described without claiming abolition of Ayutthaya's own institutions.

### D3. Imperial system and collapse
- Military mobilization is explained through supplies, roads, boats, elephant provisioning, service households, and agrarian disruption, without inventing troop or casualty totals.
- Core administration, appointed provincial government, and distant tributary sovereignty are distinguished; later Restored Toungoo administrative institutions are not projected backward.
- Bayinnaung's death in October 1581, Ayutthaya's assertion of independence in 1584, repeated campaigns, manpower crisis, defection, Arakan–Toungoo intervention of 1598–1599, and Nanda Bayin's surrender in 1599 are treated as connected but analytically distinct events.
- Causes of collapse are presented as structural **and** contingent, not as the inevitable product of one ruler's character.

### Consolidated historical and source audit
1. **Founding and southern campaigns** — Distinguishes Restored Toungoo breakdown, Hanthawaddy's 1752 capture of Ava, Alaungpaya's coalition at Moksobo, the 1755 Yangon renaming, the 1756 capture of Thanlyin, and Bago's fall in 1757. Treats ethnic interpretations and later royal legitimacy narratives critically. Reference: Jacques P. Leider, *Aséanie* 33 (2014): 13–41, https://doi.org/10.3406/asean.2014.2317; Lieberman, *Burmese Administrative Cycles* (1984).
2. **Golden Letter** — Source window retains explicit editorial paraphrase rather than invented quotation. Identifies 7 May 1756, Ms IV 571a, the Pathein commercial-site reference in Leider's edition, the original gold-and-ruby medium, 2015 UNESCO inscription, and the difference between an overture and an effective agreement. References: UNESCO, https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/golden-letter-burmese-king-alaungphaya-king-george-ii-great-britain; manuscript catalogue https://digitale-sammlungen.gwlb.de/resolve?id=00056452; Leider (2009; edition catalogued by Myanmar's national library).
3. **Institutions and labor** — Distinguishes Hluttaw, provincial officers, ahmudan and athi service classifications, material limits of recruitment, and household costs. Avoids uncorroborated large troop totals; follows institutional framework of Lieberman and royal-orders scholarship.
4. **Manipur and Siam** — Separates raids, tribute, captivity, occupation and integration. Does not conflate Alaungpaya's 1750s Manipur campaigns with the devastating later intervention of 1819–1826. Retains the 1760 failure in Siam and the 1767 fall of Ayutthaya under Hsinbyushin; detailed Siamese aftermath reserved for Chapter 95.
5. **Qing–Konbaung conflict** — Explains Shan frontier politics, supply/disease difficulties, end of major fighting in 1769, and the distinct process of diplomatic normalization in the 1780s–c.1790. Source: Chusei Suzuki, *Southeast Asia: History and Culture* 10 (1981): 3–16, https://doi.org/10.5512/sea.1981.3.
6. **Bodawpaya, Amarapura and surveys** — Distinguishes the 1782 succession, the 1783 establishment of Amarapura, late-eighteenth-century *sittan* inquiries, court aspiration versus practical implementation, royal work projects, and Buddhist administration. Published *Royal Orders of Burma* used as a documentary roadmap, not claimed to be exhaustively collated.
7. **Arakan (Rakhine)** — Distinguishes the 1784–1785 conquest and deportation of the Mrauk U court from later forced labor demands and the substantial movement of refugees to Bengal more than a decade afterward. Avoids fabricated or unsupported refugee figures; recognizes heterogeneous Rakhine Buddhist and Muslim experiences. See Jacques P. Leider, “The Epigraphic Archive of Arakan/Rakhine,” 2018, cited in bibliography.
8. **Society and religious authority** — Expands the administrative and economic experiences of cultivators, women, artisans, captive groups, Buddhist monasteries, Shan principalities, and frontier communities without presenting hypothetical individual testimony as actual primary evidence. The chapter evaluates the limits of court-centered sources and avoids teleological colonial histories.

**Bibliography:** Twelve verified edition, catalogue, or article links. The original historical sources themselves were not systematically collated against manuscript images or original-language editions.

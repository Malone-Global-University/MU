### Consolidated corrections and substantive expansion

1. **Dell–Olken reversal corrected:** §102.16 now states that the published *Review of Economic Studies* analysis finds higher, not lower, present-day average consumption near historical sugar factories. The adjusted comparison is approximately 10%; the study's current economic outcomes are distinguished from the coercive conditions experienced by nineteenth-century workers. The comparative mortality findings of de Zwart, Gallardo-Albarrán, and Rijpma are retained without suggesting the studies measure identical outcomes.
2. **Unsupported 1834 legal-codification claim removed:** §102.2, the timeline feature, source study, review question, and chapter summary have been revised. The Nationaal Archief historical introduction identifies a governing regulation proclaimed **19 January 1830** and a subsequent regulation of **26 September 1836**, without supporting a claim of a single comprehensive cultivation code enacted in 1834. The year 1834 remains important for the extant government cultivation report.
3. **Article 56 of the 1854 Regeringsreglement clarified:** the window now explicitly analyzes the tension between maintaining government cultivation and requiring food security, respect for land rights, fair labor allocation, adequate compensation, investigation of grievances, and movement toward voluntary arrangements. Dates of enactment (2 September 1854) and operation (1 May 1855) are distinguished. The accessible printed text was reviewed in a **1914 amended compilation** (printed p. 27), not represented as an exact transcription of the original 1854 publication.
4. **Belgian Revolution chronology corrected:** the 1830 Belgian Revolution is treated as strengthening subsequent Dutch fiscal pressures, not as a cause of the already initiated January 1830 cultivation policy.
5. **Scholarly bibliography corrected:** full bibliographic metadata now identify Dell & Olken (2020), de Zwart, Gallardo-Albarrán & Rijpma (2022), Fasseur's translator/editor roles, and relevant sources for legal, archival, and social history.
6. **Primary-source windows strengthened:** (I) the 19 January 1830 governing regulation and Van den Bosch papers, Dutch National Archives 2.21.028; (II) the 1834 Cultivation Report, Ministry of Colonies 2.10.01, no. 4405, with further 1835–1837 inventory references; (III) Article 56 and its identified printed witness; (IV) a specific petition scene in *Max Havelaar*, dated 29 March 1856, with links to an accessible edition and independent chronology. Each remains explicitly labeled as a **paraphrase**, not a quotation.
7. **Historical narrative expanded holistically:** added crop-specific discussions of indigo/tea/tobacco, sugar-factory coordination, export warehouses and auction accounting, water allocation and rice markets, gendered and household labor, regional comparisons, mortality research, financial methodology, and the 1878–1890 sugar transition.
8. **Mobile navigation restored:** an accessible HTML5 disclosure index enables navigation to all 20 sections while retaining the existing desktop sidebar. Tables remain horizontally scrollable on narrower screens.

### Disclosed verification limitations

- The Van den Bosch archive finding aid and the bibliography of the mortality study identify original records, but the full handwritten or typeset archival items **have not been collated line by line**. In particular, the 1834 Cultivation Report's tables and page-specific contents have not been transcribed. Source windows explicitly distinguish documentary locators from text directly inspected.
- The Art. 56 witness inspected is a **1914 printed compilation incorporating later amendments**. Its specific wording should be compared with the 1854 Staatsblad if a diplomatic transcription or legal-critical edition is required. The present educational source window is a labeled paraphrase, not an asserted original-language transcription.
- The novel's specified petition scene has been located in an accessible English edition; English wording was not treated as authoritative Dutch original text.
- No claim of having checked every claim against original eighteenth- or nineteenth-century records is made. The consolidated research verifies the publication-critical corrections and key cited findings.
- Browser QA tested the self-contained file, not its eventual live website deployment or all external destinations. The actual original Chapter 1 shell was not independently compared byte for byte.
- Numeric fiscal totals and historical mortality estimates remain identified as approximate or model-dependent where that is the evidence's status.

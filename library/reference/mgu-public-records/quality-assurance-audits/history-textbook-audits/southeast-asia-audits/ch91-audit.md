## 2. Historical corrections and source decisions

1. **Shaykh Yusuf:** Chronology now separates **1684** removal to Ceylon, **1693** decision for Cape transfer, and **1694** arrival. His arrival in 1694 is supported by South African Government and South African History Online sources. The religious-travel section distinguishes exile from voluntary mission and trade.
2. **Japanese red-seal chronology:** Distinguished late-sixteenth-century Japanese overseas commerce from the formal Tokugawa red-seal licensing framework instituted in **1604** and the restrictive policies of the **1630s**. National Archives of Japan documentary exhibit used for verification.
3. **Banda's nutmeg ecology:** Replaced the categorical claim that Banda was the only source in the world with the more defensible description of an overwhelmingly predominant source of traded nutmeg and mace in that period, recognizing modern scholarship's careful language.
4. **Banda 1621–1630s:** Added identifiable **5 May 1621** Pulau Ai land-allocation act and the distinction between company plans and delayed, uneven implementation on Banda Besar; Mardijker allocation; company price and tax constraints; expertise of enslaved workers; resistance and environmental continuity. Directly supported by S. J. Miske, published online September 2026.
5. **1631 Banda enumeration:** Source window adds 797 enslaved Bandanese, 1,164 recorded as from Malabar, 410 others, explicitly as categories in a dated VOC enumeration **as presented by Miske**, not as definitive preconquest or complete-population totals.
6. **Makassar and Wajorese:** Clarifies that Bugis, Wajorese, and Makassarese were not equivalent categories; develops the 1666–1669 war and displaced communities with Leonard Y. Andaya. Includes Kathryn Anderson Wellen's analysis of matoa leadership and commercial-law networks.
7. **Johor–Riau 1722:** Expands the settlement from a general note to the formation of a recognized Bugis deputy-rule institution, **Yang Dipertuan Muda**, with Daeng Marewa commonly identified as the first holder. Avoids narrating its later eighteenth-century trajectory in detail.
8. **1686 Makassarese affair and 1679 Chinese refugee commanders:** Keeps the dated events with explicit context and source caution, separating the 1686 Ayutthaya conflict from 1688 and attribution of 1679 migration figures to later Vietnamese histories.
9. **Dependency and gender:** Strengthens legal distinctions among chattel slavery, debt bondage, corvée, inherited service and patronage; adds women, children, familial labor, coercion, and the limits of historical evidence. Based on comparative works by Reid and Brewster and Barbara Watson Andaya.
10. **Historical method:** Avoids invented first-person accounts, unsupported precise population totals, and falsely attributed original-document quotations. Distinguishes primary documents, published translations, modern scholars' interpretations, and editorial inference.

### 2. Consolidated historical review
1. **1611 Phú Yên ambiguity:** replaced categorical annexation with two carefully distinguished interpretations. Danny Wong Tze-Ken sees Nguyen Hoang defending and consolidating existing/contested frontier districts without a new invasion beyond the 1471 boundary; other historical reconstructions describe territorial annexation. Section 88.5 and chronology now explicitly distinguish administrative changes from disputed territorial inference. Section heading revised to match.
2. **1712 Nguyễn–Cham regulations:** replaced a generic document summary with a five-provision editorial paraphrase grounded in the published English rendering discussed by Danny Wong Tze-Ken in *Kyoto Review of Southeast Asia*: petition fees, mixed versus Cham-only adjudication, guard stations, travel/trading passes, and treatment of displaced Cham people near the Cambodian frontier. Explicit provenance, interpretation, five critical questions, source locator, and limits were added. No unverifiable first-person historical quotations were created.
3. **1728:** connects the revolt after Po Saktiraydaputih's death to attested grievances involving settlement, debt, land and economic competition while clearly separating scholarly interpretation from recoverable eyewitness motives.
4. **1679 refugees:** identifies Dương Ngạn Địch, Trần Thượng Xuyên (Trần Thắng Tài), Hoàng Tiến and Trần An Bình, with the transmitted approximate three-thousand-person/fifty-vessel tradition explicitly qualified. Notes scholarly doubts that the men arrived in one precisely dated episode.
5. **Prey Nokor c. 1623:** preserves the later-tradition date and clarifies that customs permission, settlement and territorial cession are not equivalent.
6. **Hà Tiên:** retains conventional 1708 dating, notes that some retellings give 1714, and refuses to invent an unsupported two-stage treaty.
7. **Pāṇḍuraṅga:** preserves Cham political and cultural continuity beyond the 1690s while distinguishing tributary kingship, diminished autonomy and later nineteenth-century incorporation.
8. **Historiography:** develops a dedicated narrative in §88.19 explaining why a retrospective *Nam tiến* pattern must not be mistaken for contemporaneous, unbroken national intent.

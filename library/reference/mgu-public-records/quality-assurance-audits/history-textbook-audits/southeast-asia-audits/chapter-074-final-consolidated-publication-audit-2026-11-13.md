## Historical-source checks and editorial interventions

1. **1565 Pacific return route:** Andrés de Urdaneta's role and Alonso de Arellano's separate earlier return distinguished; no unqualified “first crossing” claim. Navigation is explained through winds and northward routing rather than a single deterministic course.
2. **1593 regulations:** 11 January 1593 regulations identified as legal restrictions on vessels and declared trade values, not as observed shipment totals. The figures of two ships, up to 300 *toneladas* each, 250,000 pesos outbound merchandise and 500,000 pesos return flows are distinguished as regulatory ceilings. Published documentary transcription in Blair and Robertson, vol. XVII: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/15530/pg15530-images.html
3. **Manila urban chronology:** The Parián circa 1580–81, Binondo 1594, Audiencia 1583/1589/1598, and spatial distinctions between Intramuros, riverside communities, and Cavite are treated with necessary chronological variation.
4. **Silver flows:** Distinguishes Mexican/Andean mining origins, legal ports, private capital versus state remittances, Chinese monetary demand, and uncertainties arising from smuggling and incompatible valuations. No unsupported absolute silver-flow series inserted.
5. **Boletas, loans, situated finance:** Clarifies allocation versus beneficial ownership, the commercial structure of sales in Acapulco, maritime-risk financing, and why private merchant profits did not imply a balanced royal treasury.
6. **1603 and 1639 violence:** Neither crisis is reduced to a generalized ethnic clash; colonial policies, rumor, the role of particular authorities and allied troops, and the responses of merchants and families are distinguished. Supplemented the original with evidence of Ming-Fujian diplomatic responses to 1603 rather than categorical Chinese state indifference. See Xiaolin Duan, *Sillares* 4, no. 7 (2024): https://doi.org/10.29105/sillares4.7-135
7. **Documented shipyard labor:** Replaced a generic documentary-pattern window with a dated contemporary petition by **Fray Pedro de Sant Pablo**, “Compulsory Service by the Indians,” Dilao, 7 August 1620, published in Blair and Robertson, vol. XIX, pp. 71–77: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/16086/pg16086-images.html. Identifies author, date, proposal, purpose, perspective, and the limits of his evidence. Cross-references Sebastián de Pineda's 1619 shipbuilding report in vol. XVIII: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/15564/pg15564-images.html
8. **Revolts:** The 1649 Palapag/Sumuroy uprising and 1660 Maniago rising connected to documented labor, extraction, and other grievances, without assuming a single cause or inventing causal detail.
9. **1671 historical plan:** Identified as Ignacio Muñoz, *Descripción geométrica de la ciudad y circunvalación de Manila y de sus arrabales*, Archivo General de Indias, **MP-FILIPINAS,10**, catalogue date 8 November 1671: https://pares.cultura.gob.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/description/18779/imprimir. Students are instructed to inspect the authentic map; its view is not fabricated as a transcribed quotation.
10. **1587 and 1672 transpacific migration:** Treats Luzon-born persons on Unamuno's 1587 California landing as documented presence rather than a sweeping “first ever” settlement claim. The 1672 shift pertains to the legal status of people categorized as *chinos* in New Spain and is not misrepresented as universal abolition of slavery. See Tatiana Seijas, *Asian Slaves in Colonial Mexico* (Cambridge, 2014): https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/asian-slaves-in-colonial-mexico/1136CF8D42E50A4F5BA6DF10C091F6ED
11. **Naval losses:** Woodes Rogers's 1709 capture and George Anson's 1743 capture treated in their proper temporal context; no uncertain specific calendar day invented for the latter.
12. **Historiography:** Flynn and Giráldez on 1571/global trade, Schurz on the maritime-institutional history, Tremml-Werner on multilateral Asian political-commercial connections, and Seijas on enslaved Asian migration are attributed without creating an artificial three-way scholarly dispute.
13. **Paraphrase integrity:** Morga's 1609 account, Muñoz's map, and Sant Pablo's 1620 petition are identifiable; all three windows distinguish their editorial rendering from verbatim transcriptions. No invented source quotations were added.
14. **Selected references:** Thirteen annotated documentary and scholarly entries give students independent access paths, while explicitly noting that citations to published sources are not equivalent to manuscript-facsimile collation.

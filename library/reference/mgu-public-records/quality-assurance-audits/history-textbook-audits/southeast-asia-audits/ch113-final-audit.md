## C. Historical and documentary corrections

**1874 emancipation law (§113.7 and Source II).** Replaced an unresolved historical-note placeholder with the identifiable Thai Royal Gazette original, **vol. 1, sheet 23 (21 August 1874)**. Distinguished the statute's declining redemption valuation for children born from Chulalongkorn's accession from the more sweeping **1905 law**. The English translation in Hamilton King's 21 April 1905 dispatch, *Foreign Relations of the United States* 1905, document 930, supplies **sections 1–6**, including commencement, territorial exceptions, prohibition of newly creating bondage, and the four-tical monthly reduction of existing debt. No invented quotations were used. Sources: https://th.wikisource.org/wiki/หนังสือราชกิจจานุเบกษา/เล่ม_1/แผ่นที่_23 and https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1905/d930.

**1892–1894 ministerial chronology (§§113.9–113.10, chronology and Source III).** Important correction: **Prince Damrong was assigned the northern/Mahatthai portfolio in April 1892; provincial administration became consolidated in a nationwide Interior structure in December 1894**, as shown by Tej Bunnag's archival analysis. The earlier draft conflated these stages. The revised primary-source exercise is explicitly **a retrospective participant recollection by Damrong**, crosschecked against the Thai Finance Ministry chronology; it is **not presented as a verified transcription of the unexamined royal command**. Sources: https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:08391faa-da5e-41f5-8c0f-eafa67c696b3 and https://www.mof.go.th/about/history/.

**1902 Sangha Act (§113.13 and Source IV).** Located the actual Thai law as a transcribed text and identified its Royal Gazette print source, **vol. 19, no. 13 (29 June 1902), pp. 214–223**. Added distinct and auditable section-level summaries: **section 1** (territory-specific commencement), **section 3** (denominational practices and shared governance), **section 4** (four senior ecclesiastical heads and council), and **sections 5–13** (temples and abbots). Corrected the prior oversimplified claim of instant nationwide uniformity under one new Supreme Patriarch. Source: https://th.wikisource.org/wiki/ประกาศพระราชบัญญัติแลพระราชกำหนดต่าง_ๆ_รัชกาลที่_5/เล่ม_6/เรื่อง_6 ; scan metadata: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:พรบ_คณะสงฆ์_๑๒๑_(๑).pdf .

**Currency and administration (§§113.11, 113.15).** The 1860 coinage introduction and **1902–1908 transition** to a gold-exchange system were separated from unverified single-day claims. Policing in the 1860s is presented as a succession of initiatives rather than an entire national force supposedly established in a single year. Sources include the Ministry of Finance institutional history and scholarship on Siam's gold standard.

**Sources and bibliography.** Completed explicit edition metadata for Baker and Pasuk (Cambridge University Press, **fourth edition, 2022**) and Wyatt (Yale University Press, **second edition, 2003**), and supplied documentary/archival entries and direct links. The original narrative's unverified production notes were removed.

**Section 113.19.** Expanded analysis distinguishing decree, commencement, administrative implementation, and social effect. Reinforced how fiscal, provincial, military and religious reforms built upon one another while generating different forms of local resistance.

## E. Bibliographic and archival limits

The primary texts of the 1874 law, 1905 abolition translation, and 1902 Sangha Act are identifiable and linked. **The exact 1892 royal designation of twelve ministers was not directly collated**; its source window accurately uses participant testimony and a contemporary institutional context rather than claiming to reproduce the command. The historical Thai legal texts have not been independently certified as English legal translations. These limits do not invalidate the core chapter narrative, but they should remain transparent. No comprehensive archival collation, full WCAG audit, third-party live-host testing or deployment is claimed.

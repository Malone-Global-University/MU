## 4. Historical and documentary consolidated audit

**Verified scholarly/archival chronology and interpretations**

1. **1511–1530s**: Portuguese conquest of Melaka; dynastic exile to Bintan; Portuguese attack on the Bintan base in 1526; succession by Alauddin Riayat Shah II and establishment of Johor River centers. Pekan Tua / Johor Lama dates and later court locations are presented as approximate where sources vary. See National Library Board Singapore, *Old Johor Kingdom*; Peter Borschberg, *Urban Impermanence* (2021).
2. **1606 treaties**: Differentiate **17 May 1606** from the **23 September 1606 supplementary agreement** after the unsuccessful Melaka campaign. Source window names ANRI *Corpus Diplomaticum*, volume 1, pp. 41–45 and 47–48, and Borschberg's 2009 published study and English translation. Military cooperation, access to timber, trade restrictions and jurisdictional arrangements are identified as editorial summaries of recorded provisions, not invented quotations.
3. **1609–1610 diplomacy**: Separate news of the European Twelve Years' Truce from the earlier treaties and outline court factionalism and Johor's October 1610 accommodation with Portugal. Borschberg (2009).
4. **1613–1641**: Situate Acehnese conquest and dynastic captivity within Iskandar Muda's regional expansion, without duplicating Chapter 80. Differentiate Johor's role in the VOC–Johor victory **January 1641** from the VOC's retention of Portuguese Melaka.
5. **1673**: Identify Jambi as an autonomous Malay political rival whose attacks disrupted Johor's riverine court and trade, rather than portraying the period as exclusively European–Asian competition. Batu Sawar's history is grounded in Borschberg (2021) and Andaya (1975).
6. **September 3, 1699**: Distinguish the assassination of Mahmud Shah II and the accession of Bendahara Abdul Jalil IV from unverified dramatic stories about motives. The direct Melakan succession was interrupted and rival descent claims remained politically important; the text does not claim every genealogical possibility disappeared. Nishio (2001); ANRI *1713 Letter*, introduction.
7. **April 26, 1713 letter**: Source II specifically identifies a Malay royal message transmitted through the Dutch translation in the Batavia Castle journal, fols. 230–234. Reconstructs the Siak rights issue (connected with a 1689 treaty), requested powder and bullets, rice import from Java, and seized goods shipped to the Coromandel Coast. The source box clearly states editorial paraphrase, not a quotation. Official original edition: https://sejarah-nusantara.anri.go.id/id/hartakarun/item/07/translation_en.
8. **1718–1722 dynastic settlement**: Present Raja Kecil's claimed descent from Mahmud Shah as a *politically effective but disputed claim*, not authenticated biological succession. Describe his Siak–Minangkabau and maritime supporters, the displaced Bendahara family's Bugis alliance, and Raja Sulaiman's 1722 installation.
9. **Early Yang di-Pertuan Muda office**: Name Daeng Marewa (first officeholder, **1722–1728**) and Daeng Chelak (**1728–1745**) and explain negotiated power, marriage, commerce, and military resources without modern constitutional categories. Scholarship treats Bugis diaspora groups as politically varied, not one unified faction.
10. **Tuhfat al-Nafis**: Treat as Raja Ali Haji's text of c. **1866**—a primary source for nineteenth-century political memory, not eyewitness testimony for 1699–1722. Named critical editions: Matheson & Andaya (1982) and Matheson Hooker (1991). No specific manuscript passage or page is falsely claimed as collated.
11. **Riau trade chronology**: Distinguish the earlier eighteenth-century commercial system from the later English private-trader boom of the 1760s–1780s. The later 1824 Anglo-Dutch territorial settlement is not imported as a contemporary boundary of 1750.
12. **Orang Laut**: Explain varied *suku*, service, kinship, bargaining, and changing allegiances. Avoid treating all maritime obligations as slavery or all raiding as identical to modern piracy.

**Source limits:** The three primary-source features are **attributed editorial source studies**, not original-language transcriptions or facsimile-collated translations. The two historical maps are text-guided geographical exercises; a contemporaneous historical atlas/plate was not embedded. Scholarly verification was targeted to publication-critical dates, named persons, principal institution(s), archives, and interpretive hazards; **not** an exhaustive archive-by-archive collation of every factual sentence.

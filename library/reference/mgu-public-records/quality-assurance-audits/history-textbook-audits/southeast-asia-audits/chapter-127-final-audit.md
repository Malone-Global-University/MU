## IV. Substantive factual verification and corrections

**1. Nguyễn Thị Giang (Cô Giang).** The original text incorrectly called her Nguyễn Thái Học's sister. It now correctly identifies her as his **fiancée**, a VNQDD activist in her own right, and the sister of Nguyễn Thị Bắc (Cô Bắc). Both the introductory people reference and Section 127.15 were corrected. The expanded discussion resists reducing women's political action to a romanticized martyr narrative. Scholarly basis: historical research on Nguyễn Thị Giang and the VNQDD, including a Library of Congress-hosted scholarly work (https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/19/66/72/76/2019667276/2019667276.pdf).

**2. Alfred François Bazin.** The draft's **Hervé Bazin** identification was wrong. The revision identifies **Alfred François Bazin**, murdered in Hanoi on **9 February 1929**, and describes his private labor-recruitment business rather than calling him simply a French colonial official. It distinguishes individual attackers' possible party connections from proof of authorization by the VNQDD central committee. Verification: Michael G. Vann, “White Blood on Rue Hue: The Murder of ‘le négrier’ Bazin,” *Proceedings of the Western Society for French History* 34 (2006): 247–262, https://scholars.csus.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/White-Blood-on-Rue-Hue-The/99258044563801671.

**3. Nguyễn-dynasty examinations.** The original historical context prematurely ended the examination system in Annam in **1918**. It now states that the traditional examination system ended in Tonkin in 1915 and culminated in the **last court examination at Huế in 1919**. The Vietnamese National Archives document the 1919 court examinations and the 1918 memorandum planning them: https://www.archives.org.vn/gioi-thieu-tai-lieu-nghiep-vu/khoa-thi-hoi-cuoi-cung-trong-lich-su-viet-nam.htm. This distinction avoids conflating reforms introduced in 1918 with the final exam sitting.

**4. Yên Bái executions.** Thirteen executions on **17 June 1930**, including Nguyễn Thái Học and Phó Đức Chính, are supported by the Vietnamese National Archives' presentation of the contemporary case records. The report from **18 June** supplies a named contemporaneous administrative source. The revision distinguishes the February uprising from the June executions, legal prosecution, and later commemorative interpretation. https://www.archives.org.vn/gioi-thieu-tai-lieu-nghiep-vu/viet-nam-quoc-dan-dang-qua-tai-lieu-luu-tru-hai-ngoai-phap-ky-iii-phien-toa-xet-xu-tai-yen-bai-sau-that-bai-cua-cuoc-tong-khoi-nghia.htm.

**5. Wartime recruitment figures.** The narrative identifies **48,922 Vietnamese soldiers and 48,254 workers**, a combined **97,176 recruited for service in France during 1915–1919**, in the relevant scholarly accounting. These are **not** presented as 97,176 combat soldiers or as proof of uniform voluntarism. Source: Kimloan Vu-Hill's study in *Race, Empire and First World War Writing*, https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/race-empire-and-first-world-war-writing/sacrifices-sex-race-vietnamese-experiences-in-the-first-world-war/8EF5CE21A035DA1C090696C4E571F51B. Alternative scholarly tallies use somewhat different categories; the chosen figures are explicitly attributed.

**6. Phan Châu Trinh's 1906 letter.** The document remains identified as *Đầu Pháp Chính phủ thư*. A scholarly biography dates composition to **15 August 1906**, while an accessible transcription labels its text **1 October 1906**. The source window now states the disagreement and distinguishes source witnesses and later printing, including the 1926 *Nam Phong* republication, rather than claiming to have solved the variation. References: https://vietnamjournal.ru/2618-9453/article/view/109107 and https://vi.wikisource.org/wiki/%C4%90%E1%BA%A7u_Ph%C3%A1p_Ch%C3%ADnh_ph%E1%BB%A7_th%C6%B0.

**7. Phan Bội Châu's *Việt Nam Vong Quốc Sử*.** The 1905 first printing and the relationship with Liang Qichao are supported by a National Archives of Japan exhibit, https://www.archives.go.jp/event/jp_vn45/english/ch03.html. The source window identifies its content as a **paraphrase** and refrains from inventing direct quotations from the original Chinese-language text.

**8. The Saigon press.** *La Cloche Fêlée* began on **10 December 1923**. A documented retrospective bibliography identifies an article in **issue 3, 24 December 1923**, describing a gathering criticizing colonial capitalist interests. The source window identifies the specific issue but **does not invent a French article title or claim to have transcribed the original**. Bibliographical verification: https://www.leminhquoc.vn/hoi-hoa/tu-lieu-le-minh-quoc/1823-nguyen-an-ninh-a-la-cloche-felee.html?start=2.

**9. Vietnamese women's journalism.** The founding of *Phụ Nữ Tân Văn* on **2 May 1929**, together with its representation of women from all three regions on the cover, is supported by ISEAS – Yusof Ishak Institute's library history: https://www.iseas.edu.sg/library/blog/highlights-happenings/first-womens-newspapers-in-vietnam. The revision discusses women's independent organizational and intellectual agendas, not only their participation in parties led by men.

**10. Ba Son, Cổ Am, and student mobilization.** The Ba Son strike in August 1925, protests and school disruption following Phan Châu Trinh's death in 1926, and the February 1930 Cổ Am bombing were retained as historical events. The revision distinguishes documented actions from claims about the uniform political intentions of workers, participants in commemorations, or residents and insurgents in different localities. No unsourced death or membership totals were introduced.

**11. Geographic and administrative differentiation.** The revised text consistently distinguishes Cochinchina (colony), Annam (protectorate), and Tonkin (protectorate with strongly direct French administration), and treats the Indochinese Union as a larger colonial structure rather than a Vietnamese nation-state. Cambodian and Lao political futures are not assumed to be part of a Vietnamese nationalist program.

**12. Nonteleological interpretation.** Phan Bội Châu's royalist-to-republican transition, Phan Châu Trinh's democratic civic reform, Saigon constitutionalism, the VNQDD, religious institutions, women's journalism, and popular protest all retain independent political significance. Communism remains a contextual emerging force, not the predetermined destination of the narrative; the detailed history is reserved for Chapter 128.

## VII. Remaining limitations and editorial disposition

The following limitations are specifically **disclosed rather than concealed**:

1. The original French text of *La Cloche Fêlée* issue 3 and the French manuscript of the 18 June 1930 report were **not directly transcribed or compared line by line**. The chapter uses traceable issue/document identification and clearly labeled paraphrases. Original quotations from these documents should not be added without facsimile verification.
2. The 1906 letter's exact composition/dispatch/publication chronology remains disputed among accessible witnesses. The chapter does not force reconciliation.
3. Contested estimates, notably wartime service categories, political participation, arrests, and the intentions of Ba Son strikers, are handled cautiously. They are not silently converted into a single authoritative total or motive.
4. Some older monographs and editions in the bibliography have not undergone edition-by-edition catalogue and page-range collation; the major source-specific corrections above were checked. Full bibliographic verification of every entry would require an independent library-catalogue pass.
5. **Map art is intentionally deferred** by the publisher, with no penalty to the scholarly audit. Both geography studies and exercises are present.
6. Live-browser display, external-link uptime, and publication-server integration have not been independently tested during this revision.

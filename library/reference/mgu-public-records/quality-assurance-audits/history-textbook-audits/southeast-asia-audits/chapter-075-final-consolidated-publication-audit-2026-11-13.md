## 4. Historical verification and targeted corrections

1. **Orders:** Augustinians accompany Legazpi in 1565; subsequent major arrivals Franciscans 1577, Jesuits 1581, Dominicans 1587, Augustinian Recollects 1606. Arrival is distinguished from sustained regional missionary organization. Early primary expedition evidence is available in *The Philippine Islands* volume II: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13280/pg13280.html .
2. **Ecclesiastical organization:** Manila diocese erected 1579 and archdiocese established 1595 with three suffragans. Institutional legal boundaries are not equated with uniform conversion.
3. **Manila Synod:** Meetings were intermittent 1582–1586; the surviving handbook has incomplete and later-transmitted manuscript history. The chapter separates Salazar's dated 1582 letter from any purported complete transcript of synodal decisions. Scholarly reference: https://unipress.ateneo.edu/product/manila-synod-1582-draft-its-handbook-confessors-translated-english .
4. **1589 Plasencia:** Two Tagalog relations are identified as missionary reports with select informants. The 1589 source window is based on the edited translation, not a reconstructed indigenous voice: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13701/pg13701-images.html .
5. **1593 *Doctrina*:** Verified as printed Spanish/Tagalog catechetical material; the Library of Congress maintains an original digitized item showing Roman and indigenous scripts. No fixed circulation or literacy rates are inferred: https://www.loc.gov/item/48031307/ .
6. **Santo Tomas:** Founded as a college in 1611; raised to university status in 1645. Correct distinction verified through the institution's history and formal published statutes: https://www.ust.edu.ph/university-history/ .
7. **Property:** Early land ownership and contested transactions are distinguished from later fully developed friar estates. A specific later Calamba ownership history is used as a caution against backward projection: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-southeast-asian-studies/article/colonial-sugar-production-in-the-spanish-philippines-calamba-and-negros-compared/9247E908CBE71C724902321EDB1BC6AB .
8. **Resistance:** Tamblot (Bohol, 1621–1622), Bankaw (Leyte, 1622), and Tapar (Oton, Panay, 1663) are treated as distinctive movements rather than a single coordinated revolt. Colonial hostile source descriptions are distinguished from reliable proof of the participants' motives: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/50111/50111-h/50111-h.htm .
9. **Interpretation:** Phelan (1959), Rafael (1988), and Gerona (1998) are identifiable works on institutions/indigenous response, translation/power, and Bikol provincial religious politics, respectively. The historical debate presents complementary emphases without manufactured opposing camps.
10. **Regional agency:** Repeatedly distinguishes Philippine-language mediation, local religious specialists, lay assistance, ordinary households, Muslim political traditions, indigenous leadership, and administrative jurisdiction from simplistic conquest-to-conversion determinism.

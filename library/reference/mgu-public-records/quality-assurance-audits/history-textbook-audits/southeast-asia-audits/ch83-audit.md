## Historical verification and correction log

1. **1628 siege command (publication-critical, FIXED):** Tumenggung Baureksa led an early force in 1628; Mandurareja and Upasanta belonged to a later-arriving contingent **in the same 1628 operation**. The de Graaf historical reconstruction dates Baureksa's death with two sons to **21 October 1628** and the executions of Mandurareja and Upasanta to **December 1628**. The revised §83.14 makes this clear. §83.15 no longer assigns the executed commanders to the **1629** siege. The second expedition is instead discussed with its different chain of command, including Panembahan Purbaya in de Graaf's reconstruction. The first siege withdrawal is now accurately situated by early December 1628 rather than generically 'autumn.'
2. **1616–19 expansion:** Siwalan is recognized as a 1616 engagement in the accepted de Graaf–Pigeaud reconstruction. Lasem and Pasuruan are discussed with approximate 1616–17 chronology rather than unsupported exact-day dates; Tuban's conquest remains in 1619.
3. **Madura and Prasena:** §83.6 identifies the 1624 campaign and the subsequent accommodation of Prasena/Cakraningrat in qualified dynastic-tradition terms, with no artificial certainty about a title-conferral date.
4. **Surabaya, 1625:** §83.7 now analyzes sustained pressure, the reported water-supply interference, the political surrender, relocation or incorporation of defeated elites, and the Surabayan prince Pangeran Pekik's subsequent marriage relationship with Agung's family. Siege techniques recounted in historical tradition are attributed rather than presented as direct eyewitness certainty.
5. **Pati, Giri, and Blambangan:** §83.8 distinguishes the Pati rebellion of 1627, religious authority at Giri and its reduction around 1636, and an unsuccessful 1635 Blambangan offensive from renewed campaigns in 1639–40, without claiming durable complete incorporation of the eastern salient.
6. **1629 logistics:** §83.15 differentiates planned food depots from the field siege, the VOC attacks on coastal provisions (Tegal verified in de Graaf/Pigeaud), the military and environmental consequences, and the death of Governor-General Coen on **21 September 1629**. It does not invent troop/casualty totals or assume an exact unverified road itinerary.
7. **1633 calendar:** The simplified, incorrect description of the old Saka calendar as merely 'solar' was refined to **Indian-derived lunisolar reckoning**; the reform kept Saka-era year numbering while adopting Islamic lunar year length. Adoption is not assumed uniform across Java.
8. **1641 sultan title:** Preserved the scholarly account of Agung's diplomatic connection with Mecca and adoption of the title in 1641, without claiming that foreign recognition guaranteed political obedience at home.
9. **1645/1646 death:** Preserved the conventional 1645 versus de Graaf's first-half-of-February-1646 distinction, explicitly disclosed in the chronology and §83.19. The 1646 succession and reconciliation with the VOC follow the attributed de Graaf/Pigeaud reconstruction.
10. **Amangkurat I:** Expanded §83.19 into phases: 1646 peace, development of Plered, contested accounts of repression, port supervisory arrangements and trade tensions in the 1650s/early 1660s, Van Goens's 1648–54 embassies, and the coalition and succession crisis of 1677. Repression and port restrictions are not described as a single uniform 31-year embargo.
11. **Historical interpretation:** The agrarian-maritime distinction is maintained as a matter of relative institutional emphasis, not a claim that maritime polities lacked agriculture or that Mataram had no merchants. The VOC's intervention after the crisis is not portrayed as an inevitable unilateral European conquest.

## Primary-source studies and limits

The chapter retains three documented historical source studies:

- **Javanese dynastic chronicles — *Babad Tanah Jawi*:** correctly labeled as later, multi-recension court memory, not a literal eyewitness account of Senapati. The chapter draws no supposed verbatim quotation from an unverified recension.
- **VOC *Daghregister* relating to the 1629 Batavia campaign:** the published daily journal and de Graaf's documentary reconstruction are identified. The editorial paraphrase is not falsely represented as a page/entry-specific literal transcription.
- **Rijklof van Goens, missions 1648–54 and retrospective *Javaense Reyse* of 1656:** properly limited to the court of **Amangkurat I**, not Sultan Agung. The summarized impressions are attributed and source-critical.

All three windows retain creator, context, type, paraphrase, analysis, perspective, limitations, and student questions. The new source register identifies scholarly editions and repositories. The **boundaries of verification** are deliberate: exact recension/page-level collation of the *Babad Tanah Jawi*, a particular printed 1629 *Daghregister* entry, and Van Goens's original Dutch passage are not represented as accomplished. This is a scholarly **textual first edition**, not a facsimile documentary edition. Atlas-based map exercises are not represented as original reproduced historical maps.

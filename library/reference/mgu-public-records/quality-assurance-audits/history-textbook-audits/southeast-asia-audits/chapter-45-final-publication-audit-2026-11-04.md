### Historical-source review and corrections

1. **1044 and 1069:** distinguished Vietnamese chronicle traditions from modern identifications of Phật Thệ/Vijaya, Chế Củ/Rudravarman, and the hypothesis that the 1069 attack concerned Châu Sa. Included discussion of Cham inscription **C.30A1 (1084)** and the regional political complications of the documentary record. Source: Michael Vickery, *Champa Revised* (2005), particularly pp. 48–54 of the paper.
2. **Twelfth-century Cham–Khmer wars:** removed the assertion that a 1177 Cham capture of Angkor is directly established by Chinese and Khmer records. The *Song huiyao* attests a boat-borne Cham attack on Cambodia, whereas the supposed occupation of Angkor is a disputed reconstruction; the narrative now presents Vickery's objection and the distinction between documentary statements and later historiography. See Vickery, pp. 56–67.
3. **1306–1312:** preserved the attested marriage and reported territorial transfer but did not turn the alleged funerary requirement into a verified Cham custom. Distinguished the **traditional 1312 story** from Brian A. Zottoli's argument that the ruler **Chế Đa Da** is not securely identifiable with the encampment leader **Chế Chí / Phan Trắc**, and that an attack on Câu Chiêm belongs to **1311**. See Zottoli (2011), Chapter 2, printed pp. 59–61.
4. **1370s–1390:** expanded contextual analysis of Cham strategy, Trần political vulnerability, river warfare and civilian costs. Preserved the conflict between the Vietnamese battlefield account of Chế Bồng Nga's death and the *Ming shi* narrative about A-da-a-zhe and the minister Ge-sheng; neither incompatible narrative was quietly combined or adopted unqualified.
5. **1402–1446:** distinguished territorial and dynastic claims from occupation and effective administration. Expanded the connection between Ming intervention, Cham diplomacy and the 1446 campaign, including the contrast between the temporary seizure of Vijaya and the later conquest. Interpretive details depend chiefly on Wade's translation and Vietnamese chronicle tradition.
6. **1470–1471:** grounded the royal-edict discussion in John K. Whitmore's published translation on Columbia's *Asia for Educators* site, explicitly identifying ancestral rhetoric, allegations, and rhetorically large mobilization claims. Balanced with the *Ming shi* report of Cham claims, devastation and post-conquest succession; did **not** adopt disputed casualty figures as measured totals.
7. **After 1471:** maintained the documented Cham claimant diplomacy and the survival of southern polities, especially Panduranga, without treating the fall of Vijaya as the disappearance of all Cham governments and culture.
8. **Social and economic history:** substantially developed the accounts of captives, households, land, settlement, waterways, upland trade, religious patronage, artistic transmission and diaspora; explicitly distinguished evidence from explanatory inference where ordinary people's voices do not survive.
9. Removed work-in-progress remarks about “this draft,” “the audit,” unconsulted readings, and Wikipedia-level summaries from student-facing historical prose. Included a **Selected Historical Sources and Evidence Guide** inside the chapter.

### Evidence limitations — not concealed

The resulting manuscript is a **source-qualified historical synthesis**, not a new philological edition of all Cham inscriptions or an independently collated translation of every Vietnamese annal. Exact chronicle entries for some early and late campaigns—including 1104, 1252, disputed episodes in 1318 and 1326, and tactical details of 1377/1446—were not all independently inspected in a primary edition. The chapter therefore marks such claims as chronicle reports, avoids unverified precise troop and casualty figures, and does not pretend that its modern scholarly interpretations are unanimous. Whitmore's full 2004 and 2011 specialist publications were not independently inspected in full; specific facts drawn only from secondary summaries were removed or reframed as uncertainty.

These limitations should remain disclosed to users seeking a **critical source edition**; they do not require another speculative narrative-expansion cycle before the current qualified textbook edition can be published.

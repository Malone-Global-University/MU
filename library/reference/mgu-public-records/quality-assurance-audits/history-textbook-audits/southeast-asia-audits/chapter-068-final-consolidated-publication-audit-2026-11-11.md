## 3. Historical and documentary corrections applied

1. **Cannanore/Kannur (1507):** corrected the major siege to 1507 (not 1506), and explained succession conflict, Kolathiri politics, Calicut-related opposition, and the issue of garrison relief. Supporting research: *Encyclopaedia of Portuguese Expansion*, “Cannanore” — https://eve.fcsh.unl.pt/en/places/cannanore.
2. **Goa 1510 versus capital transfer c. 1530:** identified Goa as a major territorial, fiscal, and naval base after its recapture in 1510, but distinguished it from the Estado’s principal administrative headquarters at Cochin until roughly 1530. Supporting research: Zoltán Biedermann, “The Portuguese Estado da Índia (Empire in Asia),” *Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Asian History* (2020), DOI 10.1093/acrefore/9780190277727.013.329.
3. **1513 Red Sea campaign:** removed the assertion that Albuquerque “wintered” in the Red Sea, noting the unsuccessful attack on Aden, shelter near Kamaran for months, and return by approximately August 1513. Reference context: accounts of Albuquerque’s Aden campaign and the Cambridge historical synthesis; precise itinerary remains subject to primary-source comparison.
4. **Asian shipbuilding and naval tactics:** replaced an inaccurate generalized contrast between sewn Asian craft and supposedly superior European fastening with regional variation in sewn, lashed, pegged, and iron-fastened traditions. Included archaeological evidence from the Thaikkal-Kadakkarappally boat: Tomalin et al., *International Journal of Nautical Archaeology* 33, no. 2 (2004): 253–263 — https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1095-9270.2004.00022.x.
5. ***Roteiro* attribution:** retained anonymous contemporary participant authorship; distinguished the traditional Álvaro Velho attribution from E. G. Ravenstein’s preference for João de Sá. National Library of Ireland catalogue for Ravenstein, Hakluyt Society (1898): https://catalogue.nli.ie/Record/vtls000383726/TOC.
6. **Albuquerque letters versus later *Commentaries*:** separated contemporaneous correspondence from his son’s later retrospective narrative. Verified the complete *Cartas* seven-volume publication **1884–1935**, per Imprensa Nacional: https://imprensanacional.pt/?w3n_bib_digital=cartas-de-affonso-de-albuquerque.
7. **Third primary-source study:** added the later-sixteenth-century Arabic *Tuḥfat al-Mujāhidīn* from a Malabar Muslim scholarly standpoint, with its polemical objectives, retrospective character, textual layers, and limits of representativeness. Verified S. Muhammad Husayn Nainar’s translated/annotated edition: Islamic Book Trust / Other Books (2006 reprint of 1942), https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BB11166651. Further interpretive source: “The Pagan King Replies,” *Itinerario* — https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/itinerario/article/pagan-king-replies-an-indian-perspective-on-the-portuguese-arrival-in-india/1ED539544CB9F19550CBC77E85D6B6FD.
8. **Crown monopoly and *cartaz*:** separated Portuguese royal intentions from practical enforcement, retaining evidence of the continued importance of Gujarati, Malabar, Red Sea, and Persian Gulf commercial networks.
9. **Melaka knowledge:** replaced unsupported certainty that Melaka’s rulers knew particular Portuguese incidents with a qualified, evidence-conscious account of information transmission through Asian merchants; 1511 campaign details remain allocated to Chapter 69.
10. **Historical interpretation and social history:** expanded treatment of the 1502 *Miri* atrocity, violence at Goa, slavery, merchants, households, labor, naval debate, and the historiography of Panikkar, Steensgaard, Pearson, Chaudhuri, and Subrahmanyam. Fatality totals and broad commercial-displacement estimates remain appropriately qualified.

## 2. Historical and editorial revisions

- **§§96.1–96.4:** Reconstructed political fragmentation, Phraya Tak's service background, Chanthaburi's maritime and agricultural resources, return by river, the defeat of occupation positions at Pho Sam Ton, and limits of the claim that the entire kingdom had already been reunited.
- **§§96.5–96.8:** Developed Thonburi's capital geography and contested coronation chronology; the rival centers of Phimai, Phitsanulok, Phra Fang, and Nakhon Si Thammarat; unsuccessful and successful campaigns of 1768–1770; conditional pardons and administrative integration; rise of Thongduang and Bunma.
- **§§96.9–96.12:** Expanded the Phrakhlang, phrai service, supply and agricultural recovery, Chinese-connected trade and royal finance. A corrected and substantial primary-source study explains two Chinese translations of three letters sent in 1768, the dubious depiction of Taksin's request for imperial investiture, Li Siyao's recommendation to reject the communications, and Qianlong's approval. The lost Thai originals are not claimed to have been consulted or reconstructed verbatim.
- **§§96.13–96.16:** Expanded continued Burmese warfare, Lanna campaign and local Tai agency, Cambodian and Hà Tiên diplomatic complexity, and the Lao campaigns against Champasak and Vientiane. Political suzerainty, tributary arrangements, and effective administration are distinguished. Captive resettlement and reverence for the Emerald Buddha and Phra Bang are covered without fabricated troop, casualty, or resettlement totals.
- **§§96.17–96.20:** Expanded rural households, women's economic roles, artisans, migrants and captives, Buddhist manuscript recovery and royal patronage, disputed allegations used to explain the 1782 overthrow, and the historical legacy of Thonburi for the early Chakri monarchy.
- **Chronological caution:** The conventional dates of December 28, 1767 versus December 28, 1768 for royal coronation are represented as debated; assumption of kingship, ritual consecration, and formal government are kept distinct. Differing traditions concerning the precise execution/deposition chronology and some campaigns are acknowledged.
- **Source attribution:** The bibliography corrects *A History of Thailand* (2022) to the **fourth edition** (verified against Cambridge), and the primary-source window cites Hiroshi Kawaguchi's 2026 open-access study with a stable DOI. Nidhi Eoseewong's 1986 first edition is separately identified and linked to a library catalog; David K. Wyatt's second edition (2003) is linked to its publisher.

## 6. Disclosed verification limits

- The original probable Thai versions of the 1768 correspondence do not survive; the Qing-source window uses Kawaguchi's scholarly analysis of the surviving Chinese texts, clearly labeled as editorial paraphrase.
- No exhaustive paleographic collation of royal chronicles, court manuscripts, or local oral traditions was performed. Chronicle variants, dynastic political interests, uncertain battle-day chronologies, and contested personal accounts are disclosed in the chapter.
- The design reuses the tested **Chapter 95 publication edition** shell with Chapter 96-specific CSS and navigation adjustments. Exact byte-for-byte identity with the historical **Chapter 1** template has not been independently established.
- The Geography Study is a text-based guided map inquiry as in the MGU model; no historic cartographic reconstruction or embedded interactive map is represented as part of the downloadable HTML.
- The local Chromium validation establishes functional rendering on representative viewport widths, not deployment to maloneuniversity.org or exhaustive testing on every physical device and browser.
- Bibliographic links were checked at the source level during editorial preparation; this is not a claim that every link was live-checked from inside the air-gapped browser test.

## Historical corrections incorporated

1. **Gajah Mada:** Canto 49 documents a 1331 shift in responsibilities; the exact date of the senior-title promotion and the Palapa oath remain qualified as later-tradition issues. The office of *mahāpatih* is explicitly distinguished from sovereignty.
2. **Palapa oath:** Clearly attributed to the retrospective *Pararaton*; no invented verbatim words or securely dated contemporary transcript.
3. **Bali (1343):** Explicitly anchored to *Deśawarṇana* canto 49, while later details of commanders, itinerary and local political arrangements remain qualified.
4. **Dompo:** Canto 72 specifically mentions Pu Nala in association with an eastern military campaign; the courtly description is not treated as a factual casualty count.
5. **Bubat:** Accounts separated by authorship and date; Hall’s interpretation of an allusion in the Canggu preamble is presented as a hypothesis, not an incontrovertible primary narrative.
6. **Hayam Wuruk:** His leadership, ritual authority and dynastic role are preserved; the account does not reduce him to a ceremonial monarch subordinate to Gajah Mada.
7. **Canggu charter (1358):** About 79 ferry districts, legally defined status, and Hall’s interpretation of *dĕrwaya haji* / *pamuja* obligations are specified.
8. **Deśawarṇana and imperial extent:** Geographical lists from cantos 13–16 analyzed as a royal claim rather than a census of directly administered territory.
9. **Gajah Mada’s death (1364):** Correctly grounded in canto 71 and related reorganization in canto 72; no inaccurate “merely traditional” dating.
10. **Ming diplomacy:** Missions beginning in 1369 and subsequent contacts are treated as diplomatic transactions rather than evidence of extraterritorial government.
11. **Spice geography:** Clove networks in northern Maluku differentiated from the Banda nutmeg/mace region.
12. **Chapter boundaries:** Foundation in Chapter 49; detailed social economy in Chapter 51; political fragmentation in Chapter 52.

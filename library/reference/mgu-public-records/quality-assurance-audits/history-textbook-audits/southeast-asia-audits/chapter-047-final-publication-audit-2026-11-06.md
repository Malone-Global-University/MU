## Historical and source verification: significant findings

1. **Padang Roco language and date — corrected.** Arlo Griffiths's 2026 scholarly critical edition classifies the 1286 inscription as **Old Malay plus Sanskrit, written in Kawi script**, and dates it to **22 August 1286**. The introductory narrative, source-method table and specific source window have been aligned with this evidence. Source: https://dharmalekha.info/texts/INSIDENK00007 .
2. **Sanfoqi label — corrected.** The misleading literal reading of the Chinese characters as the name's etymology was removed. Similar Chinese transcriptions cannot establish unchanged dynastic or territorial identity.
3. **992 Java–Sanfoqi war reporting — corroborated.** Song-era records preserve reports from both a Srivijayan envoy who had difficulty returning and a Javanese embassy concerning hostilities. K. A. Nilakanta Sastri, “I. Sri Vijaya,” BEFEO 40 (1940), pp. 239–313: https://www.persee.fr/doc/befeo_0336-1519_1940_num_40_2_4796 . This is presented as diplomatic testimony rather than direct observation of battles.
4. **1003 Buddhist temple and bell — supported.** Song sources describe a Sanfoqi mission involving a Buddhist temple dedicated to the emperor and imperial bestowal of a name and bell. The manuscript treats this as diplomacy rather than direct Chinese rule.
5. **1025 Chola campaign — supported in broad outline**, including royal claims about captured Sangramavijayottungavarman and raids against named ports; individual place-name identifications and the exact motives remain contested. Hermann Kulke, “Śrīvijaya Revisited”; scholarship on Rajendra I.
6. **c. 1067–1068 Kadaram restoration — qualified** as a Chola royal inscriptional claim, not independent proof of continuous occupation.
7. **1079–1082 Jambi prominence — qualified.** Chinese records support embassies and diplomatic prominence, not a conclusively documented transfer of a single capital. A recent source-critical study: Johannes L. Kurz, “Did Jambi Really Become a Capital in Sumatra in 1079–1082?” https://repository.crossasia.org/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/crossasia_derivate_00000286/Did%20Jambi%20Really%20Become%20a%20Capital%20in%20Sumatra%20in%201079%20rev.pdf .
8. **1088 Tamil merchant-guild evidence — corroborated.** Lobo Tua/Barus inscription documents a merchant collective, not direct Chola sovereignty. Museum catalog: https://repositori.kemendikdasmen.go.id/8221/1/TREASURES%20OF%20SUMATRA.pdf .
9. **Atiśa–Suvarṇadvīpa** is treated as a biographical and religious transmission tradition with uncertain itinerary detail, not independently verified travel documentation.
10. **1286/1347 Amoghapāśa texts — distinguished:** the 1286 pedestal inscription refers to Kertanegara and Dharmasraya; the later Adityawarman inscription is separate and not evidence for the earlier event.
11. **Chola embassies in 1015, 1033, 1077:** the manuscript follows the conventional dates reported in Song diplomatic discussions, without claiming that direct embassies prove deliberate Chola efforts to destroy Srivijaya. No original Song entry-level collation has been undertaken in this audit.

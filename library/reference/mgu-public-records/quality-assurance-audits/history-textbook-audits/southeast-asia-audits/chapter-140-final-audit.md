## 4. Historical method and chapter boundaries

- Political agency is differentiated among the Thai state, government, factions, resistance organizations, ordinary civilians, Japanese authorities and Allied governments.
- The manuscript distinguishes initial coerced accommodation, the subsequent alliance, belligerence, territorial expansion, domestic resistance and postwar normalization. These are not treated as interchangeable.
- Nationalist territorial claims are placed in context rather than accepted as proof of prior exclusive sovereignty or contemporary popular consent.
- The economic discussion identifies the mechanisms linking exchange-rate policy, wartime expenditure, note issuance, import scarcity and the distribution of costs.
- Resistance is treated as a developing political coalition with rivalries and uncertain prospects, not a predetermined national success.
- The war crimes discussion remains brief and the wider history of forced labor, the railway, regional resistance, atrocities, and Allied return remains assigned to Chapters 141–145. Chapter 185 is foreshadowed rather than narrated.

## 7. Evidence limitations

Online primary-source records, searchable text, archival descriptions, publisher summaries and institutional histories were consulted. The original archival images of the alliance treaty and Thai-language proclamation were not collated line by line, and the online treaty transcription warns that it is not human-proofread. Both source windows therefore use paraphrase rather than purported exact quotations. The IMF full page returned an access restriction during direct retrieval; its indexed text was available and was used for the specified monetary claims in conjunction with other scholarship. Full monographs and archival collections were not examined page by page. Exact casualty totals and unverified secret-protocol wording were not introduced. The chapter's broad social-history examples distinguish analytical mechanisms from directly documented individual experiences.

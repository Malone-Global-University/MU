## 4. Factual corrections and source-critical review

1. **1629 relief coalition**: §§80.11 and 80.12 and the summary now identify **Patani** alongside Johor and Portuguese forces. Pahang is described as part of the broader allied political environment rather than assumed to be an independently enumerated force in every phase. Jorge Santos Alves, “Patani and the Luso-Asian Networks (1516–1642),” *Archipel* (2022), reports Patani's naval assistance and cites Portuguese testimony; fleet size is presented as a reported, not verified, count. D. K. Bassett's work contextualizes the alliance.
2. **The *Hikayat Aceh***: §§80.3 and 80.16 and the source window distinguish possible post-1636 dynastic commemoration from secure contemporary authorship; Leiden University/UNESCO says Safiatuddin was a **probable**, not certain, commissioner. The oldest manuscript Or. 1954 is dated **c. 1675–1700**; the text is not treated as an eyewitness administrative diary.
3. **Succession and the reported killing of the sultan's son**: §80.19 now distinguishes the contemporaneous **March 1637 Dutch reports**, discussed by Ingrid Saroda Mitrasing, from later moralizing tales that attach a particular reason or quotation to the execution. No later legend is promoted to independently authenticated fact.
4. **Regional institutions**: §80.4 states that later descriptions of *mukim*, *sagi*, and *uleebalang* cannot automatically be retrojected as a fixed standardized administrative map for 1607–1636.
5. **Nias and 1635**: §80.9 identifies c. 1624 Nias campaigns as a qualified secondary-source tradition without inventing details; §80.19 adds documented Portuguese reporting concerning renewed Acehnese pressure on Johor/Pahang and Patani in 1635, challenging a simplistic post-1629 collapse.
6. **Trade evidence**: §80.17 includes a contemporary Malay royal letter of 1615, linked to Annabel Teh Gallop's scholarly study and the Bodleian manuscript locator, as a check against assuming that European company reports fully represent Acehnese commercial objectives.
7. **Ottoman relationship**: §80.18 retains the distinction between Ottoman-Acehnese communications documented in the sixteenth century and direct seventeenth-century diplomatic claims that cannot be supported equivalently. See Anthony Reid, “Turkey as Aceh's Alternative Imperium,” *Archipel* 87 (2014): 81–102.
8. **Critical dates**: The reign remains 1607–1636; the failed Portuguese Melaka siege is assigned to 1629; the Dutch–Johor capture of Melaka is correctly in 1641, **after** Iskandar Muda's death. Al-Raniri's decisive religious program and *Bustan al-Salatin* belong under Iskandar Thani, rather than being retrojected into the earlier reign.

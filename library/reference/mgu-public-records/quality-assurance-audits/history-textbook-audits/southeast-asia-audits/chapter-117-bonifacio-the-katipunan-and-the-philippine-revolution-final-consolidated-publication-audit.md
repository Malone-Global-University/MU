## 2. Historical correction and expansion record

1. **1892 chronology:** Corrected the opening and chronology table to distinguish the founding of La Liga Filipina (July 3), Rizal's arrest (July 6), Katipunan foundation (July 7), departure for deportation (July 15), and arrival in Dapitan (July 17). The revolution was organized after his arrest but before his departure into exile.
2. **The Cry of 1896:** Expanded examination of contested commemorative dates and locations, witness recollection, differing contemporary and retrospective testimony, the reported tearing of *cédulas*, and the historical meaning of choosing a single moment of beginning. The official NHCP memorial date (August 23 at Pugad Lawin) is distinguished from proof of a single uncontested meeting.
3. **Institutional and military development:** Added substantive treatments of the Katipunan's evolving hierarchy, clandestine vulnerabilities, recruitment, supplies, colonial coercion, Cavite logistics, regional government and the jurisdictional roots of factional conflict.
4. **Social and regional history:** Expanded the roles of women's networks, artisanal and clerical constituencies, rural households, civilian responses to repression, and local political actors; incorporated the Cebu uprising of April 3, 1898, the Negros proclamation of November 5, and the Revolutionary Government of the Visayas inaugurated at Santa Barbara on November 17.
5. **Tejeros and Bonifacio:** Expanded analysis of rival institutional claims, the electoral meeting, wartime military emergency, contradictory partisan testimony, stages of the proceedings against the Bonifacio brothers, and the location of their execution at Nagpatong near Mount Buntis on May 10, 1897. No unsupported definitive account of disputed individual responsibility is asserted.
6. **Biak-na-Bato:** Replaced blanket numerical uncertainty with an attributed schedule of **800,000 Mexican pesos (400,000 + 200,000 + 200,000)** from Aguinaldo's account, while separating promised sums, delivered funds, and disputed subsequent allocation. A historical compilation identifies an initial 400,000 pesos taken to Hong Kong but conflicting claims regarding later amounts.
7. **1898 sovereignty:** Expanded the distinction among the independence proclamation, territorial control, foreign recognition, the August surrender of Manila, international treaty arrangements, and the challenges of organizing a representative government.
8. **Malolos chronology:** Clarified that work on the constitutional document began in 1898 but that formal congressional adoption occurred **January 20, 1899**, followed by presidential promulgation January 21. The comparative Primary Source Window explicitly labels the 1899 constitutional document as a forward-looking comparison, not an operative December 1898 law.
9. **Bibliography:** Corrected the 1996 publisher of Glenn Anthony May's *Inventing a Hero* to **University of Wisconsin, Center for Southeast Asian Studies**; added a document-level reference register and actual source links in all four Primary Source Windows.

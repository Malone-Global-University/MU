## Main factual and documentary resolutions

1. Separated Naresuan's conventional 1584 rejection of Burmese overlordship from his 1590 accession; treated famous battle stories as source-dependent chronicle traditions.
2. Explained that sakdina rank is not automatically equivalent to actual private landholding; phrai categories and labor schedules vary by period.
3. Distinguished debt bondage, other forms of slavery, and registered compulsory labor; considered women and the nonelite household without fabricating testimony.
4. Treated Chinese, Japanese, Persian-connected, Muslim and European communities as historically active, heterogeneous, and changing groups. Verified Yoneo Ishii's journal paper and a contemporary scholarly reassessment of seventeenth-century Muslim identity.
5. Made the 1688 debate multi-causal, including the religious-mobilization interpretation (Alan Strathern) alongside court succession, French armed presence, and commercial competition.
6. Replaced a generic, nearly empty VOC paraphrase with an extended **source study of the Phrakhlang's letter of 12 February 1689 and Batavia's response of 4 May 1689**, grounded in the published English archival edition. It distinguishes Phetracha's official accusations against Constantine Phaulkon from independently established facts.
7. Identified the primary letter as an ANRI-held Dutch record of a Malay letter, not a directly collated Siamese original. English translation credited in the external archival publication to Rosemary Robson. Archive HR 2504, fols. 127–139.

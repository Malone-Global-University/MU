## 2. Substantive historical corrections completed

1. **Penfui (November 9, 1749):** Corrected the reversed battle outcome. Gaspar da Costa's Topass-led coalition was defeated by the Dutch East India Company and its multiethnic local allies. Explained the death of da Costa, western Timorese shifts in allegiance, and limits to the outcome. Reference: Hans Hägerdal, *Lords of the Land, Lords of the Sea*; Portuguese maritime academy's study of Lifau (1702–1769). The event also appears in the opening chronology.
2. **Dampier (1699 voyage; 1709 text):** Identified the correct publication as *A Continuation of a Voyage to New Holland, &c. in the Year 1699*, London, 1709, and the section on Laphao. Corrected the source window and bibliography, and linked to the full text rather than representing the 1703 volume as the source.
3. **Timor's status (1896/1909):** Corrected 'separate province in the 1890s' to an autonomous district separated from Macau by a decree of October 15, 1896; recognized provincial status in 1909. Revised sections 120.8 and 120.14 and the administration timeline.
4. **1914 border award:** Identified the narrower question under Article 3(10) of the October 1, 1904 convention, concerning the frontier from the Noèl Bilomi to the source of the Noèl Meto. Explained the tribunal's adoption of the Netherlands' interpretation; it did not establish the entire border anew. Updated section 120.19 and Primary Source Window IV.
5. **Manufahi (1911–1912):** Expanded background, tax policy, Portuguese republican change, local alliances, campaign phases, logistics and Timorese auxiliary participation, civilian effects, and the consequences of the defeat. Retained the retrospective **15,000–25,000 deaths** estimate with explicit qualification. The number is not a contemporary exact death register. Did not invent an unverified account of Dom Boaventura's final fate.
6. **Colonial knowledge and customary authority:** Expanded historiographical distinction between Portuguese administrative categories (*reinos*, *reis*, military districts) and diverse Timorese political or ritual institutions, informed by Ricardo Roque's 2022 study.

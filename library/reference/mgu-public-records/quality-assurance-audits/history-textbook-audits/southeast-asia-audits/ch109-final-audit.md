## Historical corrections and documentary controls

1. **Raja Muda Hashim** — Corrected death to **February 1846** in the reference entry, rather than 1845.
2. **Brooke accession** — Distinguished 1841 claims or ceremonies from 1842 confirmation/installation accounts. The dated chronology no longer pretends one unqualified ceremony is universally established; see the Sarawak Government history and National Archives material.
3. **Sarawak protectorate** — Replaced unsupported blanket '14 June signing' description with the published agreement **5 December 1888**, while disclosing that negotiation and execution are dated differently in other sources. Source Window I identifies Articles **I, III, VI** using a public reproduced text. Source: https://www.sarawaktribune.com/11-protectorate-treaty-of-1888/ .
4. **North Borneo government** — Distinguished Brunei's **29 December 1877** and Sulu's **22 January 1878** instruments, Royal Charter **1 November 1881**, administration from **1882**, and protectorate agreement **12 May 1888**. Acknowledged disputed translations of Sulu grant and the separation of paper claims from effective local jurisdiction. Sources: https://sagc.sabah.gov.my/sites/default/files/law/CharterGrantedToTheBritishNorthBorneoCompany.pdf and https://sagc.sabah.gov.my/?q=en%2Fcontent%2Fagreement-between-her-majestys-government-and-british-north-borneo-company-dated-12th-may .
5. **Sarawak institutions** — Historical Council founded **1867**, later Council Negri title **1903**, rather than projecting a continuously named body backward. Source: https://duns.sarawak.gov.my/web/subpage/webpage_view/122 .
6. **Limbang and Lawas** — Retained Limbang's **1890** incorporation; distinguished Lawas **1905** from earlier Brunei-to-Brooke cessions because company-administered rights were transferred to Sarawak. Used year only, not an unverified exact day. Source: https://limbang.sarawak.gov.my/web/subpage/webpage_view/314/lawasdo .
7. **Brunei 1905–1906** — Kept Sultan Hashim's **3 December 1905** agreement and **2 January 1906** British signature distinct. Source Window III references supplementary Agreement **Articles I–II** and the contemporary assurances as reproduced by B. A. Hussainmiya, *Brunei: Revival of 1906* (2006), Appendix 4 p. 77 and Appendices 5–6 pp. 78–79. Source: https://fass.ubd.edu.bn/staff/docs/BAH/revival.pdf .
8. **North Borneo census** — Identified reported totals **104,527 (1901)** and **208,183 (1911)** and the compiled 1911 Chinese classification **26,002**. Explicitly disclosed the impact of changing enumeration coverage and administrative categories; did not infer a precise migration rate from the arithmetic difference. Contemporary directory reproduction: https://www.histsyn.com/1980/01/directoryandchronicle1921part037.html .
9. **Miri and resistance** — Kept oil commencement at **1910**, without projecting the mature industry backward; distinguished Rentap, the Bau uprising of **1857**, and Mat Salleh's resistance, avoiding a fictitious single anti-colonial movement.
10. **Historiography** — Added verified scholarly references: Robert Pringle (1970), Ian Black (1983), Graham Saunders (2nd ed.), and B. A. Hussainmiya (2006). Colonial terms such as 'piracy' are analyzed, not uncritically adopted.

## Limits and release disclosures

- Primary-source windows provide **marked analytic paraphrases** and exact document/article pointers; the file does not claim that every surviving treaty copy or Malay/Jawi original has been collated with a critical edition.
- Particularly sensitive territorial claims (Sulu grant and Limbang) are presented as historically contested interpretations, not as a present-day adjudication.
- The 1911 population figures are **reported colonial returns**, and the printed source used for a comparative total is a contemporary reproduction, not independently checked against every underlying enumeration sheet.
- Historical maps are requested through geography studies rather than reproduced as licensed cartographic images. No claim of cartographic publication is made.
- Linked documentary destinations were identified, but every downstream link, scan, and catalogue page was **not comprehensively tested for permanent access**. Two principal documentary URLs point to PDFs, for which precise edition and transcription checks may remain useful in later scholarly apparatus revisions.
- The original HTML shell and sidebar model were retained; a mobile index, responsive table wrappers and new CSS classes were introduced as accessibility repairs. Full assistive-technology certification (multiple screen readers, contrast audit and keyboard-only regression suite) has not been performed.
- This audit concerns **textual scholarly and technical HTML readiness**, not deployment, legal review, translation rights, or a certification by an external academic board.

## Historical and factual revisions

1. **Trịnh Lục Phiên chronology:** corrected from 1721 to **1718**, under Trịnh Cương, in chronology, main text, glossary, and the linked editorial explanation. The complete six-department system was formalized in 1718; earlier departments and later reforms are distinguished. Supported by Shin’ya Ueda (2008, 2015) and historical studies of the lordly court.
2. **1670 fiscal source:** identified **Trịnh Tạc** as the issuer of village-level *lệnh chỉ* on the twentieth day of the sixth lunar month of Cảnh Trị 8, not edicts issued in the emperor's name. Rewrote the source window using Kazuki Yoshikawa's analysis: household registers, fiscal equalization, land and poll taxes, supplementary dues, and exemptions. Clearly flagged editorial paraphrase and limits of actual-collection inference.
3. **Phú Xuân:** qualified political-residence history: **1687–1712**, transfer to **Bác Vọng (1712–1738)**, return to Phú Xuân **1738**; improved glossary.
4. **Political self-representation:** added Nguyễn Phúc Chu's diplomatic claims in correspondence with Tokugawa Japan during the 1690s, prior to Nguyễn Phúc Khoát's **1744** royal title, and distinguished audience-sensitive titulature from modern sovereignty.
5. **Institutional and regional balance:** both regimes relied on agriculture and commerce, though in differing proportions and institutional frameworks; removed interpretive overreach about a fixed opposition between a tax-based north and mercantile south.
6. **Social history and source method:** developed military provisioning, households, women, craftspeople, mobility, village burdens, migrant/merchant mediation, documentary silences, and the limits of retrospective court chronicles.
7. **Commercial evidence:** added documentary context for the VOC's northern presence **1637–1700**, changing silk/silver profitability, and Hội An's Chinese, Japanese, and inter-Asian trading networks, avoiding European-centered narratives.
8. **Mission history:** expanded the collaborative romanization history with Francisco de Pina, Gaspar do Amaral, António Barbosa, Alexandre de Rhodes, and Vietnamese teachers and catechists; did not ascribe invention of the script to one person.

## 2. Historical/source corrections directly incorporated

**Minye Tujuh: personal identification, two inscriptions, and dating.** The source window no longer treats *Nur Ilah* as a settled decipherment or attributes that interpretation to Willem van der Molen. The Malay text is described according to van der Molen's revised reading as commemorating a royal daughter; *al-malika Wabīsa* is explicitly attributed to the Arabic-epigraphic reading used by Perret and colleagues. The personal name remains contested between scholarly traditions. The 781 AH/1380 CE Malay and 791 AH/1389 CE Arabic inscription dates are preserved, but the nine-year discrepancy is now explained primarily through the scholarly hypothesis of an incorrectly inscribed year, not through an unjustified assumption of separate commemorations. The material's provenance, social limits, and scripts remain clearly stated. Sources: van der Molen (2007), https://doi.org/10.1163/22134379-90003689; Perret et al. (2020), https://doi.org/10.4000/archipel.2061.

**Pengkalan Kempas: dual dates and scripts.** Removed the erroneous **874 AH / 1467–1468** pairing. The chronology, §§65.3/65.5, related tables, and people/place reference now specify **1385 Śaka / 1463–1464** in Palaeosumatran script and **872 AH / 1467–1468** in Jawi. This distinguishes the two records and avoids confusing the location with Troloyo's unrelated 874 AH epitaph. Source: Perret et al. (2020), https://doi.org/10.4000/archipel.2061.

**Trowulan versus Troloyo.** Reworked the narrative, chronology, §65.5 feature timeline, regional comparisons, Java discussion, people/place list, and review questions. The **1368–1369** stone belongs to **Trowulan** and is not secure proof of a Muslim burial. The unequivocal **Zain ud Din** epitaph associated with **Troloyo** is **874 AH / 1469–1470**, and the original object no longer survives. Earlier Troloyo stones are not presented as securely identified Muslim epitaphs. Sources: Hadi Sidomulyo, “Gravestones and Candi Stones: Reflections on the Grave Complex of Troloyo” (2012–2013), https://doi.org/10.3406/befeo.2012.6153.

**Al-Malik al-Salih.** Restored the distinction between **the recorded 1297 death** and the probable much later manufacture of the surviving funerary markers, aligning Chapter 65 with the Chapter 63 scholarly standard. Corrected direct narrative, chronology and learning table, as well as the person glossary. This does not deny earlier Muslim state formation; it prevents treating a later-produced artifact as automatically contemporary to its dated event.

**Nahrasyiyah (1428).** Retained the established royal memorial date but replaced claims that the tomb alone proves sovereign rule or a specific exercise of political power. The chapter now refers to a high-status royal woman and treats the extent of authority as a separate question.

**Indian Ocean exchange.** Replaced the overgeneralization that Muslim merchants dominated the whole Indian Ocean with an explanation of significant but variable Muslim involvement within a multiconfessional trading system.

**Bali as a regional comparison.** Strengthened the §65.19 example with a qualified discussion of royal institutions, temples and local ritual continuities; revised §65.20 to distinguish evidence of nonconversion from certainty about the causes. Avoids presenting Bali as a static survival untouched by historical change.

**Retained accurate or well-qualified elements:** Terengganu's competing 1303/1308 interpretations; Gresik's 1419 Malik Ibrahim burial inscription versus later hagiography; three clearly marked **editorial paraphrases** (not collated literal translations); caution concerning late *Wali Sanga* memory and *santri–abangan* anachronisms; the sixteenth-century status and traditional attribution of *The Admonitions of Seh Bari*.

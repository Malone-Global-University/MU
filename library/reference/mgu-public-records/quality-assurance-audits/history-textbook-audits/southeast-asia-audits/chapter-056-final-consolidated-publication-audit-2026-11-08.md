### V. Historical verification and interpretation
**Dates and founding:** The incorporation of Hariphunchai is stated as **1281 or 1292**, reflecting divergent traditions and modern treatments; the Chiang Mai foundation is conventionally **1296**. Mangrai's death is marked traditionally **1311**. The chapter distinguishes political transfer, intermediate Wiang Kum Kam, and growth of Chiang Mai's longer-term authority. Evidence checked against Hans Penth (2004), Sarassawadee Ongsakul (2005 English edition), Wyatt and Aroonrut (1998), and the official Chiang Mai tentative-list entry (2015), whose heritage narrative is not accepted uncritically.

**Administration and population:** Distinguishes mueang, panna, and nai sip; avoids presenting these institutions as universal at every date. Includes **1404 Ming documentary recognition** of commissions associated with Chiang Mai and Chiang Rai/Chiang Saen, mediated through Grabowsky's 2005 use of *Taizong Shilu* 31:563–564. Explains local authority, dynastic provincial appointments, rice, military manpower, and recurrent regional autonomy. Source: Grabowsky 2005, *Journal of the Siam Society* 93:1–68, and his 2004 NUS ARI working paper.

**Irrigation and trade:** The muang fai discussion recognizes local expertise and avoids anachronistic projection of modern community practices into the thirteenth century. Vanpen Surarerks's 2006 article in *Journal of Developments in Sustainable Agriculture* 1(1):44–52 (DOI 10.11178/jdsa.1.44) is identified as comparative contemporary research, not medieval evidence. Commerce and craft descriptions distinguish well-supported networks and plausible activity from uncertain quantities and market details.

**Dynasty and warfare:** Successors are contextualized without overconfidence in reign chronologies; Tilokaraj's conventional 1441–1487 reign includes southern campaigns and conflict around Chaliang/Si Satchanalai, with population transfers analyzed cautiously through Grabowsky's scholarship. Yot Chiang Rai and Mueang Kaeo receive dedicated discussion at the end of the century.

**Religious history:** Hariphunchai's pre-Mangrai Buddhist inheritance is preserved; Sumana's and Wat Suan Dok's dates are qualified; *Jinakālamālī* is identified as a **later Pali religious history**, rather than a contemporary account. Wat Pa Daeng/Wat Suan Dok religious competition is considered with respect to monastic legitimacy and property claims. The **1477 council** is a later-tradition report, not established as the single moment of religious unification. The 1968 Jayawickrama edition was checked against publisher/library records.

**Wat Mün Lò:** Grabowsky (2005) specifically discusses an inscription dated **1487/1488** as evidence for tax relief for new rural settlers. The final window clearly says it **summarizes Grabowsky's scholarly analysis** and has not been collated with the inscription's original-language text. Crucially, the chapter distinguishes this form of settlement incentive from monastic gifts of fields and dependent persons. No unsupported royal quotation or confident personal attribution to Tilokaraj has been introduced.

**Regional identity:** Does not assume that Tai Yuan, Mon, Lawa, Khmu, Shan and other populations had a single ethnicity, culture, or political position. Does not treat legends about Cāmadevī or local ritual guardians as independently demonstrated eyewitness history. Modern descriptions of the political system are not projected onto the Middle Ages without disclosure.

### IX. Disclosed edition limitations
- There has been **no exhaustive original-language collation** of the *Chiang Mai Chronicle*, *Jinakālamālī*, the Wat Mün Lò stone, or all relevant Chinese and northern Tai epigraphic witnesses.
- Source windows are clearly labeled **editorial paraphrases** or a **scholarly mediated summary**; they are not falsely represented as manuscript-verified quotations.
- Map and geography exercises are **text-based instructions** requiring an external historical atlas; this HTML contains no embedded historical map plates.
- Several early dates and dynastic relationships depend on later chronicles and are accordingly described as traditional, approximate, or contested.
- The publication decision concerns an evidence-conscious **first-edition textual HTML**, not an exhaustive new epigraphic edition or a peer-reviewed critical edition of every source.

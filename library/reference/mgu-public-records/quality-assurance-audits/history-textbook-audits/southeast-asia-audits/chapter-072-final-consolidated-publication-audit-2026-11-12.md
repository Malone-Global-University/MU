## 4. Substantive expansion completed

- **Political communities and commerce (II):** added a more explicit distinction between chiefly authority and stable bureaucratic jurisdiction; clarified the connection between Visayan regional exchange and datu followings; deepened Butuan's riverine networks, shipbuilding cooperation, and evidence limitations.
- **Sulu and Maguindanao (III):** differentiated documented Ming diplomacy, later sultanate genealogies, and the regional contributions of different linguistic and maritime communities; explained why Islamization and alliances in the Pulangi basin should not be presumed uniform or simultaneous.
- **Social history (IV, the main expansion):** developed the difference between Visayan *timawa* and dependent *oripun*; inheritance and relational status; debt, bondage and the temporal gap between the 900 inscription and 1589 Plasencia; gendered marriage arrangements, property/inheritance disputes, household decision-making, and the variability of women's practical agency; farming, boatbuilding, craft skills and source-preservation bias.
- **Religion, literacy, and interpretation (V):** added regionally situated ritual authority, variable literacies and the survival of different media, historical change between the 1521 encounter and 1565, and a four-source comparative synthesis.
- All expansion is integrated as **direct prose inside the existing 20 historical sections**, not as extra feature boxes or padded review material.

## 5. Historical and documentary audit

**Laguna Copperplate Inscription (900 CE).** The narrative now follows the publication's important distinction between the **chief and commander of Tundun**, acting for **Jayadewa**, associated with **Pailah**, and other named officials. It treats the identification of Tundun with later Tondo as a scholarly proposal, not proof of continuous political authority. It also avoids the false certainty of an exact 1989 recovery date and identifies the **late-1980s dredging / uncontrolled provenance** problem. Source window I specifically identifies the document's **lines 2–10** and Postma's translated passage on **pp. 186–187**; it does not claim an original facsimile collation. Postma's interpretation of kin relationships and titles is not treated as beyond dispute.

**Butuan boats and Mazaua.** The introduction and reference apparatus now use **excavated boats** rather than the misleading phrase “boat burials”; boat finds and human burials are separate types of archaeological evidence. Radiocarbon determinations are distinguished as applying to individual vessels and samples. The Pigafetta section differentiates Mazaua, the association with Butuan and the 1521 Easter observance rather than treating them as one location.

**Social ranks, enslavement and women's legal standing.** The Tagalog *maharlika* and Visayan *timawa* are not equated. Plasencia's 1589 evidence is tied explicitly to communities affected by Spanish rule, not silently generalized to c. 900 or to the whole archipelago. A sweeping comparison of women's property rights with European society has been replaced by source-bound language. Recognition of different forms of dependence never implies absence of coercion.

**Highland history.** The discussion retains Acabado's evidence-based reassessment of intensive wet-rice terracing while declining to infer that all earlier highland settlement or cultivation began in the sixteenth century.

**Chronological boundary.** Indigenous history before 1565 remains the principal subject, with 1521 as a contact episode and 1565 as the transition to Chapter 73; colonial conquest is not narrated here.

### Primary-source windows: explicit publication status

1. **§72.3 — Antoon Postma (1992), Laguna Copperplate Inscription**, lines 2–10, translation on pp. 186–187. The embedded passage is an *editorial paraphrase*, not a quotation. Full published edition is linked; the complete reading and paleography are more complex than the student summary.
2. **§72.11 — Antonio Pigafetta, April 1521 Cebu/Mactan passages**, published in Blair and Robertson, Vol. XXXIII (1906), with a source link. The window summarizes a dated episode and notes limits of interpretation, manuscripts, language and perspective.
3. **§72.15 — Juan de Plasencia, “Customs of the Tagalogs” (1589)**, Blair and Robertson, Vol. VII (1903), **pp. 173–185**. The adjacent, distinct relation on worship follows **pp. 185–196**; both should not be collapsed into a single 24-page document. The window focuses on social rank, marriage and inheritance and identifies the later colonial source problem.

All windows clearly label their accounts as editorial paraphrases. **They are not original-manuscript or original-edition-line-collated quotations.** Students undertaking exact textual comparison must consult the linked editions.

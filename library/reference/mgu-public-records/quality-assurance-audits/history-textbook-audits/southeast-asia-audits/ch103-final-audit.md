## Consolidated scholarly revisions

1. **Palembang (§103.5):** Distinguished military defeat in 1821 from the *18 August 1823 agreement* by which Sultan Ahmad Najamuddin III transferred governing authority while retaining specified ceremonial rights, and the subsequent elimination of the titular arrangement in 1825. The 1823 document is reproduced/discussed in the scholarly article linked below.
2. **London treaty (§103.2 and Source Window I):** Corrected the earlier false claim of 1825 ratification. Treaty signed 17 March 1824, ratifications exchanged **8 June 1824**; Article XIII provided for delivery of ceded settlements on 1 March 1825. Now explains Articles I–IV and VIII–XIII specifically, without treating bilateral agreements as consent by indigenous rulers.
3. **Padri War and West Sumatra (§§103.6–103.7):** Expanded religious reform, adat politics, commercial networks, competing Minangkabau institutions, Bonjol, colonial declarations, inland governance, and compulsory coffee. Qualified the date of the Plakat Panjang sequence rather than asserting a falsely simple chronology.
4. **Jambi (§103.8, §103.20, Source Window II):** Distinguished incorporation under Palembang in **1903**, the death of Sultan Taha in **1904**, and formation of a separate residency in **1906**. Added the 1833 political arrangement and specific provisions reported for the 1834 supplementary agreement (customs and salt). Reported dates in later secondary reproductions vary and are **not silently reconciled**.
5. **West Borneo (§§103.9–103.12):** Developed political structure and internal variation of Chinese mining federations, Montrado campaigns of 1850–54, the distinct 1884–85 Mandor/Lanfang episode, indigenous relations, Banjarese dynastic politics, and Anglo-Dutch delimitation in 1891.
6. **Banjarmasin (§103.11):** Corrected the characterization of Prince Hidayatullah's fate: **surrender under severe colonial pressure in early 1862**, removal, and exile, not simple capture on the battlefield. Clarified continuation of resistance after the abolition of the sultanate in June 1860.
7. **South and North Sulawesi (§§103.13–103.14):** Distinguished earlier Bone expeditions from the 1905–06 conquests; expanded Minahasa coffee, missionary education, local institutions, and military recruitment, rejecting the equation of less warfare with absence of coercion.
8. **Bali (§103.15 and Source Window III):** Developed Buleleng and Karangasem, the Jagaraga campaigns of 1846/1848/1849, Jelantik's leadership and uncertain precise death date, and Kusamba. Clarified that Michiels died of wounds on **25 May 1849**. The Buleleng regional summary reports a combined **300,000-guilder indemnity**, but other instructional summaries give differing figures; editorial text flags this as a source disagreement and does not pretend to collate the original treaty.
9. **Lombok (§103.16):** Distinguished the damaging Balinese counterattack on 25–26 August 1894 and **Van Ham's death on 26 August during the withdrawal** from the later renewed Dutch offensive and conquest. Expanded Sasak agency and post-conquest consequences.
10. **Lesser Sunda Islands (§103.17 and Source Window IV):** Distinguished the numbered territorial exchanges in the **20 April 1859 Dutch-Portuguese treaty**: Articles 1–8 cover Timor, Maubara/Oikoussi, Flores, Adonara and Solor; Article 9 covers the 80,000-guilder debt and 120,000-guilder payment; Article 10 covers freedom of worship. Corrected the generic source window with reference to the treaty's United Nations republication.
11. **Moluccas (§103.18):** Expanded commercial, religious, and diplomatic transformations without repeating the 1817 Pattimura narrative of Chapter 100.
12. **Western New Guinea (§103.19):** Identified Fort Du Bus as named for Governor-General **L. P. J. du Bus de Gisignies**; qualified the abandonment to the mid-1830s (1835/1836 vary in sources); developed the distinction between 1828 claims and the actual 1898 Manokwari/Fakfak posts.
13. **Synthesis (§103.20):** Expanded comparative categories and maintained Chapter 104's Aceh War as a distinct treatment.

## Primary-source reliability and provenance

- **1824 London treaty:** A directly identifiable text with numbered articles. The paraphrase is tied to actual provisions, not merely to the supposed character of colonial treaties.
- **1833–1834 Jambi agreements:** Specific clauses are documented in Indonesian regional historical reproductions and Locher-Scholten's treatment. **Original signed-language versions have not been collated directly**, and accounts disagree in some dating details. No passage is labeled an original quotation.
- **1846 Buleleng/Karangasem agreements:** Specific clauses are documented in the Buleleng government's regional historical account; the indemnity figures vary across educational accounts. **Original signed treaty not collated**. Contradictions are disclosed rather than hidden.
- **1859 Lisbon treaty:** United Nations reprint of the French treaty text in *Reports of International Arbitral Awards*, vol. XI, pp. 510–12. Article-based paraphrase is explicitly distinguished from a quotation. The large PDF could be text-inspected but was too large for the browser screenshot service; no claim of image-level collation is made.

## Remaining disclosed limitations

1. The 1833–1834 Jambi and 1846 Buleleng source windows are **documented secondary reproductions and paraphrases**, not diplomatically collated editions of original signed contracts. This is explicitly disclosed in chapter text; no unverified quotation is presented as a transcription.
2. Original atlas maps were **not embedded**. All three Geography Studies are substantial, text-based map exercises. Their adequacy as instructional material was checked structurally; a map-illustrated critical edition would be a separate project.
3. Exact comparison to an independently supplied Chapter 1 model file was not possible; the core CSS and script were preserved from the uploaded model-derived Claude manuscript.
4. External hyperlinks were curated to identified source/catalogue pages; not every remote target was exercised in a live deployed environment.
5. Rendering QA used local Chromium DOM injection, not live institutional deployment. No claim is made that the file has been uploaded to maloneuniversity.org.
6. Source collation at an academic critical-edition level and formal institutional peer review remain distinct from this completed textual first-edition audit. They are not represented as having occurred.

**Final decision:** Chapter 103 is complete and suitable for first-edition MGU textual HTML publication **with these documented limits**. No unresolved structural or narrative-depth blocker requires another chapter-wide revision; any future archival collation should be treated as a separate specialist improvement rather than a reason to reopen the established five-division plan.

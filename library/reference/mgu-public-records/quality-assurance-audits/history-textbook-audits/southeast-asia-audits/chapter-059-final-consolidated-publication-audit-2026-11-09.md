## 3. Historical corrections applied and verified

### Kyawswa and Yuan recognition

- Kyawswa's accession/anointing is **30 May 1289**, not the date of formal Yuan recognition.
- The Yuan emperor's official appointment is documented as **20 March 1297**.
- An inscription dated **17 December 1297** already calls Kyawswa a dethroned king; Chinese records instead place the removal in **June–July 1298**. These differing source traditions are preserved rather than flattened.
- Corrections made to opening, context, chronology preview, major-people apparatus, §59.4, §59.5, teaching timelines and source windows, and the review register.
- Primary basis: Than Tun (1959), printed pp. 119–120.

### Two sons, distinct careers

- **Maṅ Lulai / Saw Hnit**: Pagan successor supported by the Myinsaing brothers; Than Tun gives first audience **8 May 1299**.
- **Kumārakassapa**: different son, took refuge in Yunnan; the Yuan emperor recognized him **22 June 1300**; he entered Pagan with the **1301** Yuan expedition but returned with the departing troops.
- Source: Than Tun (1959), pp. 121–122. The manuscript now distinguishes these persons and their rival legitimate claims. No claim of continuous Yuan-backed rule by Kumārakassapa at Pagan remains.

### Yuan operation versus administrative end

- The invading army's retreat began **6 April 1301**; the Yuan abolition of **Cheng-mien** was **4 April 1303**.
- These dates are distinguished in §59.5, preview chronology and teaching timeline. Source: Than Tun (1959), p. 122.

### Geographic correction

- The geography study correctly places **Pagan (Bagan) downstream and southwest** of Pinya, Sagaing and Ava on the Irrawaddy. The previous upstream error has been eliminated.

### 1375 donor inscription

- The teaching window identifies the **widow of Toniū Man**, the date **Burmese Era 737 / 1375 CE**, and Than Tun's published locator **L.686b 8–10, S.737**.
- Her record refers to three military expeditions by Ava's king, hunger, cooked food for 37 monks, food for 200 others, and rice relief. All specifics come from Than Tun's printed English rendering (1959), pp. 129–130; the original rubbing was not directly collated.
- The old editorial placeholder was removed. The widow's social position remains a limited perspective, not proof of a general famine statistic.

### Ming–Ava diplomacy

- Replaced undated documentary gestures with a specific *Ming Shi-lu* entry dated **11 March 1396**, as cited by Fernquest (2006), and a **1447** Ming military instruction context discussed in Wade (2004).
- These are explicitly **mediated through published scholarship**, not presented as independently collated Chinese text or a translation newly made for this chapter.

### Chronology and ethnicity left properly qualified

- Pinya c. 1313; Sagaing c. 1315–1316; Thado Minbya's 1364 political takeover versus Ava's 1365 foundation; Swa Saw Ke c. 1367–1400; Mohnyin Thado's accession 1426 are retained as appropriately qualified conventional dates.
- The chapter still distinguishes Tai-speaking Shan principalities rather than asserting a unified ethnic army; limits on retrospective royal genealogies remain explicit.

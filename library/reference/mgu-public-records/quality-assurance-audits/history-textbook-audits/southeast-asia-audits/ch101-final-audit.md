## II. Historical corrections and expansion

1. **Ratu Ageng identity corrected across the chapter.** Diponegoro's great-grandmother, Ratu Ageng of Tegalrejo, died **17 October 1803**, rather than during the war. A distinct queen mother, **Ratu Ibu** (also styled Ratu Ageng), died in **June 1826**. The infant ruler's mother **Ratu Kencana** is also distinguished. Updated the regency account, biography, women and recruitment, chronology, and review references.
2. **1826 campaign reconstructed.** Added the dry-season resistance successes in July–August, conflict over the strategic approach to Surakarta, and the **15 October 1826** Dutch victory at **Gawok**; retained caution over incompatible troop counts. Explained why the subsequent phase did not mean immediate defeat.
3. **Kyai Maja (Mojo) revised.** His transition to Dutch custody on **12 November 1828** followed negotiations; different source traditions characterize it as surrender, capture or defection. The chapter does not impose an unqualified label.
4. **Mangkubumi's 1829 surrender added.** His September 1829 surrender is separated from **Sentot's October 1829** arrangement with the government, clarifying the loss of both dynastic prestige and military command.
5. **Benteng stelsel expanded.** Developed the connected system of forts, patrols, road and market control, supply and intelligence, civilian labor obligations and displacement. Compared tactical and economic effects of territorial containment.
6. **Magelang enriched.** Distinguished Diponegoro's expectations, De Kock's objectives, interim negotiations with Cleerens, the question of safe conduct, the **28 March 1830** arrest, and the representations created later by Dutch artists and Raden Saleh.
7. **Postwar state geography clarified.** The territorial consequences include annexations affecting **Kedu, Bagelen, Banyumas/outlying districts** and the **September 1830** reorganization of remaining court territories. Court rituals survived despite reduced revenue and autonomy.
8. **Civilian losses contextualized.** Approximately 200,000 Javanese and 15,000 Dutch-side troop fatalities remain described as **conventional estimates**, not exact counts. The text distinguishes European and Asian-recruited troops and explains agricultural disruption, requisitions, refugees and disease.

## IV. Primary-source transparency

- **Manado autobiography:** UNESCO's Memory of the World description identifies Diponegoro's autobiographical *Babad Diponegoro* as composed in exile during **1831–1832**. It is explicitly distinguished from Peter Carey's **1981 edition of the Surakarta court version**, a different narrative. <https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/babad-diponegoro-or-autobiographical-chronicle-prince-diponegoro-1785-1855-javanese-nobleman>; <https://www.si.edu/object/siris_sil_239698>
- **Dutch intelligence:** Jan Izaäk van Sevenhoven's **4 December 1826** memorandum, enclosing Mas Selaya's **17 October 1826** report, provides an identifiable research trail (Carey, *The Power of Prophecy*, chapter II, note 8; archival locator Dj.Br. 42).
- **Leadership fragmentation:** Lieutenant J. J. Roeps's **16 November 1828** letter to H. G. Nahuys van Burgst, discussed in connection with *Javasche Courant* (2 December 1828), and the studies of Kyai Maja's negotiations provide documentary points of comparison with the prince's narrative.
- **Magelang:** Kedu Resident F. G. Valck's **22 March 1830** memorandum concerning the prince's removal is identified as Netherlands National Archives, De Kock papers, NL-HaNA 2.21.005.33, inventory 24 (also a copy cited at Leiden/KITLV H 452a). Compare Carey, *The Power of Prophecy*, chapter XII and appendix XIII.

Primary source windows offer **attributed interpretations and research locators**, not original manuscript transcription. The records were not exhaustively collated manuscript-by-manuscript or translated anew for this edition. Precise folio, stanza and archival page citation is an outstanding requirement only for a critical documentary edition or any future use of verbatim excerpts.

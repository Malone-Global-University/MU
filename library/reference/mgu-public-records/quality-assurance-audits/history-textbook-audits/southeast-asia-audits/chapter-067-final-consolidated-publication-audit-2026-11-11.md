## 3. Historical corrections and chapter-depth improvements

1. **Ayutthaya’s *sakdi na* (67.6):** Replaced inaccurate language suggesting that numeric rank values were literal allocations of labor. Describes rank/status nominally expressed in units of cultivated field area; makes clear these are not automatically actual acreage or numbers of workers and that the fifteenth-century provenance of legal compilations remains debated. Publisher evidence: Chris Baker and Pasuk Phongpaichit, *A History of Ayutthaya* (2017), Cambridge University Press, https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/a-history-of-ayutthaya/52FC0674CC0532A8F1EDB01CC37E703B; comparative discussion of *sakdina* as ranks in land units: https://doi.org/10.1017/trn.2022.7.
2. **Melaka’s political power (67.4, 67.17):** Qualified categorical claims that commercial prominence did not involve conquest or that Melaka had negligible territory. Distinguished mercantile/harbor strength from military expeditions and subordinate claims, including later traditions regarding Pahang; avoided treating later chronicles as independently attested details for every campaign. Supporting framework: Andaya and Andaya, *A History of Early Modern Southeast Asia* (2015); Pires/Cortesão (1944) for early-sixteenth-century port evidence.
3. **Late Majapahit (67.10):** Retained inscriptional evidence dated 1486 while removing the unwarranted assertion that the precise location of Girindrawardhana’s court at Daha is established. Explicitly described disputed chronology, reconstructed courts, and rival claimants. Specialist: J. Noorduyn, “Majapahit in the Fifteenth Century,” *Bijdragen tot de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde* 134, nos. 2–3 (1978): 207–274, https://brill.com/downloadpdf/journals/bki/134/2/article-p207_2.pdf.
4. **Bali (67.10):** Removed a c.1500-specific claim about Balinese exports of enslaved people which lacked sufficiently identified contemporary evidence; preserved Bali’s distinct religious and political trajectory and historical trade connections, with appropriate qualifications.
5. **Ternate’s Islamization (67.11):** Kept traditional royal names but made explicit that conversion dates and a secure succession rely on later chronicles and retrospective early-sixteenth-century reporting. Did not claim complete island-population conversion by 1500. Specialist: Leonard Y. Andaya, *The World of Maluku* (1993), https://search.worldcat.org/title/27431362.
6. **Banten/Girang (67.13):** Distinguished coastal markets connected with the older Banten Girang polity from the later independent Muslim sultanate of Banten. Archaeological evidence of substantial pre-Islamic Banten commercial life: Guillot, Nurhakim and Wibisono, *Banten avant l’Islam* (1994), École française d’Extrême-Orient, https://publications.efeo.fr/en/livres/155_banten-avant-l-islam-etude-archeologique-de-banten-girang-java-indonesie-932-1526.
7. **Regional demography (67.13):** Specified Anthony Reid’s approximately **23 million for c.1600**, explicitly a retrospective regional estimate and **not** a population census or estimate for 1500. Original source: Reid, *The Lands below the Winds* (1988), Table 2, “Population Estimates for Southeast Asia in 1600.”
8. **Analytical depth (67.14, 67.16–67.20):** Added substantive direct paragraphs about port and household bargaining, grain storage and mobility, rank/claim versus effective authority, testing Reid/Andaya/Lieberman periodizations against different regions, Asian rulers’ pre-Portuguese strategic choices, and a comparative typology of political capacity. Kept detailed Portuguese expansion and the Melaka conquest in Chapters 68–69.
9. **Kalyani documentary chronology:** Preserved the c.1476–1479 reform/inscriptional-program framing and clarified source status in the bibliography. Taw Sein Ko’s 1892 edition title contains “1476 A.D.”; descriptions of the surviving stone program sometimes give 1478. See https://www.burmalibrary.org/en/the-kalyani-inscriptions-erected-by-king-dhammaceti-at-pegu-in-1476-a-d-text-and-translation.

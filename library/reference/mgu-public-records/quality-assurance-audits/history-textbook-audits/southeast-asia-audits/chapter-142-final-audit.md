The authoritative outline assigns the chapter the comparative examination of choices available to Southeast Asian political leaders, soldiers, civilians, minorities, and communities under occupation without reducing behavior to a collaboration–resistance binary. The revision preserves that purpose, all twenty numbered sections, and the five original divisions. It preserves the distinct responsibilities of Chapters 140 (Thailand), 141 (forced labor), 143 (atrocities and civilian society), 144 (Allied return), and 145 (regional consequences). The reference to the chapter immediately following this one as the Allied-return chapter was corrected to Chapter 144. No main section was added or removed.

## 4. Primary-source instruction

The three existing source windows retain their original locations and anchors. Each includes identifiable authorship or witness, date, source type, context, attributed paraphrase, analysis, limitations, perspective, source links, and five questions. No unverified verbatim excerpt was introduced.

### 4.1 Joint Declaration — §142.3

The declaration of 6 November 1943 was identified in JACAR, accession C14020058100. Its contemporary English-language version in *Contemporary Japan*, November 1943, pp. 1385–1386, was consulted through a digital transcription. The paraphrase now includes the preamble's explicit commitment to successful prosecution of the war against British-American domination, in addition to the five principles. The transcription is unproofread; the feature explicitly states this limitation. The paraphrase does not purport to reproduce exact wording.

### 4.2 Laurel — §142.7

The 5 November 1943 speech was consulted through the contemporary periodical's digital transcription and the catalogue for Tokyo trial exhibit 2352. The new paraphrase accurately conveys Laurel's publicly expressed praise of Japan, dependence of Asian freedom on Japanese victory, gratitude for sponsored independence, pledge of cooperation, and cultural and educational rhetoric. The analysis explicitly separates public conduct from unproven private convictions. The original periodical scans were not compared line by line; no direct quotation is claimed.

### 4.3 Chu Shuen Choo — §142.16

The generalized Lee Kuan Yew memoir summary was replaced by traceable oral history of civilian household provisioning and resettlement. The National Archives of Singapore identifies Chu Shuen Choo, accession 000462, a twelve-reel interview collection. Lee Geok Boi's BiblioAsia article (2019), notes 15 and 20, identifies interviews of 22 August 1984 and 25 March 1985, reel 5 p. 69 and reel 9 pp. 120–121. Variations in names between the article's notes and the catalogue are identified. The paraphrase uses only the published excerpts concerning informal purchases, food production and family provisioning; the full recording was not inspected. It is not an anonymous composite. Review questions were revised to match oral testimony rather than memoir.

## 5. Substantive development and interpretive quality

- Expanded comparison of education and propaganda, and institutional mechanisms of local administration.
- Developed the difference between Indonesian political platforms and military organizations; explained institutional experience without treating later nationalist outcomes as predetermined.
- Explained coalition composition in Burma and the different social bases and political programs of the MPAJA, Hukbalahap, Viet Minh, and Burmese resistance.
- Added meaningful treatment of Brunei, regional distinctions within Borneo, and Timorese constraints.
- Expanded Allied institutional comparison and distinguished military cooperation from diplomatic recognition and agreement on postwar sovereignty.
- Developed occupation financing and the separation of regional food production from household access to food; strengthened the comparisons of Singapore, Java, Burma and Thailand.
- Added Sybil Kathigasu and Elizabeth Choy alongside ordinary household work, and discussed differences among women and families in access to resources.
- Added religious/community and class distinctions in wartime mobilization and resettlement; avoided treating identity as predetermined political allegiance.
- Replaced generalized civilian examples with documented household testimony and made the distinction between survival and deliberate opposition explicit.
- Strengthened comparative legitimacy and legal synthesis. The historical debate identifies analytical positions as such rather than asserting without evidence that named historians endorse them; linked scholarship provides a basis for testing the positions.

## 2. Historical corrections made

1. **British conquest, August–September 1811 (§100.9):** British landings on 4 August and capture of Batavia by 8 August preceded the 26 August assault on Meester Cornelis; the capitulation of Janssens at Tuntang followed on 18 September. The incorrect sequence asserting that Batavia fell after Meester Cornelis was corrected.
2. **British versus Dutch East India Company (§100.9):** removed the misleading statement that Java was ruled by an East India Company for the first time in two centuries; distinguished the British EIC from the dissolved Dutch VOC.
3. **Daendels and Banten (§100.8):** distinguished the 1808 annexation/restrictions from the final abolition of the remaining Banten sultanate in the British period in 1813. Institutional analysis now includes the nine-prefecture administration and the limits of its authority.
4. **1804 charter (§100.4):** clarified its preparation following a report in 1803, its state-sovereignty logic, and the distinction between metropolitan design and implementation.
5. **Raffles and Yogyakarta (§§100.10–100.12):** expanded revenue assessment and land tenure; explained the 1811 treaties and contested claims of sovereignty, 19–20 June 1812 assault, Kedu and other territorial cessions, loss of toll and market revenues, the Pakualaman settlement, and the importance of Javanese-authored evidence.
6. **Dutch restoration (§§100.13–100.15):** distinguished the joint Commissioners-General administration from the regular governor-general's authority beginning January 1819; specified the 22 December 1818 government regulation and 1819 judicial rules.
7. **1823 land-leasing (§100.19):** specified the 6 May decision, later May publication, targeted leases in the Javanese principalities, January 1824 termination provisions, financial advances and compensation issues. Avoided implying abolition of all landholding.
8. **1824 treaty (§100.17):** separated British transfer of its actual Sumatran establishments, including Fort Marlborough, from a fictional cession of sovereignty over the entire island. Distinguished articles IX, X and XII, and noted that indigenous rulers were not parties.
9. **Pattimura (§100.16):** expanded the 1817 revolt with historical context, labor and monopoly concerns, Thomas Matulessy's military experience, the petition of 29 May, educational and religious grievances, and the limitations of later nationalist retellings.
10. **Everyday life (§100.18):** reconstructed different kinds of rural obligation, urban labor, trade, village intermediaries, women's work, health, environmental shocks and limits of elite documentation.

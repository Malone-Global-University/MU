## 3. Source-level correction record

1. **1039 alleged Champa inscription — corrected across opening, chronology, archaeology box, and timeline:** the purported Champa Arabic inscriptions were reassessed as originating from Tunisia, and therefore cannot be treated as securely provenanced eleventh-century Southeast Asian Muslim burial evidence. The treatment distinguishes the fall of this piece of evidence from a claim that all maritime contact with Champa was impossible. Verification: Haw (2017 online/2018 print), *Journal of the Royal Asiatic Society*, <https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-royal-asiatic-society/article/abs/islam-in-champa-and-the-making-of-factitious-history/F3189E4E37FCDF4EE59A40353E133D19>; Kalus (2003), *Archipel* 66, <https://doi.org/10.3406/arch.2003.3785>.
2. **Trowulan versus Troloyo — corrected in chronology, evidence table, and research narrative:** the dating and Islamic interpretation of earlier inscriptions is explicitly qualified; the credible Zain ud Din epitaph at Troloyo is dated 874 AH (1469–70 CE). Hadi Sidomulyo (2012–13), <https://www.persee.fr/doc/befeo_0336-1519_2012_num_99_1_6153>.
3. **Terengganu inscription — corrected in chronology and source window:** discovery commonly reported in 1887, not unqualified 1899; prominent date 702 AH/1303 CE and Ahmat Adam's alternative 708 AH/1308 CE are presented as competing readings. The window makes the editorial summary and disputed commissioning/date explicit. Ahmat Adam (2017), library catalogue <https://www.parlimen.gov.my/terengganu_inscription.html?_print=true>.
4. **Leran stone — qualified:** 475 AH/1082 CE is marked conventional, with date/provenance uncertainty; it is not used alone as evidence of a general Javanese conversion.
5. **Arab/Persian commercial preponderance — qualified:** surviving geographic references do not establish numerical domination of total regional maritime trade.
6. **Barus Tamil inscription — qualified:** a guild's record documents organized commerce, not every individual member's religious identification.
7. **Legal geography — qualified:** avoids describing the Hanafi legal school as uniform throughout medieval Persia, where legal schools varied by location.
8. **Belitung wreck — sustained:** ninth-century sewn-plank construction and Chinese cargo support a western Indian Ocean construction tradition and trade links, but do not establish the crew's religion. Flecker (2001), <https://doi.org/10.1080/00438240120048662>.
9. **Ma Huan window — completed:** edition identified as J. V. G. Mills's 1970 Hakluyt Society translation from Feng Chengjun's edited text, <https://iucat.iu.edu/iub/6311122>. The summary is labeled editorial paraphrase, not an independently quoted extract.
10. **Synthesis and boundaries — maintained:** consultation of Geoff Wade (2010), <https://doi.org/10.1017/CHOL9780521850315.012>, and Michael Laffan (2011), <https://history.princeton.edu/about/publications/makings-indonesian-islam-orientalism-and-narration-sufi-past>, supports methodologically cautious, multi-causal interpretation rather than a single origin or instantaneous mass conversion.

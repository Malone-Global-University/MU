## 3. Consolidated historical corrections

### H1. 1613 Batu Sawar — attribution corrected

**Before:** The chronology and §70.17 wrongly assigned the destruction of the Johor court at Batu Sawar in 1613 to a Portuguese expedition, treating an Acehnese intervention as a separate concurrent event.

**After:** Both locations state that **Acehnese forces invaded Johor in 1613 and burned Batu Sawar**, and they distinguish this event from the **October 1610 peace between Johor and Portuguese Melaka**. The correction avoids conflating different campaigns or misrepresenting early-seventeenth-century diplomatic alignments.

**Source:** Peter Borschberg, *The Johor–VOC Alliance and the Twelve Years’ Truce: Factionalism, Intrigue and International Diplomacy, 1606–13*, IILJ Working Paper 2009/8, section 9 (Acehnese intervention of 1613), https://www.iilj.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Borschberg-The-Johor-VOC-Alliance-and-the-Twelve-Years%E2%80%99-Truce-IILJ-WP-2009_8-HTIL.pdf.

### H2. 1568 siege duration — chronology corrected

**Before:** The opening and §70.11 claimed siege fighting lasted for several months.

**After:** The opening identifies an offensive over **several weeks**; §70.11 makes the source-qualified reconstruction **approximately 20 January–25 February 1568**, distinguishing the main fighting from preceding preparations and retrospective narratives. Attacker strength and casualty numbers remain attributed *claims*, not independently determined totals.

**Sources:** Pierre-Yves Manguin, “Of Fortresses and Galleys: The 1568 Acehnese Siege of Melaka, after a Contemporary Bird's-Eye View,” *Modern Asian Studies* 22, no. 3 (1988): 607–628, https://doi.org/10.1017/S0026749X00009719; scholarly reconstruction referenced in the prior independent audit using University of Edinburgh thesis excerpt. The precise five-week chronology was **not independently facsimile-collated across every published narrative**; final HTML labels it a reconstruction rather than indisputable documentary fact.

### H3. Goa and Cochin headquarters — periodization corrected

**Before:** Historical context juxtaposed Goa's seizure in 1510 with its status as administrative capital, implying the transition was immediate.

**After:** Distinguishes **Goa's capture in 1510** from its emergence as the Estado da Índia's principal administrative seat **around 1530**, after the earlier Portuguese headquarters at Cochin. This does not alter the longstanding relationship of Melaka with the Estado in subsequent decades.

### H4. Dutch command and Johor's role in 1640–1641 — nuance added

**Before:** §70.19 assigned siege command throughout to **Minne Willemsz Caertekoe**, obscuring changes after the deaths of earlier Dutch commanders, and discussed allied action without distinguishing the final assault.

**After:** States that **Caertekoe assumed final command in January 1641**, and that **Sergeant Major Johannes Lamotius** directed the decisive storming while he was ill. Credits Johor's political, logistical, and regional contributions to the prolonged siege, but **does not inaccurately describe the 14 January storming as an evenly joint combat assault**. Source reports distinguish Dutch participation in the breach from the wider Dutch–Johor partnership.

**Sources:** P. A. Leupe, ed., published VOC documents, https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_ber002185901_01/_ber002185901_01_0004.php (notably council decision of 5 January 1641 and reports of 14 January); Dutch historical account of commander Caertekoe, https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/aa__001biog04_01/aa__001biog04_01_0004.php; André A. M. Murteira, “Two Sieges of Portuguese Melaka: 1629 and 1640–1641” (2026).

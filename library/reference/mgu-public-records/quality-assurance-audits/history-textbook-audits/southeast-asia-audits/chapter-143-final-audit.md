## 5. Primary source verification

### 143.5 — Chua Choon Guan, Nishimura proceedings (1947)

The anonymous synthesis was replaced with an identifiable witness, Prosecution Witness 20. W. L. Cheah’s article, *Reconsidering 'Sook Ching' victimhood: A microhistory of Singapore's Nishimura trial*, section 5.1 and notes 101–116, identifies relevant transcript material at SP 120–122. The published article was inspected; its discussion establishes Chua’s prewar occupation, civilian experience, testimony and limits of knowledge. The witness profile is a useful locator, but direct retrieval returned an access error; its indexed description and Cheah’s published discussion were available. The chapter accurately labels its source exercise as an attributed paraphrase mediated by published scholarship, not a transcription of a complete archival record. No invented quotation or precise casualty count is supplied. Questions address direct observation, corroboration, evidence limits, and survivor agency.

### 143.12 — Maria Rosa Henson, Inter Press Service interview, 13 April 1996

The memoir was not available for page-by-page inspection. Rather than pretend to verify a passage, the source window now uses Henson’s identifiable published interview, consulted in its accessible text. It treats her account of disclosure, family support, writing and public recognition, distinguishing her testimony from the reporter's framing. The 1996 memoir remains in further reading, with the Filipinas Heritage Library catalogue locator; the later 1999 edition is not presented as the source of the interview paraphrase. Both source windows contain creator, date, type, context, attributed paraphrase, analysis, limits, perspective and five questions. The closing source questions were aligned with the revised source choices. This replacement is a deliberate editorial decision made to satisfy the source-integrity requirement without unsupported quotations.

## 7. Statistical and interpretive safeguards

The revision does not attempt to manufacture a reconciled count from conflicting Mandor, Petagas, Singapore or Manila figures. Commemorative numbers, specific criminal proceedings, broad war mortality, and famine estimates measure different populations. The source-specific Timor and famine estimates remain explicitly qualified. Population change is separated from deaths, and the chapter cautions against adding overlapping categories. The broad regional mortality figures and other ranges retained from the draft remain historical estimates, not claims of complete enumerations. Historical and legal responsibility are distinguished; civilian harm is neither minimized nor attributed indiscriminately to an ethnic or national population. Descriptions focus on institutions, consequences, survivor experiences and the evidentiary record rather than reconstructing operational sequences of atrocities.

## 9. Remaining limitations and disposition

The manuscript is a completed editorial revision, not an exhaustive archival monograph. The complete original trial transcript, the entire Henson memoir, all underlying demographic studies and every cited book were not independently inspected. The source windows explicitly identify the available documentary intermediaries. Specific uncertain claims were narrowed or removed; original regional material is retained where not contradicted by the available evidence. Regional coverage of Cambodia, Laos, Sarawak and Brunei is stronger but still less detailed than that of Singapore and Manila, reflecting both the comparative chapter remit and the limits of accessible sources. Maps remain for the user's illustration workflow. The overall manuscript exceeds the recommended total-word ceiling, but the direct narrative is comfortably within the intended depth; no routine expansion is recommended.

## 3. Specific historical and documentary corrections

1. **Canggu Ferry Charter (1358):** §51.9 and its source window now identify approximately 79 ferry-crossing *districts*, not a count of boats; specify the change from older *dĕrwaya haji* collections mediated by landed elites to *pamuja* obligations linked to the crown and ceremonial contributions. Contributions in cash, flowers, and textiles are distinguished from an ordinary passenger fare. These institutional implications are attributed to Kenneth R. Hall’s interpretation of the charter.
2. **Biluluk/Tanggulunan:** §51.7, its primary-source window, and the chronology now distinguish Hall’s letters dated **1366, 1393, and 1395**. The 1366 letter concerns saline-spring access, festival rights, *pamuja* and the separate *pagagarem* levy; subsequent artisan-directed letters preserve claims of local chiefs of trade. Occupations and financial rules are not falsely assigned to every letter indiscriminately.
3. **Walandit:** §51.16, the chronology preview and the people/places review now state that a grant attributed to **1381** survives as a **1405 copy**, with reference to sacred Mount Brahma (Bromo) and exemption from *titileman*. The date of an earlier royal grant is not equated with the date of the surviving document.
4. **Trowulan/Troloyo and Islam:** §51.14 and chronology distinguish the dates and possible Muslim associations of inscribed stones from proven original funerary use. Hadi Sidomulyo’s reinterpretation is acknowledged without denying likely Muslim presence in the wider Majapahit period.
5. **Archaeological inference:** §51.12 differentiates archaeological deposits, displaced or reused artifacts, stylistic dating and the UNESCO Tentative List nomination. The latter is not treated as confirmation of an archaeologically established complete plan for the city.
6. **Court culture, sacred endowments and cultural transmission:** §§51.13, 51.14, 51.15, 51.19 and 51.20 were substantively expanded with attention to the material costs of ceremony, differentiated religious institutions, Rajapatni’s commemorative sequence, artifact provenance and mixed forms of governance.
7. **Source-register corrections:** Winaya and Munandar (2021) are now fully cited with the SPAFA Journal DOI; UNESCO’s exact official tentative-list title, number and submission date are recorded; Brandes’s 1913 posthumous transcriptions are correctly attributed to editor N. J. Krom. Added Hall (2000), Noorduyn (1968), and Sidomulyo (2012). The register contains **17 entries** and no unfinished citation placeholders.

## 6. Scope and publication caveats

- No comprehensive collation of all surviving original-language manuscript recensions, copper-plate facsimiles or archaeological datasets was undertaken. Three documentary teaching passages remain transparent editorial paraphrases, not purported verbatim archival translations.
- Map and geography studies are text-based exercises requiring an external historical atlas; no embedded map plates are included.
- Inferred daily life and poorly documented terminology are presented cautiously; archaeological remains and iconography are not treated as direct testimony of universal social practice.
- This approval covers scholarly **historical-text and HTML publication**, not exhaustive original-language diplomatic editions or an illustrated atlas edition.

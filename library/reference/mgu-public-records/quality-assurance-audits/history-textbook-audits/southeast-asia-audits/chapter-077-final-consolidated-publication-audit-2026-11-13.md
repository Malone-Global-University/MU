## Historical accuracy and source verification

### Essential corrections completed

1. **1595–1597 first voyage:** Removed the inaccurate assertion that fewer than one-third of the approximately 249 original crew returned; text now reports approximately **89 survivors** while treating precise accounting cautiously. Also removed unsupported certainty that the expedition precisely broke even. A contemporary-voyage-derived historical reference is cited in the earlier draft audit, and the revised primary-source window identifies an extant 1598 Lodewijcksz edition.
2. **Heeren XVII's seventeenth seat:** Corrected the rotating seat to the **five non-Amsterdam chambers, including Zeeland**. The agreement gave Amsterdam eight regular seats, Zeeland four, the other four chambers one each, and the final seat by rotation. The Dutch National Archives' institutional history is explicitly linked in the chapter.
3. **Amsterdam chamber capital vs operational shares:** Distinguished Amsterdam's more-than-half share of subscribed capital from the charter's allocation of half of common operations to that chamber. These are not the same measurement.
4. **Banda 1609 commander:** Corrected the name to **Pieter Willemszoon Verhoeff** and placed his death amid opposition to fort-building and imposed commercial exclusivity; the historical account avoids depicting it as unprovoked isolated violence.
5. **Pieter Both:** Distinguished the governor-generalship's establishment in **1609** from the specifically catalogued **30 August 1610** instruction, Dutch National Archives VOC 1.04.02, **inventory 567**. The source window reports archival metadata honestly and makes no unverified clause-level claims.
6. **Chapter cross-references:** Corrected the relationship to later Chapter 92 and maintained clear boundaries with Chapters 78–79.

### Additional verified institutional particulars

- The Dutch National Archives catalogues the founding charter as VOC archive 1.04.02, inventory no. 1, a privilege valid for 21 years.
- The company's chambers were Amsterdam, Zeeland/Middelburg, Delft, Rotterdam, Hoorn, and Enkhuizen.
- Initial subscription capital was approximately 6.4 million guilders, and the original charter's ten-year capital-repayment provisions were not carried out exactly as projected.
- Early monopolistic efforts were geographically and politically uneven; spice-producing communities and Asian merchants retained independent interests and means of resistance.

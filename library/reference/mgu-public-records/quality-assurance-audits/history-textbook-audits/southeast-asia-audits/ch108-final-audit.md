## 4. Historical and source corrections completed

1. **Pangkor Engagement:** the Resident’s binding advice is **Article VI** and fiscal / administrative oversight is **Article VII**. The contemporary House of Commons debate of 26 May 1876, volume 229, columns 1290–1309, discusses both. The source window now gives explicit treaty article numbers and an accessible source; paraphrases remain labeled.
2. **Birch crisis:** the generic promise to find parliamentary dispatches was replaced with a genuinely identified source, the 26 May 1876 House of Commons debate, including Ernest Noel’s remarks. The window distinguishes the speech’s political argument from judicial fact and acknowledges the absence of many Malay perspectives.
3. **Federated Malay States:** the agreement of 1895 is separated from the federation’s July 1896 commencement. The non-elected and executive-dominated design of the 1909 Federal Council, and the 1911 Chief Secretary designation, are explained.
4. **Tin dredging:** the narrative distinguishes the commercially operating 1913 Kinta Valley dredge from earlier proposals or machinery trials and from later mass adoption. No unsourced precise pre-1914 world tin-production share is asserted.
5. **Rubber acreage:** a dated, sourced, explicitly *British Malaya* (not FMS-only) reconstructed series reports approximately 46,000 acres (1905), 541,000 (1910), 1,074,000 (1913) and 1,168,000 (1914), with separate 1913 European/Asian category figures. The chapter identifies the later compilation and states that planted acres do not equal mature-producing acres or export volume.
6. **State-level agricultural evidence:** Negeri Sembilan rubber acreage from the *Manual of Statistics, Federated Malay States*, as reproduced and qualified by Michael G. Peletz, is used to demonstrate local variation and changes in reporting scope.
7. **FMS demographic comparison:** 1901 total **678,595** (Chinese 299,739; Malay 312,486; Indian 58,211) versus 1911 total **1,036,999** (Chinese 433,244; Malay 420,840; Indian 172,465); this is explicitly the four Federated Malay States, not all British Malaya. The 1901 published summary and 1913 contemporary handbook are individually identified. Historical administrative categories are not treated as neutral contemporary ethnic classifications.
8. **Tamil labor:** distinguished 1907 legislation from reported 1908 operation of the immigration fund and the 1910 phase-out of *new* indentured recruitment from surviving contracts continuing to 1913.
9. **Malay Reservations Enactment:** date of enactment **1913**, operative from **1 January 1914**, with careful treatment of potential protective and restrictive consequences; the chapter avoids assuming that Malay villagers rejected commercial rubber production.
10. **Anglo-Siamese Treaty, 10 March 1909:** uses the accessible U.S. Office of the Historian diplomatic reprint. **Article I** transfers Siamese rights, **Article II** provides transfer within 30 days of ratification, **Article III** establishes a mixed delimitation commission, and the **Boundary Protocol** supplies geographic description. Terengganu’s full 1919 Adviser remains clearly distinguished from an earlier Agent and lies outside the main historical cutoff.
11. **Johor / differentiated states:** expanded the 1885 treaty, 1895 constitution, kangchu production, 1914 General Adviser, and differences among federated, unfederated, and Crown-colony jurisdictions without treating an advisory title as uniform power.
12. **Selected bibliography:** verified the cited edition of Andaya and Andaya (2001), Colin Barlow’s full 1978 book title, and Wong Lin Ken’s 1965 study; removed the open-ended instruction to verify bibliographic entries before publication.

**Boundaries of verification:** Primary-source windows use **explicitly labeled editorial paraphrases**, not unverified verbatim quotation. The precise Malay-language witnesses to the 1874 agreement, all original FMS annual return volumes, and the original 1901 census schedules were not independently collated in full. The 1901 figures are reported through a contemporary published summary; the rubber acreage series is later secondary reconstruction and is not an independently audited contemporary estate census. British parliamentary testimony is evidence of political positions, not a transparent record of Malay intentions or a substitute for trial records. Not every publisher detail of the retained long-form secondary bibliography received a fresh catalogue-by-catalogue check. These limits are disclosed in the chapter’s source register and do not prevent first-edition **source-qualified textual** acceptance.

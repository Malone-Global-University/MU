### Targeted verification of remembered claims

1. **Kuy / Kuay ironworking:** Archaeometallurgical research confirms important iron production in northern Cambodia. A 2014 study specifically tested a proposed connection with Kuay ironworkers and concluded that this connection **could neither be sustained nor rejected**. The chapter expressly labels its link to later communities as hypothetical, which is appropriate. Research: Pryce et al., *Journal of Archaeological Science* (2014), https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0305440314001447 ; *Scientific Reports* (2025), https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-27581-x .
2. **Ceramic chronology and kiln geography:** A revised radiocarbon chronology establishes important production from the late ninth through twelfth centuries and later thirteenth–fourteenth-century provincial phases, subject to site and sampling variation. The chapter's cautious description is supportable. Marriner et al. (2018), https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/abs/new-dates-for-old-kilns-a-revised-radiocarbon-chronology-of-stoneware-production-for-angkorian-cambodia/871EA0C5EA9CBCE66B5BDF8BF18A0754 ; see also https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352409X21004430 .
3. **Land payments in commodities and metals:** Old Khmer inscriptions and scholarship document land transactions paid in silver, cloth, rice, animals, salt and other commodities. The chapter correctly avoids asserting an entirely monetized economy. https://angkordatabase.asia/publications/angkor-society-and-state and https://sealang.net/sala/archives/pdf8/jacob1993pre-angkor.pdf .
4. **Zhou Daguan and social rank:** His account describes distinctive house sizes/materials by rank, official insignia, parasols/palanquins, and clothing. The chapter's general statements are supported, but an unsupported inference about elite houses standing closer to the royal center was removed. National Library of Australia, https://www.library.gov.au/learn/digital-classroom/angkorkhmer-empire-802-1431/way-life-khmer-empire ; Smithsonian, https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/passing-notes-157400693/ .
5. **Primary-source windows:** Ta Prohm K.273 is a Sanskrit-verse inscription (not a bilingual Sanskrit/Old Khmer foundation text). Sdok Kak Thom is bilingual and the interpretation of the *devarāja* rites remains contested. The windows identify paraphrase as such and distinguish documentary claims from independent enumeration. https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tak/23/1/23_KJ00000131203/_article/-char/en ; https://www.hawaii.edu/khmer/KhmerMaterials/SdokKakThom.html .

## 3. Historical correction and expansion register

**3.1 Departure chronology corrected.** The original chronology and §69.14 placed Albuquerque's departure in *late 1512*. The revised edition places his departure **around late 1511**, while acknowledging that Portuguese accounts differ on exact departure and return dates. It notes the wreck of *Flor de la Mar* on the return voyage and arrival in India in early 1512. This corrects a concrete historical error without inventing a certain day. **Grounding:** Armando Cortesão's introduction to the *Suma Oriental* and its notes on conflicting chronicle dates; contemporary and retrospective Portuguese histories.

**3.2 First and second assaults distinguished.** The revised sections make a firm distinction between the first assault traditionally dated **25 July 1511**, a tactical penetration and withdrawal, and the renewed effort in **August**, which secured sustained occupation. Exact dates of each movement and the final seizure remain attributed to narrative tradition where the surviving record differs. The chapter avoids manufacturing a universal casualty count or uncritical contemporary-equivalent eyewitness narrative.

**3.3 Tactical reconstruction expanded.** §§69.9–69.12 now explain the bridge as a strategic crossing, the need to hold a defensible position after landing, and the use of a **large captured Asian junk** as an elevated firing platform. The discussion covers grounding near a sandbank, reported attempts to burn the vessel, the changing tide, preparation of protective materials, and adaptation before the renewed assault. Details that depend on retrospective Portuguese accounts are identified as such. The revised interpretation does **not** equate European artillery with effortless military superiority. **Grounding:** Michael W. Charney's specialized comparison of the 1511 campaign and Portuguese narrative evidence.

**3.4 Asian participation and leadership contextualized.** The revised text emphasizes the agency of Asian merchants, boat owners, interpreters, pilots, specialists, and contingents. It does not assign uniform motives to Gujarati, Tamil, Javanese, Chinese, Malay, or other resident communities. The role of Prince Ahmad and the court crisis around Bendahara Tun Mutahir remain expressly qualified because evidence is uneven and later traditions carry distinct political purposes.

**3.5 Conquest's social experience enlarged.** §§69.13–69.15 examine the loss of household resources, destruction around warehouses and streets, uncertainty about the distribution of violence, selective protection and confiscation, labor and rebuilding, and the distinct purposes of Portuguese victory reports versus later Malay dynastic memory. This discussion avoids attributing invented testimony to ordinary residents.

**3.6 Portuguese Melaka's early institutions expanded.** §§69.14–69.16 distinguish the original fortress-building effort from later architectural modifications and the modern surviving gateway, and explain the dependence of commercial administration on existing merchant networks. The early coinage is no longer presented as mere legend: Albuquerque's **letter of 1 April 1512** describes samples of gold, silver, and copper coins struck in King Manuel's name. Precise denominations and modern specimen attribution remain subjects for separate numismatic examination.

**3.7 Political continuity preserved.** §§69.17–69.20 distinguish the **fall of the port city in 1511** from the survival of Sultan Mahmud Shah's dynastic authority and followers until his death in **1528**. The conclusion recognizes genuine Portuguese military and institutional victory while rejecting claims that the conquest delivered the whole peninsula, the Strait's trade, or all communities formerly associated with Melaka. Detailed later Johor/Aceh/Straits conflict remains reserved for the subsequent chapters.

**3.8 Original interpretation conserved.** The chapter retains its balanced treatment of the 1509 diplomatic breakdown, the disputed conspiracy narrative in Portuguese accounts, Muslim and non-Muslim commercial interests, sultanate military capacity, the interplay of religion and politics, and the retrospective character of the *Sejarah Melayu*.

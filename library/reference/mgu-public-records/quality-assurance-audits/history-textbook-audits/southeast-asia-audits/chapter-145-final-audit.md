## 4. Historical corrections and verification register

| Subject | Correction and evidence basis |
|---|---|
| Dutch colonial history | Replaced the assertion that capitulation ended three centuries of uniform Dutch presence. Distinguished commercial presence, uneven territorial consolidation, interrupted administration, and continuing Dutch sovereignty claims. Consistent with the outline's Dutch expansion chapter. |
| Philippine constitutional order | Qualified the generalization about prewar political exclusion; Commonwealth electoral and administrative institutions preceded Japanese conquest. |
| Putera / Jawa Hokokai | Corrected January 1944 replacement to February 29 dissolution and March 1, 1944 inauguration, using the Frederick collection's searchable text. Putera's March 1943 establishment retained. |
| PETA and Giyūgun | Retained PETA's October 1943 origin and differentiated Sumatran organization in late 1943 instead of implying one founding day. Distinguished auxiliaries, volunteer formations, and the later republican military. The specific PETA ordinance date was established in the preceding audit; an original ordinance facsimile was not re-inspected here. |
| PETA dissolution | Located the dissolution after the August 15 announcement but before the September 2 formal surrender. Distinguished dissolved organizations from personnel in later formations. |
| Anti-Fascist Organisation / AFPFL | Corrected the inaccurate assertion that AFPFL began as a collaborator; differentiated individual leaders' earlier cooperation from the coalition's anti-fascist institutional origins. |
| Force 136 and MPAJA | Distinguished early contact from the more extensive liaison in 1945. December 1945 formal demobilization is retained; the dissolution of a military formation is distinguished from the continued political existence of the Malayan Communist Party. |
| Philippine recognition | Executive Order No. 68 establishes demobilization without prejudicing later recognition on appropriate evidence. Executive Order No. 69 establishes commemorative recognition. U.S. archival records and the White House retrospective identify the recognition program extending to June 1948. Avoided equating wartime participation, administrative recognition, and veterans' benefit eligibility. |
| Burma | Kandy agreed conclusions of September 7, 1945 identified via the published Aung San collection. Added the distinction between partial integration, veterans' status, and political representation. Avoided implying wholesale incorporation. |
| Thailand | Corrected the claim Thailand avoided enemy status altogether: the United States did not accept its declaration of war against it, whereas Britain negotiated a formal termination. January 1, 1946 Anglo-Siamese agreement distinguished from later French territorial settlement. |
| Borneo | Corrected blanket attribution to British Military Administration; identified Australian Army's British Borneo Civil Affairs Unit and subsequent transfer to British authority. Distinguished North Borneo, Sarawak, Brunei, and Dutch Borneo. |
| Indochina | Differentiated Vietnamese, Cambodian, and Lao institutions; added October 1945 Lao Issara provisional government and contested French restoration into 1946. Avoided treating French authority as uniformly restored at the end of 1945. |
| Portuguese Timor | Replaced ambiguous statement that independence came in the 1970s with the 1975 proclamation, subsequent invasion, and 2002 restoration of sovereignty. Distinguished wartime fighting, famine, and disease. |
| Dutch reform proposal | Identified Queen Wilhelmina's December 6, 1942 address and the proposed Commonwealth framework, not a promise of immediate independent Indonesian sovereignty. |
| French reform proposal | Identified declaration of March 24, 1945, five-part Indochinese federation within French Union. Distinguished reform proposals from acceptance of independence. |
| Colonial legitimacy and ethnicity | Qualified categorical statements about political loyalty and examined the intersection of class, community, and territory. Avoided attributing a uniform loyalty to Karen, Chinese, Malay, Indian, Eurasian, or other populations. |

## 5. Primary Source Windows

Both primary-source windows retain their original location in §145.18, feature classes, metadata, analytical context, limitations, perspective, and five student questions. The source texts are now identifiable and short exact excerpts replace the secondhand paraphrases. The Atlantic Charter text was checked through the NATO official-text reproduction, with FDR Library context. Churchill's Hansard statement is identified as a separate contemporary interpretation. The UN excerpts were checked against the official UN Chapter I and XI text and search-accessible full-text records; direct access to the full-text URL returned HTTP 403, so the chapter-specific official reproductions were used instead. No claim of accessing a facsimile or the complete UN treaty via the blocked endpoint is made.

- Atlantic Charter, third principle: a brief excerpt concerning the choice of government. Its date, joint-declaration status, and lack of an enforceable timetable are explained. Churchill's September 9, 1941 statement is not represented as the sole possible interpretation of the Charter.
- United Nations Charter, Article 1(2) and Article 73(b): two brief excerpts distinguish the principle of equal rights and self-determination from the obligation to develop self-government and respect political aspirations. The exercise identifies the scope and limitations of the provisions, including the separate trusteeship system.

## 9. Residual limitations and disposition

The audit is a targeted consolidated scholarly and structural review, not an assertion that every claim in a 25,000-word manuscript was checked against original archival documents. Selected primary documents, searchable reproductions, institutional records, and accessible abstracts were used. The complete listed scholarly books, complete Kandy text, original PETA ordinance, and all regional archival sources were not re-read in full. Bibliographic metadata for several further-reading titles was retained or normalized rather than independently verified against every edition. Some claims rely on the preceding chapter's established findings and the governing outline. The draft's inherited CSS and script were preserved, but the original Chapter 1 model was not separately compared. No visual browser rendering, live site deployment, formal HTML validator run, or comprehensive external-link testing was performed. The UN full-text endpoint returned a direct-access error; accessible official article records supported the excerpts. Map images remain a separate publication task, not a blocker.

Within these explicit limitations, the major deficiencies identified in the preliminary audit have been addressed: the narrative shortfall is resolved across all five divisions, documentary exercises are grounded in identifiable text, the major chronological and institutional errors are corrected, the bibliography and historiographical treatment are improved, and the Volume VII synthesis is complete. Recommend editorial lock without another routine expansion pass. A normal browser smoke test at deployment and spot-check of source links are appropriate production controls, rather than reasons to reopen substantive drafting.

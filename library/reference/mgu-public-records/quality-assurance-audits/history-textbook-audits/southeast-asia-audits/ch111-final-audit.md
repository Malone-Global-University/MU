## 4. Consolidated historical corrections

1. **Franco-Cambodian treaty (August 11, 1863):** corrected the formal French plenipotentiary to Admiral Pierre-Paul de La Grandière, not Doudart de Lagrée. Distinguished de Lagrée’s envoy role and clarified that defined facilities/concessions did not amount to a wholesale transfer of Cambodian territorial sovereignty. Treaty Articles I–II documented. Source: https://mjp.univ-perp.fr/constit/kh1863.htm ; additional edition https://eveilfrancokhmer.fr/histoire-khmere/archive-protectorat-francais-sur-le-royaume-du-cambodge-le-traite-complet/
2. **Franco-Cambodian convention (June 17, 1884):** documented Articles I–IV, VI, VIII (abolition of slavery), IX (property) and the distinction between reform authority in law and actual implementation. Source: https://mjp.univ-perp.fr/constit/kh1863.htm
3. **Cambodian Royal Ordinance No. 54 (July 11, 1897):** identified the five-minister Council and the presidency of the French Resident-Superior, removing a retained editorial verification instruction. Source: https://nagoya.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/22840
4. **Franco-Siamese treaty (October 3, 1893):** corrected the formal French signatory to Charles Marie Le Myre de Vilers (Siam: Prince Devawongse Varoprakar), not Pavie; cited Articles I–IV; distinguished the separate convention on Chanthaburi. Source: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1893/d543
5. **Laos administrative decrees, 1899:** distinguished Governor-General Doumer's February 6 decree joining Upper and Lower Laos from the April 19 presidential decree conferring Resident-Superior status; explained that no treaty with a unitary indigenous Lao state established all of Laos as a treaty protectorate. Source: Kennon Breazeale, *Laos Mapped by Treaty and Decree, 1895–1907*, https://www.luangprabangculture.com/uploads/1/8/3/0/18306261/laos_mapped_by_treaty_and_decree_1895-1907.pdf . These dates were corrected in the main text, chronology and learning objective.
6. **Franco-Siamese convention (February 13, 1904):** clarified Articles I–V (boundaries, Luang Prabang rights, evacuation conditions) and the June 29 protocol, avoiding the assertion that Chanthaburi was vacated unconditionally on signing. Source: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1905/d927
7. **Franco-Siamese treaty (March 23, 1907):** identified signatories Collin de Plancy and Devawongse and Articles I–IV, V–VI (territorial exchange, boundary commission, jurisdiction), distinguishing treaty language from subsequent demarcation. Source: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1907p2/d373
8. **Angkor conservation:** separated the 1898 archaeological mission, the creation of the EFEO in 1900, the 1907 Angkor handover, the creation of a conservator position in March 1908 and Commaille's appointment on July 14, 1908. Source: https://archives.efeo.fr/index.php/conservation-dangkor-1907-1975
9. **Ong Keo and Ong Kommadam:** added the broad sequence of resistance, surrender narratives, Ong Keo’s November 1910 death (circumstances disputed), and Ong Kommadam's continuity beyond the chapter cutoff, without importing later events into the narrative. Sources: https://thesiamsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/1974/03/JSS_062_1e_Murdoch_1901to1902HolyMansRebellion.pdf and scholarship listed in the bibliography.
10. **Chronology and historical synthesis:** §111.19 substantially expanded to compare legal acquisition, bureaucracy, boundary demarcation, and practical enforcement; §§111.16–111.20 strengthened as a coherent interpretation of unequal colonial jurisdictions.

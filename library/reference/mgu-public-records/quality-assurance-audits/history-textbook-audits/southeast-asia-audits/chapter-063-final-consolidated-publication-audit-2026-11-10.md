## 3. Integrated historical corrections

- **Late royal memorials:** Distinguished the death in 1297 recorded on al-Malik al-Salih’s markers from Elizabeth Lambourn’s proposed manufacture **c. 1470s–1520s**. Identified his particular stones as later local batu Aceh works rather than implying they were thirteenth-century imported Gujarat marble. Other Pasai burials (such as Nahrasiyah’s) are treated separately as part of the imported Cambay tradition.
- **Rulers and succession:** Clarified that a 1326 death inscription concerns **Muhammad al-Malik al-Zahir**, and that the recurring al-Malik al-Zahir title cannot establish that this ruler was Ibn Battuta’s host in the 1340s. A retrospective date was not treated as contemporaneous epigraphic proof of a court in 1297.
- **Ming-era political contest:** Supplied the **c. 1414–1415** sequence with Zain al-Abidin, the challenger Sekandar/Su-gan-la, Zheng He’s intervention, flight toward Lamuri, and captivity in China, while retaining the important methodological caution that Ming labels such as “legitimate ruler” and “usurper” express the Ming court’s interests and record.
- **Manuscript witnesses:** Distinguished Raffles Malay 67 from British Library **Or. 14350** (incomplete; associated with a 1797 Semarang manuscript). Added the Hill **1960** and Russell Jones **1987** and **2013** edition/translation history without claiming an original medieval manuscript survives.
- **Economic and social evidence:** Added differentiated discussion of coin nomenclature, probable gold and tin monetary media, undated coins, cautious pepper-growth claims, court offices and their poorly attested titles, and the limited inferences available from Nahrasiyah’s elite monument about women generally.
- **Conceptual integration:** Enlarged Divisions IV and V, particularly interpretation of regional variation, social and political continuity, multiple routes to Islamization, and historical synthesis. Kept the Chapter 62 network perspective, Chapter 64 Melaka history and Chapter 65 broader conversion narrative in their separate divisions of historical labor.

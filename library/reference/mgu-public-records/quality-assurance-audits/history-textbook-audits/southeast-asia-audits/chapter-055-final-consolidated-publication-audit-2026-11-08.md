## 3. Historical corrections directly applied

### Wat Si Chum, early dynastic memory (Section 55.3)

The previously generic 'Buddhist religious figure' is now identified with **Mahāthera Si Sattha (Śrīśraddhā)**, whose religious career and patronage circle are involved in the inscription's self-presentation. The revision **does not assert that the monk personally carved every line or witnessed the founding events**. The source window also records its historical role and the philological significance of Mon affinities. Edition: A. B. Griswold and Prasert na Nagara (1972), Epigraphic and Historical Studies No. 10, *Journal of the Siam Society* 60(1): 21–152; Christian Bauer's specialized 1993 analysis provides additional linguistic context.

### Wat Pa Mamuang and King Lithai (Section 55.14)

The original text conflated or incompletely named the language evidence. The revision accurately distinguishes **Inscription No. 4 (Khmer), No. 5 (Thai), No. 6 (Pali), and No. 7 (Thai)**, associated with the **1361** corpus analyzed by Griswold and Prasert (1973). The episode of palace novice ordination followed by higher ordination at the Mango Grove is attested in these related records. The narrative now distinguishes these ritual acts from permanent abdication, and explains the reconstructed **1347** contested succession with appropriate uncertainty. Source window metadata and paraphrase were corrected; no original-language quotation is claimed.

### Monastic patronage, waterworks, cosmology and regional synthesis

Five sections received the substantive narrative additions needed for full-depth treatment: §55.10 discusses decentralization and changing use of waterworks; §55.13 examines monastic endowments, dependents and donor bias; §55.14 explains documentary languages and the disputed succession; §55.15 addresses pedagogical use and the limits of the surviving *Traiphumikatha* recension; §55.20 compares Sukhothai with contemporary mainland political trajectories and later national memory. Assertions about Ramkhamhaeng's extensive territory, the precise founding year, the authenticity controversy, the dating of later ceramics, and the staged incorporation into Ayutthaya remain appropriately qualified.

## 6. Limitations disclosed, not publication blockers

1. The source windows are editorial paraphrases, not fully collated critical editions or newly verified verbatim translations. Full autoptic checks of inscription stones, rubbings, and manuscript recensions remain outside this edition’s scope.
2. The historical maps are text-led location/route exercises requiring a separate atlas; no historical cartographic plates are embedded.
3. The authenticity of Ramkhamhaeng Inscription No. 1, the founding chronology, *Traiphumikatha* transmission and early political succession remain contested. The chapter presents debate rather than false certainty.
4. The browser review checks page functionality and representative viewport interactions, not an independent automated accessibility-certification process.

### 4. Historical accuracy and documentary corrections

1. **Founding date and evidence (§57.3):** The conventional foundation in **1351** is stated as a retrospective chronology; UNESCO uses **about 1350**. The Luang Prasoet recension was compiled by royal order in **1680**. Its dated founding notice has now been distinguished from later elaborated legends—especially epidemic and relocation motifs preserved in the seventeenth-century traditions associated with Jeremias van Vliet. The founding source window contains **two explicitly separated editorial paraphrases**, *not* one invented combined quotation. An earlier misconception attributing the entire epidemic narrative to the Luang Prasoet document is removed.
2. **Ming diplomatic evidence (§57.8):** The Ming study window now names *Taizu shi-lu* **juan 55.3a (26 August 1370)**, the court's embassy to Siam, and **juan 68.4b (30 October 1371)**, the arrival of Siamese representatives with trained elephants and other offerings and the return presentation of Chinese textiles. Those records are discussed as near-contemporary court compilations, **not** verbatim event-day diaries or proof of Chinese administrative sovereignty. Geoff Wade's translation database and published analysis are individually identified.
3. **Ayutthayan social and legal institutions (§§57.9–57.11, 57.17):** The **1805 Three Seals Code** is recognized as a later compilation preserving materials of different dates. The manuscript does not treat all assigned *sakdina* ranks and administrative categories as securely enacted in the reign of Borommatrailokkanat. Vickery's methodological caution and Baker–Pasuk's research on composite law are included.
4. **Angkor (§57.15):** The traditional **1431–1432** campaign remains qualified as chronicle testimony. The narrative now describes the Ayutthayan-style Buddha images identified by Polkinghorne, Pottier, and Fischer as **potential archaeological evidence of occupation**; it does not claim the campaign instantly depopulated Angkor. The later, gradual ecological, demographic, political and cultural transformation remains a separate question for Chapter 61.
5. **Sukhothai and Lan Na (§§57.16–57.17):** The traditional **1378** and **1438** milestones and the **1470s** arrangements are qualified. Submission, dynastic incorporation, military campaigning, and effective territorial administration are kept analytically separate.
6. **Period boundaries:** Chapter 57 closes at approximately **1500**. It does not import seventeenth-century European-company institutions or a fully modern national-state bureaucracy into the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries.

**No publication-critical historical contradiction was identified in the checked passages after correction.** This is a targeted historical and bibliographical review, not a claim of comprehensive archival verification. The three source windows are explicitly scholarly **editorial paraphrases**, not literal manuscript-collated translations.

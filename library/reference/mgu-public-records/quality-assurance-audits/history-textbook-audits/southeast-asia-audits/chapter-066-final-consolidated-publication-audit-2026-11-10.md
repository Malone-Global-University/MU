## 3. Consolidated historical corrections

### H1. Kumalalang place-name versus ruler-name — corrected across all relevant surfaces

**Prior defect:** §66.14 referred to “where exactly Kumalalang ruled” and §66.20 listed Kumalalang among human figures; the people/reference and review materials made the same mistake.

**Correction:** Kumalalang is explicitly a **Mindanao polity**. Its ruler was **Kanlai Ipentun**, who appeared at the Ming court in **November 1420**, died in **Fujian in May 1421**, and was succeeded by **La-pi**. Kumalalang's association with the Dumanguilas Bay region is attributed to William Henry Scott and treated as a historical identification, not a settled territory map. Updated historical §66.14, §66.20, the chronology preview, introductory people glossary, and major-people review glossary.

**Source:** William Henry Scott, “Filipinos in China before 1500,” *Asian Studies* 21 (1983): 1–19, especially pp. 11–12. https://www.asj.upd.edu.ph/mediabox/archive/ASJ-21-1983/scott.pdf .

### H2. Third Sulu royal participant of 1417 — source-critical account corrected

**Prior defect:** Sulu's third delegate, Paduka Prabhu/Prabu, was consistently described as the “cave” or “interior” king, implying a reliably established inland territory, and the source window repeated this as if uncontroversial.

**Correction:** The revised §66.11 identifies Paduka Batara and Maharaja Kolamating as eastern and western rulers respectively, while presenting **Paduka Prabhu** as a third royal figure with **disputed wording and status in the Chinese textual tradition**. It avoids treating “cave king,” an earlier erroneous translation/identification, as a secure jurisdiction. Chinese categories and diplomatic recognition are not taken as evidence of three neatly demarcated kingdoms. The people glossary, §66.11 analysis, and Primary Source Window II were corrected together.

**Source:** Scott, “Filipinos in China before 1500,” pp. 8–9. Same article URL as H1.

### H3. Santubong's archaeological chronology — updated

**Prior text:** A broad, undifferentiated tenth–thirteenth-century range for finds and ironwork.

**Correction:** §66.4 distinguishes an excavated **mid-eleventh through late-twelfth/thirteenth-century iron-smelting furnace** horizon at Sungai Jaong, Santubong, from the broader late-tenth–thirteenth-century industrial activity. The archaeological material is not converted into evidence of an early Brunei territorial/dynastic state.

**Source:** Daniel Perret, Mohd. Sherman bin Sauffi, Stéphanie Leroy, and Yohan Chabot, “The Ongoing Sungai Jaong Archaeological Project (2018–2024), Sarawak, Malaysia: Preliminary Results,” *Archipel* 107 (2024): 47–73; DOI 10.4000/12fvh, https://doi.org/10.4000/12fvh .

### H4. Existing source precautions deliberately retained

- Distinguish Chinese **Boni** references across centuries from a demonstrably continuous Brunei royal lineage; preserve 1408 Nanjing embassy and burial while withholding definitive identification within Brunei's sultan list (Kurz 2014).
- Treat **Awang Alak Betatar/Muhammad Shah**, the retrospective Johor marriage account, **Karim ul-Makhdum**, **Sharif Abu Bakr**, and **Sharif Kabungsuwan** as traditions with varying chronological reliability (Saunders, Druce/Abu Bakar, Majul, Saleeby).
- Preserve caution that **Master Pu's stone dated 1264** does not independently prove a Muslim occupation of Brunei in that year.
- Keep **Paduka Batara's death and tomb at Dezhou** distinct from the Boni ruler's death and burial at Nanjing.
- Preserve **Pigafetta's 1521 Brunei description** as a post-1500 source; do not retroject sixteenth-century Brunei territory or developed Maguindanao/Buayan sultanates into 1400.
- Treat Islamization, local autonomy, and datu/royal authority as variable; avoid applying later colonial ethnic/religious terms uncritically.

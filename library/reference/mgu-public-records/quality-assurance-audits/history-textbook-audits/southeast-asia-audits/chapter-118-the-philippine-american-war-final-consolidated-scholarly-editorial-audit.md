### 4. Principal historical corrections

1. **The Philippine sovereignty dispute** is presented as competing American treaty-based and Filipino republican claims. Neither legal position is asserted as an uncontested historical fact.
2. **February 4–5, 1899:** the draft now identifies William W. Grayson as the American sentry whose shots are documented, while distinguishing the first shots from the disputed responsibility for escalation. NHI Board Resolution 07 (2003) concerning Santa Mesa and the older San Juan Bridge marker is named and linked.
3. **Aguinaldo:** capture on March 23, 1901; oath on **April 1, 1901**; public proclamation on **April 19, 1901**, correctly treated as separate episodes.
4. **Balangiga:** identifies a reported range of **44–48 killed** depending on treatment of dead and missing. General Jacob H. Smith's documented *howling wilderness* phrasing is attributed to 1902 military testimony, not reproduced as a fabricated speech or used to infer the exact conduct of every detachment.
5. **Malvar:** surrender commonly dated **April 16, 1902**; an NHCP marker records **April 18**. The difference is disclosed and should be understood in relation to surrender arrangements, formalities and commemoration rather than erased.
6. **Laws of war:** contrasts Lieber Code Article 16's restrictions with provisions governing irregular combatants and with specific later field instructions, including Bell's December 8, 1901 circular.
7. **Casualties and disease:** distinguishes the widely cited estimates of over 4,200 U.S. and over 20,000 Filipino combatant deaths and up to 200,000 Filipino civilian deaths from a precise demographic count. The overlapping cholera epidemic and disease attribution are expressly qualified.
8. **Formal endpoint:** Roosevelt's July 4, 1902 proclamation is differentiated from continuing post-1902 resistance and the distinct history of campaigns in Moro territories.

### 7. Remaining scholarly limitations disclosed in the publication text

- Conflicting contemporary testimony on the opening clash, responsibility for escalation, and some retaliatory operations remains subject to interpretation.
- The precise number and causes of civilian deaths remain contested; no methodologically uniform contemporary count exists.
- The precise calendar date associated with Malvar's surrender differs between a commonly used date and one historical marker.
- Sakay's 1904 manifesto is accessed through a later scholarly/heritage account, not asserted as a wholly verified original manuscript translation.
- Original geography diagrams are explicitly illustrative and not at scale. Users are directed to period maps held by the Library of Congress for positional evidence.
- The historical discussion synthesizes contemporary documents and verified bibliographic records; it is not a claim to comprehensive line-by-line verification of the full texts of every cited scholarly volume.

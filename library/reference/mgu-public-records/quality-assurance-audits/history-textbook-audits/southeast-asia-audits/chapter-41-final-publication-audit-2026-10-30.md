## Historical and source verification

1. **Myazedi (1112–1113)**: UNESCO registers the quadrilingual stone; Siddham entry **PYU7** identifies the date, patron Rājakumāra, shrine, three villages and dependent people. The original draft's generic donation exercise at §41.11 is replaced with an explicitly sourced paraphrase of this actual inscription. Its second use in §41.22 analyzes the document's linguistic significance. Sources: https://siddham.network/inscription/pyu7/?section=metadata ; https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/myazedi-quadrilingual-stone-inscription
2. **Shwegugyi royal inscription**: the traditionally common **1131 CE** reading is supported in a 2024 Cambridge *JRAS* article; other specialist scholarship gives **1141 CE**. The final chapter records both rather than calling 1131 conclusively settled. The inscription is chiefly Pali verse with a Sanskrit dating statement. See: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-royal-asiatic-society/article/pyu-urns-indian-calendars-and-myanmar-culture/CCADB229AD2898DDB4088CF73284D423 and https://e-space.mmu.ac.uk/623548/3/Myanmar%20Epigraphy%20EFEO%20final.pdf ; published epigraphic edition *Epigraphia Birmanica* I/II no.6 pp.149–150.
3. **Kalyānī inscriptions**: Pegu, **1476**, commissioned by Dhammaceti, a *retrospective* source for the Sri Lankan ordination history and Chapata. Edition: Taw Sein Ko, *The Kalyānī Inscriptions Erected by King Dhammacetī at Pegu in 1476 A.D.: Text and Translation* (1892), https://openlibrary.org/books/OL24152210M/ ; introductory context explicitly warns against treating these as contemporary Pagan records.
4. **Bodh Gayā repairs**: the Burmese inscription dates work from **December 16, 1295 to October 13, 1298**, as corrected by J. F. Fleet and discussed by Michael Willis. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-royal-asiatic-society/article/tibetan-burmese-and-chinese-inscriptions-from-bodhgaya-in-the-british-museum/5B24658C7C41B8979D2E6E0462067A3E
5. **Specific 1242 donation**: replaced §41.25's invented composite with a *source-grounded documentary study* of the female donor identified as the wife of Kankasu and named occupational service groups. Published secondary analysis Goh Geok Yian, *Bagan and the World* (2017), pp.181–182, citing Than Tun (1978), pp.68–69. The panel carefully discloses that the underlying inscription's full edition/inventory identifier was **not independently inspected**, and it is **not presented as a quotation or verbatim primary-source transcription**. https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/bagan-and-the-world/02195A4E5747CCBBE0DFB82061217E94/listing
6. **Burmese Era conversion**: qualified the misleading blanket rule of adding 638; actual Common Era equivalence can be +638 or +639 by month, and inscriptions can employ other eras (notably the Śaka era). See general scholarly cataloguing note: https://rep.adw-goe.de/bitstream/handle/11858/2345/2419_VOHD_XXIII1.pdf
7. **Saddanīti 1154**: retained as established in Pali grammatical scholarship. **Ananda four images** and the women's-renunciant debate are cautiously phrased; no unsupported categorical claim added.

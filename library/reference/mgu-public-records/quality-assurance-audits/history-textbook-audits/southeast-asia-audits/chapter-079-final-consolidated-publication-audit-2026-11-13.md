### 4. Historical corrections and verification

**A. The 1619 conquest and English diplomacy.** The May 30, 1619 capture of Jayakarta is explicitly distinguished from the Anglo-Dutch Treaty of Defence concluded in **July 1619**, after the conquest. Subsequent arrival of treaty news and its impact on Asian company relations are not treated as causes of the earlier fighting. Cross-checked against early company correspondence and relevant historical syntheses.

**B. The naming of Batavia.** The narrative now distinguishes planning discussions in **1617**, use of the name in the fort's early history, and the directors' **1620 resolution** formally reaffirming Batavia for the castle and town following Coen's May 1620 correspondence. The Nationaal Archief identifies this as VOC archive **1.04.02, inventory 100, folio 569**. It is not described as an original 1619 naming resolution.

**C. Fort Jacatra source specificity.** The first source window now identifies the catalogued copies of Jacatra resolutions from **20 November 1618 to 11 January 1619**, VOC archive **1.04.02, inventory 1068, pp./folios 405–420**, and Coen's letter to Pieter van den Broecke dated **3 January 1619**, same inventory, p. 421. The standard ANRI Daily Journals of Batavia Castle from 1624 are not mistaken for an exact 1619 volume.

**D. High Government policy studies.** The second source window identifies the **July 1740 deportation policy episode**, including a **25 July proposal** described in published scholarship. It accurately describes the broader ANRI General Resolutions archival series, but does **not** claim a folio-collated original transcript of that individual decision. Specific wording and attribution to participants therefore remain subject to direct archival consultation.

**E. The 1740 catastrophe.** The massacre is separated from armed conflict outside the city walls; civilians inside the walls are not imputed with the actions of particular rebels. The customary casualty estimate of **approximately 10,000** is treated as uncertain, not a census. The narrative identifies the disputed responsibilities of Adriaan Valckenier and Gustaaf Willem van Imhoff without reducing recorded killings to a single unverified order. It covers 1741–1743 consequences without retelling Chapter 83.

**F. The environment and labor.** The 1730s mortality crisis is presented through environmental, administrative, and epidemiological analysis based especially on Leonard Blussé and P. H. van der Brug. Malaria is treated as a research-supported explanation rather than a retrospective confirmed diagnosis of each death. The labor narrative distinguishes slavery, corvée, wage work, and manumission; household and rural slavery are included.

## Historical audit — evidence and changes

1. **1862 Saigon treaty:** Direct documentary witness in Reinach's *Recueil* (1902), pp. 94–98, identifies Article 3's cessions (Gia Định, Biên Hòa, Định Tường and Côn Đảo), Article 4's limitation on future foreign territorial agreements, and Article 8's four-million-treaty-dollar indemnity over ten years. The textbook explicitly avoids equating these treaty dollars with modern U.S. dollars or French francs. https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Trait%C3%A9_de_Sa%C3%AFgon_(1862).
2. **1863–1864 alternatives:** Expanded Phan Thanh Giản's embassy and the unratified draft associated with Aubaret, distinguishing real negotiation from counterfactual claims of inevitable restoration; Brocheux and Hémery historical synthesis provides context. https://content.ucpress.edu/chapters/10448.ch01.pdf.
3. **1867 southern annexations:** Differentiates military occupation, formal territorial claim, local administrative disruption, and the contemporary dilemmas faced by Phan Thanh Giản. No unsupported precise local casualty figures have been introduced.
4. **1873 and 1882–1883 Tonkin episodes:** Explains Dupuis, Garnier, Rivière, the Black Flag Army, Nguyen decisions, and the transformation of short-lived military occupations into new diplomatic advantages. Historiographical treatment grounded in Taylor, *A History of the Vietnamese* (2013), chapter 10. https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/history-of-the-vietnamese/french-conquest/6C14579A72274909AB5968934CAD15A6.
5. **1874 Saigon treaty:** A clearly marked thematic paraphrase tied to the bibliographically identifiable March 15 treaty in De Clercq's *Recueil*, vol. XI. Article-number quotations were deliberately avoided because complete edition-level collation was not claimed. https://documentsdedroitinternational.fr/ressources/recueils/De_Clercq/De%20Clercq%20-%20Recueil%20des%20trait%C3%A9s%20de%20la%20France%20-%20Tome%2011.pdf.
6. **Harmand 1883 vs Patenôtre 1884:** Specifies foreign-relations transfer and return of Bình Thuận, Thanh Hóa, Nghệ An, and Hà Tĩnh to Annam's administrative zone. The contemporary English translation transmitted in *FRUS 1883*, document 485, is explicitly identified as a mediated printed text. Comparison made with the published 1884 French treaty. https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1883/d485; https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Trait%C3%A9_de_Hu%C3%A9_(1884).
7. **1884 vs 1885 Tianjin documents:** Distinguishes the May 11, 1884 preliminary convention from the June 9, 1885 treaty and identifies relevant Articles I–II in the latter; retains the essential point that Vietnamese representatives were not parties to the Franco-Chinese accord. https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1884/d48; https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1885/d264.
8. **Cần Vương 1885–1896:** Expanded court politics, religion, violence affecting civilians, and the distinct regional Bãi Sậy, Ba Đình, and Hương Khê campaigns. Correctly separates Hàm Nghi's capture in 1888, Phan Đình Phùng's death in 1895, and suppression in 1896. Avoids treating later nationalist ideology as verbatim motives of all participants.
9. **Historiography:** Differentiates naval superiority, fiscal and political pressures, religious motivations, commercial expectations, and policy contingency. Appropriate historical uncertainty is explicit instead of replaced by fabricated quantitative claims.
10. **Chapter boundaries:** Background from Chapter 99 is concise; the detailed wider Indochinese Union remains Chapter 111's responsibility, colonial economy remains Chapter 112's, and twentieth-century nationalism is not prematurely retold.

- Original treaty documents and contemporary printed witnesses were selectively checked, **not** collated against every language version, manuscript original, and all subsequent treaty amendments.

- Browser QA verifies functional local interaction, **not** a full WCAG accessibility audit. Screen-reader testing, live HTTP deployment, and production server navigation were not performed.

## 4. Corrected historical issues and targeted external verification

1. **Buisan and Buayan:** Kapitan Laut Buisan is situated within the Maguindanao political leadership, not identified simply as “of Buayan.” Rajah Sirongan is linked specifically to Buayan, with attention to the changing influence between them. National Commission for Culture and the Arts, “The History of the Muslim in the Philippines,” https://ncca.gov.ph/about-ncca-3/subcommissions/subcommission-on-cultural-communities-and-traditional-arts-sccta/central-cultural-communities/the-history-of-the-muslim-in-the-philippines/; Eberhard Crailsheim, “Negotiating Peace and Faith,” *Philippiniana Sacra* 56, no. 168 (2021), 375–408.
2. **1635 Zamboanga chronology:** Construction is attributed to Juan Cerezo de Salamanca's administration, with Juan de Chaves and Jesuit engineer Melchor de Vera; Corcuera's subsequent military role is distinguished from the original foundation. National Museum of the Philippines, Western-Southern Mindanao, https://www.nationalmuseum.gov.ph/our-museums/regional-area-and-site-museums/western-southern-mindanao/.
3. **1645 and 1646 treaties:** The June 24, 1645 agreement with Sultan Kudarat and the April 14, 1646 agreement with Sulu ruler Rajah Bungsu are treated as separate documents with separate political circumstances. Crailsheim (2021), https://philsacra.ust.edu.ph/admin/downloadarticle?id=0FAA1383E039720481E8F1970BBBDF6B.
4. **Primary-source evidence for 1645:** The chapter identifies the reported archival locator in the Arxiu Històric de la Companyia de Jesús a Catalunya, FILHIS-024, E.ii, b–088, Booklet 161a, fols. 170v–179r. This locator is **mediated through specialist scholarship**; direct manuscript collation is **not** claimed.
5. **Francisco Combés:** The narrative clearly distinguishes his death in 1665 from the posthumous publication of *Historia de las islas de Mindanao, Joló y sus adyacentes* in 1667. Instituto Cervantes, Manila, https://manila.cervantes.es/es/biblioteca_espanol/Filipiniana/XVII/Filipiniana_XVII_Combes.htm.
6. **Raiding and captive-taking:** The treatment acknowledges Spanish and allied enslavement as well as southern raiding, and distinguishes regional capture from transpacific transport. Tatiana Seijas, “Slaving and the Global Reach of the Moro Wars in the Seventeenth Century,” in *Philippine Confluence*; https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv30hx432.15 and *Asian Slaves in Colonial Mexico* (2012).
7. **Vessel chronology:** Terms including *lanong* are qualified to avoid importing extensively documented later-eighteenth- and nineteenth-century formations wholesale into c. 1600. James F. Warren's *The Sulu Zone* is explicitly cited as a later-period study.
8. **Zamboanga after withdrawal:** The 1663 withdrawal and 1718–1719 substantial refortification are distinguished from episodes of later seventeenth-century missionary activity and reported repairs. National Historical Commission of the Philippines, Fort Pilar registry, https://philhistoricsites.nhcp.gov.ph/registry_database/fort-pilar/.
9. **Campaign uncertainty:** Casualty figures and specific vessel counts are not reported without qualifying the colonial source problem. The chapter distinguishes tactical victories from sustained occupation.
10. **Source representation:** The term *Moro* is treated as a colonial category with a later political history, not a single uniform early modern ethnicity or state.

## 7. Explicit publication qualifications

- **Primary-document windows:** All three are **editorial paraphrase/source-study exercises** based on identifiable works and scholarship, not transcriptions collated line by line against manuscript facsimiles. The chapter does **not** invent quotations. The treaty archival locator was independently located in published scholarship, not verified through direct access to the archival manuscript.
- **Historical geography:** The Map Study is text-guided, requiring a suitable historical atlas plate or map for full spatial instruction; it is not a claim that a custom historical map has been drawn or embedded.
- **Bibliography:** The 10-item register supports the principal source-critical and historical claims but is selective; no claim is made of exhaustive multilingual archival verification or a comprehensive review of each primary manuscript.
- **Visual design:** The original supplied HTML shell and MGU styling pattern were preserved and improved for responsive rendering. **Exact byte-level parity with the user's original Chapter 1 template could not be verified**, since that model file was not supplied with this turn.
- **Deployment:** Chromium testing was performed on generated markup, not on the user's production website. Live-path CSS, server configuration, outbound links, and hosted behavior have not been independently deployed or tested.

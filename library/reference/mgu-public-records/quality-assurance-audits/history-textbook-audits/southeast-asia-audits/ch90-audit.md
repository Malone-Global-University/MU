## 3. Publication-critical corrections and holistic additions

1. **Post-Setthathirath recovery (§90.1):** Replaced an undifferentiated 1571–1637 interval with selected, qualified discussion of Sen Surintha, Nakhon Noy, Nokeo Koumane, and Voravongsa Thammikarath. Descent, regnal dates, Burmese involvement, and competing chronicles are explicitly distinguished. Main research: Michel Lorrillard, *La succession de Setthāthirāt* (1999), pp. 45–64.
2. **Political geography and institutions (§§90.2–90.8):** Developed mueang hierarchies, local justice, household labor, provisioning, defense, customary tribute, dynastic marriages, and external diplomacy. Corrected the Champasak/Mun River geography so it does not locate Champasak at the Mun confluence.
3. **Women, labor, uplands and religious life (§§90.9–90.12):** Added household agricultural and textile labor, women as producers and donors, local and monastic religious institutions, upland connections, swidden cultivation and historical limits on retrospective ethnic classification. Section 90.12 increased from 84 to 507 direct narrative words.
4. **Mekong commerce (§§90.13–90.14):** Added rapids, the Khone Falls, overland transfers, indirect trade routes, and the limits of extrapolating from a VOC itinerary to trade volume. Integrated H. ten Brummelhuis's verified 2010 textile-trade study, pp. 125–139.
5. **Foreign visitors and the source window (§90.15):** Distinguished the 1641–1642 Dutch itinerary from Jesuit Giovanni Maria Leria's c. 1642–1647 missionary activity. Corrected the implication of direct access to an unedited Leria diary: relevant observations circulated through later Jesuit reporting, especially Giovanni Filippo de Marini's 1663 publication. The source window now provides an explicitly editorial paraphrase, a documented itinerary, close-reading framework, limits, and published-edition locator.
6. **Golden-age historiography (§90.16):** Explained Lorrillard's contrast between abundant earlier Lao stone inscriptions (roughly 1540–1640) and their relative scarcity during Suriyavongsa's reign. Differentiated epigraphic survival, changes in inscription medium, court stability, royal patronage, and broader prosperity; no unsupported inverse myth was asserted.
7. **Succession and 1707 (§§90.17–90.18):** Expanded the succession crisis, kept the execution-of-the-prince account as chronicle tradition, and differentiated Sai Ong Hue's reported refuge in the Nguyễn/Huế sphere from the specific forms of military backing and later political recognition. Vientiane–Luang Prabang division is described as emerging rival governments, not a precisely surveyed border.
8. **Champasak, 1713, and the three kingdoms (§§90.19–90.20):** Expanded Phra Khu Phon Samek, Princess Sumangkhala and Nokasad as founding traditions, with attention to local leaders, Buddhism, commerce, and contested sovereignty. Ian G. Baird's 2025 scholarly monograph and the verified chapter dealing with the house's origins provide supporting context.
9. **Vocabulary, references and presentation:** Removed the stray `Tak Bat?` token; installed **21 concise, fully defined introductory terms**, retained the 22-term end glossary, supplied an eight-entry linked source section, and added responsive table containers.

## 7. Delimited remaining limitations

This is a first-edition scholarly **textual HTML** chapter rather than a critical edition. Lao chronicles, surviving inscriptions, Dutch originals, and the complete translated 2010 Van Wuysthoff edition were not systematically collated in their original languages or page by page. The source feature is a transparent analytical paraphrase, not a facsimile or verbatim primary text. Some political chronology remains qualified due to genuine disagreement or evidence scarcity. Geography study is text-guided and requires a historical atlas; it is not a reconstructed seventeenth-century map. The user's original Chapter 1 HTML model was not available for exact CSS comparison; this revision retained the uploaded Chapter 90's shell and script with added scoped styles. There was no live MGU website deployment, live network link checking, or full accessibility certification.

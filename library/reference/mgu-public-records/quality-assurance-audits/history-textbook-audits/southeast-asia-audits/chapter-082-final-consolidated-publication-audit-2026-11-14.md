## 3. Consolidated historical corrections made

1. **Talloq succession:** distinguish Matoaya's reign **1593–1623**, continued advisory influence after 1623, death **1636**; Karaeng Kanjilo/Tumammaliang ri Timoroq **1623–1641**; Pattingalloang's succession to ruler of Talloq **1641** and death **1654**. Cummings (2007), ruler tables pp. 109–110; Cummings (2010), reign lists.
2. **Early VOC confrontations:** distinguish Dutch hostage-taking violence in **1615** from the attack on a party from the **Eendracht** that killed sixteen Dutch sailors in **December 1616**; differentiate the later 1637 diplomatic settlement from earlier violence. Mostert (2018) and Feddersen (2017).
3. **Arung Palakka:** specify 1660 Bone rebellion and forced labor context, temporary exile to **Buton** about late 1660–early 1661, and move to **Batavia in 1663**; understand the partnership with VOC as an alliance of different political interests. Andaya (1981).
4. **Treaty of Bungaya 18 November 1667:** identify **Article 6** (Portuguese and English named, others excluded generically), **Article 7** (restricted import categories and company trade), **Article 9** (shipping permits), and the distinct military/political clauses without asserting perfect compliance. Andaya (1981), Appendix B, pp. 305–307.
5. **Hasanuddin's abdication:** **17 June 1669**, not after Somba Opu fell **24 June 1669**; the chronology, main narrative and selected source study distinguish events. Cummings (2007), ruler table, p. 110.
6. **Fort Rotterdam:** clarify Ujung Pandang's **1667 treaty cession** and later occupation/rebuilding, rather than a first acquisition in 1669. Feddersen (2017); Andaya (1981).
7. **Wajo's resistance:** Tosora's fall in **1670** defeated a major organized center of resistance, but did not end every later conflict in South Sulawesi.
8. **Port and postwar economy:** distinguish early Makassar's multi-merchant exchange, later VOC restrictions, the persistence of regional commerce, and developing eighteenth-century marine-product trade. Sutherland (2001; 2004) and Holroyd (recent research).

**Interpretive safeguards retained:** Gowa and Talloq are distinct partnered kingdoms; Bugis and Makassarese are not uniform political blocs; conversion and coerced Islamization are different processes; commercial openness coexists with labor coercion; VOC victory does not imply complete direct administration of all South Sulawesi.

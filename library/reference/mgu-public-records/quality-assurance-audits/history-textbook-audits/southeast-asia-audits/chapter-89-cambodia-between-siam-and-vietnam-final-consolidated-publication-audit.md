## 3. Publication-relevant historical corrections and verification

1. **Chey Chettha II and the Nguyễn marriage:** The original text's assertion that Cambodian and Vietnamese traditions mutually attest the marriage has been corrected in §89.10, the opening, §89.6, and associated summaries. Cambodian chronicle traditions differ on timing; the early Nguyễn chronicles investigated by specialists do not supply the supposed matching independent record. The supposed c. 1623 Prey Nokor arrangement remains clearly differentiated from a transfer of sovereignty. Principal verification: Michael Vickery, *‘1620’, A Cautionary Tale* (2011).
2. **Longvek's later political life:** Corrected the impression of permanent cessation as a royal residence in 1594. Longvek remained inhabited/commercially active and was temporarily used again as a royal center by Satha II in **1732**, before being abandoned as court residence after the 1736 crisis. The correction is integrated in §§89.4 and 89.19. Source: David P. Chandler, *An Eighteenth-Century Inscription from Angkor Wat* (1971), p. 153, note 11.
3. **1643–1644 VOC conflict:** Enriched the historical sequence with the Dutch factory (1637), Pieter van Regemortes' November 1643 embassy and its destruction, Anthony van Diemen's punitive expedition, and the **12 June 1644** battle near Phnom Penh. The text distinguishes the company record from Cambodian motivations. Sources: van der Kraan (2009); Chandler's historical review; Endo (2010).
4. **Ramadhipati I and Islam:** Expanded the relationship between court politics, Malay and Muslim Chinese trade networks, Buddhist institutions, and restored Dutch commerce after the conflict. Sources: Endo (2010), Kersten (2006), and Ian Harris (2005).
5. **1658–1659 Nguyễn intervention:** Expanded the account while distinguishing rival Cambodian factions, the Nguyễn expedition, and conflicting traditions of Ramadhipati's fate; the text does not assert permanent outside occupation following a single campaign. Source: Mak Phoeun and Po Dharma (1984).
6. **Lower Mekong sovereignty:** Replaced categorical loss-of-sea-access phrasing with geographically and chronologically qualified discussion of declining influence over specific customs points, settlements, and commercial routes.
7. **1747 Angkor Wat inscription:** Replaced the generic paraphrase-only teaching box with a full study of a dated source, distinguishing the inscription's explicit testimony from Chandler's modern identifications. Covers the official's career, kinship and women's roles, disorder and military action, merit-making, labor and dependants, and continued ritual use of Angkor Wat. The full published translation is linked; the editorial paraphrase is explicitly labelled **not a quotation**.
8. **Eighteenth-century Cambodia:** Expanded §89.19 from 175 to 600 direct narrative words, including the succession chronology of Ang Em and Satha II, the 1732 Longvek reoccupation, the 1736–1737 crisis, and concrete evidence from the 1747 inscription. Dating limitations are disclosed.

## 7. Delimited scholarly and technical limitations

Chandler's published English translation was reviewed; the Khmer original and all associated medieval and early modern chronicle passages have **not** been collated against original-language manuscripts or facsimiles. Bibliographic verification is targeted, not exhaustive. Exact byte-for-byte conformity to the original Chapter 1 model cannot be certified because that model was not furnished in this revision, although the original uploaded chapter's general design and JavaScript were retained.

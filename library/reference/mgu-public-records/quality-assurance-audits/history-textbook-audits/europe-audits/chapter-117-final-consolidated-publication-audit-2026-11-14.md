## 2. Historical revisions performed

### A. Poland and its continuing resistance

The first build's interpretive safeguards remain: separate the 1 September German invasion from the 17 September Soviet invasion, reject the cavalry-against-tanks myth, distinguish Poland's field defeat from state capitulation, and retain Polish military and government-in-exile continuity. Selectively condensed paragraphs were repaired to preserve the Polish air force's pre-invasion dispersal, casualty methodology, initial German killing of Polish civilians and elites, Soviet security repression and the recognized government-in-exile. These are important documentary distinctions, not incidental descriptive details.

### B. Denmark, Norway and the Western campaign

The consolidation retained the strategic role of Swedish ore shipped through Narvik, Anglo-French landings and air-cover difficulties, Norway's influence on British domestic leadership, the glider attack at Eben-Emael, concentrated Luftwaffe support at Sedan, French efforts to counterattack, the contested Dunkirk halt-order interpretation, and the French defense around Lille. The June 1940 French campaign retains reference to killings of captured Black French colonial soldiers; it should not be told only as an operational story.

### C. Operation Dynamo (required numerical correction)

The original text incorrectly identified only about 190,000–200,000 rescued personnel as British. The final §117.12 identifies **338,226 persons evacuated, 224,320 British and 113,906 other Allied personnel** using the English Heritage compilation. It also uses **933 vessels involved** and **236 lost**, explicitly acknowledging that other vessel totals may use different inclusion rules. It retains the East Mole's central embarkation role and the contributions of the French army, navy, civilian crews, and RAF. The naval figures refer to Operation Dynamo, not later Operation Aerial or Cycle totals. Source: [English Heritage, Operation Dynamo: Things You Need to Know](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/dover-castle/history-and-stories/operation-dynamo-things-you-need-to-know/).

### D. Churchill source window (required documentary correction)

The earlier source window conflated the **4 June 1940** speech with **18 June 1940**. The final window correctly states that Churchill delivered the latter speech in the Commons and **personally rebroadcast it over BBC radio the same evening**. The Commons itself was not broadcasting live; the later BBC recording is contemporaneous to 18 June, not simply a postwar recreation. The 4 June speech was not personally rebroadcast that day; BBC news readers summarized it, with Churchill's familiar recordings made subsequently. The source-window questions now distinguish the Hansard transcript, the contemporaneous rebroadcast, and later editorial audio. Sources: [Library of Congress, *The Finest Hour*](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/churchill/wc-hour.html) and [UK Government History Blog, 4 June broadcast clarification](https://history.blog.gov.uk/2013/12/02/we-shall-fight-on-the-beaches-three-things-you-never-knew-about-churchills-most-famous-speech/).

### E. French statehood and air defense

The final retains the separate dates for the Franco-German armistice (signed 22 June, effective 25 June), the Franco-Italian agreement (24 June), Vichy and Free French claims, the role of African colonial administrations in the Free French movement, the Royal Navy's effect on Operation Sea Lion, and the international composition of Fighter Command. The Fighter Command paragraph was checked after compression to preserve the No. 11 and No. 12 Group organizational distinctions and the Big Wing debate. Luftwaffe escort fighter endurance and the difference between continental tactical air operations and sustained British air defense were restored.

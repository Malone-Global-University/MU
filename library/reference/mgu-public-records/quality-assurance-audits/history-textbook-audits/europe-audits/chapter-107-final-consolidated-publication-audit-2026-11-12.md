## 3. Historical revisions and source controls

### A. Congress of Berlin and Novi Pazar

The original major-places description improperly grouped the **Sanjak of Novi Pazar** with Bosnia-Herzegovina as territory administered by Austria-Hungary. **Corrected**: Bosnia-Herzegovina was occupied and administered under Article 25 and formally annexed in 1908; Ottoman civil administration continued in Novi Pazar, although Austria-Hungary had rights to garrisons and roads and withdrew its troops in 1908. The expanded §107.2 and §107.14 distinguish administration, military rights, sovereignty and annexation.

**Textual verification:** U.S. Department of State, *Foreign Relations of the United States, 1878*, Treaty of Berlin, Article XXV: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1878/d523 . The contemporaneous 1908 Austro-Hungarian withdrawal is also described in U.S. diplomatic correspondence: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1908/d30 .

### B. Austro-German Dual Alliance, 1879

The §107.2 Primary Source Window retains an **explicitly labeled editorial paraphrase**, not a fabricated quotation, and links to the published English text of Articles 1–2. The text provides conditional assistance upon a Russian attack and benevolent neutrality in other situations unless Russian support triggers the further provision. This translation has been compared at the level of its provisions, not independently collated from the original archival signed treaty.

**Document:** https://avalon.law.yale.edu/19th_century/dualalli.asp .

### C. Triple Alliance renewals

The §107.3 narrative now recognizes successive 1887, 1891, 1902 and 1912 treaty texts and the tacit continuations of 1896 and 1908; the supplementary discussion distinguishes Balkan compensation and Mediterranean arrangements from unchanged automatic military commitments.

**Reference:** *Enciclopedia Italiana*, “Triplice alleanza”: https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/triplice-alleanza/ . The historical distinction between new texts and tacit continuation is retained.

### D. Italo-Ottoman War and the Treaty of Ouchy

The original wording of an outright Ottoman **cession** in October 1912 was legally overbroad. Revised §107.12 distinguishes the public treaty's troop-withdrawal provisions from Italy's claims to sovereignty, the Ottoman ruler's retained religious role, and the unrealized withdrawal from the Dodecanese. The treaty did not simply contain a public article of unrestricted territorial cession. Italian assertion of sovereignty and later effective occupation are separately discussed, as is continuing local resistance.

**Documentary controls:** English treaty transcription, https://turkeyswar.com/documents/peace-treaty-italy-1912/ ; Italian government notification to U.S. authorities, https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1912/d785 .

### E. Balkan Wars and the 1913 settlement

Retained and expanded the separate sequences of the First Balkan War (1912–13), Treaty of London (30 May 1913), Second Balkan War (June–July 1913), Treaty of Bucharest (10 August 1913), and the separate Ottoman-Bulgarian Constantinople settlement (September 1913). Main outcomes for Serbia, Greece, Romania, Bulgaria, and the Ottoman Empire are described without projecting modern boundaries backward. Area-growth calculations in the source draft remain explicitly approximate and dependent on source baselines.

**Reference orientation:** Ethnological Museum of Thrace, regional chronology, https://emthrace.org/en/thrace/history/ .

### F. Civilians, memory and evidence

Expanded §107.18 beyond military totals to households, property, food supply, migration, civilian protection, competing political claims, and source selection. The Multiple Perspectives feature now identifies documentary studies from specific chapters of the **1914 Carnegie Endowment commission** and an archival photograph; it labels interpretations as editorial analyses rather than presenting invented personal testimony. The Everyday Life feature explicitly identifies the hypothetical household as a *composite*, not a documented witness.

**Documentary source:** Carnegie Endowment, *Report of the International Commission to Inquire into the Causes and Conduct of the Balkan Wars* (Washington, 1914), digitized chapter index: https://www.promacedonia.org/en/carnegie/index.html ; Chapter II: https://promacedonia.org/en/carnegie/chapter2_1.html . Contemporary inquiry cannot stand in for a comprehensive victim registry or a full multilingual collation of depositions.

### G. Prewar military planning and historical interpretation

The narrative distinguishes operational studies, plans, cabinet decisions and actual mobilizations. Discussion of Schlieffen-era German planning avoids claiming either that all planning was mechanically binding or that recent historiography is unanimous. It explicitly separates strategic thinking and later plans from inevitability of war.

**Scholarly counterpoints:** Terence Zuber, *Inventing the Schlieffen Plan* (Oxford University Press, 2002), https://academic.oup.com/book/10471/chapter-abstract/158344900 ; Robert T. Foley, “The Real Schlieffen Plan” (2006), journal abstract accessible through DOI https://doi.org/10.1191/0968344506wh323xx . These are distinct interpretations rather than fabricated schools.

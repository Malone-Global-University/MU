## 5. Historical source and factual correction register
### Verified targeted corrections and clarifications
1. **Carolina and Locke:** Replaced unqualified claims that Locke personally drafted the *Fundamental Constitutions* with a calibrated description of his secretarial, administrative and manuscript-revision role; attribution of individual provisions remains disputed. Cambridge edition and studies by Farr, Armitage and Brewer support the necessity of distinguishing the editorial, authorial and proprietorial roles.
2. **Grotius:** The Prolegomena hypothetical about natural law and the existence of God is explicitly qualified rather than used to portray him as a nonreligious author. Grotius remained within Christian legal-theological discourse.
3. **Montesquieu and the *Persian Letters*:** Corrected publication context to the fictitious Cologne/Pierre Marteau imprint and actual Amsterdam publishing establishment of Jacques Desbordes, directed by Susanne de Caux.
4. **Rousseau and representation:** Distinguished rejection of representation of sovereign legislative authority from delegation of executive government to magistrates. The comparative review was also repaired.
5. **Coverture and married women:** Replaced an overbroad generalization across Europe with a specifically English common-law treatment and acknowledged varying continental legal frameworks. Corrected the glossary definition.
6. **Locke in French:** Retained the verified 1691 Francophone publication history of *Du gouvernement civil*, supported by the bibliographic research of S.-J. Savonius.
7. **Hume:** Retained 1748 for the initial publication of *Of the Original Contract* in *Three Essays, Moral and Political*; corroborated against bibliographical editorial notes. Some later anthologies reproduce the text under a different collected-edition date, which should not be mistaken for first publication.
8. **Condorcet:** Retained July 3, 1790 for *Sur l’admission des femmes au droit de cité*, consistent with the dated French text.
9. **Interpretive balance:** Expansion clarifies the limits of inference from a treatise to an actual political regime, from colonial administrative involvement to authorship of particular clauses, and from rhetorical universalism to enforceable legal rights.

### Edition and evidence limitations
- All ten primary-source windows remain explicitly identified as analytical **paraphrases**, not checked verbatim transcriptions from page-specific critical editions. The new source register supports historical verification and further study but does not certify complete philological collation.
- No original facsimile plates or historical atlas plates are embedded. The four map and two diagram studies are pedagogical reconstruction instructions within the HTML edition.

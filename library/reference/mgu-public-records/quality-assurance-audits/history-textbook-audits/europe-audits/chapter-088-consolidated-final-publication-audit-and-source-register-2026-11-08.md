### Editorial decision

Claude's uploaded draft was a compressed 20-section framework of fewer than 1,100 direct historical narrative words. It was comprehensively rebuilt, rather than incrementally micro-edited. The rebuilt chapter follows exactly 20 sections in five divisions and stops in the summer before the insurrection of August 10, 1792, preserving the monarchy's overthrow, the republic, the Terror and the Directory for Chapter 89.

The narrative explains the institutions and social agency behind constitutional change, distinguishes immediate laws from gradual implementation, and integrates women's activities, religious conflict, workers and the French Atlantic colonies within the chronology. The largest conceptual safeguards concern the August redemption clauses, the clergy oath, active versus passive citizenship, the May 1791 and April 1792 free-person colonial rights measures, and the 1791–1792 constitutional monarchy.

### III. Selected high-consequence factual checks

1. **1789 constitutional timetable.** Estates-General opened May 5; National Assembly was declared June 17; the Tennis Court Oath occurred June 20; the Assembly identified itself as constituent on July 9. Verified against the French National Assembly's historical record: https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/histoire-et-patrimoine/revolution-francaise/assemblee-nationale-constituante .
2. **August 1789 measures.** The abolition of privileges was not a single complete cancellation of every land-linked payment; certain dues were subject to redemption, while later legislation revisited the settlement. https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/histoire-et-patrimoine/revolution-francaise/nuit-du-4-aout-abolition-des-privileges .
3. **Declaration of Rights.** Seventeen articles adopted August 20–26, 1789, distinguished from later statutes about citizenship. https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/histoire-et-patrimoine/revolution-francaise/la-declaration-des-droits-de-l-homme-et-du-citoyen .
4. **Church reforms.** Civil Constitution of the Clergy adopted July 12, 1790; the separate oath dispute and papal response are treated as a developing sequence, not one event. https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/histoire/eglise-etat/chronologie.asp and https://archives.orne.fr/document/les-tensions-liees-la-constitution-civile-du-clerge .
5. **Occupational association.** Le Chapelier's June 14, 1791 report and the related law are identified, not treated as evidence that all workers agreed with the reform. https://archives-parlementaires.persee.fr/prt/57e34aea-0b12-4663-b164-fb9ea3da433b .
6. **Women's rights intervention.** Olympe de Gouges's 1791 text is identified as her political argument, not a legislative enactment: https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb36057180p .
7. **Colonial distinctions.** The May 15, 1791 debate addressed limited rights for certain free people of color, not emancipation. The April 4, 1792 law (decree adopted March 28) extended political rights to free people of color and free Black residents on the prescribed basis; neither measure abolished slavery. The parliamentary proceedings and the April law were verified against scanned source pages: https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/histoire/esclavage/seance_15mai1791.pdf and https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/histoire/esclavage/decret1792.pdf .
8. **Constitution of 1791.** The constitution adopted September 3 located sovereignty in the nation, delegated executive power to the king and retained electoral distinctions and the royal suspensive veto. https://www.elysee.fr/la-presidence/la-constitution-du-3-septembre-1791 .
9. **Declaration of war.** April 20, 1792, against the Habsburg sovereign formally identified as the king of Bohemia and Hungary; the Assembly acted upon a royal-government proposal. https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/histoire-et-patrimoine/revolution-francaise/declaration-de-guerre-manifestation-populaire-des-girondins .
10. **Visual study.** David's post-event study for the Tennis Court Oath is correctly described as a constructed historical representation, not a contemporaneous photograph: https://www.carnavalet.paris.fr/en/collections/tennis-court-oath-june-20-1789-sketch .

These are **selected, priority factual verifications**, not an assertion that every sentence was collated independently against original manuscripts.

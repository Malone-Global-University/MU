## 3. Historically necessary corrections verified

1. **1916 German Auxiliary Service Law:** Section 1 of the original German law states *vom vollendeten siebzehnten bis zum vollendeten sechzigsten Lebensjahre*, thus from completion of the seventeenth through completion of the sixtieth year. The edited GHDI English rendering uses “eighteen to sixty”; the chapter expressly alerts students to the discrepancy instead of concealing it. It applies to specified male German subjects not already conscripted to the armed forces, and the law's qualifying occupations and representative institutions are described separately. Primary references: [original statute](https://www.1000dokumente.de/Dokumente/Gesetz_%C3%BCber_den_vaterl%C3%A4ndischen_Hilfsdienst?setlang=de), [GHDI translation](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/wilhelmine-germany-and-the-first-world-war-1890-1918/auxiliary-service-law-december-1916).
2. **Ministry of Munitions:** The chronology uses the **9 June 1915** Royal Assent date for the Ministry of Munitions Act, while correctly identifying the 1915 shell crisis as its political context. [UK legislation](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Geo5/5-6/51/pdfs/ukpga_19150051_en.pdf).
3. **Women's Right to Serve March:** Corrected the binding plan's erroneous July 21 date to **17 July 1915**, consistent with cataloged London Museum photographs. [London Museum](https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/v/object-748829/land-girls-at-the-womens-right-to-serve-march/).
4. **British rationing:** Replaced the overprecise plan assertion that compulsory rationing became nationwide for all goods on February 25, 1918. Wartime food rationing expanded by commodity and locality in **January–July 1918**. The cited later British government memorandum specifically says rationing did not become general even for certain foods until July. This is a **1940 retrospective source**, clearly identified as such. [National Archives](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/home-front-1939-1945-part-two/the-need-for-food-control/).
5. **Austro-Hungarian economic institutions:** Corrected a claim about separate Austrian and Hungarian *customs systems*. The two halves shared a common customs area but operated different civilian food-procurement and distribution arrangements. The revision also discusses food distress within Hungary rather than a simple Austrian-starving/Hungarian-prosperous binary.
6. **German Jewish soldiers' census (1916):** Clarifies that the official *Judenzählung* results were **not published** and that the antisemitic allegations are contradicted by other evidence of substantial Jewish military service, not by a publicly released census finding. [Deutsches Historisches Museum](https://www.dhm.de/lemo/kapitel/erster-weltkrieg/innenpolitik/judenzaehlung-1916).
7. **Armenian genocide:** Preserves unequivocal naming of Ottoman state-directed systematic mass murder and separates it analytically from ordinary food shortage, public-health mortality and other occupation experiences. Uses a source-attributed Ottoman Armenian population of approximately 1.5 million in 1915 and the US Holocaust Memorial Museum estimate of **664,000–1.2 million killed** in the 1915–1916 genocide, distinguishing differing demographic baselines. Does not imply the word *genocide* existed in 1915. [USHMM](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-armenian-genocide-1915-16-in-depth).
8. **Chinese labor:** Differentiates the British Chinese Labour Corps (approximately 96,000) from the separate French recruitment commonly estimated around 40,000, while marking the combined approximately 140,000 as a rounded cross-scheme figure rather than a single corps. [1914–1918-online labor study](https://encyclopedia.1914-1918-online.net/article/labor/?format=pdf); [National Army Museum](https://collection.nam.ac.uk/detail.php?acc=2007-08-13--4).
9. **Belgian relief and occupation:** Links an identifiable contemporaneous October 16, 1914 telegram by U.S. minister Brand Whitlock, a dated November 7, 1916 protest by Cardinal Mercier, and an occupation summons reported within the latter, preserving the difference between verified archival transmission and an edited web transcript. [Whitlock telegram](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1914Supp/d1290); [Mercier transcription](https://www.firstworldwar.com/source/belgium_mercier1.htm).
10. **Household welfare:** The Everyday Life case study uses an identifiable House of Commons response of **17 October 1918** and dated army allowance schedule for a soldier's wife and two children. It explicitly avoids inventing a diary or treating an allowance as actual household spending. [Hansard](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1918/oct/17/separation-allowances-for-children).
11. **Influenza:** Describes the 1918–1919 pandemic as distinct from organized wartime persecution, notes uncertainty about its origin, and separates the various waves from aggregate mortality figures. [CDC historical summary](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/flu/pandemic-resources/1918-commemoration/1918-pandemic-history.htm).

## 6. Explicit publication limits

- **Primary-source collation:** Selectively verified original law text, translated statute, legislation, named parliamentary record, contemporary diplomatic document, archival photo catalog, and relevant modern historical summaries. The guide is a research register, **not** evidence of exhaustive original German, French, Ottoman Turkish or Armenian archival/facsimile collation. Historical interpretations and some numerical estimates remain selectively rather than exhaustively source-checked.
- **Outbound links:** Named and meaningful documentary records are embedded. Some 1914–1918-online source URLs were blocked behind automated access protection when tested from the research environment; this does not establish that those pages are invalid for ordinary users. **Not all 26 source records were independently verified for live URL health.**
- **Maps:** Geography activities are text-guided. Students require a period-specific external atlas; no historical atlas plates are included.
- **Photograph:** The National Army Museum catalog describes the archival object as out of copyright. It remains an external collection link; credit, image retrieval and any publisher-specific reproduction requirements are not asserted cleared.
- **CSS/live deployment:** The functional stylesheet and script were adapted from the approved Chapter 109 publication file, **not independently byte-compared against the original Chapter 2 model**. Production hosting, cross-page navigation and live MGU site integration were not tested.
- **Historiographical scope:** The chapter is an evidence-oriented first-edition textual synthesis. The global range of genocide deaths, influenza mortality and blockade excess mortality must be read with the named institutional and study definitions; there is no claim of original demographic recalculation.

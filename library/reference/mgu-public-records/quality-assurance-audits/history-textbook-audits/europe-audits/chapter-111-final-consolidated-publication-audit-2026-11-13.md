## IV. Historical corrections and documentary verification

1. **Constituent Assembly shootings:** The source manuscript wrongly placed the Petrograd shootings of pro-Assembly demonstrators one day before the Assembly met. Corrected to **January 18, 1918 New Style / January 5 Old Style**, the opening day of the Assembly; the body's overnight proceedings and dissolution are described separately. The election, SR–Bolshevik vote distributions, Left/Right SR split and resulting legitimacy problem remain differentiated. Source comparison: U.S. State Department, *Foreign Relations of the United States, 1918*, Russian political documents, [Establishment of Soviet Power](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1918Russiav01/ch7).
2. **Order No. 1:** Reaffirmed **March 14 New Style / March 1 Old Style, 1917** and specified the **Petrograd District garrison** as the initial addressee. Explained soldiers' committees and control of weapons, the political relationship of orders issued by the Duma commission and Soviet, and preservation of discipline on duty. The feature labels editorial paraphrase separately from a source translation, and links both [Yale Avalon](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/soviet_001.asp) and [University of Illinois Seventeen Moments](https://seventeenmoments.web.illinois.edu/1917-2/revolution-in-the-army/revolution-in-the-army-texts/order-no-i/) editions; the versions are not silently merged.
3. **Brest-Litovsk: two different treaties:** The Ukrainian People's Republic's **February 9, 1918** treaty with the Central Powers is distinguished from Soviet Russia's **March 3, 1918** treaty. The chapter contains a linked comparative document study, without miscounting it as a twelfth recurring feature. Sources: [February 9 UPR agreement, FRUS](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1918Russiav02/d785); [March 3 Soviet Russian treaty, Yale Avalon](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/brest.asp).
4. **Brest Article IV:** Corrected the manuscript's mistaken direct-restoration wording for **Ardahan, Kars and Batum**. Article IV distinguishes the return of eastern Anatolian provinces to Turkey from Russian evacuation and non-interference in the reorganization of the named Caucasian districts, involving neighboring states, especially Turkey. The explanatory treatment also identifies **Article III** (loss of Russian sovereignty west of the line) and **Article VI** (Ukraine, Estonia/Livonia, Finland and Åland commitments), distinguishing legal dispositions from military occupation and effective territorial control. Source: [treaty text](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/brest.asp).
5. **Calendar discipline:** Chronology rebuilt with a consistent Gregorian/New Style basis and Old Style cross-dates for key events before the Russian February 1918 reform. Lenin returns April 16 N.S.; the April Theses are presented April 17. The chronology now separately dates Kerensky's accession and the July Days, and places the December 15 armistice **before** the December 20 Cheka creation.
6. **Civil-war interpretation:** Expanded the geographical and organizational distinctions among Reds, Whites, Komuch, Czechoslovak Legion, peasant/Green movements, independent national forces and foreign interventions. The essay explicitly avoids one unified 'White' or 'Green' command and differentiates political coercion, pogroms, food requisition and famine causation.
7. **National sovereignty and historical geography:** Expanded and independently differentiated Finland, Ukraine, Poland, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Belarusian claims and the Caucasian republics. The Geography Study now requires dated comparative maps for **1914, 1917, March 1918 and 1921**, distinguishing claim, recognition, foreign occupation and effective administration.
8. **Historiography:** The chapter's §111.20 discussion now differentiates the broad analytical emphases of Rex Wade, Sheila Fitzpatrick, S. A. Smith, Orlando Figes and Alexander Rabinowitch without manufacturing quotations or treating them as mutually exclusive positions.

**Precision limits:** Detailed casualty and famine figures are treated as approximate; estimates depend on period, region, archival coverage, source politics and definition of death category. The documentary research links are identified secondary references and English-language editions; no claim is made to have personally collated every Russian, Ukrainian, Georgian, Polish or Finnish original or fully confirmed all historical population figures against manuscript sources.

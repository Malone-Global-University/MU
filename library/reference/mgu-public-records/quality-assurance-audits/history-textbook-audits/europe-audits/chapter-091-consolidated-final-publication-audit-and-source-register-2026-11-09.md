## Historical verification and editorial correction register

- **1805–1806:** Ulm and Austerlitz are distinguished from the December 26 Pressburg treaty, July 1806 formation of the Confederation of the Rhine, and August 1806 renunciation of the Holy Roman imperial title. The treaty is identified as a negotiated settlement under decisive French military advantage.
- **October 21, 1805:** Nelson and Collingwood, Villeneuve and the combined French-Spanish fleet are distinguished. The October 9 memorandum is identified from Royal Museums Greenwich; the battle is not treated as establishing unchallenged command of every sea.
- **October 14, 1806:** Jena and Auerstedt are explicitly paired and distinguished, including Davout's role. Eylau is not falsely called a decisive French victory; Friedland and the separate Tilsit treaties are dated correctly.
- **July 1807 treaty correction:** Franco-Russian (July 7) and Franco-Prussian (July 9) texts are distinguished. The large indemnity and detailed occupation-evacuation arrangements settled in **1808** are not misrepresented as specific July 1807 clauses. Russian and Prussian bargaining positions were not identical.
- **1808 Bayonne:** Constitutional statute dated July 6, promulgated July 8; distinguishes imposed dynastic arrangements from political consent. Spanish anti-French resistance is not equated with anti-reform absolutism.
- **March 19, 1812 Cádiz Constitution:** National sovereignty, Catholic establishment and the monarchy on both sides of the Atlantic are distinguished, with attention to citizenship exclusions and imperial context.
- **German identity:** Fichte's speeches and Frederick William III's March 17, 1813 proclamation are treated as different genres and purposes, not as proof of a homogeneous German nationalist public. Polish service with Napoleon is explained as an example of national aspirations aligning with France.
- **Russian campaign:** Smolensk, September 7 Borodino, Moscow and the October retreat are placed in sequence. Winter is not the sole explanation of disaster. The Twenty-Ninth Bulletin is explicitly interpreted as a government account with interests and omissions; the quantitative exercise prevents the misreading of missing strength as confirmed deaths.
- **1813–1815:** Sixth Coalition, Leipzig (October 16–19, 1813), unconditional first abdication (April 6, 1814), Napoleon's March 20, 1815 return, April 22 Additional Act, June 18 Waterloo, and June 22 second abdication are kept distinct. Wellington and Blücher are both credited; the multinational composition of the allied army is recognized.
- **Vienna chronology:** Congress Final Act of June 9, 1815 predates Waterloo, so Chapter 92's settlement is not incorrectly represented as starting after June 18.

## Publication scope and limitations

- Ten documentary windows are authentic **identified editorial paraphrases**, not quotations collated line-by-line with original archival manuscripts or original-language translations. Record links enable further collation; some texts are hosted as scholarly or curated transcriptions, not original facsimiles.
- Four map studies are **text-based atlas exercises** requiring a period-appropriate external map, not embedded cartographic plates.
- Goya's painting is treated as a visual-source exercise with an authoritative museum reference and is not reproduced as an embedded artwork.
- Military strength and casualty figures are deliberately not invented or conflated; the quantitative exercise requires same-definition source data and states limitations. The arithmetic example is explicitly hypothetical.
- Not every narrative claim has been independently compared with exhaustive multilingual archival sources; checks were targeted at major dates, treaty/document identity, scope and typical historiographical hazards. Publication approval is for a transparent first-edition scholarly textual HTML chapter.
- Forward navigation points to the conventionally named Chapter 92 page; its deployment has not been verified.

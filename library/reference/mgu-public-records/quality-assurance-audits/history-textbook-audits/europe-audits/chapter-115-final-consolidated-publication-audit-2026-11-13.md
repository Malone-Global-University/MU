## 3. Scholarly corrections and historical safeguards

1. **Italy 1922 versus consolidation:** Explained the October 1922 March on Rome as royal appointment amid intimidation, while distinguishing the 1923 Acerbo Law, 1924 Matteotti crisis, and 1925–1926 legislation. A 1922 mapping category now records authoritarian transition rather than treating the October appointment as an instantly finished dictatorship.
2. **Fascist racial policy:** Distinguished Italian empire's earlier racial violence and colonial domination from the additional 1938 antisemitic laws; maintained monarchy, Church, employers and army as qualified institutional constraints, not evidence of democratic governance.
3. **German 1933 legislation:** Verified that the Reichstag Fire Decree was issued on 28 February 1933 and the Enabling Act was voted on 23 March, promulgated on 24 March; incorporated 43.9 percent of the March 1933 vote, the 444–94 Enabling Act division, and the exclusion of 81 Communist and 26 Social Democratic deputies. The source window is explicitly **editorial paraphrase, not quotation** and now links to the relevant documentary editions.
4. **1935 racial citizenship:** Distinguishes the Nuremberg Laws of 15 September 1935 from the First Regulation of 14 November that expanded their implementation. Separates prewar exclusion from the later wartime Holocaust narrative for Chapter 121.
5. **Forced sterilization:** Clearly labels the approximate 400,000 figure as a cumulative Nazi-era estimate **through 1945**, not an accomplished total by 1938. Nazi persecution extends beyond only Jewish victims, with separately identified groups and practices.
6. **Stalin's party succession:** Corrects the sequence to Stalin allied **first with Zinoviev and Kamenev against Trotsky**, **then with Bukharin against Zinoviev/Kamenev and the United Opposition**, and **finally against Bukharin and the Right**. Distinguishes Trotsky's party expulsion **1927** and expulsion from the USSR **1929**.
7. **Bolshevik one-party history:** Chronology now identifies the November 1917 seizure as the beginning of a revolutionary government, with political monopoly consolidating subsequently rather than immediately and unconditionally.
8. **Soviet property:** Replaces the categorical assertion that all private activity was abolished with an account of overwhelmingly state-controlled major industry alongside cooperatives, regulated household activities and collective farms.
9. **Soviet famine mortality:** Identifies the 1932–1934 Ukrainian estimate of approximately **3.9 million direct excess deaths** and **0.6 million lost births** separately, and the larger **8.7 million Soviet excess deaths** including Ukraine. Distinguishes Kazakh pastoral dislocation, contested interpretation and non-additive demographic categories.
10. **Great Terror and Gulag:** Gives the archival **681,692 recorded executions in 1937–1938** with category and date caveats. Defines the January 1, 1939 Soviet corrective-labor camp and colony census as approximately **1.67 million**, excluding other custodial categories.
11. **Authoritarian comparisons:** Separates Portugal's **PVDE (1933)** from the later PIDE label, differentiates Fascist and conservative governments, and dates the Czechoslovak **Second Republic's authoritarian turn (October 1938–March 1939)** rather than categorizing the state as an unchanged democracy through 1939.
12. **Conceptual chronology:** Explains totalitarian terminology in Italian politics during the 1920s before subsequent, particularly post-1945, comparative scholarship. Preserves ideology, property and victims' experiences as historically distinct questions.

## 2. Content, scope and historical coherence

**Retention:** All five division banners, twenty chapter sections and eleven assigned features remain. The chapter continues to cover Italy's entry and naval warfare; Egypt, Compass, the Greek war; Balkan diplomatic context; Yugoslavia, Greece and Crete; the desert campaigns and Malta; occupation and mass violence in Croatia, Yugoslavia and Greece; Torch, Tunisia, Sicily and the Italian armistice. The completed chapter retains the distinctions between military victory and political control, between Nazi and Ustaša crimes, and between occupation jurisdictions. It does not absorb the full East Front, general occupation history, wartime Holocaust, or Italian mainland campaigns from Chapters 119–122.

**Major expansions:** §§118.5–118.7 now explain the separate strategies of Romania, Hungary, Bulgaria, Turkey and Yugoslavia; Pact diplomacy and German war decision; the flank collapse of the Metaxas Line and Allied evacuation. §§118.13–118.16 develop the jurisdictional partition of Yugoslavia and Greece, Ustaša violence, the variation of Partisan and Chetnik relations, Greek famine mortality and relief, and the political conditions of occupied Jewish populations. §§118.17–118.19 add French North African colonial politics, Tunisia's command and supply problems, military developments after Kasserine and the experience of Sicilian civilians. Military, maritime and imperial logistics are also strengthened throughout.

**Corrections independently grounded in selected sources:**

1. §118.1: The Franco-Italian armistice was **signed June 24, 1940 and took effect June 25**; no implication of an armistice being merely 'sought' on the 24th remains.
2. §118.6: The Simović government attempted to reassure Berlin after the March 27 coup; it did not simply and unequivocally leave the Tripartite Pact. Hitler ordered the invasion on March 27, while German forces attacked April 6. See USHMM, *Axis Invasion of Yugoslavia*, and contemporary Hitler Directive No. 25 (Nuremberg exhibit **C-127**).
3. §118.14: Jasenovac is now attributed to the US Holocaust Memorial Museum's **77,000–99,000** estimated deaths for the camp complex, 1941–1945. The Jasenovac Memorial Site's **83,145 identified individuals** is distinguished from any exhaustive population total, and camp murders from other Ustaša killings. Earlier generalized ranges are contextualized rather than used as a replacement for source identification.
4. §118.16: Greek famine mortality is explicitly qualified by cause, interval and geography: comparative scholarship commonly cites approximately **100,000–200,000** famine deaths for the acute 1941–42 crisis; higher figures for other definitions and periods are not treated as interchangeable. Loss of births is not counted as recorded deaths.
5. §118.18: The final Tunisia prisoners figure is attributed to the USHMM estimate of approximately **267,000** German and Italian prisoners following May 1943 surrender; this is distinct from all North African campaign casualties.
6. Chronology and §118.20: **Operation Achse commenced following the September 8, 1943 announcement**, whereas Allied landings at Salerno were on **September 9**. German operations are not falsely dated as beginning only on September 9.
7. §118.20 source study: The short Italian armistice remains explicitly a **paraphrase**, not a quotation. It is now linked to the actual twelve-article agreement and gives accurate article-level references: 1–3 (hostilities, denial of German facilities, prisoners), 4 (fleet and aircraft), 6–8 (territory and withdrawal), 10–11 (Allied military control), and 12 (future political/economic conditions). Text: **61 Stat. 2740, TIAS 1604**, reprinted in Charles I. Bevans, ed., *Treaties and Other International Agreements of the United States of America, 1776–1949*, vol. 3 (GPO, 1969).

Historical checking was **targeted rather than exhaustive**. The text uses contextual qualifications for contested statistics, especially Greek famine deaths and deaths during occupation. Avoid presenting the research guide's selection as a full critical edition of every cited source.

## III. Historical verification and targeted corrections

1. **Sarajevo (June 28, 1914):** Franz Ferdinand and Sophie were assassinated by Gavrilo Princip after the earlier bombing attempt. The narrative maintains the difference between the small Young Bosnia conspiratorial milieu, Serbian clandestine military networks, and proof of Serbian cabinet direction. It does not equate assistance by individual officers with a demonstrated order by Nikola Pašić's government.
2. **German assurance (July 5–6):** The *blank cheque* is correctly treated as a broad political assurance, not an unavoidable invocation of the 1879 defensive alliance. Vienna's choice of ultimatum and Berlin's support receive separate analytical responsibility.
3. **Ultimatum and response (July 23/25):** The ten numbered demands are compared individually. Demands 5 and 6 concerning foreign officials in Serbia are distinguished. The Serbian response is described as substantially conciliatory but qualified, not reduced to the shorthand “accepted all except one.” The feature clearly labels the two-column comparison as an **editorial paraphrase** and links a published translation of the Austrian note and a contemporary U.S. diplomatic interpretation of Serbia's response. It also points students to an archive index listing the separately translated reply.
4. **Late mediation:** Grey's proposed conference and the *Halt in Belgrade* possibility are evaluated alongside the constraints and incompleteness of their communication. None is presented as a guaranteed peace outcome.
5. **Russian military decisions:** Preparatory measures, partial mobilization, Nicholas II's **July 30** general mobilization authorization and implementation **July 31** remain distinct. Mobilization is not equated mechanically with a declaration of war.
6. **French/German decisions (August 1):** French and German general mobilization orders are described as close in time with the French order shortly earlier. The chapter distinguishes earlier preparations and the separate German declaration of war on Russia on August 1 and France on August 3.
7. **Lichnowsky / August 1 misunderstanding:** The previously oversimplified narrative was corrected: Moltke resisted reorienting the German deployment, **but Grey's apparent neutrality guarantee was also clarified as a misunderstanding**. The text no longer makes military refusal alone the decisive explanation. Source comparison: published telegrams and historical research.
8. **Luxembourg and Belgium:** German occupation of Luxembourg on **August 2** is distinguished from the August 2 German ultimatum to Belgium, Belgium's August 3 rejection and German invasion on **August 4**. Britain chose to intervene on August 4; the entente with France was not a tripartite automatic war treaty.
9. **Italy, Romania, Ottoman Empire:** Initial neutrality of Italy and Romania and the Ottoman secret agreement of August 2 are separately identified. Ottoman signature is not treated as immediate entry into active combat.
10. **Financial anxiety:** Bank Rate figures are identified as 3% before July 30, 4% July 30, 8% July 31, and 10% August 1, drawn from the Bank of England's indexed institutional wartime history. The rate is not used as a proxy for all household experiences.
11. **War aims and historiography:** The chapter does not project Germany's later *September Programme* into June 1914. The history of the *Fischer*, *Williamson*, *Clark*, and *Mombauer* interpretations is kept distinct. The account of Versailles **Article 231** identifies the legal responsibility for loss and damage, rather than treating it as a literal July 1914 confession of sole German intent to start war.
12. **Chronological boundary:** The immediate outbreak sequence ends with Austria-Hungary's **August 6 declaration of war on Russia**. Detailed fronts, years of wartime home-front mobilization, and later postwar settlement questions are reserved to their assigned chapters.

## V. First-edition limits and final decision

**Disclosed limitations:**

1. The source window and selected historical evidence are referenced to published transcriptions, parliamentary and diplomatic editions, and modern scholarship, but the source corpus was **not exhaustively collated against original-language facsimiles**. Editorial paraphrases are labeled accordingly.
2. The mobile and desktop screenshot checks validate layout/behavior, not a complete external accessibility certification or live-site deployment test.
3. The Sarajevo photograph (**Imperial War Museums Q 91848**) remains linked rather than embedded pending independent rights clearance. The IWM catalog endpoint may restrict automated access; the separately available Commons collection record provides contextual identification. The artifact does not claim a license for reproduction.
4. Map exercises are **text-guided**; suitable historical atlas plates are not embedded.
5. The original Chapter 2 template was not independently attached for a byte-for-byte CSS comparison. The resulting HTML follows the established MGU design, but literal Chapter 2 CSS parity is not certified.
6. Verification of every scholarly citation in every relevant language, as in a critical documentary edition, was not attempted. Selected high-priority factual claims and named primary documentary points were checked.

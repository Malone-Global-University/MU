## Consolidated scholarly corrections

1. **Italian family law:** Differentiated the 1970 divorce law and 1978 abortion law from the subsequent attempts to repeal them in 1974 and 1981 referendums.
2. **Ireland:** Differentiated the 1995 constitutional referendum from the 1996 Family Law (Divorce) Act, effective 27 February 1997.
3. **Esping-Andersen:** Identified the 1990 typology's focus on decommodification and stratification, while explaining feminist criticisms concerning gender and unpaid care rather than attributing those concerns to the original analytical model.
4. **Primary-source window:** Replaced an unspecified summary of factory testimony with the verifiable 1832 parliamentary evidence of Leeds flax-mill worker **Elizabeth Bentley**, published in House of Commons parliamentary papers. The Factory Act of 1833 and the Treaty of Rome Article 119 remain, with more precise context and official or document-based source links. All three accounts are labeled as paraphrases, not quotations.
5. **Black Death:** Qualified the range of Europe-wide mortality estimates without presenting a disputed exact toll as settled fact.
6. **Displaced Germans:** Assigned the figure of at least twelve million to the approximate 1944–1950 period, including wartime flight as well as postwar expulsions.
7. **EU platform work:** Added Directive (EU) 2024/2831 and the future transposition deadline of 2 December 2026. This account is explicitly capped at **20 November 2026** and does not claim to evaluate the directive's future effects.
8. **Introductory vocabulary:** Supplied 23 concise, distinct definitions in the existing model's term-card presentation.
9. **Research transparency:** Added a selected primary-source and historical reading section, with verified direct links where available.
10. **Responsive tables:** Wrapped two broad tables in isolated horizontal-scroll containers, retaining the established chapter stylesheet and script.

## 2. Editorial completion judgment

Chapter 87 now performs the intended historical role of opening Part XIV. It establishes the French monarchy's legal and fiscal institutions, the distinct political meaning of corporate privilege and estates, the crisis of French public credit and legitimate consent, and the American Revolution as an international and socially restricted constitutional transformation. It includes the agency of enslaved people and Indigenous peoples; Dutch, Irish, Genevan, and Polish-Lithuanian comparators; and a detailed transition through the 1787 Notables and the 1788 provincial/judicial crisis. The direct narrative stops with the royal council decision of **August 8, 1788** to call the Estates-General, leaving subsequent elections, May 1789, and revolutionary developments to Chapter 88.

The 20-section architecture is intact, all requested teaching categories are present, and initial draft omissions have been addressed in one holistic expansion. No publication-critical unresolved factual error was established in this final inspection. Cautions below define the limits of approval; they are not a claim of exhaustive original-manuscript collation.

## 6. Independent historical accuracy and interpretation checks

1. **French parlements:** Identified accurately as sovereign appellate courts with registration and remonstrance functions, not elected legislatures. The March 1766 address is distinguishable from retrospective constitutional interpretation. Primary-source text context: [royal address and court record, March 1766](https://royaumedefrance.fr/bibliotheque/discours-de-la-flagellation/).
2. **Turgot's reforms:** Royal edicts of February 1776, registered under compulsion, separated from later reversals. The guild edict is documented through [Turgot's collected writings and reproductions](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/%C5%92uvres_de_Turgot_(Daire,_1844)/Actes_du_Minist%C3%A8re_de_Turgot/Sur_l%E2%80%99industrie_agricole,_manufacturi%C3%A8re_et_commerciale).
3. **Public finance:** Necker's 1781 Compte rendu is treated as a political intervention whose ordinary-account categories cannot be taken as total war finance. The distinction between debt principal, annual deficit, and service costs is explained. An identifiable [1781 text](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Compte_rendu_au_Roi) is linked.
4. **French taxation:** Avoids the incorrect assertion that nobility and clergy paid zero taxes, distinguishing legal privileges, indirect duties, and actual fiscal incidence. Avoids presenting invented debt totals.
5. **American imperial chronology:** Stamp Act 1765, repeal/Declaratory Act 1766, Townshend duties 1767, Tea Act and Boston protest 1773, Coercive Acts and Congress 1774, armed conflict 1775 are distinguished. [UK Parliament: Stamp Act](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/legislativescrutiny/parliament-and-empire/collections1/collections1/stamp-act/).
6. **Independence chronology:** July 2 resolution, July 4 adopted declaration, August engrossment/signing are not conflated. [US National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/declaration-of-independence).
7. **International war:** France's formal 1778 alliance differentiated from Spain's 1779 belligerency and the Dutch Republic's later war with Britain. Peace with Britain and related 1783 European settlements distinguished.
8. **United States Constitution:** Drafted/signed in 1787, subsequent ratification and commencement in 1789 distinguished; its compromises over slavery identified. [US National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/constitution).
9. **Freedom litigation:** Elizabeth Freeman's 1781 suit differentiated from the Quock Walker cases culminating in Cushing's charge in the 1783 Massachusetts criminal prosecution. No claim of nationwide US abolition. [Massachusetts Court System](https://www.mass.gov/guides/massachusetts-constitution-and-the-abolition-of-slavery).
10. **Dutch Patriots:** Joan Derk van der Capellen's anonymous 1781 pamphlet and the 1787 Prussian restoration are correctly distinguished from the later 1795 Batavian changes. [Nationaal Archief](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/beleven/onderwijs/bronnenbox/aan-het-volk-van-nederland-1781).
11. **Notables in 1787:** Properly describes Louis XVI as the formal convener upon Calonne's advice; does not claim universal elite rejection of all reforms. Committee records show nuanced positions. [Fifth Committee protest](https://www.revolution.chnm.org/exhibits/show/liberty--equality--fraternity/item/551); [Michael P. Fitzsimmons, English Historical Review (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1093/ehr/ceae115).
12. **1788 crisis:** Lamoignon reform, Grenoble June 7, Vizille in July, August 8 council summons, and later August treasury measures are chronologically distinguished. The narrative explicitly stops before revolutionary proceedings in 1789. [ARCPA/Persee Archives parlementaires, vol. I](https://archives-parlementaires.persee.fr/doc/2c452421-3302-4236-86a5-32f84f9bef95); [Oxford chronology](https://erenow.org/modern/the-oxford-history-of-the-french-revolution/19.php).

## 8. Publication limits and explicit exclusions

1. **Source collation:** Primary-source passages remain *labeled editorial paraphrases*. Original manuscript records and original-language first impressions were not exhaustively collated; modern transcriptions/translations should not be represented as a philologically verified edition. Window 10 is grounded in a later documentary compilation, not an independently inspected royal council manuscript.
2. **Geographical illustration:** All four historical map studies are interpretive exercises requiring dated, externally sourced historical atlas plates. No atlas plates are embedded or claimed.
3. **Visual plate:** The Notables study links to a specific museum record. Its actual image is not embedded in the delivered HTML.
4. **Quantitative exercise:** The fiscal data box teaches students to construct a sourced table from identified accounts. It does not pretend to provide a finished numerical series; no unsupported fiscal numbers were fabricated.
5. **Scope:** The narrative deliberately stops at the August 8, 1788 decision and does not substitute for Chapter 88's treatment of the 1789 revolution.
6. **Bibliography:** The source register identifies documentary bases and select scholarly corroboration, but the chapter is not an exhaustive scholarly bibliography or critical transcription.

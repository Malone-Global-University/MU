## 3. Independent historical verification

### Confirmed against identified material

1. **Bodin:** *Les six livres de la République* first published in French in 1576; Bodin's Latin version in 1586 and Knolles's English translation in 1606. Sovereignty's supreme legislative authority, divine/natural-law constraints, and restrictions on arbitrary taxation are accurately summarized. Verify editions at [Tufts Perseus](https://atlas.perseus.tufts.edu/library/urn%3Acts%3Apdlpsci%3Abodin.livrep.perseus-eng1/) and see the [Stanford Humanities Center account](https://shc.stanford.edu/arcade/publications/rofl/issues/volume-2-issue-2/jean-bodin-sovereignty).
2. **English parliamentary excise:** The foundational excise ordinance was passed **22 July 1643**, imposing duties during the Civil War. The original printing is indexed in [Open Library](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL44876781M/An_ordinance_of_the_Lords_and_Commons_assembled_in_Parliament); William J. Ashworth's *Customs and Excise*, chapter 6, explains that duties were initially renewed monthly. The manuscript's language was corrected to avoid implying an instantly permanent and fully salaried fiscal bureaucracy.
3. **Villers-Cotterêts:** The official [Légifrance ordinance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/LEGITEXT000006070939/) confirms **Article 50** concerning burial registers of beneficed clergy, **Article 51** on baptism registers, **Article 110** on clarity of judgments, and **Article 111** prescribing French for specified legal records. Corrected the source analysis: Article 111 did **not** apply *only* to French-speaking regions, although practical adoption varied across multilingual regions.
4. **Peace of Osnabrück, 24 October 1648:** [German History in Documents and Images](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/from-the-reformations-to-the-thirty-years-war-1500-1648/peace-treaties-of-westphalia-october-14-24-1648) verifies the qualified right of imperial estates to make alliances under **Article VIII §2**; the treaty did not make all estates independent modern sovereign states. Confirmed the article reference and removed an unnecessary unresolved-number caveat.
5. **English Bill of Rights:** The [enacted statute](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/aep/WillandMarSess2/1/2/enacted) and [UK Parliament's historical explanation](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/parliamentaryauthority/revolution/overview/billofrights/) confirm parliamentary consent for revenue and peacetime standing armies; although commonly called the 1689 Bill, the official statute database assigns the Act 1688 under old statute-dating conventions and confirms **16 December 1689** royal assent. Corrected the misleading suggestion that the Declaration was a formal prerequisite or condition of William and Mary's acceptance of the throne. The Declaration was presented with the offer, and it acquired statutory force in December.
6. **Quantitative context:** The [UK Parliament history of the Financial Revolution](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/parliamentaryauthority/revolution/overview/financialrevolution/) reports nearly **£5 million annual tax revenue** raised on average during the war beginning in 1689. Public expenditure and tax revenue are explicitly separated. John A. Lynn's [Cambridge University Press study](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/giant-of-the-grand-siecle/preface/7F158F3DCB173E84E2FE96A04EBD5898) verifies up to about **420,000 French troops on paper** by the 1690s, not a necessarily equal number actually serving. Geoffrey Parker's *The Army of Flanders and the Spanish Road* and related reconstructions support a roughly 62,000-person Army of Flanders around 1591; replacing the original vague “c. 1600” range prevents obscuring the sharp decline following the 1609 truce.
7. **Castilian payment suspensions:** The manuscript's selected sequence (1557, 1560, 1575, 1596, 1607, 1627, 1647) is supported in several scholarly accounts, but historians sometimes combine 1557–60 into a single restructuring and count additional suspensions (notably 1652 and 1662). The quantitative study now identifies these as *selected*, not exhaustive, dates. The key interpretation, that suspensions involved renegotiations rather than straightforward repudiation of all sovereign obligations, is appropriate.

### Interpretative strengths and remaining scholarly qualifications

- Strong distinction between fiscal extraction, administrative reach, military effectiveness, consent, and legitimacy. The chapter does not equate state capacity with absolutism.
- Appropriate comparison of composite monarchies, republics, negotiated jurisdictions, and the role of contractors and creditors.
- Military-revolution and centralization debates represented as scholarly controversies rather than settled universal models.
- Composite everyday-life and perspectives features are transparently instructional, not fabricated personal testimonies.
- Military numerical estimates are **orders of magnitude**, sensitive to muster practices, paper strength, season, garrisons, and scholarly reconstruction; they should not be republished as exact year-by-year national series. This caveat is explicit in the final manuscript. No fabricated direct quotations were detected in the five paraphrase windows.

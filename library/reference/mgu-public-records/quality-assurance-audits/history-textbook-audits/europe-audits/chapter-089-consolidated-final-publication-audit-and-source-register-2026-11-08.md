## D. Historical correction and source verification register

1. **Greer and the geography of political sentencing.** Corrected the main narrative and quantitative exercise: Donald Greer counted 16,594 judicial death sentences, distinguishing his broader **western region** (8,674) from the **southeast** (3,158) and **Paris** (2,639); the remainder is 2,123. The western region is not the Vendée alone. Added a table with an explicit methodological warning; monthly graphs require actual monthly records, not inference from regional totals. Reference: Donald Greer, *The Incidence of the Terror during the French Revolution* (Harvard University Press, 1935), and prepublication cross-checking of scholarly summaries.
2. **22 Prairial and Paris executions.** Retained the approximately 1,376 death sentences in the defined June 10–July 27, 1794 Paris Tribunal period, distinguished from judicial sentences throughout France and deaths in civil war. Primary documentary edition: https://revolution.chnm.org/d/439/ .
3. **1799 Directors.** Corrected the chronology: Sieyès replaced Reubell in **May 1799**; in **June** Treilhard’s appointment was invalidated and Gohier replaced him, while La Révellière-Lépeaux and Merlin de Douai resigned under pressure and were replaced by Roger Ducos and Moulin. Barras continued. Context record: https://www.victorianweb.org/francais/histoire/revolution/directoire.html .
4. **Belgium and the Rhine.** Distinguished Belgium’s **October 1795 annexation** from military occupation and subsequent diplomatic settlement of much of the left bank of the Rhine. Campo Formio (1797) and Lunéville (1801) are not interchangeable dates of the initial occupations. Context: https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/treaty-of-luneville .
5. **Egypt expedition and scientific publication.** Distinguished research and collecting beginning in **1798** from publication of the *Description de l’Égypte*, whose first imperial edition began in **1809**. Library of Congress: https://www.loc.gov/item/2021666108/ .
6. **Constitutions of 1793 and 1795.** Checked adoption and non-implementation of the 1793 Constitution and the structure of Year III against the French presidency’s original documents: https://www.elysee.fr/la-presidence/la-constitution-du-24-juin-1793 and https://www.elysee.fr/la-presidence/la-constitution-du-5-fructidor-an-iii-22-aout-1795 .
7. **Colonial abolition.** Confirmed the Convention abolition decree of **February 4, 1794**, without treating implementation across all colonial sites as uniform: *Archives parlementaires*, vol. 84, p. 284, https://archives-parlementaires.persee.fr/prt/55da3c25-c332-4721-8345-471527d2338d .
8. **Louis XVI and republican transition.** The official Assemblée nationale dossier documents the separate roll calls in January 1793: https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/histoire-et-patrimoine/revolution-francaise/proces-et-execution-de-louis-xvi . The Élysée reproduces the Convention abolition of monarchy in September 1792 within its historical constitutional material.
9. **Food and pricing controls.** The General Maximum is tied to September 29, 1793 and its actual scope, including wages and specified goods, not a generic modern price ceiling: https://revolution.chnm.org/items/show/435 .
10. **Visual study.** Identified Jacques-Louis David’s Brussels original and avoided using the Louvre’s later workshop replica as an unlabeled reproduction. Museum collection record: https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010059773 .

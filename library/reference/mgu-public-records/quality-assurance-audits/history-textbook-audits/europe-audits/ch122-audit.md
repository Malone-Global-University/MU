### 2. Historical and publication corrections executed

1. **§122.18 Reims surrender:** Ivan Susloparov signed *on behalf of the Soviet High Command*; François Sevez signed as French witness. Soviet authorities later disputed Susloparov’s authorization. The NARA transcription in RG 218 explicitly distinguishes their roles: https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/surrender-of-germany
2. **§122.5 Eisenhower:** The brief public-order and contingency-note excerpts were checked against National Archives transcriptions. The contingency note was drafted June 5, 1944 but mistakenly dated “July 5” by Eisenhower. Direct document links were added in the primary-source feature and guide: https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/general-eisenhowers-order-of-the-day and https://docsteach.org/document/in-case-of-failure/ (NAID 186470).
3. **§122.2 Dresden:** Replaced unsupported narrow certainty with the 2010 Dresden historians’ commission’s conclusion of *up to approximately 25,000 killed*, with an official report linked from the guide: https://www.dresden.de/media/pdf/stadtarchiv/Historikerkommission_Dresden1945_Abschlussbericht_V1_14a.pdf. This was selectively confirmed from the previous research audit; the German report was not independently collated page-by-page in this final pass.
4. **§122.14 Vistula–Oder:** Changed a settled assertion that the offensive was advanced because of western requests to an explicitly attributed, contested postwar claim, noting prior planning and operational/weather considerations.
5. **Front chronology, Bagration:** Removed a specific counterfactual German reserve claim; now identifies major losses of Army Group Centre and constraints on movement between fronts.
6. **§122.16 Bergen-Belsen:** Replaced the unattributed “some 60,000” count and “13,000 soon after” estimate with memorial-defined figures: approximately 53,000 present at liberation and around 14,000 liberated prisoners dying by June 1945. Source https://www.bergen-belsen.de/en/history-bergen-belsen/concentration-camp.
7. **§122.19 casualty classification:** Attributed approximately 5.3 million German military deaths to Rüdiger Overmans, differentiated civilian deaths during war from flight/expulsion deaths during and after 1945, and added a specific bibliographic reference (Oldenbourg, 1999; *Beiträge zur Militärgeschichte* 46).
8. **Research project:** Added eight individually identified contemporary document assignments, including Casablanca, Tehran, Eisenhower’s two messages, Operation Neptune Report (RG 498/NAID 5821670), Yalta Protocol, and two German surrender instruments.
9. **Editorial consolidation:** Reduced repetitive narrative, retaining all twenty sections, five divisions, and feature placement. Clarified a research-project formulation concerning Eisenhower’s operational approach to Berlin.
10. **Mobile CSS:** Hid the decorative chapter numeral at ≤900px to prevent it overlapping header text. Preserved the existing static `<details>` mobile TOC, chronology-scroll container, and sidebar script.

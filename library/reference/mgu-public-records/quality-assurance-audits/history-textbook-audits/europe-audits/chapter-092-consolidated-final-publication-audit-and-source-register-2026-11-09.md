## 4. Independent historical and documentary checks

**Key chronological controls:** French Charter 4 June 1814; first Paris peace 30 May 1814; Poland–Saxony defensive agreement 3 January 1815; anti-slave-trade declaration 8 February 1815; German Federal Act 8 June 1815; Vienna Final Act 9 June 1815 (nine days before Waterloo, 18 June); second Paris peace and Quadruple Alliance 20 November 1815; occupation withdrawal by end of November 1818; Carlsbad Press Law September 1819; Troppau preliminary protocol 19 November 1820; Laibach 1821; Verona 1822. The narrative distinguishes the June 1815 territorial settlement from the November 1815 security and indemnity arrangements.

**Great-power bargaining:** Austria and Britain opposed maximal Russian and Prussian territorial demands in the Poland–Saxony crisis; France used dynastic legitimacy and the secret alliance to recover diplomatic agency. The document is treated as a specific defensive bargain, not evidence of an instantly uniform Concert of Europe.

**German Confederation:** The text identifies the post-accession standard 39 members, the confederal rather than national structure, and the differing roles of Austria, Prussia, Denmark for Holstein, and the Netherlands for Luxembourg. Do not treat a membership count as an all-purpose measure of population, direct sovereignty, or contemporaneous initial signatories.

**Poland and Saxony:** Congress Poland is explicitly distinguished from independent Polish sovereignty. Kraków’s protected free-city status and Prussian territorial acquisitions are distinguished from broad claims about national self-determination.

**Colonial policy:** The Vienna slave-trade declaration is not misrepresented as immediate universal abolition or emancipation. The chapter distinguishes the declaration from the later mechanisms of enforcement and colonial slavery.

**Alliances:** Holy Alliance (Russia, Austria, Prussia; religious-monarchical declarations) is distinguished from Quadruple Alliance (Britain plus the three powers; security and periodic consultation) and from the evolving Concert practice.

**Post-1820 intervention:** Corrected the original draft’s misleading suggestion that France increasingly dissented alongside Britain throughout 1820–1822. The revision explains that Britain opposed intervention in Spain while France raised the Spanish question at Verona and subsequently intervened in 1823. It avoids characterizing Verona as a unanimous binding authorization for all powers to invade.

**Social and institutional dimensions:** The completed narrative treats soldiers and families, property ownership, religious minorities, colonial subjects, local administration, civil codes, and constrained constitutional participation as integral parts of restoration rather than footnotes.

**Temporal boundary:** Later nineteenth-century wars and revolutions are mentioned only to evaluate the settlement’s limits; the substantive discussion ends with the 1822 diplomatic cycle. The subsequent industrialization and revolutionary chapters retain their own scope.

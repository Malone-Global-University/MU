## 3. Consolidated historical revisions applied

### A. §98.2, Cádiz Cortes and the Inquisition
The original attributed the abolition of seigneurial jurisdictions and the Inquisition directly to the Constitution of 1812. The corrected text separates that constitutional instrument from other legislation of the Cádiz Cortes; specifically, the abolition of the Inquisition was promulgated on **22 February 1813**, while the constitutional order retained Catholic exclusivity. The Liberal Triennium passage was aligned with that chronology rather than implying the measure originated in 1820–1823.

- Verification: [Archivo General de Andalucía](https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/organismos/culturapatrimoniohistoricoydeporte/areas/cultura/archivos/archivos-consejeria/archivo-general-andalucia/aga-recomienda/paginas/documento-mes-aga-sep-2012.html).

### B. §98.10, France's electoral decree
The incorrect **2 March 1848** date was replaced by the **5 March 1848** decree introducing direct universal male suffrage for elections to the Constituent Assembly. The electorate is described as **more than eight million eligible men**, rather than the imprecise rounded figure of nine million. The February political proclamation of the republic is distinguished from the Constituent Assembly's formal proclamation on **4 May**.

- Verification: [French National Assembly, provisional government](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/histoire-et-patrimoine/deuxieme-republique/gouvernement-provisoire-et-proclamation-de-la-deuxieme-republique).

### C. §98.11, June Days transportation counts
The original sentence conflated people **ordered transported** with people **actually deported**. The corrected text distinguishes the **4,276 transportation orders** given by military commissions from approximately **459 people subsequently transported to Algeria in 1850**, after significant clemency; some were later transferred to French Guiana. Harsh military repression and the enormous detention campaign remain fully described. Numeric categories are explicitly defined to avoid a misleading claim that more than four thousand were shipped overseas.

- Verification: [Jean-Claude Farcy and Rosine Fry, University of Burgundy historical prosopographic database](https://inculpes-juin-1848.ube.fr/index.php?page=presentation/historique).

### D. §98.13, Frankfurt Fundamental Rights primary source
The historical legislation is now titled an **Imperial Act concerning the Fundamental Rights of the German People**. Dates are separated precisely: **adopted by the Assembly 21 December 1848, promulgated 27 December, effective 28 December**, subsequently incorporated in the March 1849 imperial constitution. The paraphrase now describes judicial independence and safeguards rather than the inaccurate assertion that the act restricted the authority of courts. The student source study links to the Bundestag account of the German primary law.

- Verification: [German Bundestag, Fundamental Rights, 21 December 1848](https://www.bundestag.de/dokumente/textarchiv/1848-12-21-grundrechte-584354).

### E. §98.17, Swiss chronology and Wallachian calendar
The Sonderbund's **1845** formation replaces the ambiguous "1845 and 1846"; the description of its **November 1847** defeat no longer suggests anachronistically that Europe's spring 1848 revolutions were already keeping the great powers occupied. The Islaz proclamation is dated **9 June Julian / 21 June Gregorian 1848**. The Decembrists' dual-calendar date remains correctly distinguished.

- Verification: [Swiss historical account](https://www.myswitzerland.com/en-us/planning/about-switzerland/history-of-switzerland/the-restoration-and-sonderbund-war/); [Cambridge, Wallachian Revolution of 1848](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/historical-journal/article/locating-the-wallachian-revolution-of-1848/6B438854E4C4A26F97A386CFC35934FE).

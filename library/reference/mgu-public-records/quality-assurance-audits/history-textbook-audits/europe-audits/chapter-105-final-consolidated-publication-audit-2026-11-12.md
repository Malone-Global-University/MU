## Corrections and historical verification checkpoints

1. **Queen Victoria’s imperial title (105.4; chronology):** distinguished the statutory authorization in 1876 from the January 1877 Delhi proclamation. Historic Royal Palaces and contemporary Imperial Gazetteer descriptions support the distinction.
2. **Laos and Indochina (105.8; chronology):** replaced the incorrect 1899 incorporation date with French territorial acquisition and integration in 1893; distinguished earlier creation of the Indochinese Union in 1887. See Martin Stuart-Fox, *Modern Asian Studies*.
3. **Anglo-Russian Convention (105.13; chronology):** identified the 1907 agreement as involving Persia, Afghanistan, and Tibet, rather than falsely dividing the whole of Central Asia by treaty; used British Library record and *Encyclopaedia Iranica*.
4. **New Zealand Wars (105.15):** identified conflicts from the 1840s through the early 1870s, with disputes over Treaty texts, land, and Crown authority; distinguished Māori and English wordings. See Te Ara and Waitangi Tribunal.
5. **Australia (105.15):** explained settler denials of Aboriginal land rights and the legislative mechanics of the Immigration Restriction Act of 1901 without treating *terra nullius* as an accurate description of Indigenous political society. See National Archives of Australia.
6. **Indenture (105.17):** distinguished the 1833 abolition legislation, emancipation in 1834, and the ending of apprenticeship in 1838; differentiated indenture from slavery and voluntary migration. See Cambridge scholarship on indenture.
7. **Philippines (105.10; source feature):** distinguished the Filipino independence declaration in June 1898, the December 1898 treaty between Spain and the United States, the February 1899 outbreak of war, and later resistance. Compare Philippine NCCA and U.S. Office of the Historian.
8. **Aceh (105.9):** distinguished 1873 war commencement and prolonged local resistance from subsequent Dutch administrative claims. See Antje Missbach, 2010.
9. **Opening image:** verified Aubrey Kellaway’s 1911 Delhi Durbar panorama, Royal Collection Trust RCIN 2935183; linked to source without embedding and without asserting reproduction permission.
10. **Conceptual discipline:** distinguished formal versus informal empire; protectorate versus colony; foreign concession versus treaty port versus leased territory; Asian sovereignty versus European foreign privileges; settler political representation versus Indigenous exclusion.

## Disclosed limitations

- **Selective historical source verification:** The main regional chronology, named documents, major sovereignty distinctions, and previously flagged factual corrections were checked against suitable institutional or scholarly sources. This is not an exhaustive item-by-item archival, bibliographical, or specialist peer review of all 14,000 narrative words.
- **Source windows:** The 1858 proclamation window and Philippine perspectives are attributed editorial source studies; they are not presented as page-collated facsimiles, certified translations, or verbatim archival quotations.
- **Historical maps:** The map study is a text-guided exercise and requires separate period-accurate atlas plates. No historical map is embedded.
- **Image rights:** The 1911 Durbar photograph is linked to the Royal Collection Trust, not reproduced; external permission and licensing remain subject to review.
- **Template parity:** Chapter 104’s established style system and sidebar behavior were used as the nearest available HTML model. Byte-for-byte comparison with the actual Chapter 2 model remains unverified because that source file was not provided in this pass.
- **Deployment:** No live website testing, CSP validation, or server-side integration was performed.

## Historical verification checkpoints and corrections from production plan

1. **France 1875**: Three constitutional laws; the president was elected by houses of parliament rather than the national electorate. Confirmed against the Élysée’s official historical publication.
2. **British parliamentary crisis**: The 1911 Act constrained rather than abolished the Lords' legislative veto. The 1914 Government of Ireland Act received assent but its operation was suspended at once. Confirmed against UK Parliament.
3. **German institutional order**: Equal direct male Reichstag franchise distinguished from the Prussian three-class system; the chancellor remained answerable to the Kaiser rather than a Reichstag majority. Confirmed against German Bundestag.
4. **German Zabern Affair**: The 1913 no-confidence vote did not compel the dismissal of the chancellor. Confirmed against Deutsches Historisches Museum.
5. **Austrian 1907 suffrage**: Equal universal male voting for the Austrian Reichsrat; it did not democratize Hungary’s parliamentary franchise. Confirmed against Austrian Parliament. Restricted participation by women is expressly retained in the text.
6. **Dual Monarchy territorial status**: Separate Austrian and Hungarian constitutions and the distinct common administration of Bosnia-Herzegovina after 1878, annexed in 1908; the international crisis is reserved for Chapter 107.
7. **Russia 1905 calendar**: Bloody Sunday 9 January Old Style / 22 January New Style; October Manifesto 17 October Old Style / 30 October New Style. Articles 4, 7, 9, 17 and 86 of the 1906 Fundamental Laws distinguished. Checked against Russian text and Robinson–Beard excerpt; Article 87 is summarized as emergency authority, not quoted from memory.
8. **Ottoman constitutional chronology**: 1876 promulgation, 1877 parliament, 1878 suspension, 1908 restoration, 1909 amendments, and 1913 CUP seizure of power kept distinct. Confirmed against Türkiye’s parliament and scholarly historical analysis.
9. **Nationality and language**: Locations, jurisdictions, community identities, and administration differentiated rather than mapped onto later nation-states. Different political demands are not treated as ethnic unanimity.
10. **Non-teleological ending**: Neither constitutional instability nor nationality disputes are claimed to make the First World War inevitable; international decision-making belongs to Chapter 107.

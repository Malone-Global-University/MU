## Historical corrections and expanded analysis

1. **Second Ypres (§109.6):** April 22, 1915 initial mass chlorine release is assigned to French Territorial and Algerian sectors, while the April 24 direct attack on Canadian positions is distinguished. Confirmed against Parks Canada and Canadian government military history: https://www.canada.ca/en/parks-canada/news/2017/11/the_second_battleofypres22april-25may1915.html . The account does not claim this was the first-ever chemical weapon use.
2. **Gallipoli (§109.14):** Newfoundland troops did **not** participate in the April 25 landings; their arrival at Suvla on **September 20, 1915** is stated distinctly. Confirmed in the Newfoundland Regiment museum's service chronology, https://rnfldrmuseum.ca/history/regimental-history/first-world-war-1914-1918/gallipoli-1915/ , and Canadian defence material. The naval attempt, April landings, August offensives and January 9, 1916 evacuation remain separate events.
3. **Somme (§109.8):** Approximately 57,000 British first-day casualties and about 19,000 killed are explicitly differentiated; removed the unsupported claim that these losses were mostly concentrated in Pals or Territorial units. The chapter is careful about British, French and German aggregates and conflicting calculations. National Army Museum Eric Hall materials give documentary specificity: https://ww1.nam.ac.uk/stories/second-lieutenant-eric-hall/ .
4. **Verdun (§109.7):** Falkenhayn's postwar 'bleed France white' explanation is explicitly contested as to original intent; forts, artillery, roads, division rotation, changing commanders and French/German losses are developed. A c.700,000 combined casualties figure is identified as indicative, **not deaths**.
5. **1914 Western Front (§§109.2–109.3):** Liège and Belgian resistance, Mons and Le Cateau, the Marne counterattack, German withdrawal, Aisne lines, Yser inundations and First Ypres are differentiated by sequence. German atrocities against civilians are identified without treating them as ordinary combat deaths.
6. **Eastern Front (§§109.4, 109.9–109.12):** Tannenberg is distinguished from Russian success in Galicia; Gorlice–Tarnów begins May 2, 1915; the Russian retreat did not eliminate its army; Brusilov began June 4, 1916; Romania continued fighting after Bucharest fell; December 1917 armistice and March 3, 1918 Brest-Litovsk are distinguished. The Armenian genocide is named correctly as genocidal state persecution, not a routine combat loss.
7. **Southern fronts (§§109.13–109.16):** Serbian successes in 1914 versus the coordinated 1915 invasion, subsequent retreat/evacuation and occupation, the autonomous strategic role of Salonika, Greek neutrality and the National Schism, and Piave recovery after Caporetto receive substantive development. Naval, disease and mountain logistical constraints are treated as causes, not decorative context.
8. **1917–1918 (§§109.17–109.20):** Nivelle, Arras, Messines, Passchendaele and Cambrai are differentiated; military indiscipline is not equated with refusal to defend the country. Operation Michael and later German offensives are separate from the Allied Hundred Days; no claim that tanks, the American army, or one battle alone caused German defeat. The politics of imperial collapse and armistices remain reserved for Chapter 112.

## Documentary features and evidentiary discipline

- **Opening photograph:** Imperial War Museums object **205024527**, Indian Corps pack transport, October 1914; archived photograph linked for comparison, **not embedded**. The exercise separates visible observation, archival attribution and inference about imperial supply.
- **Somme Primary Source Window (§109.8):** dated October 1916 diary study of Second Lieutenant **Eric Peace Hall**, 2nd Battalion Hampshire Regiment, National Army Museum diary **2000-01-32-1**. The instructional prose is explicitly an editorial paraphrase of identified museum-published extracts, not a fabricated quotation or full facsimile transcription. Its source limits and corroboration questions are disclosed.
- **Everyday Life (§109.10):** non-fictional comparative examination of a frontline soldier, a prisoner and an occupied household, using identified types of records. Does not invent named witnesses or first-person quotations.
- **Gallipoli Multiple Perspectives (§109.14):** named, differentiated evidentiary studies: Ottoman lieutenant **Mehmed Fasih**, diary **December 13, 1915**; Australian lieutenant **William Cameron**, diary May–August 1915, AWM **1DRL/0185**; **Leonard Bullwinkle**, August 1–3, 1915 excerpt in National Army Museum publication; and institutionally documented Indian 29th Brigade contribution. The Ottoman extract's printed edition citation is recorded and was cross-checked against the National WWI Museum sourcebook PDF, printed page 14. No claim of independent original Ottoman manuscript collation is made.
- **Geography (§109.1):** demands dated 1914/1916/1918 maps and cross-theater spatial interpretation. Map plates are not embedded; students need an external historical atlas.
- **Parallel Timeline (§109.19):** 12 dated checkpoints and four independent theater columns. The annotations explicitly warn that an unlisted major operation does not imply an inactive front.
- **History Debate (§109.20):** offers complementary, source-testable military, logistical, command and resource explanations without false mutually exclusive interpretations.

## Source limitations and boundaries

1. This is a **first-edition scholarly text** with verified representative documentary anchors, not exhaustive reconstruction from every wartime army archive or original-language collection. Many broad operational interpretations draw on museum institutional material and the supplied specialist plan; full archival collation across German, French, Russian, Ottoman, Austro-Hungarian and Italian records was not performed.
2. The National Army Museum's displayed Eric Hall selections and educational use of Mehmed Fasih's translated 1997 edition were treated as **curated published evidence**, not unmediated full manuscript transcriptions. Australian War Memorial descriptions of William Cameron's diary are archive catalog metadata; full handwritten diary was not independently page-collated.
3. Opening image not embedded pending reproduction/permissions decision; atlas plates not embedded. Source URLs are for scholarly retrieval and must be checked before live publication for accessibility and lasting availability.
4. Casualty estimates are qualified by category, scope and uncertainty and are not represented as exact universal totals. Comparisons of cause and motive are attributed where historiographically contested.
5. Design follows the accepted **Chapter 108** styling and tested navigation conventions. Byte-identical parity to an original Chapter 2 master template was **not independently verified**. No live MGU server deployment or CMS integration was tested.
6. Chapter boundaries: Chapter 110 retains home fronts, industrial and food mobilization; Chapter 111 retains the revolutions and Brest-Litovsk political treatment; Chapter 112 retains state collapse and armistice negotiations; Chapter 113 retains the peace settlement.

### 3. Historical, source, and mathematical corrections
- Kepler’s third law is stated with orbital **semimajor axis**. The modern teaching table distinguishes rounded orbital inputs from historical data. From the displayed Jupiter value 5.203 AU, **5.203³ = 140.851500427, rounded 140.9**, not 140.8. The period column is 11.86² = 140.6596, rounded 140.7; small differences result from displayed precision and the model’s approximation. The Saturn, Mars, and Earth columns recalculate correctly.
- Calendar conventions no longer presume that the entire era used Gregorian dates. An Old Style/New Style statement is included, and the English founding-day date of the Royal Society and Newton’s birth are distinguished where needed.
- Vesalius’s change between the 1543 and 1555 treatments of cardiac septum pores is explained by edition, avoiding a false fixed opinion.
- Galileo’s *Sidereus Nuncius* is described with the distinction between etched lunar views and schematic printed Jupiter-position diagrams; Venus and sunspots are correctly presented as later observations rather than contents of the 1610 book.
- Newton’s *Principia* is distinguished across its 1687, 1713, and 1726 editions; the General Scholium is attributed to 1713, not 1687. Newton’s optics, non-scientific intellectual commitments, and disputes with Leibniz are treated without retrospective flattening.
- The Roman actions of 1616 and Galileo’s separate 1633 trial are distinguished, including the documentary uncertainty over the earlier injunction and the continuing empirical viability of Tychonic arrangements at the time.
- Non-European contributions and colonial knowledge are incorporated with explicit caution about evidence for individual transmission and informal labor.

## 3. Historical corrections, document verification, and evidence limits

1. **Sétif, Guelma, and Kherrata, May 1945:** Replaced the simplistic juxtaposition of official 1,500 and commemorative 45,000 deaths with explicit discussion of competing scholarly estimates, including roughly 6,000–8,000 and 15,000–20,000, alongside the commemorative claim. Historians cannot reconstruct a universally accepted precise total. Source: Sciences Po, *Setif and Guelma (May 1945)*, https://www.sciencespo.fr/mass-violence-war-massacre-resistance/en/document/setif-and-guelma-may-1945.html.
2. **Algerian War fatalities:** Distinguished scholarly estimates of approximately 300,000 and 400,000–600,000 Algerian war-related deaths from the Algerian government's commemorative claim of 1.5 million. Clarified differences in categories, including civilian and indirect deaths. France's Defense Ministry reports approximately 25,000 French military deaths, including roughly 8,000 accident or illness deaths. Source: https://www.defense.gouv.fr/de/node/22887 and Kamel Kateb (2001) as discussed in published scholarship.
3. **Harki reprisal killings:** Explicitly identified the widely divergent published estimates of approximately 10,000–150,000 as contested estimates, not a census. Source: Martin Evans (2017), https://doi.org/10.1080/07075332.2016.1141307.
4. **Portuguese retornados:** Qualified the 500,000–800,000 estimate as principally departures from African settlements over **1974–1979**, distinct from Portugal's **1981 census figure of 471,427** arrivals from former colonies. Source: Elsa Peralta (2019), https://www.scielo.pt/scielo.php?pid=S0003-25732019000200004&script=sci_arttext.
5. **Suriname–Netherlands migration:** Corrected the unsupported approximately 100,000 by 1980 population stock to approximately **165,000 Surinamese-origin people in the Netherlands by early 1981**, with source series indicating fewer than 30,000 in 1970. Distinguished stock from annual gross arrivals, including approximately 39,700 in 1975 and 18,990 in 1980. Source: Statistics Netherlands (CBS), https://www.cbs.nl/en-gb/news/2025/47/over-250-000-surinamese-have-settled-in-the-netherlands-since-independence.
6. **France's Algerian census:** Specified nationality-based counts of **211,675 in 1954**, **350,484 in 1962**, and **710,690 in 1975**, with explicit category limitations. Sources: Raphaël-Emmanuel Verhaeren, https://www.persee.fr/doc/rint_0294-3069_1996_num_43_1_2170; French National Museum of Immigration, https://www.histoire-immigration.fr/en/migration-characteristics-by-country-of-origin/algerian-immigration-into-france.
7. **1948 Windrush arrival:** Distinguished the ship's late arrival on **June 21, 1948** from passenger disembarkation on **June 22**; documented **1,027 people in the National Archives passenger list**. The figure of 802 refers to Caribbean last residence, not necessarily a count of passengers who boarded at one location. Source: https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/postwar-caribbean-migration/.
8. **Dutch apology for violence in Indonesia:** Confirmed Prime Minister Mark Rutte's government apology on **February 17, 2022**, distinct from King Willem-Alexander's 2020 statement of regret. Source: contemporaneous account https://nltimes.nl/2022/02/17/rutte-sincere-apologies-indonesia-violence-penetrating-decolonization-report.
9. **Race Relations Act sponsorship:** Identified **Home Secretary Sir Frank Soskice** as the presenter of the 1965 government bill and speaker moving second reading, April and May 1965 respectively; other Acts were not assigned unverified individual sponsors. Source: Hansard, https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1965-05-03/debates/28e9ee04-70cb-4d4c-b514-c681106cd10f/RaceRelationsBill.
10. **Portugal's August 1975 United Nations document:** Completely rebuilt the generic source window using **A/10207–S/11811**, letter from foreign minister Mário Ruivo dated August 21 and circulated August 22. Its paragraphs acknowledge Portuguese recognition of Guinea-Bissau in September 1974, enumerate the June–July 1975 independence dates, and describe the breakdown of the Angola transition and emergency evacuation measures. The letter's predicted 1978 Timor independence is identified as a historically overtaken official forecast, not adopted as a fact. Source: https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/447673 and document https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/447673/files/A_10207_S_11811-EN.pdf?ln=en.

## 6. Limitations and publication controls

- Historical maps remain text-guided studies; no independently cleared historical map images are embedded.
- The Windrush image is not embedded because image rights were not established; a real archival passenger list is linked.
- Eight source windows include accessible document provenance and explanatory paraphrase, but archival transcription or translation has not been exhaustively checked across every source. In particular, readers should refer to linked official texts for exact statutory wording.
- Casualty and displaced-person figures with contested evidence remain expressly identified as estimates, not closed totals.
- Publication review was of the static chapter HTML. Live deployment, any production-site routing, and pixel-identical parity with the original Chapter 2 stylesheet were **not** independently verified.
- Chapter previous/next navigation was not invented. Existing internal anchor navigation is functional.

## 4. Historical verification and targeted corrections
- **Unigenitus (1713):** Clement XI condemned 101 propositions associated with Pasquier Quesnel. Source: https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15128a.htm . The paraphrase distinguishes papal position from the responses of clergy and laypeople.
- **Wesley (1749):** A Plain Account of the People Called Methodists was written in 1748 and appears in an identifiable 1749 printed edition; not conflated with the year of original composition. British Library-origin digitization: https://books.google.com/books?id=BxdhAAAAcAAJ .
- **Mendelssohn (1783):** Jerusalem, with religion/state-power context and a source passage: https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/the-holy-roman-empire-1648-1815/moses-mendelssohn-jerusalem-or-on-religious-power-and-judaism-1783 .
- **Mary Astell (1700):** original Some Reflections upon Marriage first edition identified and distinguished from later printings. Text: https://digital.library.upenn.edu/women/astell/marriage/marriage.html .
- **Mary Wollstonecraft (1792):** original London edition of Vindication; 1792 is a disclosed selective chronology extension, not incorrectly attributed to the 1780s. New York Public Library first edition: https://old-digitalcollections.nypl.org/collections/a-vindication-of-the-rights-of-woman-with-strictures-on-political-and-moral .
- **Marriage Act of 1753/1754:** explicitly limited to England and Wales, with Scotland separate; Quaker and Jewish exceptions identified. UK Parliament: https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/private-lives/relationships/overview/lawofmarriage-/ .
- **Foundling Hospital:** royal charter in 1739, first 30 children admitted 25 March 1741; admissions tokens linked to a museum collection rather than invented individuals. https://foundlingmuseum.org.uk/our-story/history/ ; https://foundlingmuseum.org.uk/our-art-and-objects/foundling-collections/tokens/ .
- **Elizabeth Nihell (1760):** A Treatise on the Art of Midwifery identified as a professional polemic and verified in Wellcome Collection: https://wellcomecollection.org/works/mue383kq .
- **Hogarth (1751):** Gin Lane identified as persuasive engraved satire, National Gallery of Art accession 1944.5.87: https://www.nga.gov/artworks/30433-gin-lane . It is not described as a documentary photograph or empirical survey.
- **Ignatius Sancho (1782):** posthumous Letters, with the London Museum book object 2002.127: https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/v/object-720873/letters-of-the-late-ignatius-sancho-an-african/ .
- **Anna Göldi (1782):** corrected/qualified as a case involving allegations associated with witchcraft and a complex formal legal framing; the misleading simple label of Europe’s unequivocally final witchcraft trial is avoided.
- **Comparative law:** explicitly avoids describing English coverture as universally European; compares Scottish, French, Italian, Iberian and German property arrangements.
- **Historical family interpretation:** avoids the obsolete unsupported claim that affection or childhood itself originated in the eighteenth century. Printed prescriptions are distinguished from actual experience.
- **Navigation:** Chapter 85 filename verified locally, while unverified Chapter 87 destination was converted to non-clickable next-chapter text, rather than inventing a live deployed URL.

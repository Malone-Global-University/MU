## Historical and evidentiary corrections

1. Separated Bretton Woods planning in July 1944 from the later formal establishment of the IMF and World Bank.
2. Corrected the first French Monnet Plan's preparation/commissioning (1946) versus government adoption (early 1947).
3. Clarified that Marshall assistance totaling approximately $13.3 billion is based on a defined USAID-derived 1948–June 1952 accounting series; total **$13,325.8 million** in the cited CRS table.
4. Corrected recipient ranking: United Kingdom, France, Italy, then western Germany in that aid series; added a 16-row destination-and-regional allocation table whose values sum exactly to $13,325.8 million.
5. Distinguished the end of the Economic Cooperation Administration in 1951 from the conventionally reported aid accounting through June 1952.
6. Confirmed the OEEC convention was signed April 16, 1948 (initially provisionally in force), and the EPU agreement signed September 19, 1950; explained multilateral payments and BIS administration.
7. Specified the quoted **4.6%** as annual *aggregate real GDP growth* for a twelve-economy western European sample, not GDP per capita or every country's experience; included 3.8% real GDP-per-person growth in the corresponding comparison.
8. Added a quantified 1950/1973 real GDP-per-person table for Britain, France, West Germany, Italy, Spain, and Greece, attributed to Nicholas Crafts’s 2010 table drawing on Maddison (2003); 1990 international Geary–Khamis dollar units indicated.
9. Clarified NHS charging chronology: dentures and spectacles in May 1951; prescriptions and certain dental treatment in June 1952.
10. Corrected Portugal's position: Marshall Plan economic participation did not imply democratic government under the Estado Novo.
11. Corrected the Marshall aid map-study prompt's conflation of OEEC participation and recorded disbursements, including Switzerland's status and Spain's exclusion.
12. Removed the unsupported categorical claim that internal regional gaps generally exceeded gaps between countries.
13. Identified an actual Marshall Plan poster, Reijn Dirksen’s *All Our Colours to the Mast*, and linked to its Foundation archival catalogue; distinct from a fabricated or uncaptioned generic poster.
14. Replaced unspecified primary-document categories with identified document/repository access and source-use limitations in all six windows; EPU and OEEC source windows now explain specific institutional commitments without inventing verbatim quotations.

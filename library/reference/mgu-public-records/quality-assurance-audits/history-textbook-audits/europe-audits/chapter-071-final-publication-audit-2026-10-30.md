## Specialist historical checks

1. **Afonso de Albuquerque:** A letter to King Manuel dated 1 April 1512 at Cochin is independently attested. His correspondence is collected in *Cartas de Affonso de Albuquerque*, edited/directed by Raimundo António de Bulhão Pato, continued by Henrique Lopes de Mendonça, Lisbon, 1884–1935. The window correctly calls its discussion a *composite paraphrase* and does not claim verbatim quotation. Source: Portuguese Imprensa Nacional catalog, https://imprensanacional.pt/?w3n_bib_digital=cartas-de-affonso-de-albuquerque ; H. Morse Stephens, *Albuquerque* (1892), https://mybookx.com/h-morse-stephens/rulers-of-india-albuquerque/34838 . The exact edition page/folio of each paraphrased theme has not been independently checked, so the text makes no folio claims.
2. **Colenbrander, Coen:** *Jan Pietersz. Coen: Bescheiden omtrent zijn bedrijf in Indië*, 1919–1953, is a real multi-volume edition; the exact volume and folio for each Banda sentence are not established. The window now accurately presents a synthesis of VOC records with modern scholarly interpretation distinguished. Source: WorldCat, https://search.worldcat.org/title/54321320 and Corts Foundation, https://cortsfoundation.org/digitheek-en .
3. **VOC archive:** Nationaal Archief inventory `1.04.02` indeed identifies the historical Dutch East India Company archive and contains extensive Asian correspondence and company papers. Source: https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/1.04.02 .
4. **Makassar:** The 1667 Treaty of Bungaya did NOT conclude the entire war: fighting continued and VOC/Arung Palakka forces captured Somba Opu in 1669. The expanded §71.22 had already corrected this, but the chronology table inadvertently retained the old mistake. Repaired. Source: ANU Press, https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/n7094/html/ch07.xhtml .
5. **Spanish presence in Moluccas:** Spanish activity following the 1521 voyage and increasingly from the Philippines is historical; durable Spanish fortifications in Ternate/Tidore especially after 1606, lasting until withdrawal in 1663, are independently corroborated. Sources: https://www.defesa.gov.pt/pt/defesa/organizacao/comissoes/cphm/rphm/edicoes/ano3/n.5/5 ; scholarly *The Islands Beyond the Empire*, https://run.unl.pt/bitstream/10362/160003/1/The_Islands_Beyond_the_Empire.pdf (source identified through search metadata, not independently inspected page by page).
6. **Gujarat and Diu:** Bahadur Shah's 1534 concession of Bassein was distinct from authorization and construction of the Diu fortress in 1535; the main narrative reflects this distinction. Source: https://www.cyberlibrary.org/en/books/history-of-gujarat-gazetteer-of-the-bombay-presidency-volume-i-deabd6e2/chapters/20/ .
7. **Dutch Taiwan:** Dutch policy encouraged Chinese immigration and expanded rice and sugar cultivation; Cambridge scholarship supports substantial Chinese population by the late 1640s and the 1650s. No inflated exact population inserted. Source: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/itinerario/article/abs/how-the-dutch-ran-a-seventeenthcentury-colony-the-occupation-and-loss-of-formosa-16241662/A7634E0884D709477D0AE17DC9F17BFA .
8. **Cape land tenure:** 1657 free burghers and the settlement expansion are supported. The formalized eighteenth-century loan-place arrangement dates specifically to 1714, rather than the initial 1650s settlement. Clarified. Sources: https://sahistory.org.za/classroom/grade-7/colonisation-cape-17th-and-18th-centuries ; https://scielo.org.za/scielo.php?pid=S1021-545X2019000100005&script=sci_arttext .
9. **Ambon clove extirpation:** VOC *hongi* expeditions and deliberate destruction of clove trees outside permitted districts are well documented. Narrative describes regional and chronological developments without imposing a single founding date. Sources: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/modern-asian-studies/article/abs/armed-incidents-and-unpaid-bills-anglodutch-rivalry-in-the-banda-islands-in-the-seventeenth-century/D760CEE358E2FB1629EEBC2A1D9476D7 ; https://pala.westfriesmuseum.nl/1621-2/1621-article/?lang=en .
10. **VOC enslavement estimate:** 660,000–1,135,000 arrivals/shipments to VOC areas is a scholarly reconstructed range, and must not be equated with slaves transported *by company vessels alone*. The revised quantitative table preserves this distinction. Sources: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-global-history/article/towards-a-global-perspective-on-early-modern-slave-trade-prices-of-the-enslaved-in-the-indian-ocean-indonesian-archipelago-and-atlantic-worlds/F3BF7F3CF08AA23010922EB42F3DAAFA ; https://www.ascleiden.nl/content/webdossiers/indian-ocean-slave-trade .
11. **Tokugawa orders:** Restrictions culminating in the 1635 prohibition on Japanese overseas movement, 1639 exclusion of Portuguese, and 1641 relocation of the Dutch to Dejima are distinct acts. The source window explicitly identifies a composite, not a single edict. Source: https://nagasakidejima.jp/english/history/ .
12. **English Fort St George:** The 1639 land grant at Madras preceded the English settlement and construction of Fort St George from 1640. The latter date is corrected in the comparative English section. Sources: https://interactive.britishart.yale.edu/british-marine-painting/1951/fort-st-george ; https://www.indiancommunities.org/chennai-unit-1-early-madras/ .

## 4. Quantitative History Study: corrections and evidence

The draft's most important substantive weakness was an improperly scoped Austrian population estimate (8–10 million). Estimates of approximately **12.7 million** for the combined Austrian, Bohemian and Hungarian lands around 1740 have been reported from P. G. M. Dickson, *Finance and Government under Maria Theresa*, vol. I, table 2.5; wider Habsburg possessions make comparisons still more definition-dependent. The quantitative table and ratio exercise were revised to specify this territorial scope.

| Comparison | Final study treatment | Source/qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Brandenburg-Prussian army, 1688 | About 30,000 | Fiscal-military scholarship; dependent on counting conventions |
| Brandenburg-Prussian army, 1713 | About 38,000 | Same |
| Prussian army, 1740 | Approximately **72,000–80,000** | Alternative published estimates; avoid treating one definition as uncontested |
| Prussian population, 1740 | About **2.2 million** (pre-Silesia) | Historical population reconstruction |
| Prussian annual net revenue, c. 1740 | About **7.4 million thalers** | *The Rise of Fiscal States*, chap. on Prussian finance; corroborated by German historical fiscal research |
| Habsburg army near 1740 | Authorized establishment **around 160,000**, effective strength after Ottoman campaigns **around 108,000** (subsequently variable) | Christopher Duffy, *The Army of Maria Theresa* (1977), at/around p. 145 |
| Habsburg population, c. 1740 | **c. 12.7 million** for Austrian, Bohemian and Hungarian core | Dickson's population estimate; **not** an exact total for all overseas/Italian/Netherlandish territories |
| Habsburg annual revenue, c. 1740 | **c. 20 million florins**, with a subsequent policy target toward 40 million | Duffy, *The Army of Maria Theresa*; Peter Rauscher on Habsburg military contributions |
| Relative army burden | Prussia ~3.3–3.6 soldiers per 100 inhabitants; Austrian illustrative ~0.85, **subject to incompatible military and territorial coverage** | Classroom comparative illustration only; not an exact standardized cross-state statistic |

Additional scholarly source material:
- *The Rise of Fiscal States: A Global History, 1500–1914*, edited by Francisco Comín Comín et al., historical table of Prussian population, army and revenue, 1688–1806.
- Dietmar Stutzer, specialist German-language fiscal-military history of Brandenburg-Prussia.
- Christopher Duffy, *The Army of Maria Theresa: The Armed Forces of Imperial Austria, 1740–1780* (1977).
- P. G. M. Dickson, *Finance and Government under Maria Theresa, 1740–1780*, 2 volumes (1987), especially volume I, table 2.5.
- Peter Rauscher, “The Evolution of the Fiscal-Military State in the Habsburg Monarchy, 1500–1750”.

All original tables and questions have been preserved, but the quantitative categories are no longer presented as directly exchangeable currencies or uniform army establishments.

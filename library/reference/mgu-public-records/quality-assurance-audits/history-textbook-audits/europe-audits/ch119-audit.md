## Historical accuracy, source and narrative corrections
1. **Soviet military ranks (§119.16).** Corrected the inaccurate claim that 1943 restored officer ranks. Personal ranks were established in 1935, senior general ranks reorganized in 1940, and 1943 regulations restored shoulder-board insignia. Grounding: U.S. military uniform intelligence historical summaries and the 1943 Order No. 25 context. No inference that insignia by themselves won battles.
2. **Soviet evacuation (§119.7).** Replaced the unqualified 10–16 million total with the dated and defined **at least 17 million people evacuated by late 1942**, attributed to Wendy Z. Goldman and Donald Filtzer, *Fortress Dark and Stern* (2021), ch. 1 (Oxford University Press). Explicitly acknowledges discrepancies from narrower organized or industrial evacuation counts. Source: https://academic.oup.com/book/39546/chapter-abstract/339401479
3. **Kyiv prisoners (§119.4).** Clarified that German wartime announcements around 665,000 and lower Soviet-based studies refer to different measurement boundaries and categories; neither is a clean paired range.
4. **Leningrad (§119.6).** Preserved September 8, 1941 blockade, January 18, 1943 land-corridor breach, and January 27, 1944 full lifting. Explicitly distinguishes registered city starvation/malnutrition deaths, deaths of evacuees, and military losses. Uses approximately 630,000 as a period official benchmark without treating it as a total for every counting definition.
5. **German criminal directives (§119.9).** Distinguished the May 13 Jurisdiction Order (Nuremberg C-50) and June 6 Commissar Order (NOKW-1076); corrected the source-window paraphrase of the latter to distinguish execution of captured commissars from transfer of rear-area detainees to SS/SD task forces. Original/translation text: https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/nazi-germany-1933-1945/directives-for-the-treatment-of-political-commissars-commissar-order-june-6-1941
6. **Soviet POWs and Babyn Yar (§119.10).** Deaths of approximately 3.3 million Soviet POWs are explicitly an all-war count; the perpetrators' reported 33,771 Jewish victims at Babyn Yar are identified as the September 29–30, 1941 two-day count, not the complete site total. Sources: https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/nazi-persecution-of-soviet-prisoners-of-war and https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/kiev-and-babi-yar
7. **Stalingrad, Kursk, Bagration (§§119.13–119.20).** Expanded Case Blue's logistical risks; separated urban battle, Uranus, German relief failure, and February 2 surrender; maintained scholarly rejection of simplistic Prokhorovka 'largest tank battle' claims; defined Bagration casualty estimates cautiously and distinguished competing accounting periods. Source: https://tankmuseum.org/article/prokhorovka-myth-largest-tank-battle
8. **Barbarossa timetable.** Qualifies the claim that the April 1941 Balkan campaigns alone delayed the invasion, distinguishing deployment effects from readiness/weather and historical disagreement.
9. **Source apparatus.** Retains 24 entries, adds identifiable primary-document translations and Nuremberg exhibit codes, active external source links and transparent scholarly metadata limits. The research project explicitly selects **eight contemporary documents** and requires **four scholarly books**. The Commissar Order text window remains labeled an editorial paraphrase, **not** a manuscript-collated translation or a purported facsimile.

## 2. Historical accuracy and documentary corrections

### Naval refusal and Kiel revolution

- Distinguished the initial **October 29–30 fleet refusal near Wilhelmshaven** from the **November 3–4 uprising at Kiel**, where sailors and workers organized councils.
- Linked the Deutsches Historisches Museum's specific **November 5, 1918 photograph** of Kiel submarine-harbor sailors, inventory BA 90/5439, with full institutional context and a student visual-source exercise rather than an unlicensed embedded image.
- Checked against the Wilhelmshaven city historical information route, the German Historical Museum's 1918 chronology and object catalog, and the U.S. Holocaust Memorial Museum historical account:
  - https://www.wilhelmshaven.de/revolution1918/index_EN.php
  - https://www.dhm.de/lemo/bestand/objekt/revolutionaere-matrosen-in-kiel-1918
  - https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/timeline-event/holocaust/before-1933/mutiny-of-german-sailors-in-kiel

### Armistice sequence and operative terms

- **Bulgaria:** Salonika convention **September 29**; distinguishes Ferdinand's early-October succession from the published **October 4** dating of his abdication declaration. No fictitious second abdication inferred. Checked against Library of Congress comparative terms and the published royal declaration transcription.
  - https://www.loc.gov/collections/world-war-i-rotogravures/articles-and-essays/events-and-statistics/armistice-terms/
  - https://store.tax.firstworldwar.com/source/abdication_ferdinand.htm (secondary transcription of a published collection; not original Bulgarian facsimile)
- **Ottoman Empire:** Mudros signed **October 30**, cease-fire **noon October 31** (Article XXV). Distinguishes the opening of Straits and occupation rights from abolishing Mehmed VI's sultanate, which did not occur until **1922**. Articles I, VII, XVI, XXIII and XXV considered; genocide treated distinctly from general wartime hardship.
  - https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/weimar-germany-1918-1933/mudros-agreement-armistice-with-turkey-october-30-1918
- **Austria-Hungary:** Villa Giusti signed **November 3**, effective **3 p.m. November 4** in the annexed protocol; the delay and prisoner implications are explained as contested operational implementation.
  - https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Villa_Giusti (published transcription, not original signed manuscript)
- **Germany:** Compiègne signed early **November 11**, fighting ceased **11 a.m.**; the primary source box supplies a traceable published English edition and explicit editorial paraphrases of Articles **I, II, V, XV, XXII, XXVI, XXXIV**, and separately Article **X**. The nonreciprocal Allied and U.S. prisoner-return obligation and the continuation of blockade conditions are accurately distinguished from later peace-treaty terms.
  - https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/weimar-germany-1918-1933/conditions-of-the-armistice-with-germany-november-11-1918
  - https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1919Parisv02/d1
  - https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/address-joint-session-congress-concerning-the-terms-armistice-signed-germany

### Dynastic, constitutional and nationality control

- Wilhelm II: distinguished **November 9** unauthorized announcement of abdication, **November 10** flight and **November 28** signed abdication instrument; supported by German Historical Museum chronology.
  - https://www.dhm.de/lemo/kapitel/weimarer-republik/revolution-191819/abdankung-wilhelms-ii
- Charles I: distinguished the **October 16** proposed federal reform limited to the Austrian half of Austria-Hungary, **November 11** withdrawal from Austrian state affairs and **November 13** Hungarian statement, neither described as a conventional signed renunciation of hereditary dynastic rights.
  - https://ww1.habsburger.net/en/chapters/last-days-monarchy
- Czechoslovak, South Slav, Polish and West Ukrainian national claims are attributed to distinct political organizations. The **October 18** Washington Declaration is separated from **October 28** Prague government assumption, **October 29** Zagreb State of Slovenes, Croats and Serbs, and **December 1** proclamation of the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes.
  - https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1918Supp01v01/d846
- The German military's own **September 29** armistice request is distinguished from subsequent claims of internal betrayal. The *Dolchstoßlegende* is analyzed as an evidentially unsupported political myth, not presented as a coequal military explanation.
- The §112.20 scholarly feature names and compares David Stevenson, Alexander Watson, Manfried Rauchensteiner, Mark Cornwall, Pieter Judson and Robert Gerwarth, referring to identifiable published works without invented page references or quotations.

## 5. Disclosed evidence and production limits

1. **Scholarly textual edition, not a facsimile-edited primary-source publication.** The November armistice excerpts are deliberately labeled editorial paraphrases; the German, English, Ottoman and Italian original signed manuscript sets were not exhaustively collated. The guide contains traceable published source records, but not all 27 institutional URLs were individually retested for live access during this audit. Edition-specific page verification is selective.
2. **Historical maps are text-guided.** Exercises require suitable atlases or archival maps. No invented geography, route distances or unlicensed historical plates were embedded.
3. **Opening image is linked, not embedded.** The exact DHM object and its date are given; local reuse/rights clearance remains the publisher's decision. A photo caption does not establish what every sailor believed.
4. **Chapter 111 styling and functioning UI behaviors were inherited/adapted;** exact byte-for-byte identity with the original Chapter 2 template was not independently established.
5. **Live MGU deployment, real-site integrations and sitewide navigation were not tested.** Internal chapter anchors and local Chromium behavior were tested. External links may require publisher maintenance.
6. **Quantitative casualty or famine estimates are not introduced as unsourced precise totals.** This chapter principally analyzes institutional and military processes, and avoids converting uncertainty into precise claims.

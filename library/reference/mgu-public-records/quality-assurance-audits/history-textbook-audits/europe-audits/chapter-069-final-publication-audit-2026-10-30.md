## Consolidated publication repairs

1. **§69.4 / Enriquillo:** Replaced the incorrect implication that Enriquillo *possessed* an encomienda grant. His people were assigned to encomenderos; mistreatment contributed to his 1519 rebellion. Source: Ida Altman, *The Revolt of Enriquillo and the Historiography of Early Spanish America*, *The Americas*, available at https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/americas/article/abs/revolt-of-enriquillo-and-the-historiography-of-early-spanish-america/AE46A808BFFB70C9DCE84F276821E650 ; and https://tiboko.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/The-Revolt-of-Enriquillo-and-the-Historiography-of-early-Spanish-America_Print-1.pdf .
2. **Summary / New Granada:** Replaced phrasing implying that Federmann's Welser-backed party conquered the Muisca alongside Quesada. Federmann and Belalcázar reached the plateau later in 1539, after the key campaigns. Source: J. Michael Francis, *Invading Colombia: Spanish Accounts of the Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada Expedition of Conquest*, Penn State University Press, https://www.psupress.org/books/titles/978-0-271-02936-8.html .
3. **Florentine source window, metadata:** Distinguished the 1553–1555 collection of Nahua conquest accounts and completed Florentine manuscript of 1577 from Sahagún's subsequent separate 1585 revision. Sources: Getty Research Institute, https://florentinecodex.getty.edu/ ; Library of Congress, https://www.loc.gov/item/2021667857/ ; 1585 separate revision bibliography https://search.worldcat.org/title/Conquest-of-New-Spain-%3A-1585-revision/oclc/18291052 .
4. **Florentine source window, analysis/context:** Correspondingly clarified that the extant Florentine manuscript was completed in 1577 and that the 1585 revision is a separate later stage of Sahagún's conquest narrative, not a revision of the extant dated codex.
5. **Opening / 1514 Alburquerque count:** Identified the approximately 26,000 figure as a count in a repartimiento labor distribution, not a whole-population census. A peer-reviewed work reports 26,334 recorded Indians. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/americas/article/abs/revolt-of-enriquillo-and-the-historiography-of-early-spanish-america/AE46A808BFFB70C9DCE84F276821E650 .

John Hemming, *The Conquest of the Incas*, textual historical discussion at https://www.historyisaweapon.com/defcon7/%5BHemming_John%5D_The_Conquest_of_the_Incas%28BookZZ.org%29.pdf .

## Specific requested fact verification

- **1514 Hispaniola:** Roughly 26,000 in the 1514 repartimiento is supportable; this was not an exhaustive population census.
- **Cholula deaths:** The narrative's deliberately qualified 'several thousand' is supportable; archaeologist Geoffrey McCafferty notes estimates of approximately 2,000–6,000, https://antharky.ucalgary.ca/mccafferty/sites/antharky.ucalgary.ca.mccafferty/files/CholulaMassacre_2000.pdf .
- **Potosí c.1600:** 'Well over one hundred thousand' is within the published historical estimate range; one specialist account cites roughly 120,000 in the 1570s and 160,000 toward 1610, https://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?pid=S0717-73562014000300005&script=sci_arttext .
- **16th-century African transatlantic movement:** Numerical studies disagree on destinations and totals; 'low hundreds of thousands' for broader Atlantic movement is cautiously framed. UNESCO *General History of Africa* volume V (about 274,900 brought from Africa by European traders between 1451–1600, including Europe and Atlantic islands), https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ ; Frederick P. Bowser, Cambridge *History of Latin America*, https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-history-of-latin-america/africans-in-spanish-american-colonial-society/CFAA81098D96CC501C1F62EF81F862D4 .

## 3. Historical verification, qualifications and changes

**A. Established claims requiring no substantive rewrite:**

- The Edict of Nantes (April 1598) institutionalized limited Protestant rights and political compromise, not general modern religious freedom. The Edict of Alès (1629) removed military/political privileges while confirming the surviving worship/conscience settlement (BnF's record gives 27 June, with 28 June appearing in some references).
- The Fronde (1648–53), Louis XIV's personal government from 1661, the 6 May 1682 installation at Versailles, restricted remonstrance under the 1673 rule, the Nine Years' War (1688–97), and the 1715 succession are consistent with the relevant chronology.
- The emigration figure of around **150,000–200,000 Huguenots** after the revocation is a historically defensible **estimate**, with variation in methods and counting periods; not a documented exact headcount.
- Historians William Beik, Sharon Kettering, Roger Mettam, James B. Collins, Guy Rowlands, Nicholas Henshall, Ernest Lavisse, and Roland Mousnier are relevant to the debates, although their interpretations are not completely interchangeable. Henshall's *The Myth of Absolutism* dates to 1992.
- The 1685 colonial *Code Noir*, **in its French Caribbean-island jurisdiction**, included Article I calling for the expulsion of Jews and Article III prohibiting public non-Catholic worship. Its coercive regulation of enslavement is correctly central to the narrative; jurisdiction was clarified in the revised HTML.

**B. Targeted final corrections or enrichments applied directly:**

1. **§76.25, Code Noir jurisdiction and law:** changed a broad reference to all French colonies into the precise 1685 edict for the French Caribbean islands; distinguished Article I's Jewish expulsion provision from Article III's prohibition of public non-Catholic worship. Also distinguished the edict's original geographic coverage from later changes.
2. **Primary Source Window 3:** replaced an unattributed composite/genre-level example with an **identifiable 1649 pamphlet, *Rondeau du Roy contre le cardinal Mazarin***, BnF catalogue record `cb335920589`, Moreau no. 3560, digital copy `NUMM-5426071`. Its title and catalogue are analyzed without claiming to transcribe the uninspected pamphlet or to identify its actual author/readership. Generic Mazarinade themes are explicitly kept separate from document-level claims.
3. **Quantitative History Study:** inserted **verifiable, published annual gross-revenue data** from William Beik (2000), reproduced by College Board (2010), printed p. 79. Selected nominal **livres tournois**:
   - **1663:** pays d'élections 37,935,610; indirect taxes 41,634,000; total gross 88,184,002.
   - **1671:** pays d'élections 33,845,797; indirect taxes 51,248,335; total gross 99,927,820.
   - **1685:** pays d'élections 34,508,216; indirect taxes 66,043,250; total gross 120,736,724.
   These are **gross nominal figures**, not treasury net receipts or real/per-capita burdens. The table now poses comparisons and source-limit questions; the 2026 Davoine et al. historical GIS study supplies a second data reference, specifically **249 tax jurisdictions** in the mapped 1665 Grandes Gabelles system. This does not imply 249 jurisdictions across all France.
4. **§76.31 and source-window date control:** preserved the October 1685 date, identified **22 October registration**, and identified a documentary distinction between 17 October signature accounts and some chronologies placing the event on 18 October. Original edict prints give October 1685 without a day.
5. **§76.35 famine:** added named recent scholarly estimates (1.3 million early-1690s excess deaths; 300,000 for 1709–1710), presented as reconstructions, not observed counts. The specific periods/baselines should not be conflated.
6. **Source windows:** attached identifiable manuscript/edition/catalogue links for Nantes, Alès, Louis XIV's *Mémoires*, Saint-Simon, East India Company declaration, Fontainebleau, and the 1695 capitation edict. Preserved accurate paraphrase labeling and omitted fabricated quotations.
7. **Responsive HTML:** contained **four wide tables** within horizontally scrollable wrappers, with only two scoped CSS rules. Retained the model's overall design, navigation and script.

No section was removed, renumbered, or replaced. No historical prose was trimmed to meet an arbitrary word count.

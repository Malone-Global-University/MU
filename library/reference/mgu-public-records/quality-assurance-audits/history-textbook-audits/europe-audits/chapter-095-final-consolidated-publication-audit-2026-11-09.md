## 4. Consolidated historical corrections and source precision

1. **1851 urbanization:** Replaced an overbroad 'Britain' urban-majority formulation with **England and Wales**, tied to historically variable town/settlement definitions. The same precision was applied to the chronology entry. Evidence: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7611108/ ; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jue.2021.103390 .
2. **1851 Irish-born count:** Approximately **727,000 residents of Great Britain born in Ireland**, rather than merely an unspecified rounded 700,000; excludes their British-born descendants. Evidence: Roger Swift, *Irish Historical Studies*, https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/irish-historical-studies/article/abs/outcast-irish-in-the-british-victorian-city-problems-and-perspectives/85EF3CA281FF686E12E9E9685B7EB8C2 .
3. **§95.13 illustrative household:** Removed the unsupported claim that food 'commonly absorbed well over half' of the hypothetical family's budget. Reframed the composition as a **qualitative illustrative composite**, not a documented household or a statistically measured expenditure ratio. Retained the disclosure. For genuine household documentation, see Michael Anderson on Preston: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/household-and-family-in-past-times/household-structure-and-the-industrial-revolution-midnineteenthcentury-preston-in-comparative-perspective/C209C86C177FC2CB62462D5AFBF0B9E0 and Cambridge's Le Play research: https://www.campop.geog.cam.ac.uk/research/projects/gendertimeuse/ .
4. **France 1841:** Clarified that child-labor restrictions chiefly applied to factories and workshops with **more than twenty workers** and that professional inspection developed later, including 1868 and stronger reforms in 1874, rather than an immediate universal system. Evidence: https://academic.oup.com/book/45624/chapter/394885488 .
5. **Prussia 1839:** Clarified restrictions on factory employment of children under nine, while acknowledging enforcement limitations. Contemporaneous European comparative legal summaries are listed in the prepublication audit.
6. **Chronology:** Tightened the mid-century grouped chronology label from 1844–1852 to **1844–1849**, consistent with the displayed events (Rochdale Pioneers 1844, Ten Hours Act 1847, cholera 1848–1849).
7. **Primary Source Window:** Supplied complete bibliographic identification of the **1832 House of Commons Sadler Committee report** and **1833 Factories Inquiry Commission First Report** (House of Commons paper 450, ordered printed 28 June 1833). The feature remains conspicuously marked **editorial paraphrase/synthesis, not a quotation**, and explicitly directs readers to the Parliamentary Papers for named witnesses, page numbers and literal wording. References: https://books.google.com/books/about/Report_from_the_Committee_on_the_Bill_to.html?id=35JIAQAAMAAJ ; https://iucat.iu.edu/iub/82960 .
8. **Source path:** Added seven named curated documentary/scholarly research leads in the existing MGU research box, without pretending that the complete original archival corpus was collated or constructing invented quotations.

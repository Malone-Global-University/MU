## III. Historical verification, limitations and selected corrections

1. **William III** is distinguished as king of England, Scotland and Ireland, and holder of stadtholderates in several Dutch provinces, not sovereign of a unitary Dutch state. The difference matters to coalition negotiation in 1701.
2. **Blenheim, 1704:** The Bavarian elector continued his alliance with France after the defeat and occupation; it is inaccurate to say Bavaria vanished entirely as a French ally.
3. **Spanish governance:** The decrees of Nueva Planta began with Aragon and Valencia on 29 June 1707; Catalonia received a separate decree in 1716. Supporting identification: Royal Spanish Academy, DPEJ, https://dpej.rae.es/lema/decreto-de-nueva-planta/boe.
4. **Sicily and Sardinia:** Savoy obtained Sicily in the 1713 settlement and exchanged it for Sardinia under the 1720 adjustment; do not date acquisition of Sardinia to 1713.
5. **1756 diplomacy:** The First Treaty of Versailles was a defensive agreement with limits; it should not be conflated with the stronger 1757 arrangements. Frederik Dhondt, *European Journal of International Law* 29 (2018), https://academic.oup.com/ejil/article/29/3/773/5165629.
6. **Russia in 1762:** Elizabeth died on 25 December 1761 Old Style / 5 January 1762 New Style; Peter III reversed Russian policy, while Catherine II withdrew from the partnership but did not resume the anti-Prussian war.
7. **British-Prussian finance:** Britain's Prussian subsidy was not simply renewed through 1763. Its termination in 1762 and broader diplomatic reorientation are addressed in the narrative.
8. **Paris and Fontainebleau:** The February 1763 Treaty of Paris, Article VII, ceded defined French claims east of the Mississippi to Britain but **excluded New Orleans and its island**. The 1762 secret Treaty of Fontainebleau separately assigned Louisiana and New Orleans to Spain. Yale Avalon primary text https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/paris763.asp; Library of Congress historical essay https://www.loc.gov/collections/louisiana-european-explorations-and-the-louisiana-purchase/articles-and-essays/louisiana-as-a-spanish-colony/.
9. **Paris, Article XI:** Restores defined French factories in India while curtailing French fortifications or troops in Bengal and naming Indian political leaders. This does not establish British sovereignty over all India.
10. **Hubertusburg:** Separated from the Treaty of Paris; Saxony was restored and Prussia retained Silesia and Glatz. Saxon State Archives, 10026 Geheimes Kabinett, Loc. 03061/02, https://www.archiv.sachsen.de/archiv/bestand.jsp?guid=a29452f7-71a2-4584-8a31-9bb5ed0dbbfe.

**Coverage cautions:** These are targeted checks of major chronology, treaty clauses and contested strategic claims, supplemented by the document-specific references below. There has not been a line-by-line review against all original-language records. Historical interpretations concerning Indigenous diplomacy, the Bengal succession and the commercial value of colonies remain evidence-based syntheses rather than exhaustive archival analyses. Casualty numbers are not fabricated or treated as certain.

## IV. Quantitative verification

The chapter reproduces the following five *selected-year outstanding public-debt stocks* from **Bank of England, *A Millennium of Macroeconomic Data for the UK***, via Federal Reserve Economic Data, **PSDOUKA**. All values are **nominal millions of British pounds**.

| Year | Public sector debt (£ million, nominal) |
|---|---:|
| 1700 | 17.8 |
| 1714 | 50.4 |
| 1748 | 81.1 |
| 1756 | 79.6 |
| 1763 | 144.0 |

Source: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/PSDOUKA. Context: OBR *300 Years of UK Public Finance Data*, https://obr.uk/article/300-years-of-uk-public-finance-data/.

Worked values independently rechecked: **50.4 − 17.8 = 32.6** million, **32.6 / 17.8 ≈ 183.1%**; **144.0 − 79.6 = 64.4** million, **64.4 / 79.6 ≈ 80.9%**. These measure changes in debt stock, **not** annual borrowing or the direct cost of individual wars. The authorial text clearly states this limitation. Do not convert the £ series into French livres, Dutch guilders or Prussian thalers without separate reliable exchange/valuation evidence.

## 3. Historical corrections directly implemented

1. **§63.11 — Legal status of Muslim Algerians:** Replaced the anachronistic generalization that Muslims were simply legal subjects until they relinquished Islamic personal status. Distinguished earlier French nationality without ordinary civic rights from postwar extensions of formal citizenship with continued Muslim personal status and discriminatory electoral colleges under the 1947 Statute. Contemporary context supported by James McDougall and the French/UN legal record.
2. **§63.12 — Philippeville/Constantine, 20 August 1955:** Distinguished a frequently cited breakdown of **71 European civilians, 21 Muslim civilians, and 31 security personnel**, instead of “more than 100 Europeans.” Separately attributed more extensive French and settler reprisals and explicitly disclosed disputed death estimates. Jeffrey W. Mott's University of New Brunswick historical thesis cites the historical breakdown and underlying scholarship.
3. **§63.12 — FLN wilaya chronology:** Distinguished original operational zones from the more standardized six-wilaya organizational arrangements formalized by the August 1956 Soummam Congress. Preserved recognition of competing internal and external FLN leadership.
4. **§63.17 — Moktar Ould Daddah:** Distinguished his governmental role at Mauritanian independence on **28 November 1960** from presidential election in **August 1961** and assumption of office in **September 1961**. Corrected the front reference-list entry. Some historical profiles also describe an interim/acting presidential role in 1960, which should not be confused with the subsequently elected constitutional presidency.
5. **Narrative precision:** Clarified the distinct categories of displaced Algerian civilians and cross-border refugees, the limits of military victory in Algiers, the July 1961 Bizerte dispute and October 1963 withdrawal, and reasons that diplomacy and self-government did not automatically resolve internal representation.
6. **Preserved correctly documented events:** Libya's 1949 UN resolution and 1951 independence, Sudan's 1953 agreement and 1956 independence, Morocco/Tunisia's March 1956 independence recognitions, the Évian negotiations and March ceasefire, France's July 3 recognition versus Algeria's July 5 independence commemoration, and South Yemen's November 1967 transition.

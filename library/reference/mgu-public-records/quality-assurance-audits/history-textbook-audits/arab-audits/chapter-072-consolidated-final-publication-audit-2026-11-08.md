## Historical accuracy and documentary checks

1. **Arab League founding pact corrected.** The opening, chronology, and §72.20 state that six governments signed on **March 22, 1945** and that Yemen signed on **May 5, 1945**; there were seven founding members but not seven initial March signatories. Confirmed by the original UN Treaty Series, volume 70, no. 241, signature note; Yale Avalon reproduces it. Sources: https://treaties.un.org/pages/showDetails.aspx?objid=080000028015a44b and https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/arableag.asp.

2. **2026 Iran-war sequence clarified.** The manuscript distinguishes February 28 initial U.S.–Israeli strikes, disruption of Strait of Hormuz traffic, **April temporary ceasefire, June MoU after Pakistan-led mediation, August expiration/collapse, and renewed limited fighting**. It does not portray the June arrangement as a durable conclusion. Verified against U.S. Congressional Research Service, updated September 18, 2026: https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R48887.html. The October 2026 World Bank economic estimates are consistently presented as forecasts and scoped to **MENAAP**, not the League’s 22 members: https://www.worldbank.org/en/region/mena/publication/middle-east-north-africa-afghanistan-and-pakistan-economic-update.

3. **Palestine and Gaza chronology distinguished.** §72.2 preserves the Oct. 7, 2023 attack and the subsequent Israeli military campaign as separate events. It identifies the July 19, 2024 ICJ advisory opinion as an advisory proceeding rather than a criminal conviction, and clarifies that the IPC famine finding in August 2025 related to **Gaza Governorate** rather than every part of the Gaza Strip. Government recognition and territorial implementation are distinguished. Original ICJ opinion: https://www.un.org/unispal/document/advisory-opinion-icj-19jul24/. OCHA, November 5, 2026: https://www.ochaopt.org/content/humanitarian-situation-report-5-november-2026.

4. **Syria source reclassified precisely.** The October 12, 2026 document window is labeled a **UN DPPA institutional summary of Claudio Cordone’s Security Council briefing**, not a certified verbatim transcript. The new text distinguishes announced institutions from judicial independence, inclusive representation, and effective civilian authority. UN DPPA weekly source: https://dppa.un.org/en/node/137067.

5. **Sudan statistics defined by category.** The UNHCR–IOM statement dated November 6, 2026 reports **15.4 million people in need of emergency shelter and basic household items**, not 15.4 million newly displaced persons; the separate population categories are preserved. Original: https://www.unhcr.org/news/press-releases/unhcr-and-iom-warn-millions-sudan-risk-losing-emergency-shelter-funding-runs.

6. **Saudi, trilateral and ESCWA sources identified.** The Vision 2030 window links to the official 2025 report collection and explicitly treats government performance statements as such. The March 10, 2023 Saudi–Iranian–Chinese statement has a verifiable original full text. ESCWA’s Tenth Water Development Report (2025) is linked as a regional environmental assessment; its statistical observation years are not silently assigned to 2026. Original links appear in the HTML source register.

7. **Quantitative comparison reconstructed from one common source.** The seven-country table uses UNDP *Human Development Report 2025*, **2023 observation year**, Statistical Table 1. Rechecked HDI values and life expectancy: UAE **0.940, 82.9 years**; Saudi Arabia **0.900, 78.7**; Egypt **0.754, 71.6**; Morocco **0.710, 75.3**; Iraq **0.695, 72.3**; Sudan **0.511, 66.3**; Somalia **0.404, 58.8**. This is not described as 2026 performance; the page explicitly warns that the dataset predates later wars. Source: https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2025-05/human_development_report_2025.pdf.

8. **Supplementary social indicators.** The expansion identifies the ILO’s 2025 youth unemployment estimates of 26.2% for the *Arab States* grouping and 22.6% for *Northern Africa*, not an all-Arab-League measure. ITU 2026 estimates are attributed to the ITU’s own regional definition and not all 22 members indiscriminately. Source records are listed in the register.

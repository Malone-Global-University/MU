### 3. Corrections actually made

1. **Chapter opening:** replaced the misleading formulation that a formal Egyptian monarchy existed continuously since the early nineteenth century with the Muhammad Ali dynasty (established 1805) and distinguished the 1922 Kingdom.
2. **§62.7 religious courts and women's status:** corrected the claim that separate religious courts continued unchanged. Egypt's Law 462/1955 abolished separate shari'a and communal courts effective 1 January 1956, transferring jurisdiction to national courts; religiously grounded substantive family rules remained.
3. **§62.15/chronology UAR:** distinguished 1 February 1958 announcement, 21 February plebiscites, and the formal creation of the UAR on 22 February; clarified North Yemen's confederal association as distinct from Egypt–Syria union.
4. **§62.16 UAR secession:** clarified that the sweeping July 1961 nationalizations preceded the September 28 Syrian secession and contributed to opposition. Nasser's post-secession ideological emphasis is connected to the 1962 National Charter, not used to explain earlier decrees.
5. **§62.4 assassination attempt:** distinguished the perpetrator's identification from the contested extent of Muslim Brotherhood leadership authorization.
6. **§§62.5–62.8:** added substantive discussion of agrarian tenure and cooperative power, the constraints on industrial planning, dam costs and Nubian displacement, the relationship between expanded education and state employment, minority-community departures, and political mobilization versus repression.
7. **§§62.9–62.13:** expanded the Canal Zone evacuation bargain, nonalignment as strategic bargaining, dam financing and company-versus-canal ownership, the Sèvres sequence, operational phases in Sinai and Port Said, the General Assembly emergency-session response, UNEF's limitations, and military versus diplomatic outcomes.
8. **§§62.14–62.17:** developed the difference between pan-Arab media reach and genuine support for unity, the UAR's legal and administrative integration, Syrian factional and economic grievances, and differentiated rivalries among Arab governments.
9. **§§62.18–62.20:** expanded Yemen's logistical and political costs, qualified but documented chemical-weapons history, PLO/Fatah autonomy versus Cairo's strategic preferences, and the enduring state-institutional legacy.
10. **Mobile defect:** introduced a constrained, internally scrolling chronology-table rule and wrapping for long source links without replacing the Chapter 3 design shell.

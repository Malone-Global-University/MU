## Historical checks — independently checked against documentary and scholarly evidence
1. **August 21, 1915 / May 6, 1916 executions:** Twenty-one persons were executed on May 6, fourteen in Beirut and seven in Damascus. Abd al-Hamid al-Zahrawi and Shukri al-Asali were among the Damascus victims; Sa'id Fadel Aql and Petro Paoli were among those executed in Beirut. See Hasan Kayalı, *Arabs and Young Turks* and regional commemorative rosters: https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/arabs-and-young-turks-ottomanism-arabism-and-islamism-in-the-ottoman-empire-1908-1918.html ; https://syrmh.com/2024/05/06/شهداء-6-أيار-1916/
2. **Muhammad Sharif al-Faruqi:** His influence on British analysis, and his exaggeration of the mandate he purported to represent, are supported by archival scholarship and modern review: https://carnegieendowment.org/middle-east/diwan/2022/01/man-in-the-middle-january-11-2022
3. **Brémond military mission:** French military archival description dates the mission **September 1916–May 1918**, so its contribution cannot be implied to have helped in the initial June–July operations. https://www.servicehistorique.sga.defense.gouv.fr/ark/1126633 ; https://www.loc.gov/item/32005627/
4. **Ajyad/Jiyad fortress, Mecca:** Defended in June and forced to surrender in July; last Ottoman positions in Mecca surrendered by July 9, 1916. https://www.thehejazrailway.com/the-arab-revolt
5. **Fa'iz al-Ghusayn:** Syrian Arab lawyer of Bedouin origin; eyewitness to Armenian deportation violence principally around Diyarbakir and on travel into the region, author of the wartime text translated as *Martyred Armenia*. https://genocide-museum.am/eng/eye_witnesses2.php ; https://www.genocide-museum.am/eng/18.11.23.php
6. **Medina:** Prewar population estimate ~40,000 is documented in contemporary encyclopedia; Fakhri's expulsion of civilian residents, food restrictions, and religious-site military use are examined by Alia El Bakri, *International Journal of Middle East Studies* 46 (2014), 703–718. Distinguish surrender/capture in January 1919 from formal May/October 1918 events. https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:EB1926_-_Supplement_Volume_2.pdf/903 ; https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-middle-east-studies/article/abs/memories-of-the-beloved-oral-histories-from-the-191619-siege-of-medina/1EF866580C591606F76FAC4367B2F455
7. **Ottoman prisoners in the final campaign:** ~70–75,000 prisoners in September–October 1918, not necessarily in the opening days of Megiddo alone. https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/egypt-and-palestine-campaign ; https://nzhistory.govt.nz/media/photo/group-ottoman-prisoners
8. **Palestine demographics:** Around 1914 the Jewish population commonly estimated near 84,000 and roughly 10–12% of regional population; boundaries, enumerations, and later reconstructions differ, so manuscript's approximate wording is appropriate. https://palquest.org/en/highlight/294/demography-and-palestine-question-i ; https://www.palestineremembered.com/Articles/A-Survey-of-Palestine/Story6585.html
9. **Egyptian Labour Corps:** Well over 100,000 Egyptians served across successive waves/contracts; figures refer variously to point-in-time strength and cumulative recruitment. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-middle-east-studies/article/egyptian-labor-corps-workers-peasants-and-the-state-in-world-war-i/345E2D975EFD18631263DD1385EDB599
10. **Mudros:** Rauf Bey headed the Ottoman delegation and signed with Admiral Calthorpe aboard *Agamemnon*, October 30, 1918; Article XVI specifies surrender of Hijaz, Asir, Yemen, Syria, and Mesopotamia garrisons. https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/weimar-germany-1918-1933/ghdi:document-4012
11. **Wilson and Balfour:** Wilson's prior approval is recorded (Oct. 13, 1917). https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/woodrow-wilson-event-timeline ; https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1948v05p2/d79
12. **December 1918 Lloyd George/Clemenceau understanding:** Confirmed by subsequent negotiations and contemporary diplomatic retrospectives; manuscript properly frames this as informal and reserves detailed disposition for Chapter 56. https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1919Parisv05/d1 ; https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1919Parisv05/d77
13. **Famine historians:** Linda Schatkowski Schilcher, Melanie Tanielian, and Najwa al-Qattan have published on the famine, everyday survival, and memory; the text does not assign fabricated quotations. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-middle-east-studies/article/abs/when-mothers-ate-their-children-wartime-memory-and-the-language-of-food-in-syria-and-lebanon/F9B59F8A788BE59114D6E8466A9BC5FA
14. **Lahej/Aden:** Sa'id Pasha and the July 1915 Ottoman-led campaign are corroborated by British India Office holdings. https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-000565344 ; https://qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100025595977.0x000074

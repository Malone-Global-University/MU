## 5. Historical accuracy and critical distinctions

Key facts corroborated in this production review:

1. **Bahrain**: commercial discovery 1932; first exports 1934. Bapco official history: https://www.bapco.net/en/about-us/history . The chapter includes the 1973 constitutional assembly and its 1975 dissolution, omitted by Claude.
2. **Saudi Arabia**: the 1933 concession, 1938 Dammam No. 7 discovery, and 1973/1974/1980 participation milestones follow Aramco's historical chronology: https://www.aramco.com/about-us/our-history . The distinct 1988 incorporation of Saudi Aramco is explicitly identified. The 1960 public girls' schooling decision is attributed to the reign of King Saud, not retroactively to Faisal's kingship: https://kingsaud.org/en/history/history-king-saud/educator/the-beginnings-of-female-education/ .
3. **Kuwait**: Burgan commercial discovery 1938, first shipments 1946, and 1975 nationalization confirmed by Kuwait Oil Company: https://www.kockw.com/sites/EN/Pages/Profile/whoAreWe/KOC-History.aspx ; official constitutional text: https://mona.gov.kw/en/ourconstitution.aspx . The 1976 parliamentary suspension and the limits of the electoral franchise are discussed.
4. **Qatar**: initial provisional basic law 1970, amended basic law **April 19, 1972**, and Shura Council established 1972. Qatar government source: https://www.gco.gov.qa/en/state-of-qatar/state-institutions/shura-council/ . This specifically repairs the chronological error in Claude's §65.8.
5. **UAE**: six emirates founded the federation December 2, 1971; Ras al-Khaimah acceded February 10, 1972. UAE National Library: https://www.nla.ae/en/our-history/historical-periods/the-formation-of-the-federation/ . The federal window discloses that a currently available version of the constitutional text contains later amendments, which must not be read back into December 1971.
6. **Oman**: oil exports by 1967 and Qaboos's accession in 1970. Contemporary primary documentation: Sultanate of Oman Development Council, *Five-Year Development Plan, 1976–1980*: https://www.economy.gov.om/en/PDF/The%20five-year%20development%20plan1976-1980.pdf . The window uses this identified publication in place of an unverified 1970 speech.
7. **OPEC/OAPEC**: OPEC was founded September 1960 in Baghdad by Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, and Venezuela, confirmed by UN Treaty Series vol. 443 no. 6363: https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/UNTS/Volume%20443/volume-443-I-6363-English.pdf . OAPEC was founded in Beirut January 9, 1968 by Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, and Libya; industrial cooperation is differentiated from later embargo policy: https://www.oapecorg.org/Home/About-Us/History .
8. **1973–1974**: Arab exporter production cuts and directed embargoes are distinguished from broader OPEC producer pricing and longer-term market conditions. Federal Reserve historical account: https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/oil-shock-of-1973-74 . The embargo's March 1974 termination did not end elevated prices.
9. **1971 maritime disputes**: the Abu Musa memorandum and the Iranian seizure of Greater and Lesser Tunb are not conflated. Contemporary US diplomatic record, December 1, 1971: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v24/d110 . The text avoids announcing a settled final sovereignty determination.
10. **Bahrain's UN process**: the 1970 UN Personal Representative's consultation, document S/9772, is not mislabeled a popular referendum: https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/502605/files/S_9772-EN.pdf .

The chapter also distinguishes pre-oil political and commercial histories, the variation among the six states, formal sovereignty and actual executive power, petroleum revenue and capacity, and migration/citizenship. The major war narratives reserved for Chapters 62, 64, and 66 are not retold at length.

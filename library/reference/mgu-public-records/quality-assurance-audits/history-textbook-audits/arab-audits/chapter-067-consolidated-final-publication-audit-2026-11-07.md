## 5. Independent historical and documentary spot-check

1. **Algeria, 1991–1992:** The first legislative round was 26 December 1991. President Chadli resigned under military pressure on 11 January 1992; the election was interrupted in January, emergency measures followed in February, and a court **formally dissolved the FIS on 4 March 1992**. These are distinct stages. The FIS as political organization is differentiated from the AIS and GIA as armed organizations. Sources: [UN 1998 panel](https://www.refworld.org/reference/countryrep/unsc/1998/en/23818); [Human Rights Watch 1992](https://www.hrw.org/reports/1992/algeria/); [Le Monde contemporary report, March 5, 1992](https://www.lemonde.fr/archives/article/1992/03/05/la-repression-en-algerie-le-fis-est-dissous_3873012_1819218.html).
2. **Sudan, 1983:** The southern rebellion resumed **by May 1983**, before the September Laws. The legal enactments included the **Penal Code (1983)**, Evidence Act (1983), and other measures; a contemporaneous legal review raises serious concerns about procedural safeguards. Sources: [Human Rights Watch, Sudan](https://www.hrw.org/reports/pdfs/s/sudan/sudan965.pdf); [International Commission of Jurists 1986](https://www.icj.org/resource/the-return-to-democracy-in-sudan-report-of-a-mission-on-behalf-of-the-international-commission-of-jurists/); [scholarly legal account](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9789400600171-008/html).
3. **Saudi Arabia, November 1979:** The Grand Mosque seizure began on **20 November 1979**. The U.S. Embassy Jidda’s 21 November dispatch reported no direct Iran link but contained preliminary factual uncertainty; this is analyzed as contemporaneous evidence rather than relied upon for a final casualty estimate. Source: [FRUS telegram](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v18/d201).
4. **Egypt, Article 2:** The 1980 constitutional change distinguished a from the principal source of legislation concerning principles of Islamic shariʿa. Later judicial application was not automatic or uniform. Sources: [WIPO constitutional edition](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/7140); [Library of Congress](https://wwws.loc.gov/law/help/legal-research-guide/egypt.php).
5. **Jordan, 1991:** The National Charter recognized political pluralism and civil protections in normative text, not proof of unrestricted implementation. Source: [Jordanian National Charter, Refworld](https://www.refworld.org/legal/decreees/natlegbod/1991/en/96096).
6. **Tunisia, June 1981:** The MTI founding statement is associated with its 6 June 1981 public announcement. It is paraphrased, not falsely quoted as a verified new translation. Sources: [Arabic archival reproduction](https://www.habib-bourguiba.net/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%A3%D8%B3%D9%8A%D8%B3%D9%8A-%D9%84%D8%AD%D8%B1%D9%83%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AA%D8%AC%D8%A7%D9%87-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B3%D9%84%D8%A7/); [Persée scholarly presentation](https://www.persee.fr/doc/rhpr_0035-2403_1991_num_71_3_5142).
7. **Iran, constitution:** Iran’s constitutional settlement is discussed as a consequential *non-Arab* comparator. The 1979 original and 1989 revised provisions are not treated as identical. Source: [Cambridge scholarship / textual comparison](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/iranian-studies/article/abs/constitution-of-the-islamic-republic-of-iran-1989-edition/315CA81F3A6EB6F9D150D9EE367911C0).
8. **Hamas, 1988:** The document is treated as Hamas’s ideological source, not representative of all Palestinians. Its antisemitic content is identified explicitly as false. Source: [Yale Law School Avalon archival translation](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/hamas.asp).
9. **Hama, Syria, February 1982:** The chapter explains armed opposition and severe state repression without asserting an unsupported definitive casualty count. Source: [Human Rights Watch Syria historical material](https://www.hrw.org/report/2010/07/16/wasted-decade/human-rights-syria-during-bashar-al-asads-first-ten-years-power).

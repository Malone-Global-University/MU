## 2. Consolidated historical review by region

- **Interwar comparative frame (§§60.1–60.3): PASS.** Legal distinctions among Egypt, Sudan, Algeria, Tunisia, Morocco, and Libya are preserved. The text treats Wilsonian self-determination as a political language rather than an automatically implemented postwar rule.
- **Egypt (§§60.4–60.13): PASS.** The March 1919 mobilization, four reservations in the February 1922 independence declaration, 1923 bicameral constitutional order, 1924 Lee Stack crisis, Sidqi’s 1930 constitutional restrictions, 1935 restoration, 1936 treaty and unresolved Sudan question follow the documented sequence. Women's political organization and the 1923 Egyptian Feminist Union are appropriately placed; the Brotherhood's foundation is dated to 1928. Primary benchmarks: UK Hansard 28 Feb 1922, UK parliamentary treaty debates 24–25 Nov 1936, Egypt’s parliamentary history of the 1923 constitution, and Ohio State’s historical essay on Egyptian feminist organizations.
- **Sudan (§§60.14–60.19): PASS WITH PROVENANCE LIMIT.** The 1899 condominium, independent Sudanese role in the mass politics of 1924, creation of the Sudan Defence Force in 1925, Gezira Scheme, varied regional experiences, and 1938 Graduates’ General Congress are chronologically coherent. The Cambridge/Oxford scholarship of Elena Vezzadini supports the broader social reach of 1924. Original May 1938 Arabic newspaper pages still need consultation for a verbatim-excerpt edition.
- **Libya (§§60.20–60.25): PASS.** Local post-1918 alternatives are distinguished from later Fascist reconquest. The 1930–1931 deportations and camp system are treated as coercion against civilian support networks. The figures of roughly 100,000–120,000 interned and up to 40,000–48,000 deaths are appropriately attributed estimates and are not conflated with all wartime demographic change. Al-Mukhtar’s capture and execution in September 1931 and administrative unification of Italian Libya in 1934 are correctly situated.
- **Algeria (§§60.26–60.33): PASS.** The February 1919 Jonnart law is distinguished from universal equal citizenship. The Étoile Nord-Africaine (1926), the Association of Algerian Muslim Ulama (1931), the failed Blum–Viollette proposal, and the establishment of the PPA (1937) are differentiated by political strategy. The Blum–Viollette measure is explicitly identified as **not enacted**, avoiding a common error.
- **Tunisia (§§60.34–60.39): PASS.** The Destour constitutional movement (1920), indigenous CGTT led by Muhammad Ali al-Hammi (1924–1925), Neo-Destour foundation at Ksar Hellal (2 March 1934), and April 9, 1938 repression follow the historical chronology. The revised 1920 documentary window now identifies an actual published political tract instead of an ambiguous bundle of generic petitions.
- **Morocco (§§60.40–60.46): PASS AFTER CORRECTIONS.** The 1921 Annual defeat, Rif state-building, 1925 escalation of the French front, Abd el-Krim's surrender (1926), the 1930 Berber Dahir and **1934 rescission of Article 6** while customary courts survived, and the Moroccan Action Committee's 1934 reform plan are now distinguished precisely. The revised text also identifies Mohammed Hassan Ouazzani as an organizer of the program.
- **Comparative transformations and synthesis (§§60.47–60.52): PASS.** Land, labor, women, languages, social differences, religious organizations and colonial political economy are integrated comparatively. The manuscript concludes before the 1940s and does not preempt Chapters 61–63.

The inquiry found **no evidence of invented direct quotations in the chapter**. The Source Windows explicitly state that their prose is paraphrase, although several paraphrases reflect later scholarship and must not be relabeled as verbatim archival transcriptions.

## 3. Targeted corrections applied to the final HTML

1. **§60.43 — Rif War date.** Made the French-front escalation explicit: Spanish setbacks in 1924 preceded major Rif operations against French positions in **April 1925**; organized Franco-Spanish war and coordinated operations followed. Supported by the *International Review of the Red Cross* analysis of the 1925 campaign.
2. **§60.44 — Berber Dahir legal mechanism.** Replaced vague language that 'provisions were modified' with the documented rescission of **Article 6 in 1934**, distinguishing it from the continued existence of customary civil courts. Supported by Katherine E. Hoffman's historical-legal research.
3. **§60.46 — 1934 Moroccan reform program.** Identified Mohammed Hassan Ouazzani alongside Allal al-Fasi and clarified the committee's intended addressees and **December 1934** presentation. Supported by the French Ministry of Armed Forces digital-library catalog and the Mohamed Hassan Ouazzani Foundation's reproduction of the 1934 edition and publication history.
4. **All 13 Source Windows.** Replaced a repeated generic 'identifiable document of the ... setting' context with actual document-specific historical setting. Replaced the generic fifth inquiry question with a source-specific analytical question, while keeping the baseline questions about creator, claim, and source limitations.
5. **Tunisia source window (1920).** Identified Abdelaziz Thaalbi's *La Tunisie martyre* as the concrete published document rather than attributing a generalized set of petitions to one unnamed source; updated provenance disclaimer and bibliographic link.
6. **Tunisia source window (1934).** Updated source link to the Tunisian National Library’s congress-record catalog.
7. **Rif source window (1922).** Identified Abd el-Krim's *To the Civilized Nations* by title; pointed to scholarly reproduction of an excerpt and retained the caveat that the full original League archive has not been independently inspected.
8. **Moroccan reform-plan source window (1934).** Improved publication provenance, source-recipient explanation and verification status; no fabricated quotation added.

The original HTML's broad narrative, 52 section titles, nine division boundaries, chapter opening, CSS design tokens, sidebar logic, and review apparatus were not removed or compressed.

## 6. Limitations, publication classification, and lock rule

**Decision:** **APPROVED FOR PUBLICATION — LOCKED for the text-complete HTML textbook edition.** The audit supports this determination because the numerical planning target and mandated architecture are satisfied, identified factual defects have been corrected, direct quotations have not been invented, historical claims are phrased with appropriate caution, and technical validation passes.

Two enhancements remain outside the lock: **(a)** maps for the illustrated MGU Library edition—the four existing map studies are text-based map exercises without embedded map plates—and **(b)** page-level original-source transcription and facsimile verification for a documentary quotation edition. Several source windows transparently label secondary-based paraphrases and should retain those limitations unless originals are read.

This is a **consolidated editorial/source audit**, not a claim that every sentence of the 26k-word narrative was separately matched to an original manuscript folio. Historical claims were checked across all geographic divisions, and high-risk dates, legal distinctions, and figures were verified with official or research sources. The surviving provenance limitations are disclosed; they are not hidden evidence of complete archival authentication. No additional generic expansion, micro-pass, or automatic re-audit is recommended. Reopen the text only for a demonstrated material historical, source-attribution, or technical error.

## 3. Consolidated historical corrections and verification

1. **§70.4, Matsuzakaya:** Corrected the misleading statement that Matsuzakaya's Ueno store reopened in 1924 with shoes-on shopping. The firm's documented innovation belongs to its **Ginza store opened in 1924**; the Ueno site was destroyed in 1923 and rebuilt later. Primary institutional reference: J. Front Retailing, *History of Matsuzakaya*, https://d3jktohxunv90n.cloudfront.net/english/company/history_matsuzakaya.html.
2. **§70.9 source window and retail timeline, Mitsukoshi advertising:** Corrected the “Today the Imperial Theatre, Tomorrow Mitsukoshi” advertising date from **1911** to **1913**. The Imperial Theatre opened in 1911, but Mitsui's institutional history identifies the advertisement in a **Taishō 2 (1913)** Imperial Theatre pamphlet. Source: Mitsui Public Relations Committee, https://www.mitsuipr.com/history/columns/058/. The text still states that the original pamphlet should be inspected for exact wording and design.
3. **§70.10 and chronology, *King*:** Clarified publication versus cover date. Kōdansha first released *King* on **November 28, 1924**, with an inaugural issue dated January 1925. Avoided saying unqualifiedly that the publication first appeared in 1925. Source: Kōdansha corporate history, https://www.kodansha.com/history/ and Noma Memorial Museum, https://noma-museum.note.jp/n/ncee6abc328d7. The Osaka prefectural library's NDL reference service records 1927–28 issues exceeding one million: https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/index.php?id=1000101487&page=ref_view.
4. **Chronology, §70.27, summary, Japanese radio launch:** Differentiated Tokyo's **March 22, 1925 provisional public broadcasts** from its commencement of **regular service on July 12**, after the move to Atagoyama. Osaka's provisional service started in June and Nagoya's regular service in July. Reference: Japan Radio Museum (with contemporaneous broadcasting material), https://www.japanradiomuseum.com/meseum1.
5. **§70.28 and quantitative study, radio listening fee:** Corrected the claim that subscribers initially paid two yen monthly and subsequently one yen. **Two yen was the proposed initial rate; one yen was charged during provisional broadcasts and maintained for regular service.** A separate government permit charge should not be conflated with the monthly listening fee. Reference: Japan Radio Museum, https://www.japanradiomuseum.com/jyusinsho.html.
6. **Radio statistics:** Replaced “licensed receivers: a few hundred thousand at most” with a more defined **approximately 259,000 listener contracts at year-end 1925**, distinct from **5,455 contracts at the March launch**, and **approximately 1.06 million contracts in 1931**. These are *contracts*, not a count of unique listeners or individuals owning receivers. The reported series appears in academic historical research using the Japanese Broadcasting Corporation's statistics, as summarized at https://123deta.com/document/z11lwo8z-%E7%AB%8B%E5%91%BD%E9%A4%A8%E5%AD%A6%E8%A1%93%E6%88%90%E6%9E%9C%E3%83%AA%E3%83%9D%E3%82%B8%E3%83%88%E3%83%AA.html. The March launch count is independently reported by Japan Radio Museum. The table is explicitly a secondary series and is not a certified primary-data transcription.
7. **§70.30 and media timeline, electrical recording:** Clarified adoption in two stages. After a 1927 Japanese Columbia partnership brought in Western electrical-recording technology, **domestically issued electrical recordings followed in 1928**; the global technique dates to 1925. Source: NDL Research Navi, https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/en/rnavi/avmaterials/post_1198; Kyoto City University of Arts traditional music research archive, https://rijtm.kcua.ac.jp/archive-db/archives/gallery/1008ohnishi/index.html.
8. **§70.35, colonial Seoul:** Corrected Mitsukoshi's 1906 establishment from a **full branch/department store** to a **subbranch office**. It became a formal branch in 1929 and obtained a modern purpose-built department-store building in 1930. Source: *Modern Asian Studies*, https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/modern-asian-studies/article/paradox-of-authenticity-the-korean-product-showroom-of-mitsukoshi-department-store-in-colonial-seoul/DDB9626F57E341C8ABE6C692471A3F0F; Seoul Museum of History, https://museum.seoul.go.kr/www/board/NR_boardView.do?bbsCd=1026&seq=20170911163336658&sso=ok.
9. **§§70.17, 70.19, Kon Wajirō:** Clarified the 1925 Ginza street-clothing sample as **approximately 67% of men versus 1% of women wearing Western dress**, a **local sample**, not nationwide clothing prevalence or a verified comparison with other districts. Sources: National Archives of Japan (JACAR), https://www.jacar.go.jp/exhibition/glossary/tochikiko-henten/qa/qa01.html and National Diet Library, https://www.ndl.go.jp/kaleido/entry/29/2.html.
10. **§70.1 and urbanization table:** Replaced approximate shares with government population-statistical **shi-area values of 18.0% in 1920 and 24.0% in 1930**, qualifying administrative boundary changes. Historical population tables are available from e-Stat and the National Institute of Population and Social Security Research. The chapter's original city-population figures for Osaka and Tokyo remain rounded and distinguish city boundaries from metropolitan regions.

### High-risk points verified without changing the manuscript

- **1927 Ueno–Asakusa subway**: Japan's first and Asia's first modern subway, opened **December 30, 1927**, according to Tokyo Metro and Japan Subway Association. https://www.tokyometro.jp/lang_en/corporate/profile/history/index.html; https://www.jametro.or.jp/tips/history/when-did-subway-start/.
- **1925 film censorship**: National Ministry of Home Affairs Film Censorship Regulations, **Ministry Order No. 10 (1925)**, following earlier Tokyo Metropolitan Police regulations in 1917. NDL archival exhibition: https://www.ndl.go.jp/kaleido/entry/10/3.html.
- **1929 *Tokyo March***: Nikkatsu lists director **Mizoguchi Kenji**, original story **Kikuchi Kan**, release **May 31, 1929**. https://www.nikkatsu.com/movie/12731.html. A peer-reviewed 2013 study documents the complex relation between the serialized novel, silent film and recorded song, and cautions against simplifying it to a single perfectly planned multimedia launch: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/nihonkindaibungaku/89/0/89_KJ00009352286/_article/-char/ja/. Reported sales figures vary with counting windows and sources; the chapter conservatively says well over 100,000 copies, and no precise total was inserted.
- **Takarazuka *Mon Paris***: Confirmed 1927 and director **Kishida Tatsuya**. NDL exhibition: https://www.ndl.go.jp/france/en/column/s2_4.html.
- **Radio milestone and the radio-law regime**: Supported by historical material from Japan Radio Museum as above. Do not confuse the broadcaster with a modern advertising-supported commercial enterprise.
- **1925 census populations**: The city's size ranking of Osaka over Tokyo is based on their 1925 city-boundary populations, not the entire Tokyo metropolitan region.
- **Ginza *ginbura* etymology**: The asserted Brazilian-coffee connection remains explicitly labeled a contested folk etymology, not established etymology.

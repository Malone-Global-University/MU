## Targeted historical accuracy corrections

1. **Kasagi Shizuko recording:** Corrected both the prose and the source window from **Suzuki Isao** to **Suzuki Masaru**, the credited lyricist. The performer is Kasagi Shizuko and the composer is Hattori Ryōichi. Commercial release is **January 1948**, following 1947 performance/recording activity. Sources: the Columbia reissue song metadata and historic 78-rpm issue A-339 identified in the linked source register. No song lyrics are reproduced.
2. **Film censorship:** Preserved SCAPIN-658's **January 28, 1946** date and its actual administrative requirements: Japanese authorities were to list uncensored films by February 28 and require a censorship identification before exhibition. Added the counterpoint **SCAPIN-146 (October 16, 1945)**, which directed suspension of the Japanese government's earlier propaganda controls. Direct texts linked.
3. **CCD chronology:** Distinguished the **October 1949 end of censorship operations** from the **November 1949 abolition of the Civil Censorship Detachment** in accordance with the National Diet Library archival guide. The end of the CCD is not treated as ending all forms of media review.
4. **Toho dispute:** Replaced vague hearsay with the documented **August 19, 1948** Japanese police action backed by U.S. military personnel, tanks, and aircraft. The evicted workers departed without an armed battle, according to the MIT Visualizing Cultures record. Distinguished the **1947 formation of Shintōhō** from the later confrontation (Japanese Film Database studio history).
5. **Radio:** Verified WVTR opening on **September 23, 1945** (Teranishi), amateur <em>Nodo jiman</em> launch on **January 19, 1946**, and commercial radio in Nagoya on **September 1, 1951 at 06:30** (CBC institutional history). Radio programs and institutions are distinguished from their more developed 1950s forms.
6. **Literature:** The Sakaguchi Ango source window now identifies **<em>Shinchō</em>, 43(4), April 1, 1946**, for the first <em>Darakuron</em>, and distinguishes the December 1946 sequel. The featured passage is an identified editorial paraphrase, not a supposed original translation.
7. **Akiyoshi Toshiko and other musicians:** Avoided treating later jazz careers as proof of specific undated 1945–1946 engagements. Individual biographical trajectories are presented as qualified examples, not as representative measures of the profession.
8. **Film record:** The manuscript distinguishes original release dates, later canonization and box-office outcomes; avoids projecting the 1958 cinema-attendance peak onto the occupation period. The National Film Archive supplies film dates and material-catalog foundations.

## Historical Data Study — substantive correction

Replaced an event-only chronology with a quantitatively grounded table of **1946–1952 cinema sites, annual admissions (millions), and Japanese and foreign film releases**. The underlying published table is in <em>コンテンツ・プロデュース機能の基盤強化に関する調査研究</em>, Chapter 1, Exhibit 1, printed p. 9 (online PDF). The data's source credit refers to <em>Kinema Junpō</em>. The historical series is retrospective, not a set of contemporary audience surveys. Data are reproducible from the referenced table and have no unsourced precision claims about individual viewers, nationwide subscriptions, or record sales.

Key comparisons: cinema sites **1,376 (1946) → 4,109 (1952)**; admissions **732.74 million (1946) → 732.27 million (1952)**, with a drop **786.76 (1949) → 718.70 million (1950)**. The data demonstrate that commercial capacity could expand even as tickets sold fluctuated; these counts do not directly measure audience sentiment, household spending, or real revenue. The data box includes four questions requiring computation and critique.

## Source evidence and limitations

The in-page register contains 35 linked records spanning four major groups: (a) GHQ directives and National Diet Library/Prange finding aids, (b) original recorded or printed artifacts and institutional catalogs, (c) contemporary-period cinema statistics reproduced in a later industry study, and (d) scholarship on musical labor, broadcasting, film, theater, race and literary responses to defeat. Each entry is accompanied by an explicit statement of its use. Source-window commentary distinguishes editorial paraphrases from quotations, and printed publication metadata are provided where verified.

**Disclosed boundaries:** The verification was targeted to publication-critical historical facts and source identification; it did not include full archival-facsimile collation of every Japanese original, original 78-rpm shellac-disc listening, on-site study of every reel or photograph, exhaustive bibliographic page-by-page validation of all 35 records, or an embedded atlas of military bases. The geography exercise is text-guided and calls for an external historical map. The HTML was browser-tested in isolation, not on the live MGU deployment with remote shared components.

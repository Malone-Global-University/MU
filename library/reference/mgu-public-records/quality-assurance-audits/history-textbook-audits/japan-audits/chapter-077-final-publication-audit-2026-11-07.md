## 3. Historical and numerical corrections applied

### 3.1 *Kokutai no Hongi* publication history (§77.2)
Replaced a vague, potentially misleading circulation estimate with **approximately 300,000 copies in the March 1937 initial printing** and **more than two million distributed over time in Japan and the empire**. Initial and cumulative figures are explicitly distinguished. The publication data and selected text can be checked at Columbia University, Asia for Educators: <https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/main_pop/ps/ps_japan-kokutai.htm>.

### 3.2 Peace Preservation Law enforcement (§77.13)
Specified that **more than 70,000 arrests concerned metropolitan Japan from the 1925 enactment through its October 1945 repeal**, with tens of thousands more in the colonies, especially Korea. The narrative now distinguishes arrests, prosecution, and convictions. Relevant research: Max Ward, “Reconsidering the Passage of the 1925 Peace Preservation Law at Its Centennial,” *Asia-Pacific Journal: Japan Focus* (2025), hosted by Cambridge University Press: <https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/asia-pacific-journal/article/reconsidering-the-passage-of-the-1925-peace-preservation-law-at-its-centennial/B4F9F3E74832FBD90400A795AF28D5E0>.

### 3.3 Newspaper consolidation (§77.10)
Replaced an unspecific “many hundreds to a few dozen” formulation with the **approximately 1,200 daily newspapers (1937) to 55 (1943)** series, specifying that daily newspapers are the measured category. See “Media Capture and Information Monopolization in Japan,” DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-5876.2011.00538.x>.

### 3.4 Great Japan Women's Association (§77.18)
Specified **approximately nineteen million members within about one year of the February 1942 consolidation**, following the National Diet Library's institutional history. Enrollment and ideological assent are explicitly differentiated: <https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/cha4/description15.html>.

### 3.5 Student labor mobilization (§77.19, Data Study)
Specified **roughly three million students mobilized by February 1945**, and a scholarly **approximately 3.11 million industrially employed students in March 1945**. These estimates use different endpoints and may exclude or include different categories of work. Sources: Natsuki Aruga, “Uncovering Child Labor in Wartime America by Looking from a Comparative/Japanese Perspective,” *Japanese Journal of American Studies* 22 (2011), p. 38 (<https://www.jaas.gr.jp/jjas/pdf/2011/02_025-045.pdf>), and the Ministry of Education's wartime education history (<https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317693.htm>). The latter also records over 3.4 million mobilized at surrender, a different endpoint.

### 3.6 U.S. Strategic Bombing Survey aircraft statistics (Data Study)
Replaced untraceable rough values with the consistently defined aircraft-production series (all types, army and navy; not just combat types):

| Year | All aircraft produced |
|---|---:|
| 1941 | 5,088 |
| 1942 | 8,861 |
| 1943 | 16,693 |
| 1944 | 28,180 |
| 1945 (January–August, partial year) | **11,066** |

Documentary basis: U.S. Strategic Bombing Survey, Aircraft Division, *The Japanese Aircraft Industry* (May 1947), detailed tables/appendix: <https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/NHC/NewPDFs/USAAF/United%20States%20Strategic%20Bombing%20Survey/USSBS%20Japanese%20Aircraf%20Industry.pdf>. **Internal source caution:** a summary table in that report shows **11,086** for 1945, while the detailed series repeatedly gives **11,066**, a 20-aircraft discrepancy. The textbook discloses this instead of implying false numerical uniformity. The 1945 row must not be compared as a twelve-month total. No evidence is claimed that aircraft output alone demonstrates military operational effectiveness.

### 3.7 Bibliography
Corrected **Gregory J. Kasza, *The Conscription Society: Administered Mass Organizations* (Yale University Press, 1995)**, replacing the draft's incorrect 1993. The publisher catalog confirms 1995: <https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300062427/the-conscription-society/>.

## 4. Primary Source Windows — document-specific review

**Three windows retained; all labeled editorial paraphrases, not authenticated verbatim translations.** Each source now specifies its documentary basis and provides a direct research link.

1. **1937 *Kokutai no Hongi*.** The window is anchored to the **Introduction** and **Our Mission** selections from the Columbia University reproduction of *Sources of Japanese Tradition* (2005), rather than attributing the entire treatise's range of ideas to those two passages. The 1949 English translation by John Owen Gauntlett, edited by Robert King Hall, is bibliographically identified with a library record: <https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/LIB18045>. The student's first question is aligned to the selected passages.
2. **1938 National Mobilization Law.** Window identifies **Law No. 55 of April 1, 1938; Articles 1–2, 4–6, and 20**, including definitions, material resources, mobilized labor, and restrictions on publications. JACAR metadata: `C12121926100`, <https://www.jacar.archives.go.jp/das/meta-en/C12121926100>; historical statute text: <https://jahis.law.nagoya-u.ac.jp/lawdb/l/313a0055>. The window does not purport to quote the statute verbatim.
3. **1941 *Tonarigumi* educational picture story.** Correct attribution: **March 20, 1941; Nihon Kyōiku Kamishibai Kyōkai (script, producer, publisher); Koyano Hanji (illustration); Hoover Institution Kamishibai collection, item `2018C32.00013`**, <https://digitalcollections2.hoover.org/view/ark:/54723/h3n59f>. Replaced a generic description of the genre with a paraphrase of the specific archived story: neighborhood children, shared notice, family of an absent soldier, a mother working in a munitions factory, and community support for her infant. Hoover's description, Japanese transcription, and English translation were inspected; images are not reproduced.

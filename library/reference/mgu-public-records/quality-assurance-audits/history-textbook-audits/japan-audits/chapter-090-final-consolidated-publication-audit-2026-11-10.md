## 4. Consolidated historical corrections and verification

**A. 1950 media controls — corrected.** The original falsely stated that Japanese newspapers reported the June 1950 Korean War outbreak under still-operative SCAP Civil Censorship Detachment censorship. Systematic CCD media censorship ended in October 1949 and the detachment was abolished in November 1949; the revised text distinguishes the ended formal apparatus from continuing occupation political influence. Source locator: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/rnavi/occupation/CIS .

**B. Police and safety institutions — corrected.** The revision separates the July 8, 1950 MacArthur authorization, August 10 Police Reserve ordinance, April 26, 1952 Maritime Guard, **August 1 National Safety Agency** and maritime Coastal Safety Force, and **October 15, 1952 land National Safety Force**. The initial manuscript incorrectly conflated the August and October 1952 reorganizations. Official Japanese Ministry of Defense chronology: https://www.mod.go.jp/gsdf/ord_sch/history.html ; NDL MacArthur letter: https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/cha5/description13.html . The post-April 28 events are clearly presented as sovereign-state follow-on decisions.

**C. February 1952 Administrative Agreement signatories — corrected.** Signed February 28 by **Dean Rusk and Earl D. Johnson** for the United States and **Minister of State Okazaki Katsuo** for Japan. Okazaki formally became foreign minister April 30. FRUS report https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v14p2/d535 and Japan MOFA historic foreign ministers https://www.mofa.go.jp/mofaj/annai/honsho/gaisho/reference.html .

**D. San Francisco conference participation — corrected.** The Soviet Union **attended but did not sign**; neither PRC nor ROC was represented as a signatory, while Korea was excluded from treaty signing. Claude’s synthesis had incorrectly called the Soviet Union absent. The new chapter differentiates participation, signing, ratification, and legal entry into force. NDL https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/cha5/description14.html .

**E. Treaty legal sequence — verified and sharpened.** The 1951 multilateral Peace Treaty and bilateral Security Treaty were signed September 8, 1951, entering into force April 28, 1952. The 1951 Security Treaty is not the 1960 amended security agreement. Final authentic treaty repositories and U.S. legal studies are linked in the register; Okinawa’s continued U.S. *administration* under Peace Treaty Article 3 is distinguished from sovereignty, and no actual UN trusteeship is invented. UN Peace Treaty registration https://treaties.un.org/Pages/showDetails.aspx?objid=08000002801528c2 ; 1951 security text https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/japan001.asp ; Okinawa Archives https://www3.archives.pref.okinawa.jp/GRI/histories/1952/ .

**F. Minesweeper risk — sharpened.** Japanese minesweeper **MS-14** struck a mine off Wonsan on October 17, 1950; documented **one fatality and eighteen injured**. The casualty category is kept distinct from U.S. naval vessel losses. Naval History and Heritage Command: https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/about-us/leadership/director/directors-corner/h-grams/h-gram-055/h-055-1.html .

**G. Okinawan administrative and territorial chronology — sharpened.** The Ryukyu legislature election of March 2, 1952 and Government establishment of April 1 are distinguished from sovereignty; the southern Tokara islands’ return is dated February 10, 1952. U.S. reporting on 1951 reversion petitions is clearly presented as contemporary estimates (not audited totals). Okinawa Archives https://www3.archives.pref.okinawa.jp/GRI/histories/1952/ ; Kagoshima https://www.pref.kagoshima.jp/ab23/pr/gaiyou/rekishi/kindai/tokarahukki.html ; FRUS https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v14p2/d488 .

**H. 1952 nationality status and population — sharpened.** The revised text identifies **535,065** *registered* Korean/Chōsen residents in Japan in the 1952 historical series, rather than a loose estimate of 600,000; it explains that administrative counts have definitional limits. The 19 April 1952 nationality circular is linked and its marriage/adoption family-register exceptions distinguished from the general policy. Official National Institute of Population and Social Security Research dataset: https://www.ipss.go.jp/syoushika/tohkei/Data/Popular2024/T10-01.files/sheet002.htm ; circular text https://www.kokuseki.info/kitei/tsutatsu/1952-04-19.html (reproduced by an advocacy organization; institutional provenance identified).

**I. Korean War procurement and exports — corrected data method.** The Historical Data Study now consists only of source-identified, bounded measures: JACAR procurement contracts through September 17, 1950 **$89.431 million** (goods **$51.287m**, services **$38.144m**); FRUS September 1951 assessment approximately **$25 million/month** Korean War procurement plus commercial-export averages approximately **$70m/month** July–September 1950 and **$125m/month** April–June 1951. These are different quantities and are not added. The original vague, unsupported full-year 1950–1952 procurement receipts row was removed. Sources: JACAR A17111971600 and FRUS https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1951v06p1/d16 .

**J. Post-treaty periodization.** The Korean War armistice occurred **July 27, 1953**; the 1952 May Day clash, later 1952 security reorganization, and August 13, 1952 IMF/World Bank membership are explicitly distinguished as post-occupation consequences. IMF https://www.imf.org/en/countries/jpn and World Bank https://www.worldbank.org/en/about/leadership/members .

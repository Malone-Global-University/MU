## Direct factual and documentary corrections in final file

1. **§69.24: 1924 second constitutional-protection coalition.** Draft erroneously listed the Seiyū Hontō. Corrected to **Kenseikai, Rikken Seiyūkai, and Kakushin Kurabu** (the three pro-constitution factions). Distinguished Seiyū Hontō as separate from the coalition. NDL's *Modern Japan in Archives* confirms the three-party identity.  
   Source: https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/cha3/description12.html
2. **§69.8: first major Tokyo May Day, May 2, 1920.** Corrected the draft's “without a major confrontation”: the roughly 10,000-person Ueno Park gathering was followed by a police confrontation involving a demand for release of detainees. Distinguished rally from subsequent clash. Ōhara Institute dated chronology.  
   Source: https://oisr-org.ws.hosei.ac.jp/research/dglb/explanation/exp_detail/?exp_id=4-012-9000620
3. **Colonial political rights.** Corrected the chapter opening, §69.38, and the chapter summary to distinguish *no territorial Diet constituencies in Korea/Taiwan* from **qualified Korean/Taiwanese men residing in metropolitan Japan, who could vote and stand for election under the 1925 statute**. The draft already acknowledged this in §§69.26 and 69.31; the revision removes its internal contradiction.  
   Sources: https://www.jacar.go.jp/exhibition/universalsuffrage/chronology/ ; https://scalar.chass.ncsu.edu/bodies-and-structures/universal-suffrage-act
4. **§69.34: Labor Disputes Conciliation Law.** Qualified the misleading implication of extensive formal use; the Ōhara Institute records **only six formal applications through postwar repeal**. The 1926 Article 17 repeal, adoption of conciliation procedures, and failure to pass a comprehensive trade-union law remain distinct. A potentially over-specific assertion about a 1931 bill's legislative route was generalized rather than inventing a precise outcome.  
   Source: https://oisr-org.ws.hosei.ac.jp/research/dglb/explanation/exp_detail/?exp_id=4-012-9000765
5. **Statutory source windows strengthened.** The 1900 Public Peace Police Law source references National Archives file **御04291100**, the 1925 Election Law references **御15253100**, and the Peace Preservation Law references NDL Law No. 46 and National Archives **類01554100 item 005**. These are archival identifications, not claims to have translated or quoted their manuscripts verbatim.
6. **May Day source window strengthened.** Added an identified Ōhara Institute *retrospective historical chronology*, explicitly distinguished from a contemporaneous event program.
7. **Targeted narrative deepening.** Added direct analytical prose in §§69.16 (urban/rural organizing leverage), 69.27 (voter education versus political neutrality), 69.28 (distinct political rights), and 69.37 (metrics of reform success). No chapter boundaries or section titles changed.

## Verification of high-risk figures and dates, no unnecessary substitution

- **1900 Public Peace Police Law:** March 10, 1900, **Law No. 36**: https://hourei.ndl.go.jp/simple/detail?lawId=0000005996
- **1925 Peace Preservation Law:** April 22, 1925, **Law No. 46**: https://hourei.ndl.go.jp/simple/detail?lawId=0000019657
- **1925 Election Law:** May 5, 1925, **Law No. 47**: https://hourei.ndl.go.jp/simple/detail?lawId=0000019700
- **1928 election:** NDL reports Seiyūkai **217**, Minseitō **216**, proletarian parties **eight** seats. Initial/official versus later seat attribution can differ; the chapter retains approximate language: https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/cha3/description14.html ; https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/cha3/description15.html
- **1928 voter turnout:** JACAR chronology reports **12,408,678** eligible voters and **80.36%** turnout, while an Ōhara exhibit quotes approximately 12.53 million qualified voters; the chapter's rounded *roughly twelve million / around four-fifths* avoids false precision: https://www.jacar.go.jp/exhibition/universalsuffrage/chronology/ ; https://oisr-org.ws.hosei.ac.jp/english/public/posters_election/
- **1921 Kobe shipyard disputes:** a University of British Columbia thesis treats the Mitsubishi and Kawasaki workers' nearly 28,000 participants and the autonomy demands; the chapter's broad “tens of thousands” is supportable: https://files.libcom.org/files/1921%20Mitsubishi%20Kawasaki%20Strike.pdf
- **Tenancy disputes:** Kayo 1977-derived data in World Bank historical table: 1921 1,680; 1925 2,206; 1926 2,751. Chapter deliberately reports the trend rather than an uncited exact series: https://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/469971468771280762/pdf/multi-page.pdf
- **1922 Japan Farmers' Union:** founded April 9, 1922, with Sugiyama Motojirō and Kagawa Toyohiko, at Kobe; Ōhara chronology and academic history: https://oisr-org.ws.hosei.ac.jp/research/dglb/explanation/exp_detail/?exp_id=4-012-9000672
- **March 15, 1928 arrests:** circa 1,600, a supported approximation, not a total covering 1928–29: https://academic.oup.com/hawaii-scholarship-online/book/22054/chapter/182151960

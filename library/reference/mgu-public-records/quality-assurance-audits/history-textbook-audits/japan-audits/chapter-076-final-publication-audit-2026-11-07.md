### 3. Consolidated historical corrections and expansions

**Chronology and operational intent**

1. Corrected recovery dates of southern Chinese cities in §76.19: **Nanning, May 27, 1945; Liuzhou, June 29; Guilin, July 27**, with a separate explanation of the Chinese counteroffensive and spring 1945 western Hunan campaign. Documented by the Academy of Chinese Studies' surrender-and-victory historical chronology: https://mainuat.chiculture.org.hk/en/photo-story/3310. Distinguishes territorial recovery achieved *before* the final surrender from postwar handover.
2. Clarified the **January 17, 1944 Japanese planning decision**, the east/central/south-China railway and **forward airfield** objectives of Ichi-Gō, and its distinction from the **Chengdu-area B-29** base complex. Does not claim that Japan captured the Chengdu complex, Chongqing, or Kunming. Romanus and Sunderland, *Stilwell's Command Problems*: https://history.army.mil/portals/143/Images/Publications/catalog/9-2.pdf. Japanese postwar military archival record: JACAR ref `C16120281200`.
3. Corrected chronological narration so the **June 1944** B-29 strikes are not implied to have preceded Japanese planning in **January 1944**.

**Civilian losses and quantitative method**

4. Explained that the widely cited **more than 800,000 Yellow River flood deaths** concern the extended **1938–1947** environmental and social disaster, not a count of people who drowned immediately in June 1938. Consulted Micah S. Muscolino's research summary: https://disasterhistory.org/yellow-river-flood-1938-47.
5. Added the interplay of drought, locusts, grain requisitions, displacement, food prices and transport in the **1942–1943 Henan famine**; clarified that **approximately 1.5–2 million deaths** is one scholarly estimate, not a universal census. Supporting scholarly sources include Muscolino and Mark Baker: https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:2cb36d21-77a2-42bb-8a51-4a5ca5d1b86e.
6. Corrected the **Zhejiang–Jiangxi 1942** statement from an unqualified *civilian-only* quarter-million total to an explicitly attributed **combined soldiers-and-civilians casualty claim** in the Republic of China Ministry of National Defense campaign account. That number is not certified as an independently enumerated total of executed civilians: https://www.mnd.gov.tw/en/informationservices/publication/69596.
7. Preserved qualified discussion of the Nanjing Massacre and its diverse count definitions; **does not imply uncertainty about the occurrence of mass killing, sexual violence, or killing of disarmed troops**. The data study now explicitly distinguishes definitional and evidentiary uncertainty.
8. Kept the Chongqing tunnel disaster in context, distinguishing Japanese bombing from failures in crowded shelter management; did not insert an unwarranted exact toll.

**Deepened material**

9. Added explanation of Japanese and Chinese escalation incentives in 1937, limited civilian oversight, local diplomacy, military logistics, and Shanghai's costly attrition.
10. Strengthened Chinese government finance and interior relocation, Li Zongren's Xuzhou command, flood geography and aftermath, the decentralized Wuhan campaign, and political implications of the 1938 fall of cities.
11. Expanded occupation economics, collaboration choices, rural resistance and base-area governance, collective punishment, famine, refugee experiences, and the continued 1939–1943 land war.
12. Expanded Soviet and Allied supply channels, the Burma Road and Hump, conflicts among Chiang, Stilwell and Chennault, late-war battle intensity, the three phases of Ichi-Gō, the 1945 West Hunan counterattack, and the institutional crisis of receiving surrender in China.

### 4. Primary-source and reconstruction windows — exact limitations

1. **Marco Polo Bridge window** identifies **Ambassador Nelson T. Johnson's July 14, 1937 telegram no. 234**, FRUS 1937, vol. III, document 155, file 793.94/8773, https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1937v03/d155. The source explicitly expresses doubt about prior Japanese planning of the first incident but criticizes how the Japanese military exploited it. The window contains a **document-specific editorial paraphrase**.
2. **Nanjing Safety Zone window** identifies **John H. D. Rabe's December 15, 1937 letter to Tokuyasu Fukuda**, Yale Divinity Library, Bates Papers RG 10 Box 102 Folder 862, **NMP0146**, https://adhoc.yalepages.org/Collections/Nanking/findingaid.html. Specific subject: disarmed Chinese soldiers, fear for civilian safety, and the humanitarian appeal to Japanese officials. It is **not** a quantified death register, and the window avoids presenting it as one.
3. **Ichi-Gō window** is now **dual-sourced and expressly qualified**: the postwar Japanese First Demobilization Bureau's operational record, compiled 1947, **JACAR C16120281200**; and the U.S. Army Center of Military History's description of the January 17, 1944 planning decision in Romanus/Sunderland, *Stilwell's Command Problems*. The window **does not purport to reproduce the Japanese-language January 1944 order**. It is a source-identified editorial reconstruction using a postwar official Japanese record and a published historical account. JACAR metadata available at https://www.jacar.archives.go.jp/aj/search?fond=C16120279900.

**Archival scope limit:** The complete Japanese-language army order, all JACAR facsimile pages, full Nanjing document corpus, and original field statistical returns were **not comprehensively collation-checked**. Source windows are paraphrases rather than verbatim authenticated translations. The revised manuscript does not claim exhaustive primary-source certification.

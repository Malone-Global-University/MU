## Publication-critical historical proof edits made directly
1. **§63.18 Ashio:** Replaced unsupported `Daidō Danketsu` attribution with the 1906 Ashio branch of Dai-Nippon Rōdō Shiseikai; named Nagaoka Tsuruzō and Minami Sukematsu and distinguished February 4, 1907 disturbances from their organizational background. Primary historical reference: Hosei Ōhara Social Research Institute timeline (https://oisr-org.ws.hosei.ac.jp/research/dglb/explanation/exp_detail/?exp_id=4-012-9000363) and Yamakawa historical dictionary (https://www.historist.jp/word_j_a/entry/048845/).
2. **§63.21 railway workers:** The February 1898 strike preceded the formal Nippon Railway Reform Society / Nittetsu Kyōseikai; updated ~400 workers, Feb 24 start, negotiation until early April and union creation April 5. Sources: Hosei Ōhara chronology (https://oisr-org.ws.hosei.ac.jp/research/dglb/explanation/exp_detail/?exp_id=4-012-9000240) and Yamakawa entry (https://www.historist.jp/word_j_ni/entry/036007/). The outcome summary is internally consistent.
3. **§63.15 Hōjō:** Qualified the December 15, 1914 explosion's death toll because contemporary and later accounts report 671 or 687. Sources: Fukuchi municipal history and museum pages (https://www.town.fukuchi.lg.jp/fukuchi_trip/spot/1980.html) and local reporting (https://www.kinyobi.co.jp/news/?p=2863).
4. **§63.13 Besshi:** Dated the Shisakajima smelter start to January 1905 and clarified that pollution shifted and could spread back to Shikoku. Source: Sumitomo group archival history (https://www.sumitomo.gr.jp/english/history/history_tour/niihama_02/index.html).
5. **§63.14 and People and Places:** Corrected the 1888 Takashima exposé author romanization to **Matsuoka Kōichi** (松岡好一), rather than *Kōkichi*. Source: Yamakawa dictionary entry (https://www.historist.jp/word_j_ta/entry/034973/).

## Source-backed facts retained
- Ashio production made up a large fraction of Japanese copper output in the 1880s, broadly consistent with published mine history: https://d-arch.ide.go.jp/je_archive/society/wp_je_unu79.html .
- Chikuhō coal production was close to half of national output around 1900, supported by historical industrial studies: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/mooring-the-global-archive/burned-archive/1132408BC4109614FF84A3F6FD43E86A .
- Women were over 60% of industrial-period factory workers under the applicable historical count, with major differences by industry: https://d-arch.ide.go.jp/je_archive/english/society/book_unu_jpe9_d01.html .
- 1911 Factory Act: Law 46, March 28, coverage for generally 15+ workers or dangerous/unhealthy factories, minimum age 12 with specified 10+ exceptions, 12-hour daily standard for women and those under 15 with up to 2-hour permitted extensions, implemented September 1916. Primary statute: https://www.digital.archives.go.jp/file/141217 and https://jahis.law.nagoya-u.ac.jp/lawdb/l/144a0046 .
- Article 17 of the Public Peace Police Law removed in 1926; the overall law continued to 1945: https://oisr-org.ws.hosei.ac.jp/research/dglb/explanation/exp_detail/?exp_id=4-012-9000765 .
- Ashio pollution committees in 1897 and 1902 and the Yanaka 1906/1907 sequence: https://www.digital.archives.go.jp/fonds/en/2321538 and https://d-arch.ide.go.jp/je_archive/society/wp_je_unu84.html .

## B. Independent historical and documentary verification

The audit independently checked the source-sensitive questions Claude highlighted, using Ministry of Education material, National Diet Library legal and biographical research, Japanese religious institutional records, Kokugakuin's *Encyclopedia of Shinto*, Tenrikyō documents, the original-era translated Civil Code, and published accounts of Kishida Toshiko's speech. ### Source-backed corrections directly incorporated

1. **§64.2: 1898 Civil Code drafting committee.** Replaced erroneous **Hozumi Yatsuka** with **Hozumi Nobushige**. The three principal drafters were Hozumi Nobushige, Ume Kenjirō, and Tomii Masaakira. Source: National Diet Library, *Modern Japan and France*, “Establishment of the Civil Code,” <https://www.ndl.go.jp/france/en/part1/s1_3.html>; contemporary English translation and publisher identification <https://ecollections.law.fiu.edu/civil_codes/9/>.
2. **§64.7 and Source Window (Kishida Toshiko).** Identified *Hakoiri musume* (“Daughters in Boxes”) delivered **12 October 1883 at Ōtsu**, its police transcription and prosecution, and its appearance in *Jiyū Shinbun* on 20–21 November 1883. The source window now supplies document-level provenance, a historically specific **paraphrase** (not an invented translation), and a bibliographic pointer to Rebecca L. Copeland and Aiko Okamoto MacPhail's translation in *The Modern Murasaki* (2006), pp. 62–71. Sources: <https://documents.alexanderstreet.com/d/1001325826> and <https://speakingwhilefemale.co/womens-lives-kishida/>.
3. **§64.13 and Girls' High School Source Window.** Identified the Girls' High School Order as **promulgated 8 February 1899** and **implemented 1 April 1899**; the window now grounds its purpose in **Article 1**. Source: Japan Ministry of Education, <https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317331.htm>.
4. **§64.24: Konkōkyō and regulatory action.** Clarified that mediation was **suspended by government order in February 1873**, not an undocumented particular 1872 action. Its **1885** affiliation via the Shintō Konkō Kyōkai and **1900** independent recognition are independently supported. Sources: <https://konkokyo.jp/eng/our_foundation/history.html> and <https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=9816>.
5. **§64.25: Religious administration.** Added that the **Religions Bureau transferred to the Ministry of Education in June 1913**, while shrine affairs remained under the Home Ministry. Sources: <https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317483.htm>; <https://www.jacar.go.jp/english/exhibition/glossary_en/term3/0010-0030-0040-0020-0040.html>.
6. **Mobile HTML accessibility.** Appended limited chapter-only CSS and placed all **three tables** in keyboard-focusable, labeled, horizontally scrollable containers, addressing the original 320px document overflow while leaving approved model CSS and JS unchanged.

### Verified claims retained

- Girls' High School Order effective April 1899, and separate girls' secondary education: Japanese Ministry of Education, <https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317331.htm>.
- Expansion of primary attendance: MEXT reports **45.0% in 1887**, **95.6% in 1905**, **97.4% in 1907**; the chapter's rounded trend for 1890, 1900 and the 1910s is compatible with official figures: <https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317322.htm>.
- Tenrikyō's 1888 church recognition, 1896 restrictions and 1908 independent-sect recognition: <https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=9753>; <https://www.tenrikyo.or.jp/eng/history/613/>.
- Konkōkyō's 1885 affiliation and 1900 independence: <https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=9816>.
- Gunma implemented abolition of the licensed prostitution system in **1893**: National Diet Library exhibit <https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/download/digidepo_999364_po_84.pdf?contentNo=1>.
- Japan Woman's Christian Temperance/Correction society established in 1886 and expanded nationally in 1893: National Diet Library <https://www.ndl.go.jp/portrait/e/datas/6039/> and organization <https://en.kyofukai.jp/about-us/the-founding-of-kyofukai/>.
- Civil Code 1898 family/registration and spouse limitations checked against historical English edition, <https://ecollections.law.fiu.edu/civil_codes/9/>. The original code's divorce provisions are not interchangeable with current Japanese law.
- The early Kōminka/Okinawa and Ainu schooling discussion **does not assert unsourced exact school-attendance rates**; it appropriately distinguishes local differences and institutional incentives.

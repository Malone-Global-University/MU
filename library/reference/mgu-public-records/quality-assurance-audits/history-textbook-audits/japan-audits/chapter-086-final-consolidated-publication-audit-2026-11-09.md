## 4. Historical and documentary corrections applied

1. **RAA recruitment:** The Fukuoka Prefectural Library's published National Diet Library collaborative-reference investigation identifies Yomiuri morning-edition ads, p. 2, August 29 (office staff) and September 3/5, 1945 (special female workers), and Asahi Tokyo notices on August 29/31 (office staff). The RAA source window identifies these separately and presents only a marked editorial paraphrase. An exact Japanese facsimile transcription was not performed.
2. **RAA financing:** The popular ¥100 million figure is identified as not demonstrated by a collated disbursement ledger. Different figures for planned credit, actual borrowing, and spending are not merged into a false accounting total. The narrative notes other scholarship identifying smaller bank loans and preserves the more securely supported finding of public involvement.
3. **Abolition and compliance:** SCAPIN-642 (January 21, 1946) directed repeal of legal licensing and binding contracts; March 1946 restrictions on troop access are treated as a separate stage. **SCAPIN-1183 (September 6, 1946)** expressly criticized noncompliance with paragraph 3 of SCAPIN-642. The latter is included in chronology, main text, feature data and sources.
4. **RAA closure:** The March 27, 1946 troop off-limits date is attributed to scholarship, without asserting that every individual facility dissolved on that day.
5. **Tolerated districts:** Late-1946 management of red-line districts is distinguished from legalized licensed prostitution and from the *1956* Prostitution Prevention Act; later law is not backdated to occupation.
6. **U.S. war-bride legal chronology:** Identified Public Law **79-271** (Dec. 28, 1945); Public Law **80-213** (July 22, 1947; implementing regulations' Aug. 21 marriage cutoff); Public Law **81-717** (Aug. 19, 1950, with subsequent extensions); and the **1952** Immigration and Nationality Act. Marriage under Japanese law, military acknowledgment, entry permission, and naturalization are treated separately. U.S. Federal Register, Congressional materials and law review scholarship support the distinctions.
7. **Racial segregation:** Executive Order **9981 (July 26, 1948)** is dated precisely, while practical military desegregation is explained as gradual; Japanese partners' experiences are not reduced to an exclusively American narrative.
8. **BCOF and Australia:** The chapter distinguishes Australian-led BCOF's military restrictions and separate migration policy from American war-bride legislation. Australian War Memorial and National Archives materials are registered.
9. **Elizabeth Saunders Home:** The institution's own history dates its opening to **February 1, 1948** in Ōiso, initially receiving two infants. Its later formal corporation approval is treated as a different event. Not every mixed-parentage child is presumed abandoned or institutionalized.
10. **Forrester interview:** Densho's transcript places Tsuchino Forrester's first encounter with Michael approximately **1956–early 1957**, after the 1952 conclusion of formal occupation. The narrated military permission, marriage procedures and travel barriers are linked to the correct period. The earlier index-only presentation has been replaced with a transcript-based account, without fabricated dialogue.
11. **Sexual violence:** The chapter deliberately does **not** invent national rape totals or equate complaints, arrests, medical records and military convictions. Reporting barriers and jurisdictional limitations are analyzed.

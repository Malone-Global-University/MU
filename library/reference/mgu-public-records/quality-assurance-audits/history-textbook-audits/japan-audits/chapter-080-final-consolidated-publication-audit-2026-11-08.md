### II. Consolidated historical corrections and documentary verification

1. **1944 school evacuation (§80.5).** Corrected the 1944 original thirteen-city program by removing Kyoto and identifying Yokosuka among the original program cities. Japan's Ministry of Education identifies the original cities as Tokyo, Yokohama, Kawasaki, Yokosuka, Osaka, Kobe, Amagasaki, Nagoya, Moji, Kokura, Tobata, Wakamatsu, and Yahata. Kyoto's later inclusion belongs to 1945. The ministry records approximately **360,000** pupils placed in organized evacuation centers during the initial program, as opposed to children lodged independently with relatives. Exact source: https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317392.htm .
2. **Saotome Katsumoto (Everyday Life; People and Places).** Corrected life dates to **1932–2022** and age to **twelve on March 10, 1945**; included the documented survival/escape of Saotome with parents and two sisters and loss of his family home, with his published reminiscence and biographical reporting identified separately. His detailed escape route has **not** been embellished or reconstructed from unrelated witness accounts. Scholarly introduction: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/asia-pacific-journal/article/saotome-katsumoto-and-the-firebombing-of-tokyo-introducing-the-great-tokyo-air-raid/9112E398D68EE6E211462EA12778095D ; later biography: https://www.washingtonpost.com/obituaries/2022/05/12/katsumoto-saotome-firebombing-japan-dead/ .
3. **Okinawa (§80.14; Data Study).** Identified the **188,136** Japanese-side total as a prefectural reconstructed figure subdivided into **65,908 soldiers from outside Okinawa** and **122,228 Okinawa-prefecture residents**, the latter with approximately **28,228 Okinawan-born personnel/auxiliaries** and an estimated **94,000 other residents**. Clearly distinguished the **12,520 U.S. killed/missing** from Navy **4,907 killed** and from all U.S. casualties. Okinawan civilian totals can exceed 100,000 in alternative estimates; categories are not perfectly mutually stable across researchers. Sources: https://www.historipedia.jp/okinawa/en/okinawasen.html and https://www.history.navy.mil/news-and-events/news/2025/nhm-031825.html .
4. **Korean victims at Hiroshima (§80.16).** Removed an unattributed generic range. Identified *competing retrospective Korean reconstructions*, including a reported roughly **20,000** deaths associated with memorial traditions and approximately **30,000** deaths out of about **50,000** exposed in the Korean Atomic Bomb Victims Association's 1972 estimate. This is not a newly verified complete census. Source: https://www.oka.go.kr/attach/webzine/main/2023/eng/eng_2023_09_1.pdf . The primary Hiroshima total is approximately **140,000 ±10,000 through December 31, 1945**, not an immediate-death count: https://www.pref.hiroshima.lg.jp/site/hiroshimaforpeace-en/reconstruction-atomic-bombing.html .
5. **USSBS urban destruction (§80.9; Statistical feature and Source Window).** Replaced the imprecise claim of about two million *dwellings damaged* with the Survey's **2,094,374 buildings destroyed** in the **sixty-six-city** series, **8,324,000 persons rendered homeless/dehoused**, approximately **178 square miles** lost and **43%** of the studied built-up area; the report's separate **all-Japan** estimates are 2,250,000 buildings destroyed and 8,740,000 persons dehoused. These are not interchangeable denominators. Verified in USSBS, *The Effects of Air Attack on Japanese Urban Economy: Summary Report*, March 1947, Table 5 and comparative table, printed p. 7 (PDF p. 22): https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/NHC/NewPDFs/USAAF/United%20States%20Strategic%20Bombing%20Survey/USSBS%20Effects%20of%20Air%20Attack%20on%20Japanese%20Urban%20Economy%2C%20Summary%20Report.pdf .
6. **Nagasaki and comparative casualty categories.** Retained Nagasaki City's retrospective estimate of **73,884 dead** by the end of 1945 (from a 1950 research reconstruction); retained the distinction between Hiroshima's cumulative deaths through December and immediate deaths at the time of detonation. Official Nagasaki museum: https://nagasakipeace.jp/en/search/record/photo/part_2_02.html .
7. **§80.19 historical expansion.** Added approximately 390 words of substantive comparative historical explanation, not feature-box padding. The new analysis differentiates civilians' warning/escape opportunities in Tokyo, Okinawa and the two atomic bombed cities; institutional decisions from their measured consequences; the limits of counterfactual surrender claims; and the position of Korean, Okinawan and metropolitan Japanese civilians. No additional numbered historical sections or new repeated overview were introduced.

### III. Three primary-source windows and survivor attribution

**USSBS source window:** Edited to provide the *Summary Report (Pacific War)* (July 1, 1946) and the separately compiled *Urban Economy* report (March 1947) as directly identified archival sources. The Survey's judgments about hypothetical surrender scenarios are explicitly separated from empirical damage statistics. Source window prose remains an editorial paraphrase, not a verbatim extract.

**Himeyuri source window:** Replaced the previous unattributed generic assignment with a **named** recollection attributed to **Tsuneko Shiroma**, a Himeyuri student reported as fifteen during the battle. The specific episode concerns the conditions of nursing severely infected wounded soldiers in cave facilities, described in the Himeyuri Peace Museum's exhibition/guidebook and identified by Ryan M. Breeden in a report dated April 2, 2026. The museum's guidebook catalog and relevant editions are corroborated: https://www.himeyuri.or.jp/publish/ ; accessible secondary identification: https://www.stripes.com/living/pacific_travel/quick_trips/2026-04-02/himeyuri-peace-museum-travel-okinawa-21199269.html . **Disclosure:** the original testimony recording date, exact guidebook page number, original Japanese wording, and complete interview transcript were *not* independently collated. The revised window makes that mediation explicit and does **not** fabricate a direct quotation or imply the June 18 order was described by Shiroma in that specific excerpt. Himeyuri's mobilization and remembrance counts are independently contextualized with the museum's official resources.

**Setsuko Thurlow source window:** Retained the identified **November 17, 2014** testimony; inserted the direct National Peace Memorial Hall link, with age thirteen and distance approximately 1.8 km from Hiroshima's hypocenter, while keeping contemporary memories separate from later advocacy: https://www.global-peace.go.jp/en/picture/en_pic_syousai.php?dt=261002125537&gbID=755 . No invented first-person quotes.

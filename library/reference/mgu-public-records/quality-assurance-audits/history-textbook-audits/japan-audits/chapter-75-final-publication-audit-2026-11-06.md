## Historical changes and verification

1. **February 26 trial sentences:** Corrected the wrong assertion that 17 military officers received July 1936 death sentences. A July 7 U.S. diplomatic telegram records **13 officers and four civilians**, with other defendants receiving imprisonment or suspended terms; the later Kita Ikki/Nishida Mitsugi proceedings and executions are distinguished. Source: U.S. Department of State, *FRUS 1936*, July 7 telegram; National Diet Library's account of the February 26 Incident.
2. **Minobe affair:** Distinguished the two official government national-polity declarations on **August 3 and October 15, 1935**. The National Archives of Japan and contemporary U.S. diplomatic correspondence confirm the dates. The narrative now clarifies the emperor-as-organ argument, political attacks, official repudiation, and constitutional consequences. Note that one English-language NDL background page uses an inconsistent month for the second declaration; the primary National Archives chronology controls.
3. **Manchukuo administration:** Recast the first source window as a **two-source documentary comparison**, not an unverified invented verbatim 'April 1932 policy' transcription. Identifies the postwar Tribunal's discussion of military-level communications and the independently accessible 1934 U.S. diplomatic description of Japanese general-affairs control and the ambassador/Kwantung Army command overlap.
4. **Pingdingshan:** Kept the September 1932 killings as a documented act of colonial violence. A Chinese diplomatic report and an American consul's follow-up agree that unarmed villagers were killed, while the exact count remained contested. The chapter does not invent a certain casualty figure.
5. **Industrial planning and Ayukawa:** Separated formulation of Manchukuo's 1937–1941 industrial objectives around early 1937 from Nissan/Ayukawa's **later autumn/November 1937** corporate relocation and reorganization. The latter is explicitly flagged as forward context, beyond the core chronology.
6. **Settlement figures:** The 1936 target is **one million agricultural households over twenty years** (not completed settlers). The data study compares an identified secondary citation of **1,667 households during 1932–1935** (Sujin Lee citing a contemporary colonization yearbook) with **3,106 during 1932–1936** (Shinnosuke Tama's reproduced official compilation). It explains that periods and categories differ and explicitly does **not** compute a misleading short-term 'achievement percentage' against the twenty-year target.
7. **Political interpretation:** Expanded the roles and distinct interests of Puyi, Japanese administrators, Chinese officials, Koreans, Manchus, Mongols, Japanese settlers, the Kwantung Army, the Mantetsu corporate apparatus, the imperial court, military factions, civilian ministries and parliamentary parties. Distinguishes formal constitutional survival from growing military control over cabinet formation.
8. **Instructional fidelity:** All three source windows explicitly identified as paraphrase/source-criticism features, not verified word-for-word translations. The everyday-life feature remains identified as a composite, not an invented individual's testimony.

## Source limitations retained and disclosed

- **Original language collation:** The April 1932 army telegram originals, the complete original February 26 bulletin, and all original Japanese cabinet/ministry documents were not transcribed and collated word by word. Archive description and contemporary parallel records were checked where accessible.
- **Quantitative series:** The two early-settlement household counts derive from scholarly reporting of historical compilations, which may differ in categories and endpoints. The 1936–1937 million-household figure is a **policy target**, not a measured outcome.
- **Postwar sources:** Tokyo Tribunal findings are used as identified postwar judicial interpretations, not as interchangeable with contemporaneous proclamations or as substitutes for every original exhibit.
- **Geography:** Map-study exercises are text-directed; no authenticated period map plates were embedded. This is disclosed rather than represented as a finished facsimile atlas.
- **Further scholarship:** Monographs listed for scholarly background are not asserted to have been read cover-to-cover or page-verified. The source register distinguishes consulted accessible texts from bibliographic pointers.

These limitations are permissible for the intended MGU **first-edition scholarly textual HTML**, but this audit does not certify a critical documentary facsimile edition.

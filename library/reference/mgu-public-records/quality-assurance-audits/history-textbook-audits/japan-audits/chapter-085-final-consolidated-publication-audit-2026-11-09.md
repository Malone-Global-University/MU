## 4. Historical and documentary checks

**Confirmed institutional history and chronology:**

- The National Diet Library's constitutional chronology records the **Food May Day demonstration on May 19, 1946** and MacArthur's **May 20** response: https://www.ndl.go.jp/constitution/e/etc/history04.html
- The Japanese Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare documents the combined 1945–1946 food shortage, returnee pressure, public-assistance expansion, nutritional emergency, children's relief, and school meals. It also indicates that a National Nutrition Survey began in **1945**: https://www.mhlw.go.jp/www1/english/ssp_in_j/services/2nd.html
- The Japanese Ministry of Foreign Affairs dates the beginning of **GARIOA food and medical shipments to July 1946**: https://www.mofa.go.jp/policy/oda/white/2002/part2_09kiseki.html
- Shōwakan catalog item **S1H0701010651** identifies the Shinbashi station-market photograph as **February 13, 1946**, from U.S. NARA **SC-233700**, and records the **July 22, 1946** closure followed by continued station-area stalls: https://search.showakan.go.jp/search/photo/detail.php?id=90015672
- The 2017 architectural survey by **Kōsei Hatsuda, Shihori Murakami, and Masakazu Ishigure**, *Journal of Architecture and Planning* 82(733), 805–815, used municipal histories of **100 cities** and identified black markets in **99** of the cities studied. The chapter does not generalize the 99/100 result to every settlement in Japan: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aija/82/733/82_805/_article/-char/en
- **Kunio Aoki**, *Journal of Epidemiology* 18(2) (2008), 45–57, documents the development of postwar nutritional-dystrophia studies and allows close reading of the 1946 diet-survey figures: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4771577/
- The chapter's Tokyo ward/farmer energy comparison uses **May 1946: 1,351.9 versus 2,075.5 kcal/person/day; August: 1,827.8 versus 2,079.6; November: 2,051.4 versus 2,366.6**, with explicit sampling and interpretation cautions. The figures are not offered as a universal national starvation-rate estimate.
- GHQ/SCAP’s **May 1946 Summation of Non-Military Activities**, catalogued by JACAR as **B17070064000**, is referenced by its specific Part 2 rationing section: https://www.jacar.archives.go.jp/das/meta-en/B17070064000 . The archival page images have **not been exhaustively collated** against the chapter's editorial paraphrase.
- The Japanese press study is explicitly a **GHQ English translation/summary**, not an independently collated original Tokyo *Shimbun* article: https://collections.dartmouth.edu/archive/text/Press_Translations_Japanese/diplomatic/social-0173-diplomatic.html
- The documentary case study of **Yamaguchi Yoshitada** identifies his death as **October 11, 1947**, and qualifies the relationship between malnutrition, illness, professional ethics, and later memorial accounts: https://law.matsuyama-u.ac.jp/archives/5804
- The 2026 scholarly orphan-care study has been bibliographically updated to refer to the final journal issue while linking to the archived advance version: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/journalofjsis/advpub/0/advpub_JSIS1401_03/_article/-char/en

**Interpretive safeguards retained:** Food availability is distinguished from access and actual delivery. Informal exchange is distinguished from organized crime (reserved for Chapter 87). Japanese repatriate experiences are connected to imperial collapse (Chapter 81), without erasing Koreans, Taiwanese, Chinese, and other former colonial populations. Sexual relations and occupation-era gender arrangements are reserved for Chapter 86. Economic stabilization policy and the Reverse Course are briefly contextualized but primarily reserved for Chapter 89. Street children are not all described as orphans; statistical and photographic evidence are assessed with source-specific limits.

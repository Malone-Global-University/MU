## 3. Substantive historical corrections applied

### A. May 19–20 parliamentary confrontation

The Claude draft incorrectly placed police in the plenary chamber removing Socialist Diet members. The revised §92.14 instead locates the police-assisted clearance at the obstruction around Speaker Kiyose Ichirō's **office and route to the chamber**, followed by committee and plenary proceedings. The account also removes the incorrect claim that postwar police intervention in a Diet conflict had been unprecedented. National Diet Library history documents earlier confrontations in **1954 and 1956**. It identifies the extraordinary features of May 1960 as the combination of violent obstruction, expedited proceedings, treaty ratification, and resulting legitimacy controversy.

Contemporary documentary authority: [U.S. Embassy Tokyo to State Department, 20 May 1960, FRUS 1958–1960, vol. XVIII, doc. 155](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1958-60v18/d155). This dispatch also reports that **25 LDP legislators boycotted the treaty vote**. Supplementary source: [National Diet Library, *Parliament in Brawl and Lament*](https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/column/09.html). The American diplomatic report is identified as contemporary but interested, requiring comparison with Japanese parliamentary and press sources.

### B. 1993 and LDP parliamentary majority

The draft incorrectly described **1993** as the first loss of a lower-house majority. The revised opening, §92.19, and its added election evidence table distinguish **seats won**, **an outright single-party majority**, and **control of the prime ministership/cabinet**. Corrected reference examples include **249/511 (1976)**, **248/511 (1979)**, and **250/511 (1983)**; the LDP kept governing through arrangements and parliamentary support. **1993** is appropriately described as the **first loss of national governing power since the LDP's formation in 1955**, following party splits and an opposition coalition. The evidence table likewise preserves the independently confirmed **287/467 (1958)** and **296/467 (1960)** election-night figures. Cross-check material: [Congressional Record, 25 Nov. 1980, Appendix 5](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1980-pt23/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1980-pt23-6.pdf); [historical electoral data compilation](https://www.pifsinternational.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Japan-US-2009-Symposium.pdf).

### C. 1970 treaty status

§92.19 now explains that the treaty **continued without a new ratification** in 1970. Under **Article X**, after the initial ten years either government could terminate it on one year's notice; otherwise it remained in force. Authority: [Japanese Ministry of Defense, official treaty text](https://www.mod.go.jp/en/j-us-alliance/joint-declaration/treaty/index.html).

### D. Confidential understandings and 2010 review

§92.12 now distinguishes confidential arrangements concerning possible U.S. operations **in a Korean emergency** from disputes about the interpretation of nuclear-weapons **introduction, naval transit, and port calls**. The Japanese Foreign Ministry's review findings were published **March 9, 2010**; declassified U.S. records reveal relevant U.S. understandings, but the text avoids asserting identical Japanese and American interpretations where this remains disputed. Authorities: [Japanese Diplomatic Bluebook 2010](https://www.mofa.go.jp/policy/other/bluebook/2010/html/h1/h1_01.html); [National Security Archive, U.S.–Japan military alliance documentary primer](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/japan/2019-06-28/us-japan-military-alliance-documents-primer).

### E. Kōenkai chronology and contested retrospective testimony

§92.6 recognizes antecedents in the **early 1950s** followed by growth and diffusion in the late 1950s and 1960s. §92.16 retains the reported possible SDF deployment debate **only as a later attributed account** and explicitly disclaims that it is a verified cabinet order. The July 14, 1960 stabbing of Kishi and the separate formal cabinet resignation/succession chronology were retained.

### F. Photograph attribution and crowd statistics

The four document windows now have direct collection/document locators. Hamaya's image references include catalogue identifiers **anp7156** and **anp7165**, with the proper caution that the identification of the latter as Kanba Michiko is **probable rather than conclusively established** in MIT's interpretive materials. The chapter consistently distinguishes organizer, police, and other demonstration/strike estimates and avoids treating them as the same measurement. Source: [MIT Visualizing Cultures, Hamaya collection](https://visualizingcultures.mit.edu/tokyo_1960/hamaya/index.htm); [MIT, June 15 analysis](https://visualizingcultures.mit.edu/tokyo_1960/anp2_essay05.html).

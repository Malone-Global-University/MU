## Historical corrections, source-by-source
1. **§68.12, Nagoya, August 9–10:** The earlier draft compressed the first two nights and incorrectly assigned large speeches and street unrest to the first evening. Nagoya City Archives describes approximately 300–400 people gathering and dispersing on August 9, followed by a much larger crowd, speeches, confrontations, and differing attendance estimates on August 10. Final text distinguishes both nights and cites the municipal *Temporary Incident File (Rice Price Surge)*, volume ID 7480 (A-So T230). Source: https://nagoya-city.note.jp/n/n4826709fd3a4?hl=en .
2. **§68.25, reporting ban and White Rainbow Incident:** Correctly establishes the Home Ministry's August 14 reporting prohibition, the August 17 and August 25 Osaka press meetings, and the August 26-dated *Osaka Asahi* edition. Date differences between edition dates and publication dates explicitly acknowledged. Source: https://pub-ucpec2-prd.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/view?anchor.id=0&brand=ucpress&chunk.id=d0e4604&doc.view=content&docId=ft5q2nb407&toc.depth=1 ; NDL reference service https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R000000006-I1000189450 .
3. **§68.29, succession:** Fixed misleading claims that Saionji was already 'the last' genrō in 1918 and that Yamagata and Saionji promptly embraced party cabinet rule. Yamagata first wanted Saionji to return and only reluctantly accepted Hara after Saionji declined. Refined 'commoner' to first prime minister without peerage rank and first sitting lower-house member to hold office. Source: NDL, https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/cha3/description08.html , Matsumoto Gokichi's *Political Daily*, no. 38.
4. **§68.30, post-riot social policy:** Corrected the claim that the Home Ministry relief investigation body was formed after the riots. The Relief Work Investigation Council was established **June 1918**, before July–August unrest; the riots contributed pressure but did not create the council. Source: National Council of Social Welfare historical chronology https://shakyo.or.jp/tsuite/gaiyo/history/chronology.html .
5. **§68.30, Rice Law:** Verified **1921 Rice Law**, with governmental acquisition/storage and later sales, differentiated from the stronger price-adjustment tools introduced by amendment in **1925**. Source: Japan Ministry of Agriculture historical rice-trading document https://www.maff.go.jp/j/council/seisaku/syokuryo/0506/pdf/ref_data4.pdf , PDF page 2.
6. **§68.31–32, historiography:** Verified Naoko Tomie, “Two Notions of ‘Right to Live’ in 1918 Rice Riots: Moral Economy and Citizenship,” *Journal of Welfare Sociology* 14 (2017): 95–119, DOI `10.11466/jws.14.0_95`. Clarified distinction between contemporary local demands and later intellectual interpretations of citizenship. Source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jws/14/0/14_95/_article/-char/en .
7. **§§68.6 and 68.9, Toyama:** Clarified women’s participation in transportation of rice according to the Japan Center for Asian Historical Records; preserved qualified treatment of local labor and seasonal absences. Distinguished the July 22 discussion, July 23 Uozu action, and August 3 Nishimizuhashi disturbance. Sources: https://jacar.go.jp/learning/term.html?uid=Y40C100060 ; Michael Lewis, *Rioters and Citizens*; https://uozu-kanko.jp/en/history/ .
8. **§§68.19 and 68.24, mining regions:** Added local chronology and distinctions between wage demands, company rice supply, property attacks, military deployment, and lethal force; avoided an unsupported universal death toll. Source: Hōsei University Ōhara Institute for Social Research, https://oisr-org.ws.hosei.ac.jp/research/dglb/explanation/exp_detail/?exp_id=4-012-9000573 .
9. **§68.13, Kyoto:** Confirmed outbreak August 10, troops and relief measures from official Kyoto chronology. Source: Kyoto Prefectural Archives https://www.archives.kyoto.jp/websearchpe/detail?cls=371_chronicle&pkey=0000035817 .
10. **§68.15, Kobe:** Confirmed Suzuki Shōten destruction August 12; Den Kenjirō's August 13 diary and Terauchi's reported earlier ill health. Source: NDL https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/cha3/description06.html .

## Archival source-window improvements and limits
- **Den diary**: National Diet Library *Papers of Den Kenjirō* no. 24; August 13, 1918, shown and transcribed in NDL *Modern Japan in Archives* chapter 3-6. This is an identified primary document; chapter uses description rather than invented translation.
- **Army deployment**: National Archives of Japan *各地軍隊出動ニ関スル報告ノ件*, volume **纂01428**; https://www.archives.go.jp/ayumi/kobetsu/t07_1918_02.html . The archival volume identifier is provided, without inventing a sub-item call number.
- **Kyoto**: Kyoto Prefectural Archives historical chronology entry **0000035817**. This is an archival index/chronology and is **not** passed off as a verbatim 1918 police report. A separate original police document call number was not independently established.
- **Mining**: Hōsei Ōhara Institute historical research guide, plus National Archives December 1939 Home Ministry *Overview of the So-Called 1918 Rice Riots*, retrospective official call no. **返青04011020**, https://www.digital.archives.go.jp/file/1724330.html . A retrospective description is not portrayed as a contemporary eyewitness account.
- **Rice-price source**: The lesson cites the Bank of Japan long-run compilation, but reproduces no invented price point; a specific standalone 1918 statistical table or original ledger is not verified in this audit. The feature remains a documented source-selection and critical-reading exercise, rather than a reproduced table.
- **Toyama newspapers**: The teaching window remains a generic archival exercise because a precise contemporary Toyama issue was not independently authenticated in this pass. Explicitly labeled genre-level paraphrase, not falsely presented as a quotation from an identified issue.
- **Kure, fishing households and seasonal migration**: Where original testimony or local archival records have not been checked, the narrative retains explicit evidential qualifications rather than invented detail.

## 3. Approved 20-section plan compliance
**Division I §§94.1–94.4:** The economic geography of *kōgai*; the May 1956 official discovery and November 1956 University of Kumamoto heavy-metal finding; exposure, congenital disease, Chisso and delayed government action; patient organizing and the first 1973 judgment. The text separates evidence available at the time from retrospective scientific conclusions.

**Division II §§94.5–94.9:** Niigata and the Agano River; cadmium and Itai-itai disease; Yokkaichi asthma and joint responsibility of six firms; grassroots monitoring and resident organizations; fourteen measures at the 1970 Pollution Diet, 1971 Environment Agency, and 1973/1974 compensation law. Geographic and legal differences across the cases are developed rather than collapsed.

**Division III §§94.10–94.13:** Electoral rights granted in December 1945, first exercised in April 1946, and separate constitutional development in 1947; employment and household inequalities; women’s grassroots organizations; Tanaka Mitsu and *ūman ribu*; and the three parts of the unsuccessful May 1972 Eugenic Protection Law amendment.

**Division IV §§94.14–94.17:** Historical-status discrimination rather than misclassification as ethnicity; Suiheisha and the Buraku Liberation League; the **fundamental human-rights framework** of the 1965 Dōwa report and the 1969 law; contested Sayama case and distinction between advocacy allegations and court findings; Zainichi postcolonial legal status, education, and employment; the 1974 Hitachi/Pak Jong-seok case with Japanese-alias/documentation context.

**Division V §§94.18–94.20:** Ainu histories of colonization, cultural survival and material inequality; Ainu Association (1946), Utari name change (1961), 1972 activism and survey, 1974 welfare measures; differentiation between welfare advocacy and Indigenous recognition; and a comparative synthesis emphasizing shared questions without erasing distinct identities and claims.

**Chapter boundaries:** Chapter 91 provides high-growth economic institutions; Chapter 92 covers LDP politics; Chapter 93 provides militant labor, student and antiwar protest histories; Chapter 94 addresses environmental, feminist and minority-community rights; Chapter 95 covers culture; Chapter 96 addresses later economic restructuring. Neighboring chapters are referenced selectively without excessive duplication.

## 4. Historical factual corrections, independently checked
1. **Women's franchise.** The original implied the 1947 Constitution itself introduced suffrage. The revised text distinguishes the December 17, 1945 electoral reform, April 10, 1946 voting, and May 1947 constitutional equality. National Diet Library: https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/cha5/description05.html
2. **Minamata medical chronology.** Official discovery May 1, 1956; Kumamoto University November 3, 1956 heavy-metal finding; September 1968 government causation finding; March 20, 1973 judgment. The revision avoids implying that serious evidence first appeared in 1968. National Institute for Minamata Disease: https://nimd.env.go.jp/archives/english/minamata_disease_in_depth/cause_investigation/ ; Minamata research chronology: https://gkbn.kumagaku.ac.jp/minamata/en/marchives/chronology/page/3/
3. **Four pollution lawsuits.** Niigata September 1971; Itai-itai June 1971 and August 1972 appeal; Yokkaichi July 24, 1972, against six firms; Kumamoto March 1973. The revised Yokkaichi section explains multi-emitter responsibility and population-health evidence. Yokkaichi municipal museum: https://guidance.city.yokkaichi.mie.jp/kogaimirai/en/2501.html ; Environmental Restoration and Conservation Agency: https://www.erca.go.jp/fukakin/y_tebiki/situgi/q03.html
4. **Pollution Diet.** Fourteen environmental laws/amendments in 1970 and an important change in the framing of economic harmony; 1971 Environment Agency and 1973 legislation effective in 1974. Ministry of Environment: https://www.env.go.jp/en/water/wq/wemj/history.html ; https://www.env.go.jp/earth/coop/coop/document/apctm_e/01-apctme.pdf
5. **1972 reproductive-law proposal.** The revised account specifies proposed elimination of economic grounds, a disability-selective clause, and eugenic guidance. It distinguishes feminist and disability-rights concerns and states the proposal did not pass. Masae Kato (2009): https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt46n2d4
6. **Dōwa.** The revised §94.15 and source study recognize the 1965 report as a statement concerning **fundamental human rights**, social responsibility, and material improvement rather than merely an infrastructure plan. BLHRRI: https://blhrri.org/old/blhrri_e/article/20110303/article1.htm
7. **Hitachi.** The revised §94.17 and source study explain Pak Jong-seok’s application using a Japanese name, subsequent disclosure and withdrawn job offer, employer allegations concerning application information, and the June 19, 1974 Yokohama ruling. Legal digest: https://www.zenkiren.com/Portals/0/html/jinji/hannrei/shoshi/00167.html ; historical account: https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-topics/c13902/keeping-the-zainichi-memories-alive-grandmothers%E2%80%99-lives-etched-in-the-kawasaki-landscape.html
8. **Ainu chronology.** The 1946 Ainu association, 1961 name change, 1972 Ainu liberation organization and survey, and fiscal 1974–1980 Utari welfare period are differentiated. Hokkaido University: https://www.cais.hokudai.ac.jp/english/pdf/4ainu_8-appen.pdf ; Foundation for Ainu Culture: https://www.ff-ainu.or.jp/web/learn/culture/together/files/rekishi_bunka_english.pdf
9. **Sayama.** The historical treatment separates court outcomes, defense assertions, and movement interpretation. Ishikawa's March 2025 death is mentioned as a limited later-history note, not treated as a 1970s outcome. IMADR advocacy account: https://imadr.org/05-23-sayama/

These checks were targeted. No blanket certification of every scholarly interpretation or every original-language record is implied.

## 5. Documentary study and source classification
Five source-guided documentary windows are integrated after §§94.2, 94.9, 94.12, 94.15 and 94.17, covering (a) the 1956 Kumamoto research finding through a later NIMD institutional chronology; (b) the 1970 Pollution Diet through a dated government historical report; (c) Tanaka Mitsu's 1970 manifesto via published scholarship; (d) the 1965 Dōwa report through identified reproductions and interpretive sources; and (e) the 1974 Hitachi legal case through a named legal digest and historical record.

All windows explicitly say **editorial paraphrase, not independently verified quotation or translation**. Each gives document identity, date or historical period, creator or institution, provenance, interpretation, evidentiary limitations, and four student source-analysis questions. The 21-entry research register provides direct source/catalog links and flags advocacy, retrospective-government, legal-digest, and scholarship perspectives. **There are no original-text facsimiles or page-collated Japanese translations embedded.** Students need the named underlying edition to quote precisely; the windows are source-guided studies, not authenticated transcriptions.

Ainu documentary interpretation is supported in the narrative and source register, without conflating distinct Utari policy documents with the 1974 Hitachi judgment as the Claude draft had done.

## Major historical corrections and verified anchors

1. **Minister eligibility:** the active-duty-only restriction introduced in 1900 was relaxed in **1913** and restored in **1936**, not in force throughout 1931. [U.S. Foreign Relations, Grew 1936](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1936v04/d775).
2. **London Naval Treaty:** signed **April 22, 1930**, ratified in Japan **October 2, 1930**. [National Diet Library](https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/cha3/description17.html).
3. **Manchurian Incident:** Japanese Kwantung Army officers staged the Liutiaohu explosion September 18, 1931; official allegations of Chinese sabotage are treated as propaganda claims, not balanced factual alternatives. [National Diet Library](https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/cha4/description01.html) and [JACAR C14030000600](https://www.jacar.archives.go.jp/aj/search-en?fond=C14030000100).
4. **Nonexpansion and troop crossing:** the unauthorized Korean Army crossing of the Yalu September 21 and the subsequent funding/regularization are distinguished from prior sanction, while genuine temporary attempts at restraint are acknowledged. [Specialist historical treatment](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-19-9593-4_6).
5. **Chinese and Korean experience:** Chinese sovereignty, Zhang Xueliang’s position, local resistance, Chinese civilian displacement, and distinct Korean settler/independence interests are integrated without fabricated eyewitness testimony.
6. **Political assassinations:** February 9 and March 5 Blood-Pledge murders distinguished from the May 15 attack; the naval defendants' November 1933 sentences are specified from [Grew's dispatch](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1933v03/d771).
7. **Imperial and cabinet appointments:** NDL May 19 Saionji memorandum description identifies Hirohito's objection to a fascist-leaning successor; Saionji recommended Saitō on May 22; Saitō cabinet formed May 26. [National Diet Library](https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/cha4/description02.html).
8. **Manchukuo:** proclaimed March 1, 1932 and **formally recognized by Japan September 15, 1932** via a bilateral protocol, which is analyzed as an assertion of independent status by parties to the agreement rather than proof of freely expressed Chinese consent. [Published protocol](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1932v04/d271).
9. **International dates:** Lytton Commission report in autumn 1932; League Assembly decision February 24, 1933; withdrawal notified March 27, 1933; withdrawal effective **March 27, 1935**. [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/2021666890/) and [United Nations Treaty Collection](https://treaties-prod.un.org/Pages/Content.aspx?path=DB%2FLoNOnline%2FpageIntro_en.xml).

## Documentary limits and source windows

The three windows are **document-specific analytical exercises, not purported verbatim translations**:

- Kwantung Army proclamation: JACAR catalog description and available transcribed excerpt consulted; seven-image original not fully transcribed.
- Harada September 23, 1931 record: NDL exhibition description and metadata consulted; manuscript wording and later revisions not collated.
- Saionji May 19, 1932 memorandum: NDL exhibition description consulted; original handwriting not transcribed.

The published Lytton Report record was reviewed but all pages and maps were **not** collated. The everyday-life feature is explicitly a bounded synthesis of circumstances, **not invented firsthand testimony**. Scholarly works accessed through abstracts or catalogs are labeled as such in the Source Register. Full archival-text collation remains a valid objective for a later scholarly edition, not an undisclosed claim of this first edition.

The Geography Study is text-guided and does not embed an archival cartographic plate. A further edition can add properly sourced plates and facsimiles without changing the first-edition narrative.

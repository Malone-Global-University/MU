## 3. Historical and documentary corrections incorporated

1. **§67.6 source window chronology.** January **1916** Yoshino Sakuzō essay originally referred to Ōkuma's replacement by Terauchi as though already accomplished. Corrected: Terauchi's cabinet did not begin until **October 1916**. The 1913 political crisis, Siemens scandal and Japan's initial wartime expansion remain correct preceding context. NDL author and publication record: https://www.ndl.go.jp/portrait/e/datas/348
2. **§67.20, National Suiheisha founding names.** Corrected nonexistent/misidentified **“Komai Kisaburō”** to **Komai Kisaku** (駒井喜作), alongside Saikō Mankichi (西光万吉) and Sakamoto Seiichirō (阪本清一郎). Founding date **March 3, 1922, Kyoto** retained. The historic distinction between Suiheisha self-emancipation and official improvement schemes is preserved. Historical reference: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%A5%BF%E5%85%89%E4%B8%87%E5%90%89-1076587 ; Hosei Ohara Institute: https://oisr-org.ws.hosei.ac.jp/research/dglb/explanation/exp_detail/?exp_id=4-012-9000668
3. **§67.23, Taiwan political chronology.** Original incorrectly said the movement beginning in 1921 “grew out of the Taiwan Cultural Association” without observing that the **first Taiwan parliamentary petition was submitted January 30, 1921**, whereas the **Taiwan Cultural Association was founded October 17, 1921**. Corrected to identify the earlier petition movement and subsequent Cultural Association support. Taiwan Legislative Yuan historic summary: https://daap.ly.gov.tw/tw/daap/125-3993.html ; Taiwan history foundation: https://www.twcenter.org.tw/thematic_series/history_class/tw_window/e02_20011022
4. **§67.13, regional elections.** Replaced vague references to delayed regional incorporation with substantiated dates: **Hokkaido gained limited lower-house representation in 1902** (initially three urban districts); **Okinawa gained representative districts in 1912**, covering Naha, Shuri and certain parts of Okinawa Island. Do not confuse a prefecture's establishment with its first national election. JACAR government's suffrage chronology: https://www.jacar.go.jp/exhibition/universalsuffrage/chronology/
5. **§67.18, legal precision.** Clarified that the **1922** Public Peace Police Law amendment removed the ban on women's attendance at and sponsorship of political meetings, **not** the ban on membership of political associations or the electoral franchise. National Archives of Japan reproduces Article 5: https://www.archives.go.jp/learning/archive_collection_2/collection_1/
6. **§67.19, youth associations.** Anchored the previously imprecise “mid-1910s” ministry guidance to **1915** and the national **Dai Nippon Rengō Seinendan** federation to **1925**. Distinguishes ministry supervision, local autonomy and nationally coordinated associations; does not treat all youth groups as autonomous democratic organizations. Background report: https://www.kendo.or.jp/wp/wp-content/themes/kendo/assets/library/pdf/seinendan-report.pdf
7. **§67.3, Katsura's rescript.** Identified the **December 22, 1912 imperial rescript** securing naval-minister participation in Katsura's third cabinet, rather than leaving the act undated. J. Charles Schencking, *War in History* (1998), https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/096834459800500303
8. **§67.12, primary-source traceability.** Preserved the Hara Takashi diary's accurate identification as a published source collection; added a **separate, dated and catalogued** comparison: Matsumoto Gokichi, *Political Daily*, vol. 1, **September 8–17, 1918**, Matsumoto Papers #38, National Diet Library. The final carefully distinguishes Matsumoto's authorship from Hara's. NDL: https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/cha3/description08.html
9. **§67.29, source-window integrity.** Claude's “January 1924 joint statements” were generic descriptions, with no identified issue, resolution, manuscript, or archive identifier. Retained the window's instructional purpose but anchored it instead to **Mizuno Rentarō's January 1924 manuscript notes on Kiyoura's cabinet formation**, Mizuno Papers #53, National Diet Library entrusted collection. Updated sidebar and study questions, and labeled the text explicitly as an *archive description and analysis prompt*, **not a fabricated transcription or paraphrase of unexamined manuscript wording**. NDL: https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/cha3/description12.html

**1924 election totals:** Not inserted. Contemporary, secondary and later compilations disagree in reported party-seat distributions, often due to alignments, classification and timing. The authorial manuscript deliberately states only the reliably established electoral outcome: the three pro-constitutional parties secured a majority, Kenseikai led the largest party, Kiyoura resigned, and Katō formed the June 1924 cabinet. NDL: https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/cha3/description12.html . A precise seat table would require reconciliation against an authoritative contemporaneous return, not forced insertion of inconsistent web totals.

**Historical claims independently reviewed:** The two constitutional-protection movements, Yoshino's 1916 essay, 1919 voting-tax reform, Hara's appointment in September 1918, women's 1922 participation amendment, Suiheisha 1922 foundation, first 1921 Taiwan petition, 1924 party cabinet, and dual 1925 enactments are supported by institutional or documentary sources.

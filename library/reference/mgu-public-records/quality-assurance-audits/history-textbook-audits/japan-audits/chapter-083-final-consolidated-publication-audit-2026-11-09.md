## 3. Historical and documentary accuracy review

The chapter distinguishes **legal findings**, **prosecution allegations**, **defense claims**, **later scholarship**, and **ongoing historical debate**. Particular attention was given to the following frequently misrepresented issues:

1. **IMTFE establishment and legal categories:** SCAP proclamation of January 19, 1946; Charter as amended on April 26; charter distinctions between crimes against peace, conventional war crimes, and crimes against humanity, and the issue of retroactive criminalization. Primary institutional reference: [UN reproduction of the IMTFE Charter](https://www.un.org/sites/un2.un.org/files/doc.3_1946_tokyo_charter.pdf).
2. **Defendant accounting and sentencing:** the original indictment named 28 defendants; two died and one became unable to stand trial; **25 were convicted**; **seven death sentences** were imposed and carried out December 23, 1948; 16 received life terms, with Tōgō Shigenori sentenced to 20 years and Shigemitsu Mamoru to seven years. Contemporary U.S. diplomatic account: [FRUS, 1948, Volume VI, document 613](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1948v06/d613).
3. **Hirohito and non-indictment:** the chapter avoids confusing non-prosecution with an adjudication of innocence. It explains occupation policy, contested responsibility, and the historical interpretation of MacArthur's January 1946 recommendations. Starting records: the cited contemporary MacArthur correspondence and National Diet Library occupation-era holdings, identified in the chapter source register.
4. **Trial evidence and procedures:** the chapter evaluates the evidentiary treatment of Nanjing, prisoners of war, command responsibility and the limitations of translation and documentary representation, without implying that the IMTFE by itself tried every atrocity or actor.
5. **Courts outside Tokyo:** American, British, Australian, Dutch, Chinese and other regional trials are treated as distinct legal systems rather than as branches of the IMTFE. The [U.S. Supreme Court's *In re Yamashita* decision](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/327/1) is used for the procedural and command-responsibility problem.
6. **Colonial subjects and gendered violence:** coverage of the Korean former colonial subject Lee Hak-rae and the Dutch-prosecuted Semarang case is explicitly situated in the limits of trial jurisdiction and survivor access; the cases are not generalized into invented collective experiences.
7. **Unit 731 and selective immunity:** explanations distinguish evidence of biological-warfare research, U.S. intelligence interests and non-prosecution from claims of a fully established comprehensive immunity arrangement for all participants.
8. **Dissents and contested justice:** separate judicial opinions of Pal, Bernard, Röling, Webb and Jaranilla are described individually; Pal's legal objections are not misrepresented as proof that wartime atrocities did not occur.
9. **Post-trial obligation:** Article 11 of the [San Francisco Peace Treaty](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/UNTS/Volume%20136/volume-136-I-1832-English.pdf) is described as Japan's acceptance of tribunal judgments and obligation concerning sentences, not as a bar to historical inquiry.

**Primary Source Windows:** each has a clearly marked **editorial paraphrase**, source provenance, historical interpretation and limitations, and four student questions. No paraphrase is presented as a verified verbatim excerpt or an independently collated original-language translation.

## 4. Source register and traceability

The HTML includes **34 linked documentary, institutional, archival, legal and scholarly source entries**. Representative authoritative starting points include the UN's charter reproduction, the Library of Congress tribunal judgment, the U.S. State Department's *Foreign Relations of the United States* series, the U.S. National Archives records for Far East war-crimes prosecutions, the U.S. Supreme Court's *Yamashita* opinion, and the 1951 peace treaty. Specialized studies substantiate the emperor-policy debate, interpreting and translation, colonial subjects, gendered violence, and the broader regional trials.

The source register is **traceable**, not a claim of full page-by-page or archival-facsimile collation. Institution and bibliographic identities were checked against accessible catalog or research records as available; publisher and library links sometimes identify a work rather than provide its full text. A future critical scholarly edition could add archive call numbers, page-specific footnotes and primary-language facsimile references. Their absence is disclosed rather than concealed.

## 6. Boundaries, caveats and final disposition

- The book is a first-edition **scholarly historical textbook in HTML**, not a documentary critical edition of every trial record.
- No exhaustive Japanese-, English-, Dutch-, Chinese- or Korean-language facsimile collation was undertaken; cited source windows are accurately identified as editorial paraphrases.
- The Geography Study is a text-guided historical atlas exercise; there is no embedded map plate.
- The citations and evidence register permit verification and extension but are not equivalent to a complete page-specific critical apparatus.
- The chapter maintains clear boundaries with Chapter 82 (occupation governance) and Chapter 84 (constitution and reform), focusing on criminal prosecution, legal responsibility, selected non-prosecution and historical memory.

**Decision: APPROVED FOR PUBLICATION — LOCKED** for the first-edition scholarly historical-text HTML series. The existing document meets the established twenty-section structural plan, direct narrative-depth target, instructional requirements, historical-accuracy standard subject to explicitly stated research limitations, and responsive-web testing. No additional micro-revision cycle is recommended in the absence of newly discovered publication-critical evidence.

## 3. Consolidated historical corrections applied

### 3.1. §93.2 — the 1953 Mitsui proposal

**Corrected:** approximately 6,739 was a corporate personnel-reduction/restructuring proposal including voluntary retirements and other measures, **not 6,739 workers already dismissed**. The Ōhara Institute chronology additionally reports 3,464 designated dismissals and 1,815 reinstatements and describes a **113-day campaign** using partial stoppages and other industrial tactics. The wording no longer suggests one continuous, total 113-day strike.

**Verification:** Ōhara Institute for Social Research, *Social and Labor Movement Great Chronology*, `https://oisr-org.ws.hosei.ac.jp/research/dglb/explanation/exp_detail/?exp_id=4-012-9001756`.

### 3.2. §93.3 — the 1959–1960 Miike dispute

**Corrected:** **1,278 notices** on December 10, 1959 versus **1,210 dismissals** on December 16; company lockout January 25, 1960; **second union founded March 17**; fatal stabbing of first-union member **K. Kubo March 29**; larger hopper standoff principally June–July rather than conflated with late March. The ILO record's descriptions of the alleged assailant are labeled complainants' allegations, **not independently adjudicated findings**.

**Mediation sequence:** initial Nakayama proposal November 21, 1959; Fujibayashi second proposal April 6, 1960; third proposal August 10; union-side acceptance September 6; settlement implementation and ending of the commonly described **282-day strike** by early November. Avoided presenting mediation as originating only in the fall.

**Verification:** Michio Nitta, “Labour Relations Commissions and Industrial Relations: The Era of Great Conciliators,” *Japan Labor Review* 12(4), 2015, pp. 47–50, `https://www.jil.go.jp/english/JLR/documents/2015/JLR48_nitta.pdf`; ILO freedom-of-association complaint, `https://normlex.ilo.org/dyn/nrmlx_en/f?p=1000:50002:0::NO:50002:P50002_COMPLAINT_TEXT_ID:2898820`.

### 3.3. §93.9 — the Nihon University accounting controversy

**Qualified:** approximately **¥2 billion in unaccounted-for funds**, rather than the numerical discovery by itself being treated as proof of a specific adjudicated tax violation. The section retains the distinction between financial controversy and campus-governance grievances. The figure and general event correspond to contemporary and institutional reporting.

### 3.4. §93.10 — University of Tokyo chronology

**Corrected:** the cancellation decision concerning the **1969 entrance examinations was dated December 29, 1968**, **before** the January 18–19, 1969 police clearance of Yasuda Auditorium. The chapter identifies the January 10, 1969 agreement involving a seven-faculty delegation without Zenkyōtō; approximately **8,500 police** and **819 student arrests** are attributed to Patricia Steinhoff's reconstruction. Relevant chronology prose is aligned to this sequence. The June 15 and June 17, 1968 first occupation/police intervention were retained after corroboration with reporting reproduced by *The Japan Times*.

**Verification:** Patricia G. Steinhoff, “Student Protest in the 1960s,” *Social Science Japan* 15 (1999), `https://newslet.iss.u-tokyo.ac.jp/ssj15/ssj15.pdf`; University of Tokyo Archives, Katō Ichirō's March 9, 1969 explanatory text, F0015/124, `https://da.dl.itc.u-tokyo.ac.jp/portal/en/assets/bded0cdd-71e8-4850-8ae0-081517fbb15e`; University of Tokyo Faculty of Science bulletin, April 1, 1969, `https://www.s.u-tokyo.ac.jp/en/story/newsletter/past/19690401_1_6.pdf`.

### 3.5. §93.11 — 1969 university law

**Specified:** *Law concerning Emergency Measures on the Operation of Universities* was promulgated **August 7, 1969** and effective **August 17**. The description now distinguishes its time-limited powers and conditions from unrestricted authority to use police force. The Ministry of Education's retrospective materials are labeled as **retrospective government interpretation**, not contemporaneous student testimony.

**Verification:** Ministry of Education, *Japan's Modern Educational System* (English, March 1980), “(7) Student Movements,” `https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317449.htm`; original *Gakusei Hyakunenshi* (Japanese, 1972), with publication history explained in the English preface, `https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317221.htm`.

### 3.6. §93.18 — Yodogō passenger figures

**Specified:** nine hijackers; Japanese Transport Ministry's historical manifest categories **131 passengers and seven crew members, 138 total**, without implying all 138 were uninvolved civilian hostages. The North Korea-based Yodogō group remains distinct from the overseas Japanese Red Army.

**Verification:** Japanese MLIT aviation-security briefing, June 19, 2020, p. 2 (PDF page index 1), `https://www.mlit.go.jp/common/001349244.pdf`.

### 3.7. §93.19 — United Red Army purges and Asama-Sansō

**Resolved death-count distinction:** **12** people killed or dying from abuse in the late 1971–early 1972 **mountain-base purge**, plus **two earlier Revolutionary Left victims in August 1971** = the **14** commonly counted in the wider related sequence. Twelve and fourteen are not mere alternate estimates of exactly the same killings.

**Corrected fatalities:** two police officers died in the **February 28, 1972** assault; a civilian shot on **February 22** died on **March 1**. The three deaths are not assigned collectively to the final assault.

**Qualified audience figure:** the repeated ~89.7% refers to a combined broadcaster audience estimate, **not one channel's program rating**; the wording explains that precise ratings figures should not be presented without the underlying series.

**Legal outcomes:** Mori Tsuneo died in custody by suicide in **January 1973**; Nagata Hiroko's death sentence was upheld **1993**, and she died in custody in **2011**.

**Verification:** Nippon.com historical feature and timeline (2022), `https://www.nippon.com/en/in-depth/d00776/`.

## 4. Documentary source improvements and remaining limits

All four planned documentary windows remain in place, now with defined source identities and direct supporting catalog/source links:

1. **Miike:** CLRC proposals by date plus ILO complaint; scholarly reconstruction explicitly distinguished from original commission manuscript collation.
2. **University of Tokyo:** dated January 1969 seven-faculty confirmation materials, University of Tokyo archival item **F0015/124**, and contemporary Faculty of Science bulletin; later MEXT narrative separately identified as a 1980 English retrospective based on the 1972 Japanese history.
3. **Beheiren:** movement publications placed alongside Ernest P. Young's **1968** contemporary published note (an independently identifiable, dated source); no claim to have transcribed a specific *Beheiren Nyūsu* issue.
4. **Asama-Sansō:** dated *Asahi Shimbun* reports from February–March 1972, identified via a published 1981 translated selection and digital press archive instructions; no new Japanese-language facsimile translation claimed.

A **17-entry source register** gives source type, creator, chronology or publication year, and access link where available. All passages described as extracts/studies are **clearly disclosed editorial paraphrases, not exact quotations**. This is an approved **textual source-guided scholarly first edition**, **not** an edition claiming exhaustive original Japanese archival facsimile collation, manuscript-by-manuscript translation, or direct authentication of every newspaper page. Some archives may require external institutional access. This limitation is material and should remain visible to students and editors.

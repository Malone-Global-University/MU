## 3. Historical and scientific corrections applied

### A. Earthquake superlative, opening banner
The inaccurate sentence implying an event unlike any previously instrumentally recorded earthquake worldwide has been replaced by “Japan's largest instrumentally recorded earthquake, one of the largest recorded worldwide.” Japan Meteorological Agency and Reconstruction Agency corroborate the M9.0, March 11, 2011 14:46 event; the USGS largest-earthquakes catalogue documents stronger earlier earthquakes, including Chile 1960 and Alaska 1964. No other narrative transformation was necessary.

**Sources:** https://www.reconstruction.go.jp/english/topics/GEJE/ ; https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/science/20-largest-earthquakes-world-1900/

### B. Ōkawa Elementary School court sequence, §99.2
Corrected the conflated 2016 “Sendai High Court” ruling:
- **October 26, 2016:** Sendai **District** Court decision on negligent evacuation response;
- **April 26, 2018:** Sendai **High** Court decision emphasizing failures of advance preparation and disaster planning;
- **October 10, 2019:** Supreme Court did not overturn the High Court judgment.

The death count in the example — 74 pupils and ten staff — was preserved. The change distinguishes findings and institutional duties at different appellate stages.

**Comparison record:** https://www.ookawatakeakari.jp/faq ; https://www.preventionweb.net/news/japan-high-court-ups-damages-child-deaths-march-2011-tsunami

### C. Emergency institutions on March 11, §99.3
Removed the conflation between a generic declaration of a disaster emergency and the nuclear emergency. The chapter now records:
- **15:14 JST:** government established the Emergency Disaster Response Headquarters under the Disaster Countermeasures Basic Act;
- **15:37 JST:** its first meeting;
- **19:03 JST:** separate nuclear emergency declaration under the Act on Special Measures Concerning Nuclear Emergency Preparedness.

These details are supported by the contemporaneous government record and the later investigation; they refer to two different legal and operational structures.

**Sources:** https://www.cas.go.jp/jp/seisaku/icanps/eng/04IIIfinal.pdf ; https://www.env.go.jp/en/chemi/rhm/basic-info/2023/06-02-01.html

### D. Reconstruction and displacement, §99.4 and Data Study C
The undated “about 47,000” national evacuee count is replaced with **approximately 44,000 as of April 9, 2020**, from a May 2020 Reconstruction Agency snapshot. The early-period baseline remains about 470,000. The Fukushima subset is now explicitly identified as about 164,000 at a recorded peak and **about 39,000 in April 2020**, rather than a loose “several tens of thousands.” The text notes that national and prefecture-related evacuee counts overlap, so they must not be added together.

The Reconstruction Agency's separate ten-year web overview reports approximately 42,000 in January 2021. This is a later observation, not a contradiction of the dated 2020 table.

**Sources:** The Reconstruction Agency, *The Process and Prospects for Reconstruction* (May 2020, 44,000 on April 9); https://www.reconstruction.go.jp/10year/en/data.html

### E. NAIIC withdrawal dispute, §99.6
Replaced the unqualified statement that both the proposal for a full TEPCO withdrawal and the Prime Minister's intervention remained simply “disputed.” The text now explicitly attributes to the National Diet's investigation its finding that **no evidence had been found of a TEPCO decision to withdraw all site personnel**. It distinguishes that documented result from the genuine dispute over communications, personnel relocation, and Kan's role.

**Sources:** https://info.publicintelligence.net/FukushimaAccidentReport.pdf (NAIIC English executive summary, printed p.82); https://www.tepco.co.jp/en/press/corp-com/release/betu12_e/images/120620e0104.pdf (company's own account).

### F. NAIIC English/Japanese foreword and earthquake-damage evidence, §99.8 and source window
The chapter now attributes the broad “Made in Japan” cultural explanation **specifically to the English-language chairman's foreword**, clarifying that the Japanese preface does not use identical language. It marks the commission's concern about possible quake damage to safety-related equipment as a **not-excluded possibility**, not proof that pre-tsunami earthquake damage caused the accident. Other investigations emphasized tsunami-driven loss of power and cooling.

**Sources:** https://info.publicintelligence.net/FukushimaAccidentReport.pdf (English chairman's message, printed p.9); https://f-archive.jaea.go.jp/handle/faa/122/index.php?locale=eng (archival record); https://www.gepr.org/en/contents/20120709-02/ (critical commentary on version differences).

### G. Nuclear safety legislation and local consent, §99.8
The chapter now specifies the **Nuclear Regulation Authority Establishment Act (Act No. 47 of 2012), promulgated June 27, 2012**, and authority creation September 19, 2012. It also distinguishes the NRA's technical safety review from political expectations of agreement from host communities; local consent is not itself an NRA statutory safety-approval test.

**Source:** https://www.nra.go.jp/english/legislative/acts.html ; https://www.nra.go.jp/data/000480899.pdf

### H. Electricity-source denominators, Data Study B
The existing rounded 2010, 2013, 2015, and 2020 shares were preserved but assigned explicitly to a **fiscal-year Agency for Natural Resources and Energy-derived historical series**. Renewables includes hydropower. Students are warned against mixing fiscal-year and calendar-year or incompatible reporting scopes.

**Source:** https://www.ene100.jp/www/wp-content/uploads/zumen/e01.pdf

## 4. Documentary provenance and student features

All six source windows preserve their existing titled, attributed editorial paraphrases, source analysis, and questions; none is presented as a facsimile-collated quotation. The revision inserts **two directly accessible research locators in each window** (12 appearances) and **one provenance locator for each of the four data studies**, plus a **21-entry annotated documentary and statistical source register** that distinguishes contemporary records, later official investigations, independent scientific reports, government advocacy, and independent or institutional indexes. The six source windows and the register collectively provide 37 external hyperlink appearances over 21 unique URLs.

The source register is linked from the desktop sidebar. It also identifies the English/Japanese NAIIC foreword distinction, the Okinawa referendum's valid-ballot versus all-ballot denominator difference, and the limits of rounded statistical series. The 23 opening vocabulary labels have been replaced by 23 concise defined terms in the existing two-column/one-column responsive glossary styling. The original defined review terms and all other instructional features were retained.

**Limitations:** A document-specific landing page or official index is not equivalent to a page-collated quotation. A small number of hosting sites may restrict automated retrieval; a cited archival scan may be hosted by an independent mirror. Link destinations and bibliographic metadata have been selectively inspected, not exhaustively HTTP-tested across every entry at the time of publication. Where the Japanese original is relevant to precise wording, the chapter expressly requires original-edition collation before quotation.

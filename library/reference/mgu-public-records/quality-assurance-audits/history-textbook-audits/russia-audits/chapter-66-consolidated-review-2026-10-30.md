## 3. Corrections necessary before publication

### A. Clear factual or chronological corrections

1. **Front matter: Gorbachev dates.** Replace `Mikhail Gorbachev (b. 1931)` with `Mikhail Gorbachev (1931–2022)`. He died August 30, 2022. https://achievement.org/achiever/mikhail-s-gorbachev/
2. **§66.8: Georgian referendum support is understated.** Replace `a Georgian referendum registered about 90 percent support` with `a Georgian referendum recorded approximately 90.6 percent turnout and about 99 percent support among participants for restoration of independence`. Government archival figures and contemporaneous reports differ slightly in the exact yes percentage; ~99% is responsible. https://archive.gov.ge/en/sakartvelos-damoukideblobis-aghdgena-1 ; https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1991-04-02-mn-1826-story.html
3. **§66.28: Boris Pugo's wife.** Replace `Pugo, the interior minister, committed suicide with his wife on August 22` with `Interior Minister Boris Pugo fatally shot himself on August 22; his wife also shot herself, survived initially, and died afterward in hospital`. Contemporary accounts confirm she was found alive at the scene and her death was announced in early September. https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1991-09-05-mn-2437-story.html
4. **§66.2: Erroneous “more than a decade ahead” generalization.** Replace `the Baltic movements were more than a decade ahead of events elsewhere in the Union in some respects` with `the Baltic movements became an early, highly visible example of coordinated mass mobilization, although national movements elsewhere also developed during the late 1980s`. The decade claim contradicts the chapter's own 1987–1991 timeline.
5. **Baltic Timeline Study §66.2: chronological ordering.** Put the Lithuanian Communist Party split on **December 20, 1989** *before* the Congress condemnation of secret protocols on **December 24, 1989**.

### B. Precision and temporal framing

6. **§66.4: Estonian/Latvian citizenship policy.** The paragraph implies both governments had already formally adopted restored-citizenship regimes during the 1990 transition. Clarify that **citizenship restoration principles were debated in 1989–1991**, with specific decisions implemented *after* the restoration of independence; Latvia's Supreme Council formally resolved the core principles on **October 15, 1991**. https://www.refworld.org/legal/natlegcomp/unhcr/1993/39359
7. **§66.14: June 12 public-holiday nomenclature.** It was popularly called “Independence Day,” but the official holiday observed after 1992 commemorated adoption of the Declaration of State Sovereignty; the title **Russia Day** was legally formalized in **2002**. Recast as `often popularly called Independence Day, though officially the day of adoption of the Declaration of State Sovereignty; since 2002, Russia Day`. https://www.prlib.ru/history/619308
8. **§66.30 Primary Source Window:** Identify the composite source as `Verkhovna Rada Resolution No. 1427-XII and the attached Act of Declaration of Independence`, rather than conflating the wording of the Act with the companion resolution establishing the referendum. The substantive narrative can remain intact. https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/en/1427-12?lang=en
9. **§66.1:** Restrict the claim that Soviet leaders `declined to use force in 1988–1989` to the **Baltic campaigns specifically**; Union forces violently repressed demonstrators elsewhere, including Tbilisi in 1989, as the chapter itself describes.

## Historical accuracy and documentary check

The substantive narrative follows the agreed period from the succession struggles of 1999 through the end of Putin’s second presidential term in May 2008. The Second Chechen War, centralization, electoral and media changes, energy-sector political economy, economic recovery, and evolving external relations are treated separately and with appropriate historiographical caution. The 2008 Georgia war, Medvedev-Putin governing arrangement, and later financial crisis are reserved for Chapter 69.

Document-supported cross-checks:

- **1999–2000 Chechnya:** UNHCR registered approximately 195,000 displaced people in Ingushetia in mid-November 1999, exceeding 200,000 in estimated actual presence. This supports the manuscript’s *peak approximately 200,000* description; it is not a count for every month.
- **June 2000 Chechen suicide vehicle attack:** contemporary press confirms an early June suicide truck attack at Alkhan-Yurt; distinguish claims regarding exact casualty numbers.
- **Dubrovka 2002:** approximately 130 hostage deaths is an appropriate rounded statement, given differing judicial/official case counts; *Finogenov* is the ECHR judgment of December 20, 2011. The Court's criticism of evacuation planning and investigation is distinguished from its treatment of the decision to use gas.
- **Beslan 2004:** 334 people, including 186 children; the events of September 3 and later findings of *Tagayeva* (April 13, 2017) are appropriately treated as retrospective judicial evidence, without settling disputed claims about the triggering of initial explosions.
- **Yukos:** several multibillion-dollar assessments are well documented; an international arbitral record sets out around $24.18 billion in assessments for tax years 2000–2004, including fines and interest. The manuscript avoids an unsourced exact total.
- **Gazprom/Rosneft:** the 2005 state-controlling-share transaction in Gazprom and Rosneft's July 14, 2006 IPO are documented by corporate releases.
- **Elections/legal changes:** OSCE/ODIHR's June 2, 2004 final report and the December 11, 2004 governor-nomination law match the chapter's corrected chronology. The 2005 Duma proportional-election reform and 2006 NGO legislation are distinguished by passage and effective dates.
- **Economics:** CRS corroborates about 6.9% average real GDP growth (1999–2008) and the fall in the official poverty rate from roughly 29% (2000) to 13% (2007). Statistical series should be identified by time interval and definition.
- **2008 NATO:** the January 15 Ukrainian MAP request and Georgia's January 5 NATO plebiscite (final 77% in favor among valid votes) are documented. NATO’s April 3 declaration gave an eventual-membership statement without granting MAP.

## Selective editorial corrections applied

1. Clarified the 2004 chronology row to distinguish Beslan/September proposal from the December 11 legal enactment of the governor-nomination framework (Federal Law 159-FZ).
2. Specified 334 deaths, including 186 children, at Beslan, rather than a rounded 330, grounded in the retrospective European Court of Human Rights findings.
3. Reworded Dubrovka deaths as approximately 130 hostages; retrospective official and judicial counts differ slightly in scope.
4. Inserted a substantive 192-word evidence-grounded account of Putin’s February 10, 2007 Munich address at the beginning of §68.36 so that the section actually analyzes the speech before moving to Bucharest.

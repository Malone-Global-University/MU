## 3. Publication-critical historical corrections made in the HTML

1. **Nadir Shah's Delhi chronology** (§57.14): Corrected entry into the city to **20 March 1739**, the widespread rumor/attacks to **21 March**, and Nadir Shah's massacre order to **22 March**, replacing materially wrong dates of 9 and 11 March. The **24 February 1739** Karnal date was preserved. Particular causal and death-toll claims remain carefully qualified. Independent support: *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “India vii. Relations: The Afsharid and Zand Periods”; Abhishek Kaicker, *The King and the People* (2020), chapter “Anatomy of a Massacre.”
2. **Wazir succession** (§57.6): Removed the erroneous implication that Muhammad Amin Khan's son Qamar-ud-din succeeded him directly as wazir in 1721; acknowledged temporary arrangements preceding Nizam-ul-Mulk's accession to that office in 1722. Support: Irvine, *Later Mughals*, vol. II, the section on the death of Muhammad Amin Khan; the compiled Khafi Khan translation.
3. **Bhopal chronology** (§57.6): Corrected “late 1738” to **December 1737** for the confrontation and **7 January 1738** for the settlement. The preceding 1737 movement toward Delhi is retained. Support: Maharashtra government historical gazetteer account of the Nizam-Maratha conflict.
4. **Shah Alam II's return** (§§57.16, 57.18): Corrected “1771” to **January 1772**. The emperor's journey and the Maratha capture of Delhi in 1771 are distinct from the date of his arrival. Support: Maharashtra government historical gazetteer, “Return of Shah Alam to capital.”
5. **Battle of Hasanpur** (§57.4): Added **13–14 November 1720** for fighting ending the Sayyid ascendancy. Primary older source: Irvine, *Later Mughals*, vol. II, “The Battle of Hasanpur.” The fighting began on 13 November and Abdullah Khan was defeated on the following day.
6. **Battle of Manupur** (§57.15): Specified **11 March 1748**, near Sirhind. Comparative support: Ganda Singh's *Later Mughal History of the Panjab* and histories of the Durrani invasions.
7. **1717 Farrukhsiyar imperial grants** (source window §57.18): Identified the **3,000-rupee annual payment** for Company trade in Bengal; separated the imperial farman from associated ministerial directions concerning company passes, minting, and settlement; qualified permission for additional villages near Calcutta as subject to provincial approval. This is crucial to showing *nominal sanction versus provincial implementation*. Support: Shirin Akhtar, “Farrukh Siyar's Farman,” *Banglapedia*; studies of the Surman embassy and Company trade.
8. **1740s memoir/source attribution** (source window §57.14): Distinguished Anand Ram Mukhlis's account, often referenced as a *Tazkira*, from Abd al-Karim Kashmiri's *Bayan-i Waqi*. Kashmiri later served Nadir Shah, so “Mughal-service writer” cannot be applied unqualified to both. Support: *Encyclopaedia Iranica* entries on Anand Ram Mokhles and Abd-al-Karim Kashmiri; British Library's Persian manuscript catalogue.
9. **Athar Ali's historiographical intervention** (§57.19): Replaced a vague, time-limited reference to a “1980s rejoinder” with identifiable scholarship: **“Recent Theories of Eighteenth Century India” (1986–87)** and **“The Mughal Polity—A Critique of Revisionist Approaches” (1993)**. Interpretations are now expressed proportionately and without equating regional continuity with denial of the imperial crisis.
10. **Retrospective Siyar dating** (source window §57.19): Changed the overprecise single completion date to **composition in the early 1780s, with material extending approximately to 1783**. The 1789 English edition remains identifiable; published translations and manuscript witnesses should not be conflated with an autograph composition date. Support: John Rylands Library manuscript catalogue.

### Historical findings retained but specifically qualified

- The **jizya abolition under Farrukhsiyar in 1713** is broadly supportable; Satish Chandra's source-based treatment explains an initial announcement and subsequent official implementation measures. A date-only account would obscure that process, but the submitted text did not require a separate expansion to narrate it.
- The **Sirhind victory of Banda Singh Bahadur in May 1710** is correctly placed; specific day-level dates and variants in traditional narratives are not essential to Chapter 57's purpose.
- The **Nadir Shah death-toll and wealth estimates** are not treated as precise data. Contemporary and later figures vary, so the chapter does not introduce fabricated totals. *Encyclopaedia Iranica* gives a commonly cited approximate 20,000 for the Delhi massacre, but that is not a verified census.
- The **1752 settlement over Lahore and Multan**, with Mir Mannu continuing under altered suzerainty, is consonant with scholarly accounts; the revised narrative emphasizes the difference between a diplomatic claim and actual control.

## 5. Primary-source and evidence limitations

Five source/evidence windows remain, as required, but **none is a documentary transcription or source-collated verbatim translation**. All rendered passages are plainly labeled editorial paraphrases. The five windows are: Khafi Khan's *Muntakhab al-Lubab*; Bhimsen and fiscal evidence; Mirza Mahdi Astarabadi contrasted with Indian writings about 1739; the Farrukhsiyar farman of 1717; and Ghulam Husain Khan Tabatabai's *Siyar al-Mutakhkhirin*.

**Special limitation of source window 3 (fiscal):** In the original Claude file, an editorial synthesis of several unidentified fiscal documents was presented inside a primary-source window. It was not a specific excerpt. The revised box identifies Bhimsen's *Tarikh-i Dilkasha / Nuskha-i Dilkusha* and the 1972 published English translation, while explicitly separating the memoir's military observations from independent revenue tables and archival papers. This correction prevents a composite historian's inference from being misread as a quotation from a particular eighteenth-century revenue document. **A future documentary teaching edition can still improve this box by selecting, collating and transcribing one identifiable primary record.** That is not represented as already completed.

The two geography studies are **text-guided historical atlas exercises**, not finished map plates. Historical boundaries must be treated as varying claims and zones of effective control. Neither an atlas illustration nor its source permission is incorporated into this standalone first edition.

Volume-level bibliography, glossary, chronology, and index are intentionally separate deliverables; their absence inside the chapter is not an HTML defect. Authoritative original Chapter 2 source-model bytes were unavailable for a direct binary comparison; the corrected JavaScript source matches the locked Chapter 56 baseline, and the visual framework is substantially consistent.

## Consolidated final interventions — changes made to Claude's supplied manuscript

1. **§49.13, Sri Lanka chronological correction:** the first *documented* Portuguese encounter with Kotte is better dated **1506**, not 1505. Corrected and distinguished the short-lived 1518 Colombo fort, dismantled in 1524, from general Portuguese sovereignty over Sri Lanka. Added discussion of Kotte's agency and existing cinnamon trade.
2. **§49.15:** explained how ship provisioning, mortality, finance, and the cost of reliable market access shaped the transition from voyages to permanent military/commercial presence.
3. **§49.20:** deepened the 1503–1504 Cochin alliance as a military-commercial partnership dependent on Cochin's soldiers, merchants, provisioning, and local political circumstances, rather than a solitary Portuguese triumph.
4. **§49.29:** distinguished metropolitan Crown returns via the Cape route from the **Asian Estado's revenues, especially customs on private and Asian commerce and land revenues in territorial holdings**. This is a major interpretive/financial distinction supported by Portuguese fiscal historiography.
5. **§49.35:** clarified the documentary boundary of Zayn al-Din Makhdum's **Tuhfat al-Mujahidin (early 1580s)**: it illuminates earlier Malabar resistance, **not** the 1600 final siege of Kunjali Marakkar IV. Added source-critical explanation of Portuguese/Islamic/late traditions.
6. **§49.36:** removed two overlapping/repetitive summary paragraphs and substituted evidence-driven explanations of the joint Cochin–Portuguese defense, the 1507 Cannanore crisis, and shifting commercial/political allegiance.
7. **§49.37:** added military geography, relief, provision, local dependencies, and the distinction between the **1538 Ottoman-backed Diu siege**, **1546 Gujarati-led siege**, and the **1570–71 Deccan/Malabar campaigns**. Preserves the Portuguese **loss of Chaliyam in 1571**, the survival of Goa and Chaul, and differentiated motives.
8. **§49.40:** developed the contradiction between royal monopoly ambitions, local customs revenue, private commerce, and actual costs. Preserved correctly qualified estimates reported by Luís Filipe Reis Thomaz for **1569** and avoided treating royal factory purchases as a figure for *all* Portuguese trade.

All changes retain Claude's historical prose outside these narrowly targeted expansions and the explicit removal of the two redundant §49.36 paragraphs.

## Independently checked historical sources and claims

The following external sources were consulted during the audit. Their bibliographic links support the identified claims; they are not asserted to verify every sentence of the book.

1. **Zoltán Biedermann, “Ceylon” and “city of Colombo,” Enciclopédia Virtual da Expansão Portuguesa (2009).** First documented contact dated 1506 (traditional 1505 rejected in specialist accounts), first Colombo fort built 1518 and dismantled 1524. https://eve.fcsh.unl.pt/en/places/ceylon and https://eve.fcsh.unl.pt/en/places/city-colombo
2. **Zoltán Biedermann, “The Portuguese Estado da Índia (Empire in Asia),” Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Asian History (2020).** The Estado consisted of negotiated and conquered forts, factories, and towns supported by Asian cooperation, with its principal governors first at Cochin, later Goa. https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61799/chapter-abstract/546320027
3. **Luís Filipe F. R. Thomaz, “The system of travel and the Portuguese commercial network in East Asia,” Anais de História de Além-Mar 19 (2018).** Metropolitan Cape trade and Asian customs-driven finances must not be conflated; regional trade by private Asian and Portuguese merchants contributed significantly to local revenues. https://ciencia.ucp.pt/en/publications/o-sistema-das-viagens-e-a-rede-comercial-portuguesa-na-%C3%A1sia-orien/
4. **Luís Filipe Reis Thomaz, “The Portuguese control over Indian Ocean and the cartaz system,” in Os Mares do Oriente: A Presença Portuguesa circa 1507 (Academia de Marinha, 2011), 267–335, especially 294–296.** Identifies and discusses the **16 November 1611 Muscat cartaz**, preserved in an English translation in Walter Payton's 1612 voyage narrative as printed by Samuel Purchas. The individual pass postdates the main chapter and is labeled accordingly. https://academia.marinha.pt/pt/edicoes/Simposios%20Historia%20Maritima/Decimo.pdf
5. **“From Slave to King: The Role of South Asians in Maritime Southeast Asia,” Archipel 82 (2011).** Evidence that Malacca's four harbor masters included officers for Gujarati and Coromandel/Bengal/Pegu shipping; source identifies Tomé Pires's description. https://www.persee.fr/doc/arch_0044-8613_2011_num_82_1_4261
6. **“A Portuguese East Indiaman from the 1502–1503 Fleet of Vasco da Gama off Al Hallaniyah Island, Oman,” International Journal of Nautical Archaeology (2016).** Da Gama's 1502 fleet is documented as **20 ships**. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1095-9270.12175
7. **Zayn al-Din Makhdum, Tuhfat al-Mujahidin (completed about 1583)**, as described in an academic review of S. Muhammad Husayn Nainar's translation (2006). Its coverage reaches the campaigns around Chaliyam (1571), not the 1600 Kunjali IV surrender. https://paperity.org/p/266077245/tuhfat-al-mujahidin-a-historical-epic-of-the-sixteenth-century ; see also https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/itinerario/article/does-the-pagan-king-reply-malayalam-documents-on-the-portuguese-arrival-in-india/5A9056E951B5C7D5A0F0A3AB25FC577B
8. **Goitein–Friedman, India Traders of the Middle Ages: Documents from the Cairo Geniza (Brill, 2008).** Bibliographic edition and earlier-period evidentiary limits confirmed via Princeton Geniza Project and Wellcome. https://genizalab.princeton.edu/projects/indian-ocean-documents-cairo-geniza and https://wellcomecollection.org/works/gkbt343u
9. **Ibn Battuta, The Travels, Vol. IV (1994), translation completed and annotated by C. F. Beckingham**; library record verifies edition. https://wellcomecollection.org/works/w9h82rna
10. **Tomé Pires, Suma Oriental, trans./ed. Armando Cortesão, 2 vols. (1944).** Library catalog verifies edition and dates. https://natlib.govt.nz/records/22147472
11. **Duarte Barbosa, Book of Duarte Barbosa, trans./ed. M. L. Dames, 1918–1921.** Catalog verifies edition and dates. https://search.worldcat.org/title/22046523

**Specific qualifications maintained:** The *Meri/Miri* attack in 1502 is historically established and the manuscript describes casualty totals only as **hundreds**, not falsely precise numbers. Marakkar/Marakkayar and Labbai identities and trading networks varied across the Malabar and Coromandel coasts; these are not assumed to be one undifferentiated political organization. The reported 1569 pepper quantities are **contemporary estimates**, not audited comprehensive trade statistics. The 1611 Muscat pass is a real documented source **outside the chapter's main closing chronology**; its terms are explicitly not projected backwards without evidence.

## 1. Controlling purpose and disposition

Authoritative outline purpose: “Examine major states and military formations that competed for power in the eighteenth-century subcontinent.” The revised chapter covers Sikh jathas, the Dal Khalsa and misls; Mysore's institutional inheritance, Haidar Ali and Tipu Sultan; Travancore; Bharatpur's Jat state; Rohilkhand; and comparative military-fiscal and sovereignty questions. It does **not** collapse the comparison into Tipu's biography, reduce regional politics to religion, or retell the later Company and Sikh Empire histories assigned to Chapters 64–65. The governing 20-section plan supersedes the older generic ten-section outline at the chapter-specific section layer. All 20 sections and five divisions are retained.

**Decision rationale:** The submitted work was structurally sound and already met the direct-prose target, contrary to an implication in Claude's reported inclusive estimate. Targeted historical corrections and source-document upgrades were made directly in HTML. The original first-person opening misdated Marthanda Varma's presence in the early 1760s, a genuine publication-critical factual flaw: he died in 1758. It is now corrected. All division-depth planning bands are satisfied after modest substantive expansion; all features and working navigation remain intact. One complete consolidated audit follows; no further micro-pass is justified in the absence of new material evidence.

## 3. Direct historical corrections applied

1. **Opening frame, Marthanda Varma's death:** The original scene conflated three regional settings “within a few hundred days” in the early 1760s and incorrectly implied that Marthanda Varma was still reigning. Rewrote to identify **Karthika Thirunal Rama Varma** as Travancore's early-1760s ruler and Marthanda Varma as his predecessor (d. 1758); removed the spurious narrow synchronism. (Opening.)
2. **1733 Kapur Singh grant:** The jagir's valuation is **traditionally given as one lakh rupees annually**, a figure in the *Encyclopaedia of Sikhism* “Misls” entry and other Sikh histories, whereas a published SOAS doctoral thesis gives one thousand. Retained the familiar reported lakh figure but marked the valuation **not independently established**, noted conflicting accounts, and forbade inference of realized collections from nominal grants. (§59.2.)
3. **Ahmad Shah Durrani chronology:** He established power after Nadir Shah's death in **1747**, while the first invasion of Punjab/India belongs to **1748**, not 1747. Corrected the conflation. (§59.2; §59.4 already differentiated the sequence.)
4. **Tipu's *Padshah* titulature:** The draft assigned adoption, coinage and sermon changes unambiguously to **1787**. Revised to **mid-1780s, often c. 1786–1787**, distinguished documentary signals of sovereignty from definitive universal recognition and acknowledged continuing, fluctuating invocation of Mughal suzerainty; 1784 Mughal recognition preceded these claims. (§59.9.)
5. **Kannambadi/Kaveri dam:** Removed certainty that a foundation was laid in **1794** for what became the later dam. Distinguished the 1794 dating reported for an inscription from older epigraphic scholarship identifying construction or the raising of an embankment **c. 1797** and separated the eighteenth-century works from the modern Krishna Raja Sagara structure. (§59.10.)
6. **Tipu, Sringeri, Coorg/Canara and Melkote:** Retained the documented **1791 Sringeri raid and patronage correspondence**, along with documented campaigns involving captivity, population transfer and conversion claims. Clarified that contested totals are not neutral censuses, and that context cannot be used to erase coercion. The later Melkote account stays explicitly uncertain rather than being used to generate a spurious date or casualty count. (§59.12.)
7. **Colachel, August 1741:** Preserved the traditional **10 August** commemoration but identified the **16 August 1741** report by the weaver Rengas in VOC inventory no. 2581, folios 7436–7437, cited by M. de Lannoy; acknowledged a competing **7 August** surrender date. Distinguished possible stages of the siege and differing source traditions, without claiming an undisputed day. (§59.14 and source window.)
8. **Badan Singh's death:** Published sources disagree between **1755 and 1756**; added the dating uncertainty instead of maintaining an unqualified 1756. (§59.15.)
9. **Substantive, non-padding narrative strengthening:** Added evidence-driven discussion of Haidar's reliance on inherited local revenue institutions (§59.7), the methodological difference between Tipu's centralizing orders and their implementation (§59.10), the politics of competing Mughal and independent sovereignty claims (§59.9), state trading and merchants' agency (§59.11), and source limitations for large-scale deportation counts (§59.12). Divisions II and III now meet depth planning bands.

**Reviewed historical claims retained:** Dal Khalsa reorganization **1748** as a traditional, qualified date; Haidar Ali's death **7 December 1782**; the **1791 Sringeri correspondence**; the 1762 *Vadda Ghallughara* without treating partisan casualty estimates as reliable enumeration; Tipu's state-building, coercion and patronage; the Rohilla genealogical treatise of **c. 1770**. The chapter's balanced approach to Sikh religious/political agency and to Tipu's actions is historically defensible subject to source limits.

## 5. Scholarly interpretations and major evidentiary safeguards

- **Sikh formation, 1733/1748:** *Encyclopaedia of Sikhism* entries on “Misls” and court *Akhbar-i-Darbar-i-Mualla* provide identifiable traditions and editions, but late retrospective models of the twelve misls should not be read as fixed constitutional arrangements in the 1740s. Purnima Dhavan's relevant work requires page-specific citation before being represented as advancing a tightly attributed claim.
- **Mysore and military fiscalism:** Burton Stein is a credible point of reference for state formation and resource extraction; Kate Brittlebank for Tipu's legitimacy; Mohibbul Hasan for his mid-twentieth-century biography and more cautious treatment of specific coercion narratives. Their positions must not be transformed into a false partisan dichotomy; the text is framed as general interpretation rather than alleged page-specific propositions.
- **Contested Tipu record:** A historical textbook must hold documentary evidence of Sringeri patronage and specific coercive population transfers together, assessing provenance and motive separately. Estimates sometimes repeated for Coorg or the Canara Christian captives should never be silently converted into a universally proven total. The independent review intentionally retains uncertainty.
- **Travancore:** De Lannoy's study supports an organized campaign and Dutch Company archival dates. The chapter avoids the overstrong proposition that one isolated European-style battle alone created Travancore's political power.
- **Bharatpur and Rohilkhand:** Conflicting Badan Singh death years are disclosed; Hafiz Rahmat Khan's genealogy is treated as a political construction of Afghan identity, consistent with Akansha Singh (2025).

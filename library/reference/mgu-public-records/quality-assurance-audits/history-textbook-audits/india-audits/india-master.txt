# CORE HISTORICAL WRITING STANDARD

Write serious, clear, neutral textbook prose.

The chapter should explain rather than merely announce.

For major developments, address questions such as:

* What happened?
* When did it happen?
* Where did it happen?
* Who was involved?
* What circumstances preceded it?
* Why did it happen?
* What evidence supports our understanding?
* What changed?
* What remained continuous?
* Who experienced the development differently?
* What were the immediate consequences?
* What were the longer-term consequences?
* Why do historians consider it significant?
* How has it been interpreted?

Not every paragraph must answer every question.

Use these questions as the general historical reasoning framework.

Avoid teleological narratives.

Do not write history as though:

* British conquest was inevitable;
* Partition was inevitable;
* modern India was the inevitable culmination of ancient Indian history;
* religious communities always possessed fixed modern political identities;
* empires were destined to rise or collapse;

Avoid simplistic rise-and-fall explanations.

Avoid reducing complex developments to the personality of a single ruler or leader.

Explain political, institutional, military, economic, social, cultural, religious, environmental, intellectual, technological, regional, and international factors when relevant.

Do not force irrelevant categories into a chapter merely to satisfy a checklist.

# FILE NAMING

Because the series contains **111 continuously numbered chapters**, use **three-digit chapter numbering in filenames**.

Use lowercase kebab-case.

Use the format:

`chapter-###-title.html`

Examples:

`chapter-001-south-asia-as-a-historical-region.html`

`chapter-030-the-end-of-gupta-supremacy-and-the-age-of-harsha.html`

`chapter-031-chalukyas-rashtrakutas-and-the-deccan.html`

`chapter-050-babur-humayun-and-sher-shah.html`

`chapter-062-plassey-buxar-and-the-conquest-of-bengal.html`

`chapter-076-the-birth-of-organized-indian-nationalism.html`

`chapter-088-the-indian-republic-and-the-nehruvian-settlement.html`

`chapter-100-india-in-the-twenty-first-century.html`

`chapter-111-history-memory-and-the-meaning-of-south-asia.html`

Do not reset filename numbering when a new Volume begins.

Do not use:

`volume-02-chapter-01...`

as a substitute for the continuous chapter number.

**Volume:** [VOLUME NUMBER AND TITLE]

**Filename:** `[FILENAME]`

Then deliver the complete HTML file as a file artifact when supported; otherwise provide the complete file content. Do not replace delivery with a progress report.

# CROSS-VOLUME CONTINUITY

Because this is one six-volume work, maintain continuity across volume boundaries.

The final chapter of one Volume and the first chapter of the next should connect historically.

Do not treat the first chapter of a new Volume as though the historical narrative has restarted.

For example:

* Chapter 30 should lead naturally into Chapter 31.
* Chapter 49 should lead naturally into Chapter 50.
* Chapter 61 should lead naturally into Chapter 62.
* Chapter 75 should lead naturally into Chapter 76.
* Chapter 87 should lead naturally into Chapter 88.

Briefly establish continuity where needed.

Do not repeat the entire preceding Volume.

# QUALITY CONTROL BEFORE DELIVERY

Perform **one consolidated pre-delivery audit**, not a separate research-and-rewrite loop for each checklist entry. Check mechanical properties by script where available; evaluate historical substance in one holistic editorial pass. Correct publication-critical defects, then recheck affected structures. The list below specifies the *acceptance criteria*, not a series of independent tasks.

Before delivering, confirm:

1. The correct Volume and Part have been identified.
2. The continuous chapter number matches the Authoritative Chapter Outline.
3. The chapter title and historical purpose match the assignment.
4. The approved section plan for this chapter—default ten sections or specifically approved twenty-section plan—is complete and in the assigned order.
5. The designated final numbered section functions as Review and Historical Synthesis.
6. Material assigned to neighboring chapters has not been unnecessarily duplicated.
7. The chapter remains coherent within its Volume and the six-volume series.
8. The Chapter Opening, Learning Objectives, and Guiding Historical Questions are present.
9. Historical Context, Chronology Preview, Major People/Groups/Places, and Core Vocabulary Preview are present.
10. The historical narrative is substantively developed, with clear chronology, causation, institutions, social experience, and consequences where relevant.
11. Historical evidence and its significant limitations are handled accurately.
12. Ancient literary traditions are not treated automatically as eyewitness reporting; the Indus script is not represented as deciphered.
13. Indo-Aryan terminology, varna/jati/caste distinctions, and the historical diversity of religious traditions are handled appropriately.
14. Historical Vocabulary, Timeline Study, and Map/Geography Study are present.
15. Primary Source Window, Historical Debate, and Multiple Perspectives are present.
16. Continuity and Change and Cause and Effect features are present.
17. Social history, geography, regional variation, environmental conditions, and outside connections appear where the subject warrants them.
18. Chapter Summary, Key Terms, and Major People and Places review are present.
19. Chronology Check, Map Study Questions, and Primary Source Questions are present.
20. Review Questions, Short-Answer Questions, Discussion Questions, and Writing Prompts are present.
21. Research or Library Extension and Chapter Reflection are present.
22. The chapter does not project the contemporary Republic of India or modern national boundaries carelessly backward.
23. Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Bhutan, and Maldives are included where required by the assigned chapter, without imposing one uniform South Asian experience.
24. Controversial questions are explained through evidence, not partisan rhetoric or false equivalence.
25. Exact quotations are authentic and appropriately attributed; unverifiable wording is paraphrased rather than fabricated.
26. Significant figures, dates, source descriptions, and contemporaneous status claims are qualified or verified when materially uncertain.
27. The user's chapter-specific narrative-word target, if any, is checked **against direct historical narrative**, excluding front matter and apparatus.
28. The HTML model shell, CSS, component placeholders, closing tag positions, and scripts are preserved.
29. Navigation links and all local anchors resolve; no duplicate HTML IDs or unfinished placeholders remain.
30. The Table of Contents covers the approved numbered sections and the filename uses the correct three-digit continuous chapter number.
31. Previous/next chapter navigation follows the continuous series where the model provides it.
32. The delivered file is one complete, publishable MGU Library Textbook Edition chapter, not a sample or an intermediate draft.

**Audit execution:** First run mechanical structural checks, then make a focused historical review, and finally correct only defects that genuinely affect publication quality. Do not open a separate search for every item. Do not run a series of generalized rewrites after the chapter already meets these criteria. State any remaining documentary limitations concisely instead of claiming source-by-source archival validation.

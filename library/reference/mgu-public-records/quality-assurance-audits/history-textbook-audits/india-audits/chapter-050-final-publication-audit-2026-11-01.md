## Final consolidated accuracy and editing actions (not a new expansion)

1. **Panipat, §50.7:** Removed two repeated paragraphs that restated the cart fortifications and battle outcome already thoroughly presented. Retained the first identification of battle participants and the richer tactical narrative, including the figure of approximately 700 carts attributed to Babur's primary account. Updated paragraph to preserve the role of the artillery specialist Mustafa.
2. **Gunpowder and elephants, §§50.7–50.8:** Changed the assertion of elephants trampling their own lines into explicitly attributed and uncertain evidence. The best contemporary account and Smithsonian commentary identify mobile cavalry and careful tactical preparation as central and do not conclusively establish a stampede.
3. **The Safavid final Central Asian campaign, §50.2:** Confirmed Najm-i Sani's involvement and death at Ghujduwan in 1512; Babur had joined the campaign, although he was not its controlling commander.
4. **Gwalior diamond, §§50.9–50.10:** Babur's memoir records a valuable diamond presented to Humayun by the Gwalior ruling family. Later identification of that stone with the Koh-i-Noor remains an inference, not a demonstrated identification.
5. **Sur succession and Mahdavi chronology, §50.30:** The chapter's unqualified 1551 date for Shaikh Alai's death was replaced with **1549 or 1550**, the dates reflected in different major contemporary/early accounts (Elliot–Dowson and the *Ain-i Akbari*). The date of Islam Shah's death is presented as **1553/54**, with the variation acknowledged (older academic histories and Britannica-derived reference use 1553; Harvard's chronology and many newer accounts use 1554). This is more responsible than hiding divergent references behind a spurious single consensus date.
6. **Sur/Mughal restoration, §50.34:** Removed the near-duplicate paragraph retelling Machhiwara and Sirhind. Preserved May 1555 Machhiwara fighting against officers including Tatar Khan, and the June 22 Sirhind encounter against Sikandar Sur himself. Clarified Ibrahim Sur's shifting claim to Delhi and withdrew the misleading implication that Sikandar's eventual submission took place in Humayun's lifetime.
7. **Hemu and the Chapter 51 boundary, §50.35:** Reframed Hemu as a formidable Afghan-aligned rival active in eastern India at Humayun's death. The conquest of Agra/Delhi and his direct confrontation with Akbar's regency **happened later in 1556**, and the material is correctly reserved for the next chapter rather than narrated anachronistically.
8. **Opening:** Clarified that Sher Shah's personal reign (1540–1545) was distinct from the approximately fifteen-year Sur dynastic political interlude.
9. **Other flagged traditions:** Verified that the Samarkand night capture, the 700-cart figure, the Persian/Safavid Ghujduwan campaign, and May 1555 Machhiwara campaign have defensible documentary bases. The Rohtas litter story, Sanga invitation, Giri-Sumel ruse, Raisen religious justification, water-carrier story, and debated jewel identification remain appropriately qualified and must not be promoted to unambiguous fact.

## Documentary source register

1. **Babur's memoir, *Baburnama*:** First-person Chagatai text, especially the 1500/1501 Samarkand and 1526 Panipat descriptions. Annette S. Beveridge, *The Bābur-nāma in English* (1922), now available in Gutenberg, and the modern Wheeler Thackston translation. Panipat 700 carts and Rawhide lashings appear in Babur's narrative; historians must still assess tactical causal interpretations carefully. https://www.gutenberg.org/files/44608/44608-h/44608-h.htm .
2. **Smithsonian National Museum of Asian Art, “VI. From Peshawar to Panipat”:** Interpretive commentary emphasizes cavalry, mobility and planning, disputing deterministic gunpowder-and-elephant explanations. https://asia.si.edu/research/essays/vi-from-peshawar-to-panipat/ .
3. **Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Najm-e Ṯāni”:** Safavid commander killed at Ghujduwan 1512, with Babur's participation noted and distinguished from Najm's command. https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/najm-e-ani/ .
4. **Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Koh-i-Noor”:** Reports Gwalior diamond described in the *Baburnama*, and explicitly explains competing scholarly identifications with the modern Koh-i-Noor. https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/koh-i-noor/ .
5. **Gulbadan Begum, *Humayun-nama*:** Annette S. Beveridge bilingual edition/translation (1902), verified in the Smithsonian catalog; composition approximately 1586–87, discussed by Iranica. https://www.si.edu/object/history-humayun-humayun-nama-gul-badan-begam-princess-rose-body-translated-introduction-notes%3Asiris_sil_325090 ; https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/golbadan-begom/ .
6. **Wheeler Thackston, *Three Memoirs of Homayun*:** Edition and translation of Gulbadan Begim, Jawhar Aftabachi and Bayazid Bayat, published Mazda Publishers 2009 and later printings. https://www.mazdapublishers.com/book/three-memoirs-of-homayun .
7. **Abbas Khan Sarwani, *Tarikh-i Sher Shahi*:** Later Afghan chronicle translated in Elliot and Dowson, useful on Sur practices, with an evident retrospective royal-virtue ideal. https://persian.packhum.org/text/080201014/4 .
8. **Fitzwilliam Museum, CM.IN.3681-R:** Precisely catalogued Sher Shah silver rupee of Gwalior, 1543–1544, **11.39 g**, object ID 92776. The source window distinguishes this object from general types/legends. https://data.fitzmuseum.cam.ac.uk/id/object/92776 .
9. **Harvard South Asian timeline:** Places Islam Shah's reign in 1545–1554, demonstrating date variation from scholarship using 1553. https://timelinesouthasianhistory.hsites.harvard.edu/mughal-period-and-transition-c-1526-ce-1707-ce .
10. **Elliot–Dowson / Badauni, early account of Shaikh Alai:** records **1549**; *Ain-i Akbari* translated by Blochmann records **1550**. https://persian.packhum.org/text/080201014/5 ; https://persian.packhum.org/text/000702051 .
11. **Haryana/Panipat district historical account, Hemu:** Records the October 1556 confrontation with Tardi Beg, after Humayun's death; modern historical interpretations of the political framing should be treated critically. https://panipat.gov.in/second-battle/ .
12. **Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Homayun Padeshah”:** Reinstatement 1555 and fatal January 1556 fall, with important Persianate administrative context. https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/homayun-padesah/ .

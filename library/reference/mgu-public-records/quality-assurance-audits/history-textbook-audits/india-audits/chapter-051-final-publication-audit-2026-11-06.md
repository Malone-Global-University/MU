## Integrated content completion

**Division I:** Extended the accession crisis, Hemu's political ascent, Second Panipat's command and supply problems, the Sur remnants, Bairam Khan's regency, and the transition to Akbar's personal rule. Sources are distinguished from retrospective legitimating narratives rather than accepted verbatim.

**Division II:** Developed political balances among nobles, Malwa's difficult incorporation, Gondwana and Rani Durgavati's agency, the Uzbek revolts, and the material demands of imperial siege and cavalry warfare. Includes a traceable source study of the imperial camp.

**Division III:** Distinguished the political geography and rivalries of Rajput houses; treated Amber's negotiated alliance, the Chittor massacre, Ranthambore, Haldighati, and Pratap's continuing Mewar resistance with their different outcomes and source traditions.

**Division IV:** Preserved the documented **Sarnal (December 1572) / renewed Ahmadabad campaign (1573)** distinction; developed Gujarat's political economy and ports, the Karrani campaigns, Rajmahal, the Baro-Bhuiyans, Isa Khan, and the incomplete incorporation of Odisha.

**Division V:** Explained the eastern rebellions of 1580–82, Mirza Hakim's challenge, the Yusufzai and Roshani campaigns, Kashmir, lower Sindh **through 1592**, and the diplomatic transfer of Kandahar in 1595. Did not treat submission to court claims as uniform frontier pacification.

**Division VI:** Developed Deccan diplomatic geography, Chand Bibi's political and military role, the 1596 Berar settlement, the fall of Ahmadnagar fort in 1600 (distinct from extinction of the Nizam Shahi state), and Asirgarh in January 1601, plus the cost of maintaining southern positions.

**Division VII:** Reconstructed the evolving political-military logic of *mansab*, *zat*, *sawar*, *dagh*, service ranks, jagir revenue assignments, imperial recruitment, and negotiated local power. **No definitive year is invented for the full dual-rank system's introduction**; the late-stage organization is distinguished from scholarly debate about its development.

**Division VIII:** Explained ecology, financial constraints, graded sovereignty, Prince Salim's opposition, Abu'l-Fazl's murder in 1602, the death of Daniyal in **1605**, and the disputed succession. Expanded §51.48 to substantial synthesis without reproducing Chapter 52's revenue-administration/religious-program mandate.

## Source verification register and limitations

The following works and online locators were inspected or verified in this production cycle. **Identifying a primary source and its printed chapter or page does not mean that every factual sentence of a 26,101-word chapter has been checked against the complete primary record.** Narratives include carefully marked scholarly reconstruction; no fabricated direct quotations have been introduced.

1. **Abu'l-Fazl, *Akbarnama*, H. Beveridge translation, vol. II.** The accessible table of contents places the Hemu crisis at **chapter IX, p. 45**, the battle at **chapter XI, p. 58**; the Chittor account includes the chronicler's approximately 30,000 deaths and massacre order, traditionally cited as vol. II, p. 475. The translated text can be read at https://persian.packhum.org/text/000701022 and the Chittor passage at https://persian.packhum.org/text/000701022/6 . The numbers are attributed to the chronicler rather than treated as independent demographic counts.
2. **Abu'l-Fazl, *A'in-i Akbari*, H. Blochmann translation, vol. I (1873).** The original printed table of contents identifies **Book II, A'in 3, 'The Mansabdars,' p. 236; translator's note p. 238; A'in 7, 'Regulations Regarding the Branding of Animals,' p. 255**; the imperial encampment is **Book I, A'in 17**. Read at https://persian.packhum.org/text/000702051 and https://persian.packhum.org/text/000702051/2 . The present book's paraphrases are tied to identified regulations, not a claim of uniform compliance.
3. **Bada'uni, *Muntakhab al-Tawarikh*.** An excerpt from the opening Akbar-era narrative, marked **page 1**, describes the Kalanaur enthronement with Bairam Khan's approval: https://famineanddearth.exeter.ac.uk/displayhtml.html?id=fp_00179_en_muntakhabuttawarikh_vol02 . W. H. Lowe's translated volume II is catalogued at https://zenodo.org/records/5824861 . **The Haldighati source study is explicitly marked as a contextual source discussion, not a page-verified passage-level paraphrase**, because this particular page was not collated. Different modern records of the multi-volume translations can contain confusing translator attributions; follow the individual volume imprint.
4. **Nizam al-Din Ahmad, *Tabaqat-i Akbari*.** Edition facts and selected translation confirmed from the University of Exeter's record of English vol. III (Brajendranath De / Baini Prashad): https://famineanddearth.exeter.ac.uk/displayhtml.html?id=fp_00177_en_tabaqatiakbari_vol03 . **The selected online passages do not cover or prove every event discussed here; no unsupported page number is supplied for a particular Akbar campaign.**
5. **Shireen Moosvi, 'The Evolution of the Mansab System under Akbar until 1596–7,' *Journal of the Royal Asiatic Society*, April 1981, pp. 173–185.** https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-royal-asiatic-society/article/abs/evolution-of-the-mansab-system-under-akbar-until-15967/C05900120A15AA070603E6BAC2BA01A5 . The article explicitly supports a dual zat/sawar system by 1605 and cautions against treating its evolution as settled in all details.
6. ***Princes of the Mughal Empire, 1504–1719* (Cambridge), prologue.** https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/princes-of-the-mughal-empire-15041719/prologue/7C57A054E5219164421083A6673BFB97 . Supports Salim/Khusrau complexity and the distinction between Murad's 1599 death and Daniyal's **1605** death.
7. **Related Deccan scholarship and primary-oriented education sources:** https://egyankosh.ac.in/bitstream/123456789/77569/1/Unit-6.pdf and https://era.ed.ac.uk/bitstream/1842/6536/1/Finkelstein1990.pdf were identified as context; not treated here as exhaustively collated editions.

**Limits of certification:** This is a carefully sourced *scholarly textbook textual HTML edition*, not an exhaustive historical-critical edition based on manuscript collation, epigraphic review, and passage-by-passage checking of every Persian chronicle. Several campaign narratives inevitably rely on modern historiographic synthesis. The six source windows are clear about their relative documentary status. No maps are embedded as plates: four features provide atlas-directed map analysis questions. These limitations do not prevent publication as an explanatory first edition but should not be concealed in the archive.

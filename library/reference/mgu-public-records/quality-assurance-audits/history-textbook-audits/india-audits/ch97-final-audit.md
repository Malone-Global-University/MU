## Historical corrections and independent verification

- Corrected the 31 December 1988 India–Pakistan nuclear-installations agreement signatories: **Foreign Secretaries Humayun Khan (Pakistan) and K. P. S. Menon (India)**, not the two prime ministers. Verified against the agreement’s own signature block.
- Clarified Pressler annual-certification fiscal years 1986–90, with noncertification at the start of fiscal 1991 in October 1990, consistent with congressional research.
- Preserved the Pakistani January 1972 Multan meeting and A. Q. Khan’s 1983/1985 Dutch proceedings; added a directly named secondary chronology for the latter.
- Retained the 1985 Sri Lankan foreign-employment legislation and the early-1980s Bangladesh women’s-emigration restrictions, with official/ILO cross-check links.
- Corrected the mathematically unjustified claim that recorded intraregional trade share is necessarily a lower bound for all trade share.
- Added sourced historical RBI/UNCTAD regional and country-level trade statistics and contemporary IMF remittance tables. Differing definitions, denominators and year bases are flagged.

## Source windows and numerical evidence

Eight source windows now name specific diplomatic cables, a treaty, a founding Charter, identifiable SAARC agreements, an ILO 1991 record, a retrospective central-bank statistical compilation, and contemporaneous IMF analyses. Paraphrases are explicitly labeled; no fabricated quotations have been introduced. Some sources are period-near institutional studies or later statistical compilations rather than strict first-hand contemporary records. This is explained in their metadata and is not concealed. The India/Bangladesh/Sri Lanka early remittance points derive from an IMF *Finance & Development* June 1980 table, not a harmonized modern personal-remittance dataset. The trade comparison explicitly separates regional proportions from member-country proportions.

## Disclosures and remaining external limitations

This is a **textual HTML textbook**, not an illustrated historical atlas. Its two map studies are guided textual geography exercises, not embedded maps. The source windows provide scholarly paraphrases and citations to identified source texts; they are not verbatim diplomatic-text facsimiles. External URLs were located and examined where possible through available web evidence; no guarantee is made that every off-site archive/catalog URL will remain accessible indefinitely. More extensive archival validation or direct reprocessing of raw customs microdata is beyond this edition and is not represented as completed. These limitations do not prevent first-edition textual HTML publication.

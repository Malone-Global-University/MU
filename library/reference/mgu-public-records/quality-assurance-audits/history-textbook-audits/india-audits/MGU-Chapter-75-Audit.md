## 3. Historical verification and correction ledger

1. **Mauritius 1834 versus 1849:** The arrival of 36 workers aboard *Atlas* on November 2, 1834 is distinguished from construction of the Aapravasi Ghat immigration depot in 1849. Verified against the Aapravasi Ghat Trust Fund and UNESCO: https://aapravasi.govmu.org/aapravasi/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/AGTF-Newsletter-2009.pdf and https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1227/ .
2. **Bombay mills:** Removed the potentially inflated assertion that Bombay had “well over a hundred” cotton mills in the early twentieth century. Retained the 1854 founding/1856 operations sequence without overclaiming a single all-period count. Comparative reference: https://theory.tifr.res.in/bombay/history/cotton.html .
3. **Colonial factory regulation:** Kept 1881, 1891, and 1911 Acts distinct, including the narrower scope of early legislation. The 1891 statutory record provides a particularly useful specific reference: https://www.indiacode.nic.in/repealedfileopen?rfilename=A1891-11.pdf .
4. **Fiji indenture chronology:** Clarified approximately 60,000 arrivals over 1879–1916; distinguished last arrivals in 1916, suspension of new recruitment in 1917, and completion/termination of remaining Fiji indentures in 1920. Source: https://fijihistory.com/chapters/girmit-end/ .
5. **Fiji individual migration records:** The National Library of Australia confirms over 60,000 digitized emigration passes and the National Archives of Fiji's custody of the originals: https://www.library.gov.au/research/family-history/family-history-research-guide/indian-emigration-passes-fiji-1879-1916 .
6. **Ceylon and Malaya:** The new comparisons distinguish kangani recruitment and intra-regional debt relationships from overseas fixed-term indenture. General historical synthesis source: https://ebooks.inflibnet.ac.in/socp11/chapter/colonial-period-indenture-kangani-andmaistry-systems/ .
7. **Bibliography:** Catalogued editions cross-checked for Tinker (1974), Lal (1983), Carter (1995), Sen (1999), and Ahuja (2009). Precise book-level references are supplied in the HTML.

These checks support the principal revised facts and source descriptions; they are not a claim that every historical statement or all archival editions have been individually verified.

## 8. Editorial controls

The chapter distinguishes institutional records from lived experience, separates different labor-law regimes, resists the assumption that all overseas migrants were indentured, and specifies where interpretations depend on limited statistical categories. Statements about women, workers, and migrant agency are developed without fabricating dialogue, personal testimony, or statistical precision. Chapter 75 ends at the social and labor context for organized nationalism rather than duplicating its political history.

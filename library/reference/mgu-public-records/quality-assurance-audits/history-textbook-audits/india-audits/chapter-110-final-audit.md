## 4. Substantive corrections incorporated into the final HTML

### 4.1 India remittances: measurement, vintage, and attribution

- **Original problem:** The draft used approximately **US$129 billion** as the 2024 remittance figure in the main narrative and source window.
- **Resolution:** Updated the main narrative and primary-source window to **approximately US$137.7 billion for calendar 2024**, while correctly identifying approximately US$129 billion as an **earlier estimate**. The paragraph and exercise now distinguish revised data from contemporaneous estimates and calendar-year from fiscal-year measures.
- **Verification:** Reserve Bank of India annual-report data and the UNCTAD data presentation based on World Bank remittance statistics.
- **Sources:** [RBI annual report](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/AnnualReportPublications.aspx?Id=1432); [UNCTAD remittance data](https://unctadstat.unctad.org/insights/theme/296); [World Bank indicator](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/BX.TRF.PWKR.CD.DT?locations=IN).

### 4.2 Sri Lanka: eligibility for the G20 Common Framework

- **Original problem:** The general account of multilateral debt relief risked presenting Sri Lanka's debt restructuring as a case under the G20 Common Framework.
- **Resolution:** The narrative in §§110.16 and 110.18 now states explicitly that **Sri Lanka was ineligible** for that arrangement and used separate official-creditor coordination and negotiations with bilateral and commercial creditors.
- **Verification:** IMF Working Paper, *Sri Lanka's Sovereign Debt Restructuring: Lessons from Complex Processes* (2025), which directly describes the ineligibility and alternative negotiating process.
- **Source:** [IMF working paper](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2025/09/05/sri-lankas-sovereign-debt-restructuring-lessons-from-complex-processes-570102).

### 4.3 India–European Union trade agreement: negotiation versus effect

- **Original problem:** The manuscript grouped the EU FTA ambiguously among arrangements “under negotiation or ratification,” obscuring the actual January 2026 milestone.
- **Resolution:** §§110.1 and 110.13 now state that the **India–EU FTA negotiations concluded January 27, 2026**, and expressly distinguish this from subsequent signature, domestic approval, and **entry into force**. A further explanatory paragraph contrasts negotiated frameworks with rules actually affecting exporters.
- **Verification:** Indian government announcement and European Commission materials.
- **Sources:** [Government of India](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2219065&lang=1&reg=3); [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/trade/eu-india-trade-agreement_en).

### 4.4 H-1B policy: limited scope of the new payment requirement

- **Original problem:** The manuscript's generalized description of a large visa “fee” increase could suggest universal application to H-1B holders.
- **Resolution:** §110.17 now describes the **US$100,000 payment requirement for specified new petitions involving workers outside the United States**, subject to exceptions; it distinguishes this rule from existing visa holders, extensions, and changes of status. The September 2026 extension is identified.
- **Verification:** 2025 and 2026 White House proclamations and explanatory materials.
- **Sources:** [2025 proclamation](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/09/restriction-on-entry-of-certain-nonimmigrant-workers/); [2026 extension](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/09/restriction-on-entry-of-certain-nonimmigrant-workers-faad/).

### 4.5 2025 US–India defense framework

- **Original problem:** The defense framework was characterized merely as a “reported” arrangement in late 2025.
- **Resolution:** §110.5 now identifies the ten-year major defense partnership framework as **signed October 31, 2025**, while retaining the distinction between a signed framework and its subsequent implementation.
- **Verification:** Government of India defense announcement.
- **Source:** [Indian Ministry of Defence / PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2184622&lang=1&reg=3).

### 4.6 The Islamabad memorandum and the Strait of Hormuz

- **Original problem:** Draft references to the June US–Iran memorandum were too weakly characterized as reports, while November statements about Iranian shipping exemptions could be interpreted as established maritime practice.
- **Resolution:** §110.6 now identifies Pakistan's **June 18, 2026 announcement of an electronically signed Islamabad memorandum** and maintains caution about subsequent implementation. §110.9 attributes the early-November claimed exemptions to **an Iranian parliamentarian**, rather than asserting verified, general operating rules. §110.11 discusses the uncertainty over vessel eligibility and implementation.
- **Verification:** Pakistan's foreign ministry and contemporary reporting of the Iranian official's statement.
- **Sources:** [Pakistan Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://mofa.gov.pk/press-releases/transcript-of-the-weekly-press-briefing-by-the-spokesperson-on-thursday-24th-june-2026); [reported Iranian parliamentary statement](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/6/why-is-iran-exempting-india-china-qatar-iraq-from-hormuz-restrictions).

## 9. Remaining limitations and publication boundaries

- Factual verification was **substantive and selective**, focusing on publication-critical claims, chronology, quantitative data, and the major documentary sources; not every individual narrative sentence received a separately documented primary-source audit.

- The actual insertion of cartographic figures is controlled separately by the textbook publisher. The existing geography studies remain usable without those illustrations.
- Browser rendering was local; live website deployment, server routing, printing behavior, and site-specific integration have not been independently tested.

None of these limitations requires reopening the substantive chapter for another general drafting pass.

## Selected factual/source corrections and verification

1. **William Adam's educational estimates.** The much-repeated claim of approximately 100,000 elementary schools in Bengal and Bihar is expressly an estimated extrapolation in the original first report, not a district-by-district enumeration. The corrected §67.6 distinguishes Adam's 1835 preliminary assessment from later geographically limited investigations. Source: Adam's first report, p. 19 (historical pagination), reproduced in publicly available transcription.
2. **Hindu Widows' Re-marriage Act.** The Bangladesh government's historical statute gives **25 July 1856** for Act XV. The Indian Law Commission's 81st Report states that the Legislative Council passed it on **19 July**, with the Governor-General's assent on **25 July**; a later Vidyasagar biography gives **26 July**, a discrepancy identified without flattening the sources. The chapter retains the official statute's 25 July date.
3. **Vidyasagar petitions.** The Indian Law Commission's historical appendix dates Vidyasagar's petition and bill to **4 October 1855**, describing roughly a thousand supporters. Opposition included petitions by Radhakanta Deb and learned pandits; later high signature totals are not treated as a representative count of the population. The chapter clarifies statutory property and children’s legitimacy provisions.
4. **Sati and Bengal Regulation XVII.** The 4 December 1829 text identifies widow-burning/burying and sets procedures for landholders and police. Descriptions of ritual prevalence in the Regulation are identified as the government's rationale, not neutral nationwide statistics. No uncollated numerical rate of deaths was introduced.
5. **Phule schools and Fatima Sheikh.** Dates associated with **1848** and documented school organization in **1851–52** are distinguished, replacing a sharply asserted **1 January 1848** founding date. Fatima Sheikh's participation is described with appropriate qualification; biographical details remain more limited in contemporaneous sources.
6. **Macaulay, Bentinck, legislation.** Macaulay's **2 February 1835** Minute is kept distinct from Bentinck's **7 March 1835** policy resolution. The **1837 draft Penal Code** is not mislabeled the enacted **1860 Indian Penal Code**. The **1854** Education Despatch and **1857** universities retain their period-accurate relationship.
7. **Legal knowledge, reform, public sphere.** Expansion emphasizes the roles of Indian teachers, translators, petitioners, readers and printers, the limited social access to English education, and the variable impacts of colonial categories rather than sweeping claims of Western intellectual replacement.
8. **Publication design consistency.** Core stylesheet and active sidebar-navigation logic restored from locked Chapter 66 HTML, preserving Chapter 67's division banners. A horizontally scrolling chronology wrapper was added and verified at narrow widths. All additions were directly made within the supplied chapter instead of opening a new series of drafts.

## Documentary/editorial limits retained

- The five so-called primary-source windows are clearly labeled **editorial paraphrases or analytical summaries**, not manuscript-collated facsimile extracts, authentic short quotations, or original-language translations. Source details must be collated against specific witnesses before any direct quotation can be marketed as archival text.
- Two geography features are text-guided and require an external historical atlas; no authentic, dated historical plates are embedded.
- Archive and bibliographical verification is **selective**, not exhaustive across all primary languages or published scholarship.
- The chapter has **not** been validated on the live MGU website; rendered local HTML QA does not establish identical deployment, external link availability, or accessibility conformance.

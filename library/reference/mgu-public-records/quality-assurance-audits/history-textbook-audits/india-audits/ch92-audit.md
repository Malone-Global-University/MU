## 3. Historical accuracy and corrections

**Claims largely verified by independent source checks:**

- Panchsheel was incorporated in the India–China trade/intercourse agreement signed **29 April 1954**. The chronology currently places the **April 1955 Bandung Conference before the April 1954 agreement**; reverse those rows and extend the table to 30–35 entries. See India Ministry of External Affairs: https://www.mea.gov.in/Images/CPV/RTI-V-08-03-2024.pdf
- Bandung was **April 1955**, while the Non-Aligned Movement's first summit was in **Belgrade, September 1961**. The distinction is correctly stated. https://www.nam.go.ug/history
- Security Council **Resolution 211**, dated **20 September 1965**, demanded a ceasefire effective **22 September 1965 at 0700 GMT**. The **Tashkent Declaration was signed 10 January 1966** and provided for withdrawal and normalization; it did *not* initiate the 1965 ceasefire. Replace the glossary entry 'Tashkent Declaration — 10 January 1966 agreement ending the 1965 war' with a historically precise description. https://www.refworld.org/legal/resolution/unsc/1965/en/113010 and https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v25/d278
- Awami League **160 of 162 eastern general seats**, and all **seven reserved women's seats**, yielding **167 of 169 eastern seats**; PPP **81 of 138 western general seats**. The denominators and distinction are correct. https://fafen.org/wp-content/uploads/2010/08/Compendium-National-Assembly-Elections-1970-2008-Pakistan.pdf and https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php?title=Election
- Bangladesh's political chronology **26 March, 10 April, 17 April 1971** is correctly distinguished; however, the narrative omits details about the acting presidency, cabinet, legislation, legitimacy, external relations, and administration. https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-details-367.html and https://www.liberationwarmuseumbd.org/page/history
- The April **6, 1971 Blood Telegram** is correctly characterized as an American diplomatic dissent against the United States' response to repression, but the feature does not identify the precise date or provide a direct link to the primary text. https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v11/d19
- Approximately **10 million refugees** fled into India. The figure is a well-supported rounded estimate, but must not be conflated with camp registrants, internally displaced people, or deaths. https://www.unhcr.org/news/makingdifference/2012/3/4f71c0e46/unhcr-honoured-bangladesh-helping-millions-1971-conflict.html
- **21 November 1962** is the date of China's announced unilateral ceasefire, though technical discussion should also specify its effective date and territorial arrangements. Historical analyses distinguish 20 October and the November offensive phases.

**Scholarly qualifications and improvements:**

1. §92.13 says genocide 'is debated legally'; avoid making this vague claim the principal explanation. The documentary record supports mass atrocities and targeted persecution. Explain legal tests, historical interpretation, contemporaneous genocide language, and differences among mortality estimates without implying uncertainty about the overall reality of atrocities. See Gary J. Bass, Cambridge World History of Genocide (2023): https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-world-history-of-genocide/bangladesh-1971/1F1253C1C9B4F4B36909A32AB7DA76DF
2. Distinguish **Bangladesh's official three-million-deaths claim**, alternative studies, direct deaths and excess refugee mortality, the effects of malnutrition/disease, and methodological limitations. One 2025 demographic study estimated about **562,915 excess deaths among refugees** with uncertainty intervals; this is not a directly interchangeable estimate of all conflict deaths. https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0320760
3. §92.17 rightly distinguishes full-scale interstate war on 3 December from the preceding conflict; expand the period of earlier Indian support and border engagements. State attribution for the air attacks carefully, source the sequence, and distinguish the eastern and western theaters.
4. §92.20's three-war comparison needs the different causes, strategic objectives, participants, military outcomes, settlements, and political consequences, not just one-line explanations.
5. Avoid treating Kashmir's accession, the McMahon Line and Aksai Chin as undisputed legal facts; faithfully attribute competing national accounts and distinguish documentary claims from historiographical interpretation.

No pervasive invented quotations or major chronology-wide fabrications were identified in this compact text. But the text has too little analysis and almost no source documentation to support a positive scholarly publication verdict.

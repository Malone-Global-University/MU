## Independent verification of requested historical specifics

1. **Madurai/Thanjavur Nayaka traditions:** Qualified framing is correct. The foundation of a dynasty by Visvanatha at Madurai is not securely tied to one conventional 1529/1559 date; inscriptions place him in official service by 1532, and traditions about governorship must be distinguished from the later dynastic outcome. Thanjavur's Sevappa dates likewise differ by source; general regional discussion and hedged wording are adequate. Sources: [IGNCA, *The Nayakas of Madurai*](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/65812.pdf); [Noboru Karashima, *The Medieval History Journal*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/097194580100400103); [Thanjavur district administration](https://thanjavur.nic.in/nayaks-history/).
2. **Dutch at Pulicat:** Permissible under the early seventeenth-century continuation. Initial Dutch trading presence around 1609, permission for establishment in 1610, subsequent consolidation; chapter does not project Dutch control into the sixteenth-century kingdom. Sources: [Cambridge *Itinerario* 2026](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/itinerario/article/between-segregation-and-permeability-the-vocs-spatial-dilemma-in-zeelandia-facing-external-actors-and-intervening-groups-16241662/0CD6E67357FA73487FCD7A938C30ADE9); [South Indian History Congress, Pulicat](https://journal.southindianhistorycongress.org/journals/articles/2016/SIHC_2016_V36_163.pdf).
3. **Timoja/Timayya of Honavar:** Timayya was a Honavar-based corsair and Portuguese informant/collaborator in the 1510 capture of Goa, not necessarily a straightforward imperial governor. Publication phrasing corrected from unqualified 'coastal chieftain' to 'Honavar-based maritime captain and corsair'. Source: [Sanjay Subrahmanyam, Cambridge *Itinerario* (2026), *The Islamic World and the Making of the Portuguese Empire*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/itinerario/article/islamic-world-and-the-making-of-the-portuguese-empire-14501520/D929E374D7ABBFD7145A0369C4E5CB16).
4. **Chamarasa:** *Prabhulinga Lile* conventionally attributed to Chamarasa, presented to Deva Raya II's court; wording supported. Source: [Royal Asiatic Society archival description](https://www.royalasiaticarchives.org/index.php/a-partial-translation-of-the-prabhu-linga-leelai).
5. **Lepakshi:** Veerabhadra complex and murals strongly associated with Vijayanagara-period work under Achyuta Raya and local patrons Virupanna and Viranna; supported by inscriptions. Source: [UNESCO Tentative List, Lepakshi](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6607/).
6. **Mahmud Gawan:** Richard M. Eaton establishes that Dabhol was already one of the Bahmani sultanate's main western maritime ports in 1453 and that Gawan captured Goa in February 1472. Corrected sentence inaccurately grouped Dabhol with Goa as a 1472 conquest from Vijayanagara. Source: [Eaton, *A Social History of the Deccan*, Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/social-history-of-the-deccan-13001761/mahmud-gawan-14111481-deccanis-and-westerners/905D841D5CE0D775A3619A22E8071818).
7. **Krishna image at Hampi (additional publication correction):** The preceding audit mistakenly recorded the image's *installation* as 1514. Reinspection of the dated Krishna Temple inscription indicates installation/consecration on **16 February 1515** (the Udayagiri campaign/capture and temple construction can properly be assigned to c. 1513–1514). Corrected both chronology preview and §45.11. Sources: [Government Museum, Chennai, *Holistic Approach to Dating*, PDF p. 144](https://govtmuseumchennai.org/uploads/topics/16702340773243.pdf); [archaeological specialist monograph, *Balakrishna Temple at Hampi*](https://www.exoticindiaart.com/book/details/balakrishna-temple-at-hampi-hax775/).
8. **Tuluva accession:** Narasa Nayaka and then Vira Narasimha held effective power before 1505, but **Vira Narasimha's formal royal accession was in 1505**, not 1503. Corrected both §45.9 and the end glossary. Source: [Vira Narasimha historical/inscriptional summary](https://teluguhistory.org/en/people/vira-narasimha-tuluva/); comparative academic dynastic table at [University of Cologne dissertation](https://kups.ub.uni-koeln.de/73638/2/Dissertation_Nguyen_Duy_Chinh.pdf).
9. **Malik Kafur:** Southern expeditions included the 1311 Dorasamudra/Pandya campaigns, making 'first decade of the fourteenth century' inaccurate for the whole set. Changed to 'between 1307 and 1311'. Source: [*Historical Inscriptions of Southern India*](https://www.sabrizain.org/malaya/library/historicalinscrips.pdf), section on the 1311 campaign.
10. **Madras 1639:** Original grant from **Damarla Venkatappa/Venkatapathy Nayaka** to Francis Day in August 1639; Andrew Cogan endorsed the Company record, and royal confirmation was distinct. Corrected §45.29's somewhat imprecise dual-agent attribution and personal naming. Sources: [British Library India Office Records, IOR/E/3/17 ff.60–65](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/041-002450079); [Greater Chennai Corporation's history](https://chennaicorporation.gov.in/gcc/about-GCC/about-chennai/origin-and-growth/).
11. **Glossary alignment:** Defined the field of epigraphy in §45.1, named the Portuguese Estado da Índia in §45.25, clarified references to sati stones in §45.18, and replaced the glossary definition that incorrectly limited them to 'elite' widows. Restored the third vocabulary feature's sidebar link.

Earlier verified dates (Talikota's disputed day, the early 1510s Deccan and eastern campaigns, 1515 Kondavidu, 1550 Ramamatya, 1639 Madras grant) retain source-critical qualifications. Historical perspectives and traveler paraphrases remain clearly labeled as reconstructions, not primary-source quotations. No major additional factual problem was identified in the sampled chapter-wide review.

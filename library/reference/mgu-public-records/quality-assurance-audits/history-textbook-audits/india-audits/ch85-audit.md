## Historical verification and corrections

1. **1946 provincial electoral returns:** replaced original five-province mixed-definition overview with **all eleven** British Indian provinces. The final table draws consistently from Sho Kuwajima, “Post-War Freedom Movement and 1946 Provincial Elections in India,” *Ajia-gaku Ronso* (Osaka University of Foreign Studies), no. 2 (1992), pp. 18–19, table compiled from historical return documentation and other works. Stable university-hosted primary scholarly resource: https://hdl.handle.net/11094/99647 . Table labels identify the source's **Congress all-category seat totals**, Muslim League totals and legislative strengths, rather than falsely labeling Congress all-party totals as general-only victories. The manuscript identifies alternative classifications (notably Punjab, Bengal and Sind) instead of mixing sources. The Central Assembly result of Congress 57 of 102 elected seats and League 30 of 30 Muslim seats is retained.
2. **Bengal and Punjab assembly ballots:** retained correct June 1947 sectional tallies: Bengal **106–35 against** partition in Muslim-majority section, **58–21 for** in non-Muslim-majority section; Punjab **69–27 against** in western section and **50–22 for** in eastern section. Added separate joint-sitting **Constituent Assembly affiliation** votes, Bengal **126–90** and Punjab **91–77** for the new Assembly, with document anchor: https://nehruarchive.in/documents/to-lord-mountbatten-24-june-1947-yj1llv . Removed instruction saying these correct numbers still required checking.
3. **British agency in the 3 June process:** no longer accepts the British government's self-description of provincial choice as a neutral assertion that Partition was “not imposed by the British”; separates British design of options and timetable from provincial votes and party consent.
4. **Princely states:** clarified that lapse of Crown paramountcy and treaty obligations under the Indian Independence Act did not promise automatic diplomatic recognition or viability of permanent independent princely states. Detailed integration remains assigned to Chapter 87.
5. **Opening teleology:** qualified assertion that nearly everyone expected one united India despite the Muslim League's Pakistan demand. Preserves argument that colonial withdrawal and the political form of independence were distinct decisions.
6. **Parliamentary constitutional source checks:** Wavell Plan (14 June 1945), Cabinet Mission statement (16 May 1946), parliamentary grouping explanations (18 July 1946), Attlee February 1947 transfer commitment, 3 June 1947 transfer plan and Indian Independence Act (18 July 1947) identified in the documentary register. Historical inquiry remains selective rather than exhaustive critical-edition collation.

## 3. Historical correction matrix: resolved

### H1. R. C. Dutt bibliographic chronology — **CORRECTED**

The first editions are distinguished from later two-volume reprints:
- Dadabhai Naoroji, *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India* (1901).
- Romesh Chunder Dutt, *The Economic History of British India* (1902), principally through 1837.
- Romesh Chunder Dutt, *India in the Victorian Age: An Economic History of the People* (1904), covering 1837–1900.

Updated §72.19, the combined source window and the 1901–1904 chronology entry. No obsolete Dutt “1901/1903” publication dates remain. References: https://openlibrary.org/books/OL32095506M/The_economic_history_of_India ; https://catalogue.nli.ie/Record/vtls000386488 ; https://openlibrary.org/books/OL6913043M/Poverty_and_un-British_rule_in_India .

### H2. Famine-insurance budget and 1880 commission — **CORRECTED**

Separated the famine-insurance provision initiated in the financial policy of **1878–1879** from the commission reporting in **1880**. The approximately **£1.5 million annual budget provision** was **not** an untouchable, separate cash fund; contemplated applications included famine relief, protective works, and debt reduction, and its operation was disrupted by war and fiscal pressures. The commission's subsequent focus on administrative precautions, relief organization, and protective works is presented on its own terms. Updated §72.5, §72.18, and the Commission source window.

Key documentary references: https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1879/may/22/committee-first-night ; https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1881/aug/22/the-annual-financial-statement ; British Library India Office Records, IOR/L/PARL/2/173/1 and IOR/L/PARL/2/174, https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/041-001116783 .

### H3. Great Famine of 1876–1878 mortality — **CORRECTED / QUALIFIED**

Replaced the original unspecific figure of about five million “estimated by the 1880 Commission” with a careful description of **approximately 5½ million excess deaths in famine-affected British-administered areas as a frequently cited historical estimate**, distinguishing it from estimates that include princely states or apply later demographic baselines. Clarified that registered starvation deaths, excess deaths, reduced births, and census population shortfalls are different measures and cannot be added as interchangeable totals. Expanded the discussion of timing, geographical heterogeneity, relief policy, nutrition, disease, and mortality evidence. This remains a **qualified estimate, not an independently recalculated historical death count**.

### H4. Railways and colonial finance — **PRECISION IMPROVED**

- Replaced the imprecise “perhaps 9,000” miles around 1880 with approximately **9,160 miles** at end-1880, and the “on the order of 25,000” by 1900 with approximately **24,760 miles**.
- Identified **John Hurd's estimate** for railway freight social savings in 1900 as **approximately Rs. 1.2 billion or 9% of national income** under a specific counterfactual, not a direct measurement of an observed 9% rise in GDP.
- Explained the distinct issue of guaranteed shareholders' returns and the fiscal distribution of investment risks.
- Added Dave Donaldson's quantitative findings on transport costs and price gaps, carefully distinguishing average/modelled gains from the experience of every household.
- Clarified Home Charges and included a dated **roughly £16 million (1893)** benchmark as an indicative contemporary total, not a universal annual amount. Qualified silver/sterling exchange and the 1893–1899 policy sequence.

Scholarship: Dan Bogart and Latika Chaudhary, *Railways in Colonial India: An Economic Achievement?*, https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~dbogart/Railways%2C%20Chapter%2C%20June%202012.pdf ; Dave Donaldson (2018), https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.20101199 .

## 5. Publication limitations and precise authorization scope

This is **approved as a complete, scholarly first-edition historical-text standalone HTML**, **not** as a fully facsimile-collated critical edition of primary documents.

1. Three Primary Source Windows are editorial paraphrases/syntheses, explicitly disclosed; no page-by-page comparison with the Dalhousie Minute, the Strachey Report's scanned volumes, or all Naoroji/Dutt passages was conducted.
2. Source research and numerical checks were targeted at the previously flagged material and important new analysis; they do not certify exhaustive verification of every historical statement, every edition, or the complete archival statistical record.
3. Famine mortality estimates and early economic accounts remain inherently uncertain and are treated as qualified historical reconstructions, not exact counts.
4. The map study is **text-guided** and requires access to an appropriate external period atlas; an actual geospatial plate is not embedded.
5. The Chapter 2 reference file has not been byte-for-byte independently compared with this draft; the model-derived style and script are preserved from Claude's uploaded chapter, with the responsive addition.
6. Browser checks used Chromium's rendering of the standalone HTML; actual live MGU web deployment and server integration were **not** tested.

## Source verification and editorial action

1. **Khan Jahan Ali / Bagerhat (§47.6)** — Accepted. UNESCO identifies the fifteenth-century urban complex with Ulugh Khan Jahan; mosques, tanks, roads, bridges and the 1459 tomb support the chapter's measured interpretation. Source: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/321
2. **Shihabuddin Daulatabadi (§47.25)** — Accepted, carefully presented as credited works, not verbatim citations. A Jaunpur local history lists *Sharh-i-Kafiya*, *Kitab-i-Arshad*, works on legal reasoning, and *Bahr-i-Mawwaj* commentary. Source: https://www.eng.hamarajaunpur.com/2022/07/malik-ul-ulama-qazi-shihab-ud-din.html
3. **Champaner agreement (§47.20)** — The alliance against Mewar in 1456 is supported, including by historical accounts of the 1457 campaign. **Clarified that Gujarat's king in 1456 was Qutb-ud-Din Ahmad Shah II** (not the later Mahmud Begada). Sources: https://www.wbnsou.ac.in/online_services/SLM_UGC_DEB/UG/SOSS/HHI/2nd%20Year/CC-HI-07.pdf ; https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/35322.pdf
4. **Malwa / Delhi clash (§47.20)** — Fixed broad '1440s' to **1440**; contemporary Sayyid monarchy defended with forces of Bahlul Lodi. Source: UNESCO History of Civilizations of Central Asia Vol IVa, https://en.unesco.org/silkroad/sites/default/files/knowledge-bank-article/vol_IVa%20silk%20road_the%20delhi%20sultanate.pdf
5. **Chak-era Shi'i missionaries (§47.33)** — Accepted the general emergence of Shi'i networks and Shams al-Din Iraqi's influence, but restricted undocumented sweeping geographic origin and named him specifically. Source: https://www.muslimsocieties.org/vol_8_no_2_the_spread_of_shiism_in_kashmir/ and https://isca.in/IJSS/Archive/v4/i4/10.ISCA-IRJSS-2014-322.php
6. **Jaunpur Jami Masjid (§47.25)** — Clarified **foundation in 1438 under Ibrahim Shah and completion under Husain Shah in the 1470s**. Some secondary sources disagree on the exact completion year; the publication text wisely avoids a disputed exact year. Sources: Jaunpur District Government https://jaunpur.nic.in/tourist-place/jama-masjid/ and ASI old survey https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/21556.pdf
7. **Karma Shah / Shatrunjaya (§47.17)** — Qualified to the documented **c.1530** restoration with permission from **Bahadur Shah of Gujarat**, rather than an imprecise early-1530s date. Shalin Jain (2012), *Patrons, Temples and Pilgrimages: The Jain Community in Medieval India*, *Studies in History* 28(1):19–42, and Jain inscriptional traditions: https://www.scribd.com/document/850413837/jain-2013-patrons-temples-and-pilgrimages-the-jain-community-in-medieval-india
8. **Mandu tower (§47.21)** — Removed overconfident inference that its purpose was definitely to rival Chittor's Vijay Stambha; retained as a comparison, with motive explicitly uncertain.
9. **Jain officials at Hoshang Shah's court (§47.19)** — Toned down the generalization that expertise mattered 'more than' religion for all appointments, which the limited inscriptions / colophons cannot support.
10. **Jaunpur's Akbari Bridge (§47.26)** — Accepted the late-1560s date and Munim Khan's role: dated epigraphy supports construction beginning 1564 and completion in 1568. Source: Archaeological Survey https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/21557.pdf
11. **Previous audit fixes preserved.** The 1494–1519 Bengal Husain Shah chronology, Maladhar Basu chronology, Sharqi–Lodi distinction, Jaunpur Atala completion in 1408, Zain-ul-Abidin cow-slaughter policy, removal of maize anachronism, and qualified Kashmir source criticism survive the expansion.

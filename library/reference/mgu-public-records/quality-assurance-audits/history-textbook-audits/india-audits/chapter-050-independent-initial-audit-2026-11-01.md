## Historical checks and consolidated correction record

1. **§50.1 — birth place:** Original asserted Andijan unequivocally. Andijan is widely reported, but Stephen F. Dale's Cambridge biography places Babur's birth at Akhsi. The corrected text specifies the Ferghana Valley and explains the two identifications; avoids treating a contested birthplace as definitive. Evidence: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/babur/introduction/52A197DEA5C9F846739693E0BF75CE19 and https://gov.uz/en/yoshlar/news/view/128024 .
2. **§50.34 — two separate 1555 battles:** Original described the **June 22 Battle of Sirhind** as an army led for Sikandar Sur by **Tatar Khan**. Tatar Khan commanded an earlier Afghan force near **Machhiwara**; **Sikandar Sur himself** commanded at Sirhind. Corrected the sequence, preserving Bairam Khan and Humayun. Evidence: https://rarebooksocietyofindia.org/book_archive/196174216674_10155773244261675.pdf and https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/14092.pdf and https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/blogs/shooting-straight/battles-for-india-at-sirhind/ .
3. **§50.35 — Sher Mandal:** Original described Sher Shah as unequivocal builder of the octagonal pavilion. Attribution to Sher Shah versus Humayun is contested. Corrected wording; note Humayun used it as a library. Evidence: https://doi.org/10.1080/02666030.2019.1605564 and https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/delhi/palace-observatory-many-shades-of-sher-mandal-where-humayun-met-his-end-9396576/ .
4. **§50.35 — Humayun's tomb patronage:** Original wrongly credited **Hamida Banu Begum**. The primary traditional patron was **Bega Begum (Haji Begum)**, while Akbar's dynastic support is also discussed. Corrected. Evidence: https://whc.unesco.org/archive/advisory_body_evaluation/232.pdf and https://culture.gov.in/humayuns-tomb-delhi and https://www.sahapedia.org/humayuns-tomb-conversation-catherine-asher .
5. **§50.28 — coinage:** The conventional ~178-grain (~11.5g) *rupiya* standard is supported by the Reserve Bank of India and State Bank of Pakistan museum descriptions; exact individual extant coins vary. Fitzwilliam's 1543–44 Gwalior specimen provides catalogued primary material. Evidence: https://data.fitzmuseum.cam.ac.uk/id/object/92776 and https://www.sbp.org.pk/museum/suri-dynasty .
6. **§50.29 — sarai spacing:** Narrative appropriately attributes 'two kos' to Sarwani rather than universal empirically measured intervals. Different later chronicles report different spacing; preserve attribution and avoid precise regional generalization. Source: https://persian.packhum.org/text/080201014/4 .
7. **§50.32 — Safavid court:** The July 1544 meeting was in the Qazvin environs; the city only later became the Safavid capital. Manuscript calls this simply the court 'at Qazvin' and does not incorrectly assign it capital status. Source: Laura Parodi study (searchable as *Humayun's Sojourn at the Safavid Court*), and https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/tahmasp-i/ .
8. **§50.35 — death:** January 1556 fall and death shortly thereafter are consistent with substantial sources, although exact fall/death dates vary among historical traditions. The manuscript does not assign an unsupported precise death date. Source: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/homayun-padesah/ .
9. **Baburnama and source genres:** Timurid paternal descent via Miran Shah supported by Stephen Dale; Sarwani's work was commissioned under Akbar, not contemporary to Sher Shah's reign. Gulbadan's memoir produced c. 1586–87; edition Beveridge 1902. Evidence: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/babur/introduction/52A197DEA5C9F846739693E0BF75CE19 ; https://fass.nus.edu.sg/hist/eia/historical-texts-archive/ ; https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/golbadan-begom/ ; https://www.si.edu/object/history-humayun-humayun-nama-gul-badan-begam-princess-rose-body-translated-introduction-notes:siris_sil_325090 .
10. **Contested traditions:** Rana Sanga's alleged invitation, the water-carrier story, Babur's life-offering tradition, the Chittor appeal, Sher Shah's attributed remarks, and Todar Mal's early involvement are generally appropriately qualified. Maintain those caveats; do not turn them into verified dialogue or quantitative claims.

## IV. Governing outline and the Chapter 69–71 boundary: authoritative ruling

The expanded Chapter 69 assignment assigned §§69.13–69.18 to warfare, counteroffensives, repression, the Government of India Act, and the Crown settlement. However the governing `India History Chapter Outline(1).txt` explicitly reserves these as the **primary subjects of Chapter 70**, titled *Suppression, Retribution, and the End of Company Rule*. Chapter 71 is *Crown Rule and the Structure of the British Raj*.

**Resolution:** retain the approved 20-section Chapter 69 architecture but treat §§69.13–69.18 as narrative overview and necessary consequences of the rebellion, not as the exhaustive final treatment. Several repeating paragraphs were cut and explicit handoffs to Chapter 70 inserted. The editorial allocation for the next two chapters is:

- **Chapter 69:** causes, outbreak, spread, leaders, rebel and civilian participation, regional contrasts, core campaign chronology, character and meaning of rebellion, the basic constitutional outcome.
- **Chapter 70:** primary detailed inquiry into the British counteroffensive, regional limits, Indian allies, collective reprisals, Delhi and Awadh retribution, the Act of 1858, the Queen's Proclamation, army reconstruction, and the immediate imperial settlement.
- **Chapter 71:** durable institutions, powers of secretary of state and viceroy, provinces and districts, civil service, princes and paramountcy, army and law under Crown government.

**Do not remove §§69.13–69.18** as that would require redesigning the approved twenty-section architecture; develop Chapter 70 with an evidence-led analysis of suppression and settlement rather than rewriting 69. Chapter 70 has a distinct analytical responsibility notwithstanding deliberate chronological overlap.

## V. Independently checked historical details and corrections

1. **1856–57 Awadh chief commissioners:** Corrected overly simplified Outram-to-Lawrence succession: James Outram, Coverley Jackson (1856–March 1857), Henry Lawrence (from March 1857). The British Library catalogues separate correspondence from all three, `Mss Eur F699/1/1/1/23`.
2. **General service in 1856:** Corrected anachronistic or loosely applied title *General Service Enlistment Act*: the 25 July 1856 measure is documented as a **general-service enlistment order** (General Order no. 1012), applying to new recruits, not retroactively imposing identical obligations on every serving soldier.
3. **Meerut:** Distinguished the 9 May public humiliation and imprisonment of 85 troopers from the 10 May rebellion, and 11 May seizure of Delhi; replaced an unverified source-window document with a named contemporary Meerut jail report discussed by Clare Anderson (2007).
4. **Delhi's rebel government:** Replaced the **uncertain Azamgarh Proclamation source attribution** with an actual documentary witness: Delhi Administration Court regulations in the *Mutiny Papers*, 1921 press-list no. **57-539**, reproduced by S. K. Banerji in Indian Historical Records Commission Proceedings XXIV, pp. 47–50 and National Archives of India (2007), pp. 21–26. The paper is undated and unsealed; the text does **not** treat it as an indisputably executed formal constitution. The Azamgarh declaration remains mentioned as a contested-provenance document, not authenticated as an order of the emperor.
5. **Awadh rebel court:** Replaced an unidentified proclamation with the identifiable petition and royal orders associated with Birjis Qadr, published by Nandalal Chatterji in Indian Historical Records Commission Proceedings XXXV, pp. 64–65 and National Archives of India (2007), pp. 101–103. Clearly identified as a petition and orders (not falsely a proclamation). The Royal Collection supports the context and dated **December 1858** counter-proclamation of Hazrat Mahal, citing NAI Foreign Political Consultations, 31 December 1858, no. 3022.
6. **Bahadur Shah Zafar / Hodson:** Corrected surrender chronology to **21 September 1857**, with the killing of three princes on **22 September**, as recorded by the National Army Museum. The deaths are not presented as justified or as an established battlefield necessity.
7. **Kunwar Singh:** Confirmed Jagdishpur success on **23 April 1858**, death on **26 April**, with Bihar government's identified regional history and peer-reviewed evidence. The contested story of his Ganga wound is not inflated into an unverified definitive death mechanism.
8. **Lakshmibai:** Retained the campaign's **17 June 1858** fighting at Kotah-ki-Serai, documented by the National Army Museum, while noting 18 June in some commemorative accounts. Do not falsely claim the details of her death are uniformly documented.
9. **Peel Commission:** Qualified ratios as **recommendations** of approximately 1 European to 2 Indian cavalry/infantry personnel in Bengal and 1 to 3 in Madras/Bombay, **not a universal, instantly implemented whole-army ratio**. Qualified the artillery recommendation to reflect exceptions, and post-rebellion recruitment reforms as evolving rather than immediate uniform implementation.
10. **Rebel and British violence:** Retained the evidence of both, but removed an unsupported quantitative comparison from the chapter opening. Avoided invented total casualty figures; differentiated structure and agency of colonial reprisals and rebel killings without a false equivalence.
11. **Statute:** Verified the **Government of India Act 1858** as **21 & 22 Vict. c. 106**, royal assent **2 August 1858**, against UK legislation as enacted; removed unverified precise commencement date from prose. The Crown proclamation of **1 November 1858** matches BL **IOR/L/PS/20/MEMO32/1**.
12. **Bibliography:** Checked original-year/edition identities against Cambridge/Oxford bibliographies and catalogues for Rudrangshu Mukherjee (1984), Eric Stokes (1978, 1986), Tapti Roy (1994), S. N. Sen (1957), R. C. Majumdar (1957), William Dalrymple (2006), and original writing in 1858 / English publication in 1873 for Syed Ahmad Khan. Savarkar (1909) is retained as historical nationalist interpretation rather than endorsed contemporary evidence.

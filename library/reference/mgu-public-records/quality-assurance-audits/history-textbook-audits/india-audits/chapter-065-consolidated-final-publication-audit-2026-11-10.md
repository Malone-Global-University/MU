## 3. Consolidated substantive historical correction register

1. **Treaty with Hyderabad amirs, 1839 (§65.6 and Source Window 2):** distinguished Lord Auckland's March 11 signature/approval from individual amirs' counterpart documents. Added the authenticated treaty's 14-article architecture, British stationed force (maximum 5,000), separate annual contributions of one lakh each by Nur Muhammad, Nasir Muhammad and Muhammad Khan, Mir Sobdar's exemption, 3,000 auxiliary troops under specified conditions, political supervision and limits to the duty-free navigation provision. Corrected the draft's misleading implication that the 1839 treaty did not authorize a stationed force: it did; it **did not authorize outright annexation**.
2. **Treaty of Lahore, 1846 (§65.11):** distinguished March 9 treaty from March 11 supplementary articles concerning British troops in Lahore. Clarified complex relationship between indemnity, cession of Kashmir/Hazara as assessed territorial compensation and Gulab Singh's separate 75-lakh obligation. Avoid treating that number as the total indemnity paid by Lahore; the Company also received the Jalandhar Doab.
3. **Treaty of Bhairowal, 1846 (§65.12 and Source Window 4):** verified 22 lakh Nanakshahi rupees per annum for Company military establishment (Article 9), 1.5 lakh Maharani maintenance allowance (Article 10), and expected termination at age sixteen in September 1854 (Article 11); retained December 16 signature versus December 26 ratification distinction.
4. **Lahore Residents (§§65.12–65.13):** corrected/expanded timeline: Henry Lawrence leave late 1847; John Lawrence temporarily acting; Frederick Currie in office beginning March 1848; Henry resumed February 1849. These distinctions are material to assigning responsibility for the Multan events.
5. **Multan crisis and causes of the second war (§65.13):** expanded political context, differentiated Mulraj's disputed role in murders from later military rebellion; discussed Sher Singh and Chattar Singh and differentiated Punjabi collaboration with Company/Bahawalpur from resistance.
6. **Punjab annexation, March 1849 (§65.15 and Source Window 5):** distinguished acquisition of sovereign rights, seizure of state property and the Koh-i-Noor from the aggregate pension of 4–5 lakh rupees. Clarified the minor ruler's inability to negotiate freely and later ratification; treaty paraphrases not misrepresented as autograph quotations.
7. **First British Punjab administration (§65.16):** added documented March 31, 1849 creation of Board in British Library IOR/H/760 and explanatory institutional source categories; distinguish professed reforms from proof of uniform local outcomes.
8. **Sikh control of Peshawar (§65.2):** clarified indirect/tributary influence from about 1818, renewed intervention in 1823 and direct government in 1834, instead of presenting 1818 as unambiguous permanent annexation.
9. **Duleep Singh and Jind Kaur:** corrected reunion to January 1861, supported by Historic Royal Palaces, not 1860.
10. **Historiography and bibliography:** removed avoidable categorical superlative about Sikh military scale. Confirmed major works by Jean-Marie Lafont, Priya Atwal, J. S. Grewal, Hamida Khuhro, H. T. Lambrick and Tan Tai Yong; corrected *Modern Sindh* spelling and recorded 1978 original/1999 second edition.

**Preserved critical qualifications:** contested motivations of the December 1845 Sutlej crossing; unproven blanket allegations of Sikh treachery; uncertain mortality and casualty counts; reported versus demonstrated motives of Napier and the Talpur amirs; separate legal force of 1809, 1839, March/December 1846 and 1849 instruments; nonuniform extension of colonial authority to independent frontier communities.

## 3. Historical verification and necessary corrections

### Verified main chronology and governing structure

1. The 1563 pilgrimage-tax and 1564 *jizya* abolitions are standard historically cited dates, although implementation in every locality is not recoverable. The manuscript avoids claiming an independently demonstrated general reimposition during Akbar's reign. Main source: Stephen P. Blake, “Religious Conflict in Early Modern India,” University of Minnesota Libraries: https://umnlibraries.manifoldapp.org/read/religious-conflict/section/d980280b-a32a-4911-97d3-2ae0325e78dc.
2. The *Ibadat Khana* began in 1575; the *mahzar* was issued in 1579 after intensified religious debate. The document gave Akbar a bounded adjudicatory role where learned jurists disagreed, **not unqualified infallibility**. Blake, source above; *Time in Early Modern Islam*, Cambridge University Press, chapter on the Safavid, Mughal and Ottoman empires: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/time-in-early-modern-islam/safavid-mughal-and-ottoman-empires/9D55F0A0262017473EC8A9A7ED86C508.
3. The *dahsala* settlement belongs to the evolving reforms around 1580–1581 and the standardized *Ilahi gaz* to the approximately thirty-first regnal year (1586–1587), **not one single invention by Todar Mal in an arbitrarily precise year**. Primary translated regulatory passage: https://persian.packhum.org/text/000702052/2. The original chronology was broadly correct and remains suitably qualified.
4. The 1578 extraordinary hunting episode is documented, but Abu'l-Fazl's and other authors' interpretations of its mystical significance are not interchangeable with an independently recoverable psychological event. The revised §52.11 distinguishes the emperor's reported stopping of the hunt from inferred doctrinal transformation. Relevant studies: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/modern-asian-studies/article/anger-and-atonement-in-mughal-india-an-alternative-account-of-akbars-1578-hunt/ and an accessible Persian translated episode at https://hindiurduflagship.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/Episodes-in-the-life-of-Akbar.pdf.
5. A local tradition and scholarly source-critical debate distinguish Lahore from Laharpur (Awadh) as Todar Mal's birthplace. §52.2 now states the dispute rather than asserting Punjab as fact. An archival-gazetteer discussion: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Gazetteer_of_the_province_of_Oudh_..._%28IA_cu31924073057345%29.pdf/306.
6. In §52.13, Makhdum al-Mulk's death is now distinguished from Shaikh Abd al-Nabi's later death (respectively 1582 and approximately 1584). Historical reference: Vincent A. Smith, *Akbar the Great Mogul*, accessible at https://www.loc.gov/resource/gdc.18002677/?sp=209&st=text. Events and motives should be read critically because this is an older synthesis.
7. The imperial *Razmnama* was commissioned in 1582 and translated in the early-to-mid 1580s. Abu'l-Fazl's preface is now dated approximately 1586–1587, with transmission complexity acknowledged; the Persian *Ramayana* belongs to the later 1580s. Sources: Michael Willis et al., *Translation and State*, https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110501520-001/pdf; Audrey Truschke, *The Persian Text of the Doha Ramayana*, https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5fd8d19b84774a17d4cd0bf7/t/5fd8e9c59f0c5d7a93a3c4b6/1608051149170/Truschke%2BPersian%2BText%2Bof%2Bthe%2BDoha%2BRamayana%2B2020.pdf.
8. The account of *Tawhid-i Ilahi* correctly treats the circle as a small, elite spiritual discipleship rather than an organized mass religion. Stephen P. Blake, cited above; supplemented in §52.15 without imposing modern theological categories.

### Additional substantive first-edition narrative reinforcement

- §52.6 distinguishes the *aml-guzar* and *bitikchi* from locally based *qanungo* personnel, using the imperial regulations directly.
- §52.10 distinguishes religious levies from ongoing agrarian revenue obligations and explains what constitutes evidence of enforcement.
- §52.14 distinguishes the normative language of *sulh-i kul* from a universal legal guarantee of equality and traces grants and accommodations as concrete policy.
- §52.15 grounds the social composition of the disciples and avoids turning formulaic pledges into proof of changed personal beliefs.
- §52.19 uses the specific opening of the *A'in-i Akbari* to explain *farr-i izadi*, ideal moral kingship, and limits of panegyric as evidence.

These are targeted development and verification actions, **not a new section-expansion program**.

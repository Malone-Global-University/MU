## 5. Targeted historical and documentary corrections (directly applied)

1. **§53.2 Guru Arjan:** made Jahangir’s documented execution order explicit; did not reduce the event to a vague punishment or imply that source traditions agree in every detail.
2. **§53.2 Indian chapter cross-reference:** Chapter 59 properly concerns eighteenth-century Sikh military formations. Revised the sentence to distinguish Guru Hargobind’s seventeenth-century transformations, Chapter 54, and the later trajectory in Chapter 59.
3. **§53.3 Jahangirnama:** clarified Jahangir authored the narrative into the seventeenth regnal year after a first twelve-year circulated version; Muʿtamad Khan and later Muhammad Hadi continued it. Corrected source-window dating and specified the Rogers/Beveridge edition and initial regnal year.
4. **Nur Jahan documentary window:** narrowed the dating description of named coinage to the later reign, prominently the 1620s. Coins and sealed orders do not establish exclusive personal control over all policies.
5. **§53.9 Padshahnama:** distinguished a full multi-volume court-history project from the Windsor illustrated first-decade copy (Royal Collection Trust RCIN 1005025, copied 1656–57); did not present the illustration cycle as direct eyewitness evidence for the entire reign.
6. **§53.11 Ahmadnagar:** identified Husain Shah as the reigning Nizam Shahi prince captured after Daulatabad; clarified that further Nizam Shahi claimants were supported by Shahaji and did not disappear immediately.
7. **§53.11 1636 Deccan settlements:** eliminated the materially inaccurate claim that Bijapur and Golconda both undertook the same khutba and annual tribute obligations. Golconda’s terms included khutba/coinage and annual tribute; Bijapur’s settlement is more accurately described by one-time indemnity, frontier/territorial settlement, and recognition of Mughal superior power. Sources differ in broad descriptions of Bijapur’s vassal status, so specific treaty terms take precedence.
8. **§53.12 Kandahar:** removed the unsupported claim that Mughal besiegers possessed superior usable artillery; heavy siege-gun transport, logistics and Safavid defensive strength limited the attempts.
9. **§53.15 and Taj source window:** replaced an overly categorical assertion that Amanat Khan’s signature was on the great gateway. His signed dated work is securely documented inside the mausoleum, especially 1048 AH/1638–39. Attribution of work at the gateway needs distinction between design, execution and later inscription evidence.
10. **Mobile layout:** added one isolated max-width-600px CSS rule to allow both chronology tables to scroll horizontally within their containers instead of creating page-level overflow at 320px.

### Source-critical distinctions that were already handled well

- The 1615 Mewar settlement is differentiated from forced dynastic marriage or the complete destruction of Sisodiya autonomy.
- Malik Ambar’s resistance is treated as substantive rather than as a legend overcome through an allegorical image.
- Nur Jahan’s influence is treated without assigning all court decisions to a single conspiratorial faction.
- Shah Jahan’s campaigns distinguish the 1636 political settlement from the much later annexations under Aurangzeb.
- Claims about Taj construction costs, large labor-force totals and the supposed black-marble twin are explicitly qualified.
- The Aurangzeb–Murad understanding in 1657 is historically supported; religious caricatures do not determine the explanation of the succession struggle.

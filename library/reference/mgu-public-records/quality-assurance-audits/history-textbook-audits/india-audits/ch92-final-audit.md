## 3. Historical accuracy and publication-sensitive corrections

1. Reordered the chronology: **Panchsheel agreement, 29 April 1954**, precedes **Bandung, April 1955**, which precedes **Belgrade, September 1961**. No conference is misidentified as the inaugural NAM summit.
2. Separated contested sovereignty claims concerning **Aksai Chin** and the **McMahon Line** from de facto control. The **21 November 1962 announcement** is distinguished from its effectiveness on **22 November**; withdrawal and a permanent border settlement are not conflated.
3. Identified **UN Security Council Resolution 211, 20 September 1965**, as requiring a ceasefire effective **22 September at 0700 GMT**; distinguished the **10 January 1966 Tashkent Declaration** as a later withdrawal/normalization agreement, not the initial ceasefire.
4. Applied separate 1970 election denominators: **Awami League 160/162 directly elected eastern seats; seven reserved eastern women's seats; 167/169 full eastern seats; PPP 81/138 western general seats**. The comparison table and quantitative exercise reinforce the distinction.
5. Preserved March–April 1971 institutional sequence: **1 March** postponement, **7 March** Mujib speech, **25–26 March** crackdown and independence declarations, **6 April** Blood Telegram, **10 April** Proclamation, **17 April** provisional-government oath.
6. Described 1971 repression and mass atrocities without implying that disagreements over legal nomenclature negate the documented violence; categorized legal genocide analysis separately from mortality estimation.
7. Distinguished **roughly ten million refugees** from deaths in the war, and differentiated Bangladesh's official **three million** death figure from the **2025 study's 562,915 estimated excess deaths among refugees only**.
8. Distinguished the **3 December** expansion to full interstate war from the prior nine months of liberation struggle; separated **16 December Eastern Command surrender** from later western-front ceasefire and diplomatic settlement.
9. Described the **9 August 1971 Indo-Soviet treaty** as providing consultation and cooperation, not a simple automatic military-defense guarantee.

## 3. Historical accuracy corrections

1. **Surendranath Banerjea (§76.2):** Distinguished the successful age-eligibility appeal enabling his civil-service entry from his **1873 dismissal** for an alleged administrative irregularity. Verified against Oxford Dictionary of National Biography, [Banerjea profile](https://academic.oup.com/oxford-dictionary-of-national-biography/reference/63486/reference-article-abstract/579522424).
2. **Indian Civil Service age restriction (§§76.3, 76.14 and chronology):** Distinguished the **1876 decision** from the **1878 examination arrangements**. This is stated in the two sections and chronology; no implication remains that the initial decision was made in 1878.
3. **Romesh Chunder Dutt (§76.11 and register):** The 1902 original was titled *The Economic History of British India*; the 1904 companion, *India in the Victorian Age: An Economic History of the People*. Later collected editions use alternate two-volume subtitles. Verification: [Azim Premji University](https://azimpremjiuniversity.edu.in/indian-economists/romesh-chunder-dutt), [Open Library bibliographic note](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL32095506M/The_economic_history_of_India), [1904 book catalog](https://books.google.com/books/about/India_in_the_Victorian_Age.html?id=RXY7AAAAMAAJ).
4. **Cotton tariffs and excise (§76.12):** Separated the **1894** import-duty/yarn-excise arrangements from **1896** changes in the tariff and cotton-piece-goods excise. Verification: [Oxford economic-history appendix](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3Aaa6a6a91-c30b-45ac-bcd1-66b0151054de/files/sv405sb299) and [Hansard, May 18, 1896](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1896/may/18/indian-cotton-duties).
5. **Congress chronology:** Original opening and official material retain December 28, 1885, the first Bombay session, W. C. Bonnerjee, and the conventional 72 delegate count; the expansion identifies the 1888 Allahabad session's reported 1,248 delegates and acknowledges slight 1886 retrospective enumeration discrepancies. Verification: [Indian National Congress timeline](https://inc.in/brief-history-of-congress/1885-1895), [INC session synopsis](https://inc.in/congress-sandesh/archives/synopsis-of-plenary-sessions-from-1885-to-2018).
6. **Indian Councils Act, 1892 (§76.13):** Distinguished council enlargement, recommended nominees, and restricted questions/budget discussions from direct popular election or responsible government. Verified against the original [Indian Councils Act, 1892](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/55-56/14/enacted).
7. **Commons vote vs implementation (§§76.14, 76.16):** Distinguished the June 2, 1893 Commons resolution from the government's refusal to implement simultaneous competitive examinations, including the 1894 ministerial explanation. Verification: [June 2, 1893 debate and division](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1893/jun/02/civil-service-of-india-examination) and [May 29, 1894 Hansard](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1894-05-29/debates/375213c7-260a-4a5b-b968-4462caf2d3e4/ExaminationsForTheIndianCivilService).
8. **Women at Congress (§76.17):** Distinguished the female delegates documented from 1889 from Kadambini Ganguly's prominent address in 1890, avoiding inflated or uncertain numerical totals.

## 4. Documentary and scholarly source standards

The five original source-study windows remain **editorial paraphrases and comparative source analyses**, expressly not primary-source quotations. Their topics are (1) 1885 Congress resolutions; (2) Bonnerjee and Tyabji addresses; (3) Naoroji's 1901 economic text; (4) the 1892 Councils Act; and (5) the June 2, 1893 Commons resolution. Each window now has an additional explanatory document locator and limitation notice. The source register is expanded to **15 entries**, with clickable stable records for several official and catalog resources.

The chapter's evidence-based interpretation emphasizes pre-Congress associational life, political method, the difference between claims and implementation, competing political loyalties, social exclusion, and the evolving historiography. No edited passage is presented as a directly collated verbatim quotation. Competing historical interpretations, including the 'safety-valve' theory, are treated as interpretations requiring evidence.

## 6. Limits to publication approval

- **No exhaustive original-issue or manuscript-facsimile collation.** Five source windows are attributed analytical paraphrases and are not transcriptions checked against the exact original pages or speech manuscripts. Refer to printed primary editions for original wording.
- **No complete line-by-line verification of every historical statement.** Key dates, controversies, legislation, and bibliographic titles were selectively checked through identified authoritative sources; deeper archival and local-language research remains possible for future editions.
- **No live-site deployment verification.** Browser checks validated the standalone local file in the documented environment, not its hosting path, server configuration, deployed CSS, or link environment.
- **Template parity:** The complete original embedded Chapter 76 stylesheet and JavaScript were retained with an appended responsive/design extension, but no independent byte-for-byte comparison with an external canonical Chapter 2 template was performed.
- **Maps are text-guided:** Geography exercises require an identified historical atlas; this edition does not include embedded archival map plates.

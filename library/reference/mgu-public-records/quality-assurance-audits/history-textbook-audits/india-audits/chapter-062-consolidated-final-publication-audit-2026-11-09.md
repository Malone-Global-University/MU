## 4. Historical correction register

1. **Volume assignment:** Retained **Volume IV** consistently. The Chapter 62 earlier brief's Volume III labeling is superseded by the master six-volume architecture.
2. **Black Hole of Calcutta (§62.3):** Holwell's claim of 146 imprisoned and 123 dead is attributed as a contested claim, **not endorsed as an established count**. A more careful sentence recognizes that the number involved, deaths, detailed reconstruction, and responsibility remain disputed; it does not assert that an order from Siraj to kill the prisoners is documented. Relevant studies include Brijen K. Gupta, “The Black Hole Incident,” *Journal of Asian Studies*, and Partha Chatterjee, *The Black Hole of Empire* (2012), ch. 1.
3. **Plassey troop estimates (§62.6):** The draft's roughly 3,000 Company personnel (roughly 800 Europeans) and approximately 35,000–50,000+ nawabi forces are within ranges appearing in historical literature and museum accounts, but numbers for the unwieldy Bengal forces and casualties are not treated as exact audited muster returns. The role of Mir Jafar's nonparticipation and the loyalty of Mir Madan/Mohan Lal remains central.
4. **Siraj's death (§62.7):** Replaced an indefinitely dated early-July formula with the commonly accepted **2 July 1757**, accompanied by an explicit qualification about differing event sequences in later narratives. The date is supported by Partha Chatterjee's documented reconstruction and independent historical accounts; his capture and death should not be conflated with the 23 June battle or Mir Jafar's installation on 29 June.
5. **Conspiracy and financial institutions (§§62.5, 62.7–62.9):** Expanded differentiated motives of Mir Jafar, Rai Durlabh, the Jagat Seth bankers, and Armenian mediators; strengthened the distinction among corporate indemnities, personal gifts, prize distributions, and transfers of revenue rights. No claim that these actors formed a unified nationalist or religious bloc.
6. **Clive's receipts (§62.8):** The historical estimate of about **£234,000** in presents following Plassey is externally corroborated by English Heritage and contemporary parliamentary debate. It is labeled a contemporaneous/reconstructed nominal sum and **not** translated into an invented present-day equivalent.
7. **Mir Qasim (§§62.9–62.12):** Added analysis of the fiscal costs of concessions in Burdwan, Midnapore, and Chittagong; strengthened the Munger arsenal and army's logistical basis, and the conflict of revenue jurisdiction created by indiscriminate use of duty-free trade passes. The 1763 abolition of internal customs is interpreted as a defense of jurisdiction rather than modern free-trade ideology.
8. **Patna killings and military atrocities (§62.12):** Retained condemnation of the deliberate killing of captives while avoiding unsupported precision about all prisoners killed and recognizing the partisan uses of contemporary Company accounts. The Company's treatment of its own mutineers is treated independently.
9. **Buxar (§62.13):** General narratives regularly use **22 October 1764**; the British Library's catalogue of Alexander Champion's near-contemporary copied journal, plan, and casualty returns uses **23 October 1764**. The chapter transparently gives **22–23 October** and notes the provenance, rather than incorrectly claiming all traditions agree. The Company force and the allied army figures are explicitly estimates, not audited muster totals. No false precision is added to casualty comparisons.
10. **Allahabad settlement (§62.15):** Kept separate the **Diwani farman of 12 August** and the **Shuja-ud-Daula treaty of 16 August 1765**. **Corrected a material treaty error:** the English occupation of **Chunar** was tied by Article VII to completion of the stipulated indemnity, **not an unconditional permanent cession**. Article VI set the indemnity at **50 lakh rupees** in instalments; the separate placement of Allahabad and Kora under the emperor is retained. The treaty is identifiable in the British Library as Mss Eur G49 and in an eighteenth-century published transcription.
11. **Three different fiscal obligations (§§62.15–62.16):** Distinguished (a) Awadh's **50-lakh** war indemnity, (b) the emperor's **26-lakh annual** payment associated with the Diwani, and (c) a **separate** nawab's nizamat allowance of **53,86,131 sicca rupees and nine annas** set out in the 1765 agreement, with suballocations for household and establishment costs. These were not the same payment, and all should not be represented as conditions of a single treaty.
12. **Dual government (§62.17):** Distinguished the Court of Directors' **29 August 1771** decision that the Company should “stand forth as diwan” from the **1772 implementation** under Warren Hastings. The policy ended an indirect system that retained Indian fiscal and judicial administration under substantial Company supervision. Direct Company rule should not be projected backward to 1757.
13. **Social and economic effects (§62.18):** Expanded the distinct experiences of zamindars, bankers, weavers, traders, fiscal officials, and cultivators, acknowledging that revenue assessments differed from actual collections. Introduced the pressures around the 1769–1770 famine only as a bridge to Chapter 63, where the famine and its contested mortality and causes receive full treatment.
14. **Historiography (§62.19):** Preserved qualified reference to P. J. Marshall, C. A. Bayly, Sushil Chaudhury, and Sudipta Sen, distinguishing emphasis on fiscal networks, private interests, indigenous political agency, legal transformation, and the contingent emergence of empire; did not invent single-page scholarly quotations.
15. **Cross-volume positioning:** The chapter opens Volume IV, bridges directly from Chapters 60 and 61, reserves later famine/reform/courts/permanent settlement for Chapter 63 and later military conquest for Chapter 64.

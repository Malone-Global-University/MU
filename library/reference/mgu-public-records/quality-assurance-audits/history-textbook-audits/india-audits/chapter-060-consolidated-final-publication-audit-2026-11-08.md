## 3. Historical accuracy — corrections made directly

1. **Bengal diwan appointment:** The year of Murshid Quli Khan's appointment varies between 1700 and 1701 by source. Opening chronology and §60.3 now use **c. 1700–1701** rather than presenting one year as uncontested. Earlier titles, including **Murshid Quli Khan in December 1702**, were clarified.
2. **Makhsusabad/Murshidabad:** The previous language implied that Makhsusabad was created by the revenue-office relocation. Corrected: an existing town, Makhsusabad, was **later renamed Murshidabad**; the revenue establishment transferred in stages around 1703–04, rather than the full political capital instantaneously moving at that point.
3. **Bengal deputy vs governor:** **1713** is the deputy governorship; the full Bengal governorship is generally dated **1717**. The section now distinguishes these offices and notes prior interruptions and Odisha responsibilities.
4. **Jagat Seth title:** Fateh Chand's Mughal imperial title **Jagat Seth** was conferred in **1723**, distinct from Manik Chand's earlier **Nagar Seth** title. The date no longer remains merely “in the 1720s.”
5. **Shuja-ud-Daula's wazirate:** Formal investiture by Shah Alam II was **15 February 1762**. Added this to §60.8 rather than leaving the event undated.
6. **Awadh Begums:** Clearly identifies **Sadr-un-Nissa Begum (Nawab Begum)** as Saadat Khan's daughter, Safdar Jang's wife and **Shuja-ud-Daula's mother**; identifies **Bahu Begum** as Shuja-ud-Daula's chief consort and Asaf-ud-Daula's mother. Updated main prose and reference apparatus.
7. **Masulipatam:** Colonel Francis Forde's assault and capture occurred in **April 1759**, followed by a **May 1759** arrangement with Salabat Jang; the chronology and diplomatic terms are distinguished from later Northern Circars settlements.
8. **Udgir:** Explicit **February 1760** campaign and fighting near Tandulja, with Maratha victory under Sadashiv Rao Bhau and major treaty cessions. Avoids rigid unverified exact troop or revenue figures.
9. **Asaf Jahi capital:** The transfer from Aurangabad to Hyderabad is qualified as a **process in the early 1760s**, conventionally dated around **1763**, rather than a single instantaneous relocation.
10. **Opening numismatic claim:** Removed unsupported blanket assertion that rupees were contemporaneously minted at both Faizabad and Lucknow throughout the earlier eighteenth century. Coinage example now appropriately focuses on **Murshidabad and other mints**; later Awadh coinage uses complicated mint names and dates.
11. **Imperial ceremonial continuity:** Nuanced the degree and dates to which each successor regime retained Mughal offices, titles and appointments; avoids implying identical practices across every state.
12. **Political-economy narrative:** Added direct analysis of revenue assessment versus collection and fiscal risk (§60.13); banking credit, exchange and transaction records (§60.14); artisan advances and logistics (§60.15); and the unequal distribution of requisition/warfare effects (§60.16).

Other key chronologies checked without material alteration: Murshid Quli Khan's death in 1727; Bengal succession through Alivardi Khan, 1740–56; Saadat Khan appointment in 1722; Safdar Jang's wazirate from 1748; Asaf Jah's victory over Mubariz Khan at Shakar Kheda in 1724; the 1748–51 Hyderabad succession; the 1764 Buxar battle and 1765 Allahabad settlement; the shift of Awadh's court toward Lucknow after 1775. Dates stated as estimates or disputed are not converted into invented certainties.

## 4. Five evidence windows — final source status

**1. `Riyaz-us-Salatin` (Ghulam Husain Salim):** Work completed in **1788** under the patronage of George Udney, recounting early Bengal history retrospectively. The window is correctly marked **editorial paraphrase**, not verbatim extract or contemporary Murshid Quli Khan testimony. Relevant to provincial political memory. Original-language printed page/facsimile not collated.

**2. `Siyar al-Muta'akhkhirin` (Ghulam Husain Khan Tabatabai):** Retrospective Persian account from the early 1780s; useful for Awadh/Delhi elite political perceptions but not an eyewitness record of all earlier events. Editorial paraphrase, without original-language collation.

**3. `Muntakhab al-Lubab` (Khafi Khan):** Contemporary Mughal-service historical account relevant to the Deccan and Asaf Jah. General editorial paraphrase, not tied to a collated exact passage. Correctly identified as an edition limitation; *do not* market as a verified source extract.

**4. Bengal Company consultations, 15 November 1742:** Replaced the generic claim with a **specific, dated financial record** reporting interest notes associated with Jagat Seth Fateh Chand and Seth Anand Chand, reproduced in J. H. Little, “The House of Jagatseth,” Part II, *Bengal: Past & Present*, XXII (1921), pp. 2–3. The published extract includes entries recording 100,000-rupee principal and interest at 9% per annum as well as exchange adjustments. **Evidence inspected through a searchable transcription, not the original physical manuscript consultation.** Window is explicitly a documented editorial paraphrase with remaining collation requirements, not a purported word-for-word primary transcript.

**5. Museum coin, Murshidabad mint:** Replaced generic coins with **Fitzwilliam Museum (Cambridge) object 257385, accession CM.645-2002**, a silver rupee, 11.53 g, naming **Muhammad Shah**, with **regnal year 6**, conventionally c. 1724–1725. The museum's online record lists “production date AD 1719” despite the sixth regnal year: the discrepancy is explicitly disclosed and not silently reconciled. Physical numismatic inspection and full Persian legend transcription have **not** been performed. The specimen is used as evidence for imperial naming conventions, not as proof that the emperor controlled the mint.

### Source-window publication limitation

All five windows are **critical editorial paraphrases or documentary/object descriptions**, with appropriate historiographic questioning. They are suitable for a first-edition scholarly textual educational chapter when labeled as such. They are **not** substitutes for a manuscript-collated critical source anthology. Direct archival/facsimile collation is reserved for a later enhanced source edition; do not mislabel the existing paraphrases as verbatim translations.

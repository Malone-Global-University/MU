## Historical and source corrections applied directly

1. **§56.4 — Dars-i Nizami chronology (material).** Replaced an assertion that the Dars-i Nizami was already an established late-seventeenth-century curriculum. Distinguished late-Mughal Farangi Mahall patronage, Mulla Nizam al-Din (1677–1748) and his primarily eighteenth-century work, and later nineteenth-century standardization. Source: Muhammad Qasim Zaman, p. 303.
2. **§56.4 — Astronomical chronology and chapter boundary.** Reframed Sawai Jai Singh II's observatories as an eighteenth-century development grounded in older traditions, not as a subject covered by Chapter 57's political chapter.
3. **§56.4 — *Lilavati* and *Bijaganita*.** Specified the dates 1587 and 1634–35, and identified Ata'ullah Rushdi/Rashidi for the latter. Source: Ansari, conference abstracts, p. 6.
4. **§56.4 — Medical compendium.** Removed an unsupported completion date "around 1700" for the *Tibb-i Aurangzebi*; provided its alternate title *Tibb-i Aurangshahi* and attribution to Darvish Muhammad, while declining to invent an exact date. Source: Encyclopaedia Iranica.
5. **§56.15 — Jain teachers and Akbar.** Distinguished Hiravijaya Suri's invitation in 1582 from arrival in 1583, and Jinachandra Suri's invitation in 1591 from arrival at Lahore in 1592.
6. **§56.6 — Mansur's zebra.** Replaced "about 1621" with March 1621 and added the surviving imperial attribution to Mansur, supported by the V&A object description.
7. **§56.17 — Gulbadan Begum.** Changed an overprecise 1576 pilgrimage departure to 1575–76, acknowledging differing chronicle dates.
8. **§56.17 — Female authorship.** Preserved the cautious discussion of Zeb-un-Nissa's contested *Diwan-i Makhfi*, and explicitly documented that Salima Sultan Begum, Nur Jahan, and Zeb-un-Nissa appear in later attribution traditions under the same pen name. The chapter does not assign unattributed poems uncritically.
9. **§56.18 — Rajput painting dates.** Distinguished seventeenth-century Kishangarh courtly painting from its better-known mature eighteenth-century school, preventing unmarked importation of later art into the main period.
10. **§56.19 — Persian under Company rule.** Replaced a simplified empire-wide replacement claim with the limited legal meaning of Act XXIX of 1837 and regional variability.
11. **Chronology Preview — Aurangzeb.** Corrected the reign from 1659–1707 to **1658–1707** (1659 may appear in accounts of a formal coronation but is not the standard regnal beginning).
12. **Akbarnama source window.** Clarified Prince Salim's involvement in the ambush that killed Abu'l Fazl in 1602, as attested by Jahangir's memoir.
13. **Historical Debate 1.** Rephrased the attribution to Audrey Truschke: her research demonstrates intensive collaboration between Sanskrit scholars and Persian-speaking Mughal elites, so it should not be treated as unambiguously opposing court-level exchange.
14. **Mobile CSS.** Restored the narrow-screen chronology-table scrolling rule used in the locked Chapter 55 HTML; this removed actual overflow on 390px and 320px screens without modifying the shared main CSS/JS or the chapter's content architecture.

## Independently checked historical points retained

- Abu'l Fazl's political role and August 1602 death at the instigation of the future Jahangir — substantiated.
- Ahmad Sirhindi's imprisonment under Jahangir in 1619, with release around the following year — supported by specialist historical analysis; avoid asserting a wholly uncontroversial interpretation of its theological causes.
- Mansur's zebra painting and March 1621 court arrival — substantiated by museum-held object and inscription.
- Two Persian renderings of Bhaskara's mathematics within Mughal chronology — substantiated, and dates made precise.
- Hiravijaya and Jinachandra interactions with Akbar — substantiated; differentiated invitation and arrival dates.
- Salima Sultan Begum's "Makhfi" attribution — preserved as an attribution, not as proof of authorship; competing uses of the sobriquet documented.
- *Tibb-i Aurangshahi* by Darvish Muhammad — attested but no reliable specific "1700" composition date established in consulted source, therefore removed.
- Historical debate authors — Kinra, Alam, Busch and Truschke checked against publishers' descriptions; regional-versus-imperial framing explicitly recognizes overlap, not a false consensus or exclusive dichotomy. Tara Chand and Mohammad Mujeeb treated as earlier composite-culture interpretive lineage; historian-specific claims about Ebba Koch are broadly aligned with her architectural work, though not every argument has been checked against each full monograph.

## Source windows: status and evidentiary limitations

**Five windows retained:** Abu'l Fazl's *Akbarnama*; Jahangir's *Jahangirnama*; the *Razmnama* and Badauni account; Gulbadan Begum's *Humayun-nama*; Dara Shikoh's *Majma' al-Bahrayn*. Each is explicitly labeled **"Editorial paraphrase (not a verbatim quotation)."** They are interpretive descriptions of source content, not verified contiguous excerpts and not substitutes for a page-collated, critical translation. Source metadata, provenance, student inquiries and methodological cautions were reviewed for consistency. Specific manuscript or translation page references are not provided for every paraphrase. That limitation must remain disclosed if these are retained under the heading "Primary Source Window." No language is falsely represented as a verbatim historical quotation.

**Map limitation:** Two Geography Studies are textual instructions for students using an external period-appropriate historical atlas. There are no embedded map plates. The chapter should not be advertised as illustrated-cartographic publication until maps are separately supplied and checked.

**Back matter:** Chapter-specific educational review apparatus is complete. Volume glossary, cumulative bibliography, indexes, and series-wide back matter are not part of this standalone chapter's publication gate.


### Research and source-verification register

References below were consulted for targeted claims, attribution controls, comparative historiography, or the corrective historical chronology. The list is not a claim of having collated every page of every early modern manuscript.

1. **Abu'l Fazl's assassination and political context.** Encyclopaedia Iranica, “ʿAllāmī, Abu'l-Fażl”: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/%CA%BFallami-abul-fazl/ . Supports the August 1602 murder arranged by Prince Salim; the chapter prudently omits a contested day-of-month in modern conversion.
2. **Mughal painting, named artists and zebra.** Victoria and Albert Museum, “The Arts of the Mughal Empire”: https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/the-arts-of-the-mughal-empire . The work of Mansur and Jahangir's own inscription support the March 1621 zebra date and attribution. Compare National Council of Educational Research and Training painting chapter hosted at IIT Kanpur: https://sathee.iitk.ac.in/pa/sathee-ssc/student-corner/ncert-books/class-12/an-introduction-to-indian-art/chapter-03-the-mughal-school-of-miniature-painting/ .
3. **Persian *Bijaganita* and *Lilavati*.** S. M. Razaullah Ansari, “Persian Translations of Bhaskara's Sanskrit Texts during the 16th–17th Centuries,” conference abstracts, p. 6: https://www.vpmthane.org/bhaskara900/abstracts/Abstracts_Bhaskara_04062014.pdf . Gives Faizi's *Lilavati* in 1587 and Ata'ullah Rushdi/Rashidi's *Bijaganita* for Shah Jahan in 1634–35.
4. **Aurangzeb-era medicine.** Encyclopaedia Iranica, “India XXXIII. Indo-Muslim Physicians”: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/india-xxxiii-indo-muslim-physicians/ . Identifies Darvish Muhammad as author of *Tibb-i Aurangshahi / Tibb-i Aurangzebi*; does not establish 1700 as its date.
5. **Dars-i Nizami formation and historicity.** Muhammad Qasim Zaman, “Religious Education and the Rhetoric of Reform,” *Comparative Studies in Society and History* 41.2 (1999), p. 303: https://www.ilmgate.org/wp-content/uploads/2011/02/Religious-Education-and-the-Rhetoric-of-Reform-The-Madrasa-in-British-India-and-Pakistan.pdf . The curriculum's association with Mulla Nizam al-Din (d. 1748) is later and its stabilization belongs chiefly to the nineteenth century.
6. **Jain scholars at Akbar's court.** “Interaction with 'The Lords': The Jain Community and the Mughal Royalty under Akbar,” *Social Scientist* 40.3–4 (2012): https://www.academia.edu/1793319/_Interaction_with_The_Lords_The_Jain_Community_and_the_Mughal_Royalty_under_Akbar_in_Social_Scientist_Vol_40_No_3_4_March_April_2012 . Dates Hiravijaya's arrival at Fatehpur Sikri to June 1583. For Jinachandra invited in 1591 and arriving in Lahore in February 1592, compare Jain biographical study: https://jainqq.org/booktext/bhanuchandragani_charit_Romanized/002310 . Chronologies in some later summaries are less exact, so the editorial distinction between invitation and arrival is retained.
7. **Female authorship and the pen name Makhfi.** Sunil Sharma, “Tazkirahs of Premodern Women Poets,” *Encyclopaedia Iranica* poetry project: https://poets.iranicaonline.org/article/tazkirahs-of-premodern-women-poets-2/ . Notes the potentially competing attributions to Nur Jahan, Salima Sultan Begum, and Zeb-un-Nissa. Also *Persian Literature in Translation*, “Zīb al-Nisāʾ”: https://persian.packhum.org/author/0224 .
8. **Gulbadan Begum's pilgrimage chronology.** Ruby Lal, “The Mughal Princess Who Led a Pilgrimage to Mecca,” Yale University Press (2025): https://yalebooks.yale.edu/2025/03/05/the-mughal-princess-who-led-a-pilgrimage-to-mecca/ . The Akbarnama's departure dates are not uniform; "1575–76" is a more defensible summary than a fixed departure in 1576.
9. **Kishangarh painting's chronology.** Getty Research Institute, Art & Architecture Thesaurus, “Kishangarh”: https://www.getty.edu/vow/AATFullDisplay?subjectid=300019036 . Distinctive mature school is eighteenth-century; earlier court art may be mentioned as seventeenth-century background. Compare NCERT's *Rajasthani Schools of Painting*: https://philoid.com/ncert/chapter/lefa102 .
10. **Persian and British Company courts.** *Language of Judicial and Revenue Proceedings*, Act XXIX of 1837, Government of India: https://www.indiacode.nic.in/repealedfileopen?rfilename=A1837-29.pdf . Authorizes, rather than instantly implements uniformly, replacement of Persian in relevant Bengal-code proceedings from December 1837.
11. **Imperial and regional historiography.** Muzaffar Alam, *The Languages of Political Islam: India 1200–1800*, University of Chicago Press: https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/L/bo3617888.html ; Richard Eaton, *India in the Persianate Age* (general scholarly framing); Rajeev Kinra, *Writing Self, Writing Empire*, University of California Press (2015): https://www.ucpress.edu/book/9780520286467/writing-self-writing-empire . Kinra's work securely grounds Chandar Bhan's Indo-Persian career.
12. **Regional and multilingual literary culture.** Allison Busch, *Poetry of Kings*, Oxford University Press (2011): https://academic.oup.com/book/6576 . The scholarly profile supports the importance of Braj literary production and regionally distributed patronage.
13. **Sanskrit intellectuals and Mughal state-making.** Audrey Truschke, *Culture of Encounters*, Columbia University Press (2016): https://cup.columbia.edu/book/culture-of-encounters/9780231173636/ . Corrects the draft's tendency to place Truschke wholly on a non-court side of an artificial debate: her book demonstrates substantive cross-language cooperation at the court.
14. **Court ideology and historically conditioned 'composite' interpretation.** The early modern interpretation in the chapter is a survey of several scholarly traditions; the two debate boxes do not purport to quote the named historians verbatim. Tara Chand's position is illustrated in Government of India historical commentary: https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/erelcontent.aspx?lang=2&reg=48&relid=79297 ; Ebba Koch and Sheldon Pollock are relevant frameworks rather than sources for unique dates or statistics. The term "composite culture" is explicitly treated as a contestable explanatory category, not a verified single historical self-description.
15. **Mughal visual arts and chronology context.** Victoria & Albert Museum and UNESCO’s *History of Civilizations of Central Asia*, volume V, chapter on Mughal arts of the book: https://en.unesco.org/silkroad/sites/default/files/knowledge-bank-article/vol_V%20silk%20road_arts%20of%20the%20book%2C%20painting%20and%20calligraphy%20BIS.pdf . These support broader painting context, while attribution specifics rely more directly on the V&A object description.

### 3. Material historical and documentary corrections made directly

1. **Shivaji's birth (58.2):** February 1630 is presented as the chronology supported by the Jedhe tradition and followed by much modern research, while 1627 is retained as an alternative older/variant chronology. The draft had characterized 1630 only as a traditional date and risked inverting their evidentiary status.
2. **Sabhasad Bakhar source window (58.2):** Prachi Deshpande dates composition approximately **1694**; some authors speak of completion around **1697**. The final identifies both rather than stating unqualified “commonly dated 1697.” Royal patronage by Rajaram and its retrospective/legitimating character are preserved.
3. **Pavan Khind (58.4):** the celebrated Baji Prabhu episode is now explicitly introduced as a historical account with later embellishment, rather than every detail being asserted as an undisputed observation.
4. **Coastal defenses (58.3):** Shivaji's Sindhudurg-period naval program is separated from works at **Kolaba around 1680**, which continued under successors. The draft misleadingly grouped the full Kolaba fortification with the 1660s.
5. **Tarabai's regency (58.8):** the final rejects reduction of her effective royal administration to merely symbolic authority. Command, appointments, fiscal claims and negotiations were real political activities, even as sardars had substantial leverage.
6. **Shahu and Satara (58.9):** Shahu's active recognition of appointments and influence over factions is distinguished from the later concentration of executive authority in Pune. Describing his kingship as already purely ceremonial by 1713 was too strong.
7. **Chauth and sardeshmukhi (58.6 and 58.13):** Shivaji-era external contributions are distinguished from the expanded *combined* eighteenth-century rights recognized in imperial grants. Sardeshmukhi is not characterized as automatically less controversial, and nominal assessments are not confused with receipts.
8. **1719 arrangements (58.10; Source Window 3):** formal grants concerning six Deccan subahs, revenue rights and swarajya are analyzed as conditioned documents rather than evidence that revenue was actually collected uniformly. They are documentary summaries, **not verbatim translated farmans**.
9. **Gaekwad/Dabhade (58.14):** a simplistic succession-by-defeat sentence was replaced with the more complex interaction among the Dabhade senapati household, Peshwas, Gaekwads and local authorities.
10. **1739 invasion context (58.11):** more carefully distinguishes Maratha consideration of a response from engagement with Nadir Shah's invading main army.
11. **1752 Safdar Jang agreement (58.12, 58.16):** the Maratha defensive treaty was negotiated **12 April 1752**, but the emperor **declined to ratify** it after separate negotiations involving Ahmad Shah Durrani. Proposed fiscal and office concessions therefore must not be presented as an unequivocal operative imperial grant.
12. **Peshwa Daftar Source Window 4 (58.12):** the hypothetical composite letter is removed and replaced with a verifiable **3 February 1760** communication from **Malhar Rao Holkar** to the Peshwa, preserved in G. S. Sardesai (ed.), *Selections from the Peshwa Daftar*, vol. II, **document 117** (1930). The new source analysis is tied to the document's actual reported subject and explicitly discloses that the original Marathi manuscript has not been facsimile-collated.
13. **Panipat source window (58.18):** Kashi Raj's eyewitness-related narrative is distinguished from the date of its later composition, **commonly c. 1780**; Jadunath Sarkar's corrected 1934 translation is added alongside the earlier Browne translation. A questionable calendrical festival assertion was removed.
14. **Madhavrao–Nizam chronology (58.19):** the sack of Pune is corrected to **April–May 1763**, distinct from the factional crisis beginning in 1762. Rakshasbhuvan remains correctly dated August 1763.

Other examined chronology: Shivaji's coronation (6 June 1674), Battle of Khed (12 October 1707), 1713 Peshwa appointment, Baji Rao I's tenure, Panipat (14 January 1761), and Shah Alam II's restoration to Delhi (January 1772) are retained. Battle strengths and casualty figures are deliberately treated as uncertain rather than given spurious precision.

### 4. Substantive expansion scope

The revision adds analytical narrative across seventeen sections: **58.4–58.20** (none to 58.1–58.3), with emphasis on the chronologically and institutionally compressed core. Expansion connects military events to actual administration, revenue collection, local officeholders, royal legitimacy, source bias, nonelite experience, Peshwa–sardar relations, and the logistical/diplomatic causes of Panipat. Newly added narrative is integrated as section-level paragraphs; the count excludes all feature-box material. It does not add a twenty-first numbered section or a second narrative ending.

Especially strengthened: Shivaji's administrative follow-through after treaties (§58.4); limits of coronation legitimacy (§58.5); cabinet-vs-royal-government distinction (§58.6); political meaning of Jinji (§58.7); Tarabai's effective regency (§58.8); Shahu's active political negotiation (§58.9); fiscal-political significance of 1719 (§58.10); costs of campaigns, local intermediaries and the Peshwa's networks (§§58.11–58.16); coalition and supply problems (§58.17); source-critical battle reconstruction (§58.18); limits and achievements of post-1761 recovery (§58.19); and institutional continuity in the final synthesis (§58.20).

### 7. Limits transparently carried into this first edition

1. **Primary-source status:** All five windows identify source material, but none claims to contain a verbatim manuscript-collated passage. Four are explicitly labeled *editorial paraphrases or documentary summaries*, and the fifth is a dated-letter summary grounded in the cited Marathi transcription. The 1719 imperial grants, coronation eyewitness report and Sabhasad Bakhar were not collated against original archival manuscript images for a line-by-line translation. These are **teaching windows for source criticism**, not a critical edition of the underlying manuscripts.
2. **Map/atlas status:** Both geography features are text-guided exercises; there are no embedded, fully sourced historical map plates. Students require a suitable dated atlas.
3. **Secondary-source limits:** Some older gazetteers and historical editions incorporate colonial or nationalist assumptions. Where they are used, they are checked against modern scholarship where feasible and not treated as single-source proof of contested ideological interpretations.
4. **Live-site status:** The HTML was tested standalone in Chromium, not deployed to the production server or validated against a website's shared global navigation. Volume-level back matter and any separately maintained master glossary remain outside this file.
5. **Coverage:** The independent source audit concentrated on named dates, documentary grants, campaign chronology, significant source attributions and major interpretive arguments; it does **not** certify exhaustive original-language corroboration of every sentence.

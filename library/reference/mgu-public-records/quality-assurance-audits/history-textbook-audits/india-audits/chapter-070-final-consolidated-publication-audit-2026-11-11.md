## C. Applied factual and documentary corrections

1. **Kunwar Singh:** corrected to **26 April 1858**, after the fighting near Jagdishpur on 23 April; updated the chronology preview, §70.9, review list, and analysis. Source: Government of Bihar, Bhojpur District, [Jagdishpur historical entry](https://bhojpur.nic.in/tourist-place/veer-kunwar-singh-jayanti/).
2. **Formal peace:** replaced the erroneous 8 July **1858** with 8 July **1859**. Corrected the chronology, §70.9, timeline interpretation and chronology question, and eliminated the incorrect argument that Canning had proclaimed peace in 1858 while major rebellion still continued. Sources: [A Brief History of the Indian Peoples](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:A_Brief_History_of_the_Indian_Peoples.djvu/234), [Indiana University collection description](https://archives.iu.edu/catalog/InU-Li-VAE4258). This is a historical published date, not a claim of independent facsimile collation of the proclamation.
3. **Charles Dickens:** replaced the weak description of the 4 October 1857 letter with an accurately qualified paraphrase of collective, racial extermination rhetoric; no quotation fabricated. Scholarship: [Depicting Conflict in India in 1857–8](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14780038.2017.1329129).
4. **Violence:** removed unsupported assertions that the number of people killed in reprisals was demonstrably greater by a precise comparative scale. Preserved documented organized retribution, civilian targeting and collective punishment, emphasizing the limits of casualty evidence.
5. **Ajnala:** clarified the connection to men of the 26th Bengal Native Infantry, deaths by execution and confinement, 2014 recovery of remains, and cautious interpretation of later forensic, genetic and commemoration studies. Source: Mark Condos, [journal research](https://doi.org/10.1080/14623528.2021.2022271).
6. **Havelock/Kanpur:** distinguished the victory against rebel forces on 16 July from occupation of Kanpur on 17 July 1857; retained the documented 24 November death date. Compare the [National Army Museum campaign chronology](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/decisive-events-indian-mutiny).
7. **Council of India:** retained the 1858 Act's fifteen-member council, initially eight Crown-appointed and seven selected by Company directors, while explaining its difference from the Governor-General's Indian council. Source: [Government of India Act 1858](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/21-22/106/pdfs/ukpga_18580106_en.pdf), §§7–8.
8. **Peel Commission:** retained the **1:2 Bengal** and **1:3 Madras/Bombay** troop ratios only in their correct context—recommendations concerning cavalry and infantry—rather than implying fixed ratios of all troops for all periods. Source: [Hansard, 26 May 1930](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1930-05-26/debates/a3e3657f-f6cb-40d6-8f22-53ec273a0a23/BritishAndIndianTroops%28Proportion%29).
9. **Victoria's Proclamation:** clarified the distinction among convicted murder participants, instigators and knowing harborers, and other rebels entitled to conditional return-to-peace amnesty before the deadline; the source window explicitly labels its language as editorial paraphrase. Compare [contemporary newspaper reproduction of the Calcutta Gazette](https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/DSC18590225.2.15).
10. **Ghalib:** identified Khwaja Ahmad Faruqi's 1970 published English translation of *Dastanbūy* as an edition for further examination; made the literary evidence's perspective and pension context explicit. Source: [bibliographic catalogue](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BD20190990?l=en).
11. **Oudh confiscation:** deepened the distinction between the proclamation's sweeping language and subsequent restoration of many estates, with reference to March 1858 Canning–Outram correspondence. Source: [British Library archival catalogue](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-003310874).

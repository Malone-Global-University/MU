## 3. Historical corrections and rationale

1. **Treaty of Mangalore, 1784.** Replaced vague March date with **11 March 1784**, independently corroborated by British Library commissioners’ journal; identified **IOR/H/684, pp.56–64**. Its final article contemplated later official endorsement/acknowledgment by multiple Company governments; do not present this as instantaneous universal ratification or a victor’s dictate.
2. **Seringapatam, 1792.** Distinguished preliminary terms in February from the **definitive treaty of 18 March 1792**; clarified **three crore thirty lakh rupees** indemnity separately from territorial cessions and hostages. Described territorial shares as apportioned by assessed revenues and strategic position rather than equal map geometries. IOR/H/568 catalogue treaty portion pp.759–808.
3. **Mysore partition, 1799–1800.** Distinguished the partition of the conquered dominions from the **8 July 1799** subsidiary treaty with the restored Wodeyar court. The Peshwa refused a proposed share in 1799; it was redistributed. Hyderabad’s 1792 and 1799 acquisitions were transferred under an October **1800** subsidiary financing settlement; do not present those later transfers as part of the initial partition.
4. **Nagpur Bhonsle succession.** Corrected collapsed genealogy: **Raghuji II died March 1816; his son Parsoji nominally succeeded; Appa Sahib initially served as regent and accepted the May 1816 subsidiary treaty; Appa Sahib succeeded after Parsoji’s suspicious death in early 1817**. Suspicions about participation in the death are not a verified criminal verdict.
5. **Holkar after 1811.** Following **Yashwantrao Holkar’s 1811 death**, the minor **Malhar Rao Holkar III** ruled under **Tulsi Bai’s** contested regency; her death at the hands of army factions shortly before Mahidpur in December 1817 is separated from the child ruler’s position. Some sources vary in numbering, so the dynastic name and context remain primary.
6. **Pindari bands and Amir Khan.** Separated major Pindari groups led by Chitu, Karim Khan and others from **Amir Khan’s Pathan military organization**, which was connected but not identical. Avoided reducing all independent mounted forces to a single category.
7. **Koregaon, 1 January 1818.** Specified **c.800–850 personnel** for the Company detachment (infantry, auxiliary horse and artillery) and distinguished the larger Peshwa host from actual attackers. Avoided an unsupported singular reason for Maratha withdrawal and retained the difference between contemporary tactical context and later Dalit commemoration.
8. **Company supremacy and aftermath.** Qualified sweeping claim that no major independent state could militarily challenge the Company by 1818; Punjab/Sikh kingdom and other independent powers continued to retain significant forces. The chapter is about predominance in the southern/western theaters, not total subcontinental conquest.
9. **Historiography.** Verified title and date of **Randolf G. S. Cooper (2003)**, whose core work concerns the **1803** campaign, not comprehensive 1817–1818 coverage. Clarified **Irfan Habib ed. (Tulika 2001; later edition 2014)**. Confirmed Roy (2011), Brittlebank (1997 as cited), Gordon (1993 as cited), Hasan (1951), and Marshall (1987) publication details in catalogues/publisher listings where available. Attribution summaries remain condensed and not page-collated.
10. **Mangalore reception.** Qualified unsourced assertion attributing a specific feeling of humiliation to Hastings; relied instead on documented institutional friction over Madras negotiations and a cautious description of Company responses.

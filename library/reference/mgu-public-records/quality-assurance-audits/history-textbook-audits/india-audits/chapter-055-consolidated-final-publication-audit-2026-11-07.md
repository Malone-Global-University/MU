## 3. Historical verification and targeted corrections

### Confirmed with explicit scholarly qualification

- **Urban share of approximately 15%:** Shireen Moosvi's *The Economy of the Mughal Empire c. 1595*, chapter 13, estimates 15%, explicitly through economic and administrative reconstruction, not a modern population census. The final chapter attributes the estimate and warns against mechanically extending it to every decade and region. See https://academic.oup.com/book/32718/chapter-abstract/272398357.
- **Jahanara and merchant shipping:** supported by maritime-history research on her vessel, conventionally spelled *Sahibi* or *Sahebi*. The statement remains bounded to her commercial interests, rather than projecting the experience of royal women onto all women. See https://mhsindia.org/all/mughal-women-in-maritime-trade-jahanara/210156/.
- **Banarasidas:** the Jain merchant's *Ardhakathanaka* (written in Agra in 1641) is a rare vernacular autobiography. Verified against Rupert Snell's scholarly study and publisher's edition record. See https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0262728005051608 and https://www.penguin.co.in/book/banarasidas-ardhakathanak/.
- **Richard Eaton's Bengal thesis:** correctly identified as an interpretation joining the eastern agrarian frontier, local religious institutions and the history of Islam, not as an uncontested explanation of conversion. Bengal-Arakan slavery and the context of the Mughal conquest of Chittagong in 1666 are supported. See https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/the-rise-of-islam-and-the-bengal-frontier-1204-1760.html.
- **Famine of 1630–1632:** the *Shah Jahan Nama* excerpt records the famine, high mortality, the emperor's orders for charity and remission; the *Peter Mundy* extracts offer a separate perspective. Their claims cannot establish verified total deaths, differential mortality percentages, or fully delivered relief. The final prose distinguishes reported official orders from demonstrated outcomes. See https://famineanddearth.exeter.ac.uk/displayhtml.html?id=fp_00383_en_shahjahannama and https://famineanddearth.exeter.ac.uk/displayhtml.html?id=fp_00060_en_travelsofpetermundy_vol01.

### Errors or overstatements corrected directly

1. **Seasonal shipping direction:** corrected the southwest/northeast monsoon reversal in §55.11; the northeast winter monsoon commonly favors westbound passages from India toward Arabia, and southwest summer monsoon favors return passages, subject to regional conditions. https://www.icwa.in/show_content.php?lang=1&level=2&lid=4033&ls_id=9578
2. **Surat factory chronology:** revised to distinguish the 1612 naval encounter, permission to trade and the 1613 English factory, and Roe's separate 1615–1618 embassy. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03086534.2024.2370638
3. **1686–1690 Anglo-Mughal conflict:** corrected the misattribution of ship seizures, distinguishing English attacks on Mughal shipping from Mughal confiscations of English factories and the siege of Bombay; the Company sought a settlement. https://academic.oup.com/hwj/article-abstract/doi/10.1093/hwj/dbx034/4082547
4. **Coromandel politics and trade:** removed the erroneous impression that all three main Coromandel ports were governed by Deccan sultanates or uniformly Mughal, and improved coverage of Telugu and Tamil commercial communities (including Balija, Komati and Chetti groups). https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/modern-asian-studies/article/abs/chief-merchants-and-the-european-enclaves-of-seventeenthcentury-coromandel/482F915AAF569BD5C7191B62962353A4
5. **Merchant-state relations:** replaced a categorical claim that the Mughal state did not systematically promote trade with a more defensible description of imperial charters, protection, transit policies and persistent local exactions.
6. **Epidemic nomenclature and mortality:** removed an overconfident disease label and a categorical gender/age mortality ranking that the sources cannot substantiate for this specific famine.
7. **Family and caste generalizations:** qualified overly uniform descriptions of joint households and social classification, distinguishing customary arrangements, regional variation and formal norms.
8. **Traveler source box:** added a historically appropriate distinction between Bernier's descriptions of workshops and courtly society and Tavernier's evidence on gems and commercial journeys; no invented quotation was introduced.
9. **Chronology:** synchronized the chronology preview with the corrected English Surat chronology and the approximate dating of the *A'in-i Akbari*.

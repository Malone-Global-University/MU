## 4. Historical and source-critical corrections

1. **Revenue systems:** Distinguished the fixed state demand of the 1793 Permanent Settlement from mutable rents paid by cultivators; clarified conditional decennial background, estate arrears, and the later tightening of revenue-sale rules. No claim of uniform landlord or tenant experience.
2. **Ryotwari:** Distinguished Alexander Read/Munro’s pre-1820 experiments from subsequent expansion. The original Claude draft cited **British Library Add MS 22080** (Financial Department) for a source window primarily about Ryotwari; this has been corrected to **Add MSS 22078–22079**, Munro’s Revenue Department minutes (1820–1827). Add MS 22080 is separately identified, not misrepresented. No fabricated individual minute.
3. **Mahalwari:** Reviewed Regulation VII of 1822, noting its interest in multiple forms of rights and the inadequacy of portraying villages as equal collective owners; later amendments to consolidated texts explicitly disclosed.
4. **Rural debt and land rights:** Distinguished registered payers, tenants, co-sharers, workers, creditors, and gendered household labor; avoided treating debt as universal poverty or credit as invariably benign.
5. **Santhal rising:** Limited to a bounded economic illustration in 1855–1856, leaving full resistance history for Chapter 68.
6. **Commodity chronology:** Distinguished existing commercial agriculture from new Company-era arrangements; described the Bengal/Benares government opium agencies separately from Malwa opium and princely-state trade. No invented volumes or profits.
7. **Indigo:** Kept the major 1859–1860 Indigo Rebellion explicitly after the end of Company government (1858); warned that the 1860 archival compilation includes both periods and does not prove an unnamed individual witness’s words.
8. **Deindustrialization:** Distinguished textile export decline from output, employment, earnings, and manufacturing share; discussed technological, price, political, climate, and institutional explanations rather than declaring a single unverified causal estimate.
9. **Charter Acts:** Established that the 1813 Act limited the commercial monopoly, the 1833 Act ended the Company’s remaining commercial role, and government continued until transfer to the Crown in 1858.
10. **Bibliography:** Supplied identifiable editions and a specifically relevant verified Bagchi 1976 article instead of presenting later private-investment scholarship as a primary study of Company-era textile employment.

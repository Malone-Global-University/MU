## 4. Historical accuracy and documentary corrections completed

1. **Military headcounts clarified.** Initial draft conflated a popular figure of “nearly 1.4 million men served” with its denominator. Revised opening, §78.20, chapter summary, and army source window distinguish **1,440,437 new recruits to Indian ranks through December 1919** (877,068 combatants, 563,369 noncombatants), **239,561 in Indian ranks at the outbreak**, and the approximately **1,096,013 personnel estimated to have served overseas**. Recruitment years and theater service are no longer interchanged. Corroboration: Santanu Das, [Cambridge chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/race-empire-and-first-world-war-writing/indians-at-home-mesopotamia-and-france-19141918-towards-an-intimate-history/8154C52599CB9161571D040939205138).
2. **King's commissioned rank qualified.** Revised §78.2 distinguishes officers in the combatant regiments from medical/other services and explicitly identifies Montagu's **August 20, 1917** announcement about removing the bar and nine recommended Indian officers, while noting the limited implementation. Evidence: [Hansard, Army Commissions to Indians](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1917-08-20/debates/e851a4ac-67bb-4d20-9cdb-52c0a58e8bea/ArmyCommissionsToIndians).
3. **Komagata Maru voyage sharpened.** Revised §78.9 describes departure from Hong Kong and the route via East Asian ports/Japan, explicitly distinguishing it from the continuous voyage from the country of origin demanded by Canadian regulations. The 376 passengers and May 23 arrival are retained. Evidence: [Parks Canada historical summary](https://www.canada.ca/en/parks-canada/news/2016/08/the-komagata-maru-incident-of-1914.html).
4. **Definite bibliography error corrected.** Gajendra Singh's work is now correctly cited as *The Testimonies of Indian Soldiers and the Two World Wars: Between Self and Sepoy* (Bloomsbury Academic, 2014). Verified against [publisher catalogue](https://www.bloomsbury.com/us/testimonies-of-indian-soldiers-and-the-two-world-wars-9781780936277/).
5. **Influenza reference corrected.** David Arnold's *Death and the Modern Empire: The 1918–19 Influenza Epidemic in India* is now properly identified as a journal article, not an independent monograph. The qualified mortality treatment is retained; [journal record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/transactions-of-the-royal-historical-society/article/abs/death-and-the-modern-empire-the-191819-influenza-epidemic-in-india/7108A9A3EC7F29ED7566C9387ED24B73).
6. **Document traceability improved.** Added the historical military-recruitment denominator reference, Hansard commissioning announcement, Parks Canada, and the British Library [censorship archive catalogue](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/038-000340714) to the Source Register. Each is expressly an identified record or published analysis, not a fabricated facsimile quotation.

### Historical and evidentiary safeguards retained

- Wartime loyalty is not equated with popular consent; recruitment's voluntary legal framework is distinguished from coercive district pressures.
- Diverse South Asian regions (Nepal, Burma, Ceylon), soldiers' families and women, the overseas diaspora, and Indian workers remain represented.
- Ghadar revolutionary goals, Home Rule constitutional campaigning, and Congress–League bargaining remain analytically distinct.
- 1915 Ceylon disturbances are distinguished from the ensuing colonial martial-law repression.
- The 1917 Declaration, 1918 report, and 1919 legislation are not conflated; Chapter 79 retains primary ownership of the postwar Gandhi/Rowlatt/Amritsar/Non-Cooperation narrative.
- All seven primary-source study windows are identified as **editorial paraphrases/source studies**, not quotations collated from manuscripts or original official editions. The everyday-life reconstruction is labeled illustrative, not a verified named household.

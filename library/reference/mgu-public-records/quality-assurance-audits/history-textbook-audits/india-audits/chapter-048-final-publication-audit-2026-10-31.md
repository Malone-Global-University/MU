## 3. Independent historical spot-checks and final refinements

1. **Kabir's early manuscript evidence (§48.23):** Clarified the Fatehpur manuscript (1582) and even earlier Kabir-attributed material in the Goindval pothis (generally 1570s). Scholarly evidence: Kabir in the Rajasthani Tradition (University of Osaka repository), https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/94758/triveni_all.pdf and Gurinder Singh Mann, *The Making of Sikh Scripture*, https://academic.oup.com/book/25839/chapter-abstract/193497471.
2. **John Stratton Hawley, *A Storm of Songs* (2015) (§48.1/48.5):** Verified historical claim that the unified bhakti-movement construct took shape through early-modern recollection and modern literary-national intellectual histories, including key figures Shukla and Dwivedi: https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1c84d6f and https://universityseminars.columbia.edu/books/a-storm-of-songs-india-and-the-idea-of-the-bhakti-movement/.
3. **Goindval pothis and Kartarpur bir (§§48.39, 48.41):** Verified as subject of manuscript-critical scholarship, distinguishing traditional histories and extant manuscript witnesses: Pashaura Singh, https://academic.oup.com/book/10787/chapter-abstract/158919311 ; Gurinder Singh Mann, https://academic.oup.com/book/25839/chapter-abstract/193497599.
4. **2010 Ravidassia declaration (§48.25):** Verified Dera Sachkhand Ballan's public announcement, noting it is a modern afterlife rather than medieval event: https://indianexpress.com/article/news-archive/web/religion-or-a-prayer-for-identity/.
5. **Vithoba origins (§48.8):** Qualified the Kannada/pastoral hypothesis. R. C. Dhere documented pastoral connections but argued that evidence did *not* prove the specific hero-stone/original deified cattle-protector theory: https://academic.oup.com/book/27754/chapter-abstract/197963731 and https://academic.oup.com/book/27754.
6. **Firdausi lineage (§48.32):** Confirmed the Firdawsiyya traced its lineage to the Kubrawiyya and emerged in Delhi before thriving in Bihar; *Encyclopaedia of Islam THREE* entry (2012): https://isamveri.org/pdfdkm/14/DKM140258.pdf.
7. **Nandanar and Kanakadasa stories (§§48.3, 48.7):** In the manuscript they are presented as later hagiographical traditions, not independently verified events; maintained the qualification.
8. **Basavanna, Bijjala, and the inter-caste marriage narrative (§48.18):** The manuscript correctly identifies the dramatic account as a later hagiographical tradition, not established political fact. No further change required.
9. **Shrinathji/Nathdwara (§48.11):** A move around 1670–1672 is documented in later community history and historical records; manuscript correctly labels it tradition and places it after Vallabha: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/236490/?type=print and https://www.nathdwara.in/shreenathji.php.
10. **Mian Mir foundation story (§48.33):** Strengthened the wording to identify a *later tradition*, not corroborated by early Sikh/Sufi materials. Evidence of late attribution: https://auqaf.punjab.gov.pk/mian-mir and https://gurmatveechar.com/books/English_Books/English_Thesis_Papers/Guru.Arjan.Dev.Ji.1581.-.1606.a.Study.of.Sikh.Institutions.Practice.and.Outlook.by.Joginder.Singh.Rekhi.%28GurmatVeechar.com%29.pdf. Official Sikh institutional retellings exist, but those are not equivalent to early corroboration.
11. **Guru Amar Das's Anand (§48.39):** Clarified that its use in Sikh marriage rites is later, preventing the modern ceremonial context from being projected without qualification into the sixteenth century.
12. **Guru Arjan, Adi Granth, and Jahangir (§48.41):** Preserved nuance concerning scripture formation, Khusrau, imperial suspicion, and Sikh martyrdom tradition. The Adi Granth of 1604 is not identical in history to later Sikh recensions, and the source windows do not invent scriptural verse wording.

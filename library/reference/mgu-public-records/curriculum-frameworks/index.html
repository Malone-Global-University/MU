Curriculum Frameworks Overview

The Curriculum Frameworks collection documents the educational structures, classifications, learning progressions, and instructional principles used to organize learning at Malone Global University. These records explain both the frameworks themselves and the academic reasoning behind their design, so students and educators can understand how MGU coursework is organized.

The MGU Nine-Category Pastry Classification System is documented here as an institutional approach to reconciling different ways of teaching and organizing pastry knowledge. Its purpose is not to rank pastries or to force hybrid products into one exclusive family. It is to make techniques, dough families, production methods, and culinary traditions intelligible together, while ensuring that the necessary skills are taught.

Substantive framework publications in this collection are provided as PDFs. Instructional pages and course navigation belong in the relevant academic department; the Public Records collection maintains the documentary rationale and the history of its development.

MGU Nine-Category Pastry Classification System — Revised November 2026

Revised framework white paper and student orientation. Explains why MGU developed its own classification approach, how technique-based and regionally organized pastry traditions differ, and what difficulties the new framework is intended to address. Intended to help students familiar with other systems transition confidently into MGU pastry coursework and to introduce beginners to the existence of different classification traditions.

Using the Pastry Classification Documents

Students beginning MGU pastry instruction should read the revised edition first. It provides an orientation to the nine categories, explains the differences between MGU's framework and other culinary organizational systems, and prepares students to navigate coursework without assuming that a pastry can belong to only one category.

The original 2025 edition preserves the first published formulation of the system. It is provided for historical comparison and for readers interested in how the classification developed. Both documents are explanatory resources: they do not replace the practical lessons, demonstrations, assessments, or recipe instruction delivered through the Culinary Department.

Collection Scope and Filing Standards

Curriculum Frameworks houses the formal description and educational justification of academic classifications and cross-cutting instructional structures. Documents should distinguish between an institution's chosen organizational conventions and the underlying technical competencies learners must master.

Earlier editions are retained with clear dates and edition labels rather than silently overwritten. A framework may be revised as educational requirements evolve, while the historical record remains accessible. Course pages, instructional articles, and practical learning resources are maintained in the appropriate academic departments and linked from this collection where their locations are confirmed.

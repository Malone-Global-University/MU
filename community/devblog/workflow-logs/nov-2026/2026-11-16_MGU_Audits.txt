WORKFLOW NOTE

Title
MGU History Textbook Audit Archive — Index Creation, Navigation Integration, and Deployment Verification

Date
2026-11-16

Area
MGU Public Records — Quality Assurance and Audits

Primary directory:

`C:\MGU\library\reference\mgu-public-records\quality-assurance-audits\history-textbook-audits\`

Affected components:
- Quality Assurance and Audits parent index.
- History Textbook Audits master index.
- Twelve subject-specific historical textbook audit indices.
- Chapter-level audit document navigation.
- Public Records website navigation and shared components.

Problem
MGU had accumulated hundreds of historical textbook chapter audit reports across multiple history departments. These records existed in subject-specific directories but lacked a complete, centralized public navigation system.

The principal deficiencies were:

1. No unified directory providing access to all history textbook audit collections.
2. No consistent HTML indices linking individual chapter audit records within each subject directory.
3. Several directory names contained spelling errors or inconsistent naming conventions.
4. Some textbook chapters had no audit documents because separate audits were unnecessary; therefore, generating links for every chapter would have introduced nonexistent targets.
5. The Quality Assurance and Audits parent page did not provide a direct navigation route to the history textbook audit collection.
6. There was no completed verification of the navigation hierarchy connecting institutional records, subject indices, and individual audit documents.

These conditions limited public discoverability and made the historical textbook quality-assurance record system more difficult to navigate.

Intended Goal
Establish a complete, publicly accessible, hierarchical navigation system for MGU's historical textbook audit records.

The required structure was:

MGU Public Records → Quality Assurance and Audits → History Textbook Audits → Subject Audit Index → Individual Chapter Audit

Each subject index was required to:

- Link exclusively to existing audit records.
- Preserve exact filenames and valid relative paths.
- Omit chapters without documented audits.
- Accommodate multiple audits for the same chapter.
- Maintain the standard MGU website layout and shared components.
- Provide consistent organization and navigation across historical textbook collections.

The master History Textbook Audits index was to link only to subject-level indices, not directly to individual audit documents.

Root Cause
The historical textbook production and review workflow generated audit documentation faster than the centralized Public Records indexing infrastructure was developed.

Audit files were retained in departmental subject directories, but corresponding navigation pages had not been systematically created.

Directory naming inconsistencies developed during file organization, and the parent archive initially referenced several outdated directory names.

The underlying issue was a mismatch between the growing documentary archive and its public-facing catalog structure, rather than missing audit work or defective chapter content.

Steps Taken

1. Reviewed the existing directory inventory. Examined the PowerShell recursive listing of the History Textbook Audits collection to identify subject directories, chapter audit filenames, and other documents.

2. Established the master index. Created `history-textbook-audits/index.html` using MGU's existing Public Records HTML structure, including the shared stylesheet, dynamic header, breadcrumbs, bottom navigation, and footer.

3. Integrated parent navigation. Prepared the HTML snippet linking the History Textbook Audits collection from `quality-assurance-audits/index.html`, under the Audit Collections section.

4. Standardized directory naming. Corrected and reconciled directory references, including `chaina-audits` to `china-audits`, `Indigenous North America Audits` to `indigenous-north-america-audits`, and `iran` to `iran-audits`. Preserved other actual directory names, including `latin america-audits` and `midde-east-audits`.

5. Created twelve subject-specific indices. Generated individual `index.html` pages for:
   - Arab History
   - Chinese History
   - European History
   - Indian History
   - Indigenous North American History
   - Persian and Iranian History
   - Japanese History
   - Korean History
   - Latin American History
   - Middle Eastern History
   - Russian History
   - Southeast Asian History

6. Organized audit records by chapter. Grouped existing audit reports under their associated chapter numbers and retained multiple reports where independent, initial, consolidated, verification, or final publication audits existed.

7. Excluded nonexistent and unrelated records. Omitted chapter numbers without recorded audits and excluded identifiable non-audit materials, including chapter HTML files, instructional plans, master outlines, and source ledgers where applicable.

8. Preserved the website's shared architecture. Used `/component/lib.css` and the established dynamic component loaders rather than introducing a separate page design or extensive embedded styling.

9. Performed static link validation. Compared generated audit links against the supplied directory inventory. The completed review reported 532 matching audit links across twelve subject indices and no duplicate HTML identifiers.

10. Addressed the missing India index. During the final navigation review, identified that `india-audits/index.html` had not yet been created. Generated the missing page, completing the twelve-subject hierarchy.

11. Attempted independent live verification. Tested public website navigation through an external checker. The MGU homepage was accessible, but the checker could not retrieve the audit pages because of environmental access and DNS limitations. Cache-bypassing attempts did not resolve the limitation.

12. Completed deployment and user-side validation. The site administrator corrected the remaining directory references, deployed the indices, tested the live navigation, and confirmed that all parent and subject-level indices were functioning.

Key Change
Converted MGU's distributed historical textbook audit directories into a unified, twelve-subject public archive with chapter-level navigation exclusively to existing audit records.

Rules Going Forward

1. Existing records only. Never create audit links for chapters unless the corresponding audit document exists.

2. No mandatory audit for every chapter. The absence of a separate audit is not automatically a deficiency. Audit requirements depend on the chapter's editorial history and review needs.

3. Preserve exact filenames. Use the deployed file's actual name, extension, capitalization, and path when generating links.

4. Maintain directory consistency. Ensure parent and subject indices use the correct current folder names. Encode spaces and special URL characters where necessary.

5. Preserve the navigation hierarchy. The History Textbook Audits master index links to subject indices; subject indices link to individual audit records.

6. Maintain shared MGU components. New and updated pages must retain the established stylesheet, dynamic header, breadcrumbs, bottom navigation, and footer.

7. Update indices when new records are published. Add new audit documents to the relevant subject index after the file has been placed in its intended directory.

8. Retain multiple audit stages when applicable. Initial reviews, independent audits, corrective reviews, and final publication audits may remain separately accessible to preserve documentary history.

9. Distinguish verification methods. File-inventory validation, structural HTML checks, and live website validation must be reported separately.

10. Preserve documentary integrity. Do not imply that an audit establishes independent expert peer review unless that review actually occurred.

11. Follow the Public Records publication policy. The existing audit links currently reference Markdown records. Any subsequent transition to the established PDF-only formal report standard must preserve document identification and update the corresponding index links.

12. Avoid unnecessary restructuring. The established navigation framework should remain stable as new audit records accumulate.

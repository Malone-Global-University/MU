WORKFLOW NOTE

Title
Physics Department Branch Index and Content Taxonomy Planning

Date
2026-05-21

Area
MGU Physics Department, Library Encyclopedia, Ebook Planning, Physics Branch Navigation

Problem
The Physics Department page began as a narrow branch list that mixed page purposes. It was unclear whether physics content should be organized as department lessons, encyclopedia articles, branch index pages, or a downloadable textbook-style ebook. The page also needed a cleaner physics taxonomy so the department could scale beyond a flat list of topics.

Intended Goal
The Physics Department should function as a structured instructional area, while the Library Encyclopedia should hold reusable topic articles, and ebooks should exist as finished downloadable publications. Physics branch pages should serve as department navigation hubs, not encyclopedia articles or textbook chapters.

Root Cause
The same physics topics can appear in multiple formats, but each format has a different purpose. Without a clear separation, the department risked becoming a mixed reference directory, course system, and ebook table of contents all at once.

Steps Taken

1. Clarified the main Physics Department structure around three divisions: Classical Physics, Modern Physics, and Applied and Interdisciplinary Physics.
2. Built index-page drafts for the Physics Department homepage and the three division pages.
3. Built branch index-page drafts for Classical Physics: Mechanics, Electromagnetism, Thermodynamics, Waves and Oscillations, Optics, and Fluid Mechanics.
4. Built branch index-page drafts for Modern Physics: Quantum Mechanics, Relativity, Atomic Physics, Nuclear Physics, Particle Physics, Condensed Matter Physics, and Plasma Physics.
5. Built branch index-page drafts for Applied and Interdisciplinary Physics: Astrophysics, Cosmology, Geophysics, Atmospheric Physics, Biophysics, Medical Physics, and Computational Physics.
6. Confirmed that all planned branch-level index pages had been completed.
7. Separated three content models: Department HTML lessons, Library Encyclopedia articles, and self-published ebook downloads.
8. Defined the Library Encyclopedia taxonomy as a knowledge-reference structure under `/library/encyclopedia/science/physics/`.
9. Defined the Physics Department taxonomy as an instructional course structure under `/department/science/physics/courses/`.
10. Clarified that department lessons should teach in sequence, encyclopedia articles should explain concepts as reusable references, and ebooks should package finished material for download.

Key Change
Physics content was separated into three distinct systems: department lessons for instruction, encyclopedia articles for reference, and ebooks for finished downloadable publications.

Outcome
The Physics Department now has a clean branch-navigation model, and the broader MGU physics content system has a clear division of labor. Branch index pages are complete for the planned physics taxonomy. Future work can now move into either structured department lessons, encyclopedia topic articles, or ebook production without confusing the formats.

Validation
The planned branch list was checked against the completed pages: Classical, Modern, Applied, all classical branches, all modern branches, all applied branches, and the Physics Department homepage were accounted for. The remaining work was identified as topic-level content, not missing branch index pages.

Rules Going Forward
Department pages should teach through courses, units, lessons, objectives, exercises, and assessments.
Library Encyclopedia pages should explain reusable concepts through taxonomy-based reference articles.
Ebook pages should present finished downloadable products such as PDF or EPUB editions.
Branch index pages should not become full textbook chapters unless they are explicitly part of a course or ebook.
Topic articles should live in the Library Encyclopedia unless they are written as structured lessons inside a department course.
Course lessons may link to encyclopedia articles as assigned reading or supporting reference.

Notes
The current branch taxonomy is stable enough to use as the foundation for future physics work. The next major decision is whether to begin department course lessons, encyclopedia topic articles, or ebook compilation.

WORKFLOW NOTE

Title
Martial Arts System Structure and Aikido Content Planning

Date
2026-05-21

Area
Defense Department / Martial Arts / Aikido, Karate, MaloneRyu

Problem
The martial arts section had strong early folders and home pages, but the relationship between Aikido, Karate, and MaloneRyu needed clearer conceptual separation. Aikido and Karate function as technical systems, while MaloneRyu functions as a training, validation, and integration methodology. The Aikido technique portal also had thin sections that needed expansion without making the page too busy.

Intended Goal
Keep the early pages clean, scalable, and readable while preserving a clear doctrine structure. Aikido should be developed first as a technical dataset for balance disruption, redirection, and structural control. Karate should remain the striking system. MaloneRyu should remain the governing methodology that evaluates, modifies, and integrates techniques.

Root Cause
The site structure was still in an early build stage. Folders had been created consistently, but the deeper purpose of each system had not yet been fully distinguished in the page language. There was also a risk of overloading home and index pages with theory that belongs in videos, training notes, analysis pages, or practice materials.

Steps Taken

1. Reviewed the standardized martial arts folder structure for Aikido, Karate, and MaloneRyu.
2. Confirmed that Aikido and Karate should be treated as technical systems, while MaloneRyu should be treated as the governing training and application framework.
3. Identified the Aikido technique master page as the current priority.
4. Drafted missing markdown sections for the Aikido technique portal, including Core Principle, Flow Logic, System Relationship, and Common Failure Points.
5. Confirmed that detailed relationships such as Aikido throws appearing in Karate kata bunkai should not clutter top-level pages and should instead live in training notes, videos, analysis, applications, or later lesson maps.
6. Reviewed the current text for the Martial Arts, Aikido, Karate, and MaloneRyu home pages.
7. Recommended keeping early pages concise and using them as clean entry points before deeper material is added.
8. Noted the larger source base for future Aikido development, including YouTube training video, 127 long-form written analysis files, and a 44-page authored Aikido PDF.

Key Change
The martial arts section was clarified as a layered system: Aikido and Karate provide technical material, while MaloneRyu governs training methodology, validation, and integration.

Outcome
The structure now has a clearer operating model. Aikido can be fleshed out first without forcing Karate or MaloneRyu to be completed at the same time. The home pages can remain clean, while deeper content is reserved for subfolder index pages, technique pages, analysis pages, training notes, and later lesson maps.

Validation
The folder structures were compared across Aikido, Karate, and MaloneRyu. Shared folders now provide consistency, while MaloneRyu’s unique folders such as constitution, integration, sparring-drills, and validation-standards correctly reflect its role as a governing methodology rather than a normal technique collection.

Rules Going Forward
Keep top-level pages simple and navigational. Do not overload home pages with detailed bunkai, technique comparisons, or long theory. Place detailed technical relationships in analysis, applications, training, techniques, or integration pages. Develop Aikido first, then use lessons learned from that buildout to polish Karate and MaloneRyu.

Notes
The current priority is to get early index pages and subfolder pages online before heavy polishing. After the Aikido structure is filled in, the larger body of videos, written analysis, and the authored PDF can be mapped into the system more carefully.

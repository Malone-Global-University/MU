WORKFLOW NOTE

Title
Malone Doctrine Pillar Restructuring and War College Rebuild Planning

Date
2026-05-21

Area
Malone Doctrine architecture, War College curriculum structure, doctrine organization system

Problem
Malone Doctrine had become structurally ambiguous because it was simultaneously functioning as a personal moral philosophy, political framework, defense doctrine, and economic strategy system without clear separation of concerns. Courses, modules, and lesson concepts overlapped across tiers, resulting in multiple competing drafts, unclear lesson placement, and organizational drift. The existing “12 volumes” approach forced unrelated domains into linear structures that caused recurring conceptual bleed between civilian conduct, statecraft, warfare, institutional theory, and economic systems.

Intended Goal
Rebuild Malone Doctrine as a unified doctrine divided into clearly defined pillars with hard conceptual boundaries. Establish a scalable architecture where all future courses, lessons, tiers, and institutional structures flow from domain-based pillars rather than mixed-topic volumes. Ensure each concept, course, and lesson has one primary functional home.

Root Cause
The doctrine was originally organized primarily through tiers and sequential volumes rather than through distinct operational domains. This caused multiple categories of thought—political governance, civilian ethics, warfare, and economic sovereignty—to compete inside the same structures. Because concerns were not separated at the architectural level, lesson development repeatedly drifted into overlap and duplication.

Steps Taken

1. Reviewed the existing Initiate, Strategist, and Commander tier structures and identified recurring overlap between political, social, defense, and economic themes.

2. Examined surviving course pages (MD201–MD304) to determine the actual operational domains each course was addressing.

3. Identified that the real structural issue was not tier sequencing but doctrinal ambiguity regarding the purpose and scope of Malone Doctrine itself.

4. Reframed Malone Doctrine as one unified doctrine expressed through four distinct pillars instead of twelve mixed-purpose volumes.

5. Defined the four foundational pillars:

   * Political Pillar (Power & Governance)
   * Social Pillar (Conduct & Order)
   * Defense Pillar (Conflict & Security)
   * Economic Pillar (Production & Sustainability)

6. Established the requirement that every future lesson, module, course, and institutional component must map cleanly to one pillar before development begins.

7. Determined that tiers (Initiate, Strategist, Commander) should remain but operate inside individual pillars rather than across the entire doctrine globally.

8. Decided to rebuild the War College structure from the ground up before large-scale lesson production begins.

Key Change
Malone Doctrine was redefined from a mixed-volume ideological system into a four-pillar doctrine framework with explicit domain separation and functional boundaries.

Outcome
The doctrine now has a scalable conceptual foundation capable of supporting political theory, civilian ethics, defense strategy, and economic sovereignty without overlap. Future lesson writing can proceed with significantly reduced ambiguity because each topic now has a designated domain. The War College structure is positioned for cleaner curriculum development, more disciplined institutional tone, and improved long-term maintainability.

Validation
The new pillar model successfully explained and resolved the recurring overlap problems observed in previous course drafts and module structures. Existing courses could immediately be classified into primary doctrinal domains, confirming that the new architecture provides clearer organizational separation than the previous volume-based structure.

Rules Going Forward

* Every new lesson, course, module, or doctrine document must belong primarily to one pillar.
* Pillars must maintain strict conceptual boundaries.
* Political, Social, Defense, and Economic domains must not be casually blended within single lessons without explicit justification.
* Tiers are subordinate to pillars, not replacements for them.
* Avoid creating mixed-topic “volume” structures that force unrelated concepts together.
* Establish pillar definitions before large-scale curriculum production resumes.

Notes
The rebuild is occurring at an ideal time because lesson production had not yet fully scaled. Existing course concepts remain valuable and can largely be repurposed under the new pillar structure rather than discarded. The new framework also positions Malone War College to evolve into multiple schools or academies under one unified doctrine system in the future.

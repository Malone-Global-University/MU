WORKFLOW NOTE

Title
Regional Hip-Hop Poster Image Batch

Date
2026-05-20

Area
MGU Music Department image assets / hip-hop artist index visuals

Problem
The Music Department hip-hop section needed matching hero-style images for several genre and regional categories. The existing East Coast image established a strong visual format, but additional categories needed equivalent poster graphics with the same cinematic, gritty, typography-heavy MGU style.

Intended Goal
Create a consistent image set that visually supports the hip-hop category pages while changing the subject matter, city references, slogans, and cultural details for each section.

Root Cause
The original East Coast image served as a strong design template, but each additional category required its own visual identity. The new images needed to match the overall MGU poster style without simply repeating the same regional cues, skyline, artist grouping, or text emphasis.

Steps Taken

1. Used the uploaded East Coast rap poster as the visual reference for composition, scale, typography, dark background, gold accents, artist collage layout, and MGU Music Department branding.
2. Generated a Southern Hip-Hop and Crunk image focused on Atlanta, Houston, Memphis, New Orleans, Miami, Virginia, bass music, bounce, crunk, chopped-and-screwed production, trap roots, and club culture.
3. Generated a Midwest Hip-Hop image focused on Chicago, Detroit, Cleveland, St. Louis, rapid-fire rhymes, soulful samples, battle rap, melodic flows, and heartland innovation.
4. Generated an Alternative and Experimental Hip-Hop image focused on abstract lyricism, jazz influence, live instrumentation, underground production, electronic textures, and unconventional hip-hop design.
5. Generated a Trap, Drill, and Modern Street Rap image focused on heavy bass, dark melodies, digital-era street rap, mixtape culture, streaming-era influence, and contemporary urban production.
6. Generated a Contemporary Hip-Hop image focused on lyrical rap, melodic rap, pop-rap, alternative rap, internet scenes, fashion, digital media, politics, and global pop-culture reach.
7. Generated a West Coast Rap, G-Funk, Hyphy, and Bay Area to L.A. Movements image focused on California, G-Funk, lowriders, Compton, Bay Area connections, funk roots, political voices, and West Coast legacy.

Key Change
The single East Coast visual style was expanded into a consistent regional and genre-based poster set for the MGU Music Department hip-hop pages.

Outcome
The session produced a coordinated set of hip-hop category images that now cover Southern Hip-Hop, Midwest Hip-Hop, Alternative and Experimental Hip-Hop, Trap/Drill/Modern Street Rap, Contemporary Hip-Hop, and West Coast Rap. Each image keeps the same general branding system while changing the cultural references and visual details to fit the category.

Validation
Each image was visually checked in-chat after generation for overall match to the uploaded East Coast reference, including poster layout, dark cinematic palette, large distressed typography, artist-collage arrangement, record crates or music objects, city/regional background cues, and MGU Music Department footer branding.

Rules Going Forward
Use the East Coast poster as the base visual grammar for future hip-hop category images, but change the regional cues, slogans, background details, wardrobe references, and music-culture objects for each category. Keep the MGU footer branding consistent. Avoid overloading the image with too much small text because generated poster text can become distorted. For image requests involving public figures, continue producing broad cultural poster-style compositions rather than relying on exact photographic identity accuracy.

Notes
The image tool initially refused some requests, but rerunning with the clarification that public-figure themed cultural poster images are allowed produced usable results. The generated text inside images may contain minor misspellings or imperfect slogans, so final production assets should be visually inspected before site deployment.

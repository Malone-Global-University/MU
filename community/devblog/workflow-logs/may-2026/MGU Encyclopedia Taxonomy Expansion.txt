WORKFLOW NOTE

Title
MGU Encyclopedia Taxonomy Expansion

Date
2026-05-21

Area
MGU Library / Encyclopedia taxonomy and category structure

Problem
The encyclopedia category system needed a cleaner long-term structure before hundreds of new articles were added. Some domains were missing, some were too broad or overlapping, and several subject areas needed expanded folder trees.

Intended Goal
Create a polished institutional taxonomy with stable top-level domains, scalable subdomain trees, and alphabetized public category links.

Root Cause
The original encyclopedia structure grew organically, mixing production folders, public-facing domains, academic disciplines, practical fields, and thematic categories at the same level.

Steps Taken

1. Reviewed the existing visible encyclopedia category list and separated public taxonomy from internal production folders such as `content` and `markdown`.
2. Created and delivered a nested `science/` folder hierarchy ZIP, including biology, chemistry, physics, astronomy, earth science, environmental science, and scientific foundations.
3. Rewrote the main `science/index.html` and created subdomain index pages for astronomy, biology, chemistry, earth science, environmental science, physics, and scientific foundations.
4. Trimmed and normalized the public encyclopedia domains into a cleaner top-level taxonomy.
5. Added missing major knowledge domains, including social science, geography, and education.
6. Expanded folder trees for art, business, communication, computer science, conspiracy studies, culinary arts, defense, engineering, geography, history, humanities, language, law, mathematics, medicine, political science, religion, science, social science, education, and Illuminati.
7. Updated category-link snippets to reflect the revised taxonomy and alphabetized domain presentation.

Key Change
The encyclopedia was reorganized from a loose category list into a scalable institutional taxonomy built around stable top-level knowledge domains.

Outcome
MGU Encyclopedia now has a clearer structure for future growth, with public-facing domains, subdomains, and folder trees ready to support large-scale article expansion.

Validation
The taxonomy was checked for alphabetical presentation, domain consistency, reduced redundancy, and clearer placement of broad subjects such as science, business, social science, geography, and education.

Rules Going Forward
Keep top-level domains stable and avoid adding new root categories casually. Place new topics under the most appropriate existing domain whenever possible. Use cross-links for interdisciplinary overlap instead of duplicating folder structures. Keep visible links alphabetized.

Notes
The final 21-domain model includes the added Illuminati domain as a distinct thematic category. Future work should include creating folder bundles and index pages for each expanded domain tree.

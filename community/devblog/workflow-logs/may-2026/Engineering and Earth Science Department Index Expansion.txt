WORKFLOW NOTE

Title
Engineering and Earth Science Department Index Expansion

Date
2026-05-21

Area
MGU Department Structure / Engineering Division / Earth Science Division / Biology Division

Problem
The department structure for Earth Science and Engineering lacked fully developed index hierarchies and descriptive catalog-style navigation pages. Many course links existed only as titles without supporting descriptive paragraphs or deeper nested index structures. The taxonomy needed clearer academic organization so visitors could understand subject relationships and navigate progressively through engineering and science disciplines.

Intended Goal
Create scalable multi-level HTML index structures for Biology, Earth Science, Geology, Engineering, and Engineering subdisciplines using consistent MGU styling, descriptive hero sections, and explanatory course-link paragraphs. The system should resemble a structured university catalog with expandable academic pathways.

Root Cause
The original department pages functioned primarily as flat navigation lists without sufficient semantic hierarchy or explanatory context. Subdisciplines were not yet scaffolded into reusable nested index systems, making the department structure feel incomplete and limiting long-term scalability.

Steps Taken

1. Added descriptive hero paragraphs and course descriptions to the Biology Division index page for Animal, Cellular, and Plant Biology.

2. Created a new Earth Science Division index page including:

   * Geology
   * Meteorology
   * Oceanography
   * Environmental Science

3. Built a Geology index hierarchy including:

   * Mineralogy
   * Petrology
   * Structural Geology
   * Stratigraphy
   * Geomorphology
   * Geophysics
   * Paleontology

4. Created second-tier Geology sub-index pages with hero sections and nested course structures for:

   * Mineralogy
   * Petrology
   * Structural Geology
   * Stratigraphy
   * Geomorphology
   * Geophysics
   * Paleontology

5. Expanded Engineering Division taxonomy into structured subfields including:

   * Mechanical Engineering
   * Electrical Engineering
   * Civil & Structural Engineering
   * Computer & Software Engineering
   * Aerospace & Systems Design
   * Materials Engineering
   * Industrial Engineering

6. Added descriptive paragraphs to Engineering featured-page links on the main Engineering page.

7. Built full HTML index pages for primary Engineering branches including:

   * Mechanical Engineering
   * Electrical Engineering
   * Civil Engineering
   * Computer & Software Engineering
   * Aerospace Engineering

8. Created second-tier Mechanical Engineering indices including:

   * Thermodynamics
   * Fluid Mechanics

9. Created second-tier Electrical Engineering indices including:

10. Created second-tier Computer & Software Engineering indices including:

    * Software Engineering

Key Change
The department system evolved from mostly flat subject lists into a scalable multi-level academic taxonomy with consistent nested index structures and descriptive educational navigation.

Outcome
The MGU science and engineering departments now resemble a structured academic catalog with clear disciplinary hierarchy, explanatory descriptions, and expandable pathways for future lesson/course development. Navigation depth, semantic clarity, and organizational scalability improved substantially.

Validation
Validation was performed through structural review of generated HTML sections, consistency checks across hero layouts and course-link formatting, and comparison of taxonomy depth between related disciplines. Cross-discipline naming consistency and nested hierarchy organization were visually verified.

Rules Going Forward

* Every department index should contain a descriptive hero paragraph.
* Every course link should include a supporting descriptive paragraph.
* Major academic branches should receive second-tier sub-index pages before lesson-level expansion.
* Maintain consistent HTML structure across all department pages.
* Keep taxonomy broad enough for future expansion while avoiding unnecessary fragmentation.
* Use nested academic hierarchy to separate divisions, disciplines, subfields, and lesson-level content.

Notes
The Engineering and Earth Science structures now closely mirror the scalable taxonomy previously developed for Physics and other MGU academic systems. The next logical phase would be lesson-level scaffolding or automated index generation tooling for large departmental expansions.

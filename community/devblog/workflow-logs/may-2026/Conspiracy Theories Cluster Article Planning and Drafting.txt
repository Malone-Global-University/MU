WORKFLOW NOTE

Title
Conspiracy Theories Cluster Article Planning and Drafting

Date
2026-05-21

Area
MGU Encyclopedia / Illuminati / Conspiracy Theories Cluster

Problem
The Conspiracy Theories cluster needed a clear article structure and multiple long-form encyclopedia sections. The cluster had to avoid treating conspiracy claims as automatic fact while still taking real institutional power, media influence, financial pressure, political capture, and symbolic interpretation seriously.

Intended Goal
Create a consistent article model for the Conspiracy Theories cluster that explains claims, evidence standards, institutional mechanisms, overreach risks, and relationship to Illuminati mythology in an analytical, evidence-first style.

Root Cause
The cluster covers sensitive and easily distorted subjects. Without a disciplined structure, the pages could collapse into vague hidden-control claims, repeat unsupported conspiracy assumptions, or fail to distinguish documented influence from speculation, symbolism, and mythology.

Steps Taken

1. Established the Conspiracy Theories cluster as a claim-pattern cluster rather than an organization-based cluster.
2. Defined the five major child article roles: Global Control Theories, Financial Control Theories, Political Infiltration Theories, Media Control Theories, and Symbolic Conspiracy Frameworks.
3. Drafted the main Conspiracy Theories overview article sections, including Overview, What This Theory Claims, Historical Background, Core Narrative Patterns, Real Institutions and Documented Influence, Where the Claims Overreach, Evidence/Source Criticism/Verification, Relationship to Illuminati Mythology, Article Summary, and Related Topics.
4. Created a full section structure for the Financial Control Theories article.
5. Drafted Financial Control Theories sections covering overview, claims, history, money/debt/power, central banks, banking networks, markets and asset control, regulatory capture, overreach, antisemitic and scapegoating risks, evidence standards, Illuminati mythology, and summary.
6. Created a full section structure for the Political Infiltration Theories article.
7. Drafted Political Infiltration Theories sections covering overview, claims, history, parties and elections, bureaucracy and agencies, courts and law, intelligence and covert operations, lobbying and policy capture, overreach, evidence standards, Illuminati mythology, and summary.
8. Created a full section structure for the Media Control Theories article.
9. Drafted Media Control Theories sections covering overview, claims, history, news agenda-setting, entertainment and social programming, advertising/public relations/perception management, digital platforms and algorithms, ownership and funding incentives, overreach, evidence standards, Illuminati mythology, and summary.
10. Created a full section structure for the Symbolic Conspiracy Frameworks article, positioning it as the decoding-method article for the cluster.

Key Change
The cluster was framed around evidence-based analysis of hidden-control narratives rather than around accepting or dismissing conspiracy claims as a whole.

Outcome
The Conspiracy Theories cluster now has a stable taxonomy, a repeatable article format, and multiple completed long-form sections. The pages distinguish real influence from unsupported claims and connect each topic back to Illuminati mythology without collapsing modern conspiracy narratives into the historical Bavarian Illuminati.

Validation
The structure was checked across multiple related articles for internal consistency. Financial, political, media, and symbolic theories now each have a distinct role: money and debt, state access, perception management, and symbolic decoding. Repeated sections such as overreach, evidence standards, and relationship to Illuminati mythology provide a consistent method for evaluating claims.

Rules Going Forward
Keep every conspiracy-theory article evidence-first. Separate influence, coordination, capture, corruption, and total control. Do not treat symbols, association, wealth, secrecy, repetition, or benefit as proof by themselves. Include source criticism and overreach sections wherever claims could become exaggerated or harmful. Avoid collective scapegoating and keep analysis focused on specific institutions, mechanisms, documents, and actors.

Notes
The Symbolic Conspiracy Frameworks article should be written next using the same structure. It should explain how symbols become evidence in conspiracy thinking, why symbolic interpretation feels persuasive, and how to evaluate symbolic claims through context, creator intent, historical usage, alternative meanings, and documented links.

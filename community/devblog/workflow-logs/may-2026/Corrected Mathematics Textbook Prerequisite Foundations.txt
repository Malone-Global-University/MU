WORKFLOW NOTE

Title
Corrected Mathematics Textbook Prerequisite Foundations

Date
2026-05-17

Area
MGU Mathematics Series textbook outline planning

Problem
Several textbook outline prompts had been accidentally framed as “calculus-based,” even when calculus was not the appropriate foundation. This created a risk that books such as Discrete Mathematics, Combinatorics, and Linear Algebra would be positioned too late in the MGU pathway or written with unnecessarily advanced prerequisites.

Intended Goal
Each mathematics textbook should have a prerequisite structure that matches its actual content. Books that naturally require calculus should remain calculus-based, while books built from algebra, logic, proof, vectors, counting, matrices, and finite structures should be labeled and outlined as non-calculus-based.

Root Cause
The phrase “calculus-based” had been hardcoded into multiple outline requests, even though the books belong to different branches of mathematics. The issue was not with the subject outlines themselves, but with the prerequisite classification being applied too broadly.

Steps Taken

1. Reviewed the earlier classification question about whether Numerical Methods, Data Science Foundations, Discrete Mathematics, Combinatorics, Scientific Computing, Machine Learning Mathematics, and Artificial Intelligence Foundations should be calculus-based.

2. Clarified that only some of the listed books should be calculus-based. Numerical Methods, Scientific Computing, and Machine Learning Mathematics should remain calculus-connected, while Data Science Foundations, Discrete Mathematics, Combinatorics, and Artificial Intelligence Foundations should not be strictly calculus-based.

3. Rewrote the Discrete Mathematics outline as a non-calculus textbook based on algebra, logic, proof, sets, counting, recursion, graph theory, algorithms, Boolean algebra, and computer-science applications.

4. Rewrote the Combinatorics outline as a non-calculus textbook based on algebra, counting, finite structures, recursion, combinatorial proof, recurrence relations, generating functions as formal algebraic tools, graph counting, designs, and symmetry.

5. Discussed Linear Algebra separately and clarified that the main MGU Linear Algebra textbook should not be hardcoded as calculus-based.

6. Rewrote the Linear Algebra outline as a non-calculus bridge between Precalculus and Calculus, based on Algebra II, coordinate geometry, trigonometry, vectors, systems, matrices, transformations, dimension, orthogonality, eigenvalues, diagonalization, and modeling.

7. Adjusted the recommended MGU mathematics pathway so Linear Algebra can sit after Trigonometry and Precalculus and before Calculus as a bridge course.

Key Change
The major correction was separating “mathematically advanced” from “calculus-based,” so each textbook now uses the right foundation for its subject.

Outcome
The MGU Mathematics Series now has a cleaner prerequisite structure. Discrete Mathematics and Combinatorics are positioned as algebra/discrete/proof-based books, while Linear Algebra is positioned as a non-calculus bridge from Precalculus into Calculus. This prevents unnecessary narrowing of the audience and gives students a stronger pathway into higher mathematics, computing, data science, and applied modeling.

Validation
The revised outlines were checked against the intended role of each subject. Discrete Mathematics now centers on logic, sets, proof, counting, graphs, algorithms, and Boolean logic. Combinatorics now centers on arrangements, selections, counting methods, recurrence, generating functions, graph counting, and designs without analytic combinatorics. Linear Algebra now centers on vectors, matrices, systems, transformations, dimension, orthogonality, and eigenvectors without requiring derivatives or integrals.

Rules Going Forward
Do not automatically label a mathematics textbook as calculus-based just because it is advanced.

Use “calculus-based” only when derivatives, integrals, limits, differential equations, continuous probability, optimization, or numerical calculus are structurally necessary.

Use “algebra-based,” “proof-based,” “discrete-based,” “vector/matrix-based,” or “statistics/programming-based” when those are the true foundations.

For MGU foundation texts, keep prerequisites as accessible as possible unless the subject genuinely requires calculus.

Use advanced companion titles later when a more rigorous calculus-heavy version is needed.

Notes
A useful future split would be to keep the main books accessible while creating later advanced versions where needed:

Data Science Foundations → Mathematical Data Science
Artificial Intelligence Foundations → Mathematical Foundations of Artificial Intelligence
Combinatorics → Advanced or Analytic Combinatorics
Discrete Mathematics → Advanced Discrete Mathematics
Linear Algebra → Numerical Linear Algebra or Linear Algebra for Machine Learning

The main Linear Algebra text now has a particularly strong role in the MGU sequence because it can prepare students for Calculus, Multivariable Calculus, Differential Equations, Physics, Engineering, Machine Learning, Computer Graphics, and Data Science without requiring calculus first.

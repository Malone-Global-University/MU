WORKFLOW NOTE

Title
College-Style Arithmetic Ebook Lesson Template

Date
2026-05-21

Area
HTML ebook lesson page / textbook-style layout / arithmetic lesson template

Problem
The first version of the HTML lesson page looked too modern, oversized, colorful, and rounded. It resembled an app-style learning page more than a serious textbook page. The fonts were too large, the colors felt too decorative, and the dialog boxes were too rounded for the intended physical-textbook style. The subject matter also began as a generic operations lesson rather than a basic arithmetic lesson.

Intended Goal
Create a more serious, proportionate college-style ebook lesson page that resembles a traditional published textbook, closer to a Holt-style math book. The page should use a left sidebar, formal typography, restrained colors, sharper boxes, structured lesson sections, and bottom ebook navigation with Previous Page and Next Page links.

Root Cause
The original design used modern web-card styling: large display fonts, warm decorative gradients, soft shadows, rounded panels, and oversized section blocks. That made the page visually attractive but less appropriate for a formal mathematics textbook or ebook lesson. The initial content topic also did not match the intended arithmetic subject.

Steps Taken

1. Reworked the visual style from a modern app-like page into a traditional textbook/eBook layout using smaller proportional typography, restrained academic colors, sharper boxes, and a cleaner page structure.
2. Changed the color palette to navy, steel gray, white, tan, and gold, reducing decorative effects and removing most rounded corners.
3. Updated the left sidebar into a formal contents panel with chapter navigation and a study note.
4. Replaced the original “Systems Thinking for Operational Decisions” topic with “Basic Arithmetic.”
5. Updated the chapter label to “Chapter 01 · Number Foundations.”
6. Revised the lesson metadata to fit foundational mathematics and arithmetic fundamentals.
7. Rewrote the overview, objectives, core concept, key principle, operation descriptions, worked examples, common mistakes, summary, and practice questions around basic arithmetic.
8. Added bottom navigation with “Previous Page” on the far left, “Back to Top” in the center, and “Next Page” on the far right.
9. Reviewed the page conceptually for subject fit and identified strengths and weaknesses for arithmetic instruction.

Key Change
The page was converted from a decorative modern lesson template into a serious college-style arithmetic ebook lesson with textbook structure, restrained styling, and proper ebook navigation.

Outcome
The page now looks more like a formal textbook lesson. It has a clearer academic tone, more appropriate font sizing, sharper instructional boxes, a left contents sidebar, structured lesson sections, arithmetic-focused content, and bottom navigation suitable for a multi-page ebook.

Validation
The revised page was checked against the user’s stated design goals: more serious, more proportionate, less rounded, less colorful, and closer to a traditional textbook style. The subject was also checked for instructional alignment, confirming that the page now introduces place value, the four operations, common arithmetic errors, and practice questions.

Rules Going Forward
Future ebook lesson pages should avoid oversized fonts, excessive gradients, heavy shadows, and rounded app-style cards when the goal is a traditional textbook appearance. Math lessons should use restrained colors, clear hierarchy, formal section headings, compact instructional boxes, and bottom page navigation. Arithmetic lessons should include visible worked examples and enough practice to support actual learning, not just overview reading.

Notes
The current page works well as a unit-opening or introductory arithmetic lesson. If it is intended to function as a full textbook lesson, it should eventually include vertically aligned arithmetic examples, one worked example for each operation, and a larger practice section divided into guided practice, independent practice, word problems, review, and challenge problems.

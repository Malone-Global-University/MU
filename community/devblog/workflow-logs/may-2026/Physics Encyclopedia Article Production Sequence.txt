WORKFLOW NOTE

Title
Physics Encyclopedia Article Production Sequence

Date
2026-05-21

Area
MGU Encyclopedia / Library / Physics article pages

Problem
The physics encyclopedia needed a large first-pass article foundation. The existing structure had branch indexes and planning logic, but many core topic articles did not yet exist as standalone HTML pages. There was also a structural concern that early overview articles should not be buried inside deep branch folders or mixed into index pages.

Intended Goal
Create a clean production sequence for physics encyclopedia articles using full HTML article pages. High-level overview pages should function as front matter in an `/anchor/` folder, while actual topic articles should live in their appropriate physics branch folders. Each article should use the MGU encyclopedia template, include article sections, breadcrumbs, related links, footer dates, and be delivered in zipped bundles.

Root Cause
The encyclopedia article system was still moving from outline/index planning into actual page production. The difference between anchor/front-matter pages, branch index pages, and topic article pages needed to be clarified before large-scale article generation could proceed cleanly.

Steps Taken

1. Clarified that broad overview articles should be treated as anchor/front-matter pages rather than being placed as branch `index.html` pages or buried in deep folders.

2. Recommended the `/library/encyclopedia/anchor/` folder for early overview articles such as physics, classical mechanics, waves and sound, thermodynamics, electricity and magnetism, optics, modern physics, nuclear astrophysics, applied physics, and scientific foundations.

3. Generated the first physics anchor article bundle using the provided MGU encyclopedia HTML template.

4. Generated the scientific foundations article set, including what-is-physics, measurement, units, scientific notation, significant figures, dimensional analysis, scalars and vectors, coordinate systems, graphs and models, and experimental error and uncertainty.

5. Began the classical mechanics sequence with kinematics-overview and distance-vs-displacement.

6. Continued classical mechanics with speed-vs-velocity-vs-acceleration, kinematic-equations, projectile-motion, and free-body-diagrams.

7. Generated the Newton’s laws article bundle: newtons-first-law, newtons-second-law, and newtons-third-law.

8. Generated the force, circular motion, rotation, and statics bundle: friction, inclined-plane-problems, circular-motion, centripetal-force, torque, rotational-motion, angular-kinematics, angular-momentum, and equilibrium-and-statics.

9. Generated the energy and momentum bundle: work-formula, kinetic-energy, potential-energy, conservation-of-energy, power-formula, mechanical-advantage, simple-machines, efficiency, energy-transfer-systems, momentum, impulse, conservation-of-momentum, elastic-collisions, inelastic-collisions, and center-of-mass.

10. Generated the gravity and orbital motion bundle: universal-gravitation, gravitational-fields, orbital-motion, escape-velocity, satellites, and keplers-laws.

11. Generated the waves foundation bundle: vibrations-and-oscillations, wave-properties, frequency-period-wavelength, wave-speed, interference, standing-waves, and sound-waves.

12. Generated the waves, sound, and optics continuation bundle: doppler-effect, resonance, light-as-a-wave, reflection, refraction, lenses, mirrors, diffraction, and electromagnetic-spectrum.

13. Generated the thermodynamics and fluids bundle: temperature, heat, thermal-energy, specific-heat, calorimetry, phase-changes, laws-of-thermodynamics, pressure, density, buoyancy, fluid-mechanics, bernoullis-principle, fluid-dynamics, and aerodynamics.

14. Generated the electricity and magnetism bundle: electric-charge, electric-force, electric-fields, electric-potential, voltage, current, resistance, ohms-law, circuits, series-and-parallel-circuits, capacitance, magnetic-fields, electromagnetism, electromagnetic-induction, and motors-and-generators.

15. Generated the modern physics bundle: atomic-models, photoelectric-effect, quantum-theory, matter-waves, special-relativity, mass-energy-equivalence, atomic-nucleus, radioactivity, nuclear-fission, nuclear-fusion, and binding-energy.

16. Generated the astrophysics and applied physics bundle: stellar-life-cycle, hertzsprung-russell-diagram, black-holes, neutron-stars, galaxies, dark-matter, big-bang-theory, medical-physics, engineering-physics, materials-physics, and environmental-physics.

Key Change
The physics encyclopedia moved from outline planning into a structured article-production pipeline, with anchor pages separated from branch/topic pages and large topic sets delivered as reusable HTML bundles.

Outcome
The MGU Physics Encyclopedia now has a broad first-pass article base covering scientific foundations, classical mechanics, energy, momentum, gravity, waves, optics, thermodynamics, fluids, electricity, magnetism, modern physics, astrophysics, and applied physics. The articles are organized by dependency order rather than alphabetical order, making later cross-linking and curriculum expansion easier.

Validation
Each bundle was generated as a downloadable ZIP file containing the expected `.html` files. File lists were printed after generation to confirm that the requested slugs were included. Breadcrumbs, hero sections, article sections, related-topic sections, bottom navigation, footer metadata, and the shared `/component/main.css` and `/component/main.js` references were preserved in the generated pages.

Rules Going Forward
Anchor/front-matter pages should go under `/library/encyclopedia/anchor/`.

Branch indexes should stay navigational and should not be overloaded with long overview content.

Specific topic articles should live inside the appropriate branch folder, such as `/library/encyclopedia/physics/classical-mechanics/`, `/waves-sound/`, `/thermodynamics/`, `/electricity-magnetism/`, `/modern-physics/`, or the relevant applied/astrophysics branch.

Article production should continue in dependency order, not alphabetical order.

Each article should keep the full MGU encyclopedia HTML shell, including metadata, hero, breadcrumb, article sections, related links, bottom navigation, footer date, and main script reference.

Related links should point laterally within the same branch when possible and back to prerequisite concepts when useful.

Notes
The first-pass articles are good foundational encyclopedia entries, but they can later be expanded with diagrams, equations, examples, glossary callouts, practice questions, MathJax where needed, and stronger branch-specific navigation. Some cross-links may need path adjustment after final folder placement, especially when files are moved from flat ZIP bundles into nested site folders.

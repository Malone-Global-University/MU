WORKFLOW NOTE

Title
Physics Encyclopedia Taxonomy and Index Bundle Setup

Date
2026-05-21

Area
MGU Library Encyclopedia / Science / Physics

Problem
The physics encyclopedia had placeholder branch folders but no real article structure yet. The original layout was too flat because major fields such as mechanics, electromagnetism, thermodynamics, optics, quantum mechanics, relativity, astrophysics, and nuclear physics all sat at the same level. That made the encyclopedia harder to scale and blurred the difference between reference articles, branch indexes, department lessons, and ebook chapters.

Intended Goal
Create a clean physics encyclopedia architecture that organizes physics by knowledge taxonomy, separates index pages from article pages, and supports future article writing without duplicating the high school physics textbook.

Root Cause
The initial folder structure treated physics branches as equal top-level categories instead of grouping them into broader reference divisions. It also used bottom-level folders with `index.html` files for topics that should function as individual encyclopedia articles.

Steps Taken

1. Reframed the physics encyclopedia as a taxonomy-first reference system rather than a course sequence or ebook structure.

2. Proposed a three-part top-level structure under `/library/encyclopedia/science/physics/`: `classical-physics/`, `modern-physics/`, and `applied-and-interdisciplinary-physics/`.

3. Reorganized classical physics into branch folders such as mechanics, electromagnetism, thermodynamics, waves and oscillations, optics, and fluid mechanics.

4. Reorganized modern physics into branch folders such as quantum mechanics, relativity, atomic physics, nuclear physics, particle physics, condensed matter physics, plasma physics, and statistical mechanics.

5. Reorganized applied and interdisciplinary physics into branch folders such as astrophysics, cosmology, geophysics, atmospheric physics, biophysics, medical physics, and computational physics.

6. Clarified that top-level and branch folders should receive `index.html` pages, while bottom-level topics should become article files such as `kinematics.html`, `ohms-law.html`, and `dark-matter.html`.

7. Reviewed the encyclopedia index-page template and adapted its use for physics root indexes, branch indexes, and topic category indexes.

8. Identified that article pages need flexible structures depending on topic type, including concept articles, law/equation articles, system articles, process articles, evidence/observation articles, technology/application articles, and branch/field articles.

9. Created a zipped folder bundle named `mgu_physics_encyclopedia_tree_bundle.zip` using the earlier nested-folder version.

10. Corrected the architecture after deciding bottom-level topic folders should instead become article files.

11. Created the final bundle named `mgu_physics_encyclopedia_indices_and_article_placeholders.zip`, containing 25 full `index.html` pages and 302 bottom-level article placeholder `.html` files.

Key Change
The physics encyclopedia was converted from a flat branch-folder plan into a scalable taxonomy where folders represent major categories and `.html` files represent individual articles.

Outcome
The MGU physics encyclopedia now has a clear working architecture for future production. The index system can support navigation by discipline, branch, and topic, while the article placeholders provide a ready path for writing reference entries without confusing them with textbook chapters or course lessons.

Validation
The final structure was checked conceptually against the intended MGU content model: encyclopedia pages follow knowledge taxonomy, department lessons follow course sequence, and ebooks follow book/chapter order. The final ZIP bundle was generated with the expected index-page/article-page separation and delivered as `mgu_physics_encyclopedia_indices_and_article_placeholders.zip`.

Rules Going Forward
Folders should represent disciplines, branches, categories, or major hubs. Bottom-level encyclopedia topics should usually be article `.html` files, not folders with their own `index.html`. Index pages should use the encyclopedia index template. Article pages should use flexible article structures based on the nature of the topic. Encyclopedia breadcrumbs should remain taxonomy-based, not course-sequence-based.

Notes
The physics encyclopedia should not duplicate the high school physics textbook. It should function as a reusable reference layer for textbook chapters, department lessons, infographics, and future advanced physics volumes. Future article writing should begin with high-value core concepts such as motion, force, energy, waves, light, heat, electric charge, current, atoms, nuclei, relativity, and quantum theory.

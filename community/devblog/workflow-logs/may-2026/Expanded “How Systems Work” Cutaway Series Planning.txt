WORKFLOW NOTE

Title
Expanded “How Systems Work” Cutaway Series Planning

Date
2026-05-21

Area
MGU Visual Brand / Engineering-Style Infographic Series

Problem
The “How Systems Work” engineering-style cutaway series had a strong initial identity, but the next expansion path was not yet organized. The completed examples showed the concept clearly, but additional candidate topics needed to be grouped into useful production categories so the series could grow in a deliberate way instead of becoming a loose list of unrelated diagrams.

Intended Goal
The series should develop into a recognizable MGU visual brand built around clear cutaway explanations of machines, infrastructure, energy systems, communication networks, medical technology, aerospace systems, and everyday engineered objects.

Root Cause
The early examples proved the format worked, but the planning layer still needed a broader taxonomy. Without grouped topic categories, future image batches could drift across subjects without a consistent structure, naming logic, or curriculum value.

Steps Taken

1. Reviewed the existing examples: jet engines, nuclear reactors, GPS, fiber optics, MRI machines, Wi-Fi, rocket staging, batteries, refrigerators, and hydroelectric dams.

2. Identified the shared pattern behind the successful examples: hidden internal structure, inputs and outputs, energy or signal flow, system components, control points, and visible cause-and-effect sequences.

3. Proposed expansion categories for the series, including Energy and Power Systems, Transportation and Machines, Communications and Computing Systems, Medical and Biological Technology, Water/Climate/Infrastructure, Aerospace and Navigation, Everyday Technology, and Defense/Safety/Security Systems.

4. Listed strong candidate infographic topics under each category, such as the electrical grid, solar panels, electric motors, the internet, cell towers, CPUs, water treatment plants, HVAC systems, CT scanners, space suits, radar, sonar, and emergency backup power.

5. Recommended a strongest next-set list focused on systems with broad educational value and high visual potential.

Key Change
The series was reframed from a collection of individual “how it works” posters into a structured MGU visual brand with expandable engineering categories.

Outcome
The project now has a clearer roadmap for future batches. The candidate topics are grouped by system type, making it easier to produce themed bundles, plan folder structures, avoid duplication, and connect the graphics to MGU departments such as physics, engineering, computer science, medicine, infrastructure, and defense.

Validation
The proposed topics were checked against the successful qualities of the existing examples: each topic can support labeled cutaways, flow diagrams, component explanations, and practical educational value. The strongest recommendations also span multiple MGU content areas instead of concentrating only on spectacular machines.

Rules Going Forward
Choose topics that reveal an internal process, not just an object. Favor systems with clear inputs, transformations, outputs, controls, and failure points. Organize future batches by category so filenames, folders, lessons, and visual branding remain consistent. Prioritize topics that can serve both as standalone educational posters and as support visuals for MGU course pages.

Notes
The strongest next expansion direction is civilization-scale systems: electrical grid, solar power, electric motors, internet infrastructure, cell towers, computer processors, water treatment, HVAC, medical scanners, and space systems. This gives the series a wider identity beyond machines alone and positions it as a signature MGU explanatory visual brand.

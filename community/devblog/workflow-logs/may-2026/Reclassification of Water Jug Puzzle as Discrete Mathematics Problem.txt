WORKFLOW NOTE

Title
Reclassification of Water Jug Puzzle as Discrete Mathematics Problem

Date
2026-05-21

Area
Mathematics taxonomy analysis / logic puzzle classification / conceptual problem-structure mapping

Problem
The “water jug” puzzle from the film *Die Hard with a Vengeance* was initially being interpreted similarly to a work-rate or arithmetic transfer problem because it involved moving measurable quantities between containers. This obscured the deeper mathematical structure underlying the puzzle and risked categorizing it incorrectly within algebraic word-problem frameworks.

Intended Goal
Identify the correct mathematical classification of the water jug puzzle and distinguish it from continuous-rate algebra problems by analyzing its operational structure, constraints, and theoretical foundations.

Root Cause
The puzzle presents itself superficially as a practical quantity-transfer scenario, which resembles common arithmetic and work-rate problems taught in elementary algebra. However, the underlying structure is discrete and state-based rather than continuous and rate-based. The confusion originated from focusing on the surface narrative instead of the mathematical mechanics governing solvability.

Steps Taken
1.
Analyzed the water jug puzzle as a formal mathematical problem rather than a cinematic scenario.

2.

Compared the puzzle structure against classical work-rate and mixture problems to identify differences in operational behavior.

3.

Mapped the puzzle to discrete mathematics concepts including state-space traversal, constraint satisfaction, and Diophantine solvability.

4.

Connected the solvability condition to greatest common divisor reasoning and Bézout-style integer combinations.

5.

Compared the puzzle structurally to the classical “wolf, goat, and cabbage” river-crossing puzzle to identify a shared state-transition framework.

6.

Distinguished monotonic continuous-change problems from reversible constrained-transition systems.

Key Change
The water jug puzzle was reclassified from a superficial arithmetic-transfer interpretation to a discrete mathematics and constraint-based state-transition problem grounded in number theory and Diophantine structure.

Outcome
The mathematical identity of the puzzle became substantially clearer. The discussion established that the puzzle belongs more naturally to logic puzzles, recreational mathematics, discrete mathematics, and state-space search theory rather than to traditional work-rate algebra. The comparison with river-crossing puzzles also clarified the importance of reversible constrained operations in this category of problems.

Validation
Validation was performed through structural comparison:

* Work-rate problems rely on continuous rates and proportional relationships.
* Water jug and river-crossing puzzles rely on legal state transitions and constrained move sequences.
* The jug puzzle’s solvability condition aligns with greatest common divisor and integer-combination theory rather than continuous algebraic modeling.

Rules Going Forward

* Classify mathematical problems according to operational structure rather than surface narrative.
* Distinguish continuous quantitative-change systems from discrete state-transition systems.
* Treat reversible constrained-move puzzles as discrete mathematics or logic frameworks unless explicit continuous-rate dynamics are present.
* Use state-space reasoning as a classification signal for transport and transfer puzzles.

Notes
The discussion suggested a potentially useful taxonomy category for educational organization:

* State Transition Puzzles
* Constraint Satisfaction Puzzles
* Transport Logic Problems

The comparison between the water jug puzzle and the wolf-goat-cabbage puzzle highlighted how many classical logic puzzles share graph-theoretic and search-based foundations beneath simple storytelling forms.

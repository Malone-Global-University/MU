WORKFLOW NOTE

Title
Capital Markets Lesson 1.1 Template Alignment

Date
2026-05-21

Area
MGU Professional Studies / Financial Systems / Capital Markets & Securities Operations Track / Lesson 1.1

Problem
The draft for Capital Markets & Securities Operations Lesson 1.1 was thinner and less structurally disciplined than the Wealth & Asset Operations lessons, which are currently the gold standard. Several sections repeated material, the hero block was too long for the intended visual design, and Core Concepts overlapped with Key Terms instead of serving a distinct instructional purpose.

Intended Goal
Lesson 1.1 should follow the proven Wealth & Asset lesson pattern while adapting it to Capital Markets content. Each section should have a distinct role, avoid unnecessary repetition, and make operational risk more explicit without bloating the lesson.

Root Cause
The Capital Markets draft treated the 21-section template as a content checklist rather than as a section-by-section instructional system. Core Concepts were written like expanded glossary entries, Key Terms repeated the same material in shorter form, and risk was often implied instead of tied to explicit failure points.

Steps Taken

1. Identified the Wealth & Asset Operations lessons as the benchmark standard for density, tone, and repeatable structure.
2. Rewrote the Lesson 1.1 hero paragraph to match the shorter, visually workable length of the Wealth & Asset hero style.
3. Clarified that Core Concepts should contain 2–3 high-level theoretical insights that make the lesson “click,” while Key Terms should remain simple glossary-linked terminology.
4. Rejected the earlier Core Concepts rewrite because it was still behaving like a glossary.
5. Established a cleaner rule: Concepts = insight; Terms = language.
6. Reframed risk as something that should be stated explicitly at hazard points instead of merely implied through general discussion.

Key Change
Core Concepts and Key Terms were separated into two different instructional layers: Core Concepts explain overarching system logic, while Key Terms provide simple glossary-ready definitions.

Outcome
The lesson development process is now more aligned with the Wealth & Asset gold standard. Capital Markets Lesson 1.1 has a clearer direction: compact hero, non-redundant sections, explicit operational risk, and stronger separation between theoretical insight and basic terminology.

Validation
The revised approach was tested against the user’s own minimal Bank Operations example, where Core Concept functioned as explanatory narrative and Key Terms functioned as basic vocabulary. The comparison confirmed that the Capital Markets draft needed conceptual compression rather than more glossary-style expansion.

Rules Going Forward
Core Concepts should contain only a few high-level ideas that explain how the system works beneath the surface. Key Terms should be short, linkable glossary definitions. New Capital Markets lessons should be compared section-by-section against the Wealth & Asset benchmark, not judged only by length. Risk should be explicit wherever a process can fail, especially in workflow, example, mistakes, and practical application sections.

Notes
The Wealth & Asset lessons should not be redesigned right now. They remain the current benchmark. The immediate task is to bring Capital Markets & Securities Operations lessons up to that standard while making only targeted improvements: clearer risk language, less repetition, and stronger section discipline.

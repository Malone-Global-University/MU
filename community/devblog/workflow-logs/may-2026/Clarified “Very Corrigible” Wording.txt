WORKFLOW NOTE

Title
Clarified “Very Corrigible” Wording

Date
2026-05-21

Area
Writing, vocabulary, phrasing

Problem
The phrase “very corrigible” was technically understandable but sounded stiff, uncommon, and potentially awkward depending on context.

Intended Goal
Identify clearer alternatives that preserve the meaning of being highly open to correction, improvement, guidance, or reform.

Root Cause
“Corrigible” is a formal word, and intensifying it with “very” can sound unnatural compared with more idiomatic phrases.

Steps Taken

1. Defined the meaning of “very corrigible.”
2. Separated alternatives by tone: formal, positive, growth-oriented, and critical.
3. Recommended phrases such as “highly coachable,” “readily correctable,” “highly receptive to feedback,” and “amenable to correction.”

Key Change
Replaced the awkward phrase “very corrigible” with more natural context-based alternatives.

Outcome
The wording options became clearer, more flexible, and easier to apply in academic, professional, or character-description contexts.

Validation
The alternatives were checked against tone and usage: some fit positive growth, some fit formal writing, and some carry more critical implications.

Rules Going Forward
Use “corrigible” sparingly unless the tone is formal or philosophical; for ordinary writing, prefer “coachable,” “receptive to correction,” or “amenable to guidance.”

Notes
Best general replacement: “highly receptive to correction.” Best professional replacement: “highly coachable.”

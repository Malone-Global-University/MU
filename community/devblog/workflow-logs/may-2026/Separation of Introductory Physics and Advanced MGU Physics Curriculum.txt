WORKFLOW NOTE

Title
Separation of Introductory Physics and Advanced MGU Physics Curriculum

Date
2026-05-21

Area
MGU Physics curriculum architecture, advanced physics volume planning, introductory physics textbook structure, long-term textbook ecosystem organization

Problem
The developing MGU Physics outlines had begun blending multiple educational levels into a single framework. The original five-volume physics structure mixed introductory survey physics with upper-division and near graduate-level theoretical material such as tensor methods, Hilbert space formalism, quantum field theory concepts, advanced statistical mechanics, and relativistic field formulations. This created scope creep, inconsistent audience targeting, oversized volumes, and unclear progression between beginner and advanced learners.

Intended Goal
Create a clean two-tier physics curriculum system:

1. A self-contained high-school/introductory physics textbook using algebra-based mathematics and broad conceptual coverage.
2. A separate advanced five-volume theoretical physics sequence that progressively develops rigorous mathematical and conceptual physics in an accessible but serious format approaching advanced theoretical treatments.

Root Cause
The earlier outline evolved organically from a general physics textbook into a comprehensive physics curriculum library without explicitly separating introductory pedagogy from advanced theoretical instruction. As more advanced material was added, the boundaries between survey physics, undergraduate physics, and theoretical physics became structurally blurred.

Steps Taken

1. Evaluated the relationship between the original master physics textbook outline and the proposed five-volume MGU Physics series.
2. Identified optics placement inconsistency between Volume II and Volume III and discussed pedagogical implications of grouping all wave phenomena together.
3. Distinguished the introductory “Physics: Foundations of Physical Science” textbook from the advanced MGU physics sequence.
4. Redesigned the advanced series into a coherent theoretical progression:

   * Volume I: Classical Mechanics and Mathematical Foundations
   * Volume II: Waves, Fields, and Continuum Physics
   * Volume III: Electromagnetism and Classical Field Theory
   * Volume IV: Quantum Theory and Statistical Physics
   * Volume V: Relativity, Modern Physics, and Frontiers
5. Reorganized the previously drafted advanced HTML outline into the new advanced-series structure.
6. Created a dedicated algebra-based high-school/introductory physics textbook outline intended to scaffold students toward the advanced series.
7. Established the conceptual distinction between:

   * introductory physics literacy,
   * advanced theoretical progression,
   * and mathematical methods/reference materials.
8. Recommended separating the mathematical methods handbook from the instructional physics volumes to reduce structural overload and maintain pedagogical clarity.

Key Change
The MGU physics ecosystem was formally divided into a beginner-friendly introductory physics textbook and a separate advanced five-volume theoretical physics curriculum.

Outcome
The physics curriculum now has a clear educational ladder and audience segmentation. The introductory textbook remains accessible to high-school and early college learners, while the advanced series is free to pursue deeper abstraction, mathematical rigor, and theoretical sophistication without compromising introductory pedagogy. The resulting structure better supports long-term expansion into a full university-scale physics library.

Validation
Validation was performed through:

* comparison of topic scope against standard physics curriculum progression,
* review of pedagogical sequencing,
* structural analysis of volume balance,
* consistency checks between volume titles and included subject matter,
* and alignment of mathematical prerequisites with intended audience level.

The final structure now shows:

* coherent escalation of difficulty,
* reduced overlap between volumes,
* cleaner specialization boundaries,
* and clearer relationships between survey physics and theoretical physics.

Rules Going Forward

* Keep introductory physics separate from advanced theoretical physics.
* Do not place linear algebra, tensor methods, Hilbert space formalism, or advanced field theory inside the high-school-level physics book.
* Treat the advanced five-volume series as a distinct curriculum line rather than an extension of the introductory survey text.
* Keep optics consistently grouped according to the chosen pedagogical philosophy.
* Separate instructional textbook content from handbook/reference material whenever possible.
* Maintain clear mathematical prerequisite boundaries for each educational tier.

Notes
The advanced MGU Physics series now resembles a structured accessible-theoretical curriculum rather than a traditional oversized survey textbook. The progression aligns well with the long-term MGU philosophy of layered educational systems:

* introductory foundations,
* advanced theoretical development,
* and dedicated mathematical/reference support materials.

Future work may include:

* full chapter-level breakdowns for each advanced volume,
* prerequisite dependency maps,
* laboratory/manual integration,
* computational physics companion materials,
* and textbook-to-ebook semantic HTML conversion planning.

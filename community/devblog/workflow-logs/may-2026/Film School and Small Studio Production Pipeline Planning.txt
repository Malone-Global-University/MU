WORKFLOW NOTE

Title
Film School and Small Studio Production Pipeline Planning

Date
2026-05-21

Area
Film education, production roles, studio planning

Problem
The film production process was broad and difficult to organize into clear roles, responsibilities, skills, and studio growth stages.

Intended Goal
Create a clear map of what film students learn, what film careers do, how production flows from idea to release, and how a small studio can scale from one person to a larger crew.

Root Cause
Film work combines creative, technical, business, and logistical responsibilities, so the roles can blur unless they are separated by production stage and crew size.

Steps Taken

1. Defined the major areas learned in film school: writing, directing, cinematography, editing, sound, production management, film history, and industry practice.
2. Mapped common career titles to actual responsibilities, including producer, director, screenwriter, cinematographer, editor, sound designer, production designer, and assistant director.
3. Built a full film production pipeline from development through distribution.
4. Converted the pipeline into two HTML visual formats: a detailed page diagram and a cleaner boxed flowchart.
5. Identified the user’s initial studio roles as executive producer, director, and head writer.
6. Mapped a lean growth path from one-person studio to 5-person crew to 10–15 person short film crew.

Key Change
The filmmaking process was reorganized from a vague career field into a structured production system with clear stages, roles, outputs, and scaling paths.

Outcome
The user now has a practical framework for building a small short-film studio, understanding which responsibilities they will personally carry at first, and knowing which roles to add as production complexity increases.

Validation
The structure was checked by following the full production chain: idea, script, planning, filming, post-production, and release. The crew progression also matched increasing responsibility separation from solo work to collaborative short-film production.

Rules Going Forward
Keep film studio planning organized by production stage, not just by job title. Add new crew roles only when they remove a real bottleneck in writing, production, sound, camera, logistics, or post-production.

Notes
The likely next practical additions after the user’s three core roles are cinematographer, sound recordist, production assistant, and eventually assistant director or production manager.
